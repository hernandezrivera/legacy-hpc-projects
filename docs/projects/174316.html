Project Summary

The IRC’s health response interventions will continue to align to the Health Sector response objective to provide access to life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian health assistance to affected IDPs, returnees and host population; to establish, expand and strengthen communicable diseases outbreak prevention and response systems; and to strengthen access to health care, health system recovery and build humanitarian and development linkage.
IRC’s health program locations cover 14 LGAs in BAY states. MMC, Jere, Monguno, Gwoza, Bama, Mafa, Konduga, Askira Uba and Magumeri LGAs in Borno state, Michika in Adamawa and Damature, Gujiba and Gulani in Yobe.
The IRC, being the implementer and of the leading NGO in Monguno and MMC/Jere LGAs in providing health care service) will work closely with the Health Cluster and SMOH, community and other cluster partners to directly implement the health response in this two (MMC/Jere and Monguno) LGAs.
The IRC interventions will include:
• Maintain primary health care services in targeted areas through provision of basic equipment, drugs, medical supplies, basic lab equipment and supplies and reproductive health supplies
• Communicable disease control and outbreak response including supplies
• Support immunizations via fixed and mobile health clinics targeting returnees, displaced people, and other vulnerable groups including emergency mass vaccination campaigns
• Capacity building interventions will include
• Emergency preparedness and communicable disease control and outbreak response
• Community based interventions including awareness raising, hygiene promotion, education and participation in health-related issues
• Support to referral system for emergency health care
• Support to minor rehabilitation and repairs of health facilities.
The IRC will continue with both internal and external collaboration with Nutrition, Women empowerment and protection (WPE) WASH, and other Sectors / health actors for a coordinated response interventions

1 - Needs

The continued insecurity and increased action of armed opposition groups, has limited access to life-saving primary and reproductive healthcare services and routine immunization resulting in significant populations with restricted access to Government run health facilities either due to the destruction of existing health facilities or an overwhelming of the existing health facilities. Due to associated challenges, there are 2,381,943 people in Need and 2,198,294 have been targeted at the Health Cluster level across health actors. In addition, the Nigeria Demographic Health Survey (DHS) 2018 shows that the Northeast states have some of the worst health indices. The percentage distribution for modern contraceptive use among women 15-49 years who are either married or sexually active is 18.25 in Adamawa, 5.4% Borno and 17% Yobe. The unmet contraceptive need among women of the same age groups is Yobe -21-25%, Borno-26- 30% and Adamawa-10-15%. Data on ante-natal care shows that the percentage distribution among women of reproductive age is 3.7%, 8.9%, and 0.7% respectively for Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states. The percentage for facility delivery among women of reproductive age in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa is 24.6%, 16.2% and 37.7% respectively. The immunization coverage, for Yobe is 20.6 while Adamawa is 36.8%. 46.5% and 27.9% of children in Borno and Yobe have had Fever, within the two weeks preceding the survey and the prevalence rate of diarrhea among children is 15.6%, 12.2, and 40.5% in Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe respectively. The average under 5 mortality for Adamawa, Borno and Yobe is 104, 86, and 152 respectively, whilst infant Mortality is 90 in Yobe, 68 in Adamawa and 52 Borno state. The Health Resources Availability and Monitoring Systems (HeRAMs) for the BAY states reports that 19% of health facilities are fully damaged and 14% partially damaged from the activities of AOGs, other factors that impede the availability of the health services include the lack health care workers, limited training and lack of medical supplies. In Borno the HeRAMs reports that 33% of the assessed health facilities were partially damaged. Physical barriers, distance, lack of finance and inadequate number of qualified healthcare workers also contributed to the limited access to lifesaving healthcare services for IDPs andthe majority of host community members. With COVID-19 in the BAY states affected population continues to be vulnerable Prevailing vulnerabilities across the states with weak health systems, congested or overcrowded camps and settlements, high poverty rates and limited humanitarian access have all increased the potential of overwhelming the capacity of the health systems. Since the reported outbreak of COVID-19, there is reduction in immunization services, ante-natal care and curative care. The WHO’s early warning alert and response system (EWARS) indicates that the most prevalent morbidities across the BAY states are Malaria, acute respiratory tract infection and severe diarrheal diseases. As the rainy season approaches the risk of malaria is expected and the outbreak of cholera is anticipated. In addition to anticipated increases in cases loads of endemic disease, there is an ongoing measles outbreak across the Bay states. Access to secondary/tertiary health services remains a challenge, due to a weak and inefficient referral systems and cost implications on the population. Additionally, ambulance services are not readily available, and in some instances there are no secondary healthcare facilities available.

2 - Activities

The delivery of integrated comprehensive primary and reproductive health servicestargeted individuals, however there will be a focus on children under-􀂦ve, adolescent girls,women of child bearing age, pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) and persons withdisabilities. The IRC will provide health systems support to ten existing healthcarefacilities, run by the State Primary Healthcare Development Agency (SPHCDA) and providedirect health service delivery in thirteen LGAs in the BAY states to strengthen the provisionof primary and reproductive healthcare services. The IRC ensure that the provision ofprimary health care services includes both preventative and curative services, with a focuson integrated management of childhood illnesses for children under 5, provision of routineimmunization services, prevention and treatment of communicable illnesses and thetreatment of non-communicable diseases. Sexual and Reproductive healthcare servicesprovision will be strengthened to provide quality ante-natal, post-natal, syndromicmanagement of sexually transmitted illness, family planning services, basic emergencyobstetrics and neonatal and newborn care including safe deliveries, clinical care of sexualassault survivors and post-abortion care. The Health systems support provided at allhealth facilities will include capacity building of healthcare workers through mentorshipand on-job-coaching, supportive supervision through the use of systemic qualityassurance tools, and the distribution of essential medicines. The IRC strengthen thereferral pathways and mechanisms across all intervention locations, through theestablishment of ambulance services, payment of medical bills and feeding of admittedpatients with one care giver. The IRC adapted its ongoing provision of essential primaryand reproductive healthcare services, in light of the covid-19 pandemic by strengtheninginfection, control and prevention measures through the distribution of disinfectants andcleaning supplies. The triage process will be modi􀂦ed at all supported health facilitiesthrough the use of a covid-19 speci􀂦c screening tool thereby allowing for effectivescreening of individuals who gain entry to IRC supported health facilities; with sourcecontrol of identi􀂦ed cases of suspected COVID-19 cases for onward referral to isolation/treatment centers. The IRC also strengthen community based surveillance through the useof trained community health workers who will carry out morbidity and mortalitysurveillance tracking and report noti􀂦able diseases. In addition, through ongoingcontinuous monitoring and analysis of disease trends, endemic diseases such as malaria,acute watery diarrhea/cholera and measles will be monitored. In situations of increasedmorbidities or even declared outbreaks the IRC will adapt its services to be responsive tothe outbreak needs. To respond to the possible cholera outbreak, the IRC plans to set upORPs for case management and carry out risk communication and communityengagement activities through the development and dissemination of information,education and communication materials, and provision of community health educationon prevention of cholera.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The IRC will prioritize the needs of women and girls to narrow the gender gap. Womenwill be encouraged to participate in mother to mother support groups (MtMSGs), whichare groups of mothers who receive training on best mothering practices and use theirknowledge to educate the community. They will also be encouraged to become CHVs.Their ability to visit homes more easily than men will be particularly useful for followupof family planning clients, home deliveries, malnourished children and defaultertracing. At the same time, the IRC will explore ways to also reach men withoutreinforcing gender norms. For instance, men’s participation in health awareness-raisingsessions and men-to-men support groups will be encouraged. The IRC will integratethe four principles of protection mainstreaming in their workings in all of its healthfacilities which will be accessible to the bene􀂦ciaries. The IRC will ensure that allvulnerable groups safely access all facilities; where necessary, the IRC’s Health andPRoL teams will collaborate to ensure that basic structures like ramps are constructedand all pathways made comfortable for persons with limited mobility. Health facilitieswill have private consultation rooms for privacy and con􀂦dentiality purposes, andscreens/curtains provided for privacy. The health team will continue to engagemembers from both host communities and IDPs as CHVs while adhering to the age,gender & diversity mainstreaming principles to ensure that issues affecting thedifferent groups are well articulated. The health team and the protection teams willjointly conduct community dialogue sessions with Bulamas, community structures,community/opinion leaders and community members to discuss uptake of projectservices, key challenges, and gaps including issues on protection. Through FGDs andkey informant interviews (KIIs), the IRC will collect information from different groups(women, men, girls and boys). The IRC will also gather information through existingstructures (i.e. community leaders, Protection Action Groups (PAGs), MtMSGs, healthand nutrition data). This will help the IRC assess the speci􀂦cs that exist among theneeds of the population, depending on age and sex. IRC conducts regular protectionrisk analysis will enable the IRC to identify protection risks speci􀂦c to women, men,girls or boys, Protection responses will be speci􀂦cally tailored to reduce thevulnerabilities of the speci􀂦c segments of the population, while simultaneouslyincreasing their resilience.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The IRC has established Accountability Feedback Mechanism (AFM) in all itsoperational areas. This AFM will be maintained to receive feedback and complaintsand provide responses on actions taken or to be taken will be provided to communitymembers. In addition, the IRC will also receive feedback from the established IDPcamp compliant mechanism, and action plans to be taken and responses will beprovided to community members. In addition, other approaches the IRC uses arethrough dialogue meetings with the community leaders. The project will integrateclient responsive feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all groups,including women, girls, and people with disabilities. These will include regular focusgroup discussions (FGDs) and listening sessions that include sessions speci􀂦callywith women, girls, and persons with disabilities, the elderly, and other vulnerablegroups. It will additionally make use of the existing suggestion boxes and communitydiscussion forums to collect bene􀂦ciary feedback on quality emergency responseactions. The IRC also has a toll-free hotline with dedicated hotline handlers who recordand refer complaints to the appropriate staff. The number for the hotline is advertisedon IRC banners at program implementation sites. The IRC’s hotline, suggestion boxesand community engagement meetings/focus group discussions will ensure thatcommunity voices are inclusive of the implementation process and furthermore serveas reporting mechanisms in case of maltreatment or abuse. Client exit interviews willbe conducted to receive feedback directly from beneficiaries attending clinics, whichwill allow the IRC to make informed adjustments in project implementation.
