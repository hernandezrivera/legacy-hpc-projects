Project Summary

According to the rural water information management system (RWIMS) data, in rural areas of Zimbabwe of the 55,593 water sources, only 30 per cent have water, and are functional and protected, which increases the risk of WASH-related diseases, especially in 23.8 per cent of households lacking improved access. About 16 per cent of households travel more than a kilometre to fetch water from the nearest primary water source. The first coronavirus was recorded on the 21st of March 2020, and by the 24th of June, the number had risen to 530 confirmed cases. The World Health Organisation (WHO) stipulates that ideally every person should access between 50 and 100 litres of water per day to ensure the most basic needs are met and the outbreak of disease is prevented. However, the combined effects of drought, economic challenges and the COVID-pandemic have affected the ability of households to access basic services. CARE seeks to support public health responses to contain the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic by decreasing morbidity and mortality. This will be achieved by creating an enabling environment for the COVID-19 response through immediate interventions to improve governance, human rights and gender equality, coordination, social cohesion and service provision at district level. Key activities will include the following;
• Support the setting up of hand washing stations at various public places.
• Support mass media campaigns on COVID-19 prevention using various channels of communication (billboards, radio shows, radio jingles etc) taking into account literacy levels and access of different groups.
• Support the health system to ensure availability of basic water and sanitation services in the key prioritized health care facilities.
• Providing access to critical handwashing facilities including soap and alcohol-based sanitizers, WASH-related hygiene kits.
• Ensure field and front-line staff engaged in WASH humanitarian responses have adequate access to PPE and practice key risk reduction practices like social distancing.
Project participants will be engaged throughout the project life cycle to ensure activities meet their needs related to water, sanitation and hygiene in the face of COVID-19.

1 - Activities

The project seeks to support the setting up of hand washing stations at various public places. These facilities will enhance handwashing for people in public places. Mass media campaigns on COVID-19 prevention using various channels of communication (billboards, radio shows, radio jingles etc) taking into account literacy levels and access of different groups will be supported. Messaging will reinforce the preventative measures against COVID-19. It will also enhance access to accurate and current information on corona virus and the safety preventative measures. Different information, education and communication materials that is suitable for different audiences as well as the level of accessibility. The health system will be supported to ensure the availability of basic water and sanitation services in the key prioritized health care facilities. The provision of critical handwashing materials including soap and alcohol-based sanitizers, as well as WASH-related hygiene kits will enhance handwashing at the critical times. In addition, the project will ensure field and front-line staff engaged in WASH humanitarian responses have adequate access to PPE and practice key risk reduction practices like social distancing.

2 - Indicators

The following key indicators will be used to measure the project progress and success; -Number of handwashing stations at various public places-Number of men, women, girls and boys reached with mass media campaigns-Number of health facilities supported with water and or sanitation facilities-Number of handwashing materials distributed-Number of Personal Protective equipment distributed.
