Project Summary

Jarablus, is a Syrian city administratively belonging to Aleppo Governorate, lies on the western bank of the Euphrates river. The city population has increased significantly during the Syrian civil war. It is considered to be the de facto capital of the opposition-controlled North-western part of Syria.
Over the past period, the city and its countryside had considered safe and had received IDPs from rural areas of Southern Idlib, eastern Hama, Damascus, and Homs, its population had reached 180,000 people including 100,000 IDPs, and now the city and its countryside are receiving many IDPs due to the military campaign in the northern Hama countryside and the southern countryside of Idlib.
There are five schools in the city of Jarabulus, which can accommodate 60,000 students. They were equipped and fully supported by the Turkish government through the local councils. They had rehabilitated schools’ classrooms and its water and sanitation facilities, also the proved schools with operation cost including fuel and with teaching materials and teachers’ salaries, schools are provided with water permanently through the Jarabulus water station that reaches the school via the main water network, on the other hand, there is no support for Jarablus rural schools except of teachers’ salaries. The schools in the countryside suffer from a lack of services, especially those related to WASH services.
The assessment carried out shows the followings:
- Severe damage to sanitation facilities,
- There is a significant lack of sanitation facilities in the assessed schools, and for this reason, students are often obliged to make use of the neighbors’ houses,
- Some schools have partially functional facilities that they need maintenance, repairment, and connection to the water and sanitation network;
- Other schools require an expansion of the existing facilities by constructing/providing new facilities.
- There is no water pumping through the network for schools, as the main stations are sufficient for Jarablus only and do not cover the countryside There are artesian wells in some schools, but the cost of repairing and operating them is high and ineffectual, and accordingly schools should be provided with water through trucked water.
- The number of cases of waterborne diseases such as diarrhea and leishmaniasis is remarkably high comparing to other communities.
White Hats intervention will focus on the rehabilitation of water and sanitation facilities, provision of drinkable water to the schools as well as hygiene awareness-raising campaigns, aiming to enhance children's access to schools and avoid dropouts due to the lack of infrastructure and critical services in schools. By accomplishing the project objectives, the intervention targets students, teachers, administrative staff, with a focus on students with disabilities by providing the facilities with special equipment for disabled people.
The project aims at improving the condition of WASH facilities and ensures healthy and hygienic facilities for students, teachers, administrative staff, and children with disabilities.
Through improving access to water and sanitation services for children in schools and hygiene promotion campaigns, their hygiene awareness will be increased and this is primarily reflected in the caregivers through daily practices that will move from schools to homes and this will have impact on the community as a whole. We aim from this project that children and youth people be an element of change in communities as the future group that will contribute to the improvement and development of society.

5 - Implementation of activities

Improve access to WASH facilities will be achieved through a set of interventions:1- Rehabilitation/renovation/expansion of latrines to ensure the following parameters;1.1- Gender segregation and culturally appropriate1.2- Adequate students to toilet seat ratio1.3- Accessible by Children with disabilities1.4- Child-friendly1.5- Light and Vent appropriate1.6- Door locks2- Hand WASH points (hand washbasin)3- Sufficient water storage capacity for drinking, flushing, and cleaning4- Adequate, accessible and safe drinking water points5- Provision of garbage bins and solid waste containers 6- Provision of hygiene materials 7- Drain-pit emptying and rehabilitation.8- Trucked water at rate 3 L per day per student for four monthsAdditionally, two awareness campaigns will be implemented to address hygiene promotion, water conservation, safe water handling, and Water Borne Diseases WBDs. During the campaigns, IEC materials will be distributed and posters will be posted at the target schools. The campaigns will be carried out through meetings with parents, children, and community key persons. The awareness campaigns aim to enhance community behaviors and knowledge regarding hygiene-related issues. Pertaining to this aim, White Hats will distribute hygiene kits to the students to emphasize cleanliness and encourage best hygiene practices. The hygiene kits will include soap for handwashing, a toothbrushsh, toothpaste, and towels.At the end of this project, children will have better access to healthy, hygienic, and child-friendly WASH facilities at schools, a better understanding of personal hygiene practices and hence more protection against skin diseases and other WBDs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

security measures will be taken during the implementation of rehabilitation/construction works while children inside schools. Would be there any security fences or separate entrance for trucks and labours than children entrance to the school, would there be any temporary WASH facilities while repair works are ongoing? Is there any protection or quick medical support in case of children injured as a result of work. White Hats will include a term for implementation as safety measures according to which the contractor will have to work after school hours and during weekends. In this way, White Hats will make sure children remain safe during school hours.Currently, all latrines blocks are partially functioning. Therefore, in each of the schools that have more than one block, the contractor will work on one block out of two, and when it is finished and usable for children, the contractor will execute works on the other block.In schools that have only one block, the contractor will first build the new latrine block, and then when it is ready and usable for children the contractor will rehabilitate the existing block.
