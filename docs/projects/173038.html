Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims at ensuring that vulnerable population (IDPs, refugees and asylum seekers and vulnerable among non-displaced) in the Southern region of Libya, (with specific focus on Ghat and Murzuq distrcts and in particular to Ghat, Alsharguiya and Taraghin municipalities) improve their health status, by ensuring their access to quality basic healthcare services.
Main pillars of the action are:
1) Provisioning of primary live-savings and reproductive health services, including SRH, child health, NCD, CD, immunization, nutritional assessment, COVID-19 triage, through two mobile medical teams operating within the Ministry of Health primary health care facilities
2) Design and provision of a tailored technical capacity-building package addressed to health teams operating the mobile Units and to health staff assigned to existing health facilities
3) Supporting the existing MoH staff operating in the target facilities with Training on the Job and mentoring
4) Strengthening community outreach and awareness with the engagement of Community Health Workers (CHWs)
5) Enforcing referral pathways and ensuring its functioning in the target areas
6) Provisioning of minor rehabilitation works to health facilities
7) Provisioning emergency drugs and supplies for emergency response and having also a special focus on COVID19
The project will be implemented by Helpcode in partnership with the Libyan NGO ODP – Organization for Development Pioneers.
Helpcode delivers assistance in Libya since 2017 with a sectoral focus on Health, Protection, Education and WASH. The geographical focus is mainly Fezzan, with activities also in Tripoli and Western Libya.
Helpcode offices are located in Ghat and Tripoli. Comprehensive Memorandum of Understanding is in place with the Ministry of Health and Helpcode has long-term partnership with the Libyan NGO ODP.
The two Organization have been partnering since 2019 and currently are related by a Cooperation Framework Agreement.
Helpcode and ODP bring as advantage a consolidated presence and experience in the South of Libya in the health sector and a privileged access thanks to previously build networks in the target areas. In particular:
• Currently Helpcode, in partnership with ODP, is implementing an 18 months project aiming at improving health services in Ghat, Tahala and Awainat (funded by the EUTF). The project will be completed by August 2021.
• Through the project, Helpcode has established a consolidated relationship with Ghat Municipality stakeholders as well as in other districts like Murzuq, Ubari, Brak Al Shati thanks to a network of staff and volunteers.
• Helpcode is currently providing mobile emergency health services, trainings to health workers and structural support in Ghat thanks to a team of four Libyan doctors and health professional, backed by experts from the Organizations.
• In the past years, Helpcode has worked in supporting four health centers in Ubari through trainings, direct service provision and rehabilitation intervention and still maintains good relation with the stakeholders in the area
• Helpcode and ODP developed strong working relation with authorities and stakeholders all over the Southern districts, which was confirmed during the assessment
Thanks to the connection described above, in the past weeks Helpcode and ODP conducted a rapid assessment on the situation of the Primary Health Care system in the area and identified some target areas in dire need of support.

1 - Needs

The proposal is informed considering the actual needs in the target areas. Needs were assessed using the following methodology:1) In the first phase, data from the HNO and the REACH MSNA were used. The potential project target areas were selected who rank as severity scale 4 and 3 and among those Helpcode and ODP have good access. 2) Each Municipality was then visited by Helpcode and ODP staff/volunteers to collect relevant information on the health system. Key informants (i.e Director or Health Service – attached to MoH and the Member of Municipal Council in charge of Health) were interviewed and Information where cross checked with other stakeholders3) A more in-depth Health Facility Assessment on some of the target facilities and related community was conducted to sustain the resultsAfter the process, it was agreed that the project will focus on: Ghat, Alsharguiya, Taraghin municipalities. In the target municipalities the health system is in a dire situation. Of the 10 covered health facilities, only 5 is providing some kind of curative services and they do it at a minimum level capacity, due to significant challenges in the accessibility and functionality of its infrastructures. Infrastructure, electric power and water system of the facilities are in critical conditions and require rehabilitation, to allow the delivery of essential health services. In REACH assessment (2020), the economic affordability of procuring medicine, for instance in Ghat (ranked 4 in severity scale) is compromised. Overall 10% vulnerable and 23% returnees complained they cannot afford. Nearly half (48%) of the vulnerable population in the community stated that there is a lack of essential medicine in the health facilities.This is also corroborated in HNO 2021, which cites availability of essential medicines is a major challenge (70% of functioning PHC facilities do not have any of the top 20 essential medicines).There are gaps in the availability of drugs and medical supplies. Amoxicillin is the only antibiotic present across the facilities, but none of them have a stock longer than a month; ibuprofen, prednisolone, salbutamol and fluconazole are also poorly available in the facilities. Among the maternal health drugs, only Folic Acid and Iron are available in at least one facility. Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) and zinc are available in all the facilities, but the latter only in small quantities. Most of the vaccines are in place, The health workforce is not adequate, neither in number nor in qualification, to provide basic health services to the community. The assessment provides evidence that there is a significant lack of medical personnel across the HFs. With reference to Ghat for instance, there are only two doctors from the MoHì over a total population of 28346 people (20015 PiN).As far as the nursing staff is concern, this is relatively in good numbers in some the PHCCs, but not working properly for the lack of suitable spaces for healthcare delivery, therefore compromising the provision of basic services A common problem to municipalities is the prevention and control of infections. IPC guidelines are not available, staff is not trained, there is no Focal Point appointed to monitor prevention precautions to ensure patients’ safety, safety boxes are not widely in use across the facilities. Inadequate supply of PPE (mainly gloves, masks and aprons), soap and environmental detergents (alcohol and chlorine) compromise the constant application of universal standards, putting health staff and patients at high risk of infection transmission, especially in the current critical situation of the COVID-19 pandemic The first priority should be the deployment of qualified staff to provide basic and life-threatening healthcare services to support the available staff and advocate with the Ministry of Health for deployment of additional staff, provide trainings to Health Workers, provide support to health facilities (rehab, equipment, ...)

2 - Activities

1.1 - Provide essential package of integrated health services at primary (integrated services cover emergency and trauma care, management of communicable and non-communicable diseases, maternal, neonatal and child health (MNCH), - two Mobile Emergency team will be deployed. Teams will be composed of at least 3 professionals: 1 Medical Doctor specialized in emergency and trauma management, to cover the high number of cases of injuries and ensure the management of any general OPD consultation, 1 Medical Doctor specialized, in OBGYN, pediatrics or Family Health, to cover the provision of essential MCH services, 1 nurse/midwife, to support the arrangement and organization of service delivery, facilitate the contact with the female population and 1 community mobilizer to support doctors and nurses/midwifes in bridging linguistic and cultural gaps with targeted communities, and liaise with key persons from the communities.1.2 - Provide continuous and interrupted immunization services to children - by supporting the distribution of vaccines and improve the cold chain 2.2 - Provide health facilities with essential medicines, medical supplies and equipment – Provisioning emergency drugs and supplies for emergency response, having also a special focus on COVID19 and proper follow up on their utilization2.3 - Increase access to health services by establishing functional health facilities and mobile medical teams by providing small rehabilitation / refurbishing of the target facilities and proper space for the Mobile Medical Team to operate2.4 - Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services - Design and provision of a tailored technical capacity-building package addressed to health teams operating the mobile Units and to health staff assigned to existing health facilities; Supporting the existing MoH staff operating in the facilities with Training on the Job and mentoring, establish and support CHWs
