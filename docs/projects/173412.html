Project Summary

Action Against Hunger’s Mental Health, Care Practices, Gender, and Protection program implements comprehensive, community-based MHPSS services for the crisis-affected Forcibly Displaced Myanmar Nationals (FDMNs - Rohingya refugees) and vulnerable host populations in order to enhance resilience and holistic well-being with special attention to transversality of gender and ensuring protection needs of the population. Project activities are channeled through established treatment facilities (e.g.PHCs, Upazila Health Complexes, OTPs, SCs, BFS, SSWGs, and MHWC) in 20 camps and 5 Unions; and through referral and follow-up coordination with partner agencies. Besides integrated centers, ACF is operating specialized MHPSS from 11 stand-alone Mental Health Wellness centers.
ACF engages in health system strengthening by building capacities of health care staff, ensuring adequate monitoring and supervision by mhGAP trained Clinical Psychologists. We strongly adhere to WHO proposed clinical interventions and IASC guidelines to provide specialized MHPSS services including ACF's internal guidelines. Adolescents, women, and men are provided safe and easy access to peer support groups, combined psychosocial life-skills along with trauma-informed counseling and psychosocial supports through clinical case management, appropriate referral( including Upazilla Health complex ), and follow-ups. The referral procedures will follow the Health Sector and Protection WG 4W referral pathways. ACF will incorporate timely changes in referral networks and service mapping relevant to refugee camps and host communities and accordingly update the inter-agency referral mechanism operationalized at the camp and health centers level. In providing support to the affected individuals ACF targets the whole social ecology of the person which includes the family and the community. All the MHPSS activities are contributing to the Health Sector activities.
Following the context-specific protocol, a team of trained psychologists ensures tele-counseling in the host community to provide MHPSS to the individuals and potential contacts who are apprehended.
The field-level activities are accompanied by behavior-focused sensitization complying with WHO-approved key messages; aimed at prevention and containment of the pandemic. Orientation on service mapping and referral are facilitated for quicker treatment.

1 - Identified Needs

National Mental Health Survey (NIMH, Oct 2019) suggests a prevalence of 16.8% mental disorders in the adult population (18-99 years) and 13.6% in children/young adolescents (7-17 years) with an average treatment gap for all diagnosed mental disorders as high as 93.4% - staggeringly high by all means. It indeed has implications for any health programme, especially non-communicable diseases prevention and treatment, as well as WHO’s push for Universal Health Coverage. From the 2017 refugee crisis, boys and girls represent 55% of all refugees; the majority of whom need MHPSS services, especially due to the emergence of non-communicable diseases/NCD risks from displacement, over-crowded settlements in camps, and growing caregiver stress. While significant progress has been made in delivering psychosocial support to large numbers of the affected populations, the need to scale up specialized mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) that addresses chronic trauma symptoms and violence experienced by the majority are essentially required to improve resilience and well-being of affected populations. Community-based care arrangements through primary health centers need to be prioritized for the larger communities to seek timely, easily accessible, and quality mental health supports. In addition, host communities living in Cox’s Bazar District, especially those residing in close proximity to refugee camps; in particular have unaddressed basic needs and are also exposed to serious mental health and protection risks due to a combination of factors, including poverty, limited to no access to specialized mental health services. The Rohingya influx has inevitably had a significant impact on competition for limited resources, access to key health services, and the environment in host communities as well as inter-community relations. Enhancing communication and community-based mental health initiatives for particularly vulnerable groups, as well as implementing community-owned need-based interventions are required to mitigate health burden with a reduction in common mental illnesses, morbidities, and injuries. Supporting sustainable community-based initiatives will be required to mitigate exposure to further protection risks and enhance social cohesion. The need for MHPSS has increased tremendously due to the pandemic. Women are likely to experience high levels of stress and mental health issues due to the increase in GBV, household work, and financial problems. Men also experience stress, mostly due to the containment measures’ impact on livelihood opportunities and the subsequent inability to provide for their families (In the shadows of the pandemic: the gendered impact of covid-19 on Rohingya and host communities, October 2020).

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Provision of equitable and quality MHPSS services through established ACF's mental health wellness centers at strategic locations in coordination with Health Sector and Camp-in-Charge (CIC)Activity 2: Appointment of qualified, skilled, mhGAP trained Psychologists in the centers for the provision of MHPSS servicesActivity 3: Health system strengthening to improve MHPSS service provision by capacity-building, supportive supervision, and monitoring of healthcare staff, community mental health workers, and volunteersActivity 4: COVID-19 risk communication and community engagement that includes awareness-raising on psychological distress, promotion of resilience and well-being among vulnerable families to improve trust and access of health services following WHO and Health Sector approved key messages Activity 5: Provision of tele-counseling to the COVID-19 positive and suspected cases in the host community to promote well-beingActivity 6: Use of digital innovations, such as tele-mental health services to provide psychosocial support and for early detection and management of mental health conditionsActivity 7: Providing mental health care and psychosocial support and promoting self-care strategies for frontline responders from MHPSS WG and Health Sector partners Activity 8: Using multiple communication approaches to build public confidence and encourage continued utilization of essential services during the disease outbreak and other emergencies. Engage with communities to inform the adaptation of services so they are more responsive to local needs.Activity 9: Provide practical and emotional support through informal networks and health professionals. Activity 10: Peer-to-peer support groups of adolescent boys and girls, women and men on MHPSS and health-focused essential life-skills.Activity 11: Individual and group psychosocial and psychological consultations for the vulnerable population with mild to moderate psychological distress Activity 12: Establishing and regularizing community and facility-based MHPSS support groups for enhancement in care-seeking and referral Activity 13: Screening, identification, and referral for common mental health conditions and illness by community volunteers and lay counselorsActivity 14: Support integration of early recognition of and treatment for antenatal and postnatal MNS disorders into modified ANC and PNC protocols in coordination with Civil Surgeon and MHPSS WG
