Project Summary

This project aims to enhance improvement of health standards for the prevention of cholera among 13,000 individuals living in the villages of Luri County (of Gurei, Kugi, Nanloni, Joppa, Gudele West, and provision of safe water supply for conflict affected populations in Lemon-gaba Rombur Payam. Based on the existing cholera risks, project activities will be geared towards reinforcing gains made in previous WASH interventions and recent assessment needs for reduction of vulnerabilities. Major effort will be on improving the access to basic WASH services for mitigation of morbidity and mortality related to diarrhea-illnesses by at list 85% among target group. Main activities will focus on behavior change that ensures water, food and hand safety at household levels, schools, public and community gathering places are at better standard.
The project will also ensure protection of women and girls by creating access to safe water supply which is secure to reduce further risk of Sexual Gender Based incidences in Rombur Payam and Northern Bari Payam. In these locations, girls and women spend most hours of the night at wells or move long distances to bushy streams to collect water. In the target areas, incidences of sexual Gender based violence were reported in the course of accessing water.
Capacity of community members will be strengthened alongside provision of sustained access to basic WASH services; and to ensure active participation of community members, key stakeholders will be involved at grass-root level. Recent WHEEL needs assessments will jointly be reviewed at each project site to tackle existing gaps. 10 Community Hygiene Promoters will be trained to improve access to hygiene knowledge with practical demonstration of messages of food hygiene, hand washing with soap, water treatment and storage. 3 Community Hygiene Supervisors will be recruited and trained to trace and report cases of acute watery diarrhea; and also to supervise bucket chlorination, disinfect households of persons with confirmed cholera cases. Homes of malnourished children benefiting from feeding programmes will also be traced and mothers helped to observe good hygiene habits. Supervisors will also support events of Focused Group Discussions that are aimed at involving the community members to play active role in cholera prevention and elimination of open defecation through community based approach to curb water contamination at the sources. 4 boreholes will be drilled to reduce risks associated to consumption of contaminated surface water; and further risk of sexual Gender Based Violence women/girls face in the course of collecting water from isolated wells and streams in Kugi, Nakulujo, Joppa and Gurei. 3 boreholes will be rehabilitated in Northern Bari Payam. 7 water user committees will be formed and trained (at list 5 females and 2 males in each group). Four (4) six stance VIP latrines will be constructed in schools in Rombur Payam. 4 school hygiene promotion clubs will be formed and teacher, PTA and pupils will be trained to support sustainability of school hygiene. WHEEL has own capacity to complete activities and will collaborate with the Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation during borehole drilling and rehabilitation, water quality tests, and training of water user committees and ensure handover is properly done. During project period WHEEL will keep participating in WASH cluster activities; and provide weekly and monthly reports. Support will also be given for other cholera mitigation activities under the WASH/HEALTH cluster especially mobilization of target communities during Oral Cholera Vaccination campaigns, World Water Day, Global Hand washing Day and World Toilet Day.

1 - Needs

Conflicts and high costs of living forced more than 610,000 people living in South Sudan’s urban areas to struggle with poor health standards due to limited WASH services. Since the conflict begun in 2013, WASH related diseases including cholera have been registered every year. The largest in magnitude and geographical extent registered in 2016 and 2017. Investment in WASH services declined significantly. Access to improved sanitation facilities and safe drinking water is estimated to be less than 10% and 60% respectively. The government's household survey shows that one in three people have no choice but to consume contaminated water. High contamination and turbidity reduces the effectiveness of chlorine treatment throughout the rain season. The private sector is not monitored during provision of WASH services.More than 85% of the population practice open defecation. In target areas like Kugi, Nanloni, Joppa, Lemon-gaba, Gudele West, Gurei and Nakulojo in Luri County, vulnerability is high among children.The cases of AWD/cholera attack rate per 10,000 is 44.5% according to the WASH cluster report on 6th of October 2017. In 2016/17 alone 20,703 cases were reported with 379 deaths (Death Fatality Rate of 1.83%).There is no guarantee that rules out another outbreak. Majority of the affected communities have not fully adopted proper hygiene practices or have full access to basic WASH services.During project implementation, WHEEL South Sudan will strengthen community capacity to expand project activities geared towards reinforcing gains made in the previous WASH interventions in schools for reduction of risks under Unicef Core pipeline. Community members will be mobilized to meet with their leaders and discuss their roles in cholera prevention; and will be engaged to take part in increasing advocacy for use of latrines by every household.August 2nd 2019 WHEEL Needs assessments for WASH in the targeted communities will be reviewed in collaboration with the rural water department, the Payam department of public health and the local community members. Proper consultations will be done with community members including conducting Focused Group Discussion to capture the specific needs of boys, girls, men and women. WHEEL will actively participate in the National Cholera Task Force, WASH cluster, Urban Wash Technical Working Group; and support in mobilization of target communities for other events related to cholera mitigation like OVC and Global Hand washing Day.For borehole drilling and rehabilitation, consultations meetings will be done in each borehole sites with women and girls, technical assessment of drilling sites and the boreholes to be rehabilitated, hand pump installation, water quality test, construction of platform, training water user committees, and hand pump mechanics. Monitoring WASH facilities will be continuous after hand over is done to the local authorities and communities. WHEEL has internal capacity to repair and rehabilitate boreholes and will contract the drilling of boreholes through its internal procurement processes. Rehabilitation's of the boreholes will range from minor hand pump repair to major platform reconstruction and clean-up of well by compressor. WHEEL will construct separate VIP latrines after consultation with girls and women, the latrines will be gender dis-aggregated for males and females each with hand washing facilities.Joint monitoring of activities with Payam/local Authorities will be conducted throughout the project cycle. GPS coordinates will accompany activity reports using WASH cluster reporting tools to be shared with partners and the government. Special attention will be paid on the quality of implementation to meet the project objective; and gaps will be continuously checked and other partners will be involved during decision making to bridge existing gaps.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Conducting WASH Focused Group Discussions (60) with target community members (women and girls) of each project site in Luri county.Activity 2: Repair and rehabilitation of 3 boreholes in schools and communities in target Payams with cholera hot spots in Luri county.Activity 3: Drilling of 4 new boreholes in communities/schools in target payams with cholera hot spots in Luri County.Activity 4: Training of water user committees and hand pump technicians in maintenance and sustainability of boreholes.Activity 5: Construction of four (4) VIP six stances latrines with hand washing facilities and bathroom within WASH-GBV latrine checklist standards.Activity 6: Training of 10 community hygiene promoters and 3 supervisors.Activity 7: Conducting participatory hygiene promotion sessions at households, water points, schools and public gatherings.Activity 8: Tracing of cases of Acute Water Diarrhea/cholera and disinfection of households and neighborhoods of persons affected by cholera among 13,000 community members in Northern Bari Payam, and Rombur Payam in Luri County.Activity 9: Distribution of WASH NFIs to 500 most vulnerable community members and Hygiene Kits to 200 most vulnerable girls and women.Activity 10: Formation and training of four (4) hygiene promotion clubs in four schools (including pupils, female and male teachers, PTA members and prefects) to support hygiene activities in schools.Activity 11: Conducting 6 public hygiene promotion campaigns for advocacy on the use of latrine among 13,000 community members that features all stake holders especially Payam authorities, health representatives and other partners for a period of six months. Activity 12: Joint monitoring of activity sessions conducted with Payam authorities at project sites and reporting on monthly bases to the WASH cluster.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, Luri County has internally displaced persons mainly from Lainya County Yei River State making up small proportion of the population who have been displaced during the 2016 crisis and yes they would benefit from this project because they live in this community and they share basic services such as water points, schools, health facilities and markets.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the project is linked to grass root safe water and improved sanitation objective of the national Ministry of Water Resource and Irrigation and Ministry of Health who are mandatory bodies that support service delivery of WASH related services and mitigation of health risks in the country. This project was developed in consultation with the community members and Luri county department of water sanitation and hygiene during 2nd August 2019 assessment conducted by WHEEL South Sudan. The project is also engaging community resource persons and training them as hygiene promoters, water user committees, and hand pump mechanics.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, this project is aligned with Ministry of Water Resource and Irrigation objectives that's aimed at providing clean and safe water for the people, improved sanitation and hygiene services and ensure peaceful coexistence for economic growth and development.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, this project is targeting predominantly host communities in which some IDPs and returnees are living amongst the host communities. Therefore, the beneficiaries do not need specialized protection compared to IDPs, returnees or refugees in designated locations. In addition, Luri county interns of security is stable.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the project design includes sustainability starting from its development where community leaders and beneficiaries have been consulted, proper inception process and launch to buy in the idea of sustainability, the training of water user committees, hand pump technicians and use of local community members who speak the local language to conduct hygiene promotion sessions. All this are meant to drive into sustainability. Hand over of completed facilities to communities will be done involving Luri county department of water sanitation and hygiene and provision of well endorsed hand over certificate.
