Project Summary

The project aims to provide life-saving support to 337,500 people (WASH), 14,700 (Education), 4,250 (G Protection) 3,800 (CP), 4,050 (GBV), 2,750 HH (Shelter & NFIs) and 6,000 (FSL). IAS/LM targets IDPs and host communities (vulnerable women, children, PWDs & elderly, considering gender-sensitive issues) affected by the current conflict between RSF and SAF in SD (Beleil, Nyala Shima & Ganoub and Elsalam), WD (El Geneina, Habila and Sirba), WK (Keilak, Lagawa and Al Meram) SK (Dilling, Abbasiya and Elreef Elshargi), NK (Sheikan and Um Rawaba localities), BN (Damazine, Kurmuk, Gissan, Wad Elmahi and Alruseris), RN (Atbara, Shendi, Ed Damer and Barbar) and RS (Agig, Algonab, Dordeib, Hya, Port Sudan and Sinkat). The project will implement WASH as a lifesaving intervention including new Installation and rehabilitation of HPs, solarized MWY, water distribution points, water trucking, O&M of water points, water points quality monitoring and WMC, rehabilitation of institutional latrines and O&M, disinfection and desludging of the existing latrine, solid waste management, vector control & cleaning tools, multi-purpose soap for individuals, jerrycans and hygiene kits, construction of HWF, hygiene training for community volunteers and campaigns in communal setups. Displacement has overwhelmed host communities, straining their fragile education systems and limited services. 49 Access barriers remain significant, with an additional 6.9 million children out-of-school including children with disabilities, many living in vulnerable communities. The crisis caused significant psychological distress among children, potentially impacting their long-term mental health and education progress if not urgently addressed with the mentioned lack of trained female teachers (HRP 2024). IAS/LM will provide educational recreational materials to 14,700 students, rehabilitate 30 Temporary learning spaces, rehabilitate 30 HW stations, psychosocial support to 3800 students, establish referral pathways between schools and child protection services for 1000 children, teachers & Parent Association members with increased knowledge on MHPSS skill, teaching & learning materials for 5000 children. HRP 2024 reflected that conflict targeted population requiring psychosocial support and individual assistance to respond to rising protection needs. PSN require specialized support amid severe economic hardship and rising inflation. IAS targeting GBV 4,050 (810 boys, 1013 girls, 810 men, and 1417 women), CP target 3,800 (760 boys, 950 girls, 760 men, and 1330 women), and general protection target 4,250 (850 women, 1,063 men 850 boys and 1487 girls), including PSEA awareness in PSS approach and provide PSS services to children with disabilities with a referral mechanism for the affected population. Under the GBV activities 1000 people at risk will be supported, rehabilitate 2 WCs, conduct training in GBV prevention and response for 3347 people and conduct 10 large-scale public community awareness-raising campaigns on GBV. GP includes (IPA)/ Cash Grants, referral to specialized services, psychosocial support, protection training and assessment. Shelter & NFIs target 13,750 beneficiaries (2,750 HHs) with NFI Kits to 500, cash/vouchers for NFIs to 500, Tents to 500, ESKs to 750 and cash/vouchers for shelter to 500 HHs. The FSL component aims to provide life-saving support to 6000 (1200 women, 1500 men 1200 boys and 2100 girls), contribute to the reduction of morbidity associated with malnutrition, prevent livelihood depletion of vulnerable communities through responding to immediate FSL needs of the target people in WD, SD and RN States, through improved access to agricultural inputs and services, provision of livelihoods for conflict-affected HHs and direct provision of agricultural inputs and services, quality assorted crops, legumes & vegetable seeds and tools besides multipurpose cash transfer and half ratio food voucher for 3 months during the lean period.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IAS/LM International will apply different standards and best practices in emergency response including SPHERE standards, LEGS, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Natural Resource Management (NRM) and "Do No Harm'' approaches in the implementation of this project activities. It will be taken into consideration that the transfer of resources (project inputs and services) into a resource-scarce environment can trigger conflict in the communities. Careful assessment of the local context, examining how humanitarian assistance would interact with the current situation in the target areas and the creation of options for project revision to improve its quality will be carried out. this assessment will include analysis of (i) dividers and sources of tensions between groups: systems and institutions; attitudes and actions; values and interests; experiences; symbols and occasions; etc. (ii) connectors across sub-groups and the local capacities for peace; (iii) the project in terms of why; where; what; when; with whom; by whom and how and its impact on dividers and connectors/local capacities for peace. The partners will work together to ensure that the project is implemented in a way that can strengthen local capacities for peace, build connections that bring communities together, and reduce the divisions and sources of tensions that can lead to destructive conflict. To ensure that no negative impact should result from this project the following will be undertaken: just distribution of resources, creating or strengthening networks of relationships across divisions, strengthening good governance, the use of participatory processes for decision-making, supporting traditional or indigenous mechanisms for conflict resolution and reconciliation, the inclusion of a diversity of ethnic or religious groups, gender, and youth in project activities and leadership structures. Selection of beneficiaries will be performed with due consideration of “Do No Harm” principles, ensuring fair representation of different vulnerable groups regardless of their ethnicity, religion, or gender. Criteria for selection of beneficiaries will be socialized with communities and ample opportunity will be provided to raise concerns/complaints. Selection criteria for all project activities will focus on female-headed HHs, child-headed HHs, orphans, elders and persons with disabilities. The population of the targeted localities; in Red Sea, Blue Nile, River Nile, South, North and West Kordofan states, and South and West Darfur states include a high proportion of vulnerable individuals, targeted HHs might have lost a family member during the current ongoing conflict between Rapid Support Forces (RSF) and Sudanese Arm Forces (SAF) conflict, mainly men, which explains the considerable number of women-headed HHs among the targeted communities; those women have to act as the breadwinners for their children and often their elders on their own. Enabling them safe and dignified access to basic services.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Affected populations will be involved in all stages of this project. For example, when the project is approved, engagements will be held with affected populations, including women, boys, and girls to share about the objectives, the duration, and the key activities. IAS/ LM International will also disseminate the complaints reporting mechanisms to ensure that the affected persons can understand how to raise complaints and know what to expect from IAS when a complaint has been raised. The targeted communities will participate in Project Focal Persons and selecting of water management committees. In addition, the community members’ views will be sought during project monitoring visits to ensure that the project results in the desired outcomes. IAS/LM International will continue to popularize the whistleblowing and complaints response mechanism with the community members to enable the communities to raise complaints or give feedback on the projects or conduct of the IAS staff or the staff of implementing partner organizations. Quarterly project reflection meetings will be held in which affected populations will provide feedback on what is working, and what is not working, and solicit feedback from the targeted communities on the changes, both expected and unexpected caused by the project. Project monitoring tools will also be designed to capture feedback from the affected populations to ensure that the project is effective in responding to the needs, challenges, and feedback from the community. The most appropriate mechanisms will be established in consultation with community members, taking into consideration literacy rates, accessibility to communication devices, and the presence of suitable sites for the establishment of complaint/suggestion boxes; to ensure accessibility of all community members (men, women, children, elders and disabled people) and provision of timely response. Good internal procedures and confidential reporting systems will be implemented to ensure the confidentiality and safety of all community members. The communities’ feedback will be acquired and verified through different approaches including regular monitoring activities and feedback, complaints and response mechanisms including CFRM-Complaints Feedback Response Mechanism, focus group discussions, semi-structured interviews, key informant interviews, community meetings, establishment of complaint/suggestion boxes in project sites and communicating the partners’ contacts and addresses with community members and committees.
