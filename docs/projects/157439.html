Project Summary

Spontaneous return of IDPs has already taken place in Darfur locations where improvements have occurred. Since the beginning of 2017, more than 280,000 displaced persons have returned voluntarily and permanently to their places of origin across Darfur . However, return communities still suffer from a lack of access to basic services, shortage of economically and environmentally friendly shelters, as well as limited access to land and environmental assets. In addition to return processes, it is also expected that a significant number IDP's camps approved to re- integrated e.g Abu Shouk and Alsalam IDP's camps around El Fasher city.
This project will provide environmentally friendly transitional shelters using Soil Stabilized Block (SSB) technology and train re- integrated IDPs, returnees and host communities on construction of shelters using the above SSB technology as alternative to Fired Bricks
The project will cover five localities in North Darfur:
1 - Abu Shouk and Al Salam IDP's camps in El Fasher locality are approved by State Government to re-integrate on El Fasher city, these IDP's camps will be targeted by the project.
2 - Rural area of Kutum locality according to HAC there are verified returnees return from Kassab and Fatta Barnu IDP's camps to their original villages and also from Republic of Chad, these returnees and their host communities will be targeted by the project
3 - Rural area of Mellit locality show evidence of return from Al Abbasi IDP's camp in Mellit town, these returnees and their host communities will be targeted by the project
4 - Tawila locality endorsed Ruwanda, Argo and Daly IDP's camps from which thousands of IDPs recently returned to their villages in rural area of Tawila locality, these returnees and their host community will also be targeted by the project.
5 - Dar Al Salam locality will also be targeted by the project
Fired Bricks approach of construction of shelters cause damage to the environment through cutting of trees, it was shown by studies that one Fired Bricks Factory of 100,000 bricks that only construct three houses will need to cut completely 27 big trees in order to fire the bricks.
This project will introduce alternative and sustainable technology of SSB substitute the negatively environmental impact Fired Bricks.
The project is a consortium project implement by Oxfam America and Sustainable Action Group (SAG)
Oxfam America will lead the consortium and supply the SSB Machines and other materials, while SAG will construct the sample shelters and train beneficiaries on SSB construction technology under supervision of Oxfam America through engagement of 1600 youth (50%male and 50% female) on training and construction of shelters (Tukul) using SSB machines.
The duration of the project is 12 months start on 1/1/2019 and end on 31/12/2019

Needs

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2019, Sudan still host large number of vulnerable populations including those affected by natural disasters, armed conflict and drought, some two millions are still displaced of which 75% are women and children. Spontaneous return of IDPs has already taken place in Darfur locations where improvements have occurred. Since the beginning of 2017, more than 280,000 displaced persons have returned voluntarily and permanently to their places of origin across Darfur . However, return communities still suffer from a lack of access to basic services, shortage of economically and environmentally viable livelihoods, as well as limited access to land and environmental assets. In addition to return processes, it is also expected that a significant number IDPs will opt to remain in or near to the towns, cities and peri-urban areas to which they were displaced Targeted beneficiaries for the project interventions include re- integrated IDPs in Abu Shouk and Al Salam IDP's camps around El Fsacher city, returnees in rural areas of Tawila, Mellit and Kutum localities with 10% of their host communities, their selection based on rapid needs assessment (rapid influx) conduct before the starting of the project This will allow the prioritization of most vulnerable households. Impact on environment/natural resources

The communities in Darfur use fired bricks to construct their shelters,the fired bricks factories use wood which come from cutting of trees. Study results show that a fired brick of 100,000 bricks which construct only three houses will need to cut completely27 big trees to be fired. The Stabilized Soil Block (SSB) technology is an environmentally alternative technology for the above negatively environmental impact, so the project will reduce cutting of trees and contribute positively on the climate change
