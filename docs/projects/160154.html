Project Summary

This project is designed to meet the needs of 80% IDPs and 20% host communities of the total beneficiaries targeted. Gender will be mainstreamed into the project through the specific consideration of the different needs of women, men, boys and girls during the design of activities. The project will ensure that all participants have equal access to resources and training. Monitoring and evaluation will be based on gender, age and diversity dis-segregated data. Settlement mapping exercises will be conducted to get the information of all targeted settlements for necessary services in place and gap analysis, the settlement mapping exercise data will be shared with CCCM cluster lead, which will be useful in creating a master list of the area. This master list will then be posted online for the ease of access to all humanitarian actors, which will be used by different humanitarian partners for their future planning and response.

Project Activities

• Identification and support of community structures in the IDP settlements and encourage participation of all different segments of the displaced population and, as relevant, from among the host communities and local authorities.• Provide effective and practical collaboration and coordination of humanitarian interventions. Ensure that relevant responders are mobilized towards providing relevant sectorial assistance to those identified as very vulnerable.• Provide training on CCCM principles to community governance structures (IDP leaders) to strengthen their capacities.• Conduct Detailed Site Assessments on 3-month basis and establish/update master list of sites at appropriate geographic level• Establish/update service mapping of partners operating in sites e.g. sectors and service providers at site level to ensure that there are no gaps or duplication of activities.• Establish roving CCCM teams for site monitoring and community engagement: Creation of IM product providing information on population movement and numbers and identification of gaps etc.• Coordinate/conduct intention surveys with relevant partners and clusters to ensure the intentions of populations are understood and supported.• Together with other clusters and partners, as well as the authorities, coordinate the establishment/ efficient use of referral pathways for both protection (including SGBV) and assistance/service delivery to persons with special needs.• Engage with local authorities in relevant geographical locations to develop contingency plans for future IDP influxes. • Establish/reinforce communication mechanisms per geographical area/sites i.e. Communication/ information centers in coordination with local authorities and protection actors).• Carry out community consultation awareness raising and social mobilization.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement, the community leaders and respective committees will be fully involved in project implementation, monitoring and representation. HIMILO will ensure that project committees to be inclusive and that segments of the communities are well represented at all levels such as age and gender diversity. HIMILO has a comprehensive community entry and targeting package that guides staff. The project was designed in consultation with the targeted communities, and HIMILO strives to maintain these consultations throughout all phases of the project cycle. In addition, HIMILO will implement the proposed project in collaboration with all stakeholders paying special attention to the beneficiaries. The government and respective clusters will be involved in beneficiary selection, monitoring and implementation. The project will be initiated with a familiarization meeting held with relevant authorities and communities in targeted locations, to create awareness on project objectives, activities, target locations, expected outcomes, scope, and type and selection criteria for beneficiaries. During community mobilization sessions, HIMILO field staff with sound understanding of the community dynamics will provide detailed information on vulnerability-based selection criteria. HIMILO has a strong framework for advocacy opportunities, on the issue of evictions from IDP settlements and collective centres and promotion of land tenure security for IDPs at the onset of an emergency. HIMILO will advocate with clan elders, regional and federal governments to provide land with title deeds to enable this category of Displacement affected communities (DACs) access to durable solutions. With regards to community interactions and feedback, HIMILO has set number of mechanisms (such as complain feedback mechanism) which is free of charge, easily accessible to everyone, and well-known throughout the targeted communities uphold the accountability of the proposed project to the affected communities. The system will be further optimized and expanded in view of its use to serve the present CCCM project, and reports will be issued and shared with the CCCM cluster and within the wider humanitarian community, once per semester. Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age. Protection will be mainstreamed throughout all phases of the project cycle and in the implementation of all activities. Implementation of activities, including site maintenance and construction works, will be done allowing full opportunity for engagement to all site residents. HIMILO will pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services through consulting them during the designing of the constructions. Separate side meetings will be convened with women and girls, men and boys, particularly to address their specific needs. Applied vulnerability criteria for prioritization of assistance will include single women, single-headed HH, and elderly, especially if they live alone. HIMILO will also reinforce activities that focus on women and their role within communities, as an enabler for protection outcomes in conflict affected areas by leveraging women’s roles and leadership in expanding the humanitarian space, ultimately promoting community resilience, and furthering protection of civilians and civilian assets. HLP rights for women will be prioritized whenever title deeds or land rent agreements are signed. Where cash for work will be implemented, activities will be designed to allow participation of men and women, girls and boys. Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence Despite the improvements made against insecurity in Somalia and establishment of permanent political institutions, paving the way towards a future with greater peace, this is yet, however, to translate into an improvement of protection needs of the vulnerable people as there are continued.

Please provide further explanation

Durable solutions are one of the key pillars of HIMILO Somalia programming, and continuous efforts are directed towards promoting durable solution and self-sufficiency of the displaced and vulnerable people across Somalia. However, HIMILO recognizes the complexity and long milestones required to reach this vision. This project will contribute towards supporting opportunities for displaced people to achieve durable solutions given people cantered response, capacity building for both the government and IDP communities, linking community priorities with national government plans and as well as implementing community-based initiatives that can promote community recovery. IDPs have limited options for durable solutions to their displacement. According to JMCNA findings indicate that the majority of IDPs prefer local integration. This however has so far remained a challenge due to lack of land availability, repeated evictions and minimal livelihoods options. Returns to the area of origin are extremely limited, as most IDPs have lost livelihoods and are reliant on humanitarian assistance. HIMILO will make use of the CCCM coordination platforms to advocate Housing, Land and Property rights for the displaced.
