Project Summary

The year 2024 was marked by numerous protection incidents affecting children in Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga in the Far North. In addition, significant flooding has exacerbated pre-existing child protection problems such as GBV. Significant population movements continue to be recorded adding to the pre-existing difficulties of access to basic social services for children as well as the lack of opportunities for adolescents and youth to develop their potential in a region where 64% of the current displaced population are children.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The project aims at protecting children against violence, abuse and exploitation in emergencies. Conflict and climate change consequences and other natural hazards as floods have negative impact on the well-being of children and their caregivers. In the Far North, the main threats against children and their caregivers are family separation caused by forced population movements and the abduction of children often related to the forced recruitment by NSAGs, with heightened risk of sexual exploitation for girls. Most of the children and adolescents formerly associated with NSAGs struggle to reintegrate into the community, which causes further physical and psychological harm. Girls are exposed to various forms of sexual violence, child marriage, and early pregnancy, often as negative coping mechanisms and because of traditional social and cultural practices. Incidents of GBV, physical violence, negligence, and abuse also define the child protection risks that humanitarian actors will respond to. Child protection activities will include case management, the strengthening of Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms (CBCPMs), provision of psychosocial support to children, adolescents, and caregivers to prevent violence, negligence, and abuse against children in all three divisions affected by the conflict and floods. Child Protection partners will assist children and caregivers with mental health and psychosocial support, including through the identification, referral, and individual case management vulnerable children. A dedicated and specialized support will be provided to children formerly associated with armed groups to facilitate their reintegration to the community. A comprehensive Case Management will be provided to vulnerable children and support to families to better protect their children as well as the capacity building of Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms. Activities will also include support to issue birth certificates to conflict-affected children, interventions for the prevention and mitigation of risks and response to GBV affecting children and providing Humanitarian beneficiaries with PSEA measure.
