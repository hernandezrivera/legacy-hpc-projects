Project Summary

The proposed project will strengthen access to lifesaving and life-sustaining essential health services and COVID-19 response in northern Aleppo and northern Idleb. The proposed activities are in line with the needs and gaps identified by beneficiaries and DDD assessments in the target locations as well as Health Custer priorities by providing life-saving humanitarian assistance and protection to the most vulnerable people with an emphasis on underserved areas with high severity of needs. It is also in line with HRP Strategic Objective 2: provide life-saving humanitarian assistance and protection to the most vulnerable people with an emphasis on underserved areas with high severity of needs.
DDD’s project focuses on strengthening service provision for the most vulnerable groups in urban settings and in and out of camps who have limited access to regular services, through a targeted comprehensive Essential Health Service Package: PHC services, SRH, and MHPSS, and COVID-19 testing through fixed clinics and laboratories. These services will be complemented by outreach services such as health promotion activities by Community Health Workers, home-based specialized medical care focused on people with chronic conditions, people living with disabilities, and at-risk pregnancies, as well as ongoing efforts to expand in-demand MHPSS services. PHC services will also be supported through DDD’s 4 laboratories. A total of 309,387 individuals are expected to directly benefit from this intervention (direct beneficiaries). An estimated 668,783 catchment population will indirectly benefit from the intervention.
The project builds on existing DDD programmes and targets 17 locations currently served by DDD and partners in Afrin district in Aleppo (3 static, 3 mobile) and Harim district in Idleb (12 static) to both IDPs (in and out of camps) and residents in dire need of health services. The proposed project has been developed to respond to priorities and needs identified by the community itself. This project will support emergency preparedness steps to ensure appropriate health service coverage and capacity to increase service availability in case of massive IDP arrival. The project has also been designed in close coordination with the Health Cluster, COVID-19 Taskforce, MHPSS Working Group and humanitarian actors in the area to ensure that integrated and relevant services are provided to the targeted population. This includes conflict-affected children under 12 years old, high risk pregnant women, older people, people living with disabilities , people living with chronic conditions and other vulnerable groups with limited access to health care.
DDD (also known as DoTW-T) is one of the largest providers of health care services in NWS. It is also one of the only NGOs providing PCR testing. DDD clinics have been responding to the health needs of IDP and host community residents in Dana sub-district since 2012 and in Afrin since 2018. It has strong links with the local community. Support of for these 17 facilities in 2021 is essential to maintain availability of services to returning patients and people depending on the services, and to respond to the health needs of new IDPs arriving in the areas served by the clinics. Moreover, adaptation of services to respond to the specific needs of the most vulnerable groups severely impacted by hostilities and displacement is essential.

4 - Needs

This Project has been designed to meet needs identified in the 2021 HNO, as well as by DDD in Afrin, Jandairis, and Dana Sub-districts. According to the April HNAP these areas received among the highest IDPs to both camps and communities resulting significant gaps in access to health services. Specific needs include: - Access to PHC: DDD’s RNA reported continued need for free, accessible PHC services including child immunization and MUAC. Demand for mobile/home-based care is a priority, particularly for women, elderly, and PwD. COVID-19 adds additional barrier limiting mobility and threatening health. - MHPSS: Increasing demand for treatment of mental health and psychological issues in target location as well as ongoing need for GBV referrals for specialized services. - Continued need to access SRH. The disrupted healthcare system has decreased the wellbeing of Women of Reproductive Age who are unable to access essential SRH services. An estimated 57% of WRA in NWS face health issues, of which 51% are pregnancy-related issues (HSOS 2020). There is also a need for obstetric and new-born health care. - NCDs and chronic conditions particularly affect vulnerable groups such as IDP women and girls, elderly people and PwD (an estimated 28% of people over 12 years of age live with disabilities in target locations according to HNAP 2021) and require continuous care by PHC providers. CDs remain a priority, as risks of infections such as Leishmaniasis are observed. - Need for increased COVID-19 testing and vaccination. The pandemic is occurring within the context of already dire health conditions, infrastructure and high-risk factors for the transmission of COVID-19.

5 - Implementation of activities

DDD PHC services includes Essential Health Services Package (SRH, MHPSS, MUAC, NCD, CD, Community Outreach, GBV first-line support, and Referrals). PHC is provided through direct implementation of DDD staff in Afrin and Idleb. The Syrian Program Coordinator based in Istanbul (all with support staff), oversees all Syria staff and activities implemented in NWS. Field offices in Qah and Afrin are remotely managed by field coordination team in Antakya, with programme, logistical and HR support from Istanbul HQ. PHC are provided by qualified health care staff (doctors, paediatrician, gynaecologists, nurses, etc) and PCR tests by trained lab technicians, all overseen by the Medical Coordinator. Referrals will be made by case managers to protection actors for GBV, CP, and other specialized care needed. Community Health Workers inform beneficiaries of available health care services and best hygiene and care practice. PHC services are provided All equipment, medical consumables and PHCC supplies are procured in line with best practices and shipped into Syria from Turkey. EPREP is in place to guide contingency responses. DDD staff in Antakya and Syria conduct regular cross-border monitoring to ensure activities are implemented; data collected monthly is disaggregated by gender, age, and disability to improve quality of programming. Exit interviews are regularly implemented to facilitate information on beneficiary feedback or complaints, patient satisfaction, and potential challenges faced related to patient perception of safety and access to services. Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) are accessible and tailored to beneficiaries’ community members, stakeholders, and staff different forms of access and communication.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Health facilities and providers are an entry point for GBV survivors to receive care. Barriers to meaningful access for beneficiaries include safety concern, stigmatization, and staff being ill-equipped to offer survivor-centred support and health services. There is also a risk if staff cannot provide referral to specialized GBV support services. Likelihood: High. Impact: HighMitigating Measures: 1) Access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) part of the essential health package ensured at all health facilities. 2) Medical staff are regularly trained on providing trauma-sensitive, survivor-centred first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault/ rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals in line with the internal SoP’s and as applicable to their respective role. 3) All staff receive training on communicating with PwD to enable women and girls with disabilities to be identified and receive support on an equal basis Protection Risk: There is a risk of inequitable access to health services for certain vulnerable groups: children under 5, women of reproductive age, PwDs, over 50 years of age, IDPs and returnees. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigating Measures: 1. Disaggregate all data by sex, age and disability, and analyse the needs of vulnerable and minority groups, throughout the program cycle with identification of risk factors and rights violations impacting service provision for beneficiaries. 2) Ensure that complaints and feedback mechanisms are accessible and facilitate inputs for all persons. 3) Provide outreach and take other reasonable accommodations (individualized measures) to reach PwD that cannot leave their homes.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

DDD field staff have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over vulnerable community/beneficiaries that hold PSEA risks. DDD has institutionalized policies on PSEA, Whistle-blower, Code of Conduct, Gender, Safeguarding and Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM). Proposed activities have been designed and are implemented with a standardised and cohesive approach through Standard Operational Procedures guided by the principles of “Do no Harm”, confidentiality, impartiality / non-discrimination, best interest of the child. Gender, age and diversity is sensitized. Mitigating measures: 1) Effective and comprehensive mechanisms established to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel; continuous training on the topic provided by dedicated staff members. 2) Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff is ensured. 3) Ensuring regular monitoring and evaluation through impartial assessments of needs and risks, vulnerabilities and capacities of different groups, clear reporting and beneficiary/staff feedback mechanisms. 4) Ensuring provision of information to communities and people affected by crisis about DDD; its principles, how it expects its staff to behave, the programmes it is implementing and what they intend to deliver as well how to report concerns and complaints. 5) Effective community-based CFM, including victim assistance is established and monitored for all persons (gender, age and diversity).

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

DDD implements robust risk management and contingency planning measures, closely following context evolution and developing projects to prevent duplication in coordination with OCHA, Health Cluster, COVID Taskforce, local stakeholders, and other partners. DDD collaborates with Mentor Initiative to receive Leishmania treatment and WHO for NCD kits. DDD conducts periodic needs assessments, health committee meetings and exit interviews to ensure services meet vulnerable peoples’ needs. DDD’s FCRM is accessible to beneficiaries, community members, stakeholders, partners and staff. Key mechanisms in place to mitigate risks to DDD beneficiaries, personnel, assets, and operation:- Security SoPs in place and staff trained to act in insecure situations. Security context continuously monitored in close coordination with local stakeholders and other partners.- COVID-19 mitigation measures in place (triage, IPC, and PPE)and staff trained on COVID-19 protocols and prevention.- Contingency Plans updated on continuous basis, including Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan (EPREP).- Duty of care policy implemented for staff inside Syria; psychiatrist available for health facility staff to aid in managing stressors.- Coordination with key stakeholders in Turkey and Syria ensures acceptance for DDD operations and safety of staff and beneficiaries.- Active participation in Access Working Group.- Strict financial management controls in place to mitigate risk of fraud.
