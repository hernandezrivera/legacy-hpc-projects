Project Summary

Since the beginning of the year, the number of people displaced has continued to rise in northern Mozambique. According to IOM/DTM Baseline Assessment Round 21 July 2024, there were an estimated 541,400 IDPs and 576,280 of returnees. The conflict has also spread to new and previously calm locations in southern districts of the province causing additional movements and challenges in the response. UNICEF plans to implement education in crises responsive activities across Cabo Delgado in the most affected districts. The main objective is to address the needs of IDP, Host Communities and Returnees affected by the conflict, focusing on out-of-school displaced children and adolescents. A total of 113,421 beneficiaries (113,251 children and 170 adults) will benefit from education activities under this project.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Cabo Delgado Province in Mozambique have been experiencing severe consequences of the actions of non-state armed groups/insurgents particularly in the North, since 2017. This protracted crisis reached in July 2024 541,400 IDPs (IOM DTM Round 21) with continuing attacks during the year. The conflict, insecurity, the forced displacement of communities and school closure not only interrupted educational progresses, but also affects psychosocial well-being, livelihood and hinders children’s access to essential basic services including school feeding, health information on disease prevention, protection, health, access to clean water and sanitation services and increases the risks of child protection. Attacks and other violent event and therefore displacement trend have been continuously changing. Education is under attack in some locations and schools have been targeted or use as shelter for IDPs accommodation centers, not been used for learning purposes and creating interruption on the pupils learning cycles. Beyond its EiE response programibng, UNICEF has very actively supported education local authorities and implementing partners to respond the current EiE needs in its cluster coordination role. Considering the long lasting humanitarian situation in Cabo Delgado, within this current proposal UNICEF will be adapting the response to mitigate medium to longer term impact on the education sector through an integrated approach by focusing in out-of-school children and adolescents by supporting access to formal education and providing safe non-formal learning opportunities that can support the (re)enrolment in formal education or the access to safe alternative learning opportunities. The project will target children, teachers and communities affected by conflict in the districts targeted in Cabo Delgado (target districts) focusing on internallydisplaced children and returnees, including children with disabilities. Host communities (HC) children will also be supported through HC schools. , Monitoring- the monitoring process will be jointly done with education provincial directorate and partners to ensure quality implementation, participation and accountability on the outcomes planned.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The geographical scope of the project includes the most affected districts of Cabo Delgado, aligned with Cluster prioritization for 2025. In all districts targeted, UNICEF will foster the implementation of a comprehensive education response, focusing in ensuring access to learning opportunities to out-of-school children and adolescents by s: Providing Accelerated Learning opportunities for children from 6-12 years in particular and Alternative Learning opportunities to adolescents from 13-17 years. The provision of School based MHPSS, seeks to address the psychosocial well-being needs that prevent children and adolescents from having the skill of learning to learn. Additionally, the project entails supporting learners with learning, hygiene and assistance devices. Finally, project activity include the set temporary learning spaces (TLS) and latrines to enable to respond to the enrolment of OOSC and the influx of IDP and returnees children and reinforce the capacity of existings school metting INEE and Sphere Minimum Standards By Proroviding learning opportunities that enables students todevelop life-saving skills, including self-care – hygiene, nutrition, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support, violence risk prevention and mitigation will strongly prevent and/or address child protection risks such as exploitation and recruitment by armed forces; Under another objective, the project seeks to provide support to teachers and education personnel by delivering training on EiE related topics, including Teachers in Crisis Contexts (TiCC) international Package (addressing topics like MHPSS and GBV) contextualized for Cabo Delgado specific needs; and to Strength the capacity and expertise of local Authorities at central, provincial and district level on EiE response programming.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Consultations and Complain mechanism will be in place so that beneficiaries can influence the project design and implementation. Necessarily the proposal will engage teachers, parents and caregivers, throughout Parents and Teacher Associations and School Management Committee engagement, holding UNICEF accountable for the outcomes committed with. In the monitoring of the process, UNICEF ensures that children are comfortable and engaged listened to, collect information that can inform reprogramming so the activities aim also to address their psychosocial and mental well-being needs rather than fostering learning and studying alone. In this monitoring, the communities, parents or caretakers of these children will be as much as involved as possible through creation of reference groups, discussion groups of different sex and age and also using the complaint boxes that will help us improve our implementation. From an organizational point of view, UNICEF is deeply committed to PSEA across all programs and initiatives. The some commitment is demanded from all governmental counterparts and all implementing partners supported by UNICEF. There is a PSEA dedicated team in the country office, supporting PSEA operationalization, monitoring and evaluation and a focal point is each field office, such as Cabo Delgado. From a proposal point of view, all teachers will benefit from training in PSEA and following that they will be able to support identifying and referring and preventing SEA in and around schools.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Significant measures will be put in place to prevent discrimination and promote inclusion of disadvantaged groups, and indicators are disaggregated by sex and age. The project aims at eradicating access constrains for these groups like returnees and IDPs girls’ students who might be usually excluded in accessing quality education services and opportunities. Special attention will be put on sensitization on girls’ education and GBV which can seriously affect the attendance and retention of young girls in schools, especially in areas close to the conflict. Boys have also been considered in their vulnerability: young boys who are exposed to violence and to be recruited into armed forces, but also, per the traditional social structures, families often require boys to take up works so that they cannot attend school regularly. The activities want to address the different obstacles equally preventing boys and girls from attending school.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

UNICEF will work with NGOs and staff experienced in delivery specific services and support for children with disabilities (CWD) so that they can fully participate in the activities. Therefore as explained above, these activities are organized in good collaboration with the Child Protection, Gender Based-Violence Areas of Responsibility and WASH and health clusters and their selected NGOs in order to provide an intersectoral service for all these children. Parents, caregivers and local authorities and community will be fully involved in the promotion of inclusive activities and education. In the long run, facing and overcoming challenges and reducing the prejudices from the general public and increasing their chances to actively participate in society will help them to grow stronger and more resilient. Inclusive education is one of the topics planned for teacher training activities to strengthen the capacity of teachers to address CWD specific needs and potentially referring these children and adolescents do specialized services.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The selected location of education facilities will be preceded by an evaluation of the location in collaboration with the local authorities, so that the location can be considered safe with materials that are safe for children to use, less vulnerable to natural hazards (resulting from floods, storms) and clean with basic WASH conditions, and with confirmation on responsibilities for maintaining security, WASH and protection. At the same time the activities will aim to raise environmental awareness and protection of natural resources and avoiding erosion for instance by planting fruit and shade trees, creating living fences, grass planting and directing and harvesting rainwater. Additionally, procurement processes include criteria related to environmental impact, prioritizing supplies with a longer lifetime expected. Environmental friendly behavior is one of the topics addressed in the Lifeskills component of the project.
