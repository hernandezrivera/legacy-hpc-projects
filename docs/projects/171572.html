Project Summary

Somalia is one of the most complex humanitarian crises in the world. the population is currently experiencing multiple shocks including the desert locust upsurge; riverine and flash floods during the current Gu (April-June) season and the novel corona-virus pandemic. These conflict and climatic disasters have repeatedly displaced hundreds of thousands of people in recent years. Between January and August 2020, more than 885,000 people were displaced largely due to floods and conflict. According to the UNHCR/NRC-led Protection Return and Monitoring Network (PRMN), more than 627,872 people were newly displaced by floods in 2020 alone. The target area for this proposed intervention will be Afmadow and Badhaade districts in Lower Juba of Somalia. Currently, an estimated 37,919 people including 15,997 IDPs and 2,706 returnees are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Emergency (IPC Phase 4) across the districts of Afmadow and Badhaade (FSC overall targets for 2021 by district) . Multi-sectoral rapid assessment conducted by partners on 26th April 2020 in Afmadow and Badhaade districts revealed that the Gu rains have caused flash floods affecting more than 11,800 families among them 3,400 IDP households and caused damages to more than 1,500 shelters and 1,200 latrines raising fears of water-borne diseases in the area (Multi-sector rapid assessment, April 2020) .
The proposed interventions will help affected households to meet their immediate food and livelihood needs as well as build their longer term resilience. The action will contribute to two key specific objectives:
 Improved access to equitable livelihoods assistance for vulnerable men, women, boys and girls in returnee, IDP and host communities
 Support small scale agriculture through provision of agricultural inputs and training to farming and agro-pastoral households.
The specific activities to be undertaken in order to realize these objectives will be cash based interventions in the form of cash for work and un-conditional cash to improve purchasing power and food access for households. The project will also undertake recovery and livelihood protection activities covering crop production support. The proposed action will support at least 2,800 vulnerable households (1,300 for CFW, 700 for UCT, and 600 for agricultural inputs and training) in the target districts of Afmadow and Badhaade. Beneficiary selection criteria will be developed according to the according to the HEA approach combined with nutritional criteria. WASDA will be specific on genders issues in the programmme. Boys, girls. Men, women and people living with disability will be involved in the identification, and selection of beneficiates. They will be involved in the implementation of the project implementation providing feedback to improve programme monitoring, quality and increase information for future reprogramming. People living with disability will be resented in project communities so that they can represent the needs of those PLD during project implementation. Women will also be 40% of the project committees
WASDA will also ensure fair access of the project interventions and services by men, women and persons with disabilities through sensitization and fair targeting of beneficiaries. For example, CFW, UCT, and agricultural inputs beneficiaries will be distributed fairly to men, women and persons with disabilities where persons with disabilities will be fully represented in the village relief committees. Beneficiary protection particularly for elderly and persons with disabilities will be carefully considered. During livestock and agricultural inputs distributions, the sites and distribution procedures will be informed by safety considerations to ensure that distributions are inclusive. Project teams will trained on how to identify people living with disability, understanding their specific needs and mitigate barriers and risks faced by the PWD so that they are not excluded.

Project Activities

i. Sensitization of key stakeholders about the project - WASDA will organize stakeholder’s start-up workshop which will highlight the general project information to build strong engagement with the community and other key stakeholders. Key stakeholders that will be engaged in the launch will include local authorities, community leaders, women, men and persons with disabilities. ii. Selection and registration of households targeted for CFW, UCT, restocking, agricultural inputs and training Afmadow and Badhade districts – WASDA together with community, local authority and other key stakeholders will develop vulnerability criteria according to the HEA approach combined with nutritional criteria. Based on these criteria, gender and disability disaggregated data will be collected and priority will be given to most vulnerable households including the female headed households, child headed household, families with malnourished children, elderly, households with disabled people as well as IDPs and returnees will be targeted.iii. Provision of Cash for work to 1,000 floods and conflicted affected households - The Cash for work will be used to rehabilitate and upgrade community owned infrastructure such as water pans, small irrigation canals, shallow wells as well as feeder roads. In collaboration with community and local authorities in Afmadow and Badhadhe districts, a total of 1,000 vulnerable households will be engaged on CFW activities for a single cycle comprising of three months. The participating households will be expected to work for 3 to 4 hours a day, for 18 days a month and household will receive a cash value of USD 90 per month for three months. WASDA will provide hand tools and protective gears to the CFW participants.iv. Provision of un-conditional cash transfer to 600 floods and conflicted affected households – This activity will be linked to the CFW and will majorly target vulnerable households who are not able to participate in CFW activities. These households where most of the UCT beneficiaries will come from include those with persons with disabilities, elderly, and households headed by pregnant and lactating mothers among others. A total of 600 households will be registered in Afmadow and Badhade districts for a single cycle where each household will receive a cash value of USD 70 per month for three months.v. Livestock restocking to 600 vulnerable households - To safeguard pastoral livelihoods, 600 households from the rural pastoralists will be provided with healthy and productive livestock (10 female goats) that have been vaccinated and dewormed in collaboration with Ministry of Agriculture and livestock.The animals will be procured from the local markets through local livestock traders. During distribution of livestock, the distribution sites and distribution procedures will be informed by safety considerations for elderly and persons with disabilities to ensure that distributions are inclusive. vi. Provision of basic agricultural inputs to 600 farming and Agro pastoralist households - To support recovery of agricultural production, the project will provide agricultural inputs to 600 farming households practicing micro irrigation in Afmadow and Badhade. The agricultural inputs will include certified seeds, irrigation equipment e.g. water pumps, watering cans, hose pipes as well as farm tools such as spade, shovel, hoe and pruner. During distribution of inputs, the interest of the elderly and persons with disabilities will be taken care off.vii. Train 600 farming and agro-pastoralist households on improved agronomical practices and GBV- The project in collaboration with the Ministry of agriculture, Gender and other key stakeholders will strengthen the capacity of the 600 farming and agro-pastoralist households in the target district of Afmadow and Badhade on improved agronomical practices including cultivation, management of crops, storage and preservation of agricultural produce as well as GBV issues.

Mitigation Measures

WASDA maintains safety and security of its operations and staff by ensuring active community participation throughout its project processes and strengthens its links and close collaboration with the various community groups including community elders, local administration and community based workers. Beneficiary identification, selection and verification will participatory, involving all the segments of the community. This will give the community confidence to actively participate in the implementation and monitoring of the project progress. WASDA will organize stakeholders forum at the start of the project to share with the beneficiaries and other stakeholders all the relevant information about the project so that there is ownership from the community leading to sustainability of the project after the closure. WASDA staff have access to the area and will be directly managing the facilities and providing services. However, security is unpredictable in Somalia and WASDA will closely monitor the situation and will closely work with other actors to monitor the situation. WASDA has local based security plan so that its staff and facilities are safe and secure. Staff are informed to be on the look for any suspicious elements and report to authorities and also evacuate as necessary. WASDA will be participating in cluster forums and will be implementing all security advisories and shared briefings. In the event of changes of activities, WASDA will immediately notify the communities, the Food Security Cluster and and any other concern actors in the area. This will mitigate the identified risks.
