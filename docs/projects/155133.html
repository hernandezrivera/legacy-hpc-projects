Needs

Violent events in Rakhine State led to the mass displacement of Rohingya community to Bangladesh starting in August 2017..In September 2017, the United Nations declared the situation in Cox’s Bazar, a Level 3 emergency. Concrete steps have been taken to improve the conditions of the Rohingya refugees and to mitigate the impacts of the crisis on the host community. However, conditions remain extremely precarious with congested living spaces and the potential for natural disasters. Preparedness for the cyclone seasons and the monsoon seasons should remain a priority in 2019. While life-saving humanitarian assistance must continue, it is also necessary to improve the efficiency, quality and sustainability of assistance and services for people in need. The Rohingya refugee population in Bangladesh is now estimated at 901,185 individuals as of November 15th, 2018. As per the 2019 Joint Response Plan (JRP) 284,119 refugee children and 107,162 host community children are in need of child protection assistance. As of 11th October, 6,115 unaccompanied and separated children have been registered and are at heightened risk of child trafficking, abuse and exploitation in the camps. Children face serious protection risks including psychosocial distress, neglect, abuse, separation from caregivers, child marriage, child labour, and trafficking. Mental health and psychosocial needs remain very high, with children and families suffering from high levels of distress both from prior to their flight and exacerbated by conditions in the camp. It is estimated that 284,119 refugee children and 107,162 children from the host community are in need of psychosocial support activities (JRP 2019), and there is an urgent need to provide structured psychosocial services to boys and girls of all age groups as well as to caregivers. Adolescent girls and boys continue to face specific risks. Girls are at risk of GBV, including domestic violence, forced/early marriage, and exploitation and trafficking. These risks are exacerbated by various factors including cultural practices, insecurity within the camps, limited opportunities for self-development and self-reliance for families, and inadequate access to education (JRP 2019). Adolescent boys face heightened risk of forced labour and exploitation, as well as social conservatism and isolation. They also remain vulnerable to substance use issues and harmful behaviors against adolescent girls in a context of inactivity and lack of opportunities. Due to the emergency, adolescent girls and boys face changing roles within the household, with girls having to take on more caring responsibilities, and boys taking on work. With no access to formal education, idleness, potential awareness of parental distress combined with uncertainly of the future, it is vital to engage youth in meaningful and empowering activities that will promote autonomy and positive engagement of adolescent girls and boys with their community. Enabling them to access opportunities and skills development whilst continuing to address the specific situations of vulnerabilities through specialized case management services is neededIn addition, since early 2018, incidents of violence and tensions have been observed, both within the camps, and between refugees and host communities. Community mobilization efforts must be scaled up to empower and strengthen the resilience of the refugee community and enhance social cohesion (JRP 2019). There is also need to shift approaches to empower Rohingya community, addressing aid-dependency and engaging them in protecting children and addressing key CP risks such as violence, exploitation, gender based violence (GBV), trafficking as mentioned above.

Activities or outputs

Output 1: Prevention and response services for children at risk 1.1: Comprehensive specialized case management services to at risk and vulnerable children1.2: Screening for family tracing and reunification.1.3: Structured static and mobile PSS activities 1.4: Targeted activities for adolescent boys and girls1.5: Capacity building to Tdh staff, key community figures, CP actors, CP committees and other stakeholders including the local government.1.6 Focused PSS activities with the children and adolescents at risk, and their caregivers 1.7.: Provision of mobile PSS activities for the caregivers Output 2. Community based child protection structures and actors established, strengthened and functioning 2.1: Establishment and training of community based child protection committees (CBCPC) members and support of led-initiatives (camps and host communities)2.2: Establishment of Children’s and Adolescent clubs2.3: Establishment of Caregivers and Elderly groups 2.4: Establishment of Children Committees2.5. Establishment of school based adolescents clubs and support of led-initiatives (host communities) To address these needs, Tdh will continue to provide a range of comprehensive services to both Rohingya refugees and host community populations. This will include structured static and mobile PSS activities for girls and boys between age 3 and 17. Targeted activities for adolescent boys and girls will include Adolescent centres and clubs (comprised of life skills, recreational activities, drama, sports, and arts). Adolescent-led initiatives will be supported, enabling full participation of adolescents in shaping projects and engaging with their communities positively. Vocational training and internships will also be facilitated. Tdh will continue providing specialized case management services to at risk and vulnerable children (including UASC), alternative care support and regular follow up of children. Coaching and supervision of case workers will be systematically integrated in work processes. Tdh will include activities engaging host communities. These include strengthened resilience of host communities and capacity of local government service delivery to cope with the crisis and addressing tensions with host communities through increased engagement, communication and programming to promote peaceful coexistence. In addition to working with CBCPCs in the camps, Tdh will work with existing structures, notably in close cooperation with local authorities and schools, support adolescent groups and community-led initiatives, as well as a facilitate exchanges between Rohingya refugees and host communities (e.g. for example recreational and sport activities between adolescents and learning exchanges between CPCB structures). Tdh will ensure capacity of local actors is built and will work with a local partner from May 2019 on to implement components of its CP programme. In view of this, close support and capacity building of the partner organization will be ensured as needed. Tdh participates regularly in the relevant Sector and the Inter Sector Coordination Group meetings that are held in Cox’s Bazar and Dhaka. At Cox’s Bazar level, Tdh is an active member of the Protection Working Group, Child Protection Sub Sector, Case Management Task Force, PSS Task Force, Communication with Communities (CwC) Task Force. At field level, Tdh participates at CIC meetings and any partner meetings called in the camps. Moreover, Tdh participates in Ukhia weekly coordination meetings with all actors (Army, authorities, sector leads, UN, INGOs and LNGOs) for the response, as well as in the regular camp coordination meetings. Tdh is CP FP in camps 4 and 5.
