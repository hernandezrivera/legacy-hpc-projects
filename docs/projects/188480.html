Project Summary

CARE NES proposed strategy is in line with 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) and specifically aligns with Strategic Objective 3 (Increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities). CARE’s proposed intervention will target Hasakah and Deir-ez-Zor (DEZ) governorate in NES, one of the most impoverished areas in the country. CARE proposes to implement the project in this governorates due to the scale of unmet needs.
The multi sectoral approach integrated with an innovative methodology grounding resilience in socio-economic wellbeing linking increased household economic capacities and opportunities with access to functioning basic infrastructure at community level and inclusive decision making at household and community levels, promoting more inclusive environment and improved ability of copying with stress. Based on that CARE is shifting towards a more integrated cross-cutting approach, allowing for a holistic understanding of the needs of communities and households. CARE has a strong programmatic focus around value chains since they link producers and buyers from different communities and purposefully integrate women, older persons, people with disabilities, IDPs and/or returnees. CARE’s proposed value chain approach in the short, medium and long term is expected to help Syrian families and communities affected by conflict to achieve a greater level of resilience, mitigate protection risks and participate in economic life.
Under FS & Agriculture, CARE will support the most vulnerable population of NorthEast Syria for economic recovery and the restoration of livelihoods and functioning markets related to wheat and sheep value chains as well as increasing community well-being and social cohesion: more specifically provision of agricultural and livestock inputs (seeds, fertilizers, Drip irrigation systems, sheep, fodder), establishment of veterinary services (vaccinations, trainings of doctors), establishment of vocational training centers. CARE NES team strongly coordinate with NES active clusters including Food Security & Livelihood Cluster, Agriculture working group, Cash Working Group, early Recovery & Livelihood Working Group to avoid any duplication and to be inline with cluster guidelines.

5 - Implementation of activities

CARE closely coordinates with local stakeholders including HAO, Local council, Comin and local leaders. CARE will also establish community committees formation concept to increase community ownership. CARE team conduct several meeting with local authorities to explain and orient them about project objectives, modalities, beneficiary selection criteria. CARE will identify beneficiaries through established community committees andCARE program team will conduct beneficiaries registration and using scoring lists according to pre-set vulnerability criteria (including Food Consumption Score, reducedCoping Strategies Index, family size, family status, and income) and a sample verified byCARE MEAL. CARE staff/ partner will be trained on community mobilization/sensitization, CARE implementation approach, complaint response mechanism and do no harm approach. At fi eld level, local council and comin will be approached on project orientation, modalities, beneficiaries registration and CRM. Where possible, fi eld teams will conduct community outreach activities to identify additional vulnerable families and individuals in need of support. CARE has built on the learnings from previous or existing cash programming and selected these modalities through consultations with the target groups, whilst also considering their feasibility, appropriateness, acceptance and contributions towards the overall project objective.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

CARE continuously aims to improve the effectiveness of its programming towards amore protective and empowering environment for all program participants, with a specific focus on women and girls and to enhance safety and dignity and promote and protect human rights without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation. By mainstreaming gender and protection, CARE strives to ensure that ‘do no harm,’ non-discrimination, meaningful access, safety, dignity, participation, empowerment and accountability all become integral to every aspect of its humanitarian response. Proactive measures that acknowledge and respond to the different needs, vulnerabilities and capacities of women, girls, boys and men of all ages, abilities and diverse backgrounds include Rapid Gender Analysis (RGA), PSEA training for staff and partners, gender mainstreaming training. Beneficiary vulnerability criteria are designed to assist those most in need and reflect the gendered dynamics of the context. CARE also looks at solutions to ensure that women are included for assistance through partners and local communities. CARE assesses partner organizations’ gender approaches during selection and provides recommendations, training and coaching as needed. Key disability-related needs and to inform more inclusive interventions that are truly accessible to the most vulnerable.CARE’s beneficiary scoring system also includes questions related to PLWD.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Political developments and/or security incidents leading to temporary or permanent loss of access Mitigation 1: Contingency plans are in place detailing a scale-back plan for any event in which the context does not allow safe humanitarian programming. Regular monitoring of security and geo-political dynamics by AD, CD and S&S Manager. Exploring alternative options to diversify program activities or locations, where circumstances allow. CARE has comprehensive SOPs for operating in high risk areas. For medevac support, CARE is training fi eld staff on basic trauma medical management and has scoped out medical services in our operational areas.All staff will receive awareness training for IEDs; drivers receive safe driving training and travel in convoys in high risk areas far from CARE offices. Recruitment of local staff from implementation areas to increase acceptance and understanding of contextual understanding. Risk 2: Corruption and financial mismanagementMitigation: CARE is coordinating with other humanitarian partners to review and share information on vendors and vendor practices in the NES context and has thorough due diligence and fraud prevention mechanisms in place.Risk 3: COVID-19 affecting mobility and accessMitigation 3: CARE has developed SOPs for all of its programming areas as well as for its operations. Mitigation measures are in place and regularly updated according to changes in context and coordination with relevant actors.
