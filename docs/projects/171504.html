Project Summary

During the current emergency situation associated with the spread of COVID19 in the Gaza Strip, it is extremely difficult for women in reproductive age to access Sexual & Reproductive Health Services (SRHS). As in the past few weeks, Ministry of Interior upon recommendations of MOH imposed curfew & restricted the movement of people & basic services were adversely affected especially health care services.
Whereas, MOH has closed outpatient clinics in hospitals as well as all the primary health care centers (PHCCs). On the other hand, UNRWA and all other health providers closed all their centers which prevent the women from access SRHS including family planning services, and the services were provided only through phone consultation or home visits.
Recently, MOH has taken actions to re-provide antenatal & postnatal care for identified risk pregnancies; however, it has substantially reduced family planning services, moreover, completely stopped routine antenatal, postnatal & preconception care.
While according to HNO 2021, the redirection of resources from provision of SRHS affected 80,000 vulnerable women.
Thus, the project idea responds to the needs of vulnerable women in the Gaza Strip by providing SRHS to 10,000 women of reproductive age (including PWD & GBV cases) through UHWC’s 6 PHCCs across the Gaza Strip in addition to Al-Awda hospital.
The project targets 1000 Women suffering from risk pregnancies, 5000 Women who suffer from anemia during pregnancy or lactation, 500 women with unmet need for FP & 3500 vulnerable women who need routine prenatal services but cannot afford the costs of follow-up & treatment.
Moreover, 1500 women out of the targeted risk pregnant & anaemia cases will receive normal/CS delivery services at Al Awda Hospital.
To ensure the follow up of the poor & vulnerable women who are targeted by the project but can't afford the transportation costs to reach health facilities, or can't reach the PHCCs due to the restriction movement; home visits will be arranged to deliver SRHS to them.
All the project services will be provided totally free of charge.
Furthermore, the project includes awareness-raising activities for the beneficiaries regarding PSEA, Covid-19 infection prevention measures & pregnancy related problems, through social media & radio. Knowing that Facebook ads will be adapted for PWD through sign language.
Through the project, 21 workers from the UHWC's reproductive health units will receive training in prevention measures of Covid-19 infection during providing services, & PSEA.
To achieve transparency & access to information for the community, the activities of the project will be published via radio & social media.
To achieve the protection during the provision of services, prevention & safety measures will be followed in terms of triage cases before entering health facilities, providing them with PPE and reserving advance appointment to reduce waiting time & overcrowding within the health facility.
To enable community participation in identifying their problems, needs & priorities, 50 beneficiaries have been contacted via phone from all over the Gaza Strip, moreover, 50 beneficiaries will be contacted via phone at the midterm of the project to take feedback that contributes to the project’s monitoring, addressing the deviations and modifying the activities of the project if needed, furthermore , 100 E-questionnaires will be filled by the beneficiaries to measure their satisfaction at the midterm & the end of the project.
Beneficiaries will be informed through Facebook & radio on the mechanisms of complaints mainly SEA.
UHWC’s HIS is in place to provide accurate and fast reports, where data will be disaggregated according to age & disability.
It is worth to mention that during the design of the project coordination has been done with the Health Cluster Coordinator as well as with UNFPA in order to enhance complementarily & to avoid duplication in the provided services.

1 - Needs

MOH’s report indicated that during 2019, there were 55,292 pregnant women visited the PHCCs in the Gaza Strip, moreover the report showed that, the No. of women in reproductive age (15-49 years old) was estimated by 488,413 women, which represents 22.5% of the total population of the Gaza Strip & represents 49.1% of the total female No. in the Gaza Strip. This group of females suffers from scarcity of resources such as, family planning, nutritional supplements needed to treat anemia & malnutrition, as the percentage of pregnant women suffering from malnutrition reached 38%. While risk pregnancies cases, represented 32.9% of the total pregnancies in MOH. Meanwhile, represented 48.2% of the total number of new pregnant women in UNRWA with an estimated increase by 120.6 % over 2018. (MoH Report 2019) Since the spread of Covid-19 pandemic in the Gaza Strip in August 2020, MOH has closed outpatient clinics of its hospitals, completely closed the PHCCs and thereby SRHS has been stopped. Afterward, within October 2020, MOH opened some of its clinics and PHCCs, where only SRHS are provided for risk pregnancies, moreover, family planning services are scaled down and completely stopped routine antenatal, postnatal & preconception care. The restriction of movement and the significant increase in unemployment and poverty rates in link with COVID19 pandemic have negatively impacted women access to SRHS. According to the HNO 2021, there is a significant gap in the size and the nature of the provided SRHS by the health sector in the Gaza Strip during Covid-19 pandemic that affected 80,000 vulnerable women. Needs Assessment has been conducted by UHWC in Oct. 2020, targeted 50 women from all the governorates of the Gaza Strip, the report indicated that 66% of participated women live far away from health-care centers and they sometimes use public transportation, however they cannot afford the cost, which force them to, at times, to walk to the health centers despite the long distance, moreover, 100% of them emphasize that the SRHS are affected due to the procedures linked to Covid-19 pandemic including the closure of health-care centers, restriction of movement and home quarantine in addition to their fear to get infected. While 60% of them stated that there is shortages in the family planning services, as these services have been stopped by MOH and UNRWA and not available in the private sector. Therefore, the project idea responds to the needs of the most vulnerable women in reproductive age (including PWD & GBV cases) who suffer from risk pregnancies, anemia during pregnancy or lactation & women with unmet need for FP adding to women in need of routine prenatal follow up but suffer from extreme poverty & couldn't pay the service fees. Home visits will also be provided to beneficiaries who cannot access the PHCCs due to either the state of poverty or the restricted movement due Covid-19 pandemic. In order to raise women's awareness in sexual exploitation, prevention of Covid-19 infection transmission, risk pregnancy, Animia .. etc.; awareness messages will be sent through radio & social media, in addition to individual education inside the PHCCs. To achieve the principle of protection for the beneficiaries of the project, prevention measures to prevent the transmission of the Covid-19 will be followed according to the MOH & WHO guidelines to ensure that infected cases do not enter the health facilities, through initial checks, measure temp. and providing PPE for both, the health providers and the beneficiaries and activating the advance reservation system to reduce waiting time and overcrowding. It is worth to mention that during the design of the project coordination has been done with the Health Cluster Coordinator as well as with UNFPA in order to enhance complementarily & to avoid duplication in the provided services.

2 - Activities

The project will target the most vulnerable and marginalized groups of women who suffer from anemia during pregnancy or lactation, risk pregnancy & women with unmet need for Family Planning, and women in need for antenatal services but couldn't access the service due to their hardship economic situation (including PWD and GBV cases). The project will contribute to bridging the gaps in the SRHS that were mentioned in HNO 2021, as all primary & secondary reproductive health care services will be provided to the project beneficiaries free of charge through the 7 UHWC’s facilities deployed in all Gaza Strip governorates through the following activities: 1. Follow-up of 1000 risk pregnant (900 women, 100 girls) (5 PWD), including clinical examination, ultrasound, lab. tests and providing them with nutritional supplements and necessary medications.2. Follow-up 5000 pregnant of lactating women suffering from Anemia (4500 women, 500 girls) (10 PWD), including all services that are mentioned in the first item focusing on providing them with nutritional supplements specifically folic acid. 3. Providing 500 women (470 women, 30 girls) (2 PWD) with family planning services including clinical examination, ultrasound, lab. tests and contraceptive methods. 4. Providing routine prenatal services to 3500 vulnerable women (3150 women – 350 girls) (5 PWD) who need this service but cannot afford the costs of follow-up & treatment. 5. 1680 Home visits (20 visits *12 months * 7 health facilities = 1680 visits) will be conducted to 800 women (700 women – 100 girls) (PWD 8) from the project beneficiaries who couldn't access the service due to the restriction on movement in link with the spread of COVID19 in the Gaza Strip or who don’t have the money for transportation to reach the health facilities.6. Providing delivery services to 1500 cases (1200 women – 300 girls) (PWD 15) (1000 normal, 500 caesarian section) through referring the benefited cases (women with risk pregnancy or Animia) to Al-awda hospital particularly those living in the northern are, while, others from middle or southern areas of GS, will be referred to MoH hospitals to receive normal delivery or CS unless they ask to be referred to Al Awda Hospital.7. Through the activities of the project, health-awareness will be provided through individual education to the women visiting the health facilities on Covid-19 prevention measures and sexual exploitations issues.8. Affected population & beneficiaries will receive awareness messages through 3 radio spots and 7 facebook advertisements in the following topics:- Announcement the project activities (access to information).- Prevention measures of Covid-19 infection. - Risk pregnancy, Animia, malnutrition and family planning.- Prevention of Sexual Exploitation & Abuse PSEA. - The UHWC complaints mechanisms. 9. In order to achieve the optimum results, maintaining the dignity of the beneficiaries and preventing them from sexual exploitation and Covid-19 infection, 21 workers all of them are female) in the UHWC's reproductive health units will receive two training courses using electronic platform in order to ensure social-distancing regarding Covid-19. The first course (12 hours, 3hours*4days) will be related to prevention measures of Covid-19 infection during providing services, and the second one (12 hours, 3hours*4days) will be on the ways of dealing with sexual exploitation/abuse and the referring methods. 10. To enable community participation in monitoring and evaluation of the project, 50 beneficiaries will be contacted via phone at the midterm of the project and modifying the activities of the project if needed, moreover, 100 E-questionnaires will be filled by the beneficiaries to measure their satisfaction about the provided services at the midterm & the end of the project. 11. PPE will be distributed to both the project staff and beneficiaries to prevent them from Covid-19 infection and to contribute in limiting the spread of the pandemic.

3 - Indicators

The success of the project will be measured through the achievement of the following indicators, knowing that MEAL plan will be prepared including data collection methods and desegregations of these data, tools, frequencies, responsibilities and reporting: # of women in reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women receiving quality maternal and nutrition services # of women in reproductive age, receiving family planning services # of home visits conducted # of staff participated in PSEA and providing reproductive serviced during COVID19 training courses % of knowledge increased by the trainees. # of women directly benefitting from awareness and health education % of patients satisfaction about the provided services Monitoring and Evaluation: UHWC will follow up and monitor the project activities through the MEAL plan that will be prepared by the UHWC MEAL Officer to ensure the progress of activities according to the action plans. Once the project approved, the project coordinator will conduct a meeting with UHWC project staff through ZOOM platform, to preserve physical distancing in connection with the infection conditions of the COVID19 pandemic, where he/she will present the project activities, goals, indicators and results to the project staff to prepare action plan and to distribute responsibilities and duties. The project coordinator will follow up and monitor activities on the ground on a daily basis through direct contact with the project team. UHWC project coordinator will conduct regular monitoring zoom meetings and field visits to the project locations to insure continuous flow of information on the progress of activities, and to see work on the ground. Reports and statistics will be sent from the field to the project coordinator where all statistics will be desegregated according to age and PWD. The Project coordinator will compare the financial plan with the real expenses as well as action plan with the achieved activities in order to find out if there is a deviation in the implementation of activities according to the time plan and the financial cost. UHWC project department will coordinate with the donor for issues related to the implementation of the project. The UHWC project team will also document the project activities through photos and audio-visual report that will be published at the UHWC website, facebook and YouTube channel at the end of the project. The beneficiaries will also participate in the monitoring and evaluation of the project, as 50 phone calls will be conducted with the project beneficiaries to monitor the project services at the mid of the project where necessary modification will be made to the project activities if necessary. While 100 e-questionnaires will be filled to measure the beneficiaries' satisfaction about the provided services at the midterm and end of the project.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Within the policies and the principles of UHWC in involving the local community to participate in all stages of its programs and projects that are implemented, UHWC adopts complaints and feedback system, which collect the data, complaints and any suggestions from the beneficiaries and the community members through using the following tools: 1- The complaints boxes, which are located in all UHWC's health and community facilities.2- Generic email3- Hotline.4- Paper-based or electronic questionnaires, which are distributed to measure the beneficiaries ’satisfaction about the provided services in the UHWC’s facilities. Complaints management mechanism:Complaints are managed through the following mechanism:- Enable receiving complaints from affected population.- Responding to complaints.- Accountability and lessons learned. In order to engage the local community and encouraging them to file complaints, UHWC announces complaints mechanisms and tools through social media. Moreover, the receptionists in each of UHWC's health facility inform the public face-to-face about the complaints mechanisms in the event that any violations of the beneficiaries are being addressed while receiving services. Through these methods, the beneficiaries' satisfaction can be measured in addition to the most important problems they face while receiving services from the UHWC’s facilities. However, the feedback is taken by the beneficiaries to determine their suggestions to improve the quality of the services in terms of both quantitative and qualitative. Whereas, beneficiaries can use e-mail to file more sensitive complaints about sexual exploitation while receiving services. Special committee from UHWC manage these complaints, as the committee send reports about the beneficiaries' complaints to the Executive Director to solve them and to use them in designing future intervention strategies.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project idea, services and implementation mechanisms start from respecting the principles of protection stipulated in the humanitarian charter including the right to live with dignity, the right to receive assistance and the right to protection. Moreover, these mechanisms are part of the UHWC organizational values within its strategic plan 2020-2024, where UHWC deal with its beneficiaries according to its slogan (health service is a right to whoever needs it), in addition to ensuring the safety and dignity of patients through: - In order to prevent SEA during the provision of the project services the project staff will attend PSEA training. As well as their contracts will include standard clause on PSEA in accordance with IASC principles and guidance.- In order not to expose the beneficiaries to more harm, they will be educated about SEA and the complaints mechanism.- Equal access and non-discrimination in the provision of health services.- Patient consultations and documentation are private and confidential.- Ensuring that beneficiaries are not harmed while receiving services on the basis of dignity & protection. And here it is worth to mention that 12 from Al Awda Hospital's medical staff from both sexes were trained in Sphere standards and Do No Harm, to create a positive deviation in the behavior of workers while providing health services to beneficiaries in a positive manner and away from any discrimination. - The project services will be provided to the project beneficiaries who suffer from poverty and vulnerability totally free of charge in accordance with the human rights-based approach.- All necessary information for the cases related to their status and mechanisms of intervention will be provided to them.- Gender needs will be taken into account through the presence of a female physician and nurses among the work teams to response the gender needs of women and girls.- Since the establishment of the health facilities of UHWC, they were designed to suit the people with physical disabilities to ensure easy access to the services.- Providing security and protection through the health facilities’ security staff.- The availability of ICU ambulance to refer emergency cases to MoH hospitals.- Providing work accident insurance for the project staff.- Providing the project staff with health insurance at UHWC health facilities. - The project services will be provided to the beneficiaries regardless their color, race, religious or political affiliation, where the proposed project will contribute to enable equal and impartial access to health services to the marginalized and vulnerable people.- To achieve the protection during the provision of services, prevention & safety measures will be followed in terms of triage cases before entering health facilities, providing them with PPE and reserving advance appointment to reduce waiting time & overcrowding within the health facility.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The UHWC adopts the principle of equality and impartially in providing its services to beneficiaries, especially marginalized groups that need humanitarian interventions to improve their health and humanitarian status. UHWC provides its services without any discrimination on the basis of age, sex, religion, ethic, race or political affiliation. Knowing that through this project and to achieve the principle of impartiality in the provision of services UHWC will provide the services to the targeted people on the basis that each patient has its individual characteristics and special needs, especially individuals with disabilities as the most vulnerable in the society and who need special attention and protection depend on Human dignity, confidentiality and non-discrimination. Where the UHWC provides its services according to the slogan (health service is a right to whoever needs it). Access to the project services will be easy according to the following: - Accessibility: The Union of Health Work Committees will provide the project services through its 7 health facilities across the Gaza Strip. The geographical locations of these facilities are characterized by their presence in the middle of the governorates, which is easily accessible and is known to the public since their establishment. The project services will be provided to the target groups including PWD and GBV cases in a safety and dignity manner, and without any discrimination. To ensure the access of all the targeted women to the project’s SRHS especially the women who don’t have the money for transportation to reach the health facilities, they will be followed up through arranging home visits in the event of not attending their follow-up sessions, or couldn't access the center due to restriction of movement linked to the spread of COVID19 pandemic in the Gaza Strip.- Access to information: Based on the principle of transparency with the local community, the project will be announced through UHWC website and facebook pages. The project banner will be located at the waiting areas of the UHWC health facilities. - Availability: Health services are provided in the health facilities of UHWC from 7:30 am to 8:30 pm, where they provide all medical services and medical support services in addition to awareness services for the families. While Al Awda Hospital services are provided 24 hours, 7 days a week.- Affordability: All the project services will be provided totally free of charge in link with the project's objectives on the one hand and the needs of the beneficiaries on the other hand.- Acceptability: All the services that the project will provide express an urgent need for the beneficiaries, making it more acceptable as a priority of the community and do not conflict with the values, principles, ethics and customs of the community. Note that the project team consists of both sexes (male and female), which contributes to its social acceptance
