Basic Info

Project Name

Increasing equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition special service delivery for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations in Jonglei, Fangak and Canal/Pigi targeting 18,802 boys, girls age 6-59 months and pregnant & Lactating women (PLWs).

Start Date

01/01/2022

End Date

31/12/2022

Project Summary

The project will focus on the Provision and Increasing the equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection, treatment, prevention of acute malnutrition for children 6 -59 months of age and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) and will consider people with disabilities by targeting beneficiaries of 18,802 (SAM Boys 1,841, girls 1,911, MAM Boys 2,459 Girls 2,664 age 6-59 months and suport with BSFP Boys 732 Girls 792 age 6-23 months and MAM-PLWs 4,511 and supporting PLWs with BSFP 3,892) these beneficiaries will be supported in Fangak Canal/Pigi, counties of Jonglei.
HFO project will contribute to the achievement of Nutrition Cluster strategic objectives for humanitarian response in 2022 by Identifying and establish OTP, TSFP, sites, doing MUAC screening, treating children under 6-59 years with RUTF, RUSF for SAM and MAM & BSFP for PLWs and children 6-23 months. The project will coordinate meetings with affected population, deworming services and in community outreach integrating with health. vitamin A supplementation, deworming, Distribution of LLINT to OTPs and SC MIYCN counseling.
1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventive nutrition service delivery for children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women in the prioritized locations.
2: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2022.
3: To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education, with COVID-19 preventive measures and raising of alert suspected cases of COVID-19 and work with Protection sectors.
4: To strengthen nutrition information system for evidence-based nutrition response by the end of 2022. The nutrition project will integrate with projects of health WASH, FSL, Protection, supported by HFO or other partners to energize the efforts of humanitarian response in 2022.
The recruitment of community Nutrition workers, community nutrition volunteers and Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) counselors will be done in consultations with community members and local authority for their participation in the selection criteria by making sure location stakeholders are involved and gender balance is taken in to account of getting workers from both males and females where necessary and including people with disability. The nutrition response will be designed to ensure that Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) is a part, at all stages of the response. HFO staff will continue to use the Nutrition Cluster and Global Nutrition Cluster operational framework on AAP that includes minimum and/or major key activities and indicators focused on participation, establishment of complaints and feedback mechanisms, transparency and communications, planning and implementation of nutrition responses as well as monitoring and reporting. The project response will promote a consultative approach by consulting a wider range of affected communities to ensure communities have simplicity and have their inputs on the objectives of the nutrition response, the activities, the location, and their titles where the most affected groups are, the nutrition response will consist of a package of treatment and prevention services delivered through the health facility/nutrition sites and through community outreaches, as per national BHNS. This will be supported through an ongoing capacity building effort at facility and community levels, including community mobilization and engagement for participation. Hard-to-reach areas will be covered through outreach services and Integrated packages of services with Health, Food Security, WASH, Protection, HFO will continue to report to NIS/5Ws flat forms.

1 - Needs

South Sudan ten states’ health system depends mainly on humanitarian assistance (88%) and is still unable to cope and respond to humanitarian emergencies and provide primary essential life-saving health & nutrition services. Poor health facility functionality and capacity, and limited coverage and hard access to existing system, have made it difficult to meet additional nutirition needs. Fangak and Canal/Pigi Counties in Jonglei state are deteriorating with most locations being devastated by the continue flooding (IRNA August 2021). Recent reports at Fangak and Canal/Pigi have the GAM rate of 19% beyond WHO threshold, this is significantly high due to lack of harvest that caused severe shortage of food where community only depends on water lily as their food since the beginning of 2021 (FSNMS, 2021) which highlighted the high trends of malnutrition in these counties. Communities continue to be displaced by flood, lose livestocks, farming and houseolds and are left homeless in so many villages. Mass MUAC screenings conducted by HFO and partners through nutrition and health outreaches in 2021 showed 574 PLWs and about 2,108 children 6-59 months suffer from MAM and SAM in Keew, Juaibor Manajang and Pulita where HFO implements the World Bank/UNICEF health project 2019-2022. In Fangak county 6 health facilities supported by HFO only 2 have OTPs/TSFPs and 1 with stabilization center, Juaibor PHCC destroyed by flood in August 2021 and was moved to Manajang with health facitlity barely operating under tents and trees with no nutrition services. Most OTPs and TSFPs are completely detroyed and more children under 5 and pregant and lactating women are left without nutrition services in Fangak and Canal/Pigi. The IRNA reports conducted in August and September 2021 revealed that 3 payams of Pulita, Phom and Manajang in Fangak and Atar in Canal/Pigi have a total population of 86,089 persons combined. Out of this population, 65,127 (76%) has been severe affected by the flooding with no shelter, food and water and only live in highgrounds in Manajang, Keew, Lerpingy, Pulita and Tangnyang, Atar Diel, Kaldak respectively with WASH condition very difficult. This population is in urgent need of humanitarian assistance. Food is the most requested by the community. Sanitation and hygiene practices are poor in the counties as most WASH facilities have been severely affected by the flooding. HFO is planning to improve the quality of nutrition interventions and increase coverage to mobilize up to approximately 621,450 USD to response to the current humanitarian crisis in Fangak and Canal/Pigi by the end of 2022 this will contribute to nutriton stretagic objective, in increasing equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2022. HFO is planning to scale up the program with approaches to reach the vulnerable communities affected by the increasing humanitarian crisis. Regular training, supervision and on- job training will continue to be provided to enhance the capacity of staff, empower the community so the quality of service can be improved. The COVID-19 pandemic continues to negatively impact the economy and affect households' food security. This ongoing pandemic restriction with deteriorating humanitarian crisis would continue to negatively impact household food security and increase malnutrition rates among children under 5. HFO will continue to keep strengthen COVID-19 preventive measures through adherence to preventive measures and work on risk communication and community engagement for integration of preventive activities at all levels. Community outreach activities for MIYCN counseling, mother to mother support group will be conducted under the guidance of COVID-19 protocol as per the countries and WHO guideline.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

HFO multi-sectoral approach helps in ensuring that we meet most of the needs of the targeted beneficiaries including protection, inclusion and equality. Healthcare Foundation Organization during the initial stages of the projects conducts a general risk assessment and once the project is assessed to cause risks to both the beneficiaries and the organization, mitigation measures are always employed. In previous projects, mitigation measures such as clear and open communication with the beneficiaries will help in protecting the beneficiaries from being exploited, choosing locations for registration and distribution which are close and safe for the beneficiaries and an inclusive decision making process with the beneficiaries. HFO also works with the government and the community leaders to ensure that adequate information reaches the beneficiaries and the community leaders usually take responsibility of managing the crowds as the community members listen to them more. During distributions and registrations, vulnerable groups such as sick, disabled, pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, children and the elderly are prioritized so that they spend little time in waiting. Confidentiality is also key to protecting the beneficiaries and HFO normally conducts thorough training for the staff before any activities in the field which includes data protection policies etc. 1. During consultation of OPD outreach, ANC, Immunization services, protection activities have to be considered and ensrureed by the team to keep an eye on the vulnerable groups and they are given high priority to get services under registration and treatment. It is very important no one is left under attended for. 2. By mitigating this risk, it important for the project to make this clear from the start that people with disability are part of decision making and any person with diability is identified and assisted to reach where services are or can be reached by services wherever she or he is, this can be done through outreach sessions and their representatives have to have information for where they can be reached easily. 3. The project will work collaboratively with all protection and GBV partners to mark referral pathways and ensure every survivor has access to the services. HFO will have to work with law enforcement agencies; such as police and local leaders to ensure everyone's right to services adhered to and observed for the purpose of accessing huamnitarian services. 4. Marginalized groups are the ones that bear the burden of humnitarian crisis where they are left under served and their problems are always ignored or dismissed. This project will ensure at the community levels women are at the front and during employment, both females and males will be both encouraged to participate fully during the implementation of the project. In so doing, the most marginalized person will definitely be in the picture. And the issues of marginalization shall be minimized.
