Project Summary

CARE proposes to enhance shelter and living conditions to mitigate protection, safety and GBV risks by providing safe and dignified shelter and settlement. CARE will integrate GBV risk identification into its assessment and analysis processes and center GBV risk mitigation interventions across all shelter programming. CARE will be conducting: Activity 1: Household shelter rehabilitation targeting a total of 500 Households. Activity 2: IDP site rehabilitation targeting a total of 20,000 individuals. Activity 3: Provision of dignified shelter units for a total of 300 Households Through the proposed interventions CARE will ensure community participation and planning, where specific needs, risks and concerns of diverse groups are addressed from the start of programming. CARE will ensure women and girls equally participate in community consultations and planning and address their specific needs, risks and concerns to mitigate GBV risks. Also CARE is playing an essential role in leading several TWiGs and chairing the Shelter/NFI Cluster to provide technical support and ensure following SPHERE and humanitarian standards. The intervention also includes collective shelter rehabilitation and upgrade targeting 400 HHs.

3 - Needs

With reference to HNO 2022, it is estimated that 5.75 million people in Syria will require shelter support in 2022. In accordance with the MSNA findings, over 15% of the overall population live in substandard shelter types. Of the remaining population, 28% (of 85%)reside in finished residential buildings classified as damaged and/ or displaying inadequate conditions. As such, a total of 38% of the overall population lives in substandard, damaged, and/ or inadequate shelter. Access to basic services , protection from the elements, and safety and security are among the top shelter adequacy issues reported. In conjunction with poor physical shelter, 32% of the overall population are renting or hold weak forms of occupancy agreement, resulting in ineligibility for shelter assistance and/ or heightened risks of eviction. 54% of IDPs live in substandard, damaged, and/ or inadequate shelter. The average period of household displacement is currently in excess of 6 years with more than four out of ten households being displaced three times or more. According to latest findings, 25% of IDPs continue to reside in sites of last resort, primarily in the northwest and northeast, which lack crucial infrastructure and rely on emergency shelter solutions and continued humanitarian support. Across governorates, IDPs are the population group with the highest reported rate of rental occupancy arrangements, ranging from 55 to 99%. 4 - Implementation of activities

CARE will undertake three main activities: Activity 1) Household shelter rehabilitation 2) IDP settlements rehabilitation Activity 3) Provision of dignified shelter units. CARE will undertake emergency shelter repairs to ensure that housingunits provide safe and dignified shelter - with a focus on gender equitable access. This will be undertaken in areas that are deemed safe from bombardment in housing units which adhere to CARE’s selection criteria and considers the following areas: suitability of the building for work; level of repair support needed. CARE will explore alternatives to expand access for IDPs to proper and dignified shelter in adequate settlements. Given the urgent need to find shelter, the majority of newly displaced people are self-settling on land which is often unsuitable for habitation - rocky and mountainous surroundings, sloping ground, with environmental risks or Housing, Land and Property (HLP) rights challenges -accessible only via unpaved roads and lacking in basic facilities. It is extremely difficult for CARE to provide essential services such as WASH, electricity, lighting, and site drainage to these groups once the families have erected shelters or tents and informal settlements have been established. Therefore, CARE will support the planning, leveling, and arrangement of new sites, improving tent insulation and lighting.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

One of the main risk is "Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitated." As a mitigation measure CARE will ensure to abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster, do not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP Due Diligence Guidelines, include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed, have an HLP section in the M&E assessment form. Another risk that might be faced is "risk of increased community tension due to different assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to a feeling of discrimination". To mitigate this measure, CARE will include assistance variance monitoring in monitoring, monitor for correlation between assistance varying packages and inter-community tensions, include community understanding and perceptions of fairness of assistance in monitoring, presence on the ground to monitor implementation and link with local partners, volunteers and authorities to monitor.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

CARE recognizes we have a responsibility to protect people we work with, and who work for us, and we will continuously strive to prevent sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse and child abuse from happening. We take seriously all reports of sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, and child abuse. Our actions are informed by a survivor-centered approach which means that the needs and wishes of survivors guide our response, that survivors are treated with dignity and respect, and the rights of survivors to privacy and support are prioritized. CARE has a zero-tolerance approach toward sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, and child abuse. We will carefully examine allegations and investigate, and take appropriate disciplinary action where this is needed, taking into consideration the rights and interests of the survivor, consistent with CARE’s survivor-centered approach. We make very clear that sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, and child abuse in any form, perpetrated by our staff, partners or other related personnel, towards anyone, will not be tolerated. This policy applies to all CARE Employees and Related-Personnel.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security risks to CARE staff as a result of armed conflict: CARE maintains strong security protocols and closely monitors conflict developments. In spite of close monitoring and strong systems and protocols, it is impossible to fully mitigate this risk. Risks of aid diversion from both armed groups and civil governance entities: CARE works on developing strong systems, identifies growing risks trending in the context, and continues to work with UN and donors to strengthen the community-wide response to this risk. CARE also continues to directly monitor project activities at several layers at the area and country office levels. The risk of aid diversion does however continue to be a high risk that is difficult to fully mitigate. The spread of COVID-19 does not undermine CARE's ability to conduct field activities. CARE has developed contingency plans to continue to implement during a significant outbreak in NWS while also mitigating risks to field staff. Access –lack of access to affected population in targeted camp– mitigate with a strong ongoing engagement with the Turkish authorities, local communities and when needed push to overcome with and strengthening the field team, ambitious use of all access points. CARE will ensure to have staff trained and systems established to operate safely in this environment.
