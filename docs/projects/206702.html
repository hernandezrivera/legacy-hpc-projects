Project Summary

The proposed 12 months project will be implemented in Metuge and Chiure districts in Cabo Delgado province targeting 36 250 beneficiaries who are in crisis due to the conflict in Cabo Delgado since 2017. The conflict has had severe implications for the food security of the affected population. According to the WFP August situation report, the protracted violence and insecurity have disrupted agricultural activities, causing widespread displacement, and hindering access to essential resources (farmland, water, sea, etc) for food production. Additionally, the displacement of people from their homes and farmlands has resulted in a significant loss of livelihoods, exacerbating the vulnerability of already food-insecure communities.
The overall goal of the project is to meet immediate food needs and improve household nutrition while simultaneously reducing food aid dependency and restoring livelihoods to promote self-reliance. This will be achieved through food distribution, distribution of agricultural inputs, provide farmers with equipment, agriculture training, support Income generation activities and Village Savings and Loans Associations

The main project objective is to address the immediate needs of IDPs. The response will cover most affected IDPs in Chiure and Metuge districts. The project has the following specific objectives, with corresponding key components as follows:
• SO1: Addressing the most urgent needs of 28 750 vulnerable IDPs through food distribution to address a range of basic food needs being: rice/maize meal 25kgs/hh, cooking oil at 5 lt/hh, sugar at 5kg/hh and common beans at 10kg/hh,
• SO2: Improving the food security situation of 36 250 IDPs through increased access to agricultural inputs and enhanced ecological stability through an integrated farming approach
Drocas intends to distribute food assistance, as well as seeds, tools, and training on climate-resilient farming techniques. Priority will be given to the most vulnerable groups including people living with disability, female and child headed households, pregnant and lactating mothers, households with children under 5 years and people with chronic diseases.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Project operational risks: These are the risks related to project management and personnel, such as trained staff leaving the projector a partner organization which cannot comply with the obligations taken. This risk will mitigated by creating a solid partnershipwith committed partners and staff, always working on having the same vision and close collaboration in team building exercise.Implementation risks: This refers to set-backs in collaboration with local stakeholders, e.g. village chiefs not being interested, in the area with a different (more top-down) approach, or people/organizations just being reluctant to collaborate. At community level, it is therefore crucial to start in communities where a foundation is present, and if essential organizations in the area are reluctant, we will work on awareness raising and exchange visits, till we get them on board.Natural disasters: Climate change has globally increased the risk of these disasters, including new pests and diseases, in that case we will continue insisting that our approach aims at building resilience, and we have some seed money available for piloting innovations or opportunities that might arise and possibly mitigate the effects of the same disaster in the future.Political resistance or unwillingness: This is a risk at all community and district levels, where political leaders might not see the enefits of our project and show resistance to join, or even oppose certain activities, e.g. because of expecting hand-outs by the project. Our intensive series of workshops and awareness raising will surely mitigate this risk, but if such situations occur, we will actively seek to resolve them by means of diplomacy and personal talks, next to invitations for exposure visits.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project will encourage meaningful access to assistance and participation as well as empowerment of people specially women and elderly person through understanding of different gender impacts, and of men and women’s different needs, interests, vulnerabilities, capacities and coping strategies. The equal rights of men and women are explicit in the Humanitarian Charter. Rights and opportunities for both men and women should be enhanced and not compromised by aid interventions. Increased protection from violence, coercion and deprivation in emergency situations, particularly for women and girls, but also for specific risks faced by men and boys, are essential to effective implementation of this project. We will pay important attention to the impact of the project on women’s roles and workloads, access to and control of resources, decision making powers, and opportunities for skill development, in order to make sure that our interventions support and do not diminish the role of women.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Within the first phase of the project implementation, a context assessment will be done where the needs of the marginalised groups will be considered. The Washington set group of questions will be used to screen and select people with disabilities. A tailored barrier assessment will be done to help identify which obstacles people with disabilities face when they want to take part in the project. The beneficiary selection process will happen in an inclusive manner. To remove the barriers we will work together with the people with disabilities to jointly come up with measures to remove the barriers. We will earmark some of the budget for inclusion. This will help to ensure that funds are available for some of the specific interventions and adaptations for marginalised groups.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

No environmental assessment will be conducted during the period of project implementation. However, measures will be put in place to ensure that during project implementation, staff follows the Corporate Social responsibility guidelines of Dorcas to ensure that activities do not harm the environment. The proposed agriculture activities will be sustainable, and use of chemicals will be discouraged. As an impact of the intervention, it is expected to see more resilient target communities with easy access to livelihoods and the ability to better manage the available natural resources. However, theIDPs will be empowered with useful tools that will allow them to continue the activities, even when the intervention ends or when they return to their home of origin. The environmental Clubs, formed by the project, will also be a tool to motivate the IDPs to manage their own development beyond the duration of the project.
