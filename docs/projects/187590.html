Project Summary

Bonyan designed its inclusive education interventions to contributes to addressing the actual education needs in Syria and bridges the gaps in the education sector and comply with the strategic objectives of both the HRP and ED Cluster by improving the availability, access, and quality of education guided by an analysis of needs severity in accordance with assessment findings and key indicators
Likewise, Bonyan 2022-23 Education program will focus to bring children back to formal education through variety of interventions ensuring that children have access to lifelong healthy learning prioritizing vulnerable children including adolescents girls boys living with disabilities in hard-to-reach locations with strong coordination with presented authorities in Aleppo and Idleb targeting total of 140,000 indivdual for 2022-23 program including (60000 for 2022: 50000 children [25000G/25000B, 5-17 years old - 2000 CwD] and 10000 Teachers and Caregivers)
** Obj.1: Scale up safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children aged 5-17 years
Starting with back-to-learn campaigns targeting 30,000 indvidual (children and their parents and surrounded communities) to raise awareness of importance of education, address parents’ concerns, and outreach for the established education services including distribution of School kits for children, teacher, and schools
Schools rehabilitaiton targeting 50 schools and 30,000 children
activity will be conducted (for: gender separated WCs replacing doors windows whiteboards fixing the walls …) to expand the capacity of the educational facilities to host more children, provide equity services for females males and children with disabilities and provide better education
4 ECCE will be supported targeting 600 children (320G/280B, 3-5years old)
8 formal education school (primary & secondary) for 6,000 children (3100G/2900B – 250 CwD, 5-17 years old)
Non-formal education is tailored by age and needs to fullfill the gaps for each targeted age and gender through 260 individual TLSs will operate inside the schools and camps for 13,000 children (6700G/6300B – 500 CwD, 8-12 years old) to provide literacy and numeracy, remedial, and catch up along with PSS sessions.
FE and NFE classes will be provided with equipment children and teachers kit, learning materials consumables heating during winter, and sanitization materials with enforced COVID-19 measurements – the children will enroll in a referral pathways to re-integrate the children in formal education
Bonyan is planning to provide access to education for children with disabilities by provide targeted assistance within the schools in order to encourage these children to continue their education and integrate them with their peers
** Obj.2: Enhance the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth aged 5-17 years within a protective environment
Building the capacity of 1200 teachers and educational actors on child centered, gender sensitive inclusive education codes of conduct anti-bullying PSS referral mechanisms EiE and INEE Targeted training will be conducted for the in-school protection supervisors, and coordinators to improve project quality and compliance with protection principals and improve the established referral system
Bonyan will provide the schools incentives for the teachers and education actors along with teaching resources and supplementary kits during the whole academic year. And will integrate structured PSS life skills and citizenship programs within school activities. A complete MEAL framework will be utilized along with establishing feedback and complaints mechanisms and in-place PSEA reporting channels
for 2022: 60,000 children and Adults including (19,000 provided with educational services in classrooms)
for 2023 Bonyan plans to expand all its activities to target 80,000 children and Adults including (25,000 provided with educational services in classrooms)

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2022: There are over 7 million children (3-17 years) in need of education support.At the national level the rates of girls and boys not attending school was similar 17% of girls not attending, 19% of boys not attending. Bonyan conducted NA to assess education and protection needs in 17 sub-districts in Idlib and Aleppo, included 1077 schools, 196 ECE concluding 391K children (49% M, 51% F) 29,060 children in kindergartens 48%M 52%F. In Idlib, 417K dropout school children 69% of children are school-aged in the targeted sub-districts, of which 12,320 are registered in schools but not attending. In Aleppo 311K dropout school children 79% of children are school-aged in the targeted sub-districts, of which 82,690 are registered in schools but not attending. Only 6 centers for CwD (5 in Aleppo and 1 in Idlib). 14% of children are underachieving in Idlib and 19% are underachieving in Aleppo. 46% of schools need a maintenance works (223 schools of 487 active schools) within targeted sub-districts. Main factors of dropping out of school Poverty Displacement teachers are not paied the significant cost on parents to provide their children's stationery, books, etc. The most important needs for teachers and administrators are salaries, educational supplies, and capacity building. For children: books stationery and school bags clothing psychological support and make-up lessons in addition to personal protection equipment's against COVID-19

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will increase educational opportunities and raise the level of quality of education by ensuring a safe and appropriate environment for the educational process and by targeting schools at the beginning of the project with minor rehabilitation (gender separated WCs replacing doors windows whiteboards fixing walls…) following technical assessment The BTL activities will be conducted to rais awareness about the importance of education and gain local support to schools and education projects, the activity will target 30,000 individuals that include children parents and teachersthat will lead to enroll the children in the available education services ECE will be conducted in 4 centers targeting 600 children for 2022 and will be expanded to 6 centers and 900 children The project will link formal ,non-formal education together, as the project will provide support to8 formal school (primary & secondary) and 260 individual TLSs providing NFE (literacy and numeracy, remedial, and catch up) inside the schools and camps targeting total of 19,000 child for 2022 and providing incentives for 1,200 teachers, this activity will reach 10 FE schools and 300 TLSs in 2023 Sterilization campaigns will be carried out on an ongoing basis to protect of Covid-19,awareness sessions will be held for students on prevention methods, in addition to provided PSS sessionsThe project will also conduct PTA meetings, caregiver engagement, capacity building for teachers, heating ,and stationary

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1:Students are not sufficiently protected from adults or older students while present in a learning centers Mitigation: The codes of conduct will be applied within schools, which explain the procedures that the workers in the educational field must follow, including positive disciplinary methods as they will be fully aware of the mechanisms for reporting sexual violence and punishmentRisk 2: Violence against education facilities and education providers -Mitigation : schools will be provided with safety and evacuation plans in cases of danger and educational staff will be trained on them, in addition to providing them with security and safety equipment and guiding signs will be placed on how to useRisk 3: Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving, Mitigation: (a) PSEA policies should cover standards of conduct and dealings for the employees of the organization, especially the prevention of sexual exploitation where they will be applied in a manner that ensures accountability, It has to be in place, practiced and monitored Policies must cover standards of conduct for staff and representatives of the organization, and specifically the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, including accountability measures (b) Targeted communities and learning The community is shared with these standards and procedures, and the mechanism for reporting any abuse or exploitation is explained to them


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All humaintaerian workers are obliged to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. Managers at all levels have a responsibility to support and develop systems that maintain this environment.All staff will be trained on humanitarian principles and standards, as well as the importance of preserving the dignity of beneficiaries and the PSEA. Upon recruitment, all staff members sign the Bonayn PSEA code of conduct, and a protection specialist provides orientations and regular PSEA training sessions on child protection policy and the PSEA code of conduct.Parents refuse to send their daughters alone in the provided transportation service due to the driver's potential for sexual harassment.Probability: medium High impact Mitigating measures include having a female coordinator accompany the girls on the bus, which will alleviate parents' concerns and protect the girls from any GBV act.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

AssumptionsThe safety and security are relatively stableNo major frontlines/military operations near to the targeted areas, and no major displacement movement to the areasAll the services and goods required in this project can be easily provided locallyGood level of coordination and cooperation with local authorities and ED Risks:Children dropping out of FE education to attend NFE centers Likelihood: medium Impact: High Mitigating: Identify specific criteria to all implementing partners for accepting children into the program.Education staff transfer to another school due to different scales Likelihood: medium Impact: high Mitigating: Standardize teacher's incentives at the levels of ESWG/cluster and other major donorsIncreased risk of intercommunity tension due to inequality of services/opportunities provided Likelihood: medium Impact: medium Mitigating measures: Work with the project stakeholders and partners to ensure that the most vulnerable, including host and IDP youth are servedClosure of borders risking a delay in transporting equipment and supplies Likelihood: medium Impact: medium Mitigating measures: Storage will happen in warehouses inside Syria Diversion of supplies (BTL materials and self-learning materials) Likelihood: low Impact: medium Mitigating measures: Ensure projects in insecure locations have security plans for their resources/assets and have sufficient resources to enact those plans
