Project Summary

This project started in late 2017 and, in its efforts to decentralize services, provide supplies and train local staff proved very successful. However, service provision remains challenged by fighting and displacement, and it emerges as essential giving continuity to activities until full stabilization of the situation. To ensure a coordinated and lifesaving response UPP continues to support a work focused on improving standards of Reproductive Health through supporting and building the capacity of network of health staff that have been working with high results for more than a year.
The intervention is composite: on the one hand, mobile delivery units are particularly relevant in the current context of changing environment (as a piece of the health system and associated with a strong referral system), especially to provide integrated SRH/GBV services for displaced communities in remote villages and informal settlements. However, secondary care emerges as main need now that 385,000 people have gone back to Raqqah City and Deir Ezzor Governorate. Management of intrapartum complications and performing C-sections in a safe environment constitute crucial needs in context where surgical interventions are operated almost exclusively by private facilities at prices absolutely unaffordable for the majority of people. The key activities will include provision of essential SRH services, including Basic and Comprehensive EmONC, to 5 National hospitals, 1 PHC, and 1 mobile delivery unit. Furthermore, UPP will support the provision of essential RH equipment, medicine and supplies. UPP will also aim to strengthen existing referral mechanisms at health district level and coordinate standardization of services through continued development and validation of protocols and guidelines.
The project extends across 3 governorates and targets 115,000 beneficiaries with service delivery during a year:
100,000 Women
15,000 Girls (<18)
Selection criteria for beneficiaries will include who lives in the catchment area. Considering the nature of SRH services, UPP focuses on women and girls of reproductive age who represent about 25% of the population, and are considered among the most vulnerable by the health sector in Syria HNO 2019. SRH services are open to all women and girls, men and boys who are in need. Through EmONC, the essential neonatal services will also contribute to save the lives of newborns. UPP and partners will work with community mobilizers to increase awareness of services. Health care providers will be direct beneficiaries for most trainings, including basic and comprehensive EmONC trainings for midwives gradually conducted in UPP supported facilities across 3 governorates of North East Syria. The action is tailored to maximize delivery of SRH services to the beneficiaries living in the targeted governorates, who have limited resources, experience barriers to access health facilities due to security and cultural issues, and experience protection risks identified in the needs assessment. The action will also focus on responding to newly displaced people and returnees. Therefore, the importance of flexible response and coordination of humanitarian actors on the ground to reach the population is essential.

Needs

Syria is facing a multi-faceted humanitarian crisis, resulting from almost 8 years of conflict on multiple fronts that have taken a huge toll on the population. The consequent waves of displaced populations affect the entire country. The draft of the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2019 reports 13.2 million people in need in Syria. According to the Humanitarian Needs Assessment Programme in Ar Raqqah and in Deir Ez Zor Governorates around 70% and 60% of the population respectively is in need of health services. In Aleppo and Hassake Governorates the situation is slightly better, but still more than half of health facilities are destroyed and the majority in place are not-functioning or running at very low capacity. Among the people in need, 6,7 million are female and 1,4 million are children up to 5 years old. The need of basic services, such as health care, WASH, shelter, and protection services deeply affects the most vulnerable groups, particularly pregnant women and children. In a post-war context, as it happens in Hassakeh, Ar Raqqah, Deir Ez Zor and Aleppo governorates, health risk increases within most vulnerable groups. Inadequate nutrition of newborns and children under five and the spreading of infectious diseases due to poor sanitation and scant water sources are seen by health observers as factors that put at severe risk pregnant women and infants. This situation affects more intensively villages in remote areas and communities of internally displaced population. This worrisome situation needs to be confronted urgently with a comprehensive program of high quality reproductive health services delivery.

Implementation of activities

In 2018, UPP activated 5 BEmONC (3 of these are mobile units) and 3 CEmONC facilities and trained more than 100 local staff, now specialized in maternal and young child care. The hospitals supported are now able to attend pregnant women with CEmONC capacity, standing out as the only public health facilities equipped to offer services for complicate delivery. At present, these facilities attend an average 7,000 consultation per month, allowing an average of 1000 safe deliveries assisted by skilled health personnel per month. In 2019, UPP will continue its support focusing mainly on secondary health care. Activities will therefore entail the activation of a new CEmONC facility the extension of support to another CEmONC site already working at low capacity. The project will continue boosting surgical capacity and medical equipment in all five hospitals supported. The project will also continue to support one mobile delivery unit with BEmONC capacity that will serve the rural areas north and north west of Ath Tawrah (Raqqah Gov.) where HNO 2019 identified around 50,000 PIN. The mobile unit will work together with an ambulance for emergency referrals and will also target Twahina informal settlement and, in case it will established, Mahmoudie camp. To inform the strategy with a long term sustainable approach and to boost quality of care 100 health staff among which gynecologists, midwives, nurses, general surgeons and pediatricians will be gradually trained by UPP medical advisors on antenatal, postnatal and perinatal care. Moreover, protocols in line with WHO guidelines have been developed and health staff will be required to abide to such standards.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The assumption behind the Action is that a stable situation allows the Hospitals to be operative 24h/day. The emergency system has been built based on this primary assumption and proved to be effective in ensuring safe reproductive health services in NES. However, operational capacity of health facilities and safe spaces depend not only on the NES Forum sufficient level of information sharing / coordination capacity, but also on the general geopolitical situation. The main risks are the following:- Escalation of violence between SDF and GoT and new displacement waves (high risk);- Start of violence between GoS and SDF (medium risk);- Withdrawal of US/Coalition Forces may lead to destabilization in NES (high risk);- IS is not defeated and takes back some areas or infiltrates carrying out attacks (medium risk);- Use of chemical weapons (medium risk);- Lack of access and difficulties to provide services and supplies; difficulty to monitor the activities in case of remote management (medium risk);- Cross-border INGOs relocate staff further west and therefore are more limited in their capacity to respond while active hostilities are ongoing (medium risk);- Hospitals and project staff become a target in the conflict (medium risk);- Demining operations are stopped in Raqqa and DEZ (medium risk). Mitigation strategy consists in setting up or keeping extremely flexible the response and activating mobile units in order to move them according to emerging war scenarios. Equipment and staff may be deployed in other locations within few days (Raqqa city, Raqqa Governorate, Aleppo Governorate, Hassakeh Governorate, Deir-ez-Zor Governorate, other IDPs and refugees camps in NES) according to needs and if the scenario changes. Particular attention will be given to the commodity disposal, especially the ones included in the cold chain. Local partner is able to shift its support to facilities where is needed or use mobile response to follow population needs. In case of a serious deterioration of the situation in NES and of complete lack of accessibility for international staff, depending on the context, UPP will choose between a mixed strategy of direct and remote management, maintaining its field office with national/local staff under the supervision of senior management responsible for the Action from Dohuk office in KRI. In case UPP is obliged to close its Syria mission, the Action will be remotely managed by UPP from Dohuk office in KRI, and the activities will be carried out by the implementing partner.
