Project Summary

UNICEF WASH core pipeline supplies project will reach 1,400,000 beneficiaries through provision to people (IDPs and communities affected by other emergencies) with immediate WASH needs through planning, procurement, prepositioning, distribution, and management of critical WASH supplies. The project will contribute to Cluster objectives 1, 2 and 3 through provision water treatment chemicals, spare parts for hand pump rehabilitation/repair, latrine slabs (squatting pans), plastic tarpaulins for latrine construction, latrine digging kits, hygiene kits (jerry-cans, buckets, soap, menstrual hygiene health materials, and dignity items). UNICEF will procure the WASH supplies and in coordination with the WASH Cluster pre-position them in strategic locations throughout South Sudan. Supplies will be released to partners through the approval of the WASH Cluster, and the receiving partner will be responsible for distribution of the supplies. The WASH supplies will be accessible to all WASH partners currently operational in South Sudan through WASH Cluster structures at national and state levels. The project will distribute WASH supplies as per Sphere and South Sudan WASH Cluster standards and in coordination with other clusters (Health, and Nutrition). Through this project UNICEF will focus on delivering rapid assistance in all counties and population affected by a sudden or rapid-onset disaster and targeting at county level will be done through WASH Cluster updates or using various assessment findings (IRNAs, NAWG recommendations, WASH severity classification). Follow-up monitoring and capacity building of partners will be conducted throughout the project period to ensure proper management of supplies at various warehouses. UNICEF in coordination with beneficiary partners will conduct end user monitoring of the supplies to ensure that supplies distributed are reaching the vulnerable communities and are being used properly.

1 - Needs

Poor access to clean water, hygiene, and sanitation services combined with high levels of food insecurity compromises people’s health and nutrition conditions across South Sudan and contributes to one of the highest child mortality rates in the world. Lack of access to water has led to tension and conflict between communities over competition of already scarce resources. The highest proportion of households relying on surface water are in Upper Nile, followed by Jonglei and Central Equatoria. Every fifth person is unable to collect enough water for drinking, and every third reports insufficient access to water for handwashing. Poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continue to have a detrimental impact on the health of children, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases. Counties reporting high GAM rates have also high WASH needs and are prioritized by the WASHCluster for multi-sectoral response for famine prevention. Mothers and caretakers of malnourished children lack basic hygiene knowledge and access to safe water at domiciles, jeopardizing the benefits gained in nutrition programs. Additionally, continued insecurity, disease outbreaks, nutrition crises and other natural emergencies combined with economic crises and poor infrastructure throughout the country highlight the need for UNICEF and humanitarian partners to continue providing emergency services to vulnerablecommunities. UNICEF is the Cluster lead for WASH and manages part of the core pipeline supplies on behalf of the cluster partners. UNICEF will continue to work in coordination with Nutrition and Health Clusters in delivering the services. Targeting at county level will be conducted by partners through assessments.
