Project Summary

The project is focused on assessment (desk study) and provision of specialized psycho-social support services by mobile teams to elderly GBV/DV survivors.
At the first phase the project envisages conducting a study of households in isolated settlements in government-controlled areas (GCA), which will address gaps related to the three health inclusion areas: Data, Health access barriers and Participation.
The research findings will be used to form the activities of 5 new mobile teams of psycho-social support (PSS MTs) in Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts to cater specifically to the needs of the disabled and elderly survivors of GBV/DV. The teams will consist of 2 social workers and 1 psychologist and will conduct outreach to isolated settlements near conflict hotspots to provide psychological and social services and referrals, simultaneously collecting empirical information on the special needs of this category of beneficiaries. The information collected in the course of the study and MTs operation will be used to develop advocacy agendas to form state policy and interventions to address the immediate needs of such vulnerable and underserved populations.

1 - Needs

The protracted conflict in Eastern Ukraine has deeply affected the security, economic stability and health of older persons. The elderly, 70% of whom are women, account for 30% of the 3.4 million persons in need of humanitarian assistance (UNOCHA, 2017). Before, during and after displacement, older people are uniquely vulnerable to violence (VOICE, 2019). One in 5 IDPs have experienced at least one form of violence, with women accounting for 76% of cases (UNOCHA, 2019). Our preliminary analysis of UNFPA Ukraine’s GBV-IMS data found that economic violence was more prevalent among older adults than among the overall sample [21% vs. 15%, p<0.001]. Nearly half [49%] of the violence against older people was perpetrated by family members. Only 4% of older IDPs were referred by police, compared to 22% locals. Breakdown of legal, medical and social services systems, as well as of informal support networks (Wessells & Monteiro, 2004) in humanitarian settings leads to isolation and poses barriers to care for the elderly. This is compounded by IDP households’ dire financial situation and limited psychosocial services for GBV survivors (UCSR, 2015). While in the midst of health reform and devolution, this is a critical time to document the needs of vulnerable populations in Ukraine. Thus, we seek to understand the types of GBV faced by older people (60+), gaps in referrals and barriers to care by gender, disability, and displacement status.
