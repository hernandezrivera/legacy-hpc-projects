Project Summary

Conflict is heavily impacting women, children, persons with special needs and other vulnerable populations. The intensive fighting between SAF and RSF since 15 April 2023, which started in Khartoum and spread throughout the country has entered the third month and has also triggered ethnic-based violence in Darfur, where hundreds killed and many injured, women and girls subjected to sexual violence and other forms of violence, and putting children, persons with special needs and other vulnerable groups to further protection risks. At least 24.7 million people in need of assistance. 1.9 million are internally displaced, according to OCHA’s situation report, as of 22 June 2023. There are 117,360 IDPs in Al Jazirah, 15,997 in Blue Nile, 98,930 in Central Darfur, 298,220 in West Darfur, and 19, 523 are displaced in South Kordofan, according to IOM’s DTM report.
This project is designed to achieve the protection sectoral SO1: Ensure the provision of critical protection-related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas. GBV Sub sectoral Objective S01. Provide life-saving specialized quality GBV services, including CMR, case management, and psychosocial support, and reinforce referral pathways. (with a focus on women and girls with disabilities and adolescent girls). CP Sub-sectoral Objective 1 Acutely vulnerable children and their caregivers are outreached, and their needs are addressed through specialized child protection interventions including multi-sectoral case management services.
The project aims to enhance the capacity of the community to respond to the need of vulnerable groups like women and unaccompanied and separated children, and people with special needs. In addition, WR will support the safety and psychosocial support for women, children, girls, and boys who are affected by conflict through GBV, general protection, and child protection programs in West Darfur, Central Darfur, the Blue Nile South Kordofan, and Khartoum. The project targets 153,461 people including 34049 Men, 54696 Women, 33229 Girls, 26108 Boys, and 5379 Elders.
WR recognizes the importance of the human rights of refugees, returnees, IDPs, and especially the most vulnerable groups such as children, women, girls, and minority groups. WR will build relationships with IDP and IDP returnee communities with the goal of enabling them to identify and respond to their own protection needs through the establishment and support of community-based protection structures and the implementation of community support projects. Through these community-based and other key informants, WR will engage in protection monitoring to support the identification of protection risks, human rights violations, and gaps in available service provision. And, respond to acute and immediate protection risks and needs through the provision of legal assistance, cash assistance, identification of persons with specific needs, and referral to specialized services and mental health and psychosocial support services provided to adults. In addition, WR will provide life-saving specialized quality GBV services, including CMR, case management, and psychosocial support, and reinforce referral pathways. The child protection program aims to provide to girls and boys with specialized child protection response services, including case management for child survivors of sexual violence, child labor, child recruitment, UASCs, and children in need of alternative care. While implementing this project, the WR will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations that expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence, and abuse of their rights. WR will closely collaborate and coordinate with the protection cluster performing all the tasks required by ensuring a protection and people center approach to response and advocacy.

1 - Needs

According to OCHA Sudan's periodic monitoring Report (January - June 2022), about 80 percent of localities in Sudan lack specialized GBV services such as clinical management of rape (CMR), Psycho-social support (PSS), and GBV case management. Besides, limited access to civil documentation creates barriers to accessing basic services (42 percent), land and property rights (34 percent), and restrictions on freedom of movement (23 percent). 45 percent of respondents reported their communities to have problems with social tension, with 40 per cent reporting they have witnessed and experienced violence. 71 percent of community-based protection networks (CBPNs) reported challenges accessing livelihoods and 64 per cent accessing food, prompting child labor and child marriage as a coping mechanism. 56 percent of CPBNs reported that people with specific needs have difficulty accessing assistance and services. Secondary and new displacement impacted the mental and physical well-being of the affected people, and the psycho-social support beyond the GBV response is a key gap. There are limited general protection actors across the country, particularly in the Blue Nile. Case management, legal assistance, profiling and referral of PSNs, and emergency protection cash-based assistance are virtually absent. According to the UNICEF Sudan child protection report 2021, the political crisis after the 25 October 2021 military takeover is further diminishing the already existing services. The current economic crisis presents a significant challenge, as more children are in need, but resources are limited. There are significant disparities in service coverage remained a key challenge to narrowing equity gaps in (previously) inaccessible areas in the Blue Nile, Darfur (Jebel Marra) and Kordofan state. The number of functional CBPNs and Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) offering GBV services are limited, affecting the accessibility to information and services. The situation of children affected by conflict in Sudan continues to be a concern, with the most worrying incidents being killing, maiming, and sexual violence against children. The number of verified child rights violations steadily increased in the first two months after the military coup in October 2021 and has remained at the same level during the reporting period. The ongoing civil unrest has exacerbated the grave violations of child rights. There is a lack of implementing partners and funding for emergency Child Protection activities. The lack of community-based child protection networks in conflict-affected areas hampers immediate emergency child protection response, including monitoring and service delivery. Accordingly, to UNHCR, Darfur community level rapid protection assessment report June 2022, 52% of KII report that women and girls feel unsafe in their communities and 46% that men and boys feel unsafe; 36% report incidents of GBV occur in their communities. The deterioration of the security situation in some states has exacerbated the risks of GBV for women, girls and other vulnerable people whose movement and access to livelihood activities, health, and social services have been restricted. Lack of privacy and limited essential services such as water, health, shelter, and food remains a challenge for displaced women and girls. There are risks of harassment and violent attacks against women and girls while collecting firewood, fetching water, and working in agricultural and livelihood activities. The project shall focus on the protection of the most vulnerable groups, in particular women and girls, and support local authorities in establishing a long-term protective environment. Up to 60 percent of the population in need of protection services in Sudan are children who continue to be exposed to protection threats of recruitment, family separation, abuse, neglect, abduction, exploitation, and violence. World relief targets areas where there is high need for protection services.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

WR protection work aims at taking action to keep people safe from violence, coercion, and deliberate deprivation. WR will take steps to understand and mitigate associated risks. Protection of affected people in the WR protection program is guided by the following four commonly agreed-upon principles:1. Enhance the safety, dignity, and rights of people, and avoid exposing them to harm.2. Ensure people’s access to assistance according to need and without discrimination.3. Assist people to recover from the physical and psychological effects of threatened or actual violence, coercion, and deliberate deprivation.4. Help people claim their rightsWR protection program encompasses the environment in its objective to avoid exposing people to harm. WR can have intended impacts on the environment that lead to people’s safety, dignity, and rights being compromised, whether from an individual or group perspective.For example Environmental impacts:The community may have low self-sufficiency as a result of environmental degradation. This could include the vulnerability of socially excluded groups such as people with disability and other vulnerable groups, which may find their access to scarce resources like clean water or energy compromised. Deforestation caused by an increased need for shelter material or overuse of water resources could result in women and children having to travel further to collect firewood or water, resulting in a higher risk of exposure for these vulnerable groups. Discriminatory social norms may lead to certain groups not having access to information or being excluded from decision-making processes, which in turn may compromise their access to environmental services such as water.Dignity – forcing people to move or to take unsafe and/or culturally inappropriate lodging due to environmental stresses.Rights – people have a right to clean water and an adequate standard of living, where environmental stresses may compromise these rights.The degree of protection for affected people is directly related to the extent humanitarian actors are able to address the above-mentioned key concerns in programming and implementation. If these links are overseen, patterns might be reinforced (e.g.increased use of fuelwood and water) which not only increase environmental stresses but also increase the risk to the population. Consequently, WR will do a thorough analysis of the links between environmental factors and the protection is needed

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will be at the center of the response, and all assistance will be planned and implemented so as to promote the safety, dignity, and rights of affected people. The Child-friendly space and the women-friendly spaces will be guided by the safety audit. In addition, the community will be consulted in the establishment of the center and later provide feedback on the ways the centers are safe for the community. WR will consult the Protection Cluster within in the specific area of intervention to have access to the Protection Analysis which identifies threats, vulnerable groups, and existing capacities.In addition, training will be provided to the child protection, women support committees and WR protection staff on protection mainstreaming. To create a proper understanding of identified threats, the most vulnerable groups, and the existing capacities of the affected populations as well as services and resources already available. This will help us to identify real and potential risks that may arise in protection programs. Will confirm the threats, vulnerabilities, and negative coping strategies identified in the Protection Analysis Report by consulting with the affected population of each area of intervention. In addition, through analysis, WR identifies with beneficiary’s preventive and mitigation measures with the objective of reducing the threats and the vulnerabilities and increasing the existing capacities. In addition, the community through the community-based protection structures will have full participation in the designing and implementation of the program. Furthermore, the community will have full participation including a decision-making role in the program. AGD approach will be used to ensure the inclusiveness of the program to all of the community in the area. Active and effective participation of affected people both in planning, response, and monitoring processes will be ensured through community discussion. WR will promote peace by ensuring that all community groups including IDPs, host community members, returnees, pastoralists, and varying tribal and ethnic groups have equal access to project services by selecting project sites in areas accessible to the different groups and hiring diverse staff. This minimizes the risk of conflict due to competition over social services. The possibility of discrimination by locally hired staff against beneficiaries who do not hold the same tribal or political affiliations is mitigated by ensuring that program teams that consist of combinations of locally hired national staff representing all ethnic groups, national staff hired from outside the programmatic area, and international staff.
