Project Summary

The proposed project seeks to provide urgent protection assistance to an estimated 2130 vulnerable conflict-affected civilians of Eastern Ukraine, on both sides of the contact line. In addition, the project aims at raising awareness of population in NGCA on Gender-based violence (GBV) through GBV awareness raising activities (7000 individuals).
The overall objective of the proposed project is to mitigate the negative effects of the protracted conflict by reinforcing the protection system along the contact line for the benefit of the affected people.
The specific objective of the project is to reduce vulnerability of conflict-affected population through enhanced local capacities, awareness raising and improved access to protection services.
The defined project objectives will be achieved through the following expected outcomes:
Under Outcome 1 “Vulnerable affected population in GCA benefit from improved access to protection assistance and home-based care”, 110 community members will be trained on social work, protection mainstreaming and basic psychosocial (PSS) concepts to provide home-based care and basic PSS to vulnerable 500 conflict-affected women and men above 60 and persons with disabilities not targeted by the government social protection scheme. The action will establish an informal network of community-based social workers with the aim of ensuring access to protection and basic community-based PSS to vulnerable individuals residing along the contact line. TGH will employ peer-to-peer methodology that proved to be effective in former TGH projects where vulnerable community members were engaged to support other community members with specific needs. The selected approach will also contribute to strengthening community cohesion and solidarity.
Under Outcome 2 “Protection concerns of at-risk women and girls, survivors of Gender-based violence (GBV) in Donetsk Oblast, NGCA” are addressed and risk factors reduced through provision of tailored protection assistance and awareness-raising: capacity of 20 medical staff will be built in GBV identification and prevention as well as in provision of psychological support to victims of GBV. GBV awareness activities will be conducted in cooperation with health care facilities with which Variant, TGH local partner based in Donetsk, has long-term standing partnership and cooperation. Focus group sessions with men practicing aggressive behavior including among ex-combatant staff and persons in detention facilities displaying high levels of aggressiveness will be conducted by Variant psychologists and social workers. Assistance will be provided by the qualified project team comprised of psychologists and social workers for GBV survivors and children affected by family-based violence.
Under Outcome 3: “Access to protection services of the most vulnerable conflict-affected women and men, boys and girls in Donetsk Oblast NGCA is ensured through targeted protection assistance including PSS and documentation support”, PSS support will be provided by social workers and/or psychologists depending on individual beneficiaries’ needs. Furthermore, support will be provided to beneficiaries in need of legal documentation to confirm their disability or vulnerability status or identity documents to ensure access to social benefits and services. Support will be provided in the form of individual consultations and accompaniment to relevant institutions as required.
Activities under Outcomes 2 and 3 will be implemented jointly with TGH partner, Variant, based in Donetsk, who has relevant registration and permission to operate in NGCA. TGH and Variant have been working jointly since 2018 and the local organisation proved effective cooperation and proper project implementation.
Outcome 4. Accountability to affected populations is enhanced.

1 - Needs

In 2021 the conflict in eastern Ukraine will enter into its seventh year however the affected population along the contact line still faces physical dangers as well as lack of access to basic services and essential goods. The COVID-19 crisis represents an additional burden deteriorating the already fragile context. With the high proportion of elderly population, population living with chronic diseases such as multidrug-resistant tuberculosis, cardiovascular diseases, diabetes and human immunodeficiency viruses, Ukraine is particularly vulnerable to the COVID-19 crisis (REACH, KAP, GCA, August 2020). Near the contact line civilians continue facing physical insecurity in spite of a relative calm allowed by the ceasefire in place since 27 July 2020. Negative effects of the conflict and the COVID-19 pandemic, no adequate PSS support and insufficiency of PSS services have direct and significant PSS implications on personal health. An increase of at least 50% of cases of Gender-based Violence (GBV) has been reported as per the preliminary Humanitarian Needs Analysis 2021 (September 2020). Besides, access to healthcare and social services has been also negatively affected by the pandemic. Further, the onset of the COVID-19-pandemic and related restrictions has had dramatic consequences on the economic situation of the majority of households living along the contact line. The increased unemployment rate (9% nationwide, circa 14% in the east), the substantial rate of informal employment (circa 30%) and limited social safety nets, along with the rising prices for basic needs items have had worrisome consequences on the population (Preliminary Humanitarian Needs Analysis, 2021, September 2020).GCA: Elderly and persons with disabilities living alone without family support are in dire need for home base care assistance. Ukraine has a large proportion of elderly (30% of PIN, HRP 2020) and/or persons with disabilities (circa 14% of PIN, Preliminary Humanitarian Needs Analysis, 2021). According to the findings of an assessment from HelpAge International (Donetsk, Luhansk, GCA, May 2020), 48.37% (81.59% women) of surveyed older people were living alone. 86.97% (64.20% women) had limited mobility and partly needed help of others, 2.04% were immobile. According to TGH PDM conducted in September 2020 in GCA, 58% of interviewed elderly and persons with disabilities requiring external support did not receive required assistance, 14% received aid from relatives, 20% received support from friends/neighbours, 1% from other organisations and 6% mentioned other specific answers (mainly community volunteer or relatives support). 98% of TGH beneficiaries expressed the necessity to continue receiving home-based care support during the harsh winter months.NGCA: The protracted crisis and posttraumatic stress disorders among population force many to resort to negative coping mechanisms. Use of psychoactive substances and alcohol has drastically increased, predominantly by men leading to aggressive behaviours, gender-based and sexual violence. According context analysis conducted by Variant, GBV awareness is low in NGCA and GBV cases are not properly detected and addressed therefore enhancing capacity of service providers in identification of GBV cases is required. Further, the analysis demonstrates high level of psychological distress caused by psycho-traumatic events as a result of protracted conflict in Ukraine; long-term depression caused by lack of income and means. Besides, among the key protection concerns in NGCA remain challenges related to the receipt and restoration of documents and access to social benefits. The needs assessment survey carried out by TGH and Variant in 12 locations of Donetsk oblast, NGCA (February 2020), the major challenges faced by the communities were: high prices for commodities (28.3%), severe emotional state (17.9%), low salary (17.3%), lack of paid work opportunities (10.9%) and no access to social services (7.4%).

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome 1: Vulnerable affected population in GCA benefit from improved access to protection assistance and home-based care.Activities: 1. Identification of community-based volunteers through field visits. 2. Capacity building workshops for 110 community-based volunteers on general principles of social support, PSS, protection mainstreaming, individual case management, specific accompaniment of elderly and persons with disabilities; types of home-based services; referrals to specialised services, Do No Harm, and conflict sensitivity. 3. Identification of project beneficiaries affected by the conflict, not included in the government social protection schemes and their needs assessment. 4. Provision of home-based care and PSS. 5. Monitoring and post-distribution monitoring activities. Outcome 2: Protection concerns of at-risk women and girls, survivors of Gender-based violence (GBV) in Donetsk Oblast, NGCA are addressed and risk factors reduced through provision of tailored protection assistance and awareness-raising.Activities: 1. Capacity building of medical/social protection staff in identification of GBV cases and provision of psychological support through series of training. 2. Development and distribution of GBV awareness information materials to personnel in 20 medical institutions. Referrals to multidisciplinary project support and/or relevant service providers.3. Multidisciplinary care for GBV survivors and children affected by family-based violence provided by qualified project team comprised of medical specialists, psychologists, and social workers.4. Individual and group PSS counselling.5. Focus group sessions and individual consultations for men practicing aggressive behaviour.6. PDM and project monitoring visits based on the Project Monitoring Plan. Outcome 3: Access to protection services of the most vulnerable conflict-affected women and men, boys and girls in Donetsk Oblast NGCA is ensured through targeted protection assistance including PSS and documentation support.Activities: 1. Identification of beneficiaries. 2. Forming individual cases for each beneficiary and development of action plans. 3. PSS consultations by Variant social workers and/or psychologists depending on individual case management plans corresponding to the PSS and protection needs, psychological reactions of beneficiaries in accordance with the pyramid of mental health and PSS needs. 4. Weekly PSS individual and group sessions. 5. Individual consultations on documentation support and accompaniment to relevant institutions. 6. PDM and project monitoring visits based on the Project Monitoring Plan. Outcome 4: Accountability to affected populations is enhanced. Activity 1: Project monitoring and learning. Activity 2. Establishing a project CRM to ensure feedback from project beneficiaries and other stakeholders as part of the overall TGH CRM. Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA): TGH has a Safeguarding Policy for the PSEA with the appropriate tools to satisfy the standards defined by the UN. TGH has been assessed as a low risk partner by UNICEF HQ in NYC (cf. assessment available in the UNPP website). TGH employees and operational partners are trained on PSEA. Beneficiaries will also be sensitized and informed on the mechanisms for reporting complaints. COVID-19 preventive measures:Throughout the project, the respect of social distancing measures, wearing of face masks and restrictions on mass gatherings will be followed strictly (COVID-19 Ukraine Assistance Delivery Protocol).
