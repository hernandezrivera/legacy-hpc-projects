Project Summary

As the crisis in Syria continues into its 13th year, families across the country are facing unprecedented levels of food insecurity. More Syrians are struggling to put food on their tables today than ever before. The situation is expected to worsen throughout 2024, with 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. Hikes in food prices, the fuel crisis, ongoing inflation, increase in commodity prices, continuing conflict in some parts of the country, adverse climatic conditions and the February 2023 earthquakes have all taken a devastating toll on Syria’s most vulnerable and severely damaged infrastructure already weakened by conflict. This has resulted in an increase in poverty and continued reliance on humanitarian assistance.
Moreover, active armed clashes and aerial bombardment were regularly reported around frontline areas causing civilian casualties and displacement. In October 2023, airstrikes and shelling in Idleb Governorate led to the largest outburst of violence in north-west Syria since 2019. Tensions in northern Aleppo continued throughout the year, with Afrin, A’zaz, Al-Bab sub-districts being the mostly impacted. The humanitarian catastrophe worsened only further by multiple earthquakes in February 2023 Within this context, malnutrition is rising with every year in the Northwest of Syria. The prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM), among children under the age of 5 ranges from 4.9% to 6.5% in 2023 according to HNO 2024 data, at the “serious” threshold as identified by WHO, a clear indication of the weakened nutrition fabric in those locations. Stunting rates have consistently risen across Syria, increasing from 12.6% in 2019 to 16.1% in 2023. Alarmingly, 5 out of 14 Governorates reported unacceptably high stunting rates exceeding 20%, including Northern Aleppo and Idleb in the NWS. Maternal malnutrition, on the other hand, remains a prevailing concern, with prevalence of acute malnutrition reaching 10% in most areas across the Northwest of Syria excluding very few areas in Idleb where the maternal malnutrition rate is 5.5%.
ATAA will respond to the most vulnerable families by providing cash assistance through MPCA to the population for eight rounds each beneficiary will receive 100 USD per round in Aleppo/ Al-Bab, Atareb and Jarablous subdistricts, and in Idleb/ Dana, Salqin sub-districts.
enhancing the resilience of people in NWS socio-economically vulnerable households by responding with eight rounds of Multi-Sectoral Cash Assistance (MPCA), to support them in addressing their unmet sectorial needs for recovery.
Key interventions will include:
- 1500 vouchers (100$) in Atareb sub-district, 780 vouchers (100$) in Al-Bab, 1612 vouchers (100$) in Jarablous, 890 vouchers (100$) in Salqin, 3700 vouchers (100$) in Dana sub-districts for 11 months (1 month for signing protocols, hiring staff and preparing the procurement life cycle, one month before the distribution to choose the BNFs and conduct baseline, 8 months distribution, and 1 for Endline) 8 rounds in line with CWG recommendation including needs and markets assessment in preparedness phase.
the project team will use harmonized PDM tool to monitor project activities.
ATAA will identify, verify, and profile target beneficiaries through local councils according to the vulnerability selection criteria:
• Female/Widow headed households,
• Child-headed households (head of HH must be less than 18 years old),
• Households headed by someone with a disability (inability to work) or weakness,
• Households with two or more members with a disability (mental and/or physical),
• Elderly-headed households (over 60 years old)
• Household with more than 5 children,
• Families without a breadwinner,
• Families with pregnant and lactating women,
• HHs with low or no income to survive and lack access to aid and lifesaving support.
The total number of HHs will be 8,482 HHs.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

ATAA employs a scientific risk management approach by developing a risk matrix during project design that considers all potential hazards and mitigation measures. As part of the management of this risk, the S&S department depends on the strategy of achieving community acceptance during all phases of projects and building a trust relationship with the local authorities by clarifying for the local authorities the impact of the project on the region. Due to the unpredictable nature of conflicts, such as shelling or armed clashes, the S&S department closely monitors the project area, conducts daily risk assessments, and communicates potential scenarios and appropriate responses to the crisis cell. In addition, the department updates evacuation and contingency plans and provides field staff with training on expected scenarios and adherence to approved SOPs for a secure operational environment.ATAA is considering the PSEA in the top of priorities by active membership and participation in PSEA networks activities, and developed internal framework and control system to enhance PSEA including all the core standards, in addition to include the prevention measures in all the humanitarian interventions of ATAA.Additionally, ATAA created a solid response system starts from the active and accessible reporting channels, and investigation planning and procedures with qualified and trained staff and network of the focal points with gender balance to enhance access to survivors and provide the response interventions based on survivor based approach.Ataa is updating PSEA assessments with partners and received a UNICEF assessment result as full capacity low risk partner since mid-2023.
