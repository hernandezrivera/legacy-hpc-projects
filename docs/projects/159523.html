Project Summary

The nutrition situation in Somalia has for a very long period of time reported indicators above emergency threshold. Despite slight improvement many still face acute food insecurity, rampant disease, poor WASH factors and poor care practices for pregnant and lactating women as well as children under 5 years. This underscores the need for continued humanitarian assistance and protection through 2020. The situation is not any different in target location. More than half the population in these areas are expected to experience deterioration of their food security state from Stressed (IPC Phase 2) to Crisis IPC Phase 3, mainly riven by the impacts of below-average rains and poor access to food due to control of highways by insurgents. WASH status and care practices remain poor due to poor access to water which also impedes on the sanitation situation. These two factors then act as fuel for rampant disease outbreak coupled with poor primary prevention of disease, further compounding the nutrition situation.
According to the nutritional assessment from FSNAU 2019 the nutrition situation is above emergency threshold with some aggravating factors.. The median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has remained Serious over the past three seasons but has now deteriorated according to 2019 Post Gu assessment. The Previously experienced drought in 2017 and the following below average rains, have their impact is still felt to date led.
The two districts also continue to have one of the worst infant and young child feeding and micro-nutrient indicators in the country (HARD 2019; IPC 2019). Disease outbreak including AWD/Cholera and measles are commonly reported (Health cluster ,2019). In addition, the number of protracted IDPs in the two districts continue to increase due to insecurity caused by presence of insurgents in part of the districts and in neighboring districts. IDPs represent a huge growing number of the people in acute livelihood and food crisis in these districts. The most vulnerable in these districts, including children under 5(boys and girls) and pregnant and lactating therefore need urgent intervention.
The proposed interventions will align with cluster HRP objectives and Government policies and strategies to providing lifesaving interventions in the target districts, especially for those affected by acute food insecurity and disease outbreaks. Interventions will focus on providing adequate nutritional support to bring up and avoid further deterioration of already existing situation. To achieve this and realize sustainability, HARD will work closely with government entities, both at local and federal level including MOH at all levels. The organisation will also intensify community action in order to include affected population in interventions affecting them. Interventions will also integrate with those of health WASH, Food security and protection. Serious protection issues observed in these districts will be addressed working with the protection cluster and any protection risks likely to emanate from projects will be mitigated before projects begin through the appropriate forum that the organisation will create and engage in.
Protection will be mainstreamed through the project. All cases with protection issues will be referred to appropriate partner for assistance. Priority will be given to the disabled in all treatment sites. All disabled will also be given special staff who are trained to work with them with patience. They will also be followed up by CHW.

Mitigation Measures

HARD has gender policy that ensure that when hiring staff that women are given 50%consideration in different positions. In addition, the project target populations aresegregated into men, women, boys and girls hence giving emphasis to all gendergroups in the community. During the screening and admission of children into nutritionprograms, boys and girls will be given equal chances. Gender considerations will betaken into account throughout the assessment, planning and design of theinterventions and significant consideration will be given to pregnant women, mothers,and women of child-bearing age. In the provision of health and nutrition promotioncampaigns, messaging will be targeted for specific gender and age groups and asappropriate the events will be organized separately. Equitable and gender-sensitiveparticipation and decision-making approaches will be adopted throughout the project,including women’s participation in the community nutrition committees and access togender-specific health and nutrition information. Community consultations throughfocus-group discussions will take place during the project implementation, andstakeholders will be involved in decision-making processes to tailor the project to theneeds of different gender and age groups. Furthermore, data will be dis-aggregated byage and gender in all recording and reporting.
