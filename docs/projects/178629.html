Project Summary

The project is designed to restoring basic infrastructure to facilitate easier access to markets and the main facilities inside the surrounded communities for most vulnerable IDPs who exposed to flood and winter impacts, 18,628HHs, 113,964 Individuals (80%IDPs, 20% Host) (25,814 Men, 27,173 Women, 28,971 Boys, 32,006 Girls ), provide short income-generating job opportunities for 314(258M,56F) and supporting social cohesion between the IDPs in the targeted camps and HHs of neighboring villages in the targeted location in Aleppo and Idleb governorates (that the total length of the rehabilitated roads will be 35 km in both governorates).
Under Early Recovery, the project has three main interventions:
The first intervention: improve camps’ communities’ access to markets for 113,964 (54,785 M, 59,179 F) in the targeted location in Aleppo and Idleb governorates. subdistrict through heavy rehabilitation including rain drainage system and culverts for main roads leading to markets (35,000 meter). Binaa will contract with specialized contractors to implement the roads according to detailed technical plan and will implement the drainage and rain tunnel through CFW modality and all the works will be under BINAA engineers follow up and supervision.
The second intervention: Initiatives for adorn and improve targeted road which facilitate people's access to markets through supporting 314workers (258M and 56F, 11% PwD) in accordance with the CFW modality for 100 working days in cleaning, rubble removal, roads' painting, raining's water channels paving (with the stone that will be broken and shaped in nearby lands to the targeted camps, this will improve the lands which stones are taken from as it will reform the lands and make it more suitable for agricultural activities, and will strengthen the social integration between the host community" landlords of surrounded lands" and IDPs), installation of road signs', road bumps establishment and planting forest trees on the right and left of the road at the main entrances of targeted camps. The participation of males, females and disabilities in designing the activity was considered by allocating light activities that do not require high physical capacity for women and disabilities and by providing some special tools that facilitate the work of these groups, each one in a way that suits his need.
The Third intervention: BINAA will communicate with representatives of surrounding communities and camps management and held light trainings on the communication and assets management for camps' management committees and representatives of the neighboring villages. Monthly consultation sessions will be conducted to discuss the project progress, impacts, lessons learnt and their recommendation which will contribute to enhance the interventions, in addition to carrying out an awareness campaign to the targeted beneficiaries which aim to raise awareness about COVID-19, improving knowledge about access to health and protection centers and reporting mechanisms, and to improve social cohesion between the IDPs and HC.

4 - Needs

Based on HNO 2021, ERL needs remain considerable in scale, severity, and complexity across Syria, with an estimated 11.6 million people (51% Female, 49% Male) needing dignifying early recovery and livelihood support. Almost ten years of crisis in Syria have continued to erode the socio-economic foundations of the Syrian people, thereby affecting their ability to sustain adequate standards of living, seek decent livelihood opportunities, and access social services and essential infrastructure, in parallel to the fallout from the Lebanese financial crisis, the rapid depreciation of the Syrian currency, dramatic inflation, and the entry into effect of new trade restrictions in June 2020. Moreover, the global COVID-19 pandemic has led to additional stress on social and basic services while also depriving Syrian people of their jobs and livelihoods.According to HNO 2021,- The ERL context analysis indicate that poor economic conditions, physical destruction of infrastructure, and largescale displacement have led to competition over scarce livelihood opportunities and overburdening of available social services, resulting in an unprecedented challenge to socioeconomic resilience.- 74 per cent of the available basic infrastructures’ facilities are reported damaged, with 53 per cent of them reporting significant damage. The poor conditions of access ways and roads affected by ten years of conflict, in addition to the harm caused because of natural events such as heavy rains, negatively affect the possibility to boost the speed of local economic recovery.- The number of IDPs has increased. This has led to additional pressure on social and basic services, leading to a potential rupture in the social fabric, Health and education

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposed activities of this project are closely aligned to the Allocation Strategic Objectives of SCHF 2021 1st Standard Allocation. The first intervention which concern with heavy rehabilitation of main roads to the IDPs site is aligned with allocation strategic objective 1: Upgrading of IDP sites to ensure safe and dignified living conditions for the most vulnerable people in northwest Syria, with a focus on appropriate preventive measures to alleviate the effects of flooding and prepare for winter, through Integrated multi-sectoral response in prioritized IDPs sites and nearby host-communities according to the prioritized locations identified by the cluster. The second and third interventions which concern with securing job opportunities through CFW modality and promoting the coordination and relationships between IDPs and Host communities are aligned with allocation strategic objective 3: Enhance resilience of affected communities through provision of livelihood opportunities, through Cash for Work linked to restoration works to improve access to socio-economic or production services giving priority to floods affected areas and nearby communities.
