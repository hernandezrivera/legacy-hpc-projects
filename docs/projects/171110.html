Project Summary

As is the case with disasters, flooding heightens the risk of family separation, and causes severe distress for children due to the shock and loss of homes and disruption in daily life, as well as a potential increase in gender-based violence (GBV) due to a breakdown in community structures, law and order, and a lack of physical protection. Violence against children, both inside and outside of the home, has increased as families are in confined spaces, with limited resources and heightened stress. The reopening of schools this September offers the opportunity to provide child-friendly spaces (CFSs) that offer provide educational and psychosocial support and other activities that restore a sense of normality and continuity. However, after schools reopened mid-August, partners in Hirshabelle (middle Shabelle) reported eight (8) schools with an enrolment of 1356 (612 girls) learners have been affected by the floods.
Due to the shortage of water, firewood and food, in the IDP camps young girls and women have to walk long distances to get them. In the process, they pass through bush areas or night falls when still on their way. This exposes them to risks of sexual assaults or other GBV forms. Indicators reveal that 85% of girls and boys, men and women are victims of GBV and mostly happen within the IDPs settlements. Poor access to basic human right protection and emergency tends to pose risks of human right violation, therefore RRP with its presences in Hirshabelle region have witnessed poor protection from the rising cases of GBV, early marriages, and even FGM. The recent floods in the area rendered many home states homeless hence the cases of rape both reported and unreported were many.
RRP through this response will offer support to the protracted, recent flood and conflict affected beneficiaries to restore to their shelters hence reducing on the cases that have rendered most of the vulnerable not to get any protection concerns from rape, sexual assaults, forced marriages or to earn a living for the family among other challenges that are being faced in Hirshabelle.
Our interventions will be giving considerations to people living with disabilities, the most marginalized groups among the community with our staff ensuring that their services are taken to their proximity within the best time possible. RRP through the identified gaps will work closely with shelter partners as well as actors to ensure that the interventions are attained. The health sector will offer medical assistance to the rape and those with various assaults.
Gender will be mainstreamed into the project through the specific consideration of the different needs of women, men, boys and girls during the design of activities. The project will ensure that all participants have equal access to resources and training. Monitoring and evaluation will be based on gender, age and diversity dis-segregated data.

Project Activities

Activity 1: Enhanced the capacity of staff and local community on GBV prevention and response in Beletweyne, Bulaburte and Jalalaqsi districts in Hirshabelle region. Activity 2: Provide counselling, with referrals for medical assistance to Beletweyne hospital, combined with psycho-social and legal support to survivors in Hirshabelle. Activity 3: Strengthen community engagement by organizing monthly community awareness on GBV prevention in Hirshabelle. Activity 4: In collaboration with GBV Sub Cluster, Ministry of Health (MOH) and Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) task force, organize CMR training for community and religious elders and health care workers. Activity 5: Procure and distribute 1000 dignity kits and solar to 1000 female headed household, who are at risk of GBV or are GBV victims. Activity 6: The distribution of dignity kits to the women/girl who are at risk of GBV will contribute to the psycho-social and emotional support and healing to GVB survivors. Activity 7: Community awareness session on FGM, forced and early marriages practices targeting 300 elders, religious and gatekeepers.

Mitigation Measures

While outliving the welcome of host communities, protection of communities in conﬂict-affected areas subjected to disproportionate and indiscriminate targeting of civilians and civilian assets vital for their survival, localized conflict dynamics, consisting of inter-communal rivalries and competition, are influenced by a broader conflict between the state and allied stakeholders, and armed non-state actors. In this diverse and highly complex landscape, with a multitude of militarizes and non-state armed actors, violations of international humanitarian law (IHL) and human rights law regularly occur with impunity. Civilians bear the brunt of the ongoing conflict, through death and injury, destruction of property, taxation of communities including through forced child recruitment, land grabbing, destruction of livelihoods, limited freedom of movement and limited access to services and humanitarian assistance, and diversion of assistance. RRP will ensure that we work closely and provide women, children and people living with disability and the marginalized.Women, children and marginalized communities are especially at risk and face specific protection concerns. The protection crisis is also characterized by entrenched but fluid societal divisions, resulting in competition for resources across social groups. Three interlinked priority areas where protection is lacking in different parts of Somalia and poses a significant challenge to the entire humanitarian response have been identified: vulnerability, age, and gender. Somalia’s societal structure is highly complex, including numerous social groups, clans, sub-clans and ethnic minority groups that are not members of a specific clan. Divisions and constantly evolving relations among those groups remains one of the prevailing characteristics of the society, and a driving variable within the ongoing conflict and drought. Some groups having comparatively less social capital, resulting in reduced clan-based protection and less support, including financial, during crises. Displacement can aggravate existing inequalities, as well as introduce new dimensions of people living with disability, marginalization and exclusion. The selection and registration of the beneficiaries will be done with the consultation of local authorities, IDP committees including women by giving priority the new arrived IDPs; women headed households and households with disabilities and/or seek persons. Post distribution monitoring will be carried out to ensure that the right beneficiaries benefited from the supplies without anyone's rights being violated.
