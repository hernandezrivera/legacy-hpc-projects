Project Summary

UNHCR delivers comprehensive protection services and ensures the centrality of protection through protection and gender mainstreaming, inclusive coordination, and leadership of the humanitarian response. In 2025, UNHCR’s response will deliver integrated protection services including registration and continuous updating of records, issuance of documentation, legal assistance, and community-based protection. Continuous registration and documentation will serve as the keystone for protection interventions, assistance delivery and solutions planning. Considering the escalating conflict in Myanmar, UNHCR will strengthen its border monitoring, advocate against refoulement (deportations, pushbacks), and steer advocacy to ensure regulated access to safety and asylum including individual biometrics registration for new arrivals as well as provide material assistance such as NFI, LPG and health. UNHCR will conduct protection monitoring in all camps to identify protection incidents/trends to advocate for and address protection gaps, identify and assist refugees facing urgent and serious protection risks and facilitate multisectoral referrals. UNHCR will strengthen access to justice through capacity building of law enforcement/justice sector actors, advocacy, and the provision of legal services in all camps through its partners. UNHCR will also conduct awareness-raising initiatives in refugee communities on the risks of onward movement and human trafficking. UNHCR will empower Rohingya refugees by strengthening community capacities for leadership, self-management and engagement including through elected refugee representatives in four camps. UNHCR will support refugee volunteer networks such as Community Outreach Members (COMs), community groups, religious networks (including imams and female religious teachers) and sports for protection clubs. COMs will identify and support persons with specific needs and mobilize communities for protection activities. Male and female community groups, including youth groups, will deliver community initiatives that foster community self-reliance and social change. UNHCR will contribute to peace and security in the camps by advocating for the civilian and humanitarian character of asylum, by strengthening refugee communities’ capacity to manage and prevent conflicts and by advocating, capacity building, and re-engaging law enforcement actors in the camps. Communication with communities and AAP will be mainstreamed through regular community consultations and CAIPP (Centralized Automated Information Processing Platform), operated in all 33 camps, to ensure access to complaints and feedback mechanisms. CAIPP will also act as essential hubs to disseminate timely, accurate and life-saving information. UNHCR will continue to work with disability actors to obtain comprehensive disability data in all camps and strengthen disability inclusion in its protection interventions as well as provide targeted services for PWDs. Within the current Government policy framework, UNHCR will advocate for greater access to AGD responsive education and economic inclusion, so that young refugees retain hope and become agents for positive change in the community. Furthermore, to facilitate Gender Diverse Persons’ (GDP) access to services, UNHCR will expand its assistance to diverse facilities in collaboration with sister agencies and conduct participatory assessments with GDP, wherever conducive, to ascertain and address their specific needs. This would be coupled with FGDs and KIIs with GDPs. UNHCR will improve quality of life for refugees pending solutions building upon the framework for responsibility sharing provided by the Global Compact on Refugees (GCR). As reinforcing accountable Government leadership will build a foundation for the inclusion of refugees in the national systems in the future, UNHCR will continue to support the capacity development of both Government and humanitarian actors across its operations.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Firstly, regarding registration needs, the joint Government-UNHCR Rohingya verification for the new caseload who had arrived in Bangladesh in 2017 was completed in 2019, while the pre-2017 caseload was finalised in 2024. A verification exercise for the existing caseload, including for recently counted new arrival population, is required for the purpose of reviewing the population in the camps, systematically updating information, reissuing documents, and above all, to ensure access to asylum and assistance. While significant progress made in birth registration based on the joint SOP approved by UNHCR and RRRC in May 2024, gaps remain in the civil registration and documentation of births and deaths. Secondly, regarding safety and security concerns, the exacerbation of pre-existing protection risks, including specific risks for refugee women and girls and persons with specific needs including Gender Diverse Persons (GDPs), a deteriorating security environment, stressed community protection structures, deepened inter/intra-communal tensions and attacks and forced recruitment by organized groups continue to be highlighted by the community. Violent clashes between organized groups have also been documented in some camps. From Q1 2022 to Q1 2024, the number of reported serious security incidents – including abduction, extortion, killing, serious physical assault, torture, trafficking, and forced recruitment – has increased by 500% in the camps. Abduction cases alone have surged by 1,442% in the same time period, indicating intensified activity by organized groups. The escalation of the conflict in Northern Rakhine in November 2023 has also directly impacted the camps, with forced recruitment by organized groups first reported in 2024. The Inter Sector Needs Assessment 2024 (ISNA) corroborate such findings, with about 48% expressing a fear of organized groups and 38% expressed a fear of abduction and kidnapping. A lack of socio-economic opportunities, unequal gender norms and a culture of impunity foster pervasive violence in refugee households and communities. Thirdly, regarding access to justice, UNHCR provides legal assistance to refugees, who are often reluctant to report crimes in fear of retaliation by criminal groups and due to lengthy formal processes and lack of trust towards law enforcement institutions. Lack of access to the formal civil justice system limits refugees’ ability to assert their legal rights, including women’s right to divorce, through the formal justice system. Fourthly, regarding meaningful access to assistance and engagement of refugees, women and girls, persons with disabilities (PWD), GDPs, religious minority groups and gender diverse populations continue to face heightened barriers in accessing services and information. Focus group discussions (FGDs) reported that women, especially older women or women with disabilities, were much less likely to have access to information and complaints mechanisms (Nov 2023). PWDs also face barriers related to mobility in shelters as well as movement within the camps. This is particularly true among older and female PWDs who face the most significant challenges in accessing services. Lack of comprehensive disability data further complicates meaningful participation and tailored programming for PWDs. Strategic intervention is therefore necessary to strengthen inclusive AGD sensitive responses which support refugees to become drivers of change in their community. Fifthly, GDPs have reported challenges in accessing services due to social pressure and fear. While efforts are required to raise awareness in the community, UNHCR plans to facilitate GDPs access to services by expanding its services (such as legal counselling) in Women and Girls Centres and Multipurpose Women Centres in collaboration with sister agencies.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

UNHCR’s protection programmes are built on an analysis of community power structures and cultural norms, and aim to strengthen existing community capacities whilst ensuring meaningful access for and inclusion of the most vulnerable and marginalized groups. UNHCR and partners provide meaningful access to services through community centres that provide integrated protection interventions in a non-stigmatising manner, and also ensure that outreach activities reach those who cannot or do not feel comfortable attending centres. To ensure a participatory approach, UNHCR conducts regular community consultations, risk assessments and safety audits which engage men, women, boys and girls, including persons with disabilities, persons of diverse sexual orientations and gender identities and persons with specific needs in order to identify and mitigate risks, and to optimize safety, dignity and equity. Findings from community consultations, in which vast areas are covered such as access to food, site management, shelter and health, are shared with relevant Units to enhance meaningful access and accountability in all services. To further enhance accountability, UNHCR has developed multiple mechanisms for complaints and feedback, community consultation, as well as service quality monitoring. CFM is applied across all sectors and a protection-sensitive approach in the management of the complaint is taken mainstreaming AGD approach and allowing for multiple referrals to enable protection staff follow-up on protection-related issues. UNHCR continuously builds its capacity and for partners on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), the UNHCR Code of Conduct and other essential protection standards.

2.3 - Age & Disability

UNHCR’s protection programmes ensures that all refugees fully participate in decisions that affect them and enjoy their rights on an equal footing with others. As the age, gender, and diversity (AGD) policy is fundamentally linked to UNHCR’s rights-based and community-based approaches, UNHCR includes refugees, of all age and gender, meaningfully throughout its programme cycle to reflect their views and identify protection gaps which need to be addressed. All UNHCR partners are trained on child safeguarding, as well as prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and the Code of Conduct. Focus groups are regularly conducted with boys and girls to identify priority activities as well as risks and challenges in accessing programmes. UNHCR and partners engage outreach volunteers to reach out to and include refugees of all ages, with a focus on adolescents, youth, adults, older persons, and persons with disabilities. UNHCR works with male and female youth groups, providing training and resources for young people to identify priorities and address them. UNHCR also delivers recreational activities, awareness raising programmes, and opportunities for engagement with children aged eight years and above, including through sports programmes. UNHCR’s outreach activities ensure that all refugees, regardless of age, gender, and disability are provided with information about protection services and have the opportunity to access services. UNHCR Community Outreach Members (COMs) are trained to identify and support the most vulnerable groups including older persons, women, children, and persons with disabilities and to provide support to ensure that they can access necessary services.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

AAP is a core responsibility and central to UNHCR’s mandate. Refugees meaningfully participate in deciding the priorities for activities implemented by UNHCR and lead their own initiatives through community-based mechanisms throughout the operational management cycle. UNHCR conducts regular community consultations in all camps to identify priority issues and obtain feedback in order to adapt its protection programming as needed. UNHCR also operates CAIPP (Centralized Automated Information Processing Platform), its camp-wide CFM which centralises community feedback and response centres, complaints boxes, email service, and a toll-free helpline in order to provide information to and elicit feedback and complaints from refugees. UNHCR prioritizes two-way communication, ensuring that individual complaints are responded to and that communities are provided with collective feedback. A fundamental part of UNHCR’s work on AAP includes its commitment to the Code of Conduct (CoC) and a zero-tolerance policy towards sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and harassment for its staff.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

PSEA/SH is a crucial cross-cutting area for all UNHCR programs. Since its conception, UNHCR has played an active role as a member of the PSEA Network, a coordination structure to prevent, mitigate, and harmonize the response against SEA/SH incidents in Bangladesh, having a dedicated group organized for the Rohingya Response and a second one based in Dhaka. Together with IOM, UNHCR will lead the exercise to review the internal SoP and harmonize the document with the global template, organizing consultations and defining the main areas of improvement. UNHCR is also involved in the 2025 Inter-Agency Risk Assessment that will be conducted in all camps through FDGs and KIIs dedicated to identifying and mitigating SEA risks. All UNHCR Implementing Partners are also members of the Network and follow the revised Inter-Agency SoP in 2024. In 2023, UNHCR assessed all Implementing Partners working under a partnership agreement on their capacities to prevent, mitigate, and respond to SEA/SH, guaranteeing excellence, safe, and confidential deliverance of services. UNHCR staff, as well as all personnel working for UNHCR Implementing Partners, including volunteers, are annually trained on CoC and PSEA/SH. UNHCR delivers an annual refresher to Focal Points from NGOs and INGOs and monitors their performance to cascade the sessions, focusing on the safe identification and confidential referral of cases. Above all, it is UNHCR's priority to enhance two-way communication with communities. UNHCR’s outreach activities use non-verbal and accessible language, as well as pictorial posters that motivate the debates around important protection subjects, such as SEA. Outreach activities as well as structured-prevention activities reinforce the availability of our complaint and reporting system, highlighting the importance of a timely report. Videos, podcasts, and posters created in the Rohingya language are used to raise awareness and deliver clear information concerning the referral pathway.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNHCR will remain a central player within inter-agency preparedness and response efforts related to disasters such as monsoons and cyclones. UNHCR includes monsoon, cyclone, landslide, fire and other disaster preparedness aspects in all of its communication with communities activities, and also in community initiatives. When disasters occur, UNHCR targets protection services to respond to the needs of those affected, with a specific focus on women, children and persons with specific needs. UNHCR is an active member of the Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU) and thus are on standby to be deployed during disasters or emergencies in camp setting to identify urgent protection cases, provide emergency assistance including psychosocial first aid (PFA), map functioning and non-functioning services in the affected areas, and carry out other activities to serve and protect affected refugees. In addition, UNHCR has First Onsite Response Teams (FORT) which are deployed after disaster to ensure minimal presence of staff to monitor and follow up the ongoing emergency response in the most affected camps with partners. FORT conducts immediate onsite assessments which contributed to Sector level data gathering. UNHCR is an active member of the Emergency Protection Working Group at ISCH level and a partner organization of the ISCG Multi-Hazard Response Plan. Moreover, UNHCR built facilities in consideration of environmental and climate concerns, and disaster risk reduction (DRR). UNHCR, through its community-based approach, will conduct sessions to raise awareness and outreach on the long-term impact of climate change, emphasizing community ownership and responsibility, the need for care and maintenance of facilities that contribute to community resilience.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

UNHCR developed a Business Continuity Plan (BCP) which was implemented during the political crisis in late July and August 2024. The BCP, which does not require additional cost, relies on refugee volunteers who are present in the camps, utilising remote modalities such as mobile phones, SMS texts, and other means, to connect with refugees facing dire protection needs, conduct advocacy with the authorities, and facilitate necessary interventions. UNHCR has also developed a relationship with the authorities to allow limited convoys of staff into the camps even under crisis conditions like those that occurred in July/August 2024. Furthermore, a BCP was developed for assistance distribution using the offline modality of the Global Distribution Tool (GDT), a system to track and record the distribution of assistance connected with proGres database, was successfully implemented during the internet outage in July/ August 2024.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

UNHCR engages youth through its community-based protection activities as part of its standard programming providing a space for male and female youth to engage in discussions and identify and self-implement projects in their community. Youth participate in tailored indoor and outdoor activities in all of UNHCR community centres. The sports for protection programme in several camps targets both male and female refugees (between 15 and 23 years old) to help them develop their social competencies through meaningful sports activities. Host community youth are engaged by UNHCR through sports activities to enhance peaceful co-existence with the refugee community. Furthermore, youths are actively engaged in the peace & security campaign, with arts for peace activities, where they contribute their vision of what peace looks like for them. Through art (drama, theatre, music, painting), they express themselves and connect with each other, learning important skills for social cohesion. They also contribute to social cohesion in the wider community by sharing and presenting their artwork. In addition, UNHCR intends to provide in 2025 Legal awareness sessions focused specifically on youth, covering topics of relevance such as the risk of cyberbullying, online intimate image abuse (and legal remedies), trafficking of youth, and other topics that the community indicates their interest in.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

UNHCR promotes self-reliance and resilience through its community-based and legal activities. For instance, Sports for Protection project aims to develop refugees’ social skills, increase confidence, show higher self-esteem, greater resilience, and sense of belonging, and psychosocial wellbeing. In addition, refugee-elected leaders will continue to be supported by UNHCR in the camps where they are active. Refugee governance will be strengthened through capacity building allowing the refugee community representatives a stronger involvement in resolving camp issues. Moreover, community-led discussion and initiatives by the community groups allow refugees to identify, manage and self-implement projects that benefit the community. In addition, UNHCR conducts, through its partners, legal awareness sessions to sensitise refugees on how they could exercise their rights to seek justice despite the various restrictions in the camps.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

UNHCR supports Peaceful Coexistence Committee in four unions of Ukhiya and Teknaf, comprised of Union Parishad (UP) members, journalists, social workers, civil society, teachers, youths, PWD and community leaders who live near the camps. They will identify the constraints to peaceful co-existence at community level and advocate for peaceful co-existence and human rights. Based on its consultation, community support projects will be implemented in host community or camp areas. UNHCR will arrange football matches for youths in host communities to promote peace through sports and achieve positive social outcomes, including social inclusion, peaceful coexistence and psychosocial wellbeing. Workshops with local journalists will promote peaceful coexistence through a balanced media narrative. UNHCR will also conduct sensitizations sessions with host community members who live near the camps, village police, religious leaders, educational institutions on peaceful co-existence.
