Project Summary

The project is designed to increase access to quality and conflict sensitive education for conflict affected children and adolescents in Bade, Gujba, Geidam, Yunusari & Damaturu LGAs in Yobe state, Michika & Mubi North LGAs in Adamawa state and Gwoza LGA of Borno state. CCDRN will leverage on the existing education assessments conducted for these locations and together with the key stakeholders to establish and revive alternative and remote learning platforms targeting conflict affected Out of School Children (OOSC) and adolescents.
The 10-month project seeks to respond to the educational needs of the North-East Nigeria as outlined by the OCHA’s Humanitarian Response Plan 2021 and Education Sector Strategic Objectives. The project will target 10,000 vulnerable children and adolescents in 16 communities across 8 Local Governments in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states.
The proposed activities include establishment/reviving of 56 Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) for 2,800 children, supporting 600 home based learning clusters for 7,200 children, engagement of 112 volunteer teachers and 300 mentors, provision of learning materials and dignity kits as well as capacity strengthening of government officials, SBMC and volunteer teachers. These are geared towards ensuring access to conflict sensitive education for all children and adolescents in the targeted locations. The project is also meant to improve the capacity and commitment of SUBEB and SBMCs to participate in school development, risk reduction and transition from remote and alternative learning systems to formal education system.

1 - Needs

Despite the reduction in the Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG) activities in North-East Nigeria, the effect of the conflicts has continued to affect the lives of vulnerable households. This contributes to the country’s position of having the world’s largest population of out-of-school children. According to UNESCO (https://en.unesco.org), the estimated number of out-of-school children in Nigeria stands at 10.5 million, the majority of them being girls from the northern region. In Borno state, 74.8 percent of children aged 4-16 years have never attended school. Education indices in North-East Nigeria paint a picture of the already weak education situation since pre-crisis and worsened by the armed conflict. Among the six geopolitical zones, North East of Nigeria has the highest Out of School Children accounting for an estimated 40% of the country ratio. Girls in the North East account for 53.3% of OOSC in the country but individual states have great variations with Borno having the highest number of girls (of primary school age) out of school at 70.7%, the highest rate in the country. The completion rate (primary school) for the North East on average is 54% compared to the national average of 63%. (MICS). The prolonged crisis within north-eastern Nigeria has made severe impressions on the education system, depriving several children of the opportunity to learn which intensifies their vulnerability. About 910 schools have been destroyed, and over 1,500 forced to close. An estimated 900,000 children have lost access to learning while 75% of children in camps do not attend school, with 1,230,175 million children in need of education support. The December 2020 Humanitarian Response Information (HRINFO) indicates that the total number of people in need of education in the northeast is 1.3 million with 39% of them as IDPs, 38% returnees, and 23% host community members. 52% of this population are females while 48% are males. According to the 2020 EIEWG report on the Humanitarian Context of Education, the conflict between the Armed Opposition Groups (AOGs) and the Nigeria Security operatives in the North East has continued to adversely affect the living conditions of the people in the region. Despite the noticeable degradation of the Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs), some communities in the affected locations continue to suffer from violent attacks by insurgents, resulting in displacements of populations to relatively safer locations. Schools remain one of the most targeted areas for destruction whenever such attacks occur, thereby making them inhabitable and unsafe for learning, in addition to resultant loss of valuable school assets including teaching and learning materials. Teachers' well-being and professional development are also adversely affected by this scenario leading to diminishing ability to deliver quality instructions due to frustrations of displacement and traumatic experiences of the violent attacks by the AOGs. This situation has also led to decreasing demand for education and the willingness of parents to send their wards to schools due mainly to the security risks involved, which further results in an increasing number of out-of-school children in the region. Following the destruction of critical education assets, including schools, by NSAGs during attacks in Gujba, Education in Emergency (EiE) activities and services are urgently needed in the affected communities. This is especially needed as displaced populations, including children, continue to return. The major educational concerns peculiar to the affected communities include the impact of the insurgency on education, the massive havoc the insurgency has caused on the infrastructure of schools leading to overcrowding in existing schools, and lack of personnel especially as there has been no employment in the state civil service since the insurgency began. Others are increased level of school-related gender-based violence and lack of teaching aids.

2 - Activities

Activity1. Planning meetings with SUBEB to coordinate with other State & LGA officials, introduce the project, activities & agree on responsibilities. 2. Mobilization of community-level stakeholders, to intimate them about the project, secure their commitments and identify suitable TLSs, home-based learning platforms & qualified volunteer teachers 3. Conduct Baseline study in the target communities to determine the situation of OOSC, capacity needs of SBMCs & LGEA in the alternative education. 4. Training of SUBEB & SBMC officials on resource mobilization for alternative education and remote learning models, conflict sensitive education, risks reduction and the SSD 5. Conduct workshop with education stakeholders on development of framework for transition from alternative and remote learning centers to formal education system. 6. Routine coordination meetings with SUBEB & SBMCs to review progress, challenges and lessons learnt. 7. Adoption of curriculum for TLS and audio content for home based learning in collaboration with SUBEB. 8. Engagement of 300 volunteer teachers through SUBEB and SBMCs to identify, screen and engage volunteer teachers from the targeted communities to facilitate learning at TLS and home-based centers. 9. Establishment of the Kids & Youth Resilience group across the various LGAs to spearhead the back to school campaign, promote resilience among young children and ensure their protection & psychological development (monthly meetings). 10. Endline survey to determine progress made in the provision of safe and conflict sensitive education for vulnerable children, and roles of SBMCs and SUBEB. 11. Conduct community dialogues on the protection of school structures, and protection of children (boys and girls) in schools thus promoting the SSD and securing the commitment of community members towards schools’ protection and risk reduction. 12. Conduct massive gender responsive back to school campaign with SBMCs & Community leaders. 13. Conduct Community sensitization on alternative education and remote learning. 14. Establishment of 56 Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) and/or leverage on existing structures to establish TLSs for the provision of catch-up education for conflict-affected children. 15. Provision/repair of WASH facilities at TLS, each of the 56 TLSs, 2 structures – toilet and water point will be rehabilitated/provided. 16. Enrolment of OOSC at TLS. 17. Provision of TLS materials such as Mats, White/Blackboards, Marker pens, Chalk, dignity kits for adolescent girls. 18. Recruitment and training of 112 TLS Volunteer Teachers. 19. Payment of monthly stipends for TLS volunteer Teachers (N10, 000 each). 20. Provision of learners kits such as text books, note books, teacher manuals. 21. Establishment, Capacity building & monthly mentorship of a Community Based Education Platform who will support in identifying areas for educational support and build linkages with the community, LGA & state towards addressing these issues identified. 22. Joint Monitoring of TLS with SUBEB, SBMC& LGEA. 23. Mobilise community members for home based learning together with SBMC and community leaders. 24. Identify and engage mentors to support and monitor home based learning with the support of SMBCs, mentors will be engaged to support and monitor home based learning. 200 mentors will be engaged across the 16 targeted communities. 25. Training of mentors on remote learning through radio, use of supporting learning materials and assessment of learners. 26. Provision of materials for home based learning such as solar powered radio transistors, SD cards, mats, text books, note books, white/blackboards, marker pens/chalk. 27. Monitor Home based learning. 28. Payment of monthly stipends of volunteer teachers 10,000 naira to the 200 Mentors.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CCDRN will implement the project following the principles and procedures in its Child Protection Policy to ensure everyone accepts their responsibilities to safeguard children from harm and abuse, share information about child protection and good practice with learners, parents, community members and education stakeholders. Community sensitization, training, engagement and coordination with education stakeholders will ensure that components of protection and awareness around GBV are included. Cross-cutting trainings for volunteer Teachers, SBMCs and other community stakeholders will include topics such as Child protection, Safeguarding, GBV and SEA prevention, reporting, channels and response. CCDRN shall create a safe environment in which the OOSC feel secure while engaging in planned activities. All interventions will be child and gender sensitive as steps will be taken to address child abuse concerns in the best interest of the child. Sensitizations will also be carried out to create awareness about CCDRN’s hotlines for submission and resolve of complaints. CCDRN shall establish systems to ensure proper and timely reporting concerns and will leverage on existing Protection response components in existing projects in the target LGAs among other State level structures or actors. Every effort will be made to assure that, should individuals have concerns; they will be listened to and taken seriously. The management will ensure that information is available to, and exchanged between all those involved in the project. The project will also take all measures to ensure that all TLS/home based learning comply with nationally established COVID -19 protocols and adhere to the “Do No Harm” and “Leave No one Behind” principle.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

CCDRN’s access strategy aims at enhancing the effectiveness of its operations to meet the needs of the affected communities in a timely manner. CCDRN recognizes that the ability of vulnerable groups to access humanitarian assistance is the key element for protection which must be addressed. And so regular risk assessments through internal security analysis and communication with external forums for security updates, such as sectors, local and governmental authorities, UNOCHA, INSO and other agencies. CCDRN is already present in all the targeted LGAs, and has been working with the local expertise to ensure smooth implementation of project activities in a way that is safe and protective to both project staff and the people we serve. CCDRN will ensure feedback is collected through the various established platforms within the TLS and communities and in outmost confidentiality treated accordingly.
