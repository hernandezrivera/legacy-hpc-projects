Project Summary

Somalia still faces significant humanitarian challenges with about 5.9 million people in need of humanitarian aid. Currently, over 2.7 million people face emergency levels of food insecurity crisis likely to grow in the year. Compared to the current number of people in need of aid, this number has increased over the years compared to 4.2 million reported.
Further, about 1.2 million people have been displaced as of 2020, and more than 2.6 million people are classified as internally displaced at risk of facing marginalization, and exclusion. The humanitarian needs arise from decades of recurrent impacts of political, armed conflict, climatic shocks, and socio-economic factors driving the needs of most vulnerable including persons with disabilities, children, the elderly, and pregnant and lactating women.
In Somalia, the health sector faces significant challenges with the COVID-19 pandemic and other disease outbreaks stretching the already strained health infrastructure contributing to some of the worst health outcomes, especially in most rural areas. Additionally, the disease outbreaks straining health servicing and access include measles, Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and cholera, and vaccine-derived polio. Without adequate funding, three million people will not have access to essential health care services. The proposed intervention will provide emergency health services in Benadir, Galmudug, Nugaal, and Hirshabelle regions for internally displaced persons (IDPs) and vulnerable host communities.
The project will support integrated essential lifesaving preventive and curative services in Benadir, Galmudug, Nugaal, and Hirshabelle regions of Somalia improving the utilization of integrated primary healthcare services, including reproductive health and immunization coverage supporting static facilities and use of mobile response to deliver service in areas not currently served by static facilities increasing health care services for marginalized communities.
Under the proposed program, six static facilities (health care clinics) in Benadir and Galmudug regions and 4 Mobile outreaches in Nugaal and Hirshabelle region with facilities equipped to provide basic screening, medical, surgical, laboratory, nutrition, and pharmacy services. Additionally, patients presenting chronic conditions and complications including pregnant women with obstetric complications within the community are to be referred for management in the health facilities. Further, pregnant women in need of Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric Care (CEmONC) will be referred from the facilities to tertiary facilities in the supported regions.
This action will strengthen facility structures through recruitment of support staff, training of healthcare workforce, provision of essential equipment and essential supplies, provision of comprehensive reproductive healthcare services and skilled attended birth, and regular supportive supervision. Community health volunteers will be supported to identify cases and provide referral mechanisms among the marginalized population for instance the pregnant mothers for ANC services and safer births as well as identifying vulnerable community members with non-communicable diseases including persons with mental health needs. Further, GBV survivors receive a survivor-centered approach ensuring survivors’ rights, needs, and wishes are prioritized and always respected. The proposed intervention will address immediate needs i.e., mitigating the spread of COVID-19 and supporting the public health consequences of the pandemic in target locations including Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) including awareness-raising through media and training more community health workers on case definitions as well as to scale up hygiene response in the targeted areas.

1. - Projects activities

IRC will provide free essential preventive and curative health care including Reproductive health non-communicable diseases through free outpatient consultative services to 125,430 IDPs and host communities in need humanitarian including drought-affected persons and those with disabilities (<1%). Through the proposed intervention, the IRC will support 6 fixed healthcare facilities (1 in each region) providing treatment for non-communicable diseases (NCDs) psychosocial support, and communicable diseases including pneumonia, malaria, diarrhea, measles, skin conditions, injury care among others. Also, the facilities will provide reproductive health services including, Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (CEmONC) as well as specialized reproductive health care for pregnant and lactating women including birth deliveries attended by a skilled birth attendant. In addition, the community health worker will provide referrals to pregnant women with delivery complications to the facilities. To reduce maternal and child mortality rates the IRC will provide standby ambulance transport for mothers with a complication from IDP camps to health facilities. As well, IRC will coordinate with the MOH and UNICEF to ensure all IDP children below 5 years are vaccinated ensuring a high coverage rate of Penta3 and measle vaccines. Further, all IDP beneficiaries will be screened for COVID-19 and with other services such as clinical management of rape services (CMR) and GBV / IPV services and psychosocial counseling provided, and referral pathways established for different conditions and ensure immediate movement of emergency and critical patient to secondary level of care. In summary, the following specific activities will be undertaken: 1. Provision of outpatient consultations and treatments for communicable and non-communicable diseases adhering to the standard treatment protocols for men, women, boys, and girls with access to essential medicines within the targeted regions. 2. Support mobile outreach health promotion and preventive services through a network of community health workers identifying, providing referrals, and creating awareness on Malaria, Diarrhea, Pneumonia, Vaccine-preventable diseases, IYCF, safe motherhood, and GBV. 3. Provision of secondary or tertiary referrals for the cases such as obstetric complications and severe chronic diseases and provision of immunization services (Measles and Polio) to increase the infant and child vaccination coverage. 4. Provision of integrated Basic Emergency Obstetric and New-born Care (BEmoNC) services, by skilled health workers.5. Conduct facility-based identification and provide psycho-social support and prompt referral for appropriate services for people with disabilities and mental health issues 6. Identification, counseling, management, and prompt referral for Gender-based Violence survivors and victims7. Capacity building of frontline health workers (CHWs, nurses, clinicians) on IMCI service protocols and distribution of guidelines. 8. Provision of procurement of essential drugs and clinical supplies for health facilities. The IRC will also conduct community dialogues (targeting different community social and age groups) during and after outreach to identify key health issues in communities and to address barriers to access. Further, other services will include health awareness, community mobilization, education, and advocacy activities on MHPSS, disabilities Clinical management of rape services (CMR) and GBV treatment and prevention; distribute PPE to HCWs and community health workers for COVID19 mitigation, and rehabilitate health facility latrines and handwashing stations to promote patient and provider safety and prevent the spread of diseases.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Tp ensure inclusive representation of the community engagement through sensitization sessions will be by ensuring all community segments are represented in the community committees and groups. An inclusive selection criterion with emphasis on women, children, people with special needs/disabilities, and marginalized groups will be allowed for established to provide enough room for community engagement preventing poor inclusion of women, children, people with special needs/disability, and marginalized groups in the project. Awareness of the projects will be conducted in the community and during community outreach activities to improve community role in the implementations of project activities. The IRC’s client-responsive anonymous feedback and complaints mechanisms designed through multiple channels to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, IDPs, and people with disabilities, will collect and analyze data on clients’ perspectives on the quality and relevance of project interventions, and are used to inform timely responses, guide decision-making, and ensure that the program does not negatively impact the community. Further, clients will be able to report any IRC-related misuse of resources, lack of effectiveness, timeliness, or responsiveness. A communication structure in place in collaboration with Complaints Response Mechanism guides how to handle complaints and report them to the IRC Management. An exit interview will be conducted within the project period to get feedback from the community on barriers to accessing healthcare including lack of quality health services, among them inadequate emergency obstetric and neonatal care, and minimal staff capacity.
