Project Summary

The project's objective is to improve the health, dignity, and overall well-being of South Sudan communities through the provision of sustainable water and sanitation infrastructure and the promotion of good hygiene practices. The project's approach involves three main components: increasing access to safe and sustainable water for poor and vulnerable communities, reducing the incidence of WASH-related diseases by promoting personal hygiene and environmental practices, and integrating nutrition and health initiatives to foster institutional synergy. By implementing these measures, the project aims to improve the health outcomes of South Sudan communities while promoting sustainable development practices.
This project seeks to improve health, promote dignity and well-being of communities in South Sudan by providing appropriate water and sanitation infrastructure, and educate people on good hygiene practices. ForAfrika will contribute to the success of the WASH cluster in collaboration with the humanitarian response plan with the contribution to the WASH Strategic objectives and approaches ranging from CO1 to CO3 namely;
1. Contribution to the reduction of morbidity and mortality focusing on malnutrition through the WASH and Nutrition-integrated programming in targeted counties.
2. Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and mainstream protection with a focus on GBV mitigation (safety audits) and disability inclusion.
3. Provide durable access to services in long-term displaced sites and communities by building resilience.
Of the total 5.5 million targeted people, ForAfrika will address the WASH needs of 45,455 people facing crisis in 3 Counties across 3 States of South Sudan. 18,656 people in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State, 12,295 people in Jonglei State and 14,595 in Warrap State.
The focus of this project will provide safe, adequate water, sanitation, and hygiene kits through the development of boreholes (deep wells and hand pumps) and solarization, pipeline expansion and reticulation, rehabilitation of wells, and improved water quality and monitoring. Construction of disability-friendly and gender-segregated sanitation facilities at institutional level and household, establishment of hygiene clubs/education for children and adults to promote hand washing practices. The complexities surrounding social behavior will be addressed through the introduction of social behavior communication change. Introducing cutting-edge innovative designs and technologies, the project seeks to improve access to safe and clean water sources, as well as promote the adoption of effective sanitation practices that reduce the incidence of waterborne diseases and other related health issues.
As part of our initiative for resilience and sustainability, we will provide training to community members on the operation and maintenance of water and sanitation facilities. They will lead various committees that promote ownership, community well-being, and improvements. This approach will foster a sense of ownership among community members while promoting their well-being. Women’s participation in leadership and decision-making will be encouraged ensuring each Water user committee is composed of women. ForAfrika will lead the innovative provision through the provision of irrigation, water ponds and wells for domestic and farming purposes. To ensure Accountability to the affected population, ForAfrika will conduct community engagements addressing beneficiary entitlements, benefits and roles to play within the project implementation. In each community, ForAfrika will put in place complaint and feedback mechanisms such as Phone lines, suggestion book and utilize the monitoring feedback meeting to get feedback from the community. These summarize our approaches to the cluster objective with WASH integration with health, nutrition education food security and Livelihoods.

1. Needs

Access to essential basic WASH services in hospitals, schools, internally displaced people camps, transit camps, and communities remains heavily constrained. High levels of open defecation, unimproved sanitation, poor and unsafe hygiene practices, and inadequate access to safe water pose a significant challenge to the population's overall health. Fifty-nine percent of the population in South Sudan lacks access to safe water. Of the 6.1 million people in need of WASH services, 15% are children and 15% PWDs. Only 36 percent of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns (HNO 2023). The need for essential WASH non-food items such as buckets and soaps is also high - only 14 percent of households in South Sudan reported having at least one of such items. Critical gaps in WASH contribute to high levels of acute malnutrition and overall high morbidity and mortality among children under 5 years. Poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continue to have a detrimental impact on the health of children, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases. In providing solutions In health and nutrition a SMART survey (2021) the GAM prevalence was 21.6%, above 15% WHO threshold, and SAM prevalence of 4.6 %. Currently 56.8% of 39 nutrition facilities have no access to safe drinking water and 35.1% have no toilets. The need for essential WASH non-food items such as buckets and soap is also high - only 14 percent of households in South Sudan reported having at least one of such items. In education over 45% of schools lack latrines and even those that have them often lack access to safe drinking water and handwashing facilities. This situation has significant implication on child physical and cognitive development. Further, it affects the capacity of the parents to send their children to school (low enrolment) and increases school drop outs. The lack of gender-segregated and disability-friendly WASH facilities is a primary obstacle to accessing education in South Sudan. According to the Education Needs Assessment (ENA), more than 60% of latrines are inaccessible to students with disabilities. The national rate of open defecation ranges from 80 to 90 percent. During dry season beneficiaries walk over 15km to get drinking water from the various water sources.

2. Project activities

Activities under Output 1: Improved access to safe drinking water, adequate and equitable sanitation and hygiene facilities (WASH) services delivery; Drilling of 18 selected strategic water sources (18 solar borehole and 5 hand pumps fitted) with 36 new fetching points which are flood-resilient designs. Carry out Water Quality monitoring focusing FRC, and faecal coliform bacteria ensuring FRC levels are within 0.2-0.5 mg/l and 0/100 ml respectively. Carry out Water Quality monitoring focusing FRC, and faecal coliform bacteria ensuring FRC levels are within 0.2-0.5 mg/l and 0/100 ml respectively. Train, establish and provision of operation and maintenance tools and equipment to WMCs and CPM. As integrated approach during the drilling activities we will introduce cash for work approach for families to earn income. Output 2: Adequate sanitation which meets Sphere standards provided to children and their families: Construction of 30 latrines of 5 stances in for 7,500 people in the prioritised locations. Construction of 30 bathing shelters for transit/reception centres for 7500 people. Community-Led cleaning campaigns for IDPs, vulnerable groups and other affected communities towards solid waste management. Output 3: Hygiene-related goods (NFIs) which meet Sphere standards and training on how to use those goods is provided to the children and their families. Integration of WASH mainstreaming with other sectors FSL, Health Nutrition and Education mainstreaming GBV protection and peace building/social cohesion and community governance/feedback. Hygiene kits will be provided to each unit caretaker/SAM child and adolescent girls with training on the use of the items and hygiene promotion. In strengthening the hygiene of the various sectors governance structures will be integrated such as hygiene clubs in both schools and health centers.
