Project Summary

The project aims to improve well-being and health through integrated healthcare support to the Rohingya community living in the camps and to host communities in Ukhia in Cox’s Bazar as a continuation of previous project. The project includes the operation of 1 Primary Healthcare Centre (PHC) and the general consultation, and limited surgery section of Friendship Hospital Ukhia (FHU, a secondary Healthcare center). Outpatient and inpatient services at Ukhiya Specialized Hospital (USH) supported by Orbis International for Eye unit and the Refugee Crisis Foundation (RCF) for dental Unit. One primary Healthcare Centre (PHC) in Camp 7 will provide services in affiliation with the Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS). For the Friendship Hospital Ukhia (FHU), the maternity part (CEmONC) is funded by IOM and UNFPA, the family planning services are supported by IPAS, and the rest of the services including general consultation services are funded by the Friendship Luxemburg and provide us the required flexibility to strengthen and consolidate services across the facilities. The Maternity part (CEmONC) of the Friendship Hospital Ukhia (FHU) is identified by the health sector as a referral point to provide higher-level maternal healthcare services including Caesarean sections for both host and Rohingya. The project focuses on 1) protection against illness and equitable access to (Health Sector Indicator 2) health services without any discrimination and especially targeting the most vulnerable groups (women, children, pregnant/lactating women, people with disabilities, the elderly, GBV, and conflict-affected survivors). Disability inclusion is a core priority throughout the project, ensuring that individuals with disabilities have access to the full spectrum of healthcare services The project also collaborates with the Ukhiya Specialized Hospital (UNHCR) by sharing the expertise, accepting referral points, and receiving maternal patients from USH until the maternity part is started there. Friendship will conduct the following activities following the MESP:
- Provide essential health services to both the host and Rohingya community through 1 primary healthcare center in camp 7 and one 24/7 Secondary level healthcare service named Friendship Hospital Ukhia (FHU).
- Provide SRH services including safe deliveries (assisting Normal Vaginal Delivery) and providing basic neonatal care, Ante Natal Care (ANC) and Post Natal Care (PNC) services to both the host and Rohingya communities.
- Conduct normal vaginal delivery through the PHC and C-section through FHU to ensure safe institutional delivery, with a focus on patient with disabilities who may face additional challenges.
- Provide Family Planning, Menstrual Regulation, management of STIs, and post-abortive care services.
- Screen and refer Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Medium Acute Malnutrition (MAM) children (6 to 59 months) to Outpatient Therapeutic feeding Program (OTPs).
- Provide Mental Health and Psychosocial Support and Gender Based Violence services including Clinical Management of Rape services and clinical management of Gender Based Violence cases.
- Support Expanded Program on Immunization services in alignment with the national program.
- Provide laboratory investigation facility services for the beneficiaries at the health facility level.
- Provide essential medicine and supplements including Vitamin C and Zinc tablets to increase immunity of the beneficiaries
- Conduct awareness-raising sessions including IPC at facility.
- Strengthen the feedback mechanism by developing and implementing action plan based on feedback received from camp-level health coordination and health committee meetings.
- Community Health Volunteers are trained and supervised to identify community members in need of referral for PHC services, EPI, SRH including ANC/PNC with special attention to term mothers for skilled birth attendant deliveries with the support of outreach Midwives.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The Rohingya crisis has become one of the largest humanitarian crises in recent times, requiring relief and support at an unprecedented speed and scale and causing immense pressure. After fleeing mass-scale violence in Myanmar in August 2017, as of 30 September 2024, 967,765 Rohingya refugees have been living in the world’s largest and most densely populated refugee camp in Cox’s Bazar– one of the poorest districts of Bangladesh (UNHCR Population Factsheet), which is putting a lot of pressure on the already limited and strained infrastructure, services, and environment currently existing. Host communities and local authorities are struggling to cope with this reality as detrimental social, economic, and environmental effects of the prolonged displacement continue to increase their suffering. The Rohingya population in Cox’s Bazar is highly vulnerable, after generations of statelessness and no access to health or education before the severe traumas inflicted by the Myanmar Army, Police, and local Rakhine Buddhist mobs. Especially, the people who arrived in Bangladesh on 25 August 2017 came with very few possessions and are still almost entirely reliant on humanitarian assistance for food, shelter, healthcare, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) and other lifesaving needs while living in difficult conditions. Of the 33 refugee camps in Cox’s Bazar District, 29 of them are in Ukhia Upazila, hosting 86% of the total refugee population currently residing in different camps in Cox’s Bazar. Inter Sector Coordination Group (ISCG), led by UNHCR and IOM, continues to work with the Bangladesh government and coordinate Rohingya response efforts of all local, national, and international NGOs and UN agencies in Cox’s Bazar to arrange essential shelter, food, water, medicine, sanitation, and protection. As in 2024, ensuring adherence to quality, and respectful implementation of the minimum essential service package for health services remains a top priority for 2025 According to ISCG’s (Inter Sector Coordination Group) Need Analysis and Response Plan, a substantial gap remains in accessing quality healthcare services in the Rohingya camps and host communities, despite four-year continuous interventions by NGOs and donors. The prioritized sector objectives for health by ISCG JRP 2022 was1. Support equitable access to essential primary and secondary healthcare services for Rohingya refugees/FDMN and the host community.2. Prepare for, prevent, and respond to outbreaks of communicable disease and other hazards that have potential negative public health consequences.3. Promote health and well-being at the individual and community level. The project identified gender-specific health service needs to improve equitable access, safety, and responsiveness to diverse needs within the camp and host communities. 1. Women and adolescent girls require accessible, quality maternal and reproductive health services, including antenatal, postnatal, and safe delivery care, as well as contraceptives and menstrual hygiene management.2. Given the high prevalence of SGBV, women and girls need access to specialized services, including case management, counseling, and safe spaces. Services should address the needs of survivors, offering trauma-informed care, legal support, and safe shelter options.4. Adolescents, particularly girls, require tailored health services that cover SRH education, mental health support, and access to safe spaces for consultations and peer support.6. General health services must be inclusive and address common gendered health conditions, like anemia among women and non-communicable diseases that may affect men differently due to lifestyle factors. The project will integrate these gender-specific health needs to deliver more inclusive, accessible, and effective health services within the Rohingya camps.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Incorporating Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) into the health project led by Friendship NGO is imperative to uphold ethical standards, ensure the safety of beneficiaries, and maintain the integrity of the humanitarian effort. Friendship has already developed a comprehensive PSEA policy specifically tailored to the Friendship NGO's FDMN health project. Clearly articulate the organization's commitment to zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse, outlining preventive measures and consequences for violations. Conducted specialized training sessions for all staff and volunteers involved in the health project. Provided detailed instruction on PSEA principles, the code of conduct, and the importance of maintaining a safe and respectful environment. Ensure that all team members are well-versed in reporting procedures. Established a confidential and easily accessible mechanism for reporting incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse. Ensure that beneficiaries and staff are aware of the reporting channels and guarantee a prompt and thorough investigation of all reported cases. Implemented a robust system for holding individuals accountable for PSEA violations. Ensured that disciplinary actions were taken swiftly, and communicated transparently with relevant authorities when necessary. Scheduled regular audits to assess the implementation of PSEA measures. Use audit findings as a basis for continuous improvement, ensuring that the organization remains responsive and adaptive to emerging challenges. By adhering to this comprehensive plan, Friendship can effectively mainstream PSEA for the proposed project, contributing to a secure, respectful, and protective environment for all involved.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The Friendship Health project for the Rohingya population incorporated following Climate Action issues –1. Health facilities under the project have been built with durable materials and structural adaptations to withstand extreme weather events, including floods and cyclones. These ensure continuous service delivery even during adverse weather conditions.2. Early warning systems for extreme weather events and climate-sensitive disease outbreaks have been integrated into the project with the centrally managed EWARS system by the Health Sector of Rohingya situation. Community Health Worker/Volunteers (CHWs/CHVs) actively engage in raising awareness and preparing communities for potential health impacts related to climate events.3. The project reduces its environmental impact through established waste management practices, the use of renewable energy sources (such as solar power in health centers). These measures mitigate environmental degradation and contribute to long-term resilience.4. The project addresses health issues that are exacerbated by climate change, including vector-borne diseases, respiratory problems from air pollution.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The Friendship Health project for the Rohingya response includes several embedded Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures:1. The project has a Business Continuity Plan (BCP) in place to ensure essential health services can continue during emergencies, such as natural disasters or disease outbreaks. This plan outlines procedures for maintaining critical operations and prioritizes services during disruptions.2. The project follows established SOPs for emergency response, detailing roles, responsibilities, and protocols for staff and volunteers. The SOPs are prepared by the Health sector’s Emergency response team and Friendship already have a designated emergency medical team for camp 7. These SOPs cover rapid response mechanisms, triage processes, and patient care protocols to ensure an organized and effective response.3. The project has allocated contingency funds to support quick mobilization of resources during emergencies. These funds help procure emergency supplies, support additional staffing needs, and cover operational costs that may arise unexpectedly.
