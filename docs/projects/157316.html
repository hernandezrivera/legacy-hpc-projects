Project Summary

Needs

The influx of South Sudanese into Darfur continues, with over 150,000 new arrivals in East Darfur and South Darfur according to the Refugee Coordination Model figures. More arrivals expected in 2019 as the peace agreement still fragile and not fully implemented. Although large response is ongoing , there is a significant gap in protection, livelihood assistance, WASH and education services in East , South Darfur. In conflict contexts, women and girls bear the brunt of the violence and brutality as rape, sexual abuse, sexual exploitation and other gender based violence are common. The forms of violence and abuse encountered by minors including child marriage, child labor, child recruitment by armed groups, unaccompanied and separated children and child trafficking. Women and youth are also the most dynamic in seeking day to day opportunities for income to cover partial need of their demand which has not been satisfactorily met throughout this period; this exposes them to risks, violence, and neglect. Provision of livelihood coping mechanisms is urgently needed to help the women, girls, boys to meet their demands and contribute to self reliance. The affected school aged children both boys and girls have limited or no access to learning spaces most of the classes conducted under trees is in jeopardy especially during the rainy/flood season. The existing schools have no absorption capacity. The provision of Temporary Learning Spaces at refugee sites will enable the children to continue their education, reduce risks and increase protection. It was observed that, most of the families male and females are sharing houses with their extended relatives and accommodated in shelters built partially from local materials (bamboo, sticks and grass) but lacking preventive materials that protect them from rain. Insides the shelters, they managed to collect very few items that can assist them for cooking, fetching and keeping water, therefore there is an urgent need for ES and NFIs. Refugees living in Alait in ND are in desperate situation and lack of urgent supports not limited to water , food , education and health. Increased caseloads of CAR refugees in Umdafug areas, with the majority settled in temporary sites are posing a health risk in each of these areas, due to WASH services that are far below Sphere standards. There have been warnings of the possibility of diarrheal epidemics and water borne related diseases. Women and young girls in Umdafug continue to bear the burden of moving long distances (average 10 20 Km) and lining up at water points for hours to get clean water; increasing their exposure to the possibility of GBV. UPO will integrate GBV risk reduction strategies into the implementation of the activities through the consultation of women in locating resources and services, as well as including them as members of the decision-making committees that govern these resources and services

Activities

1.Improve access to WASH facilities at CAR refugees and refugee returnee sites through installation of hand pumps targeting 10,000 most vulnerable people (4,000 women, 3,000 young girls, 3,000 boys and 4,000 men). 2. Training 500 community members (300 female, 200 male) Solid Waste Disposal and management 3. Distribution of NFIs and improved emergency shelter materials to 4,000 SS Refugees (2,500 females and 1,500 male) most vulnerable refugees and refugee returnees. 4. Construction of 15 protective Temporary learning Space targeting 6,000 most vulnerable school age children (3,000 girls and 3,000 boys) to enable them to continue their education and reduce risks. Objective 2: Ensure effective protection of refugees, asylum seekers, refugee returnees and those with undetermined nationality status, with particular attention to vulnerable groups including children, youth, women at risk, survivor/victims of trafficking and kidnapping. 1. Conducting training for 200 members of government institutions and local authorities (100 females and 100 males) on protection and refugee and human rights. 2. Establish and train 12 community based protection committees, composed of male and female members to enable them identify and monitor protection incidents (risks, violence, kidnapping, SGBV) 3. Conduct FTR to 500 unaccompanied and separated minors 4. Establish 6 Child friendly Spaces for the provision of PSS support to 2,000 children (1,200 girls and 800 boys) Objective 3: Facilitate durable solutions including promoting self reliance and livelihoods, resettlement and voluntary repatriation where possible. 1. Provision of Agricultural inputs and seeds to 2000 most vulnerable refugees and returnees (1100 women and 900 men) to enable them produce to meet their food demands and increase self reliance. 2. Conducting vocational training for 500 most vulnerable women and young girls 3. Provision of IGAs to 1,000 vulnerable people (550 women and 450 men)
