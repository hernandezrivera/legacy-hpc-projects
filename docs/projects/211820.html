Project Summary

Severe needs are among different populations among Syra reaching 15.4 million individuals according to ERL HNO 2024, widespread reliance on unsustainable coping mechanisms, households facing significant income-expenditure gaps, insufficient incomes, lack of jobs, services and depreciation of Syrian pound value all caused households to not meet their basic needs. (ERL HNO 2024).
The LIVE project will provide livelihood grants to most vulnerable HHs in Homs, Lattakia, Aleppo and RD accompanied with business management training, this proposed activity lines under objective 2, activities 2.05 and 2.08, indicators: 2.05.1 and 2.08.1 of Sector logframe.
The above-mentioned governorates have high number PIN and severity scales (3 and above) according to ERL Sector severity and PiN 2024 where services, resources and assistance are limited.
ADRA will prioritize areas which include largest number of females headed households, people with disabilities, older persons, displaced persons –including refugees, IDPs and returnees, and chronic diseases, and sub-districts with highest severity scale. Also, where possible ADRA will implement integrated activities in the same targeted locations.
The activity will be advertised in the community and applications from beneficiaries will be received. ADRA will verify these applications in coordination with SARC and business management training will be held for potential beneficiaries to give them basic ideas about project management and budgeting.
The beneficiaries will submit their business plans at the end of the training. ADRA will study the criteria and business plans, then beneficiaries will be selected for purchasing of grants.
Protection risk, Likelihood, Impact and Mitigation measure
1-Violence against Health Facilities/providers, likelihood low, impact high. ADRA will assure the safety of intervene areas and report any attacks through the Surveillance System for Attacks (SSA)
2 Tensions between beneficiaries and/or community members, likelihood low, impact high. ADRA will work closely with local partners to ensure that the planned interventions are culturally appropriate and harmonized with other forms of assistance to ‘do no harm’ and minimize the risks to beneficiaries
3 Sensitive data is leaked to unauthorized sources, likelihood low, impact high. ADRA has a data protection policy that will be implemented to ensure the protection of sensitive beneficiary information.

Risk/ assumption and mitigations:
1-Delays in obtaining the necessary approvals to implement activities in a timely manner from relevant authorities.ADRA will maintain strong connections with all stakeholders and follow-up on necessary approvals.
2-Changes in the security situation status in Syria, which might impact ADRA, partners and the community particularly in project locations. ADRA will implement regular monitoring of the security situation with UNDSS, INGOs, sectors, stakeholders and staff. ADRA has an established and proven safety and security plan which is shared with all staff.
3-Extended delays in fund transfers to Syria or devaluation of SYP may place project activities outside the planned budget . ADRA will Closely monitor exchange rates, use competitive tender processes. ADRA has opened a variety of different channels to money transfer funds into Syria.
4-Qualified staff may not be available. ADRA recruit Staff through a clear and transparent recruitment process, and provided with the necessary training including on PSEA.
5-The procurement of supplies could be subject to corruption or conflict of interest. ADRA has developed and follows a clear procurement policy to help prevent fraud, corruption, and conflict of interest.
