Project Summary

This project is an extension of a previous project started last year aiming at integrating primary health care, community health, and mental health services
The Qatar Red Crescent provides primary health care services with integrated psychosocial support services and community health services within 15 primary health care centers in Syria. Health services include reproductive health, maternal and child care, reproductive health counseling, outreach community health services, mhGAP and PSS scalable psychological intervention; PM+, routine immunization, medications, internal and specialized clinics such as dental, ophthalmology, and this is classified as comprehensive health care centers Level 4, which provides BEmOC services such as delivery and temporary observation (inpatient) and other basic services and life-saving, which aims to stabilize the health system inside Syria and protect it from collapse during the protracted crisis.

Needs

According to HNO 2019, Provision of health services continues to be disrupted by ongoing hostilities. Attacks on health have rendered 46 percent of hospitals and primary health facilities in Syria as either partially functional or not functional129, while in some areas humanitarian partners have been unable to secure sustained andpredictable access to populations in need. Sustaining basic essential health services remains a key need across the country. 37 percent of the affected population requires routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health (RMNCH) services; an estimated 41 percent of Syria’s adult population requires treatment for one or more non-communicable diseases (NCDs); and at least 15 per cent of the population requires mental health and psychosocial services (MHPSS) as WHO estimates that one in thirty people in people living in Syria suffer from severe mental health conditions (such as severe depression, psychosis, or disabling anxiety) and at least one in 5 suffer from mild to moderate mental health conditions (such as depression or anxiety disorders). however, the infrastructure and referral system that remains functional after close to eight years of crisis is inadequate to cope with existing needs. Over the last year, QRCS has been able to provide a package of essential emergency assistance to vulnerable people in Syria and then demonstrated its capacity for expansion and replication of service provision in vulnerable districts. QRCS supported 556,635 primary healthcare consultations. In fact, as QRCS is one of the few humanitarian actors able to effectively provide essential services through integrating maternal, child health and MHPSS services with engaging the community as a stronger approach to enhance health system, achieve universal health coverage and improve health status in the country despite the unstable political and security environment, vulnerable groups outside of QRCS target districts seek services at organization support health facilities in Idleb, Aleppo and Homs governorates. Furthermore, QRCS’s emphasis on community outreach allows for delivery of services and awareness raising on life-saving interventions to the most vulnerable households. Under the prior project cycle, we have made significant progress towards addressing previously identified pressing needs in health facilities. Thus far, we have strengthened staff capacity to provide primary health care services in a quality manner, expanded a network of trained outreach workers to visit communities surrounding targeted health facilities and provided equipment, medicines and medical consumables in targeted facilities. However, the deteriorating economic, political and security situation in Syria has created further humanitarian needs, particularly amongst the most vulnerable populations. This combination of factors has also hindered the ability of several humanitarian actors to provide emergency assistance. However, given QRC strong ties with communities and local authorities, we propose to meet the needs of additional health facilities at 2 governorates in Idlib and Aleppo. These Governorates are in severe need of emergency assistance and have been identified as extremely vulnerable by health cluster (Turkey Hub). Thus, we intend to strengthen support to existing health facilities and expand support to additional health facilities in the 2 governorates. Based on ongoing assessments in two governorates, QRCS has identified the following major gaps which require immediate intervention:-Limited functionality of primary health care (PHC) facilities: Critical shortages and/or impaired functionality of equipment, supplies, and commodities were identified in most of PHC centers visited.-Inadequate training on reproductive health and child health in PHC staff: Low skill levels of staff concerning family planning and the Health cluster (Turkey hub) and Reproductive Health TWG strategy-lack of Information Systems (HIS)

Protection Risk Analysis

1-Protection risk: Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to beneficiaries through the provision of services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality> Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High.Mitigating measures: 1. ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remotely managed context; 2. Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules; 3. include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision; 4. Service mappings should only include services that have been determined to provide safe and quality services. 5. Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place. 2-Protection risk: 1. Lack of commitment to integrating and enforcing risk mitigation measures; 2 lack of accountability; 3. Sectors may put survivors at risk if they do not follow correct procedures Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium.Mitigating measures: 1. Action plans for risk mitigation interventions 2. Strong buy-in from senior humanitarian community and WOS 3-Protection risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information is linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. Likelihood: High. Impact: High.Mitigating measures: 1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. 2. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for the code of conduct and PSEA.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Access to targeted locations and beneficiaries.- Continued funds based on an approved plan.- Security situation does not deteriorate.- Acceptance and cooperation by stakeholders. 1-Targeting centers by direct shelling or changing areas of influence and the multiplicity of military bodies controlling. Severity: High. Mitigating measures are taken: Assessment of the safest and least vulnerable areas. Training all workers on first aid and evacuation of wounded. Ensure that centers are equipped with full safety supplies such as fire extinguishers, first aid kit etc. 2-Limited availability of qualified personnel that limits the ability of centers to provide services. Severity: High. Mitigating measures are taken: To provide the program with a comprehensive assessment of the current availability of staff, doctors and staff in and around the centers to ensure a realistic, feasible and appropriate management and financial proposal. 3-Limited implementation of project outputs and objectives technically. Severity: Average. Mitigating measures are taken: Training materials and executive protocols that emphasize the need to monitor and record observations in the health situation affecting women, men, boys, and girls. The Qatar Red Crescent will, as far as possible, request or advocate the use of data and information disaggregated by sex and age to monitor variables in access to health services that disproportionately affect one or more gender groups or age groups
