Project Summary

This intervention will contribute to enhance access to agricultural and farming livelihoods
for vulnerable Palestinians in West Bank (WB) to support self-sufficiency agricultural and enhance the potential and sustainability of livelihoods. It aims at addressing the priority
needs identified by the Food Security Sector Cluster (FSS) committing to the Strategic
Objective SO3. Within this
framework, WeWorld-GVC (WW-GVC) and the Applied Research Institute-Jerusalem
(ARIJ) designed a complementary intervention that capitalizes on each partner expertise
and resources. Both organizations had different meetings with Ministry of Agriculture to discuss and approve the communities and to the agree on the proposed activities. This was approved by MoA October 2nd, 2022. FSS cluster were updated about this approval. The proposed intervention will contribute to local plans in
mitigating the risks faced by communities and support resilience building towards
protracted threats and vulnerabilities. Safe access to resources will be sponsored to
sustain income generation for households (HHs) with disrupted livelihoods due to
intensification of demolition and confiscation of agricultural structures and resources, Specifically, the intervention will increase the access to
natural resources through agricultural land/road/water resources rehabilitation. Also, by
providing inputs and services, it will increase the productivity of producers both in quantity
and in quality (hygiene/safer/less-time-consuming processes). Lastly, marginalized
communities’ contracting power on rights and needs in front of duty-bearers will be
sustained through advocacy awareness sessions. Priority will be given for females, youth
and people with special needs. Hence, the action unfolds on 3 pillars:
1. Restoration of access to natural resources and improving agro-practices: 90 dunum
agricultural land in 2 communities, 7 km road rehabilitation in 4 communities; 2 wells in 2 communities, 16 cisterns in 2 communities, inputs for dryland farming for 625 dunum in 5 communities; 25 baby greenhouses in 3 communities.
2. Support to livelihood sustainability: 73 sheep and goat sheds rehabilitation in 6
communities; 1 automatic milk feeding machine for newborn in one community; 10 hybrid feeders in 1 community; 25 sheep feeders with milking barriers in 2 communities; 30 food processing assets for livestock-breeders in 3 communities; s;
3. Multi-scale (community, local/national authorities, international actors) advocacy:

group advocacy trainings in 15 communities; elaboration/dissemination of 5 food
security and livelihood factsheets at governorate level;1 advocacy/dissemination event with relevant stakeholders; elaboration of an advocacy tool-kit.
The action will benefit 750 HHs in 29 communities mostly in Area C for a total of 3,403 direct beneficiaries (1,016 women, 1,037 men, 659 girls, 656 boys, 86 elders of which 50
PWD) in 7 governorates: Hebron, Bethlehem, Ramallah, Jerusalem, Jordan Valley (Jericho), Salfit and
Tulkarem. The action serves beyond mere material needs by addressing broader issues of
safety and dignity in order to promote gender and age responsive protection, equitable
participation and access to services while preventing potential harm on different
population groups.
The partnership will capitalize on the partners’ experience in such sectorial interventions as well as outreach capacity to communities and relevant stakeholders. WW-GVC is a WASH-expert and is the focal point within the WB Protection Consortium (WBPC) for 169 communities in Area C while ARIJ has an expertise in promoting socioeconomic
development through enhancing access to food and natural resources by promoting
innovative and sustainable interventions.

1 - Needs

In the published mid- year reports for 2022 concerning the food security situation in Palestine, can see that food security continues to be affected by the ongoing conflict, economic stagnation, restrictions on trade and access to resources, and high unemployment and poverty rates. In addition to that, the Lack of means to get healthy food gap due to the national and global economic recovery from COVID-19 pandemic. The global crisis in food and energy due to the Russia- Ukraine conflict and war, had impacted on global level the supply chain disruption which are affecting the food insecurity. This global situation in addition to the local economic and political conditions had led to adaptation of negative coping mechanisms and strategies which had led to increase of vulnerable people. An estimated 1.8 million individuals (33.6% of the population) suffer from moderate to severe food insecurity. In 2022, approximately 1 out of 3 people experienced some form of food insecurity, 84% (1.54 million people) of which are expected to need humanitarian assistance. 77 per cent of those in need of humanitarian assistance are in the Gaza Strip (1.19 million people), and 23 per cent in the West Bank (353 thousand people). 81 per cent of those in West Bank (about 285 thousand people) are in Area A and B. People with a female-headed household are found to be more in need of assistance than people headed by males (29% against 26%).sustainable multi-sectorial/stakeholder/scale actions designed with community members to address the specific coercive environment. This tool is built on the Community Protection Approach (CPA)–- a participatory quantitative/qualitative data-collection process implemented by WW-GVC that informs a contextual and multi-sectorial protection analysis to identify the protection risks experienced at community and Age Gender and Diversity (AGD) level and design sustainable solutions–- and was used to identify priority activities. ARIJ instead has developed community vulnerability spatial and statistical information system at community level, which assists in identifying the most vulnerable communities based on socioeconomic, demographic, geopolitical and vulnerability data. The assessments conducted within the course of 2022 by WW-GVC and ARIJ highlighted the persistence of a coercive environment, increasing protection threats and movement restrictions, restricted access to basic natural resources, livelihood inputs and services. Also, the targeted communities were found vulnerable to climate change as well as drought conditions and suffering from limited access to water or affected by the decrease of annual precipitation and bad distribution of rainfalls, as well as the degradation in the biomass of grazing areas. Hence, there is urgent demand for immediate response via agricultural/land rehabilitation, access to water for agriculture, provision of inputs for production (seeds, animal fodder, livestock shelter and management tools, irrigation networks, processing tools) and access to more sources of water and markets in order to enable farmers to access quality products and essential resources to sustain their livelihoods. Moreover, an in-depth field assessment update, jointly conducted by WW-GVC and ARIJ in September 2022, further identified urgent needs of food security protection and livelihood sustainability at HH and community level. This assessment, along with instrumental coordination with the MoA and its directorates in the target Governorates, confirmed the substantial gap in food security and safe access to necessary resources to sustain agricultural livelihoods (such as land, water and agricultural and farming inputs and tools) in, at least, 29 priority communities, 86.0% of which are located in Area C, across 7 Governorates: Bethlehem (7 communities), Hebron (5 communities), Jerusalem (7 communities), Jericho (3 communities), Ramallah (2 communities), Salfit (1 community), Tulkarem (4 communities). Among these communities, 750 HHs in 29 communities mostly in Area C for a total of 3,403 direct beneficiaries (1,016 women, 1,037men, 659girls, 656 boys, 86 elders of which 50 PWD) were found in need of immediate assistance to increase and sustain their food security and livelihood. Furthermore, the selected communities and interventions matching the Directorate’s and MoA’s developmental and response plans were discussed and approved by the agricultural directorates within each targeted governorate. The aim is to enhance the resilience of Palestinian farmers and developing the agriculture production of the selected communities. The proposed activities reflect the geographical priorities identified within each Strategic Objective of the FSS. Hence, WW-GVC and ARIJ identified 3 main priority needs: 1. Access to natural resources disrupted by the coercive environment and movement restrictions resulting from the military occupation of the WB; 2. Support to agricultural/farming disrupted livelihoods due to the protracted crisis, intensification of demolitions and confiscation of agricultural structures and resources, climate change and Covid19; Need of coordination with local and national institutions and duty-bearers to better respond to the food security and livelihood needs of vulnerable Palestinians as right-holders.

The target communities were prioritized based on vulnerability, needs and scarcity of natural resources coupled with lack of responses and production inputs and limited purchasing power. The activities are clustered under 3 major themes: 1. Restoration of safe access to natural resources; 2. Support to agricultural/farming livelihoods; 3. 3 - Indicators

WW-GVC and ARIJ will monitor the work progress and quality during implementation and will appoint one Monitoring & Evaluation focal point each for the project. The delivery and quality control will be ensured through regular field visits to the sites. Staff will monitor progress on agreed indicators and verify the validity of reported data through review of means of verification. These measures will take into account beneficiaries’ participation and reporting throughout implementation and monitoring. Also, the monitoring implies a participatory approach that includes group feedback sessions and individual interviews (with family members’ with and without disability) to assess to which level the identified activities were suitable to single beneficiaries as well as communities’ needs. After the completion of the intervention, the qualitative and socioeconomic impact of the initiative will be further assessed through focus groups, interviews, and surveys, collecting the feedback of different vulnerable groups (women, men, youth, elderlies, PWD and their caretakers). The proposed intervention is expected to improve the overall protection of the targeted communities through improving their access to livelihood resources and inputs that will contribute to food security sustainability as well as resilience in the face of displacement as well as to contribute in building their awareness and mechanisms of approach regarding group advocacy approach towards the Duty-bearers. Lastly, both direct beneficiaries and community members will have access to an anonymized Complaint Response Mechanism jointly managed by ARIJ and WW-GVC throughout all the duration and beyond the time of the intervention. 9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Complaint response mechanism is part of WW-GVC and ARIJ accountability and commitment strategies to guarantee transparency, fairness and equity towards all our beneﬁciaries. It aims at establishing a system that gives individuals and groups the possibility to report complaints or suggestions in a secured way and receive a response in a timely manner. All complaints are reported to the CRM focal point through a hotline number. Upon registration of the complaint on the database, the focal point analyses complaints and classiﬁes each of them according to the internal classiﬁcation system, then the complaint should be resolved within 15 days by WW-GVC or ARIJ team. Once the case is solved, the complainant will receive a phone call by WW-GVC’s or ARIJ’s MEAL oﬃcer. The field staff are obliged to inform the direct or indirect beneficiaries of the presence of a complaint-response mechanism (CRM), and the referral pathway for their complaints, and to provide the contact information for the CRM focal points depending in the type of the complaint (female focal point for GBV and PSEA, and male focal point for other types of complaints). Beneficiaries are informed that the complaints are to be kept confidential specifically for sensitive issues, and they will be dealt with accordingly. The FGDs and the other communication channels designed by the field staff allows them to reach the highest number of beneficiaries in a targeted population.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

A dedicated hotline for complaints from beneficiaries and their communities will be set up by ARIJ and WW-GVC for the duration of the project and for up to six months after it ends (as such mechanism is being implemented by both organizations as part of the responsibilities of their M&E departments).. This will complement WW-GVC’s Complains Response Mechanism (CRM), accountability and commitment to guarantee transparency, fairness and equity towards all our beneficiaries. It aims at establishing a system that gives individuals and groups the possibility to report complaints, suggestions and inquiries in a secured way, and receive a response in a timely manner. All complaints/feedback are reported to the WW-GVC’s and ARIJ’s Monitoring and Coordination manager by field staff, project managers and/or focal points who refer the case to relevant staff according to it sensitivity. If the complaint requires follow up, the complainant will receive an answer within 15 calendar days. Once the case is solved, the complainant will receive a phone call by WW-GVC’s and ARIJ Monitoring and Coordination manager. Upon registration of the complaint on the database, the Admin officer analyses complaints and classifies each of them according to the internal classification system, then the complaint should be resolved within 15 days by WW-GVC and ARIJ team and/or CRM team.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

In all interviews, surveys, focus groups and field visits, beneficiaries of all population groups will be informed about the complaint mechanism in place and modalities of accessing it, Also, the beneficiaries’ agreements will contain special part about the complaint mechanisms including the dedicated number for this subject. In-person complaints may be made at any time when the personnel of the two organizations is on site visits, or complaints may be made remotely by calling a special hotline. The latter will guarantee a discrete and anonymous way to submit a complaint, ensuring that all demographic groups may use the system.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Response is carefully designed to ensure that interventions will not impair beneficiaries’ safety or expose them to further risk. Through the Community Protection Approach all community members will be involved in all the project phases. A thorough assessment will be conducted with each community to further investigate and understand the needs, risks and vulnerabilities of the diverse social groups, assuring the compliance with the Do No Harm principle, so that the rehabilitation works are based on the preferences and needs expressed by the members of the HHs, considering the different threats that affect girls, boys, women and men of all ages including PWD. Additionally, livelihood support and land/road rehabilitation intervention will be designed to comply with protection principles, such as meaningful access and accountability towards benefciariesbeneficiaries, taking into account the diverse needs, roles and dynamics of women and men, age and disability status, in each community. Furthermore, when possible, household members will work in the rehabilitation work, so the sense of dignity and ownership will also be improved. The field team participated in the protection cluster GBV training for improving their capacities in detecting and referring cases. Moreover, the field team also participated in a training with War Child to better detect and refer children violence. The toolkits for AGD and PWD are currently being mainstreamed by WWGVC into the community-based protection methodology. The WWGVC and ARIJ teams shall adhere to protective measures (wearing face masks, using hand sanitizers, and maintaining physical distances) during COVID-19 outbreaks during all project activities to stop the spread of the illness.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The Community Based Protection Approach (CPA) is a participatory methodology based on WW-GVC operational interpretation of the IASC definition of protection. An environment fully conducive to protection is obtained when multi-sector humanitarian and development needs of individuals and communities are met in a dignified and safe manner. As such, multi-sector needs are identified and ranked on the basis of the contextual protection risks of communities and individuals. A right-based analysis is used as entry point to elaborate local protection strategies to be then used as a programming framework for the definition of needs-based responses. The CPA was developed according to IHL, Humanitarian, Do No Harm and the following fundamental principles: 1. the safety of affected population (both of groups and individuals); 2. Restore the dignity of affected population putting a stop to emerging or established patterns of violations (and/or alleviating their immediate effects); 3. place the empowerment of communities at risk at the core of any action to enable self-protection and realize their rights. The use of focus groups, random interviews and transect walks, ensures the involvement of the population in terms of location, age, gender, ethnicity, diversity, profession and power. According to the access to the population, the level of trust built and other do not harm considerations, multiple specific techniques will be applied to get deeper insight (bias reduction and/or data gap filling) of a particular group, a specific situation, a particular household. Throughout the assessment and planning phases, the needs of all community members will be taken into consideration. The proposed food security and livelihood interventions address the urgent need of the targeted communities, accounting for non-discriminatory practices and considering the needs of every age, gender, and level of ability. Providing a voice for each member of the community, and requesting their direct action in the project’s implementation, preserves the dignity of each beneficiary and fosters community empowerment. The distribution of Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) cards in the communities will ensure the accessibility to all beneficiaries to provide their feedbacks and complaints about the implemented project.
