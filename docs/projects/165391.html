Project Summary

The activities under Sector Objective 1 aim to give access to livelihood opportunities to vulnerable households (HHs), while at the same time improving local infrastructure relevant for Agriculture and, more broadly, the livelihoods sector. Activities include the provision of short-term labour opportunities to skilled and unskilled workers, supporting 642 HHs, small business opportunities for 150 HHs and support to small-medium enterprises for 30 HHs. These activities are complementary to the Agricultural- and Livestock-based activities included in the submission for the FSA sector.
Temporary labour opportunities will involve the completion of essential works, both those prioritised by the local council and benefitting the wider community in the target locations, particularly in relation to agricultural infrastructure (e.g. seed storage, agricultural roads, livestock water points, etc.) as well as providing increased income to the households participating in the Action. It is anticipated that with the reinvigoration of agro-pastoralist livelihoods there will be continued demand for agricultural labour beyond the project. In addition, supporting small-scale income generating activities, and small/medium businesses and market actors to re-start or expand their activities will contribute to business development, generate income and enable them to sustain their operations, contributing to the functioning of the local market. Capacity building and training of local stakeholders in improved and sustainable agricultural practices as well as the involvement of local councils and extension workers throughout project activities will ensure that knowledge and capacity gained will remain within the community with a view to building sustainability at household and community level.
The project will involve both IDPs and host communities, selected based on selection criteria. SC will focus primarily on A&Y who have experienced chronic level of deprivation, adopted harmful coping mechanisms, have been recruited as fighters, or have been subjected to early marriages and other exploitative practices. Due to the overarching protection nature of the strategy and of the needs identified in Syria we will prioritize A&Y who are the primary breadwinner in their HH, those who have children in the 0 to 3 age bracket, those who have been recently demobilized and displacement-affected A&Y (IDPs and refugee returnees). Furthermore, special attention will be given to supporting sustainable and equitable livelihoods opportunities to all A&Y in areas where displaced populations are seeking to (re)integrate.

4 - Needs

The crisis in Syria has led to a dire economic situation in the country. Levels of unemployment are high (increasing from 14.9% in 2011 to 50% in 2018), and people previously outside of work force (such as elderly, Youth and adolescents (Y&A), women and children) have the need to access jobs and other livelihood opportunities to cover for the significant increase in cost of living. To address family basic needs children, A&Y tend to engage in negative coping mechanisms such as child marriage, child recruitment, survival sex and child labour. A&Y had to take on adult roles out of the necessity to take care of their family’s basic needs. In many families A&Y as young as 15 or 16 years old were the primary breadwinner. Many A&Y are already married, have children and some are even divorced or widowed. A vast majority of A&Y are out OOS with the highest rate of OOS youth in central Syria with 95% of boys not attending any form of education. Access to livelihoods is consistently mentioned as a key concern by recent returnees (IDPs and refugees) as well as those planning to return to their places of origin. Most of the A&Y have been OOS for an average of 4 years leaving them with very limited technical and employment skills including a complete lack of transferable skills. Furthermore, non-traumatic war-related daily stressors such as the breakdown of support networks or increased poverty and inability to meet basic needs, has impacted the mental well-being of A&Y. In Deir ez Zour, all but one subdistrict in the 2019 HNO shows a severity ranking of at least 4 of 6. In total the governorate has almost 400,000 Syrians with livelihoods needs. Ar-Raqqa also has roughly 350,000 people in need of livelihoods support, (HNO 2019) with the ar-Raqqa subdistrict with the most people in need. In Hassakeh, SC looks to respond to the needs of over 400,000 Syrians with insufficient livelihood opportunities.

5 - Implementation of activities

Save the Children will identify A&Y champions within the community who will work to promote inclusive gender sensitive working opportunities as well as a space for A&Y to meet with like minded entrepreneurs to understand better career opportunities and labor market needs. Furthermore, an action research will be conducted to look at the trickle down effects that addressing Mh-PSS needs of children affected by the conflict has in refusing negative coping mechanisms and engaging in safe working opportunities. Save the Children will work with A&Y to provide them with the information on their employment rights and safe working conditions to enable them to advocate for their right to basic need and decent livelihoods. Save the children will also create a space for A&Y to influence and shape the next HRP to ensure that A&Y perspectives are taken into account and reflected in the broader humanitarian strategy. Save the Children will work with A&Y as well as with private sector and vocational training agencies to provide skills development opportunities which focuses on but not limited to life skills, financial literacy, entrepreneurship, etc. Save the children will also work to identify policy, social and physical barriers preventing meaningful and gender inclusive access to vocational training opportunities and labor market. Save the Children will work with A&Y in their community to create safe, protective, accessible and inclusive spaces designed in consultation with them. Furthermore to address the Mh-PSS needs of A&Y a mentoring and peer support system that caters for youth and adolescents’ individualized PSS needs will be created including catering for A&Y with different displacement history and backgrounds to actively promote peaceful coexistence.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

(S&S) All staff movements must be pre-approved and refresher security trainings are regularly conducted. The Hostile Environment Awareness Training is mandatory for NES deployments; local staff have been trained on Advanced Personal S&S. SC’s partners engage with relevant stakeholders and receive timely updates. SC works on a common response to unacceptable conditions. Airstrikes SC supports partners to immediately report security incidents to be analysed and to develop and implement comprehensive safety plans. Loss of access SC and partners conduct thorough security assessments and maintain updated contingency plans. If necessary, SC will suspend programs and work to resume activities. If this is not viable, SC would assess possibilities for relocation. Only in a worst-case scenario would project termination be considered. Work permits Measures are in place to mitigate the Gov. of Turkey (GoT) legislative environment hindering SC’s ability to operate, I.e. using a third-party HR company to overcome work permit challenges. Diversion of resources to terrorist/ proscribed groups In 2018, interviews were conducted to identify areas for increased risk mitigation; an action plan was developed. SC launched an initiative with OCHA to provide external training on international humanitarian law and negotiation with armed actors. Partners have their own systems of anti-terrorism vetting checks for all contracted suppliers and SC’s Syria office vets against the third-party database for procurements $750+. Shelling Whilst the most frequent attacks of shelling take place in southern rural Idlib and northern rural Hama, no area is immune. Escalation of military activity at Turkey/Syria border SC program locations and contingency plans are under constant review and the Hibernation, Relocation and Evacuation plan is shared with staff. Offices and guesthouses are located away from military bases and have the necessary S&S infrastructure.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Barriers to access for marginalized groups Mitigating measures: Specific measures are taken to ensure input is received from marginalized groups such as women and girls, children with disabilities and the elderly. Feedback is used to inform planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluating activities. Mitigation measures are brainstormed with the community to ensure that girls and boys (incl. children with disabilities) have equal access to SC healthcare. Monitoring Mechanism: FGDs, feedback mechanism Risk: SCI or partner staff violating SCI CSG rules harms children; damages the reputation of the organisation; and can lead to loss of funding and prosecution. Mitigating measures: All SC staff, volunteers and representatives sign the Child Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct, child safeguarding risk assessments are conducted for all project sites and mitigating actions and put in place, all staff are trained on the Child Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct. Partners have received CSG training via Hurras. Partners have their own reporting mechanism as well as CSG focal points in the field and main CSG focal point in Turkey. Risk: Affected populations and SC staff and volunteers, are placed at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, including harassment. Mitigating measures: Training will be provided to SC staff and volunteers on SC’s Anti-Harassment Policy and Code of Conduct. SC will also continue to provide trainings on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) through SC’s PSEA focal points.
