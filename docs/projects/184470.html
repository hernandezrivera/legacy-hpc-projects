Project Summary

The population of people in need of humanitarian assistance in Somalia has been on a steady increase. Estimates show that of the 7.7 million people in need in 2022, 6.4 million will be in need of Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) support. Repeated cycles of drought and floods, further exacerbated by the spread of COVID 19 and resurgent conflict in the country, have resulted in disease (cholera/Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) outbreaks, severe water shortage, inadequate and non-functional water sources and sanitation facilities, and poor water supply systems. Those affected include refugees, refugee returnees, internally displaced persons (IDPs), host communities who DRC refer to as displacement affected communities. Among these groups are individuals with particular vulnerabilities such as women and girls, children, persons living with disabilities, the elderly and members of minority groups. Assessments conducted by the WASH Cluster, for example, point to the specific and growing vulnerabilities of women and girls who are at high risk of gender based violence (GBV) as they travel long distances to fetch water for household consumption due to insufficient and inadequate WASH facilities.
In light of these challenges, DRC proposes to support 236,050 individuals (34,342 men, 39,809 women, 83,604 boys, 78, 295 girls) through provision of emergency and sustainable WASH interventions that will improve water access, reduce the risk of mortality and disease, and improve overall WASH outcomes. DRC will prioritize flood prone and drought risk areas in Somalia and especially those not receiving sufficient WASH support. Furthermore, DRC will streamline gender and disability in its programming ensuring that the specific needs and vulnerabilities of women and girls and persons living with disabilities are catered to. Prior to commencing any implementation, DRC will conduct a needs assessment that will feed into the final project locations and priorities. This will also feed into the beneficiary selection criteria that DRC will adopt that will ensure only the most vulnerable groups are targeted. Through this proposed response, DRC will provide both emergency and longer term sustainable access to WASH Support. Under emergency response, and informed by the drought projections of 2022, DRC will undertake emergency water trucking especially in areas where people rely on rain water to avoid displacements, fabrication, provide hygiene and sanitation kits, construct disability friendly emergency latrines, and conduct rapid hygiene promotion. In a bid to support more sustainable water access, DRC will rehabilitate critical water supply infrastructures such as boreholes with a clear focus on optimizing outputs through upgrading of water supply systems. As part of its market based approach to WASH, DRC will establish partnerships with private and government water source owners to extend water services to settlements to areas where displacement affected communities reside. Community members will then be trained on the operation and maintenance of rehabilitated WASH infrastructure as part of building buy in by the community as well as increasing a sense of ownership within the community. In DRC’s experience, this has resulted in more care and upkeep of rehabilitated assets and longer-term service of these assets. Where distribution of hygiene kits is seen to be a more emergency type of intervention, hygiene promotion is considered to be a longer-term intervention that seeks to influence positive behavior uptake regarding personal and community sanitation and hygiene. In light of the current third wave of COVID 19, DRC will distribute hygiene kits coupled with COVID 19 message shared in mass media and through Information, Education and Communication (IEC materials). Lastly, DRC will support the desludging of latrines and construction of new latrines and handwashing stations that will support solid waste management and promote good hygiene and sanitation

1. - Projects activities

DRC will undertake both emergency and longer term WASH support activities. These include:1. Emergency water trucking with temporary distribution systems for 3 months’ minimum. DRC will also provide household water treatment and safe storage for the duration of water trucking. This will be provided to 3,000 households in Beletweyne, Belet Xawa, Baidoa and North Galkcaayo.2. Provision of 3,000 portable water tanks for household storage and transport following water trucking in Beletweyne, Belet Xawa, Baidoa and North Galkcaayo3. Monitoring of water quality at household and/or community level and distribution of aqua tabs4. Construction and rehabilitation of 8 strategic boreholes in Mogadishu (2), Baidoa (1), Guri Ceel in Galmudug (1), Beletweyne in Hirshabelle (1), North Galkcaayo in Mudug (2), and Lughaya in Somaliland (1). These will supplement water production through a functional hybrid system. Through this, DRC will meet the demand of 15 liters per person per day as per WASH guidelines5. Establishment and training of Water Management Committees on Operation and Maintenance (O&M) of rehabilitated water supply. DRC will seek to ensure that at least 50% of the members of these committees are women and 10% people living with disabilities. This will be done in all locations6. Construction and desludging of 1,150 sustainable, culturally appropriate and gender sensitive latrines for vulnerable displacement affected populations.7. Fabrication and installation of 1,150 metallic foot-operated hand washing facilities at strategic locations in identified project location that can easily be accessed by all members of the community as part of COVID 19 prevention measures8. Fecal sludge management systems to restore/maintain sanitation systems especially during disease outbreaks such as AWD and Cholera9. Distribution of hygiene and sanitation kits to vulnerable households containing essential items such as soap, aqua tabs, jerry cans among other items10. Hygiene promotion activities through working with identified community hygiene promoters. This will be supported by the distribution of IEC materials that will be located in high traffic areas within the community to raise awareness on personal and community hygiene and sanitation practices11. Awareness raising on disaster risk reduction through working with existing community structures to identify key community needs and action plans

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

In the locations that DRC is proposing to work in, issues of land dispute and general risks to violence remain prevalent. Displacement affected communities comprise both displaced and non-displaced populations but research and evidence points to lack of sufficient access to WASH for both groups of people. In working to mitigate these risks, DRC will bring support much closer to the target beneficiaries with the aim of reducing the risk of interference by external stakeholders in key processes such as beneficiary selection and registration as well as identification of key WASH infrastructure to be rehabilitated. In addition, DRC's tailored and robust beneficiary selection criteria will ensure that only the most vulnerable are identified for support and that peaceful co-existence emanates from this process. DRC will ensure that project sensitization activities and other project tools to be employed over the life cycle of the project is shared with community members beforehand to get their buy in and also ensure that they understand the intended project outcomes. Specific vulnerabilities such as those for women, children, and persons living with disabilities will be streamlined throughout the project, including in the membership of the communities, the design of WASH infrastructure, and the day to day management of rehabilitated assets. DRC will also work alongside other partners to advocate on the issue of housing, land and property rights, especially for IDPs which has long been an issue in Somalia. DRC as a member of the Protection Cluster will lobby the local authorities to allocate land for IDPs through reasonable lease agreements to minimize the risk of eviction during programming. DRC will also support these beneficiaries with other complementary projects that seek to increase their protection space and rights.
