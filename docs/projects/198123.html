Project Summary

North-east Nigeria, a region already enduring a protracted humanitarian crisis of over 13 years, with 8.4 million people in need (HNO,2022) has been severely affected by the worst flooding Nigeria has seen in a decade. The flooding has exacerbated existing vulnerabilities for people already impacted by conflict, displacement and severe food insecurity and malnutrition.
The identified needs in the target locations include the prevalence of open defecation occasioned by occurrence of acute watery diarrhea/cholera incidences, high levels of malnutrition, acute water shortages, water quality concerns, poor access to sanitation facilities, low levels of knowledge in good hygiene practices, and sustainability of the WASH infrastructures, lack of access to hygiene items including menstrual hygiene management items and loss of livelihoods. This project aims to promote the personal and public well-being of affected populations through improved access to basic water, sanitation, and Hygiene services that will avert or reduce the incidences of acute watery diarrheal diseases/cholera. The project seeks to also strengthen livelihood and economic activities in targeted locations.
The project will enhance access to safe and sustainable WASH services to 12,000 men, women, girls and boys including persons living with disabilities. WASH services will include the installation of WASH infrastructure to existing and emerging caseloads through new constructions, maintenance of WASH infrastructures including rehabilitation/upgrading and reticulation of water facilities where feasible and if needed, regular chlorination, and monitoring of free residual chlorine at the water points and households, conduct door to door hygiene promotion sessions, focused group discussions on Menstrual hygiene Management, motorized campaigns, provision of hygiene and menstrual hygiene management kits including replenishments. and the training of beneficiaries on soap and reusable sanitary pad production to enhance continuous access to hygiene items, rebuild and restore livelihoods and strengthen local markets. JDF will establish WASH clubs in schools in the location.
This project will adopt behavior change approaches like Community Led Total Sanitation. In order to increase sustainable access to improved household sanitation at scale in target locations and close the huge sanitation access gaps and promote a community-driven process however emergency sanitation facilities will be constructed in Internally Displaced person's camps.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in North East Nigeria has displaced millions, devastated livelihoods including agricultural activities, and has brought and continues to bring about a humanitarian emergency of protection, water sanitation and hygiene, and nutrition among others. The Nigerian Humanitarian Needs Overview has projected that 8.4 a million people (of whom 2.2 million are internally displaced) are in severe need of humanitarian and protection assistance in 2022. The report specifically highlighted that the well-being and living standards of an estimated 3.03 million people in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY States) will be adversely affected by shortages of clean water, and poor sanitation and hygiene practices.The needs severity of North East Nigeria as captured by the WASH Sector PiN and HRP targets 2022 by state shows that 1,611,937 in Borno and 536136 people in Yobe including women and children remain in need of acute and high humanitarian Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) Services requiring lifesaving responses. The WASH needs severity Map categorizes Mafa and Yunusari LGAs in Borno and Yobe states for example as Under Acute and High humanitarian needs respectively. The PiN estimates that 64,805 people including women (22%) and children (59%) in Mafa and 31,862 including children (60%) and women (21%) in Yunusari lack access to basic WASH services that require life-saving responses. Results of MSNA conducted by JDF across the LGA show that 52% of residents In Simari are IDPs who fled their communities while 38% are host communities and 10% are returnees. Water shortages characterized by lack of water containers, long travel times of a minimum of 20-30 minutes to collect water and non-treatment of water remain major challenges. 71% of respondents do not treat water. A majority of respondents resort to buying water and collecting water from other unprotected sources as coping mechanisms to lack of water. Insufficient (24%), lack of privacy (14%) no safety (20%) crowded, damaged facilities (23%) were identified as major sanitation gaps in Simari. Indiscriminate disposal of solid waste is common practice in the location. Lack of access to soap 71% of respondents do not have access to soap as they cannot afford soap due to loss of livelihood. 49%of respondents WASH their hands with only water while 37% have not heard hygiene promotion messages in the last 90days. Open defecation was observed in the location.40% of affected populations according to the WASH HRP PIN 2022 are adolescent girls and women of reproductive age an indication of the need for menstrual hygiene management items. Identified WASH gaps across the two states include high prevalence of open (87%) driven by the absence of latrines and toilets, damaged facilities due to flood and wind storms, unhygienic facilities, open disposal of child feces (60%) and visible signs of open defecation observed around households during assessments. Assessed communities in Yunusari are experiencing water shortages. Respondents do not collect enough water for drinking and other domestic purposes due to long queuing time at the water point, and long travel time (round trip) to collect water while 42% responded to experiencing water scarcity. A majority of respondents across all communities do not treat their water and those who do use boiling and filtration method highlighting the need for improved water treatment using chlorination.56% of respondents say they use only water and sometimes ash for handwashing, an indication of limited access to soap for handwashing across communities of interest. 49% did not present soap upon request as the majority of households said they could not afford soap mainly due to low income and lack of livelihood or income-generating activity

2 - Activities

water, sanitation and hygiene are critical first responses to help protect people from disease outbreaks. Jireh Doo Foundation (JDF) will therefore be responsible for the direct implementation of WASH activities that address lifesaving needs and improve long term access to WASH services. The project will seek to improve immediate access to adequate clean drinking water to at least 20 liters per person per day throughout the period of the response and beyond with the potential of upgrading the reach to 100% as those affected become more stabilized in line with sphere water quantity standards through the construction and rehabilitation of existing water facilities,reticulation/extension of pipelines where necessary,water quality monitoring including routinee water testing and treatment to ensure drinking water meets World Health Organization(WHO) and Nigerian Standard for Drinking Water Quality (NSDWQ) standard, operation and maintenance (O&M) by trained community structures like, water chlorinators, water and sanitation committee(WASHCOMS), Village level operation maintenance (VLOM)and the prepositioning of borehole spare parts in implementing locations. O&M activities will include but not limited to daily water chlorination and monitoring of free residual chlorine at water points and households, disinfections, repairs of broken-down water facilities, sensitization on the people on sustained usage of Water facilitiesAccess to safe sanitation will be improved through the construction of emergency latrines and the rehabilitation of existing ones where permissible in internally displaced persons camps and institutions like schools, temporary learning spaces and health facilities in implementing locations. Views of women and girls will be considered in the siting of sanitation facilities. Latrine cleaning kits will be procured and distributed to schools and Primary health Centers in the location.Regular desludging of filled up latrines will be carried out to prevent and or reduce the human contact with feces.At household levels, project will adopt behaviour change approaches like Community Led Total Sanitation (CLTs) approach in order to increase sustainable access to improved household sanitation at scale in target locations and close the huge sanitation access gaps and promote a community-driven process. Hygiene risk communication and other hygiene promotional activities will adopt a mix of approaches like door-to-door visits, Case Area Target Intervention (CATI), Focused Group Discussions and mass campaigns. Hygiene activities will further be supported by the use of Information, Education and Communication materials and the provision of hygiene items including menstrual.In the event of a cholera outbreak JDF will establish rapid response teams (RRT) to provide immediate and timely response with the potential to reduce or and or ‘slow down’ wide spread transmission. Through the systematic use of surveillance systems and available epidemiological data, RRTs will target affected households and at-risk populations in the community

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Jireh Doo Foundation (JDF) as an organization with a heart for humanity and desire for effective and efficient project delivery and keen on upward and especially downward accountability is a member of the CHS Alliance and is also an active member of the AAP/CEAL Working group. JDF will adopt an innovative approach to accountability to affected persons (AAP): The Community-Based Accountability and Feedback System (CoBAFS). CoBAFS will have two basic components, 1. The CoBAFS reporting library 2. The CoBAFS feedback committee AKA Community-Based Accountability Committee (CoBAC). Under the CoBAFS reporting library, JDF will set up a project library (a physical metal cabinet) in an agreed safe location (Bulama, Lawan, etc.) in its implementing community and an accountability board. The project library will contain JDFs project document including a large print implementation work plan. JDFs monthly reports will also be kept in the library for community access. JDF with support from Translators Without Boarders, will convert all her monthly reports into colorful pictorial animations and charts in local languages such as can easily be understood by persons of all educational backgrounds including children. These animated reports will be produced and distributed house to house for persons with disability (PWD) as would have been identified during community entry and engagement activities.The CoBAC will also be responsible for collecting complaints and feedback that will be presented during the monthly meetings, other pressing challenges that cannot wait for the monthly meetings will still have other CRMs available (Suggestion boxes, toll free lines, etc.) as agreed by the community during the community inception meeting. At the end of the project cycle, the CoBAC with support from JDF and AAP/CEAL WG will organize a close out workshop. This will be an opportunity to review the project, feedback meetings and document learnings
