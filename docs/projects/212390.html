Project Summary

The project responds to the urgent need to combat malnutrition among vulnerable populations, specifically targeting children under 5 years old and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in conflict-affected areas of Idleb and Aleppo governorates. Malnutrition is a pervasive issue in these regions, exacerbated by food insecurity, limited access to essential healthcare services, and inadequate nutrition education. Without intervention, malnutrition poses significant risks to the health and well-being of individuals, particularly children, leading to increased morbidity and mortality rates.
This project will contribute to preventing and treating malnutrition by providing IYCF interventions, Micro-nutrient deficiency prevention, control, and improve equitable access to high-quality, lifesaving, curative nutrition services through early identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished children and PLW. The project will be implemented by nutrition Rapid Response Teams (RRTs) and health and nutrition staff in QC supported health facilities. The project duration is 12 months, and the estimated budget is USD 800,658 These distinctive RRTs will conduct HH visits and orientation sessions in areas populated with IDPs and affected people in both Idleb and Aleppo governorates. These RRTs will screen, refer, and follow up children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women who are malnourished and provide emergency IYCF counselling for mothers whose children are moderately malnourished. Moreover, RRTs and health staff will distribute nutrition supplements and micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children, pregnant and lactating women. Also, they will screen the children regarding general danger signs and educate their mothers about them. They will do hygiene promotion through their activities and will distribute IEC materials for targeted PLW, girls, and boys. There will be 12 nutrition RRTS teams bound to each of the following health facilities (PHC centers):
• Mastumeh PHC
• Sijer PHC
• Al Basota PHC
• Deir Siwan PHC
• Sandara PHC
• Qabasin PHC
• Bab Al Salama PHC
• Bzaa PHC
• Ar-Ra'ee PHC
• Zoghra PHC
• Suluk PHC
• Hammam At-Turkman PHC
• Additionally, Ar-Ra'ee hospital, which will serve as a stabilization center with a capacity of 10 beds for admission to treat complicated SAM cases.
Each team will provide basic nutrition services (MUAC screening, distribution micro-nutrient, high-energy biscuits, and case referral for treatment). The project duration is 12 months, and the target beneficiaries are 60,000 from IDPs and Host communities (including 40,00 from IDPs and 20,000 host communities). The primary target beneficiaries are children <5 years and PLW who will be provided by nutrition preventive and curative services. This project is an important foundation for integrated primary health and nutrition services and community-based outreach activities, it will include a network of RRT, to provide integrated health and nutrition services focusing on IYCF counseling and screening of children under 5 and referral for treatment of malnutrition at a community level. These RRT will be linked with 12 PHC centers.
The project will also focus on building the capacity of the health and nutrition staff on IYCF counseling to provide integrated health and nutrition services. The health facility staff and RRT will be trained in aspects related to CMAM, IYCF and nutritional screening and referral, they will also be provided with sufficient education to help to raise the awareness of local communities.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection Risk Analysis (PRA):The Protection Risk Analysis (PRA) conducted as part of the project aims to systematically identify and assess potential risks to beneficiaries, particularly in the context of protection from exploitation, abuse, and violence. This analysis involves comprehensive consultations with affected communities, stakeholders, and experts to understand the complex protection challenges they face. Through the PRA process, the project will identify vulnerable groups, assess their specific protection needs, and prioritize actions to mitigate risks and ensure their safety and dignity throughout the intervention. Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA):Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) measures are critical components of the project's commitment to upholding the highest standards of integrity and ethical conduct. These measures include robust vetting and training of staff and partners to prevent and respond to incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse. Clear reporting mechanisms will be established to allow beneficiaries to safely report any concerns or incidents, ensuring swift and appropriate action. Additionally, the project will promote a culture of respect, dignity, and accountability to safeguard the well-being of all individuals, particularly women and children. Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigation Measures:The project faces various risks, including security threats, logistical challenges, and community resistance, which could impede its successful implementation. Assumptions include the availability of adequate funding, cooperation from local authorities, and stable security conditions. To mitigate these risks, the project will implement comprehensive risk management strategies, including contingency planning, close coordination with relevant stakeholders, and regular monitoring and evaluation to identify and address emerging challenges. Flexibility and adaptability will be key to adjusting project activities in response to changing circumstances, ensuring that the project remains effective and responsive to the needs of beneficiaries. By addressing these components in detail, the project will not only effectively combat malnutrition but also uphold the principles of protection, accountability, and ethical conduct, ensuring the safety, dignity, and well-being of all individuals involved.
