Project Summary

The situation in Sudan is complex with a combination of protracted emergencies, political instability, and economic crisis. All populations in Sudan are at high risk of two or more vector-borne diseases and the emergence of mosquito-borne arboviral diseases in many states caused thousands of cases and deaths. In 2020, malaria continued to be the leading cause of morbidities and mortalities, representing an average of 15 – 20% from total consultations. In particular, the Anopheles stephensi spread and invasion is a growing public health concern in Sudan as it risks increasing the transmission of Malaria. With this project, WHO aims to reduce the transmission and the burden of arbo-viral diseases among the vulnerable populations through scale up of vector surveillance, support to chemical and physical vector control measures by procurement and supplies as much as operational costs, capacity building and support to community based vector control measures. Around one-third of the population in Sudan still practices open defecation, only 38.54% have access to improved sanitation. There is a huge gap for the technical expertise due to the high turnover of the staff resulting from the administrative changes and continuous exemption of MoH staff, and migration of trained staff to other countries. Most health facilities do not have strong WASH systems, with reported waste being mixed up with general waste and disposed of at a landfill or near to health facilities. The WHO, MOH, will implement water quality activities and WASH partners at the state and locality level with sustained support from WHO. WHO will continue the successful 2020 approach in strengthening the water quality system at localities levels to promote efficiency. WHO will continue to support identified vulnerable families in cholera hot spots to ensure water quality and safety at the household level and distribute mini water filtration units, whilst UNICEF and other WASH partners will distribute WASH supplies. . The overall project including integrated vector control and WASH interventions is targeting 2.4 million people for a total project budget of 9,565,000 USD.
WHO will ensure the ease of access to the targeted population, without any constraints such as discrimination, or any other restraints. WHO will apply the standard measures for the containment of vector control remedies to ensure safety and dignity and avoid causing harm. WHO through its capacity building program is delivering key messages on increasing community ownership and leadership to ensure efficiency and sustainability of the project. The team structure is well aligned and linked to community leaders, and the communication tree established for the work will facilitate receiving community complaints, with the intended sequence to the higher level ensuring privacy. This timely feedback is aiming to inform the community on the deficiencies, their impact on health, and the responsible body for undertaking corrective measures.
This project is targeting 2.4 million people. The total project budget is 9,565,000 USD. Vector transmitted arbo-viral diseases and unsafe access to drinking water are important factors contributing to the high disease -burden among vulnerable populations. With the proposed project and budget, WHO aims to ensure that early action can be assured in rainy season and high transmission period and aims to scale up interventions. As of its mandate, WHO will address the most urgent gaps in water quality assurance and vector control due to the limited capacity of the FMOH to deliver the required interventions for vulnerable populations.

1 - Needs

The situation in Sudan is complex with a combination of protracted emergencies, political instability, and economic crisis, and circulating covid-19. According to WHO 11% of the global burden due to vector-borne diseases is found in the Eastern Mediterranean, and 50% of the regional burden is found in Sudan. Based on the analysis done by WHO CO and the Region in 2020; all populations in Sudan are at high risk of two or more of vector borne diseases. Malaria continued to be the leading cause of morbidities and mortalities, with 1.7 Million cases in the first 10 months of 2020, representing an average of 15 – 20% of total medical consultations and affecting in particular vulnerable populations, and the epidemic threshold crossed in 16 states. A new invasive vector for Malaria, Anopheles stephensi, is identified in Kassala, Red Sea State and Gedaref, with risk to spread throughout Sudan, with potential public health concern as it increased malaria cases especially in urban and peri-urban settings where people practice prolonged water storage due to inadequate or irregular water supply. Vector surveillance data confirmed the expanded geo-spatial and temporal distribution of the Aedes mosquito, the vector responsible for arboviruses transmission has expanded to the Darfur region. In 2018, and 2019; a chikungunya outbreak affected 15 states, resulting into 48,763 cases. FMoH Emergency Operation Center reported 4 vector borne disease outbreak In 2020 including malaria, VHF, Chikungunya, and sus Dengue. Viral Hemorrhagic Fever with 2915 cases, 90 deaths, and a case fatality rate of 3%, were reported from 8 states and also chikungunya in 5 states with 601 cases and an attack rate of 1.12/10,000 population. Rift Valley Fever is also endemic with outbreak declared in 2019 resulting in 576 cases with a fatality rate of 1.9%. In 2020, less dengue cases than previous year were reported probably due to targeted vector control activities at high-risk areas. However, Sudan remains at risk of major dengue outbreaks. Women are most affected (61 - 66%) with arboviral diseases and special considerations will be taken to ensure protection of women and elder people as the vast of cases occurring in the middle age 20 - 50. Older adults with underlying diseases are at higher risk. Women will also be considered as key elements for the prevention of indoor breeding; and will be empowered with knowledge on preventing breeding and ensure personal protection is applied. Access to effective WASH and the practice of good sanitation and hygiene behaviors are critical for human health, dignity and economic development. As per the recent S3M survey; 32.80% of Sudan population still practices open defecation, 38.54% have access to improved sanitation , 60.5% have access to basic drinking water sources, and only 7.54% knowing the 5 key handwashing times. Water quality monitoring results from the second half of 2020 alone, showed that from 64,804 samples collected only 46% were within the acceptable range for quality and safety. Even though no cholera cases were reported in 2020, the environmental health risk factors for cholera outbreaks exist, and the risk of transmission is particularly elevated in rainy season, population movements inside Sudan and across borders due to intercommunal or armed conflicts in neighboring countries. The Recently published progress report on Global WASH in HCFs shown that a quarter of all HFs have no basic water services, which means 712 million people have no access to water when they use health care facilities. 10% of HCFs globally have no sanitation services and one in three do not have adequate facilities to clean hands at the point of care. One in three health care facilities do not segregate waste safely. WHO/FMoH assessment for covid19 shown that WASH in healthcare facilities require extensive investment and financial support. There is a huge gap for the technical due to the high turnover of the staff.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Due to the changes in diseases causing outbreaks, and the fragility in Sudan capacity, huge support for vector borne diseases control is needed. Based on the vector control needs assessment supported by WHO, vector surveillance will be carried out targeting vectors of medical, vector surveillance sentinel sites will be visited monthly during summer and winter season and on weekly basis during the rainy season. A comprehensive package of vector control activities will be implemented including: a) environmental management for immature stages of mosquito, b). chemical control of aquatic and adult vectors, c) training of the environmental health officers, CHW workers from MOH/WES on the integrated vector management and entomological surveillance, d) community mobilization and engagement. The community engagement as critical part of the control of the emerging Aedes borne disease outbreaks will be improved. WHO prequalified vector surveillance and control tools and equipment will be procured, technical and logistic support to vector control campaigns and vector surveillance missions. A new effective, and environmentally friendly methods for surveillance will be deployed to identify areas for focused interventions and support the existing control strategies. The implementation will take into account the specific demographics of the IDP camps in targeted states with high population density, and vulnerable population where vector borne disease outbreaks are frequently reported, and be directed by health information data on prevalence, vector density and trends of the diseases. Water quality activities will be implemented by the WHO, MOH and WASH partners at state and locality level with sustained support from WHO, complementing the UNICEF and WASH partners intervention. This year will continue strengthening water quality system at localities, to promote efficiency by reducing the cost of sample transport from field to states capital cities, and enabling the local authorities to conduct regular water sampling and testing in prioritized high-risk locations. WHO will continue support identified vulnerable families in cholera hot spots to ensure water quality and safety at household, while UNICEF and other WASH partners will distribute WASH supplies to the families depending on rivers and hafirs; WHO will distribute mini water filtration units for HHs to enable reduce the water turbidity and facilitate effective chlorination. The water sources monitoring system will provide different information on bacteriological and sanitary risk categorization and detailed status on the functionality of water sources, the functionality mapping will provide data to partners in order to maintain, rehabilitate or replace water sources to increase access to sustainable drinking water supply. The main activities related to this component includes training of environmental health officers/workers, community volunteers and hygiene promoters on different WASH topics, conduct missions for water quality testing. Water Safety Plan for small community supplies will be introduced jointly with partners, and provision of testing kits, supplies and reagents. Close information sharing and consultations with Health sector will facilitate the early warning on identified public health alerts related to water and vector transmission and joint planning of control measures. WHO will work closely with both Health and WASH sectors on improving facilities and using proven behavior change techniques, ensuring safely managing water, sanitation services, and applying good hygiene practices. Provision of waste management supplies for isolation centers and main health settings involved in covid-19 will be continued, in addition to support environmental cleaning within isolation centers, improve waste management through training of the medical staff, and provision of waste management tools and equipment, and provision of Personal Protective Equipment.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Distinct attention to the protection of the environment and natural resources is given by WHO during the implementation of the project with a medium impact on the environment. The main hazard is the waste generated by the vector control chemical activities. This hazard is reduced with several strategies; promote alternative methods to the chemical management and usage of the insecticide such as the habitat and environmental control, adoption of the gravitraps for adults which contribute to the control of gravid adult mosquito while searching for egg-lay positions and reducing contact with the incriminated vectors. However chemical control is still considered as the most important element in the integrated control of vector-borne diseases especially during the outbreaks where the vector density exceeded the reference threshold. During such a situation WHO has already developed a plan for the proper disposal of the chemical generated during the process and accordingly WHO will ensure that the staff is trained and the necessary supplies are made available. Management of the potentially infectious wastes from health activities will be segregated and dealt with in a way that prevents environmental contamination and WHO will reduce reliance on waste incineration and install high-temperature quality incinerators with treatment units to reduce risks of air pollution. For medical waste management; preventing air pollution will be managed through selecting high quality, environmentally friendly treatment methods, with fumes control and high-temperature production to minimize control the potential hazards from the waste and reduce its volume to the amount that will be easilty controlled.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WHO will ensure the ease of access by the targeted population will be needs-based and without any constraints such as discrimination, or any other constraints. WHO will also enhance the ownership of the community for project activities raising the awareness of the communities during sessions and training to address the social-cultural barriers that affect GBV survivors from accessing services and assistance. Activities of risk reduction to ultimately increase the targeted communities’ level of resilience, awareness, and capacity when faced with humanitarian hardships and to improve all aspects of protection for children. The project will additionally contribute to achieving sustainable social development through, strengthening local partners on both the national and states level. WHO will also apply the standard measures for the containment of vector control remedies to ensure and prioritize safety and dignity and avoid causing harm. Efforts will be made to ensure that the proposed interventions will minimize unanticipated or unintentional negative effects on the targeted population, which could potentially expose them to further physical and risks. By design, the project targeted vulnerable communities that requires protection intervention such as IDPs and the women population. The project also adheres to WHO PSEA standards.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will participate in inter-agency initiatives and measures to promote and implement approaches that foster accountability or feedback to the affected population. Vector control measures and community-based water safety plan activities are designed, implemented, and monitored with the participation of the beneficiary communities, Federal and State Ministries of health and partners to ensure WHO accountability and transparency of the program. Accountability to affected people at all levels is a core value of WHO work. Upon the commencement of this project and following the discussions during the planning phase; WHO will sensitize the community about the project objectives and activities and the importance of community involvement in implementation and feedback. A series of Focus Group Discussions (FGD) will take place during all project phases for different gender and age groups. Joint field visit and discussion with community leaders and line ministries. WHO through its capacity building program is delivering key messages on increasing community ownership and leadership to ensure efficiency and sustainability of the project, and how this will be achieved through the collaboration of different entities and areas where the lead will be taken by communities and the role of WHO and other partners. Because WHO efforts are made to prevent morbidity and mortalities resulting from Communicable diseases; immediate feedback is usually shared at the field level with the community on vector surveillance, vector control measures, and water quality/safety and sanitary inspection results. This timely feedback is aiming to inform the community on the deficiencies, their impact on health, and the responsible body for undertaking corrective measures which sometimes include the communities themselves. The team structure is well aligned and linked to community leaders, each team is having clear SOPs and clear roles, among the communication tree established for the work community will be shared with the contact of focal points at each level for complaints, with the intended sequence to the higher level ensuring the privacy.
