Project Summary

The extensive damage to Syria's water networks and sewerage infrastructure during the crisis has resulted in restricted access to safe water. This is affecting domestic and personal hygiene and increases the burden on women (who are usually responsible for the family's household water and hygiene). Lack of safe water increases the risk of water-borne diseases and is a potential public health hazard. Providing adequate environmental health services to mitigate these risks has become even more critical during the COVID-19 pandemic.
In 2022, UNRWA will provide essential WASH services to Palestine refugees. Hygiene kits will be distributed to 8,500 Palestine refugees affected by movement restrictions in local emergencies, as well as refugees spontaneously returning to newly accessible areas that often lack basic infrastructure. In addition, baby kits (500) and adult diapers (for 3,600 individuals) will be provided to those in need based on referrals from the Agency's health department. In 2023, UNRWA will continue distributing WASH items to those affected by local emergencies and/or spontaneously returning to newly accessible areas. Similar level of needs are expected, although this will be reassessed at the end of 2021, especially depending on return numbers.
Solid waste management and sanitization services will also continue to be provided in Palestine refugee camps in 2022-2023. The Agency will conduct minor repairs and maintenance of water, sewerage and surface drainage systems in accessible refugee camps. Upgraded equipment, such as garbage compactors and water tankers, will be procured in 2022 to improve the quality and efficiency of service provision (expected procurement in 2022 to benefit in 2023).
As part of its COVID-19 prevention measures, UNRWA will ensure that all sanitation labourers continue to be equipped with PPE. Moreover, UNRWA will continue disinfection campaigns and the sanitization of garbage collection points to support cleaner environments in the refugee camps to help prevent further spread of COVID-19.

4 - Needs

The living conditions of Palestine refugees in Syria are particularly concerning in terms of WASH services. Yarmouk, Dera’a, and Ein El-Tal (Aleppo) camps, previously home to more than 30 per cent of the Palestine refugee population in Syria, have been almost completely destroyed, including water and sanitation infrastructure. More than 400 vulnerable Palestine refugee families have already gone back to Yarmouk despite the lack of basic infrastructure and WASH services . According to an assessment conducted by UNRWA in July 2020, close to 60 per cent of the Palestine refugees are not living in their own houses and living elsewhere in overcrowded conditions. 7 per cent said there is no availability of a healthy and appropriate toilet in the house. A total of 15 per cent of Palestine refugee households confirmed that vulnerable individuals in the family (including persons with disabilities, children, women, older persons) encountered challenges due to COVID-19, lack of soap and other hygiene items being one of the biggest challenges. Vulnerable refugees affected by shocks, emergencies, recent displacements and returnees are even more at risk in terms of access to basic hygiene, which is particularly concerning in the context of COVID-19, therefore the project focuses on hygiene supplies provision for these groups specifically.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

RISK 1.Beneﬁciaries face security/safety risks when travelling to and from WASH services.• Likelihood: Low, Impact: High• Mitigating measures:- UNRWA prioritizes maintaining andrestoring WASH services in camps, in order to minimize unnecessary movements ofbeneﬁciaries. - UNRWA prioritizes awareness raising and training to staff, communitymembers, including children, on reducing exposure to risks related to ERW. - UNRWAconducts regular protection audits to get feedback from the staff and the community onhow to improve its services and optimize its compliance to the protection principles. RISK2. Timing and process of distribution (hygiene kits) put women and girls at higher risk ofGBV. • Likelihood: High, Impact: High • Mitigation measures: Safe and secure distributionpoints are selected. DSS staff are present to ensure the process is going smoothly.Spacious sites are selected and the distribution is done over several days to avoidcrowdedness/frustrations. Separate queues for men and women are created. Female staffare present on site to address female beneﬁciaries’ needs. Staff take into considerationthe needs of elderly beneﬁciaries, and persons with disabilities.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS 1. Humanitarian needs of of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2.Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation andactive violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remainwithin the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. RISK 1:UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lackof funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Last year, UNRWA received onlyaround 30% of its requirements for Syria. Mitigation: 1. continue to monitor the situationand to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2. Regularly engage with donors andpartners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Ensure robust financial, planning andmanagement systems 4. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status offunding. 5. Continue to monitor its interventions through a results-based monitoringframework. RISK 2: UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and securitythreats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments forfacilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. RISK3: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operationalchallenges. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustaineddelivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. PPEs will beprovided to frontline staff.
