1 - Needs

In 2020, Sudan’s humanitarian needs have continued to grow. Humanitarian crises including protracted conflict and displacement, natural disasters, epidemics, malnutrition and food security remain. Sociopolitical instability and the ongoing economic crisis have exacerbated needs while deepening poverty in areas in the central and eastern parts of Sudan, where humanitarian partners have a limited presence. In addition, environmental factors, such as erratic climate shocks, exacerbate the humanitarian crisis, contributing to displacement and food insecurity. Food insecurity remains alarmingly high in Sudan with increased and protracted displacement, economic decline and inflation.The very high staple food prices continued impacts from COVID-19 containment measures, and the flooding are likely to contribute to above-average assistance needs through 2021. According to latest October IPC projections, an estimated 7.1 million people, representing 16% of the total analysed population continue to face crisis or worse levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and above) and are in need of urgent action. As Sudan continues to face emerging and unforeseen humanitarian needs, WFP is scaling up its emergency response across Sudan. With a commitment to zero hunger by 2030, there is a need to strengthen and continue the humanitarian response to effectively meet the food security needs of the most vulnerable.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Provide unconditional general food assistance to people affected by shocks; - Provide integrated conditional and unconditional food assistance and livelihood packages to vulnerable households. The introduction of different modalities of cash-based transfers (cash or voucher) is based on a multi-sectoral capacity assessment (MSCA) which looks into the capacity of: cooperating partners, supply chain at the local markets, financial institutions, information and communication technologies, gender and protection aspects that verify acceptance of cash transfers by intended beneficiaries, and security and safety of staff and WFP assets. Transfer values are established based on a standard local food basket and this in turn is regularly monitored through WFP's market monitoring tools and adjusted on regular basis. Given WFP’s unique capacity to undertake price monitoring on weekly basis across the country, food price inflation and other economic constraints are closely monitored and reflected in our internal transfer value review exercise. As for the duration of the assistance, cash or voucher as a modality of food assistance is implemented in the same manner as the in-kind food assistance – for those profiled and found as severely food insecure, assistance in calibrated against their daily need and cash or voucher provided on a monthly basis. For cash transfers related to asset creation activities, the overall project duration is around six months as it is contingent on the type of the asset being created, availability of the targeted population to engage in manual activities, accessibility for partners to guide and monitor project implementation and others.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The full impact of the unprecedented rainy season-the worst in Sudan in more than 100 years- that led to the worst flooding Sudan faced in more than three decades is yet to be felt. The already fragile food security situation in Sudan is likely to be compromised due to this year floods, following the destruction of thousands of hectares of crops just before the harvest. By the end of the year, WFP is planning to conduct emergency food response assessments (EFSA) in flood-affected areas of Kassala, Khartoum, North Darfur, North Kordofan, Red Sea state, and Sennar and West Darfur. The purpose of the assessments is to assess the impact of the floods on the food security of households and communities within the affected area one month after WFP’s assistance was provided and determine if further assistance is needed and if so, integrate it in next's year plan. For 2021, WFP plans to partially buy food from local markets to provide relief assistance, preposition food in strategic / most potential affected states before the rainy season starts in May 2021 and will continue, as of now to mainstream environment and climate change as a cross cutting issue through its different interventions such as: Food Assistance for Assets (FFA) with household and community assets that reduce the risk of disaster, strengthen livelihoods and build resilience over time; emergency preparedness responding to natural multi hazards such as floods/droughts.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WFP strives to ensure that beneficiaries’ safety, dignity and integrity are upheld, and that they are not exposed to any harm or protection risks through WFP programmes. WFP also seeks to enhance the integration of protection by identifying and building on opportunities to contribute to protection outcomes where protection risks to beneficiaries may be reduced by WFP assistance. All efforts will be made to ensure that targeted beneficiaries have meaningful access to assistance provided, based on need and without prejudice. Beneficiary identification is informed by vulnerability assessments that categorize households into 4 main categories varying from those that are severely food insecure to those that are not food insecure. Food assistance is geared towards the most and moderately food insecure. Empowerment is done through engagement of local communities in food management committees, mothers support groups, project management committees and community nutrition volunteers. Efforts are also underway to build beneficiaries’ capacities and assets through asset creation programmes in FFA and PSN programmes. Accountability both to beneficiaries and donors underpins all of WFP’s work. A plan for establishment of standardized CFM in accordance with WFP minimum requirements for functional CFM was endorsed in light of the AAP mapping. The plan is composed of a national call center, community Help Desks and activity specific grievance mechanisms. The national call center is planned to go live January 2021.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability is one of core values that helps WFP to provide the best possible service to the people it assists. AAP is defined by WFP as an active commitment to give account to, take account of, and held to account by the people it assists. During the preliminary stages, WFP provides accurate, timely and accessible information to affected people about its assistance. The information provided must be clearly understandable by everyone, irrespective of their age, gender or other characteristics. Upholding the ‘centrality of protection’, the design and planning phase also considers food and nutrition assistance to be provided in a safe and dignified manner to the targeted population. During implementation, regular meetings and beneficiary reference sessions will be held were held with community leaders and representatives of different community groups to ensure that they understand the project objectives and intended outcomes, and to solicit their feedback about any necessary changes or adaptations needed to make the programme a success. During implementation, programme adjustments if needed can be done in consultation with programme beneficiaries) In all monitoring and evaluation exercises, WFP monitors the proportion of assisted people informed about the programme, as well as the proportion of activities for which beneficiary feedback is documented, analyzed and integrated into programme improvement. This also applied for this project, with all complaints and/or feedback received through the Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs) being addressed with relevant programme units, and appropriate beneficiaries being informed about the action taken or resolution.
