Project Summary

AHF through this project aims to increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian health services for the most vulnerable and in need; strengthen health sector capacity to prepare for, detect and deliver a timely response to disease outbreaks, including COVID-19, and strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care.
Through the proposed intervention AHF will support the operations of its facilities on different levels of care to continue the provision of PHC, SHC and THC services as follows:
- PHC services through: Kafr Bonni PHC with an attached mobile clinic, Sijjo PHC with an attached mobile clinic, Leen PHC and Atareb PHC, with EPI supervision project, the estimated number of beneficiaries from PHC services is 281,265.
- SHC through Sarmada Children's Hospital in Sarmada, Sham pediatric hospital in Sijjo, Sham surgical hospital in Tal Elkarameh, and Sham ambulance system in many locations across Idleb and Aleppo a part of the central ambulance system, the estimated number of beneficiaries from SHC services is 191,729.
- In addition, providing specialized health services includes rehabilitation services to persons with disabilities within 2 centers in Tal Elkarameh and Sarmada, and 1 Dental mobile clinic in A'zaz, the estimated number of beneficiaries from the specialized health services is 12,650.
- Covid-19 response will be maintained through Alsham Isolation hospital in Dana, our CCTC in Maar tmisrin, and our emergency COVID-19 ambulances, the estimated number of beneficiaries from AHF's Covid-19 response is 8,780.
The mentioned facilities are also putting into consideration the easy access to people with disabilities through the prepared infrastructure of those facilities to make sure of full access.
As always, AHF will have the previous interventions fully coordinated with all health actors through the Health Cluster whether through the data in 4Ws, HeRAMS and DHIS2. or through its constant participation and active membership within the TWGs like SRH, PHC, and Non-Communicable Diseases, SIG, Rehab, referral, Supply Line and COVID-19 TF and its related groups, and on the ground, AHF constantly coordinates with the HDs.
Before the project implementation, the M&E team will make regularly collect the required data that help in making enlightened decisions. Monitoring and evaluation of the quality of the service provision through analyzing the collected data based on the Log-Frame, the project will be monitored and evaluated through three levels:
1) The operational, for which the project manager is responsible.
2) The technical, for which the technical team is responsible.
3) The objectives, for which the MEAL team is responsible.
Monitoring tools:
• Performance Indicator Tracking Tables (PITTs), issued on a monthly basis.
• Satisfaction surveys among beneficiaries and stakeholders will be analyzed and reported to stakeholders.
• Project Evaluation Report, by the end of the project term.
The project activities will be coordinated with other actors including partners through the Trauma TWG meetings and health cluster meetings, in addition to the regular meetings with IHD, so we have full coordination with all parties working in the same field, and for the community within the project areas we are coordinating with the local councils through regular visits will be conducted by the ambulance system administration manager.

4 - Needs

Based on CCCM data, 4.2M people in NWS, 2.7M are IDPs, 2.8M are PiNs, in Idleb: 2.96M, 1.86M of them are IDPs. In northern Aleppo: 1.4M, 836K of them are IDPs.According to HNO 2022, the fragile health system in Syria continues to face concurrent emergencies and chronic challenges which affect the availability and quality of health services across Syria, as well as the physical and mental wellbeing of the population. With more than 164,000 confirmed cases and close to 5,800 deaths, COVID-19 continues to disrupt the already-fragile health services and systems, strain attempts to stabilize and restart services affected by the pandemic, including gaps in routine childhood immunization services .Relatedly, with the UN Security Council resolution extending the authorization for Bab al-Hawa until 10 July 2022, health actors in Northwest Syria are heavily focused on increasing operational supply chain capacity. At the same time, increases in poverty across the country which have already resulted in economically-driven displacement may also increase and worsen determinants of health thereby negatively impacting health outcomes.HeRAMS Q3-2021 showed that the threshold CHWs availability ratio per 10K is 22 or more, while actual ratios are under 14 CHWs. which means a critical gap. Also, the threshold for inpatient beds availability is 10 beds for 10K population, while actual ratios are between 1-5 beds for 10K.

5 - Implementation of activities

Through this project AHF will work on the recruitment and provide the salaries to the HFs' staff, carry out general maintenance for the HFs and support the operational costs, provide capacity building program to staff members (505 medical and nonmedical) of the facilities covering many topics like IPC, PFA, COVID19 case mang., NRP, NICU, BLS, ACLS, ATLS etc., Provide PHC services according to revisited EHSP including CD, NCD, child health, and SRH, provide trauma, surgeries, and in-patients services and specialized centers including ICU, PICU, NICU, maternal and child health, and COVID-19 isolation hospitals and CCTC. In addition to procuring medical/non-medical supplies to the HFs, Raising awareness sessions, health-promotion, to the community through CHWs, Provision of emergency referral services (COVID and NON-COVID), Providing physical therapy sessions and prostheses through 2 rehab centers, Implementation of IPC program in all HFs, Sustain supervision of EPI services, and Provision of PSEA and safeguarding training.Coordination of all the activities with IHD for Idleb, and with Turkish health Authorities in Afrin and Azaz, technical coordination with Health cluster, WHO, and with partners working in the same areas. During the project implementation, AHF's Meal unit will contribute to improving decision making and management, by keeping the project on track to achieve the outcomes and objectives and by benefiting from prev. lessons learned.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AHF is an active member of the PSEA network, AHF has updated PSEA and child safeguarding policies and procedures in early 2021, including all levels from prevention, reporting, investigation, and survivor support. AHF has male & female PSEA focal points in Syria and in Turkey offices, they support the implementation of AHF PSEA strategy, and they carry out the following tasks:- Ensure that employees are aware of their obligations related to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.- Equip employees with the knowledge and skills to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Response to Both.- Create mechanisms for reporting protection from sexual exploitation and abuse at the community level, including establishing contact points, according to the location and settings for reporting and posting.- Engage with communities and key stakeholders in strengthening community complaints mechanisms, including determining the provision of victim assistance services in all targeted locations.- Conduct regular awareness-raising activities on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, policies, standards, and codes of conduct related to protection from sexual exploitation.- Provide specific awareness sessions to target groups such as women, girls, boys, and men in reception centers, camps, sites for displaced people, host communities, and society.- Ensure all project personnel sign the Code of Conduct for PSEA.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

AHF will coordinate closely with the Health cluster, as well as local authorities and humanitarian actors inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, informing the cluster if and when adjustment is needed. AHF has access and qualified program team, the Equipment is available, the place is available, the field staff is available, the required supplies and medication available, the electricity is available by functioning electrical generators.Risk 1: Conflict-related threats including aerial bombardment, advancement of hostile actors, and shifting alliances (L)Response: Postponing the services till safety is back or relocation if the project's activities can't be provided in the same locations.Risk 2: Change of control in the services area, access constraints will continue to be the most significant threat to humanitarian operations. (L)Response: A comprehensive security and safety plan will be prepared so that the staff and the patients will be secured and we will coordinate to relocate the services depending on the new needs.Risk 3: The number of patients exceeds the capacity of the HFs (M)Response: In this case, we will coordinate with the cluster, Donors, local authorities and the partners on the ground to fill these gaps.Risk 4: Finance: cash transfer to Syria and within Syria with the absence of a banking system. (L)Using Banks for all money transfers to Syria either for operational costs, or vendors and contractors.
