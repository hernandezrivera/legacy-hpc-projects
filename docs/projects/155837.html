Project Summary

The project designed to provide integrated services for W&G to meet all their protection needs (psychological and physical).
Which is based on the following:
GBV
• Promoting the existing community protection mechanisms through upgrading the provided services in three Safe Spaces for W&G (community centers) managed by WSA (formerly WSA).
• Investing in the positive impact achieved through the previous activities in the three centers, which made the LC more accepting and supporting protection and GBV activities.
• Upgrading the services and activities that WSA is conducting (PSS+ GBV CM+ GBV referrals+PM+PSEA+CBCM+MA+Case fund+Cash-based response).
• Increasing opportunities for meaningful access for all vulnerable W&G, throughout the following:
- Enhance the services already provided for all beneﬁciaries including W&GWDs.
- Enhance the CFS for children of the women beneﬁciaries in each center (with PSS + basic non-formal education + Child daycare activities) (depending upon the ages).
• Center-based activities will include:
- Provide quality GBV services (prevention & response) to most vulnerable, at risk, and survivors’ W&G. Group PSS sessions and individual consultations. GBV Case management, including a responsive case fund, by qualiﬁed caseworkers (trained by IRC and UNFPA), under the supervision of the case manager (Who received quality training by GBV SC and IRC). Empowerment and awareness activities for adolescent girls (Reproductive Health awareness, Life Skills sessions, RE, community messages).
- Staff training (a reminding training) on different protection topics. PSS and non-formal education for children of the women beneﬁciaries in the CFSs allocated inside the centers.
• Outreach activities will include conduct parenting skills and GBV awareness sessions, , Capacity Building for non-GBV specialized humanitarian workers regarding GBV cases and referrals.
MA:
- W&GwDs including survivors of explosive hazards incidents related activities
• W&G with disabilities including survivors of explosive hazards incidents will be reached by specialized services and humanitarian assistance which includes, PSS, referrals, Case fund, Cash-based assistance, capacity building, vocational trainings for the survivors and their families.
• WSA Field staff will receive refreshment training about the special needs of the PWDs, and best practices to work with them.
• Provide awareness sessions to community leaders regarding the rights and protection of PWDs.
• Conduct community-based survey and in-depth needs' assessment for PWDs Currently.
- Providing RE sessions in the communities and for the non-mine action specialized humanitarian worker, community RE focal points networks.
• Provide specialized and inclusive services and humanitarian assistance to W&GwD including survivors of explosive hazards incident.
• Reduce the risk and the impact of ERWs by providing RE sessions in the communities and for the non-mine action specialized humanitarian worker, including establishing a community focal points networks in the three locations targeting mainly W&G.
All these new additions and enhancements will be for the sake of reaching the most vulnerable, neglected and marginalized categories of beneﬁciaries (Survivors with disabilities-Widows-vulnerable adolescent girls) and giving more access for mothers with multiple young children. Which means, more accountability for vulnerable W&G beneﬁciaries.
GP:
• Emergency response to new IDPs arrivals (PFA, receiving and orienting activities, Protection monitoring, and GBV cases management).
• distribution of IEC material, PSS, Capacity Building for local initiatives and associations.
• Identifying the most vulnerable cases and providing case fund and cash assistance.

Needs

WSA MEAL department conducted general “Rapid Needs Assessment” in the three locations for the new arrivals from Al-Ghuota and Homs, in addition to monthly protection monitoring surveys in the three locations. The results showed that there is a large number of widows, women-headed HHs, and women with disabilities in the three targeted areas have no meaningful access to the centers. Also, the feedback from the women beneﬁciaries coming to the centers always stressed on the need to have a CFS to increase opportunity of participation of women. Based on the following quote from GBV-voices report: “Adolescent girls depicted movement restrictions as a form of psychological violence, affecting girls’ opportunities to socialize with friends. Girls suffer from psychological violence, by preventing them from going out by their own to visit their friends, or using the mobile phone” (Adolescent Girl, Community FGD, Aleppo Governorate) Page 50. This type of violence not less danger on women and girls from others this information shows the need of GBV services, awareness raising for both men and women, boys and girls, specialized services for survivors taking into consideration the context of the area and the cultural of people there. Back to the project locations, Khan Shaykun, Bennsh and Madiq castle host a big number of people with special needs, a lot of them coming from the neighbouring areas. The needs’ assessment that WSA has conducted shows that there are about 15% of the population are PWDs, out of them 40% are children, 67% males, and 33% females, adults and children without any appropriate humanitarian response for them. 79% of the children don’t go to school, 42% of the cases are survivors of war and remnant of war. The remarkable lack of awareness (regarding the rights of this category), the traditional constraints of the culture and the environment, and the deteriorated economic circumstances, all these factors led to the fact that PWDs are more and more isolated and neglected, and as a result they suffer psycho-social problems, and they became more vulnerable, and dependent on their caregivers. At the end, all of these factors have dramatically increased this vulnerable category’s sufferings. Our project aims to solve this problem by rehab and empowerment for women and girls with disabilities in order to allow them to get their rights, and to access equal opportunities as independent people.On the other hand, WSA has noticed throw the reports come from WSA offices in the field that the women particularly are the most affected beneficiary group by the risk of the ERWs directly when the move around companying the children to schools, going to work or working in the field, add to that the responsibility the lay on the shoulder of the woman when a member in the family had an accident.Even before the war, people with disabilities including survivors of ERW accidents were ignored in the rural areas, no quality or specialized services were used to be provided to them, not only but also in the big cities these services were so limited and most of it were provided by the private, proﬁtable sector, therefore, most of PWDs were not able to beneﬁt of any existing services. In addition, and to some extent, many families especially in rural areas- and due to their complete ignorance, consider their disabled children as a “Shame” (Stigma), and they keep them home without addressing their needs or providing services that suit their special situation.In order to secure access to the target groups, and particularly to facilitate discussions with female community members and children, it is planned that each Risk Education team will include females team members. WSA will benefit from its centers in the Khan shaykhun, Bennsh and Madiq Castle to conduct some of the sessions in the centers, and also the teams will find a station which is very close to their areas of intervention which saves resources and efforts.

Implementation of activities

WSA is implementing the ongoing activities in the three locations directly by its ﬁeld staff. WSA plans to apply more steps of integration and upgrade in the scale of activities to include the most neglected categories of W&G, all this will serve in achieving the proposed sustainability of the centers. This will mean applying more structured reporting lines and segregation of duties. The technical program staff members number (CM+PSS+Facilitators+Outreach staff) will be increased. All activities inside and outside the centers will implement with Women over 18 years old and adolescent girls in addition to children of women beneﬁciaries and as for young boys and older men WSA will target them outside the centers in outreach activities.WSA outreach teams will conduct monitoring activities. These will be both observational surveys and Key Informant Interviews as developed by the Protection Monitoring Task Force (PMTF), and ad hoc/rapid protection monitoring. In each community, 5 community members will be interviewed on a monthly basis with all data collected fed back to the PMTF.The outreach teams will work on identification of case which needs cash support and case fund and the cases will be evaluated, verified and then receive the support accordingly.Specialized RE teams will deliver the planned activities through establishing three teams of well-trained staff (including a team leader, a deputy team leader and a medic) and the project will use the remote management model, WSA aims to build the capacity of group of women to form a RE network to sustain the role of WSA by raising the Awareness against the hazards of Remnants of war after the end of the project and also in case of suspension of the project for any reason.WSA ﬁeld staff will implement the component of the W&GwDs including survivors of explosive hazards incident specialized activities, rehabilitation, and inclusion activities, these activities were implanted by SANAD staff who were specialized in dealing with PWD, and part of their job was the training of WSA staff in order to continue the implantation of supporting the women and girls with disabilities after the end of the contract with SANADWSA will be predicated on the project log-frame and M&E plan focusing specifically on indicator measurement, feedback for beneficiaries, and reporting. WSA MEAL officer will design & adapt the following monitoring tools and applications: 1. Surveys;2. Focus group discussion;3. Observation;4. Document review;5. Interviews;6. Pre and post-tests.M&E staff will collect and analyze the indicators’ data for monitoring and reporting purposes. Then a specific M&E plan will be developed depending upon:Project M&E framework Target-based performanceIndicators tracking table.Regular site visits will be conducted by the M&E Officer for verification and documentation of project implementation progress, including routine meetings with key informants at the local level to generate qualitative data on the effectiveness of project interventions and the beneficiaries to guarantee their satisfaction.The case supervisor will work closely with both the case managers, case workers and the beneficiaries in order to ensure the quality of the case management services. Depending on beneficiary consent, visual evidence that includes photos and videos of project activities will be documented. Exit interview.WSA has instituted feedback/complaints mechanism, including ensuring that feedback-providing tools will be available in project centers, which include a cellphone number and WhatsApp/Complaint box hotline number for beneficiaries to provide feedback or send complaints. This contact information will also be made visible in centers for beneficiaries that just want to call/message the feedback hotline directly.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks are divided into security and program risks Security risks: A- Shelling / It is one of the major threats in Syria, especially with the escalation of military conﬂict in northern Syria in this period. The shelling can be direct or indirect. Solution/ Mitigation measures: 1- Choose response centers which they are far from military locations. 2- Coordination and continuous communication with the S&S assistant and local authorities to ensure the safety of staff and beneﬁciaries. 3- Store relief items in small quantities and multiple locations. 4- Suspension of work temporarily in case of intensiﬁed shelling 5- Daily Risk Assessment- movement clearance for staff 6- Evacuation plans 7- Avoiding the crowding of beneﬁciaries during activities. B- Unforeseen Changes in the Conﬂict / This risk including that staff of organization cannot safely access locations, staff get besieged in one area of control or operational assets are lost. Solution/ Mitigation measures: 1- Continue to gather information from range of sources. 2- Develop contingency and evacuation plan to work in alternative location. 3- Continuous efforts by the access teams to gain new assurances and connections to secure humanitarian space. 4- Training to the staff about contingency and evacuation plan in addition to Information Security 5- Duty of staff C- IEDs/VBIEDs/PBIEDs/ Can be direct or indirect. It is one of the major threats in Syria. Solution/ Mitigation measures: 1- Choose centers far from military locations 2- Avoiding the crowding of beneﬁciaries during activates. 3- Commitment to all humanitarian principles, especially neutrality and impartiality to avoid Targeting. 4- Continuing coordination with the Security and Safety department during staff movement D- The intervention of Armed groups in humanitarian action/ Its common risk, especially extremist groups, to obstruct humanitarian action (blackmail) Solution/ Mitigation measures:1- The organization's obligation not to provide any information about the donor to any military faction interventions and other related information. 2- WSA policy is clear, do not to make a concession. 3- Coordination with civilian local authoritiesE- Flocculation of Dollar rate and issues of money transfer, Mitigation:1- WSA is using OWNDA &QUICK BOOKS tools of cash monitoring and follow-up.2- Sending money to Syria by PTT (Turkish government postal and telegraph organization)
