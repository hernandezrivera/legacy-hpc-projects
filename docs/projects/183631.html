Project Summary

The proposed action is designed to contribute to the recovery of the Limb Reconstruction Unit (LRU) patients in Nasser medical complex in Khan Younis, and to alleviate the socioeconomic hardship of the most vulnerable families who have one of their family members in the unit. In this regard, PUI will provide high protein meals for admitted patients in the LRU and Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) to the most vulnerable patients’ families. The transfer value of MPCA is based on Cash Working Group recommendations, which amount to 1,046 New Israeli Shekel (NIS)- the equivalent of 299 $- per month for a period of three months.
The planned action will also respond to the identified nutrition needs in orthopaedic and dialysis services in Nasser medical complex based on the recommendation and priorities of Ministry of health.
In late 2020 and in 2021, PUI, in close coordination with WHO and the MoH, provided more than 55,000 meals to admitted patients in LRU and orthopaedic service.
Under the HRP 2022, PUI coordinated with WHO and MoH the following planned activities respectively: PUI will provide an average of 20 admitted patients in LRU and approximately 60 admitted patients in orthopaedic and dialysis services with 3 high protein meals/patient/day. The daily cost per patient will be around 10 $.
Under the planned action, PUI will also assist 150 households of the most vulnerable families of admitted patients in LRU with MPCA for three months in order to enable them visiting their family members without further economic burdens. The MPCA will support these families to avoid negative coping mechanism.
Finally, PUI will continue its close collaboration with Disability Representative Bodies Network (DRBN) in raising awareness of PwDs on their rights and in referring cases when needed to other services.
Coordination with WHO, MoH and LRU focal point will be a key success for the implementation of the planned action.
The project will take into consideration the different needs of the different age, gender and diversity (AGD) groups, in terms of nutrition (e.g. pregnant and lactating women, older persons, etc.) and cash support. The selection process for the aforementioned activities will be based on vulnerability, which will include reflections around gender, age, and diversity (including disability). The action will also map and mitigate/respond to the possible protection related risks associated with the planned action, like female-headed households being at increased risk of GBV due to the received cash support

1 - Needs

Great March of Return (GMR): In March 2018, Palestinian started protesting, on a weekly basis, in the Access Restricted Area at land, in eastern Gaza Strip. The movement was called the Great March of Return. Israeli army retaliated with live ammunitions targeting protesters’ legs mainly, which resulted in hundreds of gunshot injuries and a large number of limbs were amputated. By the end of 2019, the mass demonstrations of the “Great March of Return” resulted in 215 killings, including 47 children, two women, four paramedics, two journalists, and nine persons with disability. Another 19,173 persons have been injured, including 4,987 children and 864 women. 9,506 of the wounded were injured by live fire, including 2,127 children and 190 women (Al Mezan, January 2020). Injuries were in need of specialized tertiary treatment and a wide range of support services. In light of these emerging needs notably for severely injured people from the GMR, joint efforts from WHO, Ministry of Health (MoH) and INGOs resulted in establishing the Limb Reconstruction Unit (LRU) in Nasser medical complex in Khan Younis. The unit can receive in average 20 patients per month who are in need of limb reconstruction surgeries. May 2021 escalation of hostilities: The 11-day escalation of hostilities in May 2021 has caused further burden on Gazans and health system; around 122 people are in need of limb reconstruction surgeries. According to the Health Cluster, there are an estimated 370 people in need of limb reconstruction surgeries (33% are from the May Escalation while the rest are from the Great March of Return and non-conflict related injuries), and at least 600 in need of reconstruction surgeries (HNO2022). Persons with disabilities either, because of conflict or non-conflict related continue to face access challenges due to their own mobility limitations or lack of adaptability of some health services. Limb reconstruction patients’ needs: Gaza’s health system is unable to support the hospitalized patients with food. Patients’ families are addressing the needs. This is also the case for Nasser Medical Complex: the responsible of nutrition department at NMC has confirmed that the Hospital is not in position to provide any kind food for the inpatient departments, including Limb reconstruction patients. Especially for Limb Reconstruction (LR) patients, absence of nutrient ingredients impedes the healing process. This is happening for two main reasons: - LR patients are suffering from severe soft and bone tissue loss. The regeneration of tissue requires excessive amounts of nutrient substances, in regular provision;- Bone tissue is the slowest growing tissue within the human body; no more than 1mm per month, in optimal nutritional conditions, for non-smoking patients. Insufficiency of adequate proteins and calcium has a direct impact to this growth process. Moreover, social specific reasons are factors of paramount importance:- Most of the limb reconstruction patients are from Gaza city and the north of Gaza Strip (GS). They are away from their homes and families, making it difficult for them to get food.- Food provision services have been totally stopped at the Hospital, because of absence of sufficient funding. In the past 2 years, admitted patients in the MOH hospitals are not receiving any kind of food.- The average Length Of Stay (LOS) for LR patient is longer compared to the LOS of other departments’ patients, and this adds a disproportionate burden of care to the patients’ families. Due to the financial crisis hitting the national authorities, families’ patients are obliged to provide daily meals to their family members admitted in the Unit. This additional economic burden on patients’ families have resulted in inadequate food intake. Each patient needs 3 meals with high protein on a daily basis with an average cost of 3 meals / day / patient around 10 $. Patients’ families have already difficulties in affording for transport fees notably for those coming from northern Gaza and Gaza city. Health experts highlighted the importance of balanced diet as it is important before and after surgery and it is vital that LR patients have sufficient calcium, vitamin C, vitamin D, and protein in daily diet for their recovery. Family patients’ needs: the socioeconomic situation is deteriorating year after year due mainly to the Israeli blockade and the recurrent escalation of hostilities. Admitted patients in the LRU are from all Gaza governorates. Patients’ families who live in north Gaza and Gaza city have to bear transport fees to reach Khan Younis averaging to 20 NIS (5USD)/visit/day. In addition, they have to bear the cost of meals. The most vulnerable families are in need of cash assistance to meet their basic needs. This indirectly impacts families’ members basic needs. Other Needs: In addition, the Ministry of Health and Nasser medical complex nutrition department highlighted the urgent need to assist other patients through the provision of meals notably in orthopaedic and dialysis services in the Nasser medical complex. For an optimal patients’ care, a multidisciplinary holistic approach, food is of paramount importance. Standardization of nutrition secures faster recovery and decreases possible complications. AGD Perspective: In the GS, FHHs are more likely to live under poverty. PwD are less likely to be involved in the workforce, especially women. Transportation might also be a challenge for women, girls and PwD, due to social norms. Gender norms play a role in society’ perception of men with an acquired disability who are viewed as they may no longer be able to provide for their families as socially expected. Older people represent 4% of the population in the GS but 14% of HHs are headed by an older person, and may be facing additional burdens.

2 - Activities

One on hand, nutrition is an important part of the factors involved in the healing process and play important role in decreasing infection risks after surgery. The balanced diet is important on pre & post-operative time (before and after surgical intervention). Activity 1: Distribution of high protein meals to admitted patients: PUI logistic manager and administrative/finance manager will follow PUI’s procurement procedures to contract a supplier who has the capacity to provide the required meals for LRU patients (high-protein cooked meals). Based on the planned quantities for the provision of balanced diet, PUI will follow a call for national tender. PUI will make sure that the selected supplier has all the needed certifications from the local authorities, and complies with sanitary and quality standards laid down by the MoH. PUI aims to sign a framework agreement with a supplier who is based in the area of intervention if all the conditions and standards are met in order to ensure quick and safe delivery to the targeted hospital in case of emergency and to boost the local economy of the community. Once the supplier is selected, PUI will sign an agreement that will include the weekly program for the high protein meals to be delivered for the patients. The distribution of meals will be done directly by the contracted supplier under the direct supervision of the MoH and WHO representatives. The LRU will be responsible for the distribution of the meals to its targeted patients. The nutrition department in Nasser medical complex will be responsible for the distribution of meals to orthopedic and dialysis patients. Activity 2: Distribution of cash assistance: the 1st step will be the selection of the most vulnerable families who have patients in the LRU. To do so, PUI will assess the socioeconomic situation of patients’ families in close collaboration with DRBN. Based on its vulnerability scoring, PUI will establish the list of the most vulnerable families. To avoid duplication, PUI will crosscheck with relevant key stakeholders working in the health and food security sectors if similar cash assistance was provided for the targeted beneficiaries. Selection criteria will be based on the following:- Having one member of the family admitted in the LRU in Al Nasser Hospital - The family has no regular sources of income- Big size of family and dependents- Family with People with Disabilities,- Family having members with chronic diseases - Number of children below 5 years’ old PUI will give the priority to female-headed households in scoring system and in the cash assistance.PUI will then distribute for the most vulnerable families the regular cash transfer value, which is 1,046 NIS (equivalent to 299 $). This value was endorsed by the Cash working Group in Palestine on July 28, 2021. The MPCA will be distributed in three installments; 1,046 NIS/family/month. Targeted families will receive a cheque and it will be paid to the family heads who have patients in the LRU. PUI will target 150 families (825 individuals). This cash assistance aims at supporting the family in meeting their basic needs and enable them to visit LRU patients during their hospitalization. PUI monitoring and evaluation officer will be in charge of conducting a Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) to clarify the mode of utilization of the cash and to measure the effectiveness of the cash distribution process. Activity 3: Awareness session on People with disabilities’ rights and referral pathway: PUI has been consulting with Disabled Peoples’ Organizations in Gaza both Disability Representative Bodies Network (DRBN) and Humanity and Inclusion (HI). Both organisations have strongly advised PUI to combine its response with awareness sessions to GMR injured and their families in order to increase the protective environment by raising awareness of the rights of people with disabilities.. These will also include enhanced feeling of dignity, safety, and opportunities for PwDs in Gaza (among other DRBN runs sports clubs for PwDs). PUI will target 300 patients benefiting from the intervention and provide them with awareness raising on disability rights provided through 15 sessions during the intervention. Each session will include 20 patients. In this regard, PUI will sub-contract DRBN to work with a specialist in PwDs rights. The DRBN will be in charge of renting local CBOs’ meeting rooms in the different localities from where patients come in order to facilitate access and make it accessible to the targeted beneficiaries. The specialist will be in charge of pre and post-tests on knowledge of targeted beneficiaries on their rights. The session will be held on a whole day for approximately 6 hours during which refreshment and lunch should be provided for the targeted beneficiaries. DRBN will take in charge the transportation fees for the targeted beneficiaries. It is important to mention that rental halls will be big enough to receive 20 patients and all participants should comply with precautionary measures about the outbreak of COVID-19 pandemic for their safety and protection. PUI, committed in ensuring PSEA mainstreaming, will be conducting inclusive awareness raising on PSEA and protection mainstreaming with diverse groups of people, especially those living with different vulnerabilities. Different communication channels will be used to cater to the different needs and capacities of the targeted community. The focus of the awareness raising will be on the right to report SEA safely and confidentiality and as well as how to access the reporting mechanism. Considering the highly sensitive nature of SEA PUI will employ different accessible reporting channels for receiving SEA complaints. The PUI FRM will be used, in addition to the interagency channels of SAWA and the PSEA network. Community consultations (FGDs,KIIs) will include SEA considerations and questions, thus enabling people to complaint directly.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

PUI opted for two modalities: in-kind donation through the provision of meals and multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA). In-kind food distribution: The in-kind support is done through contracting a supplier selected by a committee composed of PUI, WHO and MoH. The decision to use in-kind was taken in 2019 in close collaboration with the LRU and other partners. It allows the hospital nutritionist to closely managed the quality of the meal and ensure a balanced diet. Based on PUI experience, this choice is very relevant. MPCA: The MPCA is based on the recently endorsed cash transfer value of 1,046 NIS (equivalent to 299 $), by Cash working group, as regular assistance during three months. PUI opted for MPCA as this will give target groups more flexibility and freedom in meeting their basic needs. While PUI is focusing in its assistance to enable patients’ families paying the transport fees to visit them during hospitalization, the MPCA is an adaptable modality where families can cover other urgent needs. It is also an inclusive modality, and people are dignified when visiting family members admitted in the LRU. This modality reduces negative coping mechanism, it is preferred by most vulnerable people (Cash transfers was the most preferred form of assistance by 77 percent) – HNO2022 – MSNA July 2021). Finally, MPCA was proven to enhance the health and nutrition conditions for women and girls according to UNFPA approach on using this modality.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PUI has started working in the Hospital in 2019, in close collaboration with the medical staff. The Action’s primary goal is to respond to the affected population’s needs, expressed and identified during the needs assessment phase. Feedbacks received from the patients and the partners in 2021 were a great source of information; e.g: the idea of cooking directly in the hospital kitchen instead of doing food delivery improved the quality control during the meals preparation phase. This modification suggested by the nutritionist and PUI MEAL staff had a direct impact on the patient satisfaction. PUI MEAL team will pay attention that feedback, whether positive or negative, will addressed by PUI representatives independent from project team and be dealt with respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. Monthly internal reporting will include analysis of the received feedbacks, as well as during the yearly strategy exercises. Project modalities or objectives will be discussed with the program and technical team, in order to better respond to the needs and concerns of the served population.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

PUI has a country level Protection and Gender Mainstreaming strategy as well as a global PUI Protection Mainstreaming framework to reflect on the type of actions required on each stage of the project cycle to ensure protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. Based on this strategy and on the planned community consultations, a project specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming action plan will be developed once the project started. A protection and gender risk and mitigation measures analysis will be conducted for the project. The analysis will be further refined and triangulated with the solicitation of direct feedback from the target community, regarding the perceived protection risks, needs and capacity. The analysis will have special focus on the risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and challenges for people with disability. According to the outcomes of this initial Protection and Gender risk analysis, project specific and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identified risks and gaps. Project beneficiaries in need of more specialized protection or psychosocial support (PSS) services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed or drafted. At the same time, PUI staff members will be trained on “Safe Protection Referrals” training module, to include “Communicating and responding to GBV disclosures”. To ensure “Do-no-harm”, PUI will first keep on consulting any available studies on gender and power dynamics within the communities. Then, PUI makes an effort to consult groups that are traditionally excluded from social dialog about the safest way to include them. Finally, PUI ensures the promotion of key human rights, the rights of people with disabilities and of the humanitarian mandate messages to the targeted communities. PUI cares for the safety of the location of the implementation/distribution and ensure the distribution point/implementation premises is accessible for different groups of people and make special arrangement for people with specific vulnerabilities (e.g. Providing transportation, home distribution, etc.) and follow the safety procedures concerning COVID-19 and provides personal protective equipment and considers precautionary safety measures, including physical distancing and hygiene promotion. Protection risks, including the risks of sea, will be mapped and monitored through community consultations with diverse groups. Tailored prevention and mitigation plan (with a special focus on SEA) will be drafted to tackle these risks according to the wishes and needs of the affected communities

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through the use of interviews, focus groups and surveys, PUI aims to involve vulnerable groups and people with specific needs in all aspects of the project cycle and promote the general concept of age and gender diversity (AGD) in selecting the beneficiaries (BNF). PUI will also ensure assessment methodology complies to the standards and includes protection considerations; put additional effort to include representative from all different groups within the targeted community; ensure the assessment tools are accessible for different groups of people; conduct vulnerability mapping and socioeconomic survey to all the potential BNFs, and besides promotes the Disability Mainstreaming through the project’s cycle. Moreover, PUI has a specific budget and human resources dedicated to protection mainstreaming, and thus is able to ensure support for different people and to offer them equal access to our services. Accessibility is not only physical but psychological as well, thus, all PUI staff received several trainings on different disciplines (such as Protection, PSEA, child safeguarding and child protection) in order to ensure that they can promote and defend inclusion in our day-to-day implementation. Regarding PSEA specifically, PUI has strengthened its SEA reporting mechanism with the opening of our whistleblowing mechanism to others and different channels for beneficiaries, staff, and other stakeholders. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEA to project beneficiaries come to complete the scheme. Besides, PUI staff members will also be further trained on age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, psychological first aid, safe referrals, GBV and inclusion of people with disabilities.
