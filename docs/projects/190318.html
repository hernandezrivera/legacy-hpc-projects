Project Summary

Ethiopia continues to face severe humanitarian conditions as needs emanating from conflict, climatic shocks, disease outbreaks, desert locust and the adverse effects of the ongoing Covid-19 pandemic continue to grow. Conflict has remained the main driver of humanitarian needs in the country in 2021.
According to Ethiopian Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2021), above 23M people nationwide were projected to have humanitarian needs in 2021 those majority of whom were in Oromia (6.7 million; followed by Somali (3.8M) then thirdly Amhara 3.4M. One of the biggest impacts from conflict and climate shocks in Ethiopia is population displacement. As per the Displacement Tracking Matrix, there are 2.1 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) across the country, outside of Tigray. 67 per cent of the IDPs have been displaced due to conflict. Most IDPs live in sub-standard conditions, with limited access to essential services.
According to the Ethiopian Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) Mid-Year (MY) review for 2021, 14.8M peoples targeted with 1.488B funding requirement in country wide. Somali region is the second in constituting the regional targets next to Oromia (4.4M) having 3.9M peoples among the regionals targeted.
As the Es/NFI dashboard (published in October 2021) shown, only 1.4M peoples reached so far, which is only 37% of the cluster target (3.9M). In the other side, in Somali region only 23% reached and the gaps remain 70% with the remaining 7% under commitment. The S/NFI cluster prioritized 10 woredas those extremely at high need for Es/NFI assistance across Oromia, Somali and Benishangul Gumuz regional states.
Due the conflict erupted in July 2021 between two communities/clans (Gerri and Jarso) that were caused by a combination of disputes over the border separating the two regions (Oromia and Somali), related to resources and farming land. Thus, 1,840 households (11,040 individuals) displaced according to the government-led joint assessment report released in late October 2021.
The proposed intervention will support the most vulnerable crisis affected individuals by providing the lifesaving emergency shelter and non-food items to 1,440 HH extremely displacement affected persons with disability inclusion and will target HH whose living standards and ability to pursue normal productive and social activities, and who are unable to meet this basic needs due to, conflict, drought and disease and who are in need of emergency shelter and non-food items (ESNFI V1).
The project will provide in kind and Cash/Voucher assistance thein Emergency Shelter and Non-food Items for lowlands (ESNFI V1 ) and Emergency Shelter and Non-Food Items for disable ( ESNFI V1-Disable) for IDPs, 15% for host communities and 5% for disables based on the context of project areas. These all, the process will be done considering the COVID19 precautions both for ZOA/OWDA staff and targeted beneficiaries in line with the current pandemic disease and its means of transmission. ZOA/OWDA will ensure or minimize the COVID19 pandemic dissemination by placing the preventions measures like facemasks, handwashing facilities, using sanitizer and ensuring the social distancing during the distribution.
