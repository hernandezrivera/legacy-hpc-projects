Project Summary

South Sudan is facing a prolonged humanitarian crisis driven by recurring floods, droughts, and heatwaves, which have destroyed livelihoods, displaced families, and disrupted agriculture and infrastructure. Localized violence, including cattle raiding and disputes over resources, forces people from their homes and hampers aid delivery. Nearly 367,000 (DTM data) people were newly displaced in 2024 by flood and conflict within South Sudan.
Moreover, in 2024 alone, 400,000 returnees of South Sudan nationality have fled Sudan and sought refuge in South Sudan. Further analysis done by CCCM Cluster and DTM flow monitoring found that nearly 20% of the returnees (80,000) end up relying on humanitarian services in formal/informal camps and sites. This overwhelms the already highly fragile socio-economic situation, worsening food insecurity, healthcare shortages, and shelter needs, further straining host communities.
This project will provide Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) to vulnerable households affected by shocks in 2024, targeting
1) displaced population and new returnees arrived in South Sudan in second half of 2024. As rainy season ceased, market functionality gradually improves, MPCA will enable them to meet their immediate needs while supporting local markets and improving resilience in 2025. MPCA can help preventing local vendors from losing their livelihood due to lack of purchasing power in shock affected communities – therefore, alleviate reliance on humanitarian aid in the initial stabilization phase following the shock.
2) communities hosting high number of IDPs and returnees, especially, in Aweil and Rubkona as high area of return, to alleviate the strain on local resources and promote coexistence.
3) As guided by the current HNRP strategy, the projected needs of population affected by new shocks in 2025, to whom MPCA can be the first line life-saving assistance, is not included in the proposal. However, IOM strongly recommends inclusion of MPCA in future HNRP for rapid response planning.
IOM's PDM of MPCA interventions in 2023 and 2024 reveals that when FSL partners partially cover food needs, families tend to allocate MPCA to multi-sectoral needs, including shelter (48%), NFIs (2%), health (5%), education (6%), hygiene (7%), additional food (27%), livelihoods (1%), and communication/transportation (4%). Before receiving MPCA, 60% of household expenses were primarily spent on food.
Therefore, by aligning with the 2025 Humanitarian Needs Response Plan for MPCA, this project aims to:
1) empower families to address their priorities whether food, Shelter, non-food items, WASH, health, education, livelihood inputs and transports while reducing negative coping mechanisms.
2) Strengthen local markets by enhancing purchasing power, and in turn, reduce reliance on humanitarian aid.
3) Stabilize and empower affected households, and potentially, promote early recovery and resilience, enabling families to rebuild with dignity.
Assistance will be delivered as follows:
• One-time cash transfers to address immediate basic needs of recent displaced/returned population in Priority 1 counties Abyei Administrative Area, Fangak, Aweil Centre/East/North/South, Panyijiar, Rubkona, Twic, Raja)
• Two-month cash transfers to address immediate to recurring needs of the most vulnerable households among the displaced/returned/and residents in Priority 1 counties (Aweil East/North/South, Panyijiar, Rubkona)
• To enable transition programming, and to support medium to long term needs, three-month cash transfer in coordination with NEXUS programming partners, in Priority 3 locations (Aweil Centre) and host communities hosting large number of IDPs/returnees (Aweil East, Rubkona).
The intervention will ensure that support is efficiently and effectively delivered, contributing to a robust and responsive humanitarian system in South Sudan.

1. Needs

South Sudan remains one of the most complex humanitarian crises globally, with a convergence of conflict, climate-related shocks, economic instability, and the effects of the Sudan crisis creating widespread vulnerabilities. An estimated 9.3 million people 69% of the population are projected to require humanitarian assistance in 2025, reflecting a growing scale of need compared to previous years.The assessed needs highlight critical gaps in food security, shelter, healthcare, and livelihoods. Food insecurity affects over 7.7 million people, with families unable to afford basic staples due to rising costs and market disruptions. Many displaced families lack adequate shelter and essential non-food items (NFIs), while health systems remain overstretched, leaving vulnerable groups such as children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities without access to care. Displacement and climatic shocks have further disrupted local economies, eroding livelihoods and weakening market functionality, making it vital to support market recovery and economic stability.Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) is a proven, effective solution to address these challenges. By providing cash, families can independently prioritize and meet their essential needs, such as food, shelter, or healthcare. MPCA restores dignity and choice to affected populations while empowering local markets to recover from shocks. This approach is cost-effective, minimizing logistical challenges associated with in-kind assistance and allowing for efficient resource allocation. Moreover, MPCA supports social protection for the most vulnerable groups, including women-led households, the elderly, and persons with disabilities, ensuring inclusivity in the recovery process.The MPCA intervention in South Sudan builds upon IOM’s extensive experience with cash programming in the region. Since 2016, IOM has implemented various forms of cash-based interventions, including emergency cash transfers, sectoral cash, return packages and livelihood support, ensuring a deep understanding of local contexts, vulnerabilities, and the capacity of the market system. Aligned with the 2025 Humanitarian Needs Response Plan for MPCA, this project emphasizes strategic collaboration with the Cash Working Group (CWG), rigorous market monitoring, and coordination across sectors to enhance program quality and impact. Market functionality will be closely monitored using tools like the Market Functionality Score (MFS), ensuring that cash transfer values remain responsive to economic fluctuations and protect recipients’ purchasing power. Coordination with the CWG and other stakeholders will prevent duplication, enhance targeting, and address evidence gaps, ensuring that assistance reaches those who need it most. In conclusion, the complexity and scale of South Sudan’s humanitarian crisis call for a flexible and responsive intervention. MPCA offers a timely and dignified solution that not only addresses immediate needs but also promotes resilience and recovery for shock-affected populations. By empowering communities and supporting local markets, this intervention contributes to a more sustainable and effective humanitarian response.

2. Project activities

This project aims to deliver targeted Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) to 18,056 vulnerable households (108,336 individuals) across Abyei, Fangak, Aweil (Centre, East, North, South), Rubkona, Twic, and Raja in South Sudan. The assistance will address immediate needs while promoting early recovery and resilience. Key activities include:Activity 1.1.1: Conduct Needs Assessments and Beneficiary Verification/ Registration: The project will initiate detailed needs assessments and beneficiary verification processes to ensure targeted and effective assistance. This will involve household interviews, focus group discussions, and market assessments to refine the targeting criteria and transfer values. The eligibility criteria will focus on newly displaced IDPs, returnees from Sudan, and vulnerable host communities, aligning with the 2025 HRP vulnerability framework. Coordination with the Cash Working Group (CWG) and local stakeholders will guide the selection of the most vulnerable households, including elderly individuals, women-led households, and persons with disabilities. For MPCA responses, IOM will ensure biometric registration through the DTM team to enhance accuracy and accountability.Activity 1.1.2: Provide One-Time MPCA to address Immediate Needs of IDPs, Returnees and Resident Communities. In targeted Priority 1 locations, IOM will provide one-time MPC to vulnerable households among the recently displaced or returned population, to address urgent and essential needs.The current median MPCA transfer value in South Sudan is approximately 127 USD with 10%-30% buffer for inflation, up to max. 165 USD per household per month.This cash transfer value was determined based on the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) for MPCA established by the CWG and informed by regular market monitoring to maintain beneficiaries' purchasing power. However, deduplication with Cluster partners providing sectoral responses, especially cash for food, in-kind food and shelter NFI distributions, will be coordinated at implementation stage to determine the final transfer value for the response. Whenever feasible, through County level ICCG and in consultation with local communities, IOM will aim for joint needs assessment and joint targeting (including data sharing, when feasible) with humanitarian partners, to identity entry point for IOM’s MPCA intervention. When there’s gaps in certain prioritized counties unmet by certain Cluster partners due to resource constraints, MPCA can be the ad-hoc, interim solution to prevent deterioration of the emergency humanitarian situation. Disbursements will be facilitated through IOM’s pre-selected financial service providers. Activity 1.1.3: Provide MPCA to support Medium -Term Needs and Resilience:Up to three months of recurring cash assistance will be provided to address medium-term needs, targeting the most vulnerable households among displaced, returnees, and host communities, particularly in Priority 3 locations and areas hosting significant numbers of IDPs and returnees. Monthly disbursements will ensure flexibility for beneficiaries, with cash values periodically reviewed based on inflation and local economic conditions, leveraging data from the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) and Market Functionality Score (MFS).Priority will be given to households requiring additional support, such as those headed by women, persons with disabilities, or other vulnerable groups. Referrals from protection partners will be integrated to address socio-economic vulnerabilities, while data sharing and referrals to social protection partners will facilitate longer-term support for households receiving MPCA.Activity 1.1.4: Strengthen Local Markets: IOM will use market-based cash interventions to strengthen local markets and improve access to essential goods for crisis-affected populations. Activity 1.1.5: M&E: Ensure robust monitoring to enhance program quality and accountability to affected population.
