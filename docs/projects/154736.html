Project Summary

(1). To Improve access to lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for the Rohingya FDMN population with special focus on non-communicable disease (NCD) and communicable diseases including TB. The aim is to reduce avoidable morbidity and mortality within the target population. (2). To support comprehensive and lifesaving reproductive and maternal including ANC healthcare directly in our medical centers and via Referral to MSF hospital, UNFPA-supported maternity centers, BRAC (for complicated cases such as pre-eclampsia, child birth) to reduce morbidity and mortality. Cervical cancer screening and thermocogulator treatment for CIN1 and CIN2 for women aged 30-65 years using (3). Using our existing field-proven paperless digital EMR system [NIROG], provide high quality healthcare by systematically capturing demographic information, health data, diagnosis and treatment plans for all patients examined. This EMR system is used to electronically register and track all patients (with photo, fingerprints and government-issued registration ID) and would allow for chronic and repeat patients to be treated more efficiently and accurately by easily and instantly accessing their current and past healthcare data stored in the system. Patients are also tracked as referrals (to BRAC's DOTs, UNFPA maternity centers, WFP's nutrition center for MAM, MSF hospital, etc.) to further strengthen healthcare sector coordination and monitor the response and quality of the health services provided. Easy, accurate and timely reporting of health screening, diagnosis and treatment data is also an important objective and benefit of using this EMR system. The NIROG EMR allows the HAEFA team to be completely in line with the accurate reporting requirements established by the Government of Bangladesh (GOB) national authorities. Furthermore, the NIROG EMR system can be integrated with the GOB Shared Health Record (SHR) system in MIS, DGHS, MoHFW, to transfer any and all required data in a safe and secure encrypted manner to further support and promote the use of the GOB and MoHFW National Shared Health Records system. The data captured by the NIROG EMR would be owned by the GOB and MIS, DGHS, MoHFW. Please note that our medical centers are housed in the MOH (government medical camps) of Kutupalong (camp 1W) and Balukhali (camp 9), and we have been working in close collaboration with the government’s DGHS, MoHFW; thus our work including digital medical record system is already aligned with the government’s healthcare system. ALL THE ABOVE WILL BE IN LINE WITH THE SECTOR MINIMUM SERVICE PACKAGE FOR THE HEALTH POST.

Needs

Primary and Secondary health screening and health care services are required for the Lives Improvement 21% of total Rohingya population suffering from in Hypertension, diabetics, Asma, malnutrition including Anemia, TB & Cervical Cancer screening for the sexually active women in the Rohingya Camp and host communities.

Activities or outputs

(1). Using HAEFA’s unique solar-powered electronic medical record (EMR), To Improve access to lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for the Rohingya FDMN population along with the host communities with special focus on non-communicable disease (NCD) such as Hypertension, diabetes, anemia, malnutrition, asthma along with weekly and monthly follow up, treatment and referral for severe cases where appropriate. (2) Using MobileODT and Thermocoagulator, in collaboration with Brown University and Dr. Susan Cu-Uvin, to provide Cervical Screening for women aged (30-65 years), treatment : Thermocoagulator for CIN1 and CIN2 (in-situ cancer), at the HAEFA centers. Beyond >CIN2 cases will be referred to Cox’s Bazar hospital. (3) Passive TB screening for all patients seen and the Presumptive TB cases are referred to BRAC and DOTS.(4) To support comprehensive and lifesaving reproductive and maternal including ANC healthcare directly in our medical centers and via Referral to MSF hospital, UNFPA-supported maternity centers, BRAC (for complicated cases such as pre-eclampsia, child birth) to reduce morbidity and mortality. (5). Using our existing field-proven paperless digital EMR system [NIROG], provide high quality healthcare by systematically capturing demographic information, age and gender, health data, diagnosis and treatment plans for all patients examined. This EMR system is used to electronically register and track all patients (with photo, fingerprints and government-issued registration ID) and would allow for chronic and repeat patients to be treated more efficiently and accurately by easily and instantly accessing their current and past healthcare data stored in the system. Patients are also tracked as referrals (to BRAC's DOTs, UNFPA maternity centers, WFP's nutrition center for MAM, MSF hospital, etc.) to further strengthen healthcare sector coordination and monitor the response and quality of the health services provided. Easy, accurate and timely reporting of health screening, diagnosis and treatment data is also an important objective and benefit of using this EMR system. The NIROG EMR allows the HAEFA team to be completely in line with the accurate reporting requirements established by the Government of Bangladesh (GOB) national authorities. Furthermore, the NIROG EMR system can be integrated with the GOB Shared Health Record (SHR) system in MIS, DGHS, MoHFW, to transfer any and all required data in a safe and secure encrypted manner to further support and promote the use of the GOB and MoHFW National Shared Health Records system. The data captured by the NIROG EMR would be owned by the GOB and MIS, DGHS, MoHFW. Please note that our medical centers are housed in the MOH (government medical camps) of Kutupalong (camp 1W) and Balukhali (camp 9), and we have been working in close collaboration with the government’s DGHS, MoHFW; thus our work including digital medical record system is already aligned with the government’s healthcare system.NCD Focus with Communicable Disease (hypertension and diabetes, others including reproductive cancer screening for women) including Tuberculosis: We have two primary healthcare centers in Kutupalong and Balukhali camps, where we provide quality treatment with medication free of cost for 250 to 300 patients per day. From October 9, 2017 till November 11, 2018, the total number of Rohingya FDMN treated by HAEFA exceeded 70,000 patients. This proposal will treat 72,000 patients in 2019.Each patient receives a digital health card with unique traceable barcode, and their biometric and demographic data and medical findings are stored in HAEFA's NIROG EMR encrypted database for future follow up and coordination. The EMR is used for monitoring the treatment response and provide quality health services. All age groups, adults and pediatric are treated primarily and triaged for referral to appropriate medical facilities (e.g. BRAC, MSF, UNFPA, WFP, etc).

We will do referral of advanced cancer cases, Presumptive TB Sputum positive cases, & help to do contact tracing.
