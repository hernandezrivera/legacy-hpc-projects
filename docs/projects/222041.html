Project Summary

CRS proposes to support communities affected by climate and conflict driven shocks in Duk and Ayod Counties and the Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) through the drilling of 5 new and rehabilitation of 20 existing communal boreholes, utilizing flood-resistant designs, to serve 12,500 vulnerable individuals. 25 water point user committees (WPUC) and 5 pump mechanic groups will be trained to manage and maintain the water schemes. The project will also construct water troughs at all water facilities to provide access to drinking water for small ruminant animals, reducing the required amount of time and distance travelled to access water. The project will also integrate these WASH activities with other livelihood interventions to promote multiple water uses. By engaging vegetable producers to grow crops and fruit seedlings near water facilities, enhancing the food and nutrition security of affected communities. These activities will complement and amplify the impact of planned WFP funded programming in Ayod, Duk, and Pibor, including resilience building, livelihood, food security and nutrition activities. CRS has active field bases and extensive experience in all of these counties and GPAA. Increased access to clean water for personal and domestic use, will enhance the uptake and sustain the use of promoted hygiene and sanitation best practices, contributing to improved nutrition outcomes in the target communities.
CRS has worked in greater Jonglei for over 10 years, meeting a range of humanitarian needs, inc. food assistance, livelihoods, nutrition, health, social cohesion, and conflict mitigation. CRS has years of extensive experience implementing comprehensive WASH programming in these counties. This project will complement recent and ongoing BHA-funded resilience, food security, and nutrition programming. CRS will coordinate with local actors, WASH and Nutrition Clusters, State and County authorities, and other implementing partners. The proposed project will integrate WASH interventions with planned food security and nutrition activities to help enhance acceptance and adoption of promoted sanitation and hygiene practices, reducing the rate of malnutrition due to waterborne diseases. In addition, layering the WASH interventions with ongoing and planned gender, livestock, and social cohesion programming will facilitate greater impact. Participation of women in WPUCs as committee members, pump mechanics, and caretakers will improve agency, empower them to take on leadership roles and participate in community decision making, and improve access to information and income. The project will apply a conflict sensitive, gender-responsive “Do No Harm” approach, considering the unique vulnerabilities and capacities of men, women, boys, girls, and PWD.
CRS has strong acceptance, and a stellar reputation with local communities and stakeholders in these areas, and will leverage our local experience, reach, and expertise in this challenging context. CRS has local and intl staff expert in emergency WASH implementation, and maintains robust systems to ensure accountability and program quality. CRS' established supply chain and logistics capacity, including warehouses at field locations across these counties, to purchase and deliver required supplies and equipment during the intended time frame. CRS will ensure staff, office space, and vehicles are shared across programs to maximize cost effectiveness.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in Duk, Ayod, and the Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) remains severe, with food security crisis and emergency conditions driven by large numbers of IDPs and returnees, persistent inter-communal conflict, macro-economic challenges depleting purchasing power, five consecutive years of flooding, and limited agricultural production. Vulnerable populations, especially returnees and those that have been displaced or are isolated by floodwaters and destroyed roads face not only hunger but also dramatically elevated risks of malnutrition and communicable diseases; children, vulnerable adults such as pregnant or lactating women, and persons with disabilities are disproportionately affected. The target counties are recognized as intersectional priority level 4/5 per the WASH Cluster targeting guidance. These counties are all predicted to experience IPC 4 (food security emergency) or worse outcomes in lean season 2025. (Per IPC release 18 Nov 2024).The target counties are historically under-developed, isolated, and seriously affected by insecurity and intercommunal conflict. Recurrent conflict and violence, paired with recurring climatic shocks, has traumatized and displaced populations; destroyed or led to disrepair of many water points and other WASH infrastructure, disrupted agricultural production and adaptive capacity; eroded trade, markets, and basic services; exacerbated food and nutrition insecurity; and increased household dependency on humanitarian aid. Flooding as well as routine violence including revenge killings and cattle raiding displaces communities, forcing them to concentrate in smaller locations with limited access to clean water, hygienic sanitation facilities, and other essential services including health, nutrition, and protection services. At the same time, recurring climatic shocks such as flooding, unseasonal rains and dry spells as well as crop and animal diseases and pests have reduced production leading to reduced food availability. This has contributed to Critical (IPC AMN 4) acute malnutrition situations in these counties which is predicted to continue through the current projection period (up to June 2025) (IPC release, 18 Nov 2024). Most communities have limited access to clean water, relying on seasonal streams, unprotected wells, and water ponds for their WASH needs. The number of protected water schemes available are in short supply due to poor surface and groundwater source potentials. In addition, these protected water schemes are often overburdened, nonfunctional, or contaminated. The high reliance on unimproved, open water sources compounds the risk of waterborne and diarrheal diseases in the region. The low access to quantity and quality water also contributes to low rates of hand-washing and other hygiene practices, impacting the health and nutrition of many, especially children. The high unmet community needs for access to clean water has significantly contributed to the emergency conditions in these counties.

2. Project activities

CRS proposes to support 12,500 vulnerable ind. in most-affected communities in Ayod, Duk, and Pibor Counties through the drilling of 5 new and rehabilitation of 20 existing communal boreholes. 25 water point user committees (WPUC) and 5 pump mechanic groups will be trained to manage and maintain the water points. CRS will also construct water troughs at all water facilities to provide access to drinking water for small ruminant animals, reducing the required amount of time and distance travelled to access water. The project will also integrate these WASH activities with other livelihood interventions to promote multiple water uses. Engaging vegetable producers to grow crops and fruit seedlings near water facilities will enhance food and nutrition security. Improved water supply will complement and amplify the impact of CRS’ planned WFP - funded programming in Ayod, Duk, and Pibor, which includes resilience building, livelihood, food security and nutrition activities. Increased access to clean water for personal and domestic use, will enhance the uptake and sustain the use of promoted hygiene and sanitation best practices, contributing to improved nutrition outcomes in the target communities. While the geophysical and hydrological characteristics of the terrain makes borehole drilling a complicated process, CRS South Sudan has its own drilling rigs and experienced drilling crew to meet the task. Prior to the drilling, geographical survey will be conducted to assess potential water source locations and maximize success rates in securing productive boreholes. Water quality tests will be conducted at each site to ensure safety for human consumption and use. As needed, CRS will install surface water treatment (SWAT) plans.CRS will do hygiene promotion using Wash'Em and distribute standard hygiene kits and MHM supplies to most vulnerable HHs, prioritizing women and girls. CRS will conduct regular safety audits at all water points. The project will adhere to a “Do No Harm” approach while considering the unique vulnerabilities and capacities of men, women, boys, and girls. CRS centralizes protection and conflict sensitivity, while integrating robust PSEA measures, including: 1) CRS consults men, women, boys and girls from diverse groups and conducts risk assessments which apply gender and conflict sensitivity lenses, including considering risks around GBV and intra-household conflict/violence. 2) Regular monitoring activities actively gather information about the experience of project participants and other community members during programming, providing the project team with data which is triangulated with field monitoring visits and feedback mechanisms (CFRMs) to understand unintended consequences and adapt programming. 3) CRS interventions target women per vulnerability criteria, and provide services, interventions, and assets that are culturally appropriate for women and girls to utilize, own or govern. 4) All CRS staff as well as local authorities and community leaders are trained on reporting and referral systems in place for GBV, alongside the code of conduct, the free assistance to expect, the targeting/inclusion method. 5) Service points are located to ensure vulnerable beneficiaries can access them with safety and dignity, and without traveling long distances. 6) Beneficiaries and other community members are sensitized, particularly during distributions, on available CFRMs and how to report any concern.
