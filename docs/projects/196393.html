Project Summary

ACF will continue WASH services in line with the WASH sector strategy through operation and maintenance, quality, and durable essential WASH services along with solid waste management. ACF will also focus on building resilience and ensure sustainability through community engagement in the camps. Along with regular operation and maintenance priorities, ACF will continue advocacy to cover the existing WASH services gaps and upgradation and construction of new facilities to meet the minimum standard when addressing the needs of all communities in the proposed area.
Water quality in households and existing water points will be prioritized as it is a critical stage of the safe water chain. ACF will make provisions for gender-specific safe WASH spaces in the response area. ACF will support improved distribution networks and storage and will conduct regular and periodic assessments to gather the required data for program decision making. ACF will conduct the required evaluation and impact study through water accessibility assessment in the concerned area, mainly focusing on evaluating the impact on equitable access to safe drinking water, with improvements made where needs are identified. ACF will continue the repair and maintenance of the existing hand pumps, which will continue to serve as a source of domestic water supply. Alternative options to ensure the minimum standard of required water for the drinking and domestic purpose will be ensured in case of natural disaster.
ACF will work to improve latrines and bathing cubicles through upgrades and maintenance to ensure their functionality, durability and quality. ACF will also conduct enhanced monitoring to ensure safety, dignity and privacy and address the specific needs of the most vulnerable community members. Where possible, the construction of facilities will aim to cover existing gaps and prioritize women-friendly spaces. Latrine facilities will integrate hand-washing facilities. Fecal sludge management will focus on ensuring effective, sustainable and cost-efficient sanitation service for appropriate environmental health conditions.
ACF will strive to reinforce the solid waste management system and will work through community engagement promoting house-level waste segregation, collection (ideally at household level) and safe disposal, including valorization (recycling, upcycling, composting, etc.).
ACF is working on transition to sustainability and resilience in the context of the protracted crisis through a community engagement process in line with the WASH sector strategy. For this purpose, ACF will strive to support communities in changing their identified high-risk behaviors through participatory approaches and capacity building.
Strengthened hygienic practice monitoring and follow-up will be enhanced, focusing on the most vulnerable people through increased cross-sector linkages with Health & Education with epidemiological evidence of WASH-related diseases. ACF will ensure that the needs for critical supplies such as soap, water containers, and MHM materials will be addressed on time with quality and sufficient quantity (with support to the market system).
ACF's WASH services will be aligned with the national WASH strategies and conducted in close collaboration with the WASH sector, Upazilla focal actors and DPHE, which will help avoid the duplication of interventions. ACF will ensure proper coordination with CIC, camp focal point, site management and development, Area focal agency, and WASH sector. ACF will ensure that the COVID-19 protocols are respected at every stage of the WASH activities implementation. ACF will prioritize gender-responsive and community engagement activities to ensure participation, especially of women and girls, the elderly, youth groups, and persons with disabilities in the entire decision-making process and accountability framework for the affected populations. This will also ensure communities’ ownership and project’s sustainability.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

As per the September 2022 UNHCR population data, 22570 FDMNs are accessing safe water for domestic needs and 8,054 people in host communities. New WASH facilities have been built to improve quality of services and decrease operational costs. Main current actions are the operation and maintenance of existing services and standards (distribution of soap, MHM). In the host community, ACF will be focusing on the gaps in access to water, latrines and MHM kits:- Water: 10% of FDMS in ACF proposed area and 26% of HC reported not having enough water to meet domestic needs.- Sanitation: only 8% of fecal sludge treatment plants meet standards while 7% of sanitation infrastructure require major maintenance. On average, 53 FDMNs share bathing facilities due to a gap in required facilities. The availability of space is one of the major challenges new construction. - Hygiene: only 88% of women and girlsare accessing MHM kits as per standards. Regarding outbreaks, cases of scabies and dengue have respectively been multiplied by three and by twelve in 2022 compared to 2021.- Waste: only 78% of refugees are disposing waste in designated area, resulting in 30% of household reporting visible garbage near their shelters.- Inclusion: 8% of the population in the target area have a disability. Assistive devices aim at addressing PwDs and the elderly's specific needs.Some of the major challenges in the response is the scarcity of required space as well as poorly functional infrastructure constructed by non-traditional actors needing upgrade.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

ACF has been implementing WASH programs in Cox’s Bazar District since 2009 by supporting the Rohingya refugees in camps and in affected host communities since the refugee influx in 2017 and the natural disasters in Cox’s Bazar District affected both host communities and refugees. Consequently, there was an increase in the level of hazards related to poor WASH facilities including a high percentage of water point’s contaminated, insufficient number of latrines and poor waste management. As per WASH assessment conducted by ACF in March 2022, diarrhea prevalence is 10% in the megha camp but in ACF proposed area it’s 5%. Only 11 % latrine have a functional light system and 49% and 32% latrine and bathing space respectively do not have proper gender segregation. This increases the risks related to protection, safety and dignity for the women and adolescent girls especially at the time of using the WASH facility at night. 22% child feces not disposed properly. Latrine coverage ratio is 1:28 and bathing facility is 1:53 which is less than the standard. Hygiene: Only 88% of women and girls accessing MHM kit as per standards. On the other hand, Solid Waste produced daily in the camps raises a continuous problems and remains one of the main challenges. Only 78% of the refugee households are disposing waste in designated areas. Prevalence of the handwashing critical times especially minimum 03 critical times is 77%. As per assessment there are 8%HHs are persons with disabilities which needs to be considered in the response. The proposed initiative aims to ensure safe accessibility for Women, men, girls, persons with disabilities and transwomen regardless of age to the WASH services. These activities are complementary to the other sectors especially with the health and nutrition sector to have direct impact to reduce the severe malnutrition and moderate malnutrition as well as reduced the mortality and morbidity rate in the response area.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Lack of female leadership in the community, Limited access to freedom of choice, involvement in unpaid care work are common for girls and women that increase their vulnerably. The main reason is the taboo and stigma, cultural, religious issues and also lack of access to proper services. In context assessment (ACF area), 9% of males are involved in the HHS water collection whereas 79% of females and 12% of children are involved. Facilities don’t consider the preference of pregnant women, aged and persons with disabilities. 49% latrine is not a gender segregation sign. 97% latrine having issues with lightening system. Latrine issues with lock system, proper doors also lead to inequalities concerning access to service considered protection, safety and privacy, SGBV. Considering the protection, safety and dignity, ACF will prioritized the identified area. ACF will also address the menstrual hygiene management in the WASH facilities.

3.2 - Gender

Women, men and girls, boys, persons with disabilities and transgender regardless of age and sex are included in the project. To address the person with disabilities and special needs person, ACF will consider assistive device at HHs level and will ensure required assistance in the WASH facilities. To address the female special needs, ACF will ensure MHM kits, provision for proper MHM in the latrine and awareness session (include husbands, mothers in law also). It will also reduce the stigma and taboo in relation to Safe MHM. Involvement of women and different aged groups in the different committees and user groups will increase women empowerment and decision-making capacity. Involvement of the community people will reflect the choice of freedom, easy access and comfort use the facilities. Involvement of all people in health & hygiene education will lead to the transform of the gender that will motivate all groups to maintain personal and environmental hygiene.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Action Against hunger believes that everyone we come into contact with, regardless of age, gender identity, disability, sexual orientation or ethnic origin has the right to be protected from all forms of harm, abuse, neglect and exploitation. Action Against Hunger operates a zero tolerance policy in relation to all forms of bullying, harassment, abuse and exploitation by staff or associated personnel. ACF apply child safeguarding in the intervention. Action Against Hunger staff, Associated Personnel, Partners and Suppliers must not: • Engage in sexual activity with anyone under the age of 18 • Sexually abuse or exploit children • Subject a child to physical, emotional or psychological mistreatment, or neglect • Engage in any commercially exploitative activities with children including child labor or trafficking. ACF will ensure the safeguard for all age including the children at the time of WASH activity implementation.

3.4 - Diversity

Access to the WASH is a human right which is to ensure good health and well-being. ACF will ensure diversity in terms of access to the WASH with non-discrimination principle among the all people in the repose area. ACF will ensure equitable access to WASH services, including equitable NFI distribution (Soap, MHM kits, Baby potty, assistive device) to the people in needs. ACF will respect and prioritize the cultural sensitivity issues in all the stages of implementation. Complaint response mechanism and design of the facilities will reflect the choice of freedom, easy access and comfort use the facilities. Involvement of diverse people in health & hygiene education will lead to the transform the gender because receiving health and hygiene education will motivate all groups to maintain personal and environmental hygiene. ACF will ensure the functionalities of the WASH facilities to ensure equitable access by all people regardless of the age, religious and ethnicity.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Community people will influence the project by involving them in different phases of the implementation. Community leaders, natural leaders, youth will be included in the facilities’ user group, different WASH committees, health and hygiene education groups. The joint monitoring system will ensure by the user group and committee. To have the common understanding and information sharing ACF will continue organize the different meeting with the community leaders like Mazi, Religious leader from the beginning of the project period. The overall achievement and the challenges will be shared in the monthly coordination meeting organized by the respective Camp-in-Charge in where all the other actors are participated. ACF will Scale-up a compline feedback mechanism process and will ensure access to better WASH services and other protection issues. Besides the CFMC system, ACF will response all complain that received directly from the side of beneficiaries and others stakeholders.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, ACF will ensure preparedness and response in line with the WASH sector's strategy in case of any natural disaster and outbreak through the timely activation of the formed RRT ACF will conduct an environmental and vulnerability assessment considering the landslide, flood, and cyclone risk to mitigate high WASH disease prevalence as a preventive barrier, which is considered as a high priority. ACF is one of the first respondents in the Covid-19 outbreak. ACF will also work to control AWD, scabies and dengue in the propped area. ACF will continue to maintain its contingency plan and capacity building of its staff as well as communities and other stakeholders involved to respond to needs in an efficient and timely manner. ACF will continue coordination and communication with relevant stakeholders and teams for emergency response. To prevent consequences of natural disasters, ACF will strive to ensure resilient infrastructures.
