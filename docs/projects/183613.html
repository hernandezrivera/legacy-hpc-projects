Project Summary

The project will contribute to achieving the HRP SO3: “The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations & other root causes of threats & shocks are pursued”. The project will also contribute to achieve the FSS SO1 & SO2 Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, & their resilience, FS & productive capacity restored/enhanced” & “HHs suffering from limited access to food & to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs”.
Food security in Palestine namely in GS continued to be impacted by the nature of protracted crisis, repeated shocks of nature & man-made including but not limited to escalation of hostilities, restrictions on Ppl movement & goods in & out of Gaza, & lastly the impact of the C-19 pandemic due to the imposition of safety & protection measures to contain the pandemic. 53% of GS Ppl are poor, while 44.7% reported unemployed, & 80% receive a kind of food assistance.
WB in its recent damage assessment (June 2021), reported that the Agri sector suffered a direct damage of $ 45 M. While MoA estimated it was accounting for $ 55M. More than 26,000 dunums of orchards, vegetable crops & greenhouses, & more than 450 livestock & poultry farms, were fully or partially destroyed. According to the MoL, more than 19 thousand workers were affected by direct damages to active facilities, 37% of them working in the agriculture sector. The project aims at enhancing & protecting the livelihoods of vulnerable farmers (M/F) through the land rehabilitation, Agri wells rehabilitation & PV system, poultry farms rehabilitation, CfW & CVA. Female farmers as economically disadvantaged but form the strongest foundation for developing the status & role of Palestinian women. Statistics indicate that almost 90% of the women in informal economy work in agriculture, & rural women form 30% of the Palestinian women’s power. Homestead agriculture is the traditional job of Palestinian women in rural areas. With the change of the socio-economic conditions, women are breaking through the traditional norms & coming forward to participate in development activities outside their homestead, such as carrying out Agri field work, selling in markets their Agri products, participating in Agri exhibitions to market their products, etc.
The project will be based on BBB approach to restore & enhance the resilience of the Agri sector & productive mechanism including the most affected groups of SSF, breeders & HHs. The project will target 200 SSF (190M/10F)5% women. In total 1100 (431M/285 W/129B/255G). the Project includes the provision of Agri inputs, maintenance of one water Agri wells & PV system for 50 SSF (45 M/5 F). some of 275 people in total including (108M/71W/32B/64G). 40 SS poultry breeders will be also supported with rehabilitation & Agri inputs (35 men/5 women) in total 220 including (86M/57W/26B/51G). 200 vulnerable HHs will be benefited from CVA for 2 rounds (100 men/100 women); meaning that 1100 Ppl in total including (431M/285 W/129B/255G), In addition, CfW activity will be provided to unemployed unskilled Agri workers through the creation of 100 temporary opportunities for 50 men & 50 women in the Agri labors including( 215M143W/64B128G) Where the CFW will create opportunities that can temporarily stabilize unemployed unskilled Agri labors. The nature of the work conducted by the labors will cover pre harvesting, harvesting, and post harvesting, food processing throughout the flow of work in their previous employment.
In total, the project will target 590 SSF, breeders, workers & vulnerable HHs; 3245 Ppl including (1271M, 841W, 380B/ 753G). The project interventions were closely coordinated with the FSS, MoA, MoSD, & partner NGOs & INGOs. It was principally coordinated with UWAC, ACTED, OXFAM, & PUI. 1 - Needs

Economy of the GS has entered a general stagnation since the imposition of the Israeli blockade, which led to the almost complete closure of commercial crossings & severe restrictions on the movement of merchants & businessmen. According to the UNCTAD report issued on November 25, 2020, the economic cost of the Israeli occupation of the GS during the past decade was estimated at $16.7 billion. The recent hostilities in May 2021 caused widespread destruction to the agricultural sector. Damages were divided into direct & indirect damages including the damage of hundreds of acres of vegetables & trees as a result of direct targeting or interruption of irrigation nets during the hostilities. Official reports indicated that hostilities also caused indirect damages by disrupting economic interests such as factories, farms, fishing & other economic activities, & the result of closing the crossings preventing exports & preventing the import of raw materials, which It led to significant losses in various economic sectors. The MoA pointed out that there is a state of weakness & lack of marketing capacity at times for many agricultural, plant & animal products as a result of restricted movement & difficulty in accessing agricultural lands & animal & fish facilities, as well as market disruption, in addition to the absence of an export opportunity for many products. Gender Alert of UNWOMEN 2021 reported the fact that HHs including farmers’ HHs living in or dependent on the access restricted areas which make up nearly 35% of Gaza’s cultivable land & 85% of its maritime area (such as farmers, herders, Bedouins, & fishers) have suffered enormously due this latest round of displacement, injury, inability to continue livelihood activities. Result of this Needs Assessment are based on verification of the RNA results conducted by PARC recently by using different qualitative & quantitative tools & methods. Findings: Providing agricultural inputs In early 2021, the assessment targeted 465 open land farmers (North 29.9%, Gaza 13.5%, Der Al Balah 21.7%, Khan Younis 23.7%, Rafah 11.2%). At the time of the assessment, the prevailing percentage of them (89.4%) reported that they are suffering from lack of access to agriculture inputs, & more than 65% of them reported that their lands were affected by the escalation of hostilities. As part of the verification process, filed visits done to assess the needs of the surveyed farmers earlier, about 200 farmers (1120 benefs of which 129 F). The verification visits indicated that the situation has gotten worse, given the recent escalations, & the C19 restrictions. Farmers reported that they are in urgent need for agricultural inputs to cultivate their farms. 93% demanded the provision of irrigation networks & low farming tunnels to enhance the productivity of their farms & cultivate it all-year-round. Low farming tunnels will encourage farmers to continue farming activities & gain income. The assessment also included farmers outside the ARA, where their needs were similar to those inside the ARA, while their focus was on requesting full & partial land rehabilitation as reported by (87%) of them. The project will provide farmers with agricultural inputs,, seeds, seedlings, fertilizers, tools & irrigation networks. Rehabilitation of agricultural wells & PV system Based on PARC assessment, 50 farmers & their families in Absan El Jadeda (280 individuals including 138 F) are suffering from the inability to access to water well which is considered as the main source of irrigation in the area. As 83% of them reported difficulties in pumping the water to their lands, & 79% reported that recurrent power cuts prevent them from accessing to irrigation water in irrigation times. Based on the identified needs, the project aims to rehabilitate the well & provide it with a solar energy system to ensure maintaining continuous water flow for the farmers. Rehabilitation of poultry farms PARC assessed the needs of 100 poultry farmers (500 individuals, 215 of them are F) in Bait Lahia, Bait Hanoun, Ezbet Abd Rabu, Al Shujaia, Al Zaytoun, & Johr Al Deek, where the assessment targeted only small & medium poultry farmers, & showed that Poultry farmers in the GS suffer from many crises that beset them & have led to the reluctance of many of them to work in this profession. Poultry recently witnessed great losses due to the high production costs, the unprecedented rise in temperatures, & the deprivation of farmers of their right to export products abroad since fifteen years. To this end, the project aims to support poultry farmers to upgrade their farms to be able to better deal with the climate change, & maintain the temperature of the farm through providing them with photovoltaic system, as well as providing them with production inputs to enable them to overcome some of the challenges they face. Cash for work Based on PARC assessment in early 2021, about 78% of the surveyed workers suffer from bad economic situation due to lack of employment opportunities especially after the C19 crisis. More than 83% of the surveyed employers were forced to relieve part of their workers (especially unskilled & semi-skilled) due to their inability to cover their financial dues. 75% of the surveyed workers reported that they were not able to obtain another employment opportunity after C19. To that end, the current project aims to provide short term employment opportunities to 100 unskilled & semi-skilled (50M & 50 F). In-kind food distribution: In the verification process, 210 farmers were surveyed to assess their food security, of which(1000 individuals, 450 F) found to be food insecure. To that end, the project aims to provide the benefs with 2 rounds of food parcels & hygiene kit. The food parcels will contain both fresh & dried food items.

2 - Activities

The project will be implemented by the Agricultural Development Association as lead applicant and lead implementing agency. PARC will be cooperating with the its local partner CBOs and protection committees and will be establishing local project committee for the sake of this project. PARC will adopt the BBB as one of the most appropriate and well examined approached in post emergency settings that reduces vulnerability to future disasters and builds community resilience to address physical, social, environmental, and economic vulnerabilities and shocks. The project has 5 main activities aiming to provide emergency support and enhance resilience of the agricultural sector and other vulnerable groups and HHs including small-scale farmers, breeders, agricultural assets like poultry and wells as well as vulnerable HHs and impacted agricultural labors. The project activities are as the following: Land Rehabilitation and the provision of productive agricultural inputs for farmers Provision of rehabilitation services for 200 farmers 2 dunums each. Where the rehabilitation services will include land preparation, extension of irrigation network. As well as providing agriculture inputs including agriculture tools, seeds and seedlings, and organic fertilizers. Rehabilitation of agricultural wells and PV system This activity will involve providing engineering evaluation /design report for the ground water well rehabilitation, installing of submersible pump 20 Horsepower , replacement of polyethylene riser pipe, provision of solar energy system, construction of electrical room, and fitting and accessories (water meter, Valves, Elbow, T connection …etc). Rehabilitation of poultry farms Assisting 40 poultry farmers upgrading their farms to provide for better colling in the summer and warming in wintertime, as well as assisting the poultry farmers with suppling them of production inputs to restore their production capacities. Cash for work Providing 100 unskilled and semi-skilled workers (50 males, and 50 females) who were affected by the recent escalations and the recent layoffs as a result of Covid19 with 3 months employment opportunities. Based on the CWG CFW standards, each, 42 NIS and 65 INS per day will be based for 22 workdays for unskilled and skilled labors respectively. CVA to vulnerable HHs Provision of unconditional CVA to 200 vulnerable HHs with focus and priority based on certain vulnerability selection criteria including but not limited to (poverty, food insecurity, lack of financial source, unemployability, disability or death of main breadwinner, HHs with female Heads of HHs as well as existence of PWDs among the HHs. The activity will involve distributing 2 round of food parcels to the targeted HHs. important to mention that PARC has recently achieved the issuing of its PSEA policy. Now all projects including this is built in activities involving and dissemination of PSEA raising awareness and distribution of material including information on rights and safe complaint channels available to beneficiaries and PARC staff. Within the policy and attached procedure, certain PSEA focal point is there to receive and examine complaints and achieve the process and the required referral of complaints of PSEA, in accordance with the PARC PSEA policy recently acknowledged. The PSEA will be introduced to project staff and will be are required to attend a minimum of one PSEA training.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PARC has a well-articulated Feedback and Response Mechanism (FRM) that is put in action from the design phase until closing. FRM at PARC is ensured with respect to Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS). The CHS adopted by PARC will ensure to provide a safe/dignified space for targeted population to express their feedback and complaints. In June 2021, PARC updated its accountability policies (information sharing, complaints and feedback and participation) and the Safeguarding policy hand on hand with Accountability specialist from Christian Aid-UK. Accordingly, PARC consulted with local communities to identify the most appropriate channels suitable for them. Hence, a dedicated email (Complaints@pal-arc.org), complaint boxes distributed across the governorates, in addition to the MEAL Officer phone number (Whatts App). These three channels will be utilized to receive feedback and complaints. Then, based on PARC accountability policies the complaint shall be registered into a well-designed complaint form that will be tracked and documented through the MEAL documentation system. Additionally, all project activities will be followed by the MEAL officer within the project MEAL framework. With reference to CHS, complaint mechanism will be elaborated at targeted communities and audience will be encouraged to claim their rights. Field staff will announce the availability of the three feedback mechanisms for the local communities and ensure that any complaint or feedback shall be addressed will be taken seriously including any sensitive complaints. Any complaints received shall be protected in accordance to data protection protocols adopted by PARC. Feedback mechanisms will also be promoted and implemented in coordination with local committees (LCs).In regards to safeguarding complaints (SEA), PARC has a well-articulated policy with clear standard data protection procedures and will take any sensitive complaint seriously when proven correct no tolerance policy shall be applied.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Protection mainstreaming is getting essential and should be impeded in every activity and/or intervention planned to be implemented. “Do No Harm" is seen as a way of ensuring accountability towards the local communities. This approach utilized community participation, information sharing, feedback and response mechanism with respect to Core Humanitarian standards. Which is also in line to environment protection and gender mainstreaming. The designed project adheres to social, political, economic and health context of targeted populations and is based on comprehensive understanding verified through the participatory needs assessment and updated risk analysis. In regards to social context, PARC understands the conservative local Palestinian context and act on that in all project phases taking into consideration the different gender needs such as women’s, PwD, and children have safe access to services. Political context, all proposed interventions will take into consideration the sensitivity of its location in which a low-profile working approach will take place. This will happen by supporting the local community to document the Israeli violations. Additionally, PARC team will report any violation and incidents to official bodies like OCHA. Finally, at economic level the project interventions aid to support local population to empower their production abilities and access to their assets. Continued consultations will be conducted with the project target groups/stakeholders and HHs to analysis potential risks that will be taken into consideration during implementation. PARC will activate its Emergency preparedness and Response plan EPRP and communicate with its partners and Beneficiaries on best and worst scenarios to reach joint agreement how to proceed. PARC will maintain context and risk analysis given the changing humanitarian and political context in Gaza to inform its implementation methodology and approach. PARC will do its best to refer identified GBV cases to service providers through the agreed coordination mechanism. For this specific intervention, PARC will use A mix of assistance modalities of service delivery, cash for work and in-kind. Important to mention that PARC team adheres to the developed Standard of Operation SoPs for the implementation of the different activities, field visits, M&E, complaint and feedback mechanism to ensure all safety and security measures are taken against COVID-19. PARC believes in equal opportunities for all beneficiaries; ensuring equal access of information, neutrality and assistance through the project cycle starting with the inclusion of all relevant stakeholders in the need assessment. Coordination with the local authorities, CBOs, and community leaders in the project planning and selection process to ensure optimal community participation, outreach, and transparency in service provision. PARC ensures a transparent selection process which avoids social conflict during the implementation. The project is designed based on Human-Rights Based Approaches (HRBA) that includes women, youth and Persons with Disabilities (PwDs). HRBA; focusing on the actual needs of people and integrating principles of accountability, transparency, meaningful participation, none discrimination and linkage to global, regional and national human rights instruments are an integral part of PARC work.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PARC ensures that aid and impartial access reaches the most vulnerable since the design phase when conducting a need assessment at communities level. Gender mainstreaming and protection approaches will be integrated within the project activities. In need assessment PARC ensures to engage local community members, CBOs and other relevant stakeholders to have a comprehensive understanding of the targeted community’s needs including the participation of women and women led cooperatives and clubs. The findings of the need assessment define the proposed interventions. This is then followed at the implementation phase of the proposed project, in which PARC starts its intervention with formulating a Project Local Committee that represents the targeted population and include key actors from the area and representatives of Community Based Organizations which ensures optimal outreach, transparency and ownership to vulnerable community groups. The project Local Committee and PARC team plays a role in ensuring the access to information to all community members through a clear gender sensitive project announcement in the mosques, village councils, and PARC’s social media platforms. PARC team develops a well-defined selection criterion that responds to the needs of the targeted population and ensures accessing the most vulnerable( Children, PwDs, elderly, women headed HH… etc). This will be hand on hand with the project local committee. An open call of applications will also ensure the meaningful access, empowerment and transparent process in selecting final beneficiaries which will also be in participation with Project Local Committee. This participatory approach emphasizes ownership, transparency, good governance, gender balance at beneficiary and community level. Weekly meetings will be held with project team, project local committee, community members for each community. PARC accountability system follows a clear mechanism of complaint registration, reporting and solving, and is accessible by the local community members. Regular follow-up of complaints received from communities, CBOs, and community members are performed with PARC’s M&E unit, including when necessary, addressing OCHA for larger external complaints concerning security and overall occupation related to protection risks. Meaningful access, participation, and empowerment: through the established LCs which ensure optimal outreach to affected and vulnerable target groups. Additionally, PARC will share any human rights-based violations to relevant clusters. Special attention will also be given to people’s access to services under restrictions due to Covid19 outbreak, and online consultations and virtual meetings will be available to targeted beneficiaries to communicate their challenges and voice these needs/rights, which PARC will ensure sharing with relevant stakeholders.
