Project Summary

Polish Humanitarian Action (PAH) project aims to provide timely and appropriate integrated responses in the field of general protection, child protection and GBV through responses in areas of needs for IDPs, returnees and host communities to increase resilience and coping mechanisms including risk reduction. PAH will focus to support GBV survivors through the provision of transport services to access health and psycho-social services as well as provision of dignity kits. Child protection will be integrated mainly with Education projects with key focus on psycho-social support. PAH will ensure mainstreaming protection across sectors of WASH, S/NFI, FSL, Education and Nutrition. Mainstreaming activities will be implemented at all stages of the project by conducting protection assessments to inform the intervention modalities and context dynamics. The project will be implemented in conflict affected areas such as Jonglei State, Central Equatoria State, Eastern Equatoria State, Western Equatoria State, Unity State, Northern and Western Bahr-El-Gazal States, Lakes State and Upper Nile State. These areas are where PAH mobile and static teams have been working since 2006. Depending on local dynamics and accessibility of locations, the mobile response teams shall deploy across South Sudan. Hence, the above locations are areas where PAH has experience with multisector interventions but other locations may be flagged for responses during 2020. Support to GBV survivors is planned in areas of static presence, particularly in Jonglei and other areas or PAH's experience. The project will mainly target communities with poor access to safe water supply, communities with poor hygiene and sanitation practices, nutrition centres, schools, health units, and other locations targeted with FSL, S/NFI and Education activities, to ensure more holistic approach. As part of GBV response, women friendly spaces will be established with skills training provided, ex. fuel energy stoves making, mat waving, etc. (integrated with FSL, SNFI, WASH sectors). The project will promote inclusion of women, people with disabilities and girls through distribution of WASH-NFIs, Shelter NFIs with specific package that address issues of dignity for women and girls. This project will closely address the protracted inclusion and support of displaced people and returnees mainly women, girls, children, elderly and people with disabilities to participate freely in appropriate sectoral activities through participatory needs assessments, awareness creation on hygiene and sanitation including GBV, nutrition, child protection, child’s rights, peaceful coexistence. As part of child protection, PAH team will support family reunification in both static and mobile responses. In static locations, child friendly spaces will be established within the schools with key activities, including sport events and crafts. Psycho-social support training will be provided to the teachers, PTAs, and key community members. Project will be implemented through static presence and mobile teams based in Juba that will be deployed to field location where recommended responses for protection and other sectors of PAH’s activities (S/NFI, FSL, WASH, Education) will be flagged. To ensure continuity and improve the quality of protection services, PAH will continue to work with existing protection structures and volunteers, building their capacities through training and coaching to equip them with the relevant skills to continue addressing protection issues. PAH will conduct service providers and stake holders’ mapping to draw a clear referral pathway including coordinated response in all the project communities. PAH will promote peaceful co-existence between IDPs, returnees and the host communities under the do no harm principle, this will be achieved through working closely with key leadership at every stage of the response.

1 - Needs

The ongoing instabilities in most parts of the country still causing displacement of community and the economic downturn which estimated 7,2 million people in humanitarian need. Main vulnerabilities are linked to forced displacement, food insecurity and undernutrition. South Sudan is first and foremost a protection crisis, with widespread and acute needs in all essential sectors. In relation to protection 5,7 million people are estimated to be in need of humanitarian protection in 2019. 65% of women and girls in South Sudan (SSD) have experienced physical and/or sexual violence in their lifetime (IRC, 2017)There are wide-scale and severe violations of International Humanitarian Law and Human Rights Law, including killing and torture of civilians, along ethnic and political lines. Sexual and Gender-Based Violence is widespread. Conflict-related sexual violence is a particularly disturbing pattern, systematically perpetrated against women, girls, boys, and men. We can observe significant raise of reporting cases of GBV – 72% raise comparing the data from 2017 and 2019 years (data from the first halve of both years). It is estimated that some 1.9 million72 boys and girls face acute and severe protection risks in 2019. Children are still forcibly recruited into armed forces/groups. The ongoing fighting and the resulting displacement continue to separate families. Attacks, destruction and occupation of health structures and schools continue unabated, while lootings and destruction of aid facilities are widespread. Landmines and unexploded ordnance pose constant threats to the civilian population. The recent PAH protection assessment done in Bor South, in May and August 2019 indicate cases of rape or attempted rape of women and adolescent girls. These incidents reportedly happened at firewood collection areas and along the road. In the payams of assessment there is no SGBV actor on the ground to provide prevention and response activities such as proper awareness raising on SGBV or Psycho-Social Support (PSS) and counselling. Providing support for GBV service providers to arrange transport or escort for the GBV survivors to reach the needed services, to ensure the safety and security of the survivors or witnesses was one or the major findings . In May 2019 PAH conducted protection assessment in Yei which indicated similar situation. Although, due to high military presence the case of rape perpetrated by armed forces were reported. . Communities are not valuing the dignified comfort of women and girls but rather view them as source of income which increases the levels of abuses happening to IDPs and returnees.. Ethnic and tribal divisions affect the ability of the partners to deploy qualified national staff on the ground. Assistance needs to be delivered to both government and opposition-held areas, based on clear situation analysis in order to reduce potential protection risks. PAH will collect information on protection risks and threats and the protection oﬃcers will carry out in-depth protection analysis while supporting the static and mobile teams across the country. Hence, the specific locations with a detailed descriptions of needs will be identified at a later stage and these analyses will shape the responses accordingly in all interventions – including in other sectors.

2 - Activities

1. Protection assessments, risk analysis, stakeholders mapping and needs verification with clear AGD data to understand context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level. This will be done through focus group discussions, key informant interviews, meetings and household surveys with women, men, children, institution workers, community leaders, within IDPs, host community and returnee population. 2. Awareness creation on protection related issues (GBV, child protection, early warnings and early response, peaceful co-existence, dignified access to WASH, S’NFI, FSL, Education services, inclusion of people with disabilities etc.) through interactive sessions and use of information/education/communication (IEC) materials with protection/GBV/child’s rights, messages targeting women, girls, boys, men, people with disabilities etc. 3. Provision of transport services to GBV survivors to access health care and psycho-social services. Those will include psychological first aid, information on services that may be able to assist the survivor details on how to access these services and appropriate support to help the survivor access these services. Providing information to survivors in an ethical, safe and confidential manner about their rights and options to access care is a cornerstone element of a survivor-centred referral system. 4. Training the staff on GBV guiding principles and PFA; ensuring that staff know the GBV referral focal points (RFPs) for Jonglei state and the referral pathways; Training the staff, volunteers and community representatives on psycho-social support. 5. Provision of hygiene and dignity kits for women and girls of productive age and GBV survivors (dignity kits, menstrual pads, underwear, washing soap, bathing soap, solar lamps, etc.) 6. Women and girl friendly spaces: conducting skills training on proper techniques for constructing transitional shelter and energy-saving stoves, mat waving, etc. 7. Psycho-social Support Activities in Child Friendly Spaces, schools and communities’ institutions, including establishing the CFSs, training the teachers, PTA's, key community members, providing materials for the activities. 8. Support of family reunification within static and mobile responses, in collaboration with child protection partner working in the area. 9. Awareness-raising sessions, pieces of training of stakeholders and other community structure/actors on GBV prevention and response concepts; awareness-raising on promotion and protection of human rights, women rights, children rights and people with disability rights. 10. Mainstreaming of protection across sectors of Shelter NFIs, FSL, Education, and WASH and integrating approach to cash programming across all clusters. PAH will ensure meaningful access to services for men and women, including people with disabilities, the elderly and children. The mobile teams will encourage community participation in the project design. Safe and accessible feedback mechanism will be established. During the interventions mobile teams will focus on empowering communities and promote resilient approaches. Safety and dignity of the beneficiaries will be in the centre of interventions. 11. Post-intervention monitoring (PIM) will be done by independent PAH M&E team across the project, as long as the location is still accessible and secure. PIMs gives a clear view of whether beneficiaries have profited from the provided assistance and whether they are satisfied with the quality and safety of carried activities. These reports will provide clear lessons learnt and recommendations and will be used to improve the overall quality of PAH’s projects. Regular evaluation of the projects will ensure accountability. Static responses are planned where PAH has static presence (Bor South, Wau, Jur River, Magwi, Kajo Kaji, Yei, Koch). Mobile is planned for all the other locations mentioned in the relevant chapter of the proposal. It will be defined on the needs and cluster recommendations.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

PAH has high standards of conducting assessments and distributions, always concerning rights of beneficiaries and in full accountability. Before the activities are conducted, the beneficiaries are mobilized and made aware of the project activities, of their rights and selection criteria, what they will get and what is not in the project. Local authorities, chiefs and the entire community are enlightened of the selection criteria and how the registration is done. Beneficiaries will be asked to sign on the forms as a confirmation for receiving assistance. Distributions and other activities are conducted with high sensitivity to the special needs of women, children, elderly and disabled. PAH put strong attention to cultural norms and local law, acts always within the law of South Sudan, while humanitarian standards and international human rights are our highest guidepost. "Do no harm" principle is fully respected. All beneficiaries and affected population are informed about compliant mechanisms, there are multiple ways of asking questions, introducing queries and complaints, which are always considered and answered by our staff. Personal data of beneficiaries are protected according to European standards (GDPR). PAH professional Monitoring Team pays special attention during post-intervention monitoring to check the respect of rights of beneficiaries.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Vulnerable internally displaced people are the primary beneficiaries of PAH project. PAH will involve affected people right from the assessment in the process of needs identification and activities will be designed to ensure meaningful access to services provided based on needs of the displaced people. To ensure that services are provided to the targeted beneficiaries, PAH will also use people from within the affected people to deliver assistance and implement the project activities for example, hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, enumerators among others will also be selected from among the IDPs.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

PAH will work in close relationship with local communities focusing on traditional structures and mechanisms in coordination, strengthening relations between different stakeholders at the local level with local leaders and community leaders like the Boma chiefs, Payam administrators among others ensuring proper information sharing. During assessment, PAH will involve the community in identifying their needs and coming up with recommendations. Intervention terms of terms of references will be shared with the community leaders and the general community like women groups in FGDs to ensure the community take part in decision making and implementation.At the same time, PAH will protect the vulnerable beneficiaries (ex. women), who can face obstacles in asking for assistance, due to cultural reasons and discrimination. PAH will promote peace-building and respectful coexistence of different societal, ethnic groups.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Project is in line with all four Protection cluster’s objectives. This project is aligned to country objectives for targeting newly displaced IDPs, returnees and most vulnerable host community experiencing new vulnerability, both in Protection activities and in mainstreaming protection in PAH WASH, S/NFI, FSL and Education projects.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The protection environment is challenging, due to high needs in the affected population. There are big groups of people requiring professional assistance in terms of healing traumas, GBV case management, child-protection, disabled protection etc. PAH, as very experienced in work across South Sudan, has good capacity in the management of activities in such an environment. The monitoring team has protection-related questionnaires, developed by protection partners, approved by protection cluster. The project measures the safety of beneficiaries by establishing a complaint mechanism, consultative problem identification and solving through dialogue. The tools gather key feedback from beneficiaries on safety related to assistance delivery. Where distributions are conducted, demonstration on the use of items is done and safe disposal of packaging materials is encouraging during the demonstrations. PAH mainstreams protection throughout the project ensuring that activities target the most vulnerable, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the human rights of the beneficiaries without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence.The project focus on beneficiary’s safety through protection risk analysis survey, safety audit tools exercises and access negotiation supported by UNOCHA. PAH, field security protocol will be used to the analysis security situation, use the humanitarian hubs to share security updates in the field before any intervention. Close coordination with UNDSS to receive and report security incidences. The project will also mainstream environmental management for sustainability of the project.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

PAH takes steps towards building resilience and reducing future risks for targeted communities. This project will have a component on working in collaboration with National NGO partner where possible, having joint activities and joint trainings.
