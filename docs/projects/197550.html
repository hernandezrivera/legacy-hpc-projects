Project Summary

Darna Al-Aman project was successfully launched in 2021 and aimed to support safe return of Raqqa’s civilian population through protection and health integrated services and the set-up of safe spaces for Women and Girls and for Children respectively, as well as GBV and CP response. Funding is being sought for follow up Action and continuity of essential services.
The protection component includes enhancement of WGSS, adjacent to the Raqqa Hospital, and a CFS, acting as entry point for women, girls and boys allowing better access to services including integrated CM. The holistic and integrated approach increases access to protection services. Services targeting women and girls include informative sessions, PSS and life-skills activities, GBV CM services, and referral to other existing services. Moreover, parenting skills sessions and sessions for young mothers and caregivers will be provided. The programme supports a CFS, when IP staff provide PSS, life-skills and recreational activities aimed at young children and adolescents, specialized CM services for CP cases, and awareness sessions for caregivers and community members on CP risks.
A key element of the action will continue to be a Capacity Building plan including integrated training, supervision and mentoring for protection, medical and nonmedical staff. To ensure high standard services, all the staff will be trained/receive refreshers on PSEA, Safeguarding, CoC, CP, WPE, GBV Core Concepts, safe identification and referral of cases, etc. Refreshers for health staff will also be included for CMR training to improve the management of these cases within 72 hours.
The project addresses gender and age markers. The intervention is based on a thorough needs analysis and a participatory approach through programme lifecycle. The widespread GBV, SEA, child labor, trauma and the stigma resulting from all this kind of issues are taken into account into the project design. The Action seeks to address the specific needs of women, men, boys and girls, including persons with disabilities, female headed households and older persons; regular consultations with persons with specific needs, including persons with disabilities, will be conducted throughout the project implementation to ensure inclusion and to tailor the activities according to the specific target group. Project coordinator's staff will conduct regular Protection Mainstreaming assessments in the safe spaces and in the health facility to ensure that assistance is provided in a safe, dignified, accessible and participatory manner. If necessary, infrastructure adaptations will take place to guarantee access to services to children/persons with disability. UPP and IPs staff are trained on Disability & Inclusion and activities are adapted to the needs of the different groups. Outreach activities will ensure involvement of hard-to-reach groups. Key messages will be developed through consultations with community members, including persons with disabilities, to ensure adaptation to the context and to the needs of different groups. Data collected is disaggregated by age and gender (SAAD).
Continuity for CM will also be guaranteed, and the satisfaction of the beneficiaries will be measured thanks to anonymous surveys, internal and external referrals database, feedback and complaint boxes.
The expected result is to reach up to 300,000 community members with integrated health and protection services, including 10,312 beneficiaries of protection services incl. outreach, awareness sessions and campaigns, informative sessions, PSS and recreational activities. Moreover, at least 218 women and girls survivors or at risk of GBV and children at risk will received specialized CM services. As per the capacity building, the target is 95 staff members including Project coordinator and IP staff.
The intervention will foster synergies among medical staff and protection staff, and a closed cooperation between Health, CP and GBV WGs and CM TFs.

3 - Needs

Based on the NGO assmt., women and children continue to be the groups most at risk in Raqqa. Children continue to be severely affected by the conflict as they are exposed to multiples protection risk and grave rights violations. Raqqa remains one of the Gov. with the highest child protection needs in NES. According to the KIIs conducted during the HNO MNSA child marriage took place in 83,5% of the assessed communities of Raqqa Governorate. Other key needs highlighted by the PWG members and identified by the assmt. conducted in June-Aug ‘21 include lack of educational, recreational and PSS services such as those offered in CFS; lack of specialized CM Services; amongst widespread violence and abuse against children (incl. corporal punishment and psychological abuse, child labour, substance abuse). GBV continues to disproportionately affect the lives of women and girls (WG) in NES. Based on the assessment conducted in in June-Aug ‘21 and according to the data collected for the 2021 report Voices from Syria, IPV and family violence are amongst the main types of GBV experience by WG in Raqqa. WG in Raqqa reported experiences of violence and harassment while leaving home preventing them to access public sphere including market places and humanitarian and public services. According to HRP Syria 2022 report 71% communities mention that child marriage is an issue for adolescent girls. Safe spaces, PSS and GBV life-saving services are one of the main gaps in Raqqa.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project aims to reach 10312 community members ( 2,880 male, 6,832 female) through the protection activities implemented. The protection activities will be provided to persons of different age groups (0-5y, 6-12y, 13-17y and adult women and caregivers) and will aim to engage persons with disabilities. For the WGSS and CFS, the implementation will be constantly monitored and evaluated through satisfaction surveys, FGDs with WGBM, safety audit, KII with community stakeholders, safety & community mapping, FCRM that will be tailored to AGD specific needs. PSS activities, recreational sessions and informative sessions will be organized for women and girls. In health facility, prevention outreach workers will cooperate with health staff to identify and refer the most vulnerable cases to internal or external specialized services. The intervention includes tailored activities for different age groups will include: Community based and child friendly assessment; Public information events; PSS activities; Child-friendly activities; Awareness raising campaigns on health, protection, and rights; Activities and focus groups with parents; Service mapping; multisector referrals.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA efforts continue as part of the NGO core capacity building pillar, as well as coordination, best practice and advocacy through the Co-Chairship of the PSEA Network. According to a WoS PSEA Network/HNAP report (Oct '21), there is a low level of SEA awareness amongst beneficiaries in NES. The NG and IPs work to raise awareness on protection topics incl. SEA awareness. From a localization approach PSEA and Protection technical team work with local IPs to support in development/enhancement of IPs integrity package policies and practices (recruitment, CoCs, assessments etc). The PSEA policy is then shared with the IPs and all the staff members abide by it. Moreover, regular trainings on PSEA are foreseen as part of the capacity building plan for staff in the WGSS, CFS and the nearby Health Facility. Awareness sessions on PSEA are planned to inform the community about the policy and how to report cases of SEA. Finally, a complaint box and a hotline for FCRM including PSEA is available for PoC to be able to report if needed. The NGO and IP regularly conduct consultations with PoC, including children and caregivers, to ensure that FCRM are accessible to different groups. A child-friendly FCRM is set up based on the feedback of children and caregivers and it is adapted to the different age groups and specific needs. NGO will further promote the use of common tools developed through PSEA Network and GBV/CP sWGs to prevent/mitigate SEA risks, including the PSEA Child Friendly FCRM.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security issues in Raqqa are still a main concern. Though the situation appears stable, security alarms continue in Raqqah city. For instance, tension might be heightened by terrorist groups or Daesh sleeper cells. For operations in both Syria and Iraq, the NGO refers to the internally adapted manual "Security Risk Management NGO Approach". Moreover, the staff regularly benefit from training courses on Security in crisis contexts. Risk assessments take place on a regular basis and a thorough monitoring phase of the activities and the sites occurs constantly to prevent or deal with security issues. Concerning the pandemic, a high risk exists that the number of people infected with COVID-19 will continue to increase. This could slightly slow down the implementation of the activities and undermine the full functioning of the WGSS and the CFS. However, preventive measures as social distancing, limited number of participants and PPEs will always be in place to prevent the spread of the virus. As per the implementation of the activities, socio-cultural barriers exist in Raqqah area concerning the Protection sector and especially the GBV component. As a mitigation measure, activities will be designed and implemented as much as possible with the involvement and the participation of women and girls, who will themselves express their needs, the gaps in the society. KII, FGDs, surveys and assessments will take place in Raqqa area to identify main gaps and mitigation measures.
