Project Summary

Child Relief and Support Fund (CRSF) is proposing a 12 months’ emergency food and livelihoods resilience project to the critically vulnerable households in Kapoeta South, Bor South, Jur River, Ibba, Nzara and Kapoeta North counties. In line with the FSL cluster and 2021 HRP strategic objectives, CRSF will scale up response in the target locations through distribution of main season and dry season response kits (crop and vegetable seeds, and fishing kits), train and develop the capacity of CNVs and health workers on establishment of Kitchen gardens and modern agronomic practices, livestock vaccination, training of CAHWs, , assets creation and development, and business (VSLA) creation at community level. The overall objectives of CRSF planned FSL project is to; 1: Build households’ capacities by providing livelihoods assistance and building productive assets for more sustainable livelihoods in line with the HRP SOs and FSL cluster objective 2: Enhance emergency food production through distribution of vegetable and crop seeds, and fishing kits and livestock support. This will help to reduce vulnerabilities and risks associated with the current severe food insecurity, poor economic conditions, climatic shocks or floods and reduced market access due to the worsening road conditions among the affected population in Kapoeta South, Bor South, Jur River, Ibba, Nzara and Kapoeta North counties.
The project is proposing a $345,000 budget to support 11,500 HHs of Kapoeta South, Bor South, Jur River and Kapoeta North Counties (1,500HH in Kapoeta South, 3,000HH in Bor South, 1,500HH in Jur River, 2000HH in Ibba, 2000 in Nzara and 1,500HH in Kapoeta North counties respectively through direct provision of crop and vegetable seeds and fishing kits for main season and dry season response, and 3,750 HH which have access to water bodies with fishing kits. The targeted households will comprise of host communities, IDPs and returnees who are affected by displacements due to floods and ethnic conflicts and who may not receive assistance from WFP and FAO funded projects. These HHs will be supported with lifesaving livelihoods and food security interventions. People with disability, pregnant and lactating mothers and elderly people shall be prioritized to strengthen their capacity on best agronomic practices and trained on key actives like post-harvest and handling techniques. CRSF shall establish 40 farmers groups (10 farmers’ groups in each county and these shall receive basic training on modern and best agronomic practices with regards to improved vegetable and crop production, and establishment of kitchen gardens. CRSF will do this by recruiting and training 40 FSL extension workers who will be charged with routine engagement to train the farmers groups.
Owing to the pastoral setting where this project is planned, CRSF will ensure that livelihood assistance in terms of delivery of emergency livestock vaccination and treatment will be provided to support vulnerable livestock. CRSF will do this through recruitment and training of community-based Animal Health workers (CAHW) and Animal Health Auxiliaries (AHA) as supervisors, Stock Persons (SP) and a veterinarians. CRSF will train its FSL field team of staff on protection using a Do No Harm principle.
CRSF has extensive experience in implementation of a wide range of FSL interventions in Kapoeta South and Bor South Counties where we have ongoing FSL interventions supported by FAO through provision of livelihood inputs and thus has capacity to respond to the high food insecurity needs. Further, CRSF has a well-established relationship with the local communities in Kapoeta South and Bor South with very good mechanisms of reaching the targeted beneficiaries and efficient feed and complaints mechanisms through the already established farmer’s groups and routine focus groups discussions with beneficiaries as well as stakeholder’s mobilization meetings.

1 - Needs

The huge humanitarian needs in South Sudan remain the biggest task for all humanitarian actors and government to combat in the event of many confounding factors; COVID-19 pandemic, conflicts, flooding, diseases outbreaks, poor hygiene and sanitation and economic crisis. Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Lakes and Warrap states continue to face multiple shocks (Humanitarian Needs Overview SS Jan, 2021).As at Dec 2020, 5.82 million people (48.3% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity with 1.79 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity while 7.24 million people (60% of the population) is likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between April to July 2021, and 2.47 million are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity during the same time period (SS IPC, Dec 2020).Bor South, Jur River, Ibba and Nzara counties emanates from settings characterized by political and ethnic conflicts, poor farming practices, poor land use practices while Kapoeta South and Kapoeta North in Eastern Equatoria states emanates from a setting characterized by poor subsistence farming practices, poor livestock management, cattle raiding among the Toposa and Buya people and ethnic/tribal clashes among Toposa and Didinga people. The December 2020 IPC classifications for April to July projections categorized Bor South, Kapoeta South and Kapoeta North as emergency IPC Phase 4 (IPC, Dec 2020) while Jur River county is categorized as crisis IPC phase 3. Inter-communal conflicts, floods, poor rainfall, population displacements and asset depletion, high economic crisis, destruction to productive assets, and population displacement continue to drive the severe and high levels of acute food insecurity in Bor South, Jur river, Kapoeta South, Kapoeta North, Ibba and Nzara. At the moment, the prolonged dry spells at critical stages of crop growth especially vegetables and crops are threatening household food security and the situation could get worse at the pick of the lean season. This, therefore calls for increased support to affected households to provide emergency livelihood inputs and extension services, which are currently limited. An Agriculture KAP survey conducted by CRSF in the SHHAAIN project in Kapoeta South in January 2021 found that 75% of the households had access to agricultural land but utilization was inadequate due to lack of knowledge and awareness on modern and best agronomic practices as well as limited access to livelihood inputs. Through this project, CRSF will partner with FAO, SSHF and other donors to provide livelihood assistance to the affected households by distributing agricultural inputs (seeds, and fishing kits). CRSF will establish 12 Kitchen gardens (2 in each county) to act as demo plots/farmer field schools. Additionally, CRSF will coordinate with nutrition, health and WASH and Protection partners to build capacity of CNVs, health workers, WASH and GBV staff to build their capacity on modern and best agronomical practices (establishment kitchen gardens at nutrition sites and health facilities) as part of integration. Further CRSF will deliver livelihood assistance to the affected populations/HH by providing emergency livestock vaccination and treatment to the vulnerable livestock owned in the Kapoeta South, and Kapoeta North pastoral communities which are also targeted for this project. CRSF has operational presence in Bor South in Makwach payam, Kapoeta South, and Kapoeta North where it is implementing its ongoing SHHAAIN (Strengthening Household Assets and Agriculture in Nutrition) project supported by FAO who provides agricultural inputs (vegetable and crop seeds) support. CRSF plans to establish its operation presence in Jur River to scale up it SHHAAIN response. Therefore, CRSF has capacity and better placed to respond to the heightened levels of acute food insecurity in the said counties.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

As an Organization, CRSF has a strong and a functional policy on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse that govern its programs. Under this project, FSL staff recruited to implement this project will be given an orientation training on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse. CRSF will empower Beneficiaries to report any form of PSEA cases with in the community or locations where CRSF will implement this project. CRSF will fit suggestion boxes at all nutrition sites to confidential information with regard to PSEA. All staff for this project will be sanctioned by CRSF's policy on PSEA in line with IASC guideline.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

In its programming, CRSF uses a community based approach to implement and reach its targeted beneficiaries. In this project, CRSF will form farmers groups whose composition will largely be illiterate groups of women and adolescent girls in the community. These will be trained on best and modern agronomic practices to empower their capacity in food production. Further, these illiterate groups and people living with disabilities will be engaged in focus group discussions and consultative meetings to have their views incorporated in this project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CRSF will conduct stakeholders’ mobilization and sensitization meetings and focus group discussions through which information regarding the project implementation will be provided to the beneficiaries. Through these meetings, CRSF will expect to get complaints and feedback from the community members and beneficiaries with respect to how the project is being implemented.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

In this project, the beneficiaries and community members form a critical aspect of the project implementation strategy at CRSF. CRSF has nutrition specialist and a project manager and the entire program team. These will be charged with addressing beneficiaries and community issues that are raised during community engagement meetings and focus group discussions. to input and offer any support to the solutions made by the technical and program team and thus shall be adapted to suit beneficiaries and communities’ interests.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project consultation meeting with beneficiaries will consider the attendance to have 50% female, 50% youth, and 50% persons with a disability during consultation and decision-making processes. Beneficiaries will be informed about this criterion to ensure voices of female, youth, and persons with disability are heard in relation to project design and implementation. The beneficiaries will be provided with information about program activities before the commencement of the project to involve them in project management, create awareness and sense of ownership, and provide accountability mechanism. CRSF will ensure staff are trained on protection of persons of all age including child protection and GBV risks. CRSF recognizes the community engagement and therefore will ensure participation of community members in activities at community level hence championing community protection mechanisms. CRSF will train community existing protection structures such as church leaders, school administrations, and local authorities on protection principles including GBV risk and referral pathway existing in the community. CRSF will work closely with local leaders and authorities to create community awareness on the targeting and selection criteria. CRSF will engage some resident community and returnees’ members during the identification of project beneficiaries in order to build a strong relationship between returnees and resident community members. CRSF will consider all age group and gender in the project implementation. The confidential complain mechanism will be establish and all the issues that may arise will be addressed without discrimination. CRSF will position a complain desk and box in a visible area and place accountability to the affected population officer to receive complains. Complaints received will go through the case management process to generate appropriate solutions and feedback. All decisions will be generated through inclusive participation, harmonized agreements and decentralized problem-solving approach to concerns and needs; while ensuring accountability to the affected population through holding stakeholders’ meetings and focus group discussions with community members. CRSF will conduct planned focus group discussions to engage women, adolescents and people living with disabilities during implementation phase to review the success or failures of strategies, devise mechanisms to change if need arise in order to improve the program performance. CRSF will fit suggestion boxes to receive complaints from the community members at every nutrition site, and formation of mother support groups will empower them and adolescent girls to share the challenges, successes, what is not working well with the project and devise change of strategies to ensure achieve its objectives. CRSF will step up coordination with protection partners and ministry of gender to ensure that GBV is integrated into this project through conducting training of nutrition staff on the GBV related risks to identify, report and refer cases for management. Further, CRSF will ensure that FSL interventions are taken to the beneficiaries in hard to reach locations to prevent them from traveling long distances to access the services which may increase their risk to protection issues like rape and GBV issues.
