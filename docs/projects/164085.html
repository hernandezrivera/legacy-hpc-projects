Project Summary

This project aims at providing integrated formal and informal education services to 17500 boys and girls at school age to better meet their diverse needs and priorities and help create a safer and more secure environment for children in the most affected (100% education severity level 5 according to HNO data) locations in NW Syria (Harim, Salqin, Afrin, Atareb and Daret Azza subdistricts) including IDPs and host communities, with attention to children with special needs. Cost per beneficiary is estimated to be $101. Beneficiaries with disabilities target are 3% of beneficiaries.
SDI has implemented similar interventions during 2019; Funding received during 2019: $927840
Estimated number of people reached during 2019: 18000
Part of this project is already funded namely, 14 schools are already supported with all related activities for 7 months.
The project will improve equitable access, capacity and quality of education services and enhance the psychosocial wellbeing of children including children with special needs in NW Syria, Idleb and Aleppo targeting 5500 boys and 5500 girls in 40 schools (including formal public schools and informal schools) in Idleb and Aleppo in addition to targeting 5000 children and their caregivers with back to learning campaigns and providing informal education for 1500 boys and girls out of school, low achievers and those in camps and in informal setting.
The project aims to implement comprehensive educational activities ranging from the rehabilitation of schools and equipping them with all their needs, gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive modification, to running schools, provision of both formal and informal education and PSS services, and provision of teacher training and supervision through the following activities:
1. Rehabilitation, light repairs and modification of classrooms, WASH facilities and school building to ensure a better protective educational environment and enhance access of people with special needs and ensure gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive WASH facilities.
2. Back to learning campaigns targeting boys, girls and caregivers.
3. Equipping schools with all their needs of equipment, furniture and educational means, taking into consideration the safety & security equipment such as fire extinguishers and first aid kits and solar power equipment.
4. Providing learning materials and school bags for boys and girls customized tools will be provided for children with special needs.
5. Providing formal education for students in the targeted schools by running the schools until the end of the educational year, providing running and maintenance costs, teacher incentives and staff salaries, teaching materials and supervising the educational process, to ensure quality education.
6. Establishing 40 Parent-Teacher associations (PTAs), one in each school.
7. Providing Psychosocial Support to 11000 children including distribution of PSS materials and activities.
8. Providing comprehensive training for teachers on active learning, child-center and protective pedagogy, PSS, to 480 teachers to ensure better education with high standards in addition to safety and security training provided for all schools covering 600 teachers, headmasters, assistant headmasters, cleaners and guards.
9. Provision of winterization heating fuel and stoves in schools to protect children from cold and related health.
10. Provide informal education for 1500 children through 2 cycles of ALPs for out-of-school children in addition to remedial classes to low achievers and those with learning difficulties to help them return to schools and to catch up with their colleagues in due classes.
The project will directly contribute to HRP objective1:"Increased safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children" through activities 1,2,3,5, 9, 10 above;
and objective2."The quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth enhanced" through activities 4,5,7,8 above

4 - Needs

The project targets 5 of the most affected subdistricts in Syria (namely Harim, Salqin, Afrin, Atareb and Daret Azza) with severity level 5 and total 51,984 schools-age children including boys, girls and adolescents in need according to Education HNO 2020. In order to verify and further assess the needs in these sub-districts, SDI's MEAL team conducted a needs assessment between 8 to 10 February 2020 by visiting education directorates in the area and asking about the unsupported schools which are in need for support. The team was provided with names of 50 schools that were reported as in need of various aspects of support.SDI MEAL team conducted visits to the communities and met the KIs (School Administrations) to get information and wrote the report. Key Findings Summary:All KIs reported education as a priority need. All the visited schools provide formal education services, where 90% of KIs reported that the schools don’t get any support now. Only 10% of assessed schools reported that receiving learning supplies support before; mainly children's stationery.62% of KIs reported that the Schools are in need for rehabilitation with school damage rate as follows: 33% reported that the damage was between 10% to 25%; 25% reported that the damage was between 25% to 50%, 21% reported that the damage was between 0% to 10%, 13% reported that the damage was between 75% to 100%, and 8% reported that the damage was between 50% to 75%. The most urgent and common needs for rehabilitation were lighting, WASH facilities, windows and doors.Only 5% of all the assessed schools are full eligible to facilitate access for the students with special needs, 29% are partly eligible, and 66% are not eligible at all.20% of KIs reported that the Psychological support services are provided at the school.95% of KIs reported that the schools are in need for heating fuel (diesel), 56% of them said that they are in need to heaters, 85% of them lack the security equipment.

5 - Implementation of activities

Implemented by SDI's experienced staff in Syria, through its offices in Idelb and Aleppo and administered through its education program unit in Turkey, the project will begin by ensuring coordination with relevant bodies and stakeholders inside Syria (eg. directorate of education, local administrative bodies, school headmasters, education staff and caregivers) and partner organizations and clusters throughout the project to ensure a coordinated and complementary approach, avoid duplication and ensure information sharing and accountability to affected populations. PTAs will be created and supported to ensure direct involvement and collaboration of caregivers, educational bodies, school staff and the community representatives in the project, and the schools will be handed over to PTAs upon project closure to ensure follow-up and enhance the sustainability of the project. All activities will take into consideration the age and gender differences and the needs of disabled children (like the choice of color, size and type of distributed material), will ensure access of Girls, boys and people of disabilities, protection from weather conditions, fences for schools, stairs and roofs, grill for windows, lighting...etc. and adequate water (during rehabilitation activities) and will provide solar power, fire extinguisher and first aid kits for schools; this will ensure better hygiene and more protective and child-friendly educational environment based on INEE standards. Materials and services will be procured based on SDI's policies and procedures in a competitive and transparent manner to rule out fraud and ensure cost-effectiveness. SDI has an active monitoring and reporting system with a complaint mechanism that will ensure feedback from beneficiaries (through complaints box, hotlines, email, surveys, FGDs, PDM,and quality assurance for rehab.) and monitoring the educational process using Aser tool and monitoring and comparing the results of students in exams and quizzes.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Learning facilities could be attacked by shelling or a fire might happen in one of the schools. Mitigation: All attacks will be reported to the Attacks on Education Reporting Mechanism; All schools will be equipped with fire extinguishers and first aid kits. Staff, guards, cleaners and facilitators will be trained on safety and securitystandards and fire fighting. Risk2: Children might be exposed to physical punishment, bullying or other types of harm by teachers or other personnel. Mitigation: Learning facilities will have an enforced code of conduct and policy on bullying in addition to Child Safeguarding policies. Teachers and school personnel will be trained on prevention and reporting of, and related issues. Teachers and other school personnel will attend protection training including on preventing and dealing with bullying and on child safeguarding. Monitoring and reporting mechanisms will be in place to detect such cases; and students, their caregivers, teachers and school personnel will be encouraged to report issues. Psychosocial support services will be provided to students as part of this intervention. A survivor-centered approach will be adopted to ensure the safety and confidentiality of any affected person. The rehabilitation/ modification works will include Sufficient safe and clean sex and age-segregated bathroom facilities. Risk3. Sexual exploitation or abuse by aid workers. Mitigation3. SDI will ensure that all staff, contractors, and drivers will read and sign the code of conduct, which includes all PSEA policies; monitoring and reporting mechanisms will be in place to ensure the safety and confidentiality of sensitive complaints. Risk4. Learning facilities do not provide sufficient protection from weather or have insufficient water/sanitation facilities. Mitigation: Rehabilitation activities include protection from weather (fixing windows and leaking roof..etc), fencing and provision of gender-sensitive WASH facilities and Water supplies.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: Access to school locations will remain valid and safe.Risk1: The inability to reach some schools due to road closure or security situation/clearance change.Likelihood: medium.Effect: medium.Mitigation: We can wait and postpone activities in the relevant area for a few days until the security situation allows or else replace the targeted schools with other schools in need for intervention.Assumption 2: Skilled and unskilled workers can be hired from the local market to complete rehabilitation works.Assumption2: Needed items and materials are available to be procured locally.Risk 2: It might become difficult to locally secure one (or more) of the needed items/services of good quality or qualified workshops to implement the rehabilitation works. Likelihood: medium. Effect: high.Mitigation3: Social Development International (SDI) through previous experience was able to directly purchase the materials from larger markets within Syria and transport them to the project sites. Moreover, SDI will seek to contract high-capacity trusted vendors who can deliver the required rehabilitation works in Syria effectively.Risk4: The instability of prices and exchange rates in the targeted areas. Likelihood: high. Effect: mediumMitigation: SDI will aim to purchase the needed items from the beginning of the project to avoid the fluctuation of prices in the long term with all quotes and contracts done in US dollar.Risk5: The risk of robbery or loss of equipment and materials during moving the materials from the warehouses to the schools. Likelihood: low. Effect: highMitigation: SDI will require the provider to deliver the items directly to the schools or to SDI warehouses, and then they will be distributed to schools on small batches.Risks6: Late financial Payments by donors might affect the timely delivery of services and activities. Likelihood: low. Effect: medium. Early coordination with donors and agreement on disbursement dates and conditions.
