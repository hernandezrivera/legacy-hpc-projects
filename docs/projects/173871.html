Project Summary

In Pakistan, Afghan refugee camps/villages were established in 1979 in different districts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KPK). Some 3.3 million Afghan refugees were housed in Pakistan by 1988, of which 100,000 were hosted in the city of Peshawar while more than 2 million in other parts of KPK (DRC, 2013). According to current statistics of UNHCR, Pakistan is hosting around 1.4 million registered Afghan refugees while approximately one million is unregistered individuals . At the moment, almost 68% of Afghan refugees are living in refugee villages with majority in mud-houses for more than 30 years having poor basic infrastructure, hence making more than half of the refugee population vulnerable to health related issues. Factors like inadequate water sanitation systems and improper water supply systems contaminating the water quality directly which affects the health status of the Afghan refugees and causing different types of waterborne diseases like typhoid, diarrhea, hepatitis, cholera, skin diseases etc. The WASH communal infrastructures and basic services in refugee villages including water supplies and sanitations are quite old, poorly maintained, and even not enough to carry the burden of these huge populations.
According to the recent WASH resource mapping survey conducted by SRSP under UNHCR funded WASH project, it is revealed that the Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene conditions in refugee villages are not satisfactory in term of services and infrastructure. Drinking water is the basic need identified which require immediate attention. Currently, there is very minimal water distribution supply lines evident in refugee villages and in most of the afghan refugee villages centralized water fetching points such as hand pumps, water taps, and dug wells etc. are established where afghan communities including mostly women and children fetch water for drinking and cooking purposes. Since these water sources are mostly untreated that results in the water being un-hygienic and unsafe for drinking. Similar feedback has been received by afghan communities as well and they are on view that these water sources are mostly contaminated and is not hygienic for drinking, however, due to non-availability of alternative clean drinking water sources, they are compelled to utilize contaminated water that is one of the root cause of several water borne diseases in the refugee villages.
Thus, keeping in view this acute drinking water conditions in afghan refugee villages, the installation /establishment of water filtration plants is proposed over the existing water systems to enable the vulnerable afghan refugees with access to clean drinking water. These water filtration plants will be installed on the existing water points where community fetch water for drinking. The activities will include:
• Identification of potential communal water fetching points through existing WMCs
• Water quality tests
• Technical feasibility survey and development of BOQs
• Installation/establishment of water filtration plant
The initiative will not only allow afghan refugees with improved access to clean drinking water but at the same time it will contribute to reduce the propagation of COVID-19 pandemic.
Since SRSP is already implementing WASH and livelihood projects in afghan refugee villages (KP) since 2019, therefore, SRSP has well established linkages with Afghan communities, UNHCR, and CAR along with valid allowed to work status and valid NOC.
