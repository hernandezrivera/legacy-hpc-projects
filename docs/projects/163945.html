Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to promote and increase awareness about the Guidelines for the Alternative Care of Children in Child and Youth Friendly Language among 20,100 children, caregivers in addition to humanitarian workers in Damascus city and its rural by the end of 2020.
Children and adolescents in alternative care or may become separated from their families face the risk of abuse, neglect, and child rights violations. They often think they do not have the same rights as others.
The project aims at raising their awareness about their right to live in a supportive family environment. It explains what needs to happen to children who cannot live with their parents or are at risk of being separated from them.
The project focus on highlighting the Guidelines for the Alternative Care of Children, which outline standards of care and protection for our target group which was adopted by the United Nations General Assembly in 2009.
The Guidelines for alternative care will be promoted through a child and youth friendly booklet that was produced with the input of over 500 children and young people in care across more than 20 countries worldwide.
While the main audience is children and young people from ten to eighteen years, the easy-to-read booklet can be used to reach children younger than ten years, adults and decision makers.
The booklet speaks directly to the children and youth through the following content:
Children’s right to care and protection- children’s rights when parents face difficulties, when children cannot live with your parents and when there is an emergency in child’s country
The project will also target children with special needs who have challenges to read and understand the booklet.
Target groups: Children and youth benefiting from SOS children's villages' programs and local NGO partners including children who need special care. Children who live in alternative care programs run by local charities, Children and families who face the risk of family separation, Care providers and humanitarian workers, Children in public school and Communities committees (10 communities).
The project also aims to provide group psychological support to 5000 of the project beneficiaries. The intervention will help discharge beneficiaries’ emotions and promote the psychosocial well-being of children and their psychosocial safety.
The project will include also risk education sessions for all the project beneficiaries. The goal is to educate them about the danger of mines and remnants of war that could endanger their lives. Using officially approved brochure provided by UNMAS.
R1: Our target group are educated about the guidelines for the alternative care of children.
R2: Our target group in Damascus vicinity change their attitude regarding the rights of children without parental care and contribute to protecting their rights.
R3: real updated statistics about number of children in alternative care programs in Damascus vicinity are identified.
R4: beneficiaries who participate in the project awareness session received psychological support and risk education.
Indicators: #of beneficiaries participated in the awareness sessions and PSS activities, # of sessions #of beneficiaries who participated in the sessions planning meeting, #of roundtable meeting held with community members and local NGOs, #of community events attended to speak up about the project messages #of rights mentioned in the booklet beneficiaries can identify after awareness session, #of orphanages and NGO that run alternative care programs that participated in the study
Means of verification: 4Ws reports, Monthly reports, Annual report, Images, Awareness sessions attendance sheets, MOM with community members and local NGOs and Final statistics report.
Assumptions: The security situation is stable, Children and families will participate in the sessions and PSS activities and the orphanages and local NGOs are cooperating.

4 - Needs

During the years of the Syrian crisis, children were the most affected by the deteriorating security and humanitarian situations. They had to face violence, many protection risks and poor living conditions. These conditions caused many children to lose their parents or their parental care; children in Syria face daily risks of child protection violations. With the lack of awareness about the guideline for the alternative care of children.Based on SOS Children’s Villages experience in working with local charities and orphanages in Syria during and before the humanitarian crisis, we can confirm that many orphaned, abandoned or unaccompanied children receive alternative care through local charities, orphanages or kinship care. Many other children are still waiting to be admitted to one of these charities and forced to suffer until they find a place for them in one of the orphanages that are operating at and overcapacity. Despite the inability to secure the statistics from MOSA about the number of orphans, children who lost their parental care and who receive alternative care in Syria, unofficial statistics speak about thousands of children who need a new home and family. Still, laws in the country are impeding the process of adoption, which leave them with one choice, which is the alternative care option in orphanages.With the lack of adequate alternative care standards and strict child protection policies, the door opens for child abuse and negligence.According to 2020 HNO, more than four million children live at risk of explosive hazards, which cause severe socio-economic implications on them, their families and communities causing increasing vulnerabilities. It also prevents the safe delivery of humanitarian assistance, affects population movement and limits the scope for recovery efforts, there is a huge need to ensure that children are educated about these risks to avoid and report them.

5 - Implementation of activities

Although the primary target audience is children and young people in SOS programs, out reached schools and communities, the booklet can also be used to sensitise decision-makers and authorities.The project will focus on the following major activities.• Secure official approvals and liaise with partners to identify most vulnerable beneficiaries.• Carry out counselling sessions with partners and beneficiaries to plan the sessions and activities.• Conduct the planned sessions and activities, distribute the booklet, snacks and giveaway items.• Conduct special sessions for children with special needs. • Measure the increase in the beneficiaries’ awareness through questionnaires, group activities or discussion.• Carry out regular internal staff capacity assessment to ensure quality of the provided services.• Organize meetings with all stakeholders to discuss the project results and their involvement.• Conduct training on the guidelines for focal points from local charities and NGOs.• Attend community events to spread the project messages.• Capturing beneficiaries feedback through M&E tools.A complete integration of SOS programs happened in December 2018. The integration objective was to support and protect the rights of vulnerable children through long term development programs.SOS Children’s Villages Syria has an internal control system for all its programs and projects that are audited by external company annually. International financial control guidelines are implemented internally and it covers many areas such as cash and bank procedures, procurement process, assets management... etc.The project activities and indicators will be monitored and evaluated through SOS child protection department depending on SOS child protection standards. This process will include field visits, surveys, staff meeting, participatory reviews and participation through communication channels such as complaint boxes and regular follow-up visits.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk 1: The project is targeting children who receive alternative care in local charities and orphanages; there might be the risk of preventing the project team by the charities management from conducting the awareness sessions. This may happen because of the fear of educating children about the rights that they may be unable to obtain at present. Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigating measures: By taking officialApprovals from MOSA and prepare official letters addressed to all charities and local organizations to facilitate the project activities. After that, a meeting will be held with the management of each charity to explain the idea of the project and to persuade them to join the project as partners. Protection risk 2: The project targets children who are at risk of losing family care due to poor living conditions, their parents and children in public schools. Despite their poor living conditions, those children are not aware that they face this danger. Educating them about their rights in case of losing their parental care may cause them anxiety and fear from the future. Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Through designing awareness sessions that follow the storytelling mechanism and by talking to children about the rights of children who lost their family care with whom they live in the community or may meet in school. Protection risk 3: During the awareness sessions, some children may speak about abuse cases or child protection policy violations cases. This may cause problems for those children after the sessions by some caregivers or workers at the charities. Likelihood: medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Charities will be informed that child abuse cases may occur and we will share with them the response mechanisms to such cases that we follow. Moreover, those in charge of the charities, NGOs or schools where the awareness sessions are going to be held will be involved in the planning of the sessions.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Damascus and its countryside are considered relatively safe areas, and this will help in facilitating the movement of the project team and the implementation of related activities. However, any chance for political or security event may disrupt the project and its activities. The probability of such a risk is medium, but the impact is very high. However, we always follow up on the political and security situation in the cities where we are operating to take any necessary actions when needed. Project activities will continue to take place in areas where there are no security events, and safe spaces will be chosen for conducting training workshops. There is also the risk of an unstable economic situation with high probability. This will lead to instability in the expenses needed for the workshop. However, the project budget was established after studying the price changes in the region in addition to the rate of inflation during the previous years. Cultural traditions blocking the implementation of the interventions isalso a high risk that may prevent caregivers from participating in the awareness sessions. Conservative approaches to the responsibilities of adults in providing care for vulnerable children can block their participation or their crucial involvement in the projects activities. To mitigate this risk, the project will include the different stakeholders in the awareness sessions on child protection topics and the importance of child participation.Finally, a delay might happen in obtaining official approvals from the ministry of education to have access to public schools where awareness sessions are going to be conducted. This risk will be mitigated through ensuring good coordination with MOSA and informing them of the project implementation plan.
