Project Summary

The project aims to reduce the mortality and morbidity rate caused by high incidences of water borne diseases outbreak in the Prone and vulnerable communities in Bor South and Terekeka Counties through provision of lifesaving safe water provision through water point construction and rehabilitation (hand pumps, or provision of household water filters, depending on the needs); provision or rehabilitation of sanitation infrastructure (latrines, hand washing facilities and solid waste management) ; hygiene promotion (hand washing campaigns in communities and institutions, safe water chain and jerry can cleaning campaigns); capacity-building of community institutions (pump mechanics, water user committees, community hygiene promoters, school hygiene clubs), and distribution of WASH NFIs (soap, hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene kit, water containers, water purification filters, PUR or others).
Safe water supply (new boreholes drilling, repairs and rehabilitation), increased household rural water treatment using Pur/Aquatabs, hygiene promotion sensitization at household and institutional levels, improved safe sanitation practices awareness, and breaking the cholera transmission routes between victims and caretakers/community members by 'contact tracing', disinfection, sensitization, WASH NFIs support and monitoring the gradual progress in hygiene and safe sanitation practices.
The communities where this project will be implemented will be identified under WASH Cluster priorities in consultation with the local authorities and other stake holders on ground on the most vulnerable groups (Women headed Household, child headed household, disabled, gender based violence (GBV) survivors and chronically sick) who will be prioritized in the selection process.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan continues to increase despite the signing of the revitalized peace agreement 2 years ago. In 2020, the country experienced triple shock of COVID-19 impacts, major flooding and sub-national violence and conflicts leaving an estimated 1.6 million people internally displaced facing worsened food insecurity and extreme levels of gender-based violence and psychosocial distress particularly for women and girls (HNO, 2021). These shocks limited access to WASH infrastructure. According to the humanitarian Needs Overview, 2021, humanitarian assistance is estimated to increase in 2021 with 8.3 million people in need most of whom are living in the 72 counties including Terekeka in Central Equatoria state and Bor South in Jonglei state facing extreme and severe need. The lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation, hygiene practices, insecurity and reliance of surface water are among the drivers of high WASH severity which will negatively impact the living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 5.6 million people in 2021. In addition, the proportion of households (HHs) relying on surface water is high in Jonglei (43%) and Central Equatoria (41%) but most of the clean water available for drinking is low in quantity and below standard. Country wide, 36% of households have access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. The remaining 64% of people either rely on unimproved or surface water sources, take more than 30 minutes to reach an improved water source or are able to reach an improved source in less than 30 minutes but face protection concerns while accessing water. Access to sanitation is low, with only 17% of households own a latrine in their compound, 4% have access to a shared latrine, and 2 % have access to a communal/institutional latrine while the remaining 77% of people do not have access to a latrine. Open defecation is practiced by 73% of the population countrywide which is a threat to the environment and public health like risk of cholera, diarrhoeal and vector-borne diseases. Furthermore, the health and wellbeing of about 4.9 million south Sudanese will be impacted due to a weakened health system and disease outbreaks as a result of the ongoing flooding situation. About 1.9 million people including children and lactating mothers are anticipated to suffer from malnutrition as a result of food insecurity and lack of access to improved water sources. The number of reported cases of acute watery diarrhea and acute bloody diarrhea spiked dramatically in 2020 with 593,000 acute watery diarrhoea cases reported by end of October, a 60% increase compared to 2019. Low response rates to the flooding in 2020 and upcoming dry season are anticipated to further exacerbate WASH-related morbidity and incidences of diarrheal diseases, particularly in young children. Women and girls who are traditionally responsible for water collection face an increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water far from their homes. In 2020, 16% of the people using water facilities reported safety concerns. The COVID 19 pandemic also increased GBV incidences as a result of the measures used to curb the pandemic with 2 million people estimated to be affected by GBV in 2021.Therefore, support for critical WASH needs to continue and the provision of life saving WASH services in communities of Terekeka and Bor South is crucial in building and maintaining co-existence and gradual behaviour change on hygiene practices.

2 - Project activities

18 pump mechanics given refresher training on hand pump maintenance and repair • Drilling of 10 boreholes, Repair and rehabilitate 30 boreholes each with trained water management committee members-WMCs • Organize and conduct participatory hygiene promotion, and conduct training for 40 community Hygiene promoters • Community mobilization and sensitization on good hygiene practices and health education through a participatory approach undertaken in health facilities, churches and markets on good behavior targeting 16,500people • Carry out sanitation education and awareness to enable vulnerable and affected population to practice safe sanitary and hygienic living through secure dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services, construction of 10 sanitation facilities in communities with consideration for gender separated latrines and provision of latrine digging tools to the affected community and training of the community on how to construct the HH latrine using available local materials as an exit strategy. Undertake cross-cutting issues across all activities with particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to affected population and committing to improve effectiveness of cooperation with communities. Distribution of WASH NFIs, capacity building in terms of preparedness through; training of pump mechanics and provision of borehole spare parts, training of Water Management Committees for resilience, water quality testing both at water points and community level. RUWASSA shall apply sustainable solutions, such as installing water sources propelled by solar power, where feasible. RUWASSA has its own drilling equipment and team with geologist to conduct geophysics survey before drilling. Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among affected population to prevent and mitigate WASH related diseases through promotion activities, particularly hand washing for displaced and vulnerable communities and institutions. RUWASSA will mostly focus on health workers, teachers, pupils, and women group leaders while conducting household visits, focus group discussions and training for Community Hygiene Promoters.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The project will coordinate with other actors on ground involved in other sectors like protection and health. With the mainstreaming of GBV into WASH, SGBV and child protection specialists are consulted to identify safe, confidential and appropriate systems of care (i.e. referral pathways) for survivors who tell WASH staff that they have experienced violence. All staff and volunteers involved in the WASH response carry an up- dated list and contact details of agencies and professionals for SGBV, child protection, medical care, legal and psychosocial support services to which they can refer survivors of SGBV or children who reveal an incident of violence to them. Efforts should be made to identify agencies or pro- fessionals experienced in responding to specific risks in each context. Additionally, the project will ensure WASH facilities have maximum privacy and dignity like people have access to latrines according to their gender identity, separate latrines are accessible for persons of different genders with mobility limitations, lockable latrines,Culturally appropriate sanitary materials and underwear are distributed to women and girls of reproductive age in sensitive ways, water points are located so that people do not have to walk unreasonable distances or gradients, and they are located in areas that the community deems safe, consulting with the affected community to ensure WASH facilities and related activities are located in areas that women, girls, boys and men, and individuals from different groups deem safe.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Making clean and safe water available and accessible for the targeted population decreases the prevalence of water borne diseases and the spread of COVID-19 when used for drinking and hand washing. The construction of sanitation facilities in key places like Nutrition/Health centers, schools and the sensitization of the communities to construct their own House hold latrines using distributed latrine digging tools and locally available construction materials would reduce the practice of open defecation which in turn would decrease the risk of cholera , diarrhea and other vector-borne diseases. Continuous hygiene education will help in behavior change which is a very important element especially in the context of COVID-19.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

With core principle of Impartiality, neutrality and humanity, the project shall ensure the equitable distribution of basic services to the vulnerable populations. the project in coordination with all the relevant stakeholders and partners shall harmonize easy channels for information flow, sharing updates and necessary support for the needy and ensure that the 'Do No Harm' principle is adhered to by all stakeholders at all times.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Water is essential for the survival of human kind. The construction of new water points and rehabilitation or repair of broken existing water points will make access to clean and safe drinking water. This decreases the likelihood of waterborne diseases and increases food security which is vital for the survival of humanity particularly children. This in turn decreases the chances of children suffering from malnutrition. In addition, increase in dissemination of hygiene knowledge particularly to mothers will help in combating disease outbreaks amongst children, thereby preventing them from getting malnourished.
