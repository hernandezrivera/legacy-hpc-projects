1 - Needs

A protracted protection crisis continues in the oPt, which remains largely attributable to Israel’s ongoing occupation, now in its 53rd year, the ongoing internal Palestinian political divide and violations of international humanitarian and human rights law. As a result, significant humanitarian and protection challenges abound, as described in the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO). Recognising that the situation in the oPt is effectively a chronic protection crisis, protection concerns continue to be the primary drivers of humanitarian need. Palestinians in the oPt face a range of protection threats, including threats to life, liberty and security, destruction or damage to homes and other property, forced displacement, restrictions on freedom of movement and on access to livelihoods, and lack of accountability and effective remedy. Critical problems of concern relate to the protection of civilians and forced displacement, access to essential services, and erosion of resilience with associated humanitarian consequences that translate into needs. 2020 witnessed a significant increase in the severity of humanitarian needs across the oPt, due to the COVID-19 outbreak and measures taken by the Palestinian Authority in response to Israel’s threat to annex parts of the West Bank. In 2021, the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) estimates that approximately 2.45 million Palestinians across the oPt will require some form of humanitarian assistance. Although this is only slightly more than the estimate for 2020 (2.41 million), the number of people suffering from severe, as opposed to moderate, need has increased by 346,000. About 64 per cent of those in need of assistance, or 1.57 million people, live in the Gaza Strip and 36 per cent, 880,000 people, in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. This year OCHA oPt will support the implementation of the 2021 Humanitarian Response plan (HRP). The 2021 HRP will maintain the centrality of protection at all levels of humanitarian actions as a shared system-wide responsibility. Humanitarian actors will remain committed to ensuring principled humanitarian action in the current restrictive operational context. Humanitarian partners will continue to urge relevant authorities to uphold their responsibilities and dedicate resources for humanitarian protection and assistance. The geographical scope of the strategy continues to be the Gaza Strip and, in the West Bank, Area C, East Jerusalem, some vulnerable communities in A and B, and H2/Hebron. During 2021, humanitarian partners will continue to address the needs jointly identified by the humanitarian community in the 2021 HNO, by protecting the rights of Palestinians living under occupation, providing access to basic services for those who are vulnerable, and supporting the ability of Palestinians to cope with, and overcome, the protracted crisis, while more sustainable solutions are sought. With xx partners participating in the 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), and financial requirements reaching USD 414 million targeting 1.4 million people in 2021, there is a clear strategic need for continued coordination of humanitarian action in oPt to maximize achievements, while reducing gaps and duplication in the provision of humanitarian assistance. OCHA’s unique role in facilitating and coordinating these streams of humanitarian work continues therefore to be of critical importance. The specific context of the oPt warrants that the international community explore efficiencies in the ways in which to respond, including through strengthening of the humanitarian development linkages, where appropriate, to identify response activities that can be carried over to development sectors. There is thus a continued need for OCHA’s expertise and coordination to facilitate the inter-cluster and inter-agency work on humanitarian planning and the Humanitarian-Development Nexus as appropriate for the oPt context, while continuing to advocate for principled humanitarian action. OCHA, in partnership with the clusters will also strengthen information management and assessments that underpin the HPC to create a foundation for shared analysis of need and joint results based planning of humanitarian action. Gender analyses and sex disaggregated data will be furthered strengthened through the support of the UN Women and the Humanitarian Gender Group (HGG) of the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) . While humanitarian partners in oPt are increasingly focused on advancing gender equality in humanitarian response by mainstreaming gender in cluster response plans and projects developed for potential funding under the HRP, it is important not to lose momentum. There is thus a continued need to enhance partnerships and gender mainstreaming of humanitarian action further, including through the HGG. Similarly, there is a continued need to maintain and build on existing good practice in the areas of protection mainstreaming and Accountability to Affected Populations, this includes community engagement work and providing secretariat support to the PSEA network, throughout the Humanitarian Programme Cycle (HPC).

2 - Activities

OCHA plays a number of critical roles in the oPt humanitarian context which are unique as compared to other OCHA country offices globally. Within the politically-charged context of the oPt, OCHA plays an authoritative role in ensuring an unbiased common situational awareness of the humanitarian needs – and the drivers of those needs. OCHA’s work in this regard is widely recognized and relied upon by other UN agencies and offices, by Member States in country and at the capital level, and by operational humanitarian actors. To achieve this common situational awareness, OCHA provides thorough information collection, analysis, management, and reporting services. Information is triangulated and analyzed in detail by OCHA staff, and further verified through OCHA’s extensive network with operational partners, Israeli and Palestinian authority counterparts, civil society and affected people, to ensure accuracy and to mitigate for bias. Information is then conveyed through factual reporting, operational coordination to ensure there is timely and evidence-based emergency response, as well as coordinated humanitarian advocacy. There is also deep demand for OCHA’s coordination of operational interventions which is based on a sound evidence base resulting from strong field presence and robust IM capabilities. OCHA plays the key role in convening the humanitarian community around strategic emergency readiness activities for macro-emergencies, as well as in the development of localized emergency response plans for micro-emergencies arising largely from protection violations, affecting individual communities at risk in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. OCHA supports the development of a prioritized humanitarian response strategy, enabling six clusters, and more than 100 operational partners to deliver targeted assistance and protection to the most vulnerable across the oPt and to ensure effective monitoring and reporting on the national humanitarian response strategy. OCHA supports the HC in mobilizing system-wide funding and in ensuring pooled funds to achieve the greatest results for the highest number of people possible. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The ‘Do No Harm’ approach is mainstreamed in all aspects of OCHA’s work. Data and information collected from communities is done so with consent and a clear description of how the information will be used. Community consent, in coordination with the Protection Cluster, is obtained prior to organizing any advocacy (be it through the HCT AWG, OCHA senior staff, the ICCG, etc.) potentially impacting communities, be in public advocacy or diplomatic visits to affected communities. Field visits are organized to ensure a diverse spectrum of persons speak. Databases are built to ensure that sensitive information (phone numbers, ID numbers, details of incidents, etc.) are stored in a secure manner. Coordination between Clusters also takes place to ensure that advocacy advised by one Cluster (e.g. protection) does not place at risk the assistance provided by another to communities, or if it does, informed consent to proceed is obtained. OCHA also ensures that AAP, inc. PSEA, is mainstreamed throughout the HPC, including in development of the HNO and HRP.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The Humanitarian Programme Cycle, coordinated by OCHA through the project, ensures the efficient delivery of humanitarian aid in line with humanitarian principles, including impartiality. OCHA ensures that sound needs assessments form the basis of the HNO and HRP, with attention to sex and age disaggregated data that informs analysis on the different needs of girls, boys, women, and men, as well as persons with disability and the elderly. OCHA ensures Cluster agreement on priority vulnerable groups to be targeted with humanitarian responses. OCHA manages a number of critical data sets that ensure that the humanitarian community has access to fact-base necessary to ensure a needs-based response (e.g. Gaza early warning indicators, Gaza crossings, Gaza electricity tracker, West Bank demolitions database, etc.) OCHA also manages the 4Ws database that contributes to monitoring the equal and impartial delivery of assistance.
