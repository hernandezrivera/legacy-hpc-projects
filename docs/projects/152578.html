Project Summary

A food security and livelihood response is proposed for Upper Nile, Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria states targeting the host vulnerable households, IDPs and returnee’s communities with severe food and nutrition needs/insecurities.
According to the September 2018 IPC analysis the month of September 2018, it is expected populations are in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) in Leer and Mayardit (former Unity state), Yirol East and Yirol West (former Lakes state), Canal/ Pigi (former Jonglei state), Panyikang (former Upper Nile state), and Greater Baggari in Wau (former Western Bahr El Ghazal state). In October - December 2018, Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) outcomes are expected in Leer and Mayardit (former Unity state), Pibor (former Jonglei state), Panyikang (former Upper Nile state) and Greater Baggari in Wau (former Western Bahr El Ghazal state). In January - March 2019, Catastrophe is expected in Pibor and Canal/ Pigi (former Jonglei state) and Leer and Mayardit (former Unity state).
Of greatest concern are counties where harvests are likely to be poor; access to humanitarian assistance may be limited for a section of the population; possible returns from within and outside of the country may cause further pressure on already scarce resources; insecurity, lack of freedom of movement and extreme depletion of livelihoods prevails. As a result, extreme levels of acute food insecurity are expected to persist in many areas. This calls for immediate and unhindered provision of humanitarian assistance and close monitoring.
A total of 31 counties in the former states of Warrap, Unity, Upper Nile, and Jonglei reported ‘Critical’ (GAM (WHZ) 15.0 – 29.9%) levels of acute malnutrition while 20 counties in Lakes, Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile reported ‘Serious’ (GAM (WHZ) 10.0-14.9%) levels of acute malnutrition. Majority of the counties within the three states lie within IPC 3 where they are in an emergency state .
The widespread loss of livelihood since the onset of violence in the three states warrants a response to mitigate the loss of livelihoods and life due to hunger, malnutrition, fighting and displacements. Activities in response to the ongoing emergency will aim to improve general availability of food and strengthen resilience among the vulnerable communities.
CMD will provide inputs including Crop seeds, early maturing vegetable seeds and fishing gears in the target locations with an aim to restore food security and provide training on improved food production skills thereby enhancing their agricultural capacity.
This program will empower insecure host communities, IDPs and returnees restore and enhance livelihoods by providing them with vegetable seeds and fishing gears, thereby enhancing production of vegetable varieties and fishing to enhance food availability and nutritional diversification among the households.

Needs

The level of acute malnutrition is attributed to severe food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition services, high morbidity, extremely poor diets, poor sanitation and hygiene. Levels of acute malnutrition are expected to improve marginally between October and December 2018 due to the seasonal availability of local production, increased availability of fish and milk, and relatively better access to markets and key services. However, levels of acute malnutrition are expected to deteriorate in the first quarter of 2019 with an early onset of the lean season as most households are expected to deplete food stocks from own production (IPC Sep,’18).It is highly recommended that multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance be scaled up to meet the estimated need and that the rebuilding of livelihoods and improving access to basic services be pursued. A more optimistic and best-case scenario is likely to prevail if the current peace agreement can be sustained and unhindered humanitarian access occursFSMS round surveys indicate that at least two thirds of respondents in Jonglei and Upper Nile reported having received some humanitarian assistance. Multiply displaced IDPs have significantly higher levels of food insecure households (43%) than residents (35%). Female headed households also have higher prevalence of food insecurity (45% versus 33% for male headed households), a possible indication of gender-based vulnerabilities. The year-on-year annual Consumer Price Index (CPI) increased by 88.5 percent between June 2017 and June 2018 (Figure 4). The increase was mainly driven by high prices in non-food items such as communication and housing, water, electricity and gas. The monthly CPI decreased by 4.6 percent between May 2018 and June 2018. Notwithstanding the recent downward trend, inflation remains high. Households in both urban and rural areas remain unable to afford the minimum food basket. July ’18 SS Economic update Significant decrease in the number of households that receive income from casual (skilled and unskilled) labor passed from 20% to 30% to merely 6% to 8% in Jonglei, and Upper Nile, with a similar trend expected in Eastern Equatorial as a result of multiple displacements. At the household level, food availability is now expected to be lower than previously projected due to crop damage from Fall Armyworm Which has spread among nearly all the counties along the White Nile. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence rates are above emergency level malnutrition thresholds (GAM >15%) in the targeted counties of Jonglei, Eastern Equatorial and Upper Nile. Disease outbreaks such as cholera and other AWDs have had significant effects of food security.Populations have resorted to less preferred dietary alternatives due to loss of livelihoods to both natural and conflict fuelled causes, crop failure and multiple displacements.Livestock conditions are also impacted. Changed migration patterns due to conflict, insecurity, and concentration of animals in small areas have raised concerns about disease outbreaks, reduced access to resources and veterinary extension services, as well as increased tensions between herders and farmers. Importantly, the terms of trade between livestock and cereals have drastically declined across all markets, undermining purchasing power of agro pastoralists in Greater Upper Nile and Eastern Equatorial regions.

Activities

Activities:• Together with stakeholders, enlist, recruit and enrol 90,000 beneficiaries from 15,000HHs among vulnerable IDPs, host communities and returnees in the targeted locations in Jonglei, Eastern Equatorial and Upper Nile as primary beneficiaries. 43,698 IDPs are targeted through this response in addition to 38,910 people from the host communities. 7,392 other people affected including returnees are targeted through this project. • Mobilise 15,000 HHs to participate in opening up of farming spaces to be utilised for climate sensitive agriculture in the locations of Jonglei, Eastern Equatorial and Upper Nile. • In partnership with FAO, distribute livelihood kits to 15,000 HHs; 40% female headed. Provide crops seeds, vegetables, fruits and beneficiary friendly tools, which include micro irrigation tools and fishing gears to facilitate production and food availability. • Conduct seed fairs in locations with functional markets and apparent surplus produce to support the trading farmers and local economy while supplying locally adapted seeds to the beneficiaries.• Establish, rehabilitate and revive community owned post-harvest handling facilities in the targeted states of Jonglei, Eastern Equatorial and Upper Nile, including setting up value addition mechanisms for products such as milk. • In partnership with FAO, carry out emergency livestock initiatives amongst vulnerable communities involving vaccinations, case identification, surveillance, treatment and training of CAHWs amongst other initiatives. • Rehabilitate and revive markets through Emergency Market Initiatives aimed at strengthening livelihoods, directly targeting IDPs and vulnerable host communities. • Support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security & multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality e.g. assist traders to re-supply markets• Reducing pest and crop disease e.g. FAW surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication.• Conditional Food assistance through food for assets (FFA)CMD will leverage on its ongoing responses on thematic areas of WASH, Health, Education and Nutrition that are being implemented in these prioritized locations. CMD currently runs WASH interventions in these locations mainly through the provision of WASH services at nutrition OTPs and SCs and Health facilities run by CMD. The project will also complement the ongoing Health, Education, Nutrition response in the targeted locations. Farmers demonstration plots will also be areas of school agricultural learning points to ensure issues of nutrition and balanced diets will be taught in practice to the learners within the primary schools. This will help address health issues.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

It's entailed in the organisational values to provide beneficiaries with information, as well as a donor requirement and demand from beneficiaries. Several methodologies have been adopted which include publication of progressive reports that are availed in hard and soft copies to stakeholders, focus group constitution as a means to pass on information, involvement of beneficiary representatives in project review exercises, project literacy campaigns, use of visibility items such as banners, posters, T - Shirts, inorder to create awareness of the project. CMD also runs a social media platform through which project achievements are detailed. A suggestion box is placed at major project locations, through which formal feedback is obtained from stakeholders and responses provided.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are designed to reach beneficiaries in the language they best understand. Design of messages involves a representative of the different gender groups including women, boys, girls, men and the elderly. Special needs persons are also included, with support from key actors such as Handicap International and HelpAge. Messages are mindful of beliefs, cultures, attitudes and practices of communities, and are aimed at delivering results based on project plans and goals.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The organisation has a PSEA policy in place, alongside a child safeguarding policy to prevent harm to children. These include PSEA procedures applies at staff recruitment, induction/training, performance management, whistle- blowing, discipline and grievance, programming guidelines, partnership arrangements and complaints/reporting. Specic responsibilities have been assigned for PSEA, and the organisation ensures staff, volunteers and associates understand and work to PSEA requirements. CMD engages communities to ensure that communities are fully aware of the expected behavior of the staff, how to complain if commitments are not met and ensuring appropriate awareness raising tools are available. This also applies to partners, suppliers and contractors, by assessment of capacity to implement PSEA measures and inclusion of clauses on PSEA in contract agreements. The project seeks to further identify potential risks of sexual exploitation and abuse, and has planned activities on PSEA awareness and sensitivity. CMD has developed a comprehensive complaints mechanism that aims to instill an organisational culture in which all complaints are taken seriously and acted upon.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

CMD carried out several consultations across different strata, aiming at obtaining maximum response from all gender groups including women, girls, boys, men, elderly and persons with special needs. These included focus group discussions, house to house surveys, interviews with key informants, interviews with key beneficiaries at institutions, communities as well as village structures.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

As a commitment to IASC standards on accountability to affected populations, CMD has adopted and enforced clear feedback and complaints pathways, aimed at obtaining information on key programmatic aspects from the beneficiaries. These include provision of suggestion boxes at key project sites, that are periodically checked and feedback/complaints addressed, provision of feedback telephone/email addresses and focal persons with whom the community will be free with to provide information about the project. Review meetings are organised at every level with beneficiaries represented, aimed at getting feedback on project related matters.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Community engagement involved discussions involving all groups including underprivileged, boys, girls, elderly and women to open up farms for farming to ensure support is accorded to either groups and reduce reliance on relatives.A monitoring and evaluations framework is designed at the inception of the project, with participation of the community, different gender group representatives and people with special needs. These are orientated on the tools to be used for the exercise and then form part of the team that carries out the exercise with focus on particular areas of common interest.Conservative agriculture will be advocated and enhanced to ensure that environmental viability is maintained. Biological practices of pest control and soil fertility management will be encouraged to limit environmental pollution by the otherwise chemical methods.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project has identified specific areas of risk which include safety and dignity, humanitarian access to crisis affected populations, populations access to aid, beneficiary privacy, individuals with special needs/risks, social relations, fraud and diversion with protection implications and market impacts with protection implications. The project will include affected communities as participants in all phases of the programme cycle to ensure that affected populations identify their own protection risks, benefits and self protection mechanisms. CMD will consider whether intervention will create or exacerbate protection risks or benefits for individuals, households and communities, engage individuals with different and specifc needs and protection risks, establish two way feedback mechanisms and focal points to ensure regular communication. CMD has done a gender, age and diversity analysis.
