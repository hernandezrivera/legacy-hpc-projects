Project Summary

"Mukti Cox's Bazar has been working in Cox's Bazar since 1998 to ensure food security and livelihood of the host communities and self-reliance for the Forcibly Displace Myanmar Nationals (FDMN) after influx in 2017. Mukti Cox's Bazar will implement this project under Food Security and Livelihood sector in attaining the sector’s objectives along with humanitarian development nexus, resilience building and strengthening coordination among the humanitarian actors and stakeholders. Accordingly, strategies will be geared towards enhancing skills, Income Generating, self-reliance and market linkage ensuring fair price for durable solution of the host community and for the refugee in acquiring transferable. Mukti Cox's Bazar will also enhance the engagement of refugee and host community women in productive and Climate Smart Agriculture (CSA) activities including Disaster Risk Reduction. This will be achieved by strengthening capacity specially on modern technologies, high value vegetable production ensuring Good Agricultural Practice (GAP).
Mukti Cox's Bazar plans to support in seven refugee camp no. 3, 4, 12, 17, 19 and 22 and two host community Union Hnila under Teknaf and Palongkhali Union under Ukhiya Upazila. The proposed project will target 2380 individuals where camp-1770 and host-610. Based on the community need, project will provide capacity building training specially on modern technologies and Climate Smart Agriculture collaboration with Department of Agricultural Extension(DAE) and agricultural inputs like seed, vermicompost, fencing net, Bamboo, rope, Bio pesticide, yellow trap, Pheromone trap, watering cane, sprayer, seedling raising materials etc. Project will provide skill training on Chicken, Duck and Goat rearing including pond fish culture and will organize 12 vaccination campaign with the collaboration of Government Department of Livestock Service (DLS). After giving capacity building training to the beneficiaries, project will provide livestock support to the women BNFs for their economic empowerment (Native Chicken-300HHs, Duck-200HHs, goat-110HHs) and provide inputs support for join pond fish culture including training (Camp-5 ponds and host-5ponds) where will cover 60 people including pond dike for daily consumption and increasing their income.
In the host community, 610 small scale producers will produce their high value vegetable and project assist to establish linkage with input and output and also will organize market linkage worksho at Union and Upazila level for getting fair price. Among the refugee camp, all 1297 individual will produce vegetable in their rooftop garden. After won consumption, they will sale vegetable with in the camp area. Under this project, Mukti Cox’s Bazar will include at least 96 persons with disabilities in different income generating activities through base line survey as well as 135 youth also include.
"

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

"According to REVA 7 survey conducted in November 2023, Food remains the highest priority need among both Rohingya and Bangladeshi households in Cox’s Bazar. In 2023, 97 percent of Rohingya households in Cox’s Bazar reported fresh foods as their most pressing need, marking a significant increase of 17 percentage points from 2022. Despite these critical needs, livelihood concerns among Rohingya households in Cox’s Bazar decreased to 14 percent in 2023 from 44 percent in the previous year. Ration reductions threaten to exacerbate malnutrition, with disproportionate impacts expected across the community. Particularly at risk are children under 5, pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, and adolescent girls and boys. Given the extremely limited livelihood options for Rohingyas, nearly all individuals are vulnerable, each with unique needs and capacities. Living conditions have deteriorated in the Bangladeshi community, primarily due to price hikes and increased expenditures. Furthermore, living conditions were notably worse among female-headed households compared to male-headed households in the Rohingya communities and Bangladeshi community respectively. The diminishing trend of relying on livelihood-based coping is alarming, indicating severe depletion of assets in a context where livelihood opportunities are scarce and asset rebuilding is impractical. In the camps, over six in ten Rohingya households rely on asset depletion or livelihood-based coping strategies. In Cox’s Bazar camps, Rohingya households increasingly resorted to emergency coping strategies in 2023, particularly in response to ration cuts. In contrast, in the Bangladeshi community, 44 percent of households did not use any coping strategies, indicating more available livelihood options compared to Rohingyas in Cox’s Bazar. This marked a 15 percentage point increase from the previous year (29 percent). The proportion of households resorting to stress-coping strategies also rose from 45 to 48 percent between 2022 and 2023. These households depleted savings, borrowed money, food on credit, or sold household assets and goods, including food assistance. The Resilience four Capacity Scores (RCS) - anticipatory, absorptive, adaptive, and 23 transformative—and five livelihood capitals—human, financial, social, institutional, and informational. The results classify resilience into three categories: low, medium, and high. Figure 21 illustrates RCS for both communities, showing below-average scores (around 2.5 out of 5) in anticipatory, adaptive, absorptive, and transformative capacities. "

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

"Mukti Cox's Bazar always include protection principle into program and principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian aid and also mainstreaming in the project cycle. Mukti will continue to promote a culture of accountability to affected populations, prioritizing a community-based approach and age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming to ensure more impactful and sustainable protection outcomes. In other words, through systematic consultation and participation at all stages of the programme cycle, refugee communities in each camp, including often-marginalized groups such as women and girls and persons with disabilities, should be engaged meaningfully and substantively in all aspects of programmes that affect them; they should also play a leading role in catalyzing change and enhancing community resilience. Mainstreaming meaningful community participation should be a key priority, and Mukti Cox's Bazar will ensure that participants enjoy their rights on an equal footing and can participate fully in decisions that affect them. Project will take every opportunity to proactively engage with beneficiaries and every stage of the programme cycle must include diverse representatives from within the affected community. It is important to ensure that beneficiaries participation includes women, girls, boys and men of different ages and backgrounds to achieve comprehensive community engagement. "

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

"Mukti Cox's Bazar always address to incorporate climate change adaptation and mitigation. Site development and site improvements such as drainage, access, and slope stabilization works can help reduce the risk of structures and shelters’ vulnerability against hazards present in the camps. The mitigation works should be done with durable materials, wherever possible, even if clearance from RRRC/CiCs for the use of durable materials continues to be a significant challenge. The site improvement works should take into consideration the historical hazard maps for landslides and floods to avoid building in hazardous zones. Site improvements require regular and extensive coordination particularly at the camp level, and technical consultation/support to ensure adequate resourcing of materials and labor for improving site conditions. In this project, Mukti Cox's Bazar will introduce drought and saline tolerant variety in both community including Good Agricultural Practice(GAP). Project will facilitate to the beneficiaries for using Bio pesticide, organic fertilizer, Sag gardening, rooftop vegetable gardening considering environmental issues through climate change adaptation & mitigation. This project includes the training on modern technologies and sustainable agriculture production on diversifying crops and growing climate change resistant crops. Beneficiaries are taught from the training about the importance of different crops for different seasons, helping them become more resilient to the effects of climate change. "
