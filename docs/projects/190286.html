Project Summary

The impact of the drought in eastern and southern Ethiopia is deepening and so are the suffering and loss of the affected pastoralist and agro-pastoralist communities in these areas. The prolonged drought is the result of three consecutive failed rainy seasons since late 2020. At least 81 million people in Oromia, Somali, SNNP and Southwest regions are affected. The number of livestock dying from lack of pasture and water is staggering. Estimates from regional Governments indicated more than 1.46 million livestock deaths (67 per cent in Somali, 31 per cent in Oromia, and 7 per cent in Southwest and SNNP regions), up from more than 170,000 livestock deaths across Somali, Oromia and SNNP in January. This is a key indicator of how alarming the situation has become. Surviving animals have a significantly deteriorated body condition which has decreased their market value and has led to correlated decrease of the purchasing power of households, who are becoming increasingly vulnerable with further limited coping strategies. Food and nutritional insecurity have further soared because livestock remain the main source of nutrition and income for these communities. At least 286,000 people who have the means to travel (183,000 in Somali and 103,000 in Oromia) have migrated in search of water, pasture or assistance, leaving behind their elderly and the sick, which is exposing them to higher vulnerabilities and protection risks.
The situation is not expected to improve rapidly and will require a continuous engagement over the months to come, with a likelihood of a fourth consecutive poor rainy season. According to the most recent seasonal forecast by ICPAC, a below-average belg season is expected. The Project Activities are suitable in keeping the environmental protection and does not affect the environment directly or indirectly .The proposed project aims to undertake the following key activities below.
 Dasenech, Bena Tsemay, and Malie Woredas of South Omo Zone ,SNNP Region . Detail activities include below to reach 22,000 HHs.
1. Provision of agricultural inputs for vulnerable farming and agropastoral 7,273 households (36,365 people). Emergency seeds are determined by multiplying total targeted people by an average of 0.75 hectares with different seeds varieties and farming hand tools
2. Distribution of emergency animal feed for 2600 pastoralist households (13,000 people). Each household will be provided with 3.5kg of fodder and 2.5 kg of concentrate for 3 core breeding animals for 90 days.
3. Animal health support 30,546 core breeding animals owned by 10,182 households (50,910 people).
4. Distribution of multi-purpose cash for 1,945 households (9,725 people). Each HH will be provided with $40 as per estimated by DRM-ATF equivalent to about 90% of the PSNP wage rate.
Afambo ,Chifra ,Dawe & Telalake Woredas of Zone1 Zone 5,Afar Region. Detail activities include below to reach 10,000HHs
1. Provision of agricultural inputs for vulnerable farming and agropastoral 4000 households (22,000 people). Emergency seeds are determined by multiplying total targeted people by an average of 0.75 hectares with different seeds varieties and farming hand tools
2. Distribution of multi-purpose cash for 1500 households (8250 people). Each HH will be provided with $40 as per estimated by DRM-ATF equivalent to about 90% of the PSNP wage rate for 3 rounds
3. Rehabilitation of 10 Water Scheme for livestock drinks to provide support for 4000 HHs and 400,000 livestock
4. Distribution of emergency animal feed for 2600 pastoralist households (13,000 people). Each household will be provided with 3.5kg of fodder and 2.5 kg of concentrate for 3 core breeding animals for 90 days.
5. Animal health support 30,000 core breeding animals owned by 10000 households (50,500 people).
6. Distribution of small remunants for500 HHs each HHs will received 11 small remnant accordingly to the LEGs standard
7. Support of Vet Equipmet to FTC/Vet Center with standard package

2.1.1 - Is it better to use cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Yes, CVA is useful for Sustainable Livelihoods implementation, the connections between the capitals, the context and the strategies that households are able to develop so as to meet their needs short-, mid- and long-term. When a new strategy is developed or a crisis is faced, we can see not just an interrelation, but also an interdependence between meeting immediate needs, protecting or rebuilding livelihoods. Households will not be able to protect or recover their livelihoods if they are not in a position to meet their basic needs, and they will need their livelihoods in order to meet their needs. Any analysis to outline cash transfer programming in support of livelihoods must therefore consider all kinds of needs that’s why in this livelihood engagement program CVA is a major task to satisfy the beneficiaries needs.
