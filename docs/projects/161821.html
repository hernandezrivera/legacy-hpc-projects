Basic Info

Project Name

Safe and equitable access to inclusive education for 619,237 conflict-affected girls and boys age 3 to 17 years and an additional 15,000 youth (15+) in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States of North East Nigeria

Start Date

01/01/2020

End Date

31/12/2020

Project Summary

Overall Objective:
Strengthen access and improve inclusive, quality and equitable basic education for 619,237 conflict-affected displaced, returnees, host communities, school-aged girls and boys aged 3 to 17 years and an additional 15,000 youth (15+) in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states of North-East Nigeria.
Specific Objective:
1. Strengthen basic education for 619,237 children (ensuring 50% girls, 100,000 children who are 3-5 years old and 50,000 children from Tsangaya and Islamiyah schools ) through the provision of essential teaching, recreational materials, support to enrolment drive campaigns (EDC) and Girls for Girls (G4G).
2. Improve the capacity of 10,100 teachers and volunteer teachers (at least 40% females) in psychosocial support, education in emergencies, special education, quality Early Childhood Development (ECD), classroom management and pedagogy.
3. Increase access to safe learning spaces with gender-sensitive WASH facilities and water for the benefit of 400,000 children (50% girls).
4. Provide access to learning, training and skills development for 15,000 youth (50% females; aged 15-24)
5. Enhance the response and protection capacity of education service providers in school governance in emergencies through establishing and/or activating Monitoring of School-based Management Committees (SBMC) in targeted LGAs and building the capacity of 500 SBMC (40% females; 2,500 members) on SBMC effectiveness and provision of education services to out-of-school children, education-in-crisis, and education for vulnerable children including girls and children with disabilities with a focus on hard-to-reach areas.

1 - Needs

In 2019, according to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), 7.1 million people in Nigeria (2.3 million girls, 1.9 million boys, 1.6 million women and 1.3 million men) need humanitarian due to the complex crisis entering its tenth years. 1.8 million people are internally displaced, predominantly in the north-eastern states of Borno, Yobe, Adamawa, and new displacement continue due to ongoing insecurity. More than 50 per cent of those affected are children, including many unaccompanied minors or children orphaned by the crisis. More than 2.2 million children need urgent education services. Institutional and human capacities are overstretched to respond to the educational needs of children and youth in Northeast Nigeria. An estimated 19,000 teachers are displaced, and 645 teachers have been killed since 2009. Approximately 1,200 schools have been damaged or destroyed or are used for military purposes. In the Northeast, education facilities are often explicitly targeted by non-state armed groups in Nigeria. The deliberate targeting, burning and looting of schools began in 2009, with some schools repeatedly attacked over the years. Human Rights Watch reports that in some cases, students recruited by non-state armed groups attacked their own school and kill their own classmates and teachers. From December 2013 to June 2015 the UN Secretary General reported that all schools in the north-eastern states were closed. Currently, in Borno, 824 out of 1,359 government schools still remain closed. More than 5,202,000 children are out-of-school in North east Nigeria of whom more than 1,400,000 children are out-of-school in Borno state. In Borno, there are 27 LGAs, out of which 5 are inaccessible. Among the 22 accessible LGAs, many wards and communities are inaccessible as well. The Nigeria Education Data Survey (NEDS) 2015 shows the scope of damage caused by decades of development neglect combined with the recent education crisis in North East Nigeria. The conflict further weakened an already weak education system and pushed more children out of school. This increased children’s exposure to violence, psychosocial stress, economic exploitation, recruitment by armed groups, and early marriage. The effective provision of equitable, inclusive and quality education can be a game-changer turning years of underdevelopment, poverty, violence and radicalization into self-empowerment and a brighter socio-economic future. To increase access to education to 619,237 children (50% girls), UNICEF will construct 400 TLSs and rehabilitate 100 schools. As part of the support to inclusiveness, UNICEF will construct and/or rehabilitate 400 gender‑sensitive WASH facilities in schools and learning spaces benefiting a minimum of 400,000 children (with an average of 1,000 children per school/TLS). To improve knowledge and skills of 10,100 teachers and volunteer teachers (at least 40% females) in psychosocial support delivery, education in emergencies, inclusive education, quality ECD, classroom management and pedagogy. To increase access to education for out-of-school children, UNICEF will support the provision of educational opportunities for 50,000 Tsangaya and Islamiyah children in partnership with the State Ministry of Religious Affairs (SMoRA). To increase access to livelihood and skills training, at least 15,000 youth (15+) will be empowered with vocational skills for employment in partnership with SAME due to their technical comparative advantage. To enhance the response and protection capacity of education service providers in school governance in emergencies and effectiveness, at least 500 School Management Committees (SMBC) (2,500 SBMC members) will be trained for improved capacity in education-in-crisis, education for vulnerable children including girls, and the role of community in providing education services to out of school children.

2 - Activities

In 2020, UNICEF will focus on increasing access to quality and inclusive basic education for 619,237 school-aged conflict-affected, displaced persons, returnees and host community children (50% girls) and an additional 15,000 youth (15+) in the targeted LGAs in 3 states (Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe). The specific activities include the following: Access: 1. Construct 400 temporary learning spaces.2. Rehabilitate 100 schools. 3. Construct and rehabilitate 400 gender-sensitive WASH facilities in schools and learning spaces with connecting to water sources. Quality: 4. Train 10,100 teachers and volunteer teachers (at least 40% females) in psychosocial support, education-in-emergencies, special education, quality ECD and classroom management and pedagogy. 5. Provide essential teaching, learning and recreational materials to school-aged children: (a) 15,500 School in a Box, (b) 2,000 Recreational Kits, (c) 600,000 school bags, (d) 1,250 ECD kits and adolescent kits and (e) 40,000 school uniforms. Response Capacity of Education Service Providers: 6. Provide access to learning, training and skills development for 15,000 youth (aged 15-24).7. Establish/reactivate at least 500 SBMCs in conflict-affected schools. 8. Train 2,500 SMBC members on SBMC effectiveness, protection of schools and provision of education services to out-of-school children, education-in-crisis, and education for vulnerable children including girls and disabled with focus on hard-to-reach areas. INDICATORS:Access:• 400 TLS reconstructed in conflict-affected areas. • 100 schools rehabilitated.• 400 gender-sensitive latrines (UNICEF Standards) with handwashing facilities connecting to water at schools and learning spaces rehabilitated. • 619,237 school-aged boys and girls (50% girls) accessing safe learning spaces and continue their education. Quality: • 10,100 (at least 40% females) teachers including volunteer teachers trained in psychosocial supports, education in emergencies, special education, quality ECD and classroom management and pedagogy and 619,237 children (50% girls) who have received essential teaching, learning and recreational materials including ECD materials. System strengthening and Response Capacity of Education Service Providers:• 15,000 youth (aged 15-24) provided access to learning, training and skills development.• 500 SBMCs reactivated and/or established in conflict-affected areas.• 2,500 SMBC members (40% girls) trained on SBMC effectiveness, protection of schools and provision of education services.
