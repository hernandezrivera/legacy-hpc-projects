Project Summary

The project is strategically aligned with the 2024 HNO and 2024 PiN for protection cluster in NWS, particularly in terms of targeted locations. The proposed protection interventions endeavors to expand upon the existing community-based GBV and CP interventions initiated by Bahar in NW Syria, focusing on the integration of best practices methodologies to address increasingly complex needs and identified gaps for vulnerable groups. This comprehensive strategy involves the sustainable running of 13 community centers- 8 Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSSs) and 5 Child Friendly Spaces (CFSs)- to ensure the continuity of critical community-based protection interventions in Afrin, Azaz, Jarablus, Jebel Saman and Atareb districts which have been identified as the first-priority underserved regions.
GBV and CP still creating pressing issues in Northwest Syria, where million individuals, predominantly women and girls, require assistance. Economic instability, escalating conflict, natural disasters, and limited access to essential services exacerbate their vulnerability. Violence against women and girls, including intimate partner violence and sexual exploitation, is widespread, contributing to a pervasive sense of insecurity. Adolescent girls face heightened risks of child marriages and early pregnancies. Female-headed households struggle to meet basic needs, while reduced access to GBV services increases survivors' feelings of isolation. Traditional gender norms further constrain women's freedoms. GBV often occurs at humanitarian service delivery points, necessitating tailored risk mitigation measures. Addressing GBV requires prioritizing women's and girls' needs and challenging gender norms to promote equality and safety in conflict-affected settings. The ongoing hostilities, February earthquakes and protracted displacement caused enormous mental distress to children who have seen their caregivers killed or injured, their homes destroyed and are living in fear of shelling. According to HNO 2024, 96% of children live in communities where child labour is reported, 84% live in communities where child marriage is an issue for girls (15 -17 years), 52% of people live in communities where family separation exists, and 28% of HHs reported psychological distress among children.
Recent events in Northwest Syria have led to an increased incidence of encountering Explosive Remnants of War (ERW), with reports indicating significant new discoveries. The ongoing conflict in this region amplifies the risks associated with ERW exposure, particularly for displaced individuals relocating to unfamiliar areas affected by the conflict. It is imperative to conduct Community-based Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) and Awareness Raising initiatives to mitigate the threat of injury from mines and unexploded ordnance. These efforts involve informing both children and adults about the dangers through public awareness campaigns, educational programs, and community engagement. By promoting behavioral changes and fostering awareness, EORE aims to reduce risks to individuals, especially children, as well as minimize damage to property and the environment.
The proposed activities encompass the full package of GBV, CP and EORE interventions including specialized case management and CVA modalities. Moreover, the project seeks to reinforce ongoing outreach mechanisms, promoting awareness-raising efforts and community-led initiatives to prevent protection risks and enhance the identification of vulnerable situations more effectively. Additionally, the program aims to enhance protection mainstreaming outcomes by targeting Bahar teams of FSL and ERL with protection mainstreaming trainings and workshops. These initiatives and trainings aim to embed protection considerations seamlessly into every facet of project implementation.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

According to HNO 2024, the most affected groups are IDPs, children, particularly those with disabilities, widows, divorced, and elderly females. In addition to adolescent boys who are more likely to be killed and injured, separated, recruited by armed groups, or involved in child labour, while adolescent girls are particularly at risk of child marriage, sexual violence, and denial of resources. The aforementioned info matches the findings of the Protection assessments that Bahar conducted in Nov 2023 particularly for this concept note in Bulbul, Ma’batli, and Raju sub districts. According to Bahar’s assessment, the population of these areas exceeds 100K including 70% IDPs and 45% children. When asking about the main protection concerns, the findings stated poor child labour, early marriage, exploitation, Negligence, medical problems…etc, with many barriers including transportation, social constraints, and lack of information.
