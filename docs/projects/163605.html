1 - Needs

Over half of the 655,000 migrants in Libya are worried about not having enough food to eat, according to a study “Hunger, displacement and migration: A joint innovative approach to assessing needs of migrants in Libya”, conducted by IOM and WFP in 2019. Main challenges faced by migrants in Libya are related not only to security and finding opportunities to make a living, but also to high food prices. The recent flare in conflicts and violence in Tripoli and surrounding governorates has increased these concerns, which has also expanded to impact host communities who reported insecurity/violence as one of their top concerns. Evidence suggests that food insecurity has become a major issue for migrants and, increasingly, Libyan households during the ongoing conflict. According to Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) 2019, 64 percent of Libyan households and 78 percent of migrant and refugee households employ crisis or emergency coping strategies to afford food, including relying on less expensive food, reducing the number of meals eaten per day, reducing the size of meals, withdrawing children from schools, or selling assets. On average, 47 percent of Libyan and 31 percent of migrant and refugee household expenses are on food. In exceptional situations where the authorities are unable to address these ongoing needs, humanitarian actors provided emergency food for several thousand detained migrants and refugees as a life-saving measure. Without these assistants, affected populations would be unable to meet their basic nutritional needs, with broad ranging implications for health and wellbeing. With the unpredictable nature of the situation and the ongoing detention regime for migrants and refugees, the nutrition and food needs in detention continue to grow. While food is generally reported as available, 30 percent of households reported food items as being too expensive to afford. The median cost of the minimum expenditure basket (MEB) in September 2019 was 706 LYD, a drop of 9 percent from 2018.

2 - Activities

1. Provision of emergency food rations (up to max 5 days) – This provision of nutritious food packages for vulnerable populations pregnant and lactating women, children, elderly as well as people suffering from medical conditions. This project will assist 2000 migrants in detention center and will be able to reach up to a maximum of 10,000 migrants who may newly arrive or disembarked to detention centers in 2020. 2. DTM will conduct assessments of migrant's vulnerabilities in specific to determine levels of food insecurity via interviews with over a thousand migrants through Flow Monitoring Surveys (FMS).

4 - Protection risk analysis

Throughout 2019, migrants and refugees held at DCIM detention centres faced situations where they were not provided with sufficient and nutritious food, leading to significant health and nutrition problems. Emergency food assistance for refugees and migrants inside detention centres is considered as an emergency life-saving measure and it will only be supported in exceptional situations. This project will take into consideration the Sector’s Principled Framework for intervention in Detention Centers policy paper and will not support direct food assistance at detention centres as a regular practice due to its potential in supporting continued incarceration of persons held in detention.

5 - Mitigation measures

The approach of humanitarian actors in Libya has thus far opposed the systematic provision of food inside detention centres to avoid sustaining and institutionalizing a system of arbitrary detention. This project will focus on food only on an emergency basis. In order to avoid the creation of expectations amongst the migrant/refugee population and detention centre managers, IOM will undertake sensitization about the temporary nature of the food support.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

IOM will put in place mechanisms to prevent, monitor and respond to gender based violence or sexual exploitation that may arise during or in the process of food distributions by implementing distribution for women and men separately to minimize exposing women and girls to sexual harassment in the distribution areas. IOM will collaborate with local authorities such as DC managers to ensure physical security of beneficiaries including those with medical conditions to avoid access barriers due to conflict and access their entitlements safely such as through pre-arrangement of safe distribution locations. Gender balance of distribution teams will be considered to ensure that there are enough women available undertaking distributions especially for pregnant and lactating women. IOM will also put in place complaint and feedback mechanisms to incorporate the needs, preferences and opinions of beneficiaries. IOM will also monitor the effectiveness of response through ad hoc post distribution monitoring (PDM) assessments to inform subsequent distributions.
