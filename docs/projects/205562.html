Project Summary

This project aims to reach 97,242 beneficiaries comprising of 70,987 Host Communities, 13,128 IDPs and 13,128 Returnees (about 5% of the Health Cluster target) in 12 Counties of Juba, Terekeka, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South, Bor South, Pibor, Malakal, Panyikang, Renk, Gongrial West and Twic. The target beneficiaries will include male children under 5, female children under 5, male children aged 5-17 years, female children aged 5-17 years, male adults aged 18-60, female adults aged 18-60, male adult aged over 60 and female adult aged over 60 from all the different grouping of Host Communities, IDPs and Returnees. The selection of the 12 Counties Juba, Terekeka, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South, Bor South, Pibor, Malakal, Panyikang, Renk, Gongrial West and Twic was based on profound presence of HLSS in the locations, inter-sectoral severity level, health severity, and current contextual evaluation of each county. HealthLink South Sudan intents to achieve about 5% of the cluster target across the selected 12 counties. This project will complement the current development projects in most of the selected counties with focus on delivery of integrated lifesaving essential health care services, nutrition interventions, protection/GBV prevention, and WASH services. The implementation of this project is expected to address some of the key barriers to accessing integrated health care in the selected 12 Counties especially during increased insecurity or other public health emergencies. HLSS plans to integrate, and or co-locate staff at CHD and SMoH to ensure direct transfer of skills and knowledge while building local capacity for sustainability.
HLSS intends to implement activities to achieve 3 main Health Cluster Strategic objectives. 1. Needs

Country Profile.Barely 11 years post-independence, has South Sudan gone through two serious waves of armed conflicts, accordingly, on 15th December 2013 and July 8th, 2016. The conflicts resulted into a displacement of over 2.34 million people seeking refuge while 2.2 million people internally displaced (South Sudan HNO 2023). A masked revitalized peace agreement was signed four years ago by parties in this conflict nonetheless, the country continues to face worsening security and humanitarian conditions exacerbated by deteriorating economic conditions, endemic ethnic violence, access constraints and operational interference, notably access issues and attacks on health care system.In the first half of 2022, inter-communal violence was the primary source of violence affecting civilians, accounting for more than 60 per cent of civilian casualties, with massive displacements into and around Juba City and other counties in Upper Nile, Warrap, Jonglei and Unity among others. Persistent attacks on civilians by armed cattle keepers, cattle raids and revenge attacks resulted in the displacement of at least 257,000 people, several killings, increased incidents of SGBV, child abductions and looting/destruction of property and resources. In the recent months, following the Sudan crisis, IOM has registered an estimated 10,000 returnees from Sudan and are being hosted in Bungu Payam of Juba County. Besides, there is an internal displacement of over 4,000 persons from Ilkare, Lopur and Lumoni villages into Luri Rokwe following armed clashes in cattle raiding incidences (UN-OCHA, Humanitarian Updates Note. Wednesday, 16 August 2023). Other counties such as Bor South, Pibor, Malakal, Panyikang, Gongrial West and Twic are experiencing similar influx of returnees.Acute malnutrition remains a major public health emergency in South Sudan. As of November 2022, 6.6 million people, or over half of South Sudan’s population (54%), are classified in IPC Phase 3, 2.2 million people are in IPC Phase 4 acute food insecurity and an estimated 61,000 people in catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) (UNICEF Humanitarian Report May 2023) acute food insecurity in Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Akobo of Jonglei State; Pibor County in the Greater Pibor Administrative Area.Dilapidated physical infrastructure; South Sudan has the poorest roads, housing, electricity, and communications network in the region. Of an estimated 17,000 km of the roads network across the country, barely 500 km outside urban areas are paved. During rainy season, two-thirds of South Sudan’s roads become impassable, cutting people off from health facilities, markets, and other basic services. Impact of Climate change- South Sudan is experiencing heavy torrential rain and floods for the fourth consecutive years and causing secondary displacement of nearly one third of the total displaced persons in country. The public health impact of this include water borne disease outbreaks as well as the disproportionate access hindrance to health, education, and protection services for women and children. Climate change increases the vulnerability of rural communities due to its impact on food security and blistering malnutrition among and children. Weak Health system- South Sudan has one of the worst health indicators owed to the long history of conflicts and deteriorating economic conditions, poor state of the health system with inadequate qualified manpower among others. Accordingly, Life expectancy is estimated at 54.98 years in 2019 one of the lowest in the world, the Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR) stands at 1,223/100,000 live births (MMEIG, 2022), Under-five Mortality Rate at 99/1,000 live births, Infant Mortality Rate at 64/1,000 live births, and Neonatal Mortality Rate at 40/1,000 live births (UNIGME, 2022) representing one of the highest health indicators in the region. The health system is systemically underdeveloped with more than 71% of the population living more than five kilometers from a health facility

2. Project activities

HealthLink South Sudan intends to implement activities to achieve 3 main Health Cluster Strategic objectives. Specific activities will include but not limited to. CO1 Cluster Objectives One: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations.1. Conduct IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities.2. Conduct epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation, and response through static and mobile clinic services.3. Make use of the core pipelines and carry out Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases through static health facilities and mobile clinics. 4. Establish and coordinate community health service provision linking with facility-based services using Boma Health Initiatives (BHI and iCCM approaches). 5. Training of health care workers in IDSR, EWARS and ensure weekly reporting from all supported health care facilities (Hospitals, PHCCs, and PHCUs), IDP and Returnees Sites. 6. Support 28 health care facilities to provided integrated and quality static health care services, 2 Hospitals (Al-Sabbah Children Hospital Stabilization Centre, Magwi CeMONC centre), 10 PHCCs and 16 PHCUs across the selected Counties. 7. Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV by providing CMR services, ensure protection of the disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly with equal access to services. 8. Strengthened mental health services at the supported health facilities and referral severe mental cases accordingly.CO2: Cluster Objective Two: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone disease.9. Provision of life-saving essential healthcare services including management of non-communicable diseases through static (Hospitals, PHCC, and PHCU) and mobile healthcare services, procurement of supplementary essential medicines to compliment the pipeline supplies. 10. Training of health care workers to ensure rapid screening, early detection, and management of common diseases including Malaria, Pneumonia, Diarrhoea, Hypertension and Diabetes among others. 11. Conduct surveillance of attacks on health care in the selected counties and report any vandalization of health care infrastructure, theft of equipment, essential medicines, and supplies. 12. Conduct facility based and mobile clinic consultations as well as community-based consultations to diagnose and treat common disease including Malaria, RTIs and Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD) and non-communicable diseases to reach estimated 49,579 beneficiaries. 13. Provide BeMONC and CeMONC services at Magwi PHCC+, and 10 other PHCCs across the selected counties of project implementation. 14. Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services. 15. Support the management of SAM with medical complications at Al-Sabbah Stabilization Center estimated to reach 150 cases of SAM with medical complications. 16. Establish a network of BHWs to provide community-based diagnosis and treatment of common childhood illnesses.CO3: Cluster Objective Three: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.17. Conduct weekly planning meetings with County Health Departments 18. Conduct consultative meetings with communities’ members to identify needs, participate in response, and reduce impact on their lives disaggregated by men, women, boys and girls and disability.19. Conduct quarterly Partners Coordination meeting under the stewardship of CHD and local authorities.
