1 - Needs

Six(6) years and still counting the protracted wars amongst the SPLA factions, forced 3.7 million to flee their homes, the economy in shambles livelihoods severely disrupted and estimated 4million people living in landmines and explosive remnants of war contaminated area The Sustainable development Goal monitor estimate poverty rates in South Sudan remain as high as 66%, with close to 70% with no access to peace, and Justice compounded by lack of strong institution by 2017 , substantially behind the UN’s 2030 target of 45 percent. Arguably suggesting over 61% of the population is classified as below the poverty line, it is unlikely that South Sudan will gain a more favorable position than 169, of the 187 countries, that Sudan as a whole occupied before independence in 2011.Contamination presents significant risks to human security and exacerbates poverty by creating crippling socio-economic blockages preventing the productive use of sizeable tracts of land, impacting not only on the provision of mainstream services including health and education but also longer-term rural and economic development. More than 600 suspected and confirmed minefields remain across South Sudan, including older minefields predominately in Greater Equatoria and Jonglei and more recent Hazardous Areas (HAs) located along the border region. As a result, this affects much of the population and poses an immediate risk of death and injury to local communities, refugees, IDPs and returnees alike The UNMASS and NMAA let efforts to rid the country out of land mines and explosive remnants of war were headed for success before the eruptions of December 2013 conflict that threaten to thwart all in invests resources in cleaning the socio-economic sites for enabling the masses to rebuild their livelihoods . The lands are so contaminated so that previously cleared areas are not spared of new explosive ordinance including the globally banned cluster booms reported in Jonglei and Central EquatoriaCentral Equatoria, Jonglei, and Upper Nile endure the brute of explosive ordinance more than the rest of the country. This is because of the previous war that ushered independence from Sudan and the ongoing conflict majorly concentrated in these areas. In 2019, OSIL two clearance team unearth several landmines fields, four stockpiles for munitions along Juba Nimule road. The finding not surprising in that, the team composition and local communities easily open up to OSIL, and institution they call their own. The result was so overwhelming beyond our mandate but a right pointer in the direction of inculcating the key pillars of our mandate that is Ownership, Capacity, and Sustainability. In line with our philosophy that; any project to register significant results, it must interleave purposeful efforts for collective responsibility across stakeholders, processing of enhancing their ability to functions with precision and quality as a guaranteed measure for the lasting impact that is sustainability in line with UNMAS and NMAA startegy

3 - Project indicators

A total of 21,450 people will benefit from Risk Education activities implemented across OSIL’s 3 operational States in South Sudan by the end of the project period. It is anticipated that by the end of the project, 68% of direct EORE beneficiaries (approximately 14,586 beneficiaries) will be children . 91 hazard areas will have been marked, cordon or cleared) OSIL anticipates reaching at least 55,000 individuals indirectly through EORE, peer to peer, Social Media and Radio Talkshow.

This project has been developed with a thorough understanding and consideration of the external physical, environmental, political, economic and social environment in which the action will be implemented. A number of key assumptions and associated risk mitigation measures have been built into the project design to alleviate the impact of potential external events on project activities and provide greater assurance of successful project delivery.
