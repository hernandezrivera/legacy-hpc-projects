Project Summary

Physical and mental well-being of children and adolescents has remain a serious challenge as displacement and continued movement of IDPs and also returnees creates major protection concerns especially for children. An estimated 1.8 million children and adolescents (0.96million girls and 0.84million boys) in north-east Nigeria are in need of child protection and specialized support services. According to Humanitarian Response Plan, 2019, 1.5 million people have been identified and targeted for child protection service response across Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe states. At least 7,778 girls and 7,473 boys have been affected by protection risks including abuse, exploitation, family separation, neglect and violence; analysis of the case management cases recorded indicates that children living with vulnerable caregivers has carried the higher proportion of children affected by protection risks
More than 6,000 children (more than 50 per cent girls) are estimated to have been associated with armed groups, abducted, killed or victims of violent attacks by armed groups since the beginning of the conflict. Threats and risks to the well-being of girls and boys include family separation, explosive remnants of war, gender-based violence including child marriage and conflict-related sexual violence, recruitment and use of children, abduction and other grave child rights violations. Psychosocial distress has mostly affected IDP children and there is a clear need to assess the specific MHPSS needs for children and caregivers affected by the conflict. The ability of an estimated 110,700 parents and caregivers to care appropriately for children has been negatively impacted, putting children and families at risk of negative coping mechanisms including dropping out of school. The conflict has not only disrupted the social fabric but has also negatively affected the ability of parents and communities to appropriately care for their children. According to the HRP, 2019, most this vulnerable groups include, female headed households, adolescent girls who are forced into a role of providing for the family. Pre- existing gender inequalities due to unequal access to opportunities have contributed to limited resources and skills among caregivers of children and adolescent girls which have increased their vulnerability and exposure to abuse including sexual exploitation

1 - Needs

The IRC intends to scale up programming to improve access to quality lifesaving and well-coordinated child protection response services for children, adolescents and caregivers of children through the provision of integrated case management services, psychosocial support services through the Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS), Supporting Adolescents and their Families in Emergencies (SAFE), positive parenting for caregivers of children as well as enhance access to multi-sectoral services through established referral mechanisms. The Proposed intervention intend to improve the quality of child care and protection services for children in a coordinated and timely manner across Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states. The Intervention will also engage community structures and stakeholders to ensure the safety and well-being of children are promoted and that children are protected from all forms of violence. Adolescent boys and girls will participate in life skills activities through the SAFE activities that will empower them to increase their access to financial resources thereby reducing their vulnerability to abuse and exploitation. The IRC will work with relevant ministries (Women Affairs, Youth and sports), local organizations and community structures under this proposed intervention. In communities where no safe space exists, the IRC in collaboration with local authorities and community members will identify a safe space for psycho-social support activities and case management service provision. The Community Based Child Protection Committees formed or strengthened will support with the identification and reporting of child protection concerns in their communities as well as provide relevant child protection information to community member through dialogues and engagement activities. The IRC will strengthen its activities by focusing its intervention on adolescents and their caregivers while it continues to support UASC and other children at risk with their caregivers.

2 - Activities

The IRC will establish/or strengthen Community Based Child Protection Committees (CBCPCs). The community based structures will be gender sensitive with equal number of men and women who are from the communities and are well respected. They will be trained in child protection and will work closely with case workers in identifying child protection concerns, monitoring the trend and occurrence of child abuse and developing action plans on community engagements/dialogues based on trending child protection concerns. The committees will also be responsible for referring cases of children who have suffered any form of abuse or are at risk to the IRC case workers for case management services. Case management services will be provided for UASC and other children at risk. This case management services for the different categories of children will be community based with IRC trained case workers closely working with community structures, children and their caregivers. The IRC will also use the advantage of its specialized services like the Health, Reproductive Health, Women Protection and Empowerment with its strong internal referral system to refer cases of children and their caregivers with special need to provide them with specialized services. The IRC will leverage on the existence of other child protection actors who provide family tracing and reunification to refer cases of children in need of FTR and specialized mental health services. Beneficiaries under case management will also be provided with basic gender sensitive support materials in form of hygiene and dignity kits. Children ages 6-11 years will be enrolled in Safe Healing and learning Spaces (SHLS) Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) activities aimed at improving children's resilience, build positive relationship and enhance their conflict resolution skills and perseverance. Facilitators that will support with the SHLS and SAFE activities will be identified fro the various communities and trained. Adolescent boys and girls ages 13-19 years will be enrolled in a 12 weeks curriculum to increase their knowledge on life skills and improve their understanding on protection and resilience. And after graduation, the adolescents will form members of the youth club in their communities and will be trained on selected psycho social skills that will empower them to earn resources to cater for their individual needs. Caregivers of adolescents and children enrolled in psycho social SHLS activities will benefit for the 12 weeks curriculum on positive parenting skills and positive discipline to improve on their child care and protection and mitigate the use of negative discipline for children. Government social workers, child protection partners and IRC staff will also be training on child protection in Emergencies, response, referrals and support as well as the child protection minimum standards. Safety audits will be conducted quarterly to map out areas that are safe for children and adolescents in their communities and ares that are not safe. Mitigation measures will be discussed during community dialogue meeting and the community members will ensure those mitigation measures agreed are put in place to protect children for all forms of abuse and violence.
