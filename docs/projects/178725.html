Project Summary

This project has two components:
1-Mercy-USA aims to target 7,200 HH with monthly unconditional food assistance by using value-based vouchers and 584 HH with one-off supplementary food assistance. There are three categories of beneficiaries:
1) For 4,900 HH, each HH will receive a minimum value of USD$30 per month for up to 3 individuals. Each additional HH member will add USD$10 USD to the voucher value for 10 rounds starting from August 2021 till May 2022. According to FSL and CWG guidelines (This is secured fund to be implemented starting from August 2021)
2) For 2,300 HH, each HH will receive E-voucher with value 60 USD monthly for 8 months According to FSL and CWG guidelines (This is planned but not secured yet)
3) For 584 HH, each HH will receive E-voucher with value 60 USD one time plus top up 20 USD for big families and PLW as supplementary for the holy month of Ramadan. (This is secured fund to be implemented during Ramadan)

The distributed vouchers will be to be redeemed with list of allowed food commodities at the contracted vendor shops.
2- Gradual shift of 300 women from 300 HHs targeted under Mercy-USA's current regular beneficiaries to food production and food processing training. Once HHs begin generating sufficient income, they will be replaced with other HHs. Provision of USD$300 per woman as start-up cost, then USD$125 per woman every month over four months period for a total of USD$800 to support in the start-up including purchase of equipment and raw materials, packaging, and equipment.

4 - Needs

The crisis in Syria has entered into its 10th year, with 2020 having seen the emergence of new and complex socioeconomic difficulties that are further exacerbating the food security and livelihood situation of families across the country. The situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated with an estimated 14.2 million people in need of some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68.6% of the total population; Based on a countrywide assessment of 36,484 households and technical reviews and consultations, at least 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure, of which 1.3million people are considered severely food insecure. Furthermore, all 1.7 million people living in camps are deemed to be 100% food insecure, and 1.8 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity . The results show a sharp increase from last year, with an increase of 4.5 million people facing acute food insecurity in 2021 compared to 2020. Whereas in 2019, 39% of the total population was food insecure, that figure is now almost 60% in 2020. The number of severely food insecure people has also increased from 3% (0.6 million) of the population in 2019 to 6% (1.3 million) in 2020. (2021 HNO FSA)

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will be implemented by Mercy-USA field staff under the direct supervision of Mercy-USA management from the Turkey office Mercy-USA will attend all FSL Cluster meetings in Turkey, submit 5W's, along with regular information and updates about the project, and the region. Efforts will be made to coordinate with any humanitarian actors on the ground to ensure duplication is avoided. Field staff will coordinate with local councils to emphasize neutrality, and adherence to humanitarian principles while ensuring all stakeholders are informed in the area. When possible, temporary staff will be hired from the beneficiary community to maximize efficiency and impact while building local capacities. The regular food assistance component will be provided via monthly value-based vouchers for food with contracted markets in the targeted locations - Beneficiaries will be informed of the contracted markets and redeemed food list. Mercy-USA staff will ensure protection measures are taken to ensure equitable access particularly for women, and young girls . Options for home delivery of the needed food commodities for vouchers which will be redeemed on behalf of vulnerable persons (e.g., PWD who cannot access the markets, elderly, etc.) - Distribution schedule will be determined during the vulnerability assessment process in order to reasonably target the most vulnerable households first. - The program coordinator will oversee the project, compile and submit reports, and coordinate with the donor to ensure full compliance.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

SEA Risks: • Overcrowded during the food distributions/ redemption which may cause SEA• Mixed distribution lines for males and females • All distribution staff are males • The distribution is not accessible to all gender groups • No shared clear information about timing and content of distribution • No clear procedure regarding loss of card/voucher• Taxation on the food assistance • No complaints and feedback mechanism (CFM) in place Risk mitigation:• Ensure extend distribution days to avoid overcrowding • Separate lines for males and females in all distribution/ redemption process • Ensure gender balance in the hiring process, ideally to be 50% of the team are female• The distribution sites will be chosen to be accessible to all gender groups especially female HH and disables• Clear information about timing, contents, and procedures about loss of cards/vouchers of the distribution will be shared with all beneficiaries beforehand and will be existed in the banners in the distribution sites • Sensitization about the scope of the project to all communities with highlighting “This assistance is Free” and this statement will be printed on the beneficiary cards.• Different CFM channels will be in place such as whataspp, hotline, email, phone as well as physically by MEAL team. • PSEA awareness materials (such as posters and pamphlets) and reporting hotline will be visible to beneficiaries at the distribution site and/or are included in distribution kits.
