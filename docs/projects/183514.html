Project Summary

This project will support 600 most vulnerable households in the Gaza Strip (3600 individuals), based on their protection vulnerabilities, providing multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA), multi-disciplinary rehabilitation and assistive devices, community-based psychosocial support and information, counselling and legal aid. The project will also train a further 50 humanitarian staff and organizations in protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion. Specifically, the project aims at: (1) providing six MPCA instalments to vulnerable households to cover their basic needs including (but not limited to) any newly incurred debts, repairs and maintenance, food and water and education-related costs as well as health, non-food items, communication and transportation costs. (2) provision of multidisciplinary rehabilitation (MDR) center-based services including: physiotherapy, psychosocial support services, occupational therapy, nursing, distribution of wound dressing kits, and provision of Assistive Devices (ADs); (3) provide community mobilization activities with a focus on protection and prevention of violence and abuse, thorough group-based awareness raising sessions and psychosocial support sessions. NRC Legal and Protection Team will work in close synergy with the partner, to guarantee a continuum of care between legal aid, protection identification and referral and community mobilization.
The project preserves the physical and normative space for a two-state solution, mitigates the impact of occupation by providing cash assistance as well as protection services to vulnerable Palestinian individuals and communities. The project supports strengthening of resilience among the poorest and most vulnerable strata of the Gaza population, improves access to services and restores more dignified living conditions. It protects the most vulnerable Palestinians through a protection-oriented, multi-sectoral approach that encompasses emergency relief, community-based protection, and legal aid. Finally, the projects complements the efforts of the Palestinian National Cash Transfer Program (PNCTP), run by Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and co-financed by the European Union, to support households living in deep poverty.

1 - Needs

Over the past three years, PCBS reported an average unemployment rate in the Gaza Strip between 44% and 52%. These figures mask a deep disparity between males and females; the unemployment rate among females in 2019 reached 63.7%, the highest in the Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region and among the highest in the world. Regrettably, the COVID-19 pandemic has led to rates of employment to decrease by 17% between the first and second quarters of 2020. The low incomes and high rates of unemployment lead to extremely high rates of poverty, with over half of Gazans living in poverty today, of which one third are classified as living in ‘deep poverty’ (expenditure below ILS 1,974 per month) which makes the Gaza Strip among the poorest in the MENA region.Female-headed households are expected to be more food insecure than male-headed household due to their greater difficulties in joining the labour market. The primary reason for food insecurity is not the availability of food in local markets but the insufficient financial resources to acquire it. MoSD’s social safety net program has insufficient funding to cover all families in need of support - as of September 2021, 7,000 households are on the waiting list. Likewise, UNRWA provides in-kind food parcels to vulnerable households registered as refugees, equaling roughly $101 worth of food per family, per quarter. This assistance is designed to ensure families maintain a minimum acceptable food consumption level and is not sufficient to reduce families’ overall vulnerability. During the targeting exercise in 2020, the GPC saw no significant difference in vulnerability between refugee and non-refugee households in the Gaza Strip. The baseline household vulnerability analysis, conducted on 2241 refugee families, identified several critical issues, among this group, notably extremely high rate of debt (90%) with an average per household liability of over NIS 10,000, which would take six years to repay without assistance. In addition, consumption rates are 1.9-times higher than income, fuelling buying goods on credit. This is the most common coping strategy, observed among 78% of poorest refugees. The GPC started disbursing MPCA in October 2020 and conducted a midline post distribution monitoring (PDM) after five MPCA instalments were distributed and consumed. The findings showed significant improvements in the behaviour of beneficiaries: usage of negative coping strategies reduced from 45.5% at baseline to 11%, average rCSI score improved from 25.6 to 9, the percentage of population with an acceptable food consumption score (FCS) increased from 45% to 66%. The average needs met index approximately doubled, overachieving the target. During the current action, the protection team contacted and interviewed hundreds of families who reported myriad protection risks. The potential to engage in negative coping mechanisms were highest among households headed by or supporting persons with chronic illnesses or disabilities, as they are more likely to be excluded from the labour market. About 11% of the population is renting their dwelling, putting a significant strain on their finances, at a level where approximately half of those families are at risk of eviction. 22% of households declared women and children faced some protection challenges at home or at school. 9% of HHs specifically reported violence against children, of whom 72% self-reported domestic violence and 53% reported neglect. Many needed services are not available, in particular PSS in response to spikes in trauma: as per protection cluster sitrep (10th June) “a mass scale-up of all in-person and remote Psychosocial Support (PSS) services for children, families and service providers is required.” Based on recent HI report (May 2021), the latest escalation in May caused disability in 48 people while over 1400 people with disability were affected and currently need assistance. Many of these individuals have not been assisted since. Due to substantial losses of assets many families need assistance in retrieving them, either through information counselling or legal assistance.

2 - Activities

Result 1: Vulnerable HHs affected by the protracted crisis receive humanitarian assistance to improve their access to critical basic needs. 1.1: Selection of 400 unserved households from the MoSD PNCTP waiting list based on specific protection criteria (single headed households, elderly, persons with disabilities, HH with chronic diseases or serious medical condition, large number of dependents, children and women at risk, pregnant or lactating women, persons with specific legal and physical protection needs, SGBV survivors, lack of access to shelter, lack of access to WASH, refugees from the region) and living in deep poverty. The assessment of beneficiaries will be based on UVAT, established and endorsed by the cash working group. 1.2: Distribution of MPCA using Bank of Palestine ATM cards. The value of payments is based on the Cash Working Group (CWG) analysis, covering 60% of the value of minimum expenditure basket (MEB), and equal to NIS 1,046 per household per month. The total number of payments will be six, based on GPC evaluation of the impact of cash on beneficiaries. 1.3: After the first, third and sixth (last) transfer the GPC will conduct a post-distribution monitoring (PDM) in order to observe and analyse how the cash was spent and what needs were met. Beneficiaries will be sampled at a 95% confidence level and 5% margin of error. Best practices and lessons learned will be shared with the humanitarian sector in Gaza through the CWG. In order to mitigate mismanagement of MPCA, the GPC will include a financial management session during orientation and engage in a follow-up during post-distribution monitoring. R2: Households with specific protection vulnerabilities are supported and their needs addressed. 2.1: Assessment of specific needs of Person with Disabilities identified by the Gaza Protection Consortium within the HHs targeted by the Multipurpose Cash Assistance Action. A technical team composed by Physiotherapist, Nurse, Occupational Therapist and Psychosocial Expert will visit the beneficiaries, assess their specific needs -involving also caregivers and family members- and develop an individual treatment plan. 2.2: Provision of Multidisciplinary Rehabilitation (MDR) center-based services, in line with the explained methodologies, including: physiotherapy, psychosocial support services, occupational therapy, nursing, distribution of wound dressing kits, and provision of Assistive Devices (ADs). Outreach services will be operated in case of specific needs and impossibilities of the beneficiaries in reaching the centers. 2.3: Referral of beneficiaries to additional relevant services and update the services available in the Gaza Strip in line with the needs identified: in the Local Referral Network (lead by HI since 2009) for each Governorates. 2.4: Provision of community mobilization activities with a focus on protection and prevention of violence and abuse, thorough group-based awareness raising sessions and psychosocial support sessions. NRC Protection Team will work in close synergy with the partner AISHA, to guarantee a continuum of care between legal aid, protection identification and referral and community mobilization. Awareness raising sessions will focus on strengthening the self-protective capacity of the affected communities through information on protection related topics. Psychosocial support activities will focus on the level 3 of the MHPSS pyramid (non-focused community and family support). The activities will be provided by the local partner, AISHA. The partners have an extensive experience in the provision of protection and PSS programming in Gaza through which they seek to protect, support, and empower vulnerable women and children victims of violence and those with special needs psychosocially, legally and economically through facilitating access to protection and support services. Aisha’s has staff with a wide range of expertise such as social and psychological support and GBV issues including psychotherapist, psychiatrist, social workers and psychologists, case managers, Legal advisors, vocational trainers, projects coordinators, financial experts, and admin support staff, etc. Additionally, NRC has recently hired Protection Staff with a solid background in PSS, social-work, and Protection in Gaza and trainings have been conducted on Protection in humanitarian action, Case Management and Psychosocial Support. AISHA and NRC PSS programming will rely on a robust set of tools and guidance, in line with the IASC Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency settings. The PSS curriculum will focus on signs and impact of emergencies on well-being, stress management, positive coping mechanisms and self-care. R3: Emergency Rapid Response Mechanism 3.1: Analysis of the trigger, rapid assessment, scale, coordination with the relevant clusters, OCHA, local stakeholders 3.2: Implementation of an Emergency/Post-emergency Response to shocks at household and community level, reaching at least 200 households, through a provision of one-off/three transfers of MPCA in the value of NIS 625 each. 3.3: Post distribution monitoring of emergency and post emergency assistance beneficiaries. The project will continue highlighting the impact of the siege on Gaza on project beneficiaries, notably the socio-economic and protection-related implications and promote IHL and IHRL using NRC’s advocacy capacity/channels. The intervention will prioritize female-headed households, elderly, people with disabilities and chronic illness. Based on GPC data approximately 80% of households make joint decisions about spending of MPCA, which ensures women’s participation in access to basic needs and services. In cases of female-headed households, MPCA will strengthen their leading role as caretakers of households within their family and community. MDR activities targeting people with disabilities and elderly aim to mitigate negative impact of disabilities.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The Gaza Protection Consortium recognizes, based on its analysis (baseline, midline, endline), that most households have multi-sectoral needs. In particular, they owe substantial debt, require seasonal items such as clothes, transportation, education and medical items as well as food and other basic needs which cannot be covered with in-kind or voucher modality. Multi-purpose cash allows households the dignity to choose what their most pressing needs are at any given time. However, some services are not available in the Gaza Strip, even if households have income to pay for them. This is why the GPC is providing certain specific services to those families with particular protection vulnerabilities as part of its own program in order to alleviate the burden that cannot otherwise be addressed. This approach, GPC’s “Cash Plus”, is a comprehensive integrated approach providing MPCA and otherwise unavailable services to beneficiaries with specific needs.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Beneficiaries are selected from the Ministry of Social Development waiting list from the Palestinian National Cash Transfer Program. All households selected for the program are unserved, live in poverty or deep poverty and suffer from multiple protection vulnerabilities. Each household receives a standard MPCA transfer based on CWG recommendation. Each protection vulnerability that can be addressed by the Consortium is addressed while all out-of-scope protection vulnerabilities are referred out to existing service providers. Cash is transferred to beneficiaries accounts and can be withdrawn using a Bank of Palestine ATM card. The above measure safeguard do no harm ensuring that beneficiaries are aware why they have been selected, and what assistance they should expect. The assistance provided does not put them at risk, instead, it allows them to better sustain themselves and access basic needs and services.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The projects uses existing structures to determine its target group. The Consortium works with the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) waiting list of households, which are unserved by the National Cash Transfer Program (NCTP) despite being eligible – living in poverty, as determined by the national threshold. Households with most prevailing protection and socio-economic vulnerabilities are selected. Every households that matches the eligibility criteria (has the highest protection + socio-economic vulnerability) has an equal likelihood of being selected. Households with specific needs are priorities, that includes people with disabilities, chronically ill, female-headed households, pregnant and lactating women, high number of dependents/elderly.
