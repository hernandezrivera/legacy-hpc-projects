Project Summary

The project provides community awareness programs and targets boys and girls, men, women (including children with disabilities).Mine risk education is an important preventive health and education initiative that seeks to save the lives and limbs of civilians, in particular children living with the threat of landmines and Explosive Remnants of War (ERW).Throughout all these activities children will be identified to receive systemized individual support via case management by taking into consideration the best practice modules.The project will target the four sub-districts with severity levels 3 and 5, including Idleb, Dana, Azaz, Atma.The project will consider the needs, issues, and barriers faced by different groups in the affected population including gender and age groups (male, female), young children, children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged adults, older adults.We will give special care for people with disabilities and encourage them to apply for the project positions and will ensure that the representation of persons with disabilities is gender-balanced. They will benefit from adequate entry and movement opportunities.Indirect population:149,500.Direct population (10% of them will be people with disabilities):29,900-Girls(age:5-12):7599-Girls(age:13-17):3851-Boys(age:5-12):7599-Boys (age:13-17):3851-Women(age:18-65):3500-Men(age:18-65):3500.AFAQ will respond to the issues raised above by focusing: Output1:Community-based child protection for girls and boys is available in targeted locations Awareness-raising sessions and behavior change communication on family separation, child labor, child recruitment, child marriage,and Covid-19 will be provided for community.This will be implemented in CFS, schools, community centers via mobile teams in camps. Children and caregivers will be targeted with structured PSS support and parenting programs.Output2: Children and communities have access to mine/unexploded ordinance risk education and are better protected from the risk associated with UXOs and ERWs.AFAQ will deliver mine risk education messages in communities,IDP locations such as (mobile) CFS, and schools on the risk presented by unexploded remnants of war.AFAQ will use existing materials endorsed by the Mine Action Sub-Cluster. Output3:Case management services are delivered to child survivors and at-risk children.Case management services will target child survivors and at-risk children with inter-sectoral referrals to specialized services or sectors as identified through child protection outreach/monitoring/needs assessments.The case management will include family tracing and reunification services for Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC), in close collaboration with other child protection actors, other humanitarian services, and actors for referral purposes.Children Associated with Armed Forces and Group will be in addition provided with appropriate referrals to various support services such as non-formal education activities and vocational education.UASC will be provided with tracing, reunification, and reintegration activities.We will support 40 vulnerable children and provide them with appropriate assistance according to the assessment of the case management team.AFAQ will maintain a list of beneficiaries for verification and monitoring and we commit to submitting the data through the 4W.Output 4: Safe, accessible, child-sensitive reporting mechanisms are in place for PSEA.PSEA materials developed by the PSEA Interagency network will be distributed in the communities so that girls, boys, women, and men will be aware of how to report SEA cases.The PSEA team will provide awareness session activities on PSEA. 4 - Needs

Based on the 2022 SYRIA HNO, multiple cycles of displacement, substandard living conditions and continuing pandemic, exacerbate protection needs and increase reliance on negative coping mechanisms such as child labor, child recruitment, different forms of exploitation and early/forced marriage. Baed on the MSNA 2022, 67.3 % of key informants in NWS indicated that explosive ordance have been identified in the community since the befinning of the crisis. Explosive ordnance contamination endangers the lives of civilians, limits safe movements, and impacts economic opportunities, worsens food insecurity (in case when agricultural land is contaminated), hampers rehabilitation of public infrastructure such as schools or roads and limits the safe delivery of humanitarian aid and safe access to services for people in need.Insecurity, economic hardship, and Covid-19 exacerbate child protection concerns.These fueled harmful coping mechanisms, and increased child labor and child marriage, putting adolescent girls at particular risk.Based on 2021 Community level MSNA, child labor in 84% of communities; child marriage is an issue for adolescent girls in 71% of communities; family separation in 36% communities. Psychosocial distress among children is reported in 26 percent of surveyed households, demonstrating the increasing cumulative toll on mental well-being with immediate and if not addressed, lifelong consequences.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Girls, boys, women and men’s physical security is put at risk as a result of the violence associated with civilian populations/spaces being targeted by armed groups; a lack of consideration for the safety and security to access/receive services; lack of consideration of contextual information (aerial bombardment) and targeting of civilian infrastructure enroute to, during and directly after service provision.Mitigating Measures:1.Consider patterns of hostilities and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing fixed site or mobile service provision.2.Ensure safety audit is conducted to ensure safe access route and location by boys, girls,women and men,elderly and populations with decreased mobility due to physical, visual/hearing deficits, psychological issues.3.Engage with local authorities on safety and security in the community.4.Engage with diverse members of community on safety and security in the community to ensure diverse perspective and perceptions are considered.Risk: - Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys and their caregivers through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/services are of low quality.Mitigating Measures:- Ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remote managed context. -Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Afaq is now a member of the PSEA (Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) Network, and it commits all the network’s recommendations since the beginning of 2020. It has also an investigation committee in place and a PSEA focal point that attends its periodic meetings -All Afaq staff sign the HLG Code of Conduct after attending PSEA training. -Our team in the field provide awareness sessions on PSEA to beneficiaries and distribute posters and leaflets including the hotline and awareness messages on PSEA -Our team in the field also provide support in community consultations, monitoring of complaints and providing feedback to complainants and communities, and to support staff to sign the HLG Code of Conduct on a regular basis, including for newcomers and clearly prohibiting acts of SEA and obligating them to report such acts -Afaq ensures that procedures to guard against the hiring of persons who have a record of SEA offences are put in place and applied -Work with human resources personnel to include PSEA content in staff inductions, including all contractors, consultants, temporary staff, and casual laborer -Receive complaints, reports, and questions about alleged acts of sexual exploitation and abuse committed by the organization’s personnel and personnel of sister/affiliated institutions including the cases committed by personnel whose institutions are not known -Act as the liaison between the Organization and the Inter-Agency Community Based Complaints Mechanism.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1.Limited access to target locations because of insecurity and escalation of fighting or extreme weather conditions.2.Changes in the security situation or authorities in control of project areas.Changes could increase the risks faced, displace or restrict movements of the population, local partners relevant to the project.3.Limited monitoring by Gaziantep Hub staff due to the remote-management nature of the cross-border operations.4.Exposure of affected population to sexual exploitation and abuse.5.Fraud, aid diversion and mishandling of supplies could place a risk.6.Risk of all-out Covid-19 pandemic.Mitigating measures:1. Ensure relevant groups are aware of, and technical authorities engaged with, the project and commit to ensuring basic safety/access for its implementation.2.Closely monitor the security situation in coordination with civil authorities and security and access stakeholders. Ensure projects in insecure locations have security plans for staff and beneficiaries and sufficient resources to enact those plans.Ensure a minimum duty of care to staff including a right of refusal.3.A variety of communication/visual tools will be used to bridge gaps on WhatsApp, Skype, and for partners.4.AFAQ developed PSEA principles and shared those with all staff.Those staff that has direct contact with the affected population are aware of PSEA requirements. In all project interventions, consideration will be made to prevent PSEA.5.A complaint mechanism for beneficiaries is in place.
