Project Summary

In support of the education and skills development for all, UNICEF through this project in 2020, will further support its No Lost Generation Agenda UNICEF will focus on strengthening and improving access to skills-development programmes that are comprehensive and reach the most vulnerable adolescents especially those not in Education, Employment and Training (NEET). UNICEF will address the issue of lack of relevant and contextualized non-formal education which causes poor learning outcomes. UNICEF will promote alternative pathways and learning beyond schools and implement 21st skills development programming in line with the MENA Life Skills and Citizenship Education (LSCE) framework and support development of foundational, transferable, and technical (such as entrepreneurship) programming to prepare adolescents better for their future, including professional, technical and entrepreneurial training, for work and life.

4 - Needs

The health, education and WASH infrastructure is in critical need of attention to facilitate socio-economic recovery and addressing the 6.5 million internally displaced persons. The crisis continues to have devastating effect on the education system and infrastructure, severely affecting both access and quality of education. An estimated 2.08 million children (5-17 years old) are out of school and 1.35 million are at risk of dropping out in Syria . The crisis has left many adolescents and youth with limited opportunities to engage in their communities and country. Unemployment rates among youth are estimated to be close to 75 per cent and are significantly higher among women. There are several reasons for this, ranging from escaping conflict to disability to an education system that doesn’t provide the skills young people need for employment today resulting in a skills mismatch.With limited opportunities, and uncertain about what the future has in store, adolescents have grown increasingly, disillusioned, frustrated and disempowered. Adolescent boys are at a substantial risk of being killed and/or injured or being exploited and involved in child labour. Adolescent girls are particularly at risk of child marriage and other forms of gender-based violence, including sexual violence. Even though girls and young women are more likely to be in school, less likely to drop out, more likely to have better learning outcomes they are less likely to be in formal employment in the labour market. Response must address the underlying social norms that are preventing women from accessing employment through genders sensitive education /learning programmes and a gender transformative community engagement-based approach.Adolescents in Syria lack opportunities for skills development, engagement and participation. Adolescents are poorly skilled, and they do not acquire adequate educational and professional assets to successfully navigate the important transition into adulthood.

5 - Implementation of activities

To address the learning needs of adolescent and youth in Syria, UNICEF embraces a multi-pronged approach. This is done through cross sectoral programming with Education, Health, WASH, Child Protection and Social Policy to meet the needs of adolescents and youth and through ADAP standalone programming that ensures that adolescent boys and girls, especially the most deprived, are able to reach their full potential and contribute to a cohesive, peaceful and resilient Syrian society’through:1.• Enhance skills and knowledge for improved employability of adolescents and youth- Multi-sectoral approaches including specific second decade priorities approach will be promoted support adolescents to achieve their full potential. Provision of integrated package of services including formal and non-formal education. Design and implement Technical Vocational Education and Training (TVET) programmes that are gender sensitive and responsive to the labour market needs 2. • Spearhead and champion leadership and positive agency and engagement of adolescents and youth Create innovative platforms for engagement and equitable participation of adolescents, with special attention to girls and those from disadvantaged communities. Life Skills and Citizenship Education (LSCE) will be provided to enhance critical thinking, communication, collaboration and creativity skills. Adolescents and youth empowered with these skills will be actively and meaningfully engaged in their communities with a good number of young people leading, designing and implementing social and civic engagement activities in their communities. The community engagement activities led by adolescents and youth will contribute to social cohesion and peace building.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Adolescent and youth empowerment may lead to greater exposure for young people causing them unintended harm and also motivate youth to unintentionally put themselves in danger while addressing sensitive issues; Due to social norms, girls’ parents may not allow them to attend as planned; Sport/cultural/civic engagement activities in open spaces may lead to increased risk due to unexpected mortars. To mitigate such possible risks to young people, the principle of Do No Harm must be emphasized throughout all stages of programming. Working with parents will also be critical to ensure equitable participation and rights of boys and girls. Risk assessments of all facilities will need to be conducted and safety measures will be supported. As the project will address actual needs of young people and voices of young people will be adhered to, the principle of Do Good aims to support a process whereby young girls and boys are able to share their views freely on interventions relevant to their needs.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The project risks include; Lack of adequate resources, continued conflict affecting programme progress, restrictions and limitations affecting access and reach, Sanctions on Syria limiting online access opportunities and engagement and Community resistance to change. The key assumption is willingness of young people to participate. Mitigation measures include: Evidence based advocacy, Preparedness plan for different scenarios and LS for resilience building, supporting innovation and Syrian platforms, promoting behaviour change and engagement of community through different pathways.
