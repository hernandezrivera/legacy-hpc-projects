4 - Needs

Years of crisis have had a profound negative impact on the socioeconomic conditions in the country. Poverty is a persistent driver of chronic needs with over 80 per cent of Syrians estimated to be living under the poverty line. Disruptions in economic activity have only deepened poverty, with a lack of access to sustained employment and decreased purchasing power now widely reported. In an assessment of vulnerable households conducted by DCA in January 2020, 65% of households in the project locations reported having no employment to support their household, amongst IDPs this number rose to 87%. Households’ purchasing power has weakened over time, due to displacement, loss of assets, limited income-generating opportunities, the marked depreciation of the Syrian Pound and increasing food prices, among others. In order to address their basic household needs, many households with no viable source of income are resorting to negative coping mechanisms, including decreasing meal sizes, relying on unsafe water sources, completely depleting family savings, increasing debts, early child marriage, and child labour. Vital water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) infrastructure has been destroyed or in disrepair, and more than eight million people are without access to safe water. Up to 42 per cent of surveyed households in Syria rely on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs, which combined with high rates of discharging of sewage in open or dysfunctional sewerage systems increases the risk of diseases. WASH services are both limited and expensive - especially when there is a lack of power supply, in which case local authorities resort to using generators raising the cost of water for households. Without sources of income and decreasing purchasing power, families struggle to meet their basic needs, including WASH related needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will enable vulnerable households to access their basic WASH needs through:* The provision of market-based assistance for WASH services, in the form of Multi Purpose Cash grants. Following a market assessment vulnerable households with no source of income will be provided with MPC grants in areas where markets are functional and easily accessible. The value of the grants will be agreed with the sector working group and will be in line with SMEB value and cluster recommendations. Multi-purpose cash transfers aim to target the most vulnerable IDP and host-community households. Where possible, households will be targeted to receive 3 rounds of monthly MPC transfers.Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted on a monthly basis in order to ensure the response is appropriate, relevant and timely, and to help WASH strategic thinking in the future.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High.Mitigation: Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about what services are available and who services are for. Regular training will be provided to project staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). A complaints box will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentially. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by project or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint.Protection Risk: Tensions due to jealousies between IDPs, Returnees and Host communities.Likelihood: High. Impact: High.Mitigation measures: 1. Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status; 2. Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population to beneficiary selection criteria; 3. Where appropriate, a mix of IDP, returnee and host community households are selected.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks project locations impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas, and also threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation measures: The organization has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security SOPs and procedures, and conducts regular security-related training for staff. Project management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation and evacuation plans are in place. Duty of Care policies and procedures are in place for national staff.
