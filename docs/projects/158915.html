Project Summary

The project is an immediate response to ensure vulnerable women, children and men have sufficient storage capacity of domestic and drinking water whenever it becomes available, and have better access to safe water and safe sanitation facilities in Gaza. Public services will be improved through the rehabilitation, construction, and upgrade of existing wastewater infrastructure and the targeted population will also receive promotion of healthy hygiene practices with tailored awareness-raising activities.

1 - Needs

The political instability and 12 years of blockade have resulted in the lack of access to adequate WASH services and restricted movements for people and goods. Gaza’s population is subject to a chronic electricity crisis caused from the Gaza Power Plant that is regularly shut-down due to fuel shortages; a 40% reduction in the electricity supplied by Israel following the Palestinian Authority’s decision to cut funds to Gaza as well as the interrupted Egyptian power lines. Power outages of 18-20 hours/day have severely impacted the provision of essential services, particularly health, domestic water supply and sanitation services. Gaza’s population relies on the deteriorated groundwater aquifer which is the sole source for all water use. Chloride and nitrate concentrations are higher than WHO guidelines, which make the extracted water unsafe for drinking without treatment. The population has therefore become dependent on private and public desalination plants for drinking water. However, 68% of the consumed water is susceptible to biological contamination due to inadequate disinfection at the desalination plants, improper handling during distribution, and poor user storage habits. According to the Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU), 23% of the Gaza Strip lacks wastewater systems, forcing households to rely on cesspits for excreta disposal that flood in the streets and houses causing damage to property, environment and having a significant negative impact on basic health, mainly on women and children. Children are the most vulnerable by being exposed to waterborne diseases, and without proper sanitation facilities, their basic hygiene needs cannot be covered. UNRWA and Ministry of Health records indicate an increase in the cases of diarrhea, skin infections and viral meningitis. This causes women to be unable of carrying out daily household chores and meet their personal hygiene needs (i.e. during menstruation). During the last 5 years, UNICEF led multiple interventions at household and community levels, inter alia with support from different implementing partners. Recently, based on need assessments conducted by its partners between early 2019 and July 2019, Action Against Hunger (AAH) and OXFAM estimated that there are more than 8,000 households in need of household sanitation rehabilitation across the Gaza Strip. Data collected for about 17,000 applicants were analyzed and short-listed households were visited by social workers and the AAH technical team in the five governorates of the Gaza Strip. The verification visits were completed in July 2019 and the proposed beneficiary list prepared for the people in need of household sanitation rehabilitation including domestic water tank installation. Another assessment on sewage connections in urban areas across the Strip, excluding Gaza City, was conducted in coordination with CMWU and the municipalities. Based on this assessment; Jort Al lout and Satar in Khan Yunis governorate in addition to AlSalam area in Rafah were selected to improve their connection to wastewater networks., These key findings and interviews with community members demonstrate a significant gap in meeting the water storage and sanitation needs of the Gazan population. UNICEF and its partners are committed to gender sensitive WASH programming, as well as engaging individuals with disabilities. Following the same principles defined in the project identification, design and implementation will be inclusive of women, girls and boys’ inputs at all stages. UNICEF will use a broad range of methodologies to ensure comprehensive collection of beneficiaries’ voices, including key informant interviews, focus group discussions, and Human Interest Stories. A feedback mechanism will be put in place in concert with community leaders to ensure that the beneficiaries’ voice, including women, children and people with with disabilities, are duly heard and prompt follow-up actions are taken to address complaints.

2 - Activities

The proposed intervention is an immediate response to ensure women, children and men have sufficient storage capacity of domestic water and have better access to safe water and safe sanitation facilities and services. It aims for further addressing the significant gap in humanitarian needs, especially targeting female heads of households and war widows. Implemented activities will consider the needs of women and girls. Trainings will focus on the specific needs of men and women (e.g. menstruation and child care) with appropriate locations and timing. Separate training sessions with gender specific trainers may be required to ensure messaging is presented in a gender sensitive manner. All activities will be coordinated with Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) and relevant stakeholders: 1. Household Water Supply 1.1 Provision and installation of 300 new domestic 1000l water roof storage tanks equipped with all fittings installed for families whose water storage tanks were not filled due to deteriorated power crisis or are consuming less than 60 l/c/d. Priority will be given to large families living in critical conditions; families benefiting from household sanitation rehabilitation; women headed families; and vulnerable families with children and disabled members in Gaza North, Khan Younis and Rafah. 2. Household Sanitary Services 2.1 Rehabilitation of sanitation facilities for 1,200 households to meet SPHERE standards including GMR affected people 2.2 Rehabilitation of wastewater connections for 600 households in Khan Younis 3. Household Hygiene Promotion 3.1 1,800 households will be targeted with two home visits through the Communication for Development approach to discuss, negotiate and plan actions for behavioural change to improve their knowledge, attitude, and practices of key water handling messages, and menstrual hygiene for adolescents and women. This activity will be also promoted through focus group discussions with communities’ representatives and other key informants, 4. 4. Wastewater network constructions in AlSalam area in Rafah will be conducted to improve access to adequate sanitation services at community levels

3 - Indicators

UNICEF and its implementing partners will maintain close coordination with PWA, relevant stakeholders, related municipalities, WASH Cluster, area focal points and other organizations working in the targeted areas to avoid duplication and ensure complementarity. UNICEF and its implementing partners will supervise the projects on a daily basis to ensure timely and effective implementation. Women and men’s participation in workshops will strengthen social relations between family members and unify them in their efforts to assure their long-term water and sanitation needs are met. FGDs will ensure separation of women and men to create an open environment where women are free to discuss key issues. A detailed survey, field visits and water analysis will be conducted to supervise, monitor and evaluate the activities. A Knowledge, Attitudes and Practices (KAP) survey will be conducted to assess and evaluate the impacts of the activities on women, men and boys, girls, persons with disabilities and the elderly. UNICEF and its implementing partners will contribute to the exchange of information/lessons learned, fact sheets and stories among the cluster community, which are to be used for advocacy purposes. Project activities will be monitored by a Project Management Team composed of key stakeholders responsible for monitoring and evaluation, and the results will be communicated to humanitarian actors and project beneficiaries. Accountability and transparency will be ensured through establishment of a beneficiary feedback mechanism.
