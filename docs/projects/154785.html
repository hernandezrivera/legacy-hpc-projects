Project Summary

This project seeks to assist the most vulnerable children in three refugee camps in Anbar (Kilo 18, Habbaniya Tourist City (HTC), Bezabize and Amriyat al Fallujah (AAF)) in order to protect them from extremism, through better opportunities towards reintegration, peace-building, education, and recovery. The peace-building or peaceful co-existence activities will be limited, narrowly focused, and short term. Mercy Hands will ensure through initial assessments that the peaceful co-existence activities are supplemented and embedded in ongoing community-based protection interventions, primarily targeting areas of displacement. Although all children in the above-mentioned camps will be invited to participate in the programs, Mercy Hands desires to focus on children whose families are suspected to be affiliated to IS. These children, with their parents, mostly mothers, are currently isolated in a specific portion of the camps and lack access to most of the services provided.
Targets:
- Children in camps
- Children whose families are suspected of having IS affiliation
- Female heads of families suspected of having IS affiliation
Main Goals of this Project are:
1. Fill gaps in education, legal assistance, and psycho-social support for the IS affiliated children within these camps.
2. Address the specific needs of the targeted vulnerable population (IS affiliated children and their families).
3. Strengthen protection for females, due to the absence of male presence in the families (PSEA, complaint mechanisms, awareness sessions, etc.)
4. Establish and run reconciliation activities between the camp populations. Through establishing reconciliation activities involving families from both parts of the camps, Mercy Hands hopes to pave a way for acceptance of the children as victims of IS.
Mercy Hands will ensure referral pathways between different aspects of the program to ensure all the needs of the targeted population are identified and addressed to the proper entities.

Needs

Through the analysis of data reported by the UNHCR, assessments conducted by Mercy Hands in the field, and data reported in the MCNA, Mercy Hands has found that there is a total of 24,387 affiliated children IDPs in need of legal assistance within the AAF, Bezabize, Kilo-18, and HTC camps. The numbers breakdown of affiliated children with education and psycho-social support needs in the four camps is as follows: - AAF: 13,154 - Bezabize: 4,253- Kilo-18: 1,323- HTC: 5,657. Most of the children are between the ages of 6-18, with girls slightly outnumbering boys. Through the assessment done by Mercy Hands and data reported in the MCNA, Mercy Hands has identified four key needs for the affiliated IDP children in these camps:1. Access to legal protection 2. Access to protection for children3. Access to education4. Peace building. The internal assessment done by Mercy Hands has validated the data provided in the MCNA on the needs that the children IDPs face in these camps, specifically that of access to education and protection. Key gaps in needs are specifically access to education in both HTC and Kilo-18 camps. The accesses identified in this section are suggested to fill these gaps and further increase assistance to the vulnerable populations in these camps. Sources: REACH Maps, GBV Cluster Meetings (Central and South), Humanitarianresponse.info; Relief Web International, Mercy Hands Assessments in camps in East Anbar, MCNA, UNHCR (FSMT)

Activities

ActivitiesAwareness Sessions Free Legal Consultations Case Management Establishment of confidential complaint and feedback mechanisms Provision of psychological support Catch-up classes Informal schooling Peer-to-Peer support Distribution of supplies FGDs Provision of vocational trainings1. Access to legal protection for the targeted population. This need will be addressed through assessment, then raising awareness of the needs and response by organizing awareness sessions with lawyers, meetings inside the camps, free consultation with lawyers, and case management of legal protection cases. 2. Access to Protection for Children this need will be addressed through a quick survey, through awareness sessions, FGDs, counseling for children and mothers regarding victim-hood, establishment of confidential complaint mechanisms, and provision of psychological support to victims. 3. Access to education. This need will be addressed through a quick survey assessing the situation, catch-up classes, informal schooling, peer-to-peer support and distribution of school supplies. 4. Peaceful co-existence: this need will be addressed through first implementing a survey to assess the needs of the children and then creating programs based on those needs. This assessment will also seek to allow Mercy Hands to find out which existing community based interventions targeting areas of displacement are happening so Mercy Hands is able to design activities to supplement these ongoing interventions. Mercy Hands will attempt to find and supplement programs that are: awareness sessions focusing on building understanding and acceptance between the two groups (affiliated and non-affiliated), and provision of vocational trainings and events for females and female headed households in which there is mixing of the above-mentioned groups. Mercy Hands will ensure a strong referral pathway for PSS to other organizations already working in the camps.5. Community based approach through capacity building: according to the background and education of the affiliated population, MH will group the education community mobilizers with educated individuals in the camp populations to provide them training so they are able to lead educational courses within the camp, essentially this training will be to train teachers within the camps. This activity is not limited to children only and will work to include both affiliated and non-affiliated populations to foster reconciliation and understandingMercy Hands will also ensure that the activities are set up to ensure that the target beneficiary populations are interacting with each other. Through this the project hopes to increase cooperation among beneficiaries and pave the way for eventual reconciliation.

Protection Risk Analysis

1. Risks for Beneficiaries: Security, Camp closure, Risk of leak of confidential details and Psychological Trauma.2. Risks for Humanitarians: Security (specifically, threats originating from working with affiliated populations), Psychological Trauma, Health concerns and lack of cooperation from security forces within the affiliated population section of the camps.

Mitigation Measures

Security: Coordination with local authorities, community leaders, and camp management. Flexibility and adaptation in project implementation, specifically the reconciliation aspect, to ensure acceptance from parties involved. For humanitarians, working with camp, community and religious leaders within the camps to gain acceptance of the population in the camps. Also, keeping a low profile to reduce any risk of populations outside of the camp interfering with the work. Ensuring that the team is composed of non-local Iraqis to reduce the risk of working with affiliated populations to team members.Camp closure: MH will work in close coordination with camp management to adapt the response and ensure continuity of programs in new location of IDPs. MH will also work to ensure that the response programs are not dependent on location and can be moved with the targeted IDP population. Psychological Trauma: Specialized and in-depth training of team to address psychological needs of team members and beneficiaries. Health Concerns: Awareness of healthy habits and practices. Leak of Confidential Details: Establishment of strict Code of Conduct for team members, establishment of confidential complaint mechanisms, and establishment of strict data access mechanisms. Lack of cooperation from security forces: MH will work to ensure close coordination with camp management, and local authorities in Anbar to address and mitigate this risk. MH will also have an expert to advise and assist safety and security for both beneficiaries and humanitarians.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Through complaint and feedback mechanisms: Anonymous Hotlines, Complaint and feedback boxes, Complaint and feedback desks, Mercy Hands will ensure the program is designed, implemented, and monitored with the input shared by beneficiaries and will ensure proper and timely answers to complaints. This process will be handled by a “Complaints Officer” who will be the focal point of gathering and addressing the complaints that are received. This method will also work to ensure confidentiality of the complainants since only the Complaints Officer will have access to complaints and complainants.
