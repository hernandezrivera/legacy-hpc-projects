Project Summary

Background
The Food Security Sector (FSS) in Palestine, created in December 2012, brings together UN and NGO organizations on an equal footing to improve the food security of the needy and vulnerable. In addition, the FSS works toward building stronger partnerships to support sustainable, comprehensive, and locally-owned food security interventions. The 45 members collaborate together, leveraging the existing expertise of stakeholders across three main pillars: food assistance, agriculture and livelihood support, and cash-based programming, thus ensuring immediate access to food and livelihood protection/restoration while enhancing self-reliance. Furthermore, the coordination structure interlinks humanitarian, recovery, and development interventions while working at the inter-cluster level to contribute to a holistic response.
As such, currently, more than ever, it is crucial to:
1) Support strategic planning of FSS by coordinating a participatory and consultative platform to increase the efficiency/effectiveness of FSS partners' assistance;
2) Support and provide generation of data, analysis and information for planning and coordination of FSS interventions and informing policy adaptation;
3) Support strengthening national capacities to contribute to effective coordination and advocacy for humanitarian interventions within an HDP nexus approach.
4) Improve cooperation with line ministries and institutions in charge of coordination at the central and decentralized levels to progressively absorb FSS coordination functions.
Through a bottom-up approach, the FSS as a multi-stakeholder platform is a cornerstone to informing joint analysis of underlying causes of food insecurity and providing needed assistance services to the population.
Project Summary
Palestinians are less able to cope with manmade or natural events, such as peaks in violence or weather extremes. Moreover, these sudden shocks occur alongside an already exacerbated situation characterized by demolitions in the West Bank, trade restrictions, persistent electricity shortages in Gaza, natural and environmental hazards, and inadequate water and sanitation services. These inevitably hamper economic, social and urban development while impeding free movement and access to essential services.
Strengthening the coordination and the coherence along the HDP nexus efforts to reduce the humanitarian caseload effectively is critical in this context, as funding for humanitarian action is outstripped by growing needs.
Under Strategic Objective 3 (SO3), the proposed project intends to:
 Strengthening coordination and prioritization of humanitarian interventions and facilitating linkages with developmental programmes.
 Supporting the gathering, production and sharing of information required for effective planning and coordination for food security.
 Improving cooperation and providing technical support to the line ministries and institutions in charge of coordination at the central and decentralized levels.
The activities under the Food Security Sector (FSS) address the essential protection needs of vulnerable people in the country by enabling coordinated efforts to ensure direct access to food and critical livelihood needs and promoting protection mainstreaming in programming. The budget presented in this project represents the calendar year requirements for Jan – Dec 2023, covering the specific activities planned during this period only. The FSS coordination body does not provide any cash or in-kind tangible assistance. However, it is a service provision aiming to strengthen the coordination and linkages with the line ministries, FSS leading agencies, local and international organizations, and partners. Service provision by the FSS coordination team is delivered through meetings, workshops, and technical surveys and assessments, in cooperation with FSS partners and other governmental and non-governmental stakeholders.

1 - Needs

An estimated 1.8 million people (33.6 per cent of the population) suffer from moderate to severe food insecurity (FIES estimates based on the data collected from the MSNA survey 2022). In 2022, approximately 1 out of 3 people experienced some form of food insecurity, 84 per cent (1.54 million people) of which are expected to need humanitarian assistance. 77 per cent of those in need of humanitarian assistance are in the Gaza Strip (1.19 million people), and 23 per cent in the West Bank (353 thousand people). 81 per cent of those in the West Bank (about 285 thousand people) are in Areas A and B. Moreover, women are found to be more in need than men; 371,759 women and 28% of people headed by females need assistance. Israel's military occupation and its coercive environment in the WB, the over 14 years of Gaza's blockade and regular rounds of violence remain the root cause of food insecurity. In addition, the chronic humanitarian crisis was revealed to be not gender-neutral, showing differential impacts on women, girls, men, boys and people with disabilities. Moreover, the situation is detrimental to food production, impeding access to land, water, production inputs, and markets. The constrained productive capacities and limited economic opportunities contribute to higher unemployment rates, lowering households' income. In the Gaza Strip (with 63% of the population food insecure), the need for humanitarian assistance continues to be high with the continued blockade and recurrent hostilities. In addition, the livelihoods of many farmers, breeders, herders, and fishers are negatively impacted by the destruction and damage of their productive assets and farming infrastructures. These constraints inevitably hinder socio-economic development while preventing freedom of movement and access to essential services and affect aid actors, preventing them from delivering effective assistance.Against this background, the Food Security Sector aims to support sustainable, holistic and local food insecurity planning response, focusing on humanitarian and developmental coordination and funding streams. Under the HDP nexus umbrella, FSS Action Plan includes two main streams of broader general strategic results: 1. Response and recovery; 2. Emergency and preparedness. Four significant objectives and related activities are identified as follows:1. Coordinated production of information/analysis in support of strategic decision-making, 2. Coordinated implementation of FSS partners' responses, including the Humanitarian Programme Cycle (HPC) plan.3. Strengthened capacity to respond to sudden-onset natural or manmade disasters.4. Food Security advocacy issues are promoted within national fora initiatives, which include regular reporting and visibility5. Coordinated response informed by a gender analysis, incorporating recommendations drawn from robust analyses, which identifies the shifting needs, capacities and priorities of women, girls, men and boys and PWD.When doable, all the above activities will be carried out in cooperation with relevant line ministries institutions. This includes the continuous assessment and joint evaluation (i.e., FSS and PA) of the feasibility of increasing local authorities' role, involvement, and responsibilities concerning existing FSS functions. This will be performed in coordination within a common framework under the umbrella of the HCT, the Humanitarian Coordinator and the inter-cluster coordination group.

2 - Activities

Coordination between FSS actors is needed for facilitating interventions, guiding and informing properly, thus reducing vulnerability and maximizing resilience-building activities within the Humanitarian Response Plan. The project will ensure the continuation of the work of the FSC, including 1. Support the introduction of a more harmonized methodology to measure food insecurity and, thus allowing the possibility to have cross-country comparisons and increase visibility within international platforms (IPC). 2. Strengthen gender analysis, backed up by sex, age, and disability disaggregated data. 3. Strengthen the coordination of the participatory platform to increase the efficiency of FSS partners' assistance. Specifically: a. Within the Humanitarian Programme Cycle: collect, analyze and plan for FSS component needs, gaps, and responses involving all FSS stakeholders; b. Significantly contribute to gender equality, including across age groups. c. Inform the Humanitarian Pool Funds (HPF) and other ad-hoc funding opportunities; d. Coordinate response to demolitions of agricultural assets and settlers' violence towards farmers in the WB and different ad-hoc responses; e. Contribute to inter-sectorial coordination at the central and governorate levels, identifying needs and response modalities that may require an inter-cluster ad-hoc harmonized response. f. Coordination with other international agencies & NGOs - additional collaboration will be elaborated with Health, Protection, and WASH clusters and the Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance group. Set minimum acceptable standards of deliverables, streamline interventions, facilitate operational communication and coordination of inter-sector and intra-sector operations, and review technical specifications. The FSS will continue supporting thematic/operational/situational working groups established according to needs. 4. Supporting data generation process, analysis and information gathering for better planning. Specifically, a. Engage FSS actors in sharing information on planning and priorities to promote complementarity and joint programming using the 5Ws platform. b. Identify priorities, potential gaps or duplication to improve information and data sharing for inter-agency and government plans on food safety analysis issues and activities; c. To inform planning and operational responses, monitor, and provide technical support to assess the possible impact of shocks on the Palestinian agri-food system. d. Analyze needs related to increasing gender mainstreaming in food security programming. e. Maintain/improve FSS components of the inter-cluster contingency plan in coordination with OCHA and line ministries. 5. Support strengthening national capacities for effective coordination and advocacy for humanitarian interventions within an HDP nexus approach. Specifically: a. Facilitate roundtable discussions among all partners for a better understanding of the nexus approach and better engagement in related policy discussions at the national and global levels. b. Strengthen the capacity of NGOs and line ministries (central/decentralized) to practice and lead coordination functions. c. Ensure greater dialogue on food security emergencies, especially in the most vulnerable areas, among food, nutrition, health officials, civil society, donors, partners, and other key stakeholders.d. Support participation of the FSS partners in national efforts towards the achievementand monitoring of SDG2 (like in the activities of the SDG2 Working Group), also contributing to developing FNS Governance institutional capacities, as needed. e. Foster qualitative programming components like Protection and People-Centric issues by delivering training and supporting learning opportunities for FSS partners. 6. Supporting government-led coordination structures, LACS, AIDA, PNGO and NGOs consortia to facilitate the humanitarian-development nexus approach; specifically: a. Incorporate the commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) into all relevant policies and operational guidelines and promote them among FSS partners; b. Develop and/or support resource mobilization and advocacy strategies to strengthen relations with other clusters, UN agencies, governments, and donors and enhance humanitarian interventions within an HDP nexus.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

To ensure that the FSS partners' are continuously addressed and in line with the Humanitarian Accountability Framework, the FSS has elaborated an easy-to-access complaints mechanism that regularly informs its members and relevant stakeholders. The feedback mechanism is intended to track the performance of the FSS coordination team in terms of 1. Request for information, 2. Request for assistance, 3. The significant satisfaction/dissatisfaction with the behaviour/coordination of the FSS team.Regular and quick feedback will ensure the quality and transparency of services and gaps to enable FSS to respond accordingly. The feedback cycle will channel inputs into changes and innovations to improve the project's quality, relevance, and effectiveness. The feedback mechanism will constantly make the intervention more rapid, effective, safe, and dignified and strengthen and enhance the relations between the FSS team and its members. On the other hand, FSS will invest in information management tools and conduct a regular survey to determine how partners view activities. A Cluster Coordination Performance Monitoring (CCPM) is a self-assessment exercise. The FSS will monitor the performance and get feedback against the six core functions of the FSS and AAP. The CCPM is done annually and is supported by the gFSC, targeting all stakeholders, including UN agencies, national and international NGOs, national authorities, and representatives of crosscutting issues. The key findings of the CCPM will be discussed, identifying mitigating factors and explanations of performance and agreeing on actions that could be taken to improve performance if necessary. Finally, organizing a consultative process and meetings, including all main stakeholders, to decide on the broad areas that need to be explored, the specific questions that need to be answered, and how to proceed.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The Food Security Sector coordination system is designed to strengthen protection mainstreaming by decreasing threats, reducing vulnerability, and enhancing the involvement of the affected people. FSS will work closely with partners to improve food security status at the household level, thereby reducing the use of negative and often-irreversible coping strategies. FSS adopts a Do No Harm approaches through informed decisions around distribution and implementation modalities in program design. Particularly vulnerable communities in Area C struggling to access essential services such as water, sanitation, and health, because of violations of IHLand IHRL will be supported with livelihoods support interventions, income generation opportunities and direct access to food. FSS partners will support vulnerable households in urban communities, farmers and fisher folk in the Gaza Strip in response to the coercions and deprivations they are exposed to. Working closely with the Protection and Advocacy focal point, the FSS team and partners will advocate on the issues driving food insecurity, such as:1) Lack of access to sustainable employment and income opportunities, 2) Settler violence against farmers in the West Bank, 3) Limitation of access and dual-use restrictions for importing materials into the Gaza Strip.Moreover, the FSS will promote increased accountability of respective duty bearers and assist them in fulfilling their obligations. Through the gender focal point, the FSS team will encourage partners to contribute to gender equality and gender-sensitive programming by prioritizing identifying and responding to gender-based vulnerabilities in support of the eroded coping capacities of gender-sensitive groups like female-headed households. FSS will ensure all partners build on the success and learning of their implemented food, cash, and livelihoods programming. The team will provide refresher training to all partners on protection mainstreaming in theFSS projects.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The FSS, in close coordination with the protection cluster, will encourage partners to develop community-based strategies to ensure that the affected population are fully informed and consulted and can participate in decision-making related to the rehabilitation of disrupted livelihoodsThe FSS will integrate the efforts to ensure access to all the affected population, including women and older persons, in the formal and informal economy. The FSS will ensure that partners working in distribution activities have an inclusive approach for the affected communities in the planning, design, and implementation (organizing focus group discussions and using community organizers to identify women representatives). Moreover, FSS will ensure partners will provide women and persons with special needs with viable economic opportunities to protect them against trafficking, sexual exploitation, abuse, or other abusive and dangerous sources of income.The FSS will work closely with partners to ensure that persons with disabilities have full access to employment and training opportunities without discrimination and do not reinforce existing social or stereotypical gender divisions.
