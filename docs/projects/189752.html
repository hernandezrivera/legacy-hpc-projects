Project Summary

Under this project, Concern ensures provision of the Ministry of Health (MoH’s) Basic Package of Health Services (BPHS) to communities that are not covered by Health Pooled Fund (HPF) partners in North Bahr al Ghazal (Aweil South, West and North Counties), and Kajo-Keji County in Central Equatoria. Concern will support mobile sites that will provide primary health care for outpatient treatment of common illnesses including malaria, ARTIs and diarrhoea and coordinate with partners to ensure Concern is targeting those locations where there are critical gaps in provision of basic services. Concern will also support the SMoH and CHDs to provide the EPI services in Health facilities not covered under HPF 3 through seconding one EPI Worker in identified health facilities, to reduce the risks of communicable diseases. This project will be integrated with other Concern’s multi-donor funded programme that will make will help in coverage of health services is wide geographic area. Concern’s mobile teams will focus on providing services in hard-to-reach areas. In NBeG, these are generally remote areas that are more than 10km from fixed health facilities, where the mobile team will conduct weekly outreach services to 10 locations (Aweil west: 05, Aweil North:05). A mobile team comprised of Clinical Officers, Nurses, Midwives and EPI worker. In Aweil South County, the project will support 4 static health facilities not under HPF3, Concern will continue to provide monthly incentives to SMoH staff, medical supplies and capacity training to those seconded SMoH staff in Aweil South County. In Kajo-Keji, the approach will be provision of Health services in 5 hard-to-reach locations where limited services are being offered, either by the Ministry of Health or other Health partners, however this team will work within the framework of existing facilities where possible. In Kajo-Keji based on coordination with County Health Department (CHD), Concern will also continue supporting Lire PHCC with paying SMOH staff incentives, provision of medical supplies and staff capacity development.

1 - Needs

South Sudan remains one of the worst humanitarian crises in the world, the number of people in need (PIN) is growing; with 8.9 million people (70% of population) projected to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022. This increase is largely driven by compounded shocks triggered by continued conflict, widespread flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, high food prices, and lack of access to basic services like health facilities where there is only one physician for every 65,574 person in the country. The country’s health system and infrastructure are among the poorest in the world. Health facilities are poorly equipped and staffed. There is a severe shortage of trained health professionals in all categories. Thus, people rely on inadequately trained or low skilled health workers to provide their healthcare. A quarter of internally displaced and returnees reside in settlements further than 5km from a functional health facility. Approximately more than half of the country’s 2,300 health facilities are non-functional and nearly 60 percent of the functioning health facilities are supported by humanitarian and development organizations but many of them are close to displacement sites and returnee settlements and may not be easily accessible by established communities. Despite NBeG being relatively stable, it has the highest child under 5 mortality rate of 157/1,000 compared to a national level of 99.2/1,000 and only 44% of the population live within a 5km radius of a health facility (WHO 2017). This is due to the SMoH and CHDs having limited capacity to provide basic health services and change poor health, nutrition and hygiene practices. Aweil west, North and South Counties has 95 health facilities but 45 are non-functional due to lack of qualified staff, medical equipment, and drug, with often only 1 health worker for each PHCU. There remains a significant need to ensure beneficiaries have access to and attend focused prenatal and antenatal care, and this leaves huge gaps in health service delivery in the 3 counties with 45 health facilities in Aweil West, North and South counties not included under HPF. According to Concern Worldwide SMART Survey, (2019), 23% of children were not vaccinated for measles, 82.3% did not receive PENTA vaccines in Aweil West, 24.6% were not vaccinated for measles, and 54.9% did not receive PENTA vaccines in Aweil North. There are high childhood morbidity rates of 41% in Aweil West and 41.2% in Aweil North (Concern SMART surveys 2019). Skilled health workers respectively attended only 35.3% and 28.4% of mothers in Aweil West and Aweil North during childbirth. Aweil South County was classified under IPC 5 with 10% of population (14,000) at IPC 5. Aweil South County has experienced great flooding in 2021 and there is expected raise in Malnutrition in the area as many farms were destroyed. Similarly, Kajo Keji is greatly affected by the 2016 crisis, and it remains relatively stable and not in flood zone, however the bouts of previous conflict and displacement in 2016 depleted the livelihood, assets and damages to institutions such as vocational training centers, health and WASH facilities. There are currently 18 functioning health facilities out of 51, most of the health facilities were completely damaged, looted or destroyed during the protracted conflict. Of 18 “functioning” facilities, only 7 are operational as static health facilities while partners as mobile sites support the 11 health facilities. These gaps in health service delivery in Kajo Keji is a huge concern as populations are returning to their homes in preparation for farming.

2 - Project activities

The project will implement following activities to contribute to Health cluster objectives; O1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services;In NBeG (Aweil West, and Aweil North) Concern will support 10 mobile clinics in remote locations. In Kejo Keji county, Concern will support 3PHCCs and 2 PHCUs as static health facilities and will continue with mobile clinic in 6 hard to reach areas. These health facilities will provide Basic Health packing as per south Sudan MoH guidelines. In all the static health facilities, Concern will second SMOH staff and pay their monthly incentives as per the South Sudan MoH standardized rate. In all the mobile sites of Concern and supported static Health facilities, Concern will provide surveillance report and response to any suspected outbreak immediately, Concern will train and support EPI Vaccinators in their supported health facilities to conduct outreaches in hard to reach areas so as many children are vaccinated as per immunization schedules, the vaccinators shall use vaccine carriers to collect vaccine from CHD cold chain and Concern will continue to support CHD in immunization campaign at all times. Clinical staff shall be trained on Clinical management of rape and they will be referring cases to the partners dealing with CMR and shall keep confidentiality. CO2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases: Concern will provide health services through 5 static health facilities (Kajo Keji: 01, Aweil South: 04) and 16 mobile clinics (Aweil North: 05, Aweil West: 05 and Kajo Keji: 4) in remote areas not covered by HPF partners. Both and static health facilities and Mobile clinic site will provide health services as per MoH’s BPHS to communities not covered by HPF partners. Concern will provide health services like outpatient treatment of common illnesses including malaria, ARTIs and diarrhoea in coordination with SMoH and CHDs. Reproductive health will be part of BPHS provided to ensure attendance by pregnant mothers in remote communities at ANC, skilled birth attendant deliveries and attendance at PNC. Community health activities will focus on improving behaviors that reduce the risk of common diseases and will be linked with nutrition and WASH interventions. Concern’s health teams will continue to respond to scale-up health responses that will be integrated with its nutrition response. Although Concern does not implement specific services for survivors of SGBV and mental illness, it will continue to respond to scale-up a health response that will be integrated with its nutrition response, Concern has a referral system in place and CRM to identify and refer such cases to other Health and Protection partners. SCO3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms: Concern will work toward health system strengthening including capacity building and collaboration with CHD. Concern will continue with regular community consultative meetings and ensure equitable distribution of limited resources like health facilities and mobile clinic sites. Complaint response mechanism will be in placed in order to provide timely feedback. Frequent and regular joint supportive supervision visits with CHD will be conducted on quarterly basis. On job, training will be provided to the health workers during the joint supportive supervision while action plan will be shared with health facility in charges for follow up. Concern will continue to hold quarterly stakeholders review meeting in order to review the achievements, challenges and plan for the next quarter.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Concern engage with the target community and relevant state authorities during the needs assessment, project design and monitoring to seek their feedback and adapt project assistance/delivery accordingly. The Boma health committees will be formed to ensure consultation with target community and their involvement in program implementation. Caregivers attending mobile sites and health facilities will be asked to provide feedback on quality of the services and Concern will improve the service delivery according to the feedback from target community. Concern will consult with target community, local representative and relevant government authorities in selection of sites for mobile clinics. The Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and regular consultation with beneficiaries during monitoring will provide opportunity to beneficiaries to share their feedback which will be considered in adapting the program accordingly. Concern conduct project end-line survey to review the progress on project outcomes and seek feedback for future improvements. The lesson will be documented and shared with cluster and other relevant stakeholders.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concern understands different vulnerabilities within target communities that may exclude target beneficiaries in their access to feedback and complaint mechanism. Therefore, Concern will consult with the target community, the local chief and state authorities to understand the overall target population profile, access to communication channel and nature of vulnerabilities. While establishing the Feedback and Complaint mechanism, Concern will consider such vulnerabilities and access constrained therefore the target population will be consulted on their preferred mode of complaint/feedback channel. Different mode of feedback such as complaint boxes, phone numbers, email, verbal feedback/complaint during Concern monitoring visits, in-person to Concern office and Help desk at health sites will be used to avoid exclusion of access to feedback mechanism. Concern will use both pictorial, written and verbal channels in local languages to communicate the feedback mechanism to target beneficiaries. The individuals living with disabilities, illiterate, women and girls can also share their complaint/feedback through their local representative and relatives. In addition, during monitoring and support visits FGDs will be conducted separately with women and men to seek feedback and provide opportunity to discuss any concern/issues or complaint.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Concern has a Code of Conduct and its associated policies including Program Participant Protection Policy (P4), Child Safeguarding, and Anti-Trafficking in-person Policy. Concern staff and any one engaged by Concern including consultants, contractors, partners and visitor are required to understand and comply with Concern safeguarding policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct and Associated Policy is consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. During project inception and regular consultations with the beneficiaries and relevant stake holders, Concern informs them about right to assistance, scope of services, right to appropriate behavior including Concern’s commitment to zero tolerance on Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers. The beneficiaries will be informed about the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) to raise their voice in case of any issue pertaining to PSEA. The monitoring mechanism will ensure that the beneficiaries are aware about PSEA and how to complaint in case of any exploitation and abuse. Concern will ensure that health clinic staff is gender balance. Concern regularly coordinate with protection partners and safeguarding/protection issues are referred the Protection partner. In all locations, Concern has a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) where beneficiaries can register complaints about gender related or protection concerns. All recruitments of staff and trainings ensure that there is equal access to women and men to ensure balanced representation. Monitoring will collect data on any information on safety and protection concerns and to improve future programme designs.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Concern’s policies and operational guidelines are in line with IASC’s five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations. Prior to the initiating project activities and delivery of assistance, Concern conduct an inception meeting with the target beneficiaries and stakeholders to inform them about the project scope, selection criteria for mobile sites and entitlements to receive health services. Similarly, during the inception meetings and during regular consultations with the community, Concern will inform the beneficiaries about the feedback mechanism, their right to assistance and complaints. Depending on type of assistance, context and target population profile, Concern ensure that the banners with information on complaint channel and feedback mechanism are displayed broadly in the target areas and at the health clinics delivery points.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

During assessments, Concern will coordinate with Protection partners and identify safety, security and protection risks and findings will be used to adapt the programme. Although NBeG is relatively stable, there is still a high risk of GBV. In Central Equatoria and Unity due to on-going conflict there are considerable protection risks to women and girls including sexual and gender base violence and men and boys including forced recruitment into armed groups. The locations of mobile clinics will be identified in consultation with communities to ensure they are in safe areas. The timing of mobile sites and service provision consider that the beneficiates can reach back to their home during day light. Concern will sensitize the community and make sure that the target population is aware about free health service provision, scope of program and staff is gender balance as for as possible. Concern will incorporate conflict sensitivity to ensure that the programme does not have negative impacts on conflict or social dynamics. Consultation with communities will be conducted during the programme and sensitisations on targeting being solely based on medical needs and entitlements to health assistance will be done. This will reduce misinterpretations of the programme and provision of health services. Referrals will be made of protection cases identified during mobile sites or the CRM. Concern safeguarding policies require all staff and any one engaged in program to comply with code of conduct and reporting of breaches. The beneficiaries will be sensitised on CRM and their right to share feedback and complaint. Concern will ensure that the staff were trained on GBV, aware about referral pathways and sensitise the beneficiaries on reporting and use of GBV pathways.
