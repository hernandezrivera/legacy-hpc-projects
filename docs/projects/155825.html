Project Summary

Rescate is planning to rehabilitate 150 apartments in East Ghouta towns, and 100 apartments in Quneitra villages for returnees. The areas of intervention in Rural Damascus and Quneitra Governorates where the severity level is 5 and 4 (2019 HNO). The project objective is providing lifesaving and life-sustaining shelter support to alleviate suffering and increase access to humanitarian response for vulnerable people and those with specific needs. This intervention will accommodate returnees and people who stayed in their homes despite the events. The intervention will consider privacy for women and girls amongst others. The main objective is in consistency with HRP strategic objectives #3 and #1 at supporting dignified living conditions for the population at their homes. The towns were recommended by the governorate and local community, based on eventual returnees, and people who are willing to return and reside in the rehabilitated apartments.
Contribution to Cluster/Sector Objectives: The project will contribute to the sector objective to strengthening the resilience and cohesion of vulnerable communities and households by improving housing and related community or public infrastructure. This objective addresses the needs of the population through a focus on durable shelter support.
This will be done through an approach that include not only the rehabilitation of shelter but upgrading quality standards by including internal WASH component in each building.
At least 750 women, men and children in East Ghouta (Harasta, Arbin, Zamalka) and 500 individuals in Quneitra (Qahtanniyeh and Breiqa) will benefit from this project. The assessment is based on actual registration and count in the area of intervention, the project will improve their living conditions and will enhance the possibility that they live in safety and dignity at the end of the intervention.
1. Two rooms of suitable size for each family consists of up to 5 members, and additional rooms in case of extended families. Internal one water/ sanitation facility including WC, bathroom, and kitchen, this includes piping and fixtures. Repair of Sufficient lighting and electrical points in the repaired areas of the apartment's rooms. In Rural Damascus, will provide one battery and led lights for the living areas.
2. Safety measures in common areas, such as entrance, staircases, and roofs. The safety measures are mainly to repair necessary broken parts, and to replace / repair lighting. Connection of the building to the main networks: sewage, water supply, and electricity. In addition to ensuring insulation of the roofs by maintenance and covering of chimneys to prevent leakage.
Rescate will implement the project in the selected areas (East Ghouta, and Quneitra) directly through local contractors under the supervision of engineers from Rescate within the Project Management Unit (PMU) and in close coordination with the teams from the local partner for the coordination during implementation and receipt phases in addition to coordination with the shelter sector and local authorities.

Needs

Due to their unstable living conditions, worsened by the interruption of the local economy, livelihoods and access to basic services, they are made increasingly vulnerable due to the depletion of their resources directed to rental or covering living needs. With limited support and no means of their own, these families are in urgent need of basic shelter support. Amongst the most vulnerable returnees facing the most difficult of situations are children, female-headed households (FHH), the elderly, the disabled and pregnant women, whose needs remain urgent. Based on field assessment carried out in East Ghouta by Rescate team, and in Quneitra villages, in cooperation with the Governorates, it was found that the level of damage and partially damaged facilities, accommodation and infrastructure are massive, although water and sanitation, electricity is connected but still need further stability. School enrollment is low, water and sanitation system are deteriorated, public and private shelter situation and protection concerns exacerbated. 1) Noteworthy, East Ghouta towns, Arbin, Harasta, and Zamalka where the intervention will take place, some are assessed as level 5 on the 2019 HNO severity scale. The areas of intervention in East Ghouta are divided to several neighborhoods, each consists of certain number of buildings; Rescate will carry out its intervention in one neighborhood of each village to create coherence and synergy among the buildings of the area. Returnees are increasing to these towns, but they are unable to repair their damaged houses, so they live in miserable conditions with open walls and windows despite harsh weather because they do not have any other space for accommodation. This intervention is requested by the community, the Mukhtars based on the individuals’ applications for assistance at the Municipalities. Rescate has initial approval from the Governorate of Rural Damascus who assured to conclude the screening procedures in due course of time to assist people to live in dignity and privacy. 2) In Quneitra, Level 4 and 5/ 2019 HNO/, the governorate gave permission that Rescate carried out the assessment on that area. There are 100 damaged houses that need rehabilitation for returnees in dire need for assistance in Qahtanniyeh and Breiqa area. The direct assessment provided the following information: the average of men and youth in the area is 40%, the direct beneficiaries from the rehabilitation of the 100 damaged houses for returnees’ families around 750 individuals. It is worth to note that Qahtanniyeh and Breiqa inhabitants are trying to recover their livelihoods by farming in their lands to support their living. The close coordination with the Shelter Sector and the local authorities, in addition to complement our work with the so-considered the first phase of the damaged houses rehabilitation focusing on green buildings, hence; this intervention will enable owners or tenants in the selected neighborhoods to secure decent living space for vulnerable families with dire need, and will extend protection to boys and girls in their houses, therefore, will enhance resilience, and protection for the populations therein.

Protection Risk Analysis

A cross-cutting risks include the risk of heightened inter- and intra-community tension due to discriminatory or non-transparent beneficiary selection. Rescate will take measures to mitigate this risk by ensuring participation of the affected population in programme design and selection of areas of intervention while clearly communicating the beneficiary selection criteria, and by engagement with community leaders and local authorities. Taking into account that resources and response capacities being limited and far below known needs, liability is high that there will be perception of biased targeting of beneficiaries or of biased selection of response activities, thereby creating or exacerbating tension, therefore; Rescate will ensure that a comprehensive needs analysis informs the response formulation, and that this is openly documented and made transparent. Rescate will make sure those in similar situation of need, at least within the geographical areas of response, are reached equally. Rescate will use the existing or establish community mechanisms to identify needs and arrive at a consensus as of who should be assisted. Rescate will make sure that a process of receiving feedback and of responses to it is put in place by establishing a claims mechanism at the project management unit.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Taking previous harsh experience in shelter damaged houses approvals, Rescate has reduced the number of houses to mitigate risk for the donor. During implementation, there are risks that may face the project operation, therefore; Rescate will ensure to revert to consult the Shelter Sector in coordination with the local community and the Municipalities on how to proceed with the proposed response. Rescate will take mitigation measures in case the approvals for project launch is belated by the concerned authorities (governorate and MOLAE), Rescate will ensure timely and complete documentation submission (documentation will be collected during the field assessment from owners and tenants, to be submitted to the concerned authorities with detailed BOQs) for approval on intervention and implementation. At the same time, if the delays exceed three months, Rescate will coordinate with the Sector to request project revision to move to another close area within the same governorate (s). The overall objective of Rescate’s implementation plan is to ensure that the organization has the minimum preparedness measures and response capacities in place to meet the immediate action to mitigate for any delays and bottleneck. Rescate will be prepared to respond to any operational hindrances while ensuring (a) coordination structures and arrangements are in place between local authorities, sector and donor; and (b) a Rapid Needs Assessment mechanism is in place to mitigate on the change to other area of intervention.
