1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The aim of the project is to increase access to education on safe learning environments for children in Cabo Delgado. Specifically, to provide adequate WASH conditions for these children at school, in order to prevent diseases and infections caused by poor sanitation conditions and poor quality of the water for consumption. The target group is 12.435 people, between teachers (1.6%) and students aged 6-17 years old, in target schools. The priority activities include the construction of rainwater collection systems, water treatment, construction of semi-permanent latrines, assembling of handwashing stations, training of schoolteachers, provision of hygiene promotion training and BCC materials, and distribution of dignity (menstrual) kits for girls. In the first phase of the project, a detailed assessment will be conducted.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1. Construction of rainwater collection systems: Rainwater, especially in rural areas, is a viable alternative for reducing dependency on surface water sources. It is a simple alternative, with can be taught and implemented also at household level. In locations like Mueda, in which geographical location makes obtention of groundwater especially challenging, rainwater allow schools to have a stock of water for the dry season; however, due to water demand being increased due to IDP presence, this method needs to be optimized in order for this water to last. Rainwater catchment systems are constituted by 3 elements: catchment system (most commonly a roof), collection system (gutters with a certain inclination and a pipe connecting it to the storage unit) and a storage unit which could be an underground concrete deposit or a plastic tank). Due to the length of a classroom, school facilities made for ideal placements for rainwater systems, if the roof is maintained and the gutters are protected from clogging. 2. Water treatment: as the water from the rain and other sources is not drinkable, certeza (chlorine) will be distributed in the targeted schools for drinkable water treatment, to be consumed by the school staff and students. 3. Construction of semi-permanent latrines: Semi-permanent latrines are sustainable and affordable solutions to mitigate a sudden increase in the demand of sanitation in a school context due to arrival of IDP students. While emergency latrines with unattractive design fail to attract children into using them, and permanent latrines require time and resources that won’t solve the situation in a timely manner. The desing, based on locally available materials, is consistent with the design that students are using among their community, which allows its acceptance, and both materials for the wall structure and the slab are either renewable or revampable, which makes it more sustainable. 4. Assembling of handwashing stations: Washing the hands before eating and after using the toilet, is one of the most effective ways to prevent diseases. Handwashing stations with water and soap (always available) will be settled in strategic locations in the school. In each school a committee will be stablished to refill the water and soap. 5. Hygiene promotion training for teachers: personal hygiene, food and water hygiene and environment hygiene are also important measures to prevent diseases. A 2-day training for teachers will be provided, and they will be requested to cascade the knowledge to students during the classes. 6. Hygiene promotion BCC materials: BCC materials, like albums, posters and flyers with hygiene promotion messages will be produced and distributed in the targeted schools in order to help during the trainings and to remember the messages. 7. Distribution of dignity (menstrual) kits: the dignity kit is composed by 1 pair of underwear, reusable menstrual pads, a towel, a cloth and a soap.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

An initial risk assessment will be conducted in order to identify the potential risk that the programme may encounter or cause. Follow-up assessments and meetings with representative beneficiaries will occur throughout the programme to understand if life and condition has changed in the population and if any kind of harm is being caused. MENTOR coordinators will be trained to monitor these risks and identify potential harms. The findings of the risk assessment will be used to design specific action plans to mitigate the risks identified. All MENTOR staff will be trained on PSEA and they will be committed to respect the Code of Conduct. Specifically, for the different activities the practical points that we will implement are: - Ensuring that handwashing stations rainwater collection systems and latrines are being set up in safe spaces and do not cause harm to population. - Including programme beneficiaries in the planning and implementation phase. - Ensure that in the schools' teachers and auxiliar staff knows how to use certeza in a way that will not harm the beneficiary's health, - to provide safeguarding information materials and posters with the green line number for complains.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

This project considered the empowerment of people (teachers and students) to take care of their health, in distinct age groups and attending gender specific needs (menstrual kits distribution for girls). In all activities MENTOR will consider the gender parity, including for the team selection.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

People living with disabilities may have social and physical barriers to access assistance. In the different activities of these project, the MENTOR staff and teachers will be trained to map individuals with difficult access to assistance and will be encouraged to give attention to this. Supervisors will monitor the activities in order to include mapping of people living with disabilities. People living with disabilities will be prior identified by the teachers and staff and special conditions will be created in order to facilitate their access to the offered services.
