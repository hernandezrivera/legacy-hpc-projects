Project Summary

The project purpose is to provide immediate food assistance to save lives of 24,501 people who are in emergency phase (IPC 4) in Terekeka and 24,501 flood affected population in Maban and revive their livelihoods through cultivation of quick maturing vegetables and training to foster recovery and longer term resilience. The project will employ a social safety net approach to support targeted critical food insecure and flood affected households with unconditional cash transfers to enable them meet their immediate food needs for three months. The unconditional cash transfer will target most vulnerable/food insecure households in Terekeka and flood affected population in Maban whose livelihood was severely destroyed by the floods. These would include Female-headed households, Pregnant and lactating women, Households with children five years of age and below, Elderly and widows, Child headed households, Households with household head with a disability/who is chronically ill. Registered beneficiaries will receive $ 50 dollars per month for 3 months.
To build the resilience of the affected rural population against future shocks, AAHI will complement cash distribution with the following;
Seed and tools support – emergency vital agricultural inputs including tools and quick maturing vegetable seeds will be provided for cultivation to improve household nutrition. One of the major limitations of agriculture production is limited access to quality seeds. Most farmers rely on preserving seeds from their harvest for use in the coming season. However due to shocks mentioned above, there was limited/no production in 2019 and whatever was growing in the gardens or stored in the granaries was washed away by the floods. AAH-I proposes to support the households receiving cash transfers with a variety of quick maturing vegetable seeds for growing.
Provision of extension services – Farmers will be supported with training on modern vegetable growing best practices. AAH-I proposes to identify Community Based Agriculture Extension Volunteers who will be trained as trainers, and with the support of AAH-I Agriculture Officers, will be responsible for cascading trainings to farmers.
Financial literacy trainings - To improve efficiency of utilization of cash transfers, AAH-I will train all cash transfer recipients in financial literacy including such aspects as budgeting and saving. This will create an opportunity to introduce Village Savings and Lending, enabling beneficiaries to pull resources together and invest in longer term livelihood initiatives, thus maximizing the benefits of cash transfers.
AAHI will implement a nutrition sensitive cash based transfer project by incorporating nutrition IYCF messages and good hygiene practices throughout project implementation, during meetings and trainings.
AAHI will establish collaborative arrangements with other actors in Terekeka and Maban to build synergies especially with WFP implemented projects on GFD and BSFP .

1 - Needs

According to August 2019 IPC, 49,000 people in Terekeka were in (IPC 4)-emergency phase of food security classification. As a result of severe food insecurity there was migration of population from Tindilo and Tali in Terekeka county to Door in Awerial county of former Lakes State in search of food. The high levels of food insecurity were driven by the cumulative effects of armed conflict over the previous five years, including minimal cereals production in 2019 caused by long drought that affected the area from 2018 to 2019 and inter tribal conflict in late 2018 between Rokon and Tali communities, which have seen households’ capacity to withstand food insecurity severely eroded and household recovery will likely take several successful agricultural seasons. Due to insecurity and violence which have resulted in loss of livelihood assets and has also displaced people from fertile lands, coupled with added negative impact of extreme weather phenomenon of drought, flooding, incidences of Fall Armyworm, and lack of adequate seeds and tools, thousands of people in Terekeka have been rendered food insecure to the level of emergency. Even during the post harvest period of January to April 2020 incidents of insecurity are likely to persist and this will disrupt livelihoods, and affect trade and access to markets. During this period, Terekeka will be the most food insecure county in former Central Equatoria State. Well as in Maban, in addition to food insecurity faced by 8,000 people in IPC 4 by August, the worst ever unprecedented flooding affected the entire county, affecting the estimated 55,000 host community and 100,000 refugees. Farm lands and peoples’ property were washed away, houses destroyed and many areas were rendered inaccessible. According to the UNHCR flood situation update of 24th Oct 2019, 83% of the households in the refugee and host community were affected by the floods and the immediate needs identified were emergency shelter, food, water and sanitation.In reference to the contextual analysis above, the mentioned shocks have disrupted the livelihoods of the communities in Terekeka and Maban and eroded their coping mechanisms, such that if they are not supported, there will be famine. In view of the above, the project interventions will prevent negative coping and help protect/restore livelihoods assets, build resilience, linking emergency response interventions to longer term development programming for households and communities by addressing household/community shocks. To save lives, the project will provide unconditional cash transfers of US $ 50 per HH for three months to 8,167 households in Terekeka and 4,926 households in Maban. The cash transfer value is calculated based on 50% value of the WFP standard food basket. The 50% reduction in the cash transfer value is to allow and encourage positive coping, which is critical absorptive capacity that contributes to long term resilience building and also to enable the project to have greater coverage of people in need. The WFP basket meets the SPHERE minimum standard of 2100 Kcal per day. The project will integrate complementary nutrition activities such as providing continuous sensitization of Infant Young Children feeding (IYCF) and hygiene. The project will build synergies with GFD and nutrition partners. To ensure that the beneficiaries utilize the cash properly, the project will offer financial literacy training to build the beneficiaries skills in savings and prudent spending.

2 - Activities

AAH-I proposes the following key activities; 1. Project inception activities- these will include entry meetings with county, Payam and Boma officials, community mobilization, food security and market assessment, stakeholder consultations and project administration set up. 2. Community mobilization, sensitization- community and stakeholders in the project area will be sensitized on the project objectives, modalities of implementation, selection criteria and cash transfer value. Project management committees will be established to play a key role in community mobilization and beneficiary selection. 3. Establish complaints and feedback mechanism (CFM) - AAH-I will put in place a rigorous community based Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) in the project areas to enhance reporting and redress of beneficiaries complaints through the help desks and will be reinforced by providing a telephone number where beneficiaries will report any grievances and receive instant feedback. 4. Beneficiary identification/selection – will be conducted by the committees supported by project staff using the agreed upon selection criteria. After the registration, beneficiary lists will be validated in community meetings to address any inclusion or exclusion error. A bio metric registration will then be undertaken that will capture all beneficiary bio data, photos and figure prints. Using a Biometric registration system will ensure that the rightful beneficiaries receive the humanitarian assistance and provide precise accountability to the donor. 5. Procurement of vegetable seeds- quick maturing seeds will be procured. These include cowpeas, green grams, okra, eggplant and onions. 6. Contract a Financial Service Provider- The FSP will deliver and pay cash to the beneficiaries at agreed distribution points. 7. Conduct cash distribution to eligible beneficiaries once a month for 3 months after finger print authentication. 8. Training of project committees- project management committees will be trained on their roles and responsibilities. 9. Conduct ToT training of Community Based Extension Volunteers (CBEV) on agriculture extension approaches, practical skills for vegetable growing and establishment and financial literacy. The CBEV and project staff will cascade the trainings to the beneficiaries. 10. Conduct rollout training - Beneficiary groups will be trained on good practices of vegetable and field crop production, nutrition and hygiene. 11. Conduct monthly post distribution monitoring (PDM) - (3-5 days after distribution, a PDM will be randomly conducted to gather beneficiary views on their degree of satisfaction and ascertain the correct use of the cash, as wells as effectiveness of the cash modality. 12. Project conclusion activities- these will include project exit meetings, audit and final reports.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The community of Tali and Tindilo who migrated to neighbouring counties in search of food and the population of Maban who were displaced by the flooding will be targeted for support as they return to their original homes.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

• The proposed interventions help to protect/restore livelihoods assets, build resilience, linking emergency response interventions to longer term development programming for households and communities in Terekeka through; boosting the local economy and markets through cash transfers, building resilience of communities against another wave of food insecurity through own production and imparting skills on financial literacy to enable the project beneficiaries make sound bussiness decisions. Provision of extension services to the farmers will be done through the support of the county agriculture department that will continue to provide similar services even after the end of the project.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The social safety net approach proposed for implementation aligns to the Government National social protection policy of the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social welfare.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The proposed intervention areas are relatively safe. Accessibility to the project area in Terekeka will be through Juba-Rokon-Lui –Tindilo/tali route which is in a fairly better condition. AAHI has presence in those areas and well known since the successful implementation by AAHI of the World Bank funded emergency food crisis response project in Tali. The project stimulated food production and provided temporary employment to food insecure households through cash for work activities and distributed US $ 99,638 from April 2014-March 2015.In Maban, the entire county is safe and many payams remained inaccessible due to flood waters. But by the beginning of 2020, when the project is started to commence, the flood waters will have dried up. AAHI has very strong presence in Maban. AAHI maintains the UNHCR logistics, including the machinery/vehicles workshop, maintains the roads network, the airstrip and also prepositions WFP food items from the warehouses to the FDPs in Maban. AAHI will leverage its presence and logistical expertise in Maban to successfully support the proposed project.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

• This project is designed in a way that ensures community and local authority participation. The stakeholders will be fully informed in the project lifetime and the design ensures that the outcomes of the project result in sustainable outcomes. The project will also have the local authorities commit to ensuring that established infrastructure is maintained beyond the life of the project. Participation of the government extensionists will ensure that that capacity to provide extension services remains in the project area. In addition, the skills transfer interventions will provide longer term impact in the lives of the beneficiaries beyond the life of the Project. Acquisition of inputs for production, direct injection of cash in the local economy, skills transfer, and establishment of community systems will enhance ownership, create synergy for continuity.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

1. Beneficiaries risk exposure to violence during registration/distribution- Selection criteria will be discussed and agreed upon with all stakeholders and community. community will be involved in validating the selection criteria. Project management committees will be established comprising 50% female and 59% male beneficiaries who will participate in the registration using the selection criteria. Help desks will be established to receive any complaints and provide feedback on project interventions. These measures will ensure fairness in the registration process to avoid any conflict.2. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance- All project staff, management committees, leaders and the project beneficiaries will be sensitized on Zero tolerance to sexual exploitation and abuse and keenly monitored throughout the project implementation. Help desks will play a key role to identify any signs of SEA and quickly implement mitigation measures. A phone number will be displayed in all project areas so that anybody can call and report any case. 3. Interference of external stakeholders in impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries- Stakeholders workshop will be conducted at the onset of the project to sensitize them on the project objectives, their roles, selection criteria and project activities. The county, Payam and Bomas leaders are among the stakeholders who will participate in the stakeholders workshop. This measure will ensure that there is consent on the process and results of the project. 4. Delivery of humanitarian assistance affects peaceful coexistence and spark conflict over access to resource- The Do No Harm principle will be adopted to guard against exacerbating the situation and enhance peaceful coexistence by respecting the rights of beneficiaries, participation of beneficiaries, avoid discrimination and understand community power relations
