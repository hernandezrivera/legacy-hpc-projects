Project Summary

The international system has become more inclusive following the adoption of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development (2015), which affirms that no one should be left behind and that those who are furthest behind should be supported first. The Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction (2015) and the One Humanity Shared Responsibility: Report of the Secretary-General for the World Humanitarian Summit (2016) affirm the same principles, as do many commitments that derive from the World Humanitarian Summit, including the Charter on Inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action. The IASC Guidelines for the inclusion of persons with disabilities in humanitarian action presents a roadmap to disability mainstreaming (IASC Guidelines for the Inclusion of persons with disabilities in Humanitarian Action, 2019).
However, despite commitments to leave no one behind, persons with disabilities remain amongst the most likely to be left out of humanitarian assistance. Simultaneously, the vicious cycle of poverty and disability contributes to an accumulation of insurmountable barriers in access to information and essential services. Combined with insufficient outreach and outdated tools to identify disability, persons with disabilities remain some of the most ‘invisible’ individuals within a population.
In addressing these challenges, the project aims is to ensure that disability is mainstreamed in the humanitarian response of the oPt. This include intervening at coordination level, on the Humanitarian Needs Overview and Humanitarian Response Plan’ process, and at operation level, on the humanitarian responders. In line with objective 2 of the protection cluster, protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence, Humanity & Inclusion (HI) proposes targeted advocacy, training, and coaching to the better identification and inclusion of persons with disabilities in the humanitarian response. Ensuring that disability prevalence is not underreported will ensure better global planning, budgeting, and impact at a programmatic level, ultimately bringing the humanitarian community closer to the goal of “leaving no one behind”. Capitalizing on the cluster’s commitment to ‘piloting new methodologies to facilitate inter-sector vulnerability analysis and response planning’ HI will specifically advocate for the collection of accurate and reliable data on disability through the use of the Washington Group Questions (WGQ) on disability. Adapting the WG standards will also increase the scope of the initiative by going beyond the humanitarian scope. Advocacy efforts will be complimented by training and provision of technical assistance to humanitarian actors on the integration of the WGQs into needs assessments, support on the development of a disability inclusive analysis plan. The above will be achieved through building the capacity of members of six main clusters operating in Gaza and West Bank: protection, education, health, shelter, wash, and food security.
All of the actions will be implemented in coordination with disability working group (DWG), and will guarantee that representatives of persons with disabilities are involved and taking an active role on mainstreaming disability. This will be ensured by the direct involvement of organizations of people with disabilities (OPDs) in the implementation of needs assessment, development of action plans, supporting the implementation of training courses, and disseminating disability inclusive good practices. It will enrich the proposed capacity building and will build new level of awareness and knowledge on how to propose inclusive responses during the implementation of humanitarian responses. To maximize the impact, in Gaza project activities will be channelled through the DWG, while in the West Bank, HI will be leading the capacity building.

1 - Needs

In humanitarian contexts, persons with disabilities are disproportionately affected and exposed to heightened risks, and remain amongst the most likely to be left out of humanitarian assistance, as they encounter multiple environmental, institutional, and attitudinal barriers. Pre-existing discrimination is compounded by barriers related to the humanitarian situation, making persons with disabilities at greater risk of exclusion and marginalization. A growing momentum towards disability inclusion has been observed since the adoption of the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities in 2008, framing a rights-based approach to disability. Awareness and policy developments have led to more concrete changes and commitments on disability inclusion, including the World Humanitarian Summit (2016) with the launch of the Charter on the Inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action, the UN Disability Strategy (2019). It has also led to the development of key operational guidelines such as the IASC Guideline on the Inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action as a roadmap to operationalize these commitments. While there has been some progress towards the inclusion of persons with disabilities, they still remain amongst the invisible groups to humanitarian actors and are left out of humanitarian assistance. Important gaps therefore still remain in ensuring no one is left behind. For this reason, a focus on disability mainstreaming is essential, starting at the coordination level and extending to targeted action by humanitarian agencies. Keeping in mind the four ‘must-do’ actions outlined by the IASC Guidelines, emphasis much be placed on collection of data on disability via active outreach and accurate identification; barrier removal via adaptation of services, infrastructure, and communication; and increased participation via meaningful engagement and empowerment of persons with disabilities.Based on latest evidence from HI’s work on Inclusive Humanitarian Action, on a global consultation to collect best practices on disability inclusion, and on recent learnings of humanitarian planning processes, the following gaps and challenges contribute to a lack of disability inclusion.Turning guidelines and standards into practice: while the sector has come a long way in developing comprehensive standards and guidelines for inclusion of persons with disabilities, humanitarian actors willing to mainstream disability often lack the technical know- how to turn guidance into practice to provide an adapted response. Humanitarian coordination and programming are not inclusive of persons with disabilities. Person with disabilities access to mainstream humanitarian services and assistance is not systematically monitored and interventions for persons with disabilities are often perceived as being limited to specialized services, to be addressed by specialized actors, without consideration to their mainstream needs. Lack of systematic data collection and analysis on disability at coordination and program levels leads to humanitarian actors underestimating the scale of the exclusion faced. This aspect is evident in the difference between the % indicated by PCBS (2-4% estimated number) and the result of the MSNA (14%) where the used of Washington Group Question better identified person with disabilities and person with difficulties. The limited direct participation of persons with disabilities, coupled with a lack of accurate data and systematic monitoring, bring the humanitarian stakeholders do not have the required evidence to allocate sufficient financial and technical resources to ensure meaningful inclusion of persons with disabilities.The proposed action is design on the HI expertise and the numerous requested from different humanitarian actors of capacity building on disability mainstreaming covering the topic of inclusion, accessibility and adaptation, indicating an increasing by all the humanitarian sector.

2 - Activities

The overall objective of the project is to ensure disability mainstreaming in the humanitarian response in the oPt, including at the coordination level - Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) – as well as at operational level. This will be achieved via capacity building of estimated 200 practitioners representing humanitarian organizations in Gaza and West Bank at different levels, including local and international Non governments organization, governmental sector represented by MoHE, MoSD, MoE, and UN agencies (UNRWA, UNICEF). The capacity building will be throughout a specific series of disability related training that entail basic disability inclusion, interaction with persons with disabilities, and disability data by using WGQs; this will be followed by data analysis and disability inclusive indicators. The number of conducted training will be planned upon the number of participants per training session. The logic of intervention relies on three expected results:Expected result 1: The HNO and HRP structure adapts their tracking tools and skills to better identify transformative inclusive actions for persons with disabilities. Activity 1.1 Capacity building to the HNO team on disability mainstreaming and inclusion related issues. 1.2 Capacity building of clusters members on disability mainstreaming. 1.3 Support the development of disability scoring system considering gender with age and disability markers. 1.4 Facilitate the development of disability responsive actions under the HRP.Expected result 2: Humanitarian agencies acquired knowledge and skills to use data collected on disability to identify and remove barriers to services and improve referrals to specialized services. Activity 2.1 Capacity building of clusters members on disability sensitive indicators and on the use of Washington Group Questions (WGQs). 2.2 Provision of technical support through implementing regular exchange meetings, reflect good practices implemented by members of clusters. 2.3 Capacity building y of clusters members on identification of barriers and adaptation of services. Expected result 3: Local organizations of persons with disabilities are able to conduct non-technical accessibility assessments (NTAA) via capacity building, ensuring their ongoing participation in humanitarian efforts. Activity 3.1 Capacity building of DPOs on conducting non-technical accessibility assessments (NTAA)The proposed project will benefit from the current HI’ presence in both area, Gaza and West Bank, and its collaboration with clusters in addition to the established networking and coordination with local and international actors in different sectors including protection, education, and health. All of the actions will be implemented in coordination with disability working group (DWG) to ensure that representatives of persons with disabilities are involved. This will be ensured by involving them as well as the organizations of people with disabilities (OPDs) in the implementation of needs assessment, development of action plans, supporting the implementation of training courses, and disseminating disability inclusive good practices. It will enrich the proposed capacity building and will enhance building new level of awareness and knowledge on how to propose inclusive responses during the implementation of humanitarian responses. In Gaza, the activities will be channeled through the DWG. In the West Bank, HI will be leading the capacity building activities as the DWG is not nationalized yet. However, the latter could be a future step to extend this action and transfer the Gaza's experience to West Bank.

3 - Indicators

HI counts with solid MEAL practices, driven by a Regional MEAL Unit based in Amman. Concerning the indicators of the project, HI will report against the following standard indicator: number of humanitarian partner organisations who received specialized training in disability inclusion. The above indicator is the one identified by the protection cluster for disability mainstreaming training responding to one activity which is "specialized training in disability inclusion". The monitoring exercise will be pre & and post tests. In addition to the above protection cluster standard indicator, HI identified a set of custom indicators to better measure the impact of the intervention. This includes; 1) % of 6 clusters members integrating and strengthening the disability concept within their framework of the actions. 2) # clusters members’ receiving training on disability sensitive data. 3) # of disability scoring system developed. 4) # local organizations of persons with disability receiving training on conducting Non-technical Accessibility Assessments (NTAA). The measurement of the afore-mentioned indicators will be conducted through pre & post tests and activity reports. These M&E activities are designed according to HI Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation Policy which aims to: a) provide a common language for use by all our teams in the implementation of our projects; b) boost the organisation’s continuous improvement approach, with particular regard to the management of performance and project results; c) guide the Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation practices of the projects and ensure that they meet the quality, learning and accountability challenges that sit at the very heart of our institutional procedures, including HI Principles of Intervention.The policy is derived from HI institutional policies of Child Protection (CP), Protection Against Sexual Exploitation Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH), Prevention of and Fight Against Bribery (PFAB), Code of Conduct (CoC), and Safety and Security (SaS) Policies. In addition, the M&E framework complies with IASC guidelines on the inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action through the application of Common Humanitarian Standards (CHS) Commitments 4 and 5, SPHERE Standards, and Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP). This ensures putting the affected population at the centre of all humanitarian intervention and project cycle phases (initial assessment, design, inception, implementation and completion phases).

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The proposed intervention considers gender, age, and disability during the process of design, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation. The HI policies and DGA marker exist to ensure that the proposed intervention contribute to non-discrimination practices. The capacity building activities relies on the experience of HI in the field of implementing training on disability mainstreaming. Considering the nature of the project - no direct beneficiaries - HI recognize the importance to promote and reinforce the do no harm principle. As such, HI commits to sharing information on promotion of do no harm principle through different the capacity building activities. It is expected that the capacity on the matter will be reinforced. However, the full adherence of this principle in the daily practices and operations can solely be guaranteed by partners and their commitment towards this goal.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Accessibility is at the core of the project and will be ensured to all indirect beneficiaries including persons with disabilities considering theirs needs, age and gender, through supporting HNO process collecting and analyse disability sensitive data that will surely support partners to adopt while they are developing their interventions. In addition, the HRP process will be supported to include more inclusive and responsive actions and indicators. Additionally, specific support will be provided to the humanitarian organization on identification of barriers and adaptation of services that will allow equal access to services.
