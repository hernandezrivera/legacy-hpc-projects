Project Summary

TGH will directly contribute to Objectives 1, 2 and 3 for Syria through providing safe and equitable access to qualitative non-formal education (NFE) to OOSC and children at-risk of dropping out within public educational institutions (this project will be part of an Education, Protection, WASH and Shelter integrated programming under 2023 HRP).
The response proposed by TGH is based on its 4-year experience in implementing ECHO, SHF and AICS funded education projects in Syria. TGH is intending to carry on its integrated approach and replicate its successes in sub-districts of Rural Damascus, Hama, Idlib and Dar’a and will scale up in Homs.. These sub-districts were selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) with all the localities being rated over 3 (46% of level 3, 46% of level 4 and 8% of level 5), TGH 2022 field assessments and coordination with municipalities SARC, DoE and other NGOs. In addition, these areas are all witnessing important returns and/or displacements movements thus increasing the level of needs.Through the proposed intervention, TGH will simultaneously address immediate needs and foster coherent services by providing/enabling rapid and sustainable access to formal and non-formal education (NFE) to a total of 7,930 children (3,886 boys, 4044 girls) while reaching 21,335 children (10,454 boys, 10,881 girls) through BTL campaigns and 1,286 parents (630 male, 656 female) through PTAs in Nashabiyeh (3), Dhameer (4), Ghizlaniyyeh (4), Arbin(3) Kafr Batna (3) (Rural Damascus), in Al Rastan (3) (Homs), in Karnaz (4), Eastern Bari (4), As-Salamiyeh (4) (Hama), in Khan Shaykun (4), Ma'arrat An Nu'man (3) and Tamanaah (3)(Idlib) and in Izra’ (4), Sheikh Miskine (4), Da’ra (3) and Kherbet Ghazala(3) (Dar’a). TGH will ensure access to education by rehabilitating schools in locations where facilities are partially destroyed. Boys and girls will be both encouraged to participate in the activities through back-to-learning (BTL) campaigns. A specific focus will be made on supporting the reintegration of out-of-school children, children with disabilities, early married girls and child labour cases into the formal education system. TGH will support the most needed youth in education and livelihood opportunities through TVET.In coordination with Protection sector, TGH will provide MH and PSS to students and teachers, campaign awareness to parents. In coordination with ERL sector and others partners, TGH will explore all the possibilities to support the economic situation of parents through livelihood opportunities in order to sustain the access and retention of children at school. The proposed intervention will include:
-Rehabilitate 14 schools, incl. gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive WASH facilities; and provide prefabricated schools
-Provide the rehabilitated schools with solar power system when feasible
-Repair damaged classroom furniture
-Provide schools with school furniture, including winterization items such as heaters
-Provide OOSC and children at-risk of dropping out with NFE;
-Provide students and teachers school based PSS;
-Conduct BTL campaigns;
-Provide students with school supplies;
-Provide students with hygiene items including sanitary pads for girls; along with health and hygiene campaign awareness;
-Provide students and their families, teachers and school personnel Covid-19 and health infection risk awareness
-Provide students with winters clothes through CVA;
-Provide maintenance and running costs in schools;
-Provide cash for education given the priority to OOSC;
-Provide professional development to teaching staff and education personnel;
-Provide teachers with teaching resources and transportation
-Provide children with sup. learning materials
-Provide transportation incentives for student in school and youth in TVET
-Establish and support parents engagement initiatives

3 - Needs

With almost 50% of the schools not functioning in the country, lack of teachers and the difficult economic situation worsen by COVID-19 measures the number of OOSC and children at risk of dropping out do not decrease. After thorough needs assessment in June and Oct 2022, TGH found that a large percentage of educational facilities were still either destroyed or partially damaged in the targeted areas. As a result, most of the schools are congested and overcrowded resulting in poor quality education. Moreover, most of the families reported unaffordable high costs related to education. Coupled with unaffordability of transportation, this is further dissuading parents from sending their children to school. COVID-19 outbreak and the ongoing unprecedented economic downturn have profound impacts on the welfare and livelihoods encouraging child labour. Other limiting factors, such as early marriage and violence both at school and home, are further limiting children access to education they also suffer from the absence of sex-disaggregated WASH facilities. The situation is even more problematic for children and youth with disabilities who face challenges to access education due to the lack of inclusive facilities, adapted transportation and education pedagogy. Reliance on unskilled and unspecialized teachers with limited financial remuneration due to the lack of teaching staff is impeding learning outcomes of students in public schools.

4 - Implementation of activities

In order to provide immediate access to education to OOSC, damaged schools will be rehabilitated after debris removal in school backyard in the location. School rehabilitation will be conducted as per INEE, Ed. Sector and MoE standards and will take into account gender sensitivity and accessibility for children with reduced mobility. TGH will provide NFE to OOSC and children at risk of dropping during the academic year in the targeted schools combined with PSS in coordination with Protection sector. TVET and numeracy/literacy classes for adolescent and young adults will be delivered in safe spaces provided by the community. TGH will ensure strong monthly monitoring using CMS approach tool. Moreover, teaching will be provided with training on Active Learning, Curriculum B, Inclusive Education, life skills, PSS and will be supported by TGH trainer on monthly basis. Aiming at favouring the return to education of OOSC, TGH will implement BTL campaigns in line with UNICEF’s and MoE’s strategy through community based approach. TGH will provide multipurpose cash for education as well as winter clothes and transportation through CVA aiming at enrolling the most vulnerable OOSC in the targeted areas. The project will be implemented by TGH in coordination with DoE, MoE, SARC and Sector, and the 4Ws will be reported to the sector, as part of theM&E. Participatory approach, through a complaints and feedback mechanism, will be undertaken to identify needs, implement and evaluate programs.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Teachers or education personnel and students participate in physical punishment or bullying of students. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: code of conduct in learning facilities; training and prevention in schools; professional development training on dealing with bullying, training toward teachers and monthly follow up, PSS activities on daily basis. - Aid workers use their power of influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they serve. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: application of Child Safeguarding and PSEA measures, transparency in recruitment process; training of all staff and associates who engage with children on child protection, identification of children victims of violence, first line support and safe referral pathways, safeguarding policies and procedures; signature of code of conduct also posted on walls; establishment of child-friendly complaint mechanism; awareness sessions on children's rights and GBV; sufficient number of adult women working in the learning environment to protect girls attending activities. - Members of the community or local authorities try to over-influence the type of support provided to education services or beneficiaries. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium TGH will ensure to communicate with local education and other relevant authorities and community members about the project’s duration, goal, objectives, activities and beneficiary selection criteria, feedback mechanisms.-

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Inflation rate increases prices. Likelihood: High. Impact: medium. Mitigation: TGH included inflation rate in the budget; in case of continuous inflation TGH will adjust the BoQs accordingly. - Overcrowded schools cannot accept new coming students who benefited from SLPs and have a negative impact on attendance and dropout rates. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigating: in last resort, TGH will seek approval from MOE to implement additional sessions in two shifts on the week end. - Parents/caregivers discouraged from sending their children to NFE in school or attending awareness sessions. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: BTL campaigns through door-to-door will be organized. A dedicated TGH team will visit the families and provide them with the necessary information about the importance of sending their children to school, and supporting them at home. - Unavailability of qualified manpower resources in the targeted locations. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: TGH will introduce campaigns and new methods of recruitment to reach quality staff from the same project locations or close area. - Unavailability of teachers in the targeted locations. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high Mitigation: TGH will ensure the presence of teachers while selecting the location and will coordinate with DoE and the community. For hard-to-reach area, teachers will be supported with cash for transportation.
