Project Summary

The proposed initiative aims to address the needs of approximately 7.76 million individuals in high-risk communities across South Sudan, as indicated by the FSL Cluster's projections for 2025. The focus areas include Fangak, Canal/Pigi, and Ayod counties in Jonglei State, Twic county in Warrap State, and Nasir County in Upper Nile State. The project's multifaceted interventions are designed to avert famine and enhance food consumption, dietary diversity, and adaptive coping strategies among target beneficiaries in IPC phases 3, 4, and 5.
CAO is proposing the FSL response interventions, which seek to address the food security and livelihood needs of 54,500 vulnerable individuals (28,000 women and 26,500 men) in IPC phase 3+ in the areas proposed by this project.
Key objectives include improving emergency food production and limiting reliance on external food and agricultural inputs in line with FSL cluster objectives and the 2025 HRP strategy. The Community Action Organization (CAO) plans to apply a hybrid approach incorporating unconditional cash transfers, supplementary livelihood support, and capacity-building initiatives. These initiatives will encompass modern agronomic practices, Integrated Pest Management, and Natural Resource Management aimed at strengthening resilience at the household level.
Given resource constraints and the necessity for a more significant impact, CAO will also integrate complementary interventions focusing on health, nutrition, WASH, and protection. This will involve strategic collaboration with various stakeholders, including different donor agencies, to ensure coordinated and complementary efforts with other humanitarian actors and alignment with the mandates of relevant ministries.
In addition, CAO will incorporate a Do No Harm approach as a mechanism to ensure that FSL beneficiaries are safeguarded, and establish and train AAP committees selected from the target population, including key stakeholders, to also participate in monitoring and evaluation activities of the project as part of the sustainability approach. In addition, CAO will work with local community based organizations (CBOs), women and youth groups to implement micro grants as key alternative approach to implement and sustain direct community driven livelihoods that contribute to improving the food security situation of the affected population in the target locations for this project in line with FSL approach.
To facilitate these comprehensive efforts, CAO is seeking to secure a budget of $850,000 for the 2025 project cycle, specifically targeting the cluster's prioritized objectives.

1. Needs

Climate change continues to impact different sectors, particularly agriculture and livestock, which is one of the most climate-sensitive economic sectors. Food insecurity is highest in Jonglei, with 62% of the population likely facing severe acute food insecurity, Upper Nile at 59%, and Warrap at 40%, with the highest levels of severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4 or worse) at the peak of the lean season 2025. As South Sudan continues to face multiple shocks and stressors, including disease outbreaks, insecurity, intra-communal violence and conflict, low agricultural productivity, poor macroeconomic conditions compounded by recurrent climatic shocks (persistent flooding and prolonged dry spells leading to low agricultural productivity), and the ongoing conflict in Sudan, the continued influx of returnees and refugees has driven high humanitarian needs, putting an additional burden on host communities who are sharing scarce resources with new arrivals. The Food Security Outlook, October 2024 - May 2025 (FEWS NET) maintains a risk of famine (IPC Phase 5) in low-lying and flood-prone counties in the Sudd wetland area and along major river basins, particularly in areas with a high burden of displaced persons and returnees who are unfamiliar with traditional coping strategies such as wild food gathering and consumption. These areas include parts of north-central Unity, Jonglei, and Upper Nile. IPC reports indicate that between April and July 2025 (Projection 2), in the peak of the lean season, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate in Upper Nile State with an estimated 1.02 million people, 65% of the state’s population, especially in Panyikang, who will likely be in Phase 4, while Tambura will be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, whereas Akobo, Ayod, and Canal/Pigi will likely be in Phase 4. The deterioration in food insecurity will be driven by the depletion of cereal stocks by the majority of households, a rise in staple food prices due to low local supply to markets and inaccessibility to markets during the rainy season, and limited income-earning opportunities, along with persistent macroeconomic challenges in the country. Further recurrent flooding, droughts, and the outbreak of pests and diseases continue to impact the food security and livelihoods of vulnerable people in Jonglei, Upper Nile, Western Equatoria, and Warrap, as the majority of the population relies on climate-sensitive livelihoods (crop farming, pastoralism, fishing) to meet their food and income needs. Recurrent climatic shocks continue to destroy farmlands, reducing annual crop yields. Other sources of livelihood, like fishing, experience loss and damage to fishing equipment, thereby reducing fish trade, which is one of the key income sources. Pastoralist livestock continues to be affected by pests and diseases, significant damage to pasture/grazing land, and decreased milk production. Furthermore, communities and local authorities lack the capacity, knowledge, and skills to respond to climate shocks and new emerging disasters. Thus, there is a need to provide emergency cash, food, and livelihood responses to the vulnerable flood and conflict affected people to improve their food security situation.CAO has implemented food security and livelihood response interventions in Jonglei, Upper Nile, and Warrap states. To respond to the current FSL needs in proposed areas, CAO is seeking to continue working in collaboration with the FSL cluster to scale up resource mobilization to respond to the FSL gaps and emerging needs in South Sudan.

2. Project activities

CAO is committed to addressing humanitarian needs in South Sudan through a comprehensive set of initiatives aligned with the Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) Cluster Objectives for 2025. The first focus area, Emergency Food Assistance, aims to deliver essential food aid to prevent famine while improving dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations. This will involve various strategies, including cash and voucher distributions, conditional food transfers like Food for Assets, and humanitarian livelihood support that integrates efforts across sectors such as Protection, WASH, and Nutrition and Health. To ensure effectiveness, the CAO will also implement post-distribution monitoring. In terms of Emergency Livelihood Support, CAO plans to facilitate emergency food production by providing vital agricultural inputs like seeds, tools, and support for fishing and livestock. Key initiatives will include the distribution of agricultural resources, emergency food assistance, livestock vaccination programs, disease prevention and treatment efforts, and training for Community Animal Health Workers. Furthermore, CAO's Resilience Building Initiatives will focus on reducing reliance on external food sources by enhancing local agricultural capacities. This includes organizing seed fairs, promoting local seed varieties, supporting market functionality, and implementing training sessions on adaptive farming practices. The project will also encourage savings groups, income-generating activities, vocational training, and measures to reduce post-harvest losses. CAO adopts a results-based management approach to effectively manage these efforts, deploying a technical team for direct implementation and monitoring in the field. Active participation from beneficiaries and local authorities will be sought through quarterly joint monitoring, complemented by robust data collection and evaluation systems aligning with FAO and WFP standards. Collaboration with stakeholders such as CAD, RRC, and community members will be essential to capture lessons learned. These will inform the design and implementation of future projects, empowering local communities and strengthening their resilience in the face of challenges.
