Project Summary

Over 2023, the intervention plan will focus on supporting WASH facilities and systems (water supply systems, sanitation systems, wastewater treatment, and solid waste management) in addition to immediate emergency life-saving WASH interventions. Moreover, the intervention will enhance WASH service in schools and healthcare facilities. The intervention will focus on maintaining emergency WASH services in locations targeted by Shafak, in addition, to extend the intervention to the new locations needed in terms of cholera outbreaks or flood responses. as well as Shafak will ensure the upgrade and enhance the water and snaitation systems to promote the access to sustainable access to basic services and improve the quality of the environmental health. The intervention plan will strengthen the community resilience to adapt to living conditions in light of climate changes (drought) and apidamic situation (cholera and COVID-19 outbreak). Shafak, through this intervention, will provide emergency WASH services for 320,000 individuals living in camps and communities. The intervention will comprise the following components:
a) Rehabilitation/upgrade of 10 water pumping stations benefiting 95,000 Individuals, Shafak will maintain supporting the water pumping stations that were targeted in 2022 adding new locations. Shafak will ensure installing chlorination units in the supported pumping stations and provide the regular maintenance for the damaged electrical and mechanical equipment and installing hybrid (solar powered systems) systems to reduce the operation costs and the emission of CO2. In addition, support Water Association Units (WAUs) by providing monthly running costs for the stations (electricity subscription, fuel, chlorine, engine oil, and incentives for workers) and promote a cost recovery system. As well as train the local communities and apply water safety plans.
b) Provision of WASH emergency services for 150,000 individuals living in camps including water trucking and water quality monitoring, water containers, construction/ repair of emergency latrines, providing cleaning materials for latrines and latrine cleaning, desludging of septic tanks, and solid waste management.
c) Rehabilitation of sewage networks to connect the remote sites, camps, public facilities (schools and health centers) in addition to replacing the damaged sewer lines benefiting 115,000 Individuals in addition to construct wastewater treatment plant for irrigation.Provide desludging services where no sewage network.
d) Rehabilitation of water networks to connect the remote sites, camps, and public facilities (schools and health centers) in addition to replacing the broken water lines benefiting 30,000 Individuals.
e) Supporting municipalities to continue the garbage collection and disposal of waste from 10 communities (around 95,000 individuals), schools, health centers, and surrounding IDP sites. This support will cover operation costs. Also, the rehabilitation of 3 dumpsites and construction 2 solid waste treatment plants equipped with incinerators and medical waste treatment.
f) Conducting hygiene promotion sessions about the improvement of hygiene practices and behaviors for 60,000 individuals.
g) 60,000 hygiene kits (approx. 360,000 individuals) will be distributed to the IDPs and host communities who don’t have the capacity to secure hygiene materials.
h) provision of WASH services to 5 education centers and 5 health facilities.
Shafak will ensure support the innovative solutions as possible in its proposed interventions for dealing with water drought issues through supporting solar-powered systems, wastewater treatment. In addition to applying environmental improvement projects through developing solid waste management treatment and elevating the bad impacts of neglecting SWM support.
As for 2024, according to Shafak's strategy and fund availability, it is expected to implement the same activities with the same size and budget.

3 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian and protection emergency characterized by over 10 years of ongoing hostilities, including widespread destruction of civilian infrastructure and the most significant number of internally displaced people in the world. In 2022, 14.6 million people will need humanitarian assistance, making Syria one of the largest humanitarian responses in the world. In NWS there are 1.8 million Syrians who live in crowded self-settled camps, suffer from the lack of adequate water, sanitation, and hygiene items . Water trucking services are the only source of water for 73% oIDP site residents, and only 24% HHs are connected to the sewer network, indicating a continued need for medium-term investment in IDP site infrastructure. According to the same report, 55% of the off-camp households that reported using water trucking as the main water supply modality spend more than 5% of the household’s income on purchasing water only.Regarding the sanitation systems, at least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated, and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional. In Northwest Syria, wastewater from huge camp clusters is openly discharged, forming sewage rivers, and no wastewater treatment exists for the IDP sites, resulting in environmental pollution and increased disease prevalence. Over 30 % of garbage is inappropriately disposed of or collected with low frequency236, while the dumpsites often pose serious health, environmental, and protection risks.

4 - Implementation of activities

The WASH response will reach 320,000 people, 60% of them are IDPs. Shafak will provide comprehensive WASH services by implementing the activities directed by their teams in the field. ●Provision of WASH life-saving services in camps: Shafak will provide potable water through water trucking in alignment with the water quality monitoring process and install water storage. With regards to sanitation, Shafak will rehabilitate/ construct latrines, desludging of septic tanks, support the solid waste management and install garbage bins. ●Rehabilitation of sewage/water networks, Shafak will connect the camps and remote neighborhoods with sewage and water networks in addition to replacing the damaged parts. Construct 2 wastewater treatment plants for irrigation purposes.●Support the water pumping stations by providing/repairing electrical and mechanical equipment, equipping the boreholes, and upgrading the power sources in the station by installing solar-powered systems. ●Solid waste management; Shafak will support 10 municipalities to maintain garbage collection and removal from HC and surrounding camps, in addition, to establishing 2 solid waste treatment plants and reh. 3 landfills to ensure disposal of the garbage with no harm to the environment; ●Hygiene promotion; Shafak will distribute 60,000 HKs and implement hygiene promotion awareness sessions.● provision of full WASH services to 5 education centers and 5 health facilities and support the medical waste managements.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The WASH program will ensure a “Do No Harm” principle, and has carried out a Protection Risk Analysis as part of its sector strategy development. Potential protection risks that may arise from the implementation of the WASH sector strategy and related activities, along with a description of the relevant mitigation measures and resources required for monitoring those risks have been identified. The risks might include inequality in accessing services, work safety, gender-based violence (GBV) and exposure to explosive hazard . Regarding emergency services delivery, the sector will ensure that SPHERE/sector agreed standards are strictly followed and adapted to the context during each part of intervention implementation, especially during emergency sanitation infrastructure implementation phase when the risk of GBV is higher. WASH field teams will take adequate measures to minimize exposure of the most vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls,children, people with disabilities, female-headed households) to greater protection risks,and particularly to GBV. WASH staff will be trained on key GBV concepts and referral pathways to properly support survivors. Women, adolescent girls and people with disabilities will be consulted during the design, implementation and monitoring phase of each project, with a need to put the necessary mechanisms in place to facilitate feed back from users.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Gender remains on the top of Shafak priorities and is highlighted as one of the key objectives outlined in Shafak’s strategy. Shafak adheres to all other international minimum standards, including the SPHERE, IASC, Gender-Based Violence (GBV), Child Protection minimum standards as well as other technical guidelines of the protection cluster and its sub-clusters. Shafak employs a gender policy and procedures to operationalize the application of the Strategic objective for Gender. Key to this is Shafak’s Gender Minimum Standards, which include requirements on 1) Collection of sex and age disaggregated data 2) Inclusion of gender indicators in the monitoring and evaluation plan 3) Inclusion of gender indicators in the employment plan. Consequently, all implemented and planned activities include gender disaggregation of all information collected. This relates to the design of activities addressing women, girls, boys, men, elders, and people with disabilities as well as those activities that entail the participation of community members.
