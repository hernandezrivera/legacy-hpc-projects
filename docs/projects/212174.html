Project Summary

The project aims to enhance access to safe and fair education for crisis-affected children and youth aged 5-17 while improving its quality within protective settings. Implemented in collaboration with the Ministry of Education and related departments, it prioritizes beneficiary protection with a 'do no harm' approach. ADRA Syria will facilitate access to education for 24471 affected children and teachers, fostering safe learning environments in governorates including Rural Damascus, Homs, Aleppo, Lattakia, and Deir Alzour. Through a holistic approach encompassing psycho-social, familial, and educational support, the project aims to elevate formal and non-formal education for both genders, engaging parents in educational activities. ADRA will collaborate with stakeholders to identify vulnerable children and conduct thorough needs assessments to address out-of-school children and those with priority educational requirements.
Need assessment
The ongoing crisis in Syria, exacerbated by the earthquake aftermath, continues to detrimentally affect the education system. There's been a 4% rise in People in Need (PiN) compared to the previous year, indicating an urgent requirement for emergency education services to assist approximately 7.1 million school-aged children and teachers, including those with disabilities. PiN includes 6.9 million children aged 5-17 (46% girls, 54% boys) and 230 education personnel (48% female, 52% male), with 10% being children with disabilities, underscoring the necessity for specialized education services. Over 2.45 million Out of School Children (OOSC) (46% girls, 54% boys) exist, with over a million at risk of dropping out, particularly prevalent in governorates like, Rural Damascus (40%), and Aleppo (38%). OOSC face various gendered protection risks, and prolonged absence from school reduces their likelihood of returning. Pre-existing inadequacies in the education infrastructure persist, with around 3,700 schools needing rehabilitation (HNO 2024)
Additionally, inadequate or non-functional WASH facilities pose risks, particularly for girls and Persons with Disabilities (PwD), often resulting in dropouts and absences, hampering education progress. Secondly, the shortage of qualified teachers further hampers quality learning. Thirdly, economic difficulties strain parents, who may struggle to afford school supplies or transportation, leading children to work to support their families (ADRA assessment 2023)
Activities:
The project activities will include rehabilitation damaged classrooms and schools, ensuring equal access for all students, including those with disabilities, through gender-segregated WASH facilities and improved accessibility features. It involves repairing doors, windows, and painting, installing western-style toilets for mobility-impaired individuals, and introducing solar power systems. Additionally, it provides non-formal education programs such as remedial and summer classes for at-risk children, including those out of school. PSS counsellors will be appointed to assist OOSC in adapting to the educational setting and remedial class students to address any challenges in obtaining their educational potential. A Back to Learning campaign targets dropout students to re-enter either formal schooling or non-formal education programs. School supplies, including bags and stationery, will be distributed to children. Professional development opportunities for teaching staff, focusing on active learning and psychological support, will be available. ADRA Syria, with extensive experience in teacher capacity building, has conducted numerous training sessions benefiting thousands of teachers over the past five years To ensure sustained impact and local ownership of the Education intervention, ADRA will collaborate with parents and teachers to establish Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) where there is community willingness. This initiative is vital for enhancing local capacity and ownership

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Regular monitoring by ADRA Syria's MEAL and Program staff will evaluate project implementation progress, address issues, and recommend improvements to enhance activities.Protection risk analysis: The selection process for teachers and children to participate in activities or training may lead to tensions among those not chosen, posing risks to staff or beneficiaries. ADRA will collaborate closely with the Ministry of Education to select teachers from various schools in high-need areas based on specific criteria (Medium likelihood; Acceptable level: Yes). Distribution of learning materials and school kits could create tensions between host community members and internally displaced persons (IDPs), especially in communities with a large IDP population. ADRA Syria will establish criteria for distribution, considering vulnerability levels and needs of both IDPs and host community members (High likelihood; High impact; Acceptable level: Yes). There is also a risk of beneficiaries being put at risk due to project interventions. To address this, ADRA will engage key stakeholders and beneficiaries, adhering to education sector guidelines to ensure a standardized response and prevent harm to beneficiaries (Poor likelihood; Acceptable level: Yes).PSEA strategy Risk: The field staff or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving, likelihood low, impact high. As a mitigation measures, ADRA Syria will enforce a zero-tolerance policy. Staff and partners will undergo regular training on preventing such misconduct. All personnel must adhere to ADRA's code of conduct and safeguarding policies. ADRA's protection officer will oversee reporting and investigation procedures, working with ADCOM to take necessary actions in response to any misconduct.Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures The changing security situation. Mitigation: ADRA will monitor the security situation in coordination with partners and relevant stakeholders and conduct security assessments. ADRA will work closely with the MoE to choose locations which have been stable for some time and unlikely be directly impacted by renewed conflict. A slight risk that by reopening schools, children will be exposed to protection concerns either on the way to and from schools. ADRA will select schools in safe locations that will not result in increased exposure to danger for the children when attending school. (Likelihood: low). Delays in obtaining the necessary approvals to implement activities. To mitigate this ADRA will maintain strong connections with all levels of stakeholders to follow-up on necessary permissions and approvals and discuss any delays with the MoE (Likelihood: Medium). The lack and difficulty in securing supplies and materials, ADRA's experience in Syria in a similar field has resulted in the identification of potential contractors and staff and thus is unlikely to impact project activities.
