Project Summary

MaCDA is requesting to be part of HRP to respond to the high nutrition needs of the most vulnerable population especially children <5 years and PLWs in hard to reach areas of Kapoeta South and Aweil South counties.
MaCDA will implement the proposed emergency response for twelve months through complementing the existing nutrition services in the static health facilities in Kapoeta south and by establishing 6 mobile OTP/TSFP outreach sites in hard-to-reach areas of Aweil South county while coordinating with existing partners IRC Aweil South to improve the nutrition response coverage though rapid response and referral mechanisms. Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) will be engaged to conduct routine active case finding, rapid assessments, and continue with surveillance of the nutrition situation to ensure that the targeted population has access to timely, appropriate and care on a needs basis. MaCDA’s nutrition team will provide technical support to CNVs to conduct community mobilizations and sensitization, carryout mass screening by use of MUAC approach to ensure timely detection, identification and referral of Severe Acute malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute malnutrition (MAM) cases among children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs). Additionally MaCDA will train 64 Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) on Community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN), provide training on GBV, PSEA and child protection for all nutrition staffs. MaCDA will recruit and train 36 nutrition assistants to ensure provision of quality nutrition services are provided to all children 6-59 months and PLWs who are acutely malnourished. To improve the quality of services and ensure sustainability of the program, MaCDA will provide refresher trainings for 64CNVs and 36 Nutrition assistants on CMAM and Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN). These will be recruited locally in those Payams where static and mobile sites have been established.
MaCDA will establish a total of 6 OTP/TSFP sites in Aweil South while continuing with the 10 OTP/TSFP sites in Kapoeta South respectively. This will be in response to low service coverage of nutrition services as a result of inaccessibility for people living in hard to reach areas of Aweil south. Further, this will enhance focused rapid response mechanisms through provision of lifesaving nutritional services. MaCDA’s mobile teams will provide gender-sensitive MIYCN counselling and nutrition education sessions for mothers and fathers, with key focus on breastfeeding; nutritious complementary foods for infants and young children; care and feeding of malnourished children; referral to health services for care and growth monitoring; and information on safe storage and treatment of water, proper hand washing, and sanitation.
MaCDA will form 60 Mother Support Groups (MSG) for promotion of optimal MIYCN practices. MaCDA’s nutrition program will focus on disseminating nutrition preventive messages to the targeted community in Kapoeta South, and Aweil South. MaCDA will print and distribute to all nutrition staffs guidelines, reference tables, and look up tables with relevant key nutrition messages and cut offs, while providing regular supportive supervision to the nutrition staffs to ensure that the nutrition services are provided in accordance with national and international standards. MaCDA will rely on UNICEF, WFP and partners like IRC to provide nutrition supplies for treatment of SAM and MAM beneficiaries respectively, whilst ensuring proper coordination.
All children with MUAC <11.5 cm or WFH z-score <-3 and/or edema will be classified as having SAM. The identified SAM cases without medical complication will be admitted to our static and mobile OTPs and provided with appropriate therapeutic care as per the national CMAM guidelines.

1 - Needs

South Sudanese women and children continue to suffer the greatest proportion of effects with high levels of acute food insecurity (SS IPC, September 2019). The IPC (2019) projections estimates 4.54 million people (39% of the population) likely to face crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food from September to December 2019 and a further likely deterioration from January to April 2020 affecting about 5.5 million people (47% of the total population) who are likely to face crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. The nutrition situation in South Sudan remains critical with overall GAM rate at 11.6% and SAM rate at 2.3% with contributing factors of poor harvest, dry spells and past conflict (FSNMS round 23 August, 2019). According to the IPC, September, 2019 report for Acute Malnutrition (IPC AMN), Renk County in Upper Nile state was classified as IPC AMN Phase 5 with the highest GAM rate of 32.1%. Further, the national stunting level indicate that 17.9 % of children under age five are stunted, and 5.2% of children are severely stunted. In Kapoeta south, and Aweil South, a GAM rate of 15.5% and SAM 1.3% (IPC, August 2018), 16.3% and SAM 3.0% (IRC, 2019) and 21.0% SAM 4.9% (ACF, June 2019). According to the (SS IPC, August 2019), the three counties are classified as IPC AMN phase 4, respectively. According to FSNMS round 23 (August, 2019) indicates a severe food and nutrition insecurity status among counties of Kapoeta South, and Aweil South due to continuous poor harvests, long and heavy rain season that spoiled the crops. Findings of FSNMS report further indicated that children 6-59months and PLWs are further at recurring risks of acute malnutrition. The poor nutrition situation has been aggravated by poor infant and young child feeding. In partnership with WFP and UNICEF, MaCDA is implementing nutrition interventions in Kapoeta South County within ten static OTP/TSFP sites. Therefore, MaCDA is well vast with experience in implementation of nutrition services and has operational presence in all the 2 counties where it is proposing to implement this project. In Aweil South, MaCDA is proposing to establish 8 mobile OTP/TSFO sites in two counties to complement nutrition services offered by IRC in Aweil south and ACF in Aweil east counties within static health facilities so as to reach the most vulnerable children in hard to reach locations and reducing vulnerability of women and girls to GBV and PSEA whenever they travel long distances to access nutrition and health services at the health facilities. Secondly the proposed interventions will complement UNICEF and WFP interventions to adequately provide support to the program in Kapoeta South County. MaCDA will collaborate and coordinate with these partners who are operating in static health facilities. With this, MaCDA is applying for nutrition fund in order to provide integrated nutrition activities, establish and start 6 outreach/mobile nutrition specific and sensitive interventions in Aweil South county.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

MaCDA maintains a Stakeholder Participation Guidance, a policy manual/guidebook detailing processes and procedures for fostering greater transparency, trust and effectiveness in empowering vulnerable, poor, rural and marginalized communities, particularly groups that are likely to be ignored, misrepresented or underrepresented such as girls and women, children, and youth. MaCDA will remain accountable to all affected people in its area of operation through; Creation of linkages with Community Nutrition Volunteers and mother support groups to be involved in planning to set priorities, implementation, monitoring project activities at site level and evaluations to provide feedback. In order to strengthen feedback mechanisms from communities we serve, MaCDA will fit suggestion boxes at all its field offices to maximize on inputs from communities. Regular sampling will be done and information shared up to the information management officer who discusses it with Juba Management team to advance decision making/ action. Additionally, MaCDA has incorporated Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (CAAP) into all relevant statements, policies and operational guidelines including incorporating them in staff inductions. To effect this, MaCDA will establish project committees in all its project locations, who will be charged with holding quarterly stakeholders meetings to include women, adolescent girls and men at community to discuss issues related to the program about their perceptions and meeting their prioritized needs.Safety audits will form part of the monitoring exercise, assessing do no harm standards, as well as reviewing complaints and feedback on safety from beneficiaries. Dignity will be assessed by analyzing compliance of infrastructure to dignity standards, feedback and complaints reviews, as well as comparison of reports from protection actors in the area of operation.
