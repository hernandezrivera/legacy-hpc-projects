Project Summary

UMCOR requests USD 604,190.21 to implement a WASH & education projects in Bantui & Dar Al Salam refugee open areas in Jabal Awliya Locality in Khartoum to benefit 25,000 out of camp refugees integrated with the host communities. This project aims to provide sustainable WASH & education services in the targeted areas. Under this funding appeal, UMCOR shall do the following:
WASH
1. Upgrade 2 generator-powered pumping system to solar-powered hybrid pumping system
2. Rehabilitation of 5 hand pumps
3. Provision of hand pumps spare parts and tool kits
4. Conduct training to 15 WASH committee members on O&M, good governance, community contribution and financial management
5. Train 25 Community hygiene promoters
6. Support SMOH Conduct water quality assessment and monitoring
7. Excavation of garbage disposal pits(4x3x2m) at strategic points for solid wastes disposal
8. Organize monthly hygiene campaign (Jerican cleaning, hand washing and environmental cleanup campaigns)
9. Support the formation and running of School Hygiene clubs at 2 schools
10- Construction of 300 HH latrines
Education
1-Construction of 5 Permanent classrooms (9mx5m)
2-Construction of 1 permanent teachers office
3- construction of 2 sensitive latrines, 1 for boys and 1 for girls (each with 5 drop holes) equipped with hand washing facilities in 3 primary schools
4-Training of 10 teachers on EiE,Psycho-social support and Teaching methodologies
5-Distribution of teaching and learning materials to the 1050 pupils
6-Initiate and train PTA members(8 female ,7 male ) on EiE ,school management and psycho-social support in 3 schools
7-Provision of Uniform
8-Provision of school fee through cash transfer

Needs

In December 2017, a comprehensive inter-agency needs assessment was conducted to identifypriority assistance needs of South Sudanese refugees living in 9 ‘open area’ settlements, including: Naivasha, John Madit and Angola (Umbadah locality); Banitu, Dar El SalamBlock 7 and Dar El Salam Block 8 (Jebel Awlia locality); and Haj Yousef Block 3, Haj Yousef Block 4 and Al Takamol (Shar El Nile locality). Participants included the Commission for Refugees (COR),Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC), Immigration and Passport Police, Commission for Voluntary and Humanitarian Works (CVHW), NISS, Military Intelligence, Ministry of Health, UNHCR, WFP, UNICEF,UNFPA and WHO. Assessment findings indicate an urgent need for basic services across all sectors, including health, nutrition, education and WASH. All households are facing poor shelter conditions and a lack of basic non-food items. While no comprehensive nutrition assessment has been conducted for the settlements targeted under the plan, assessment findings indicate high risk of malnutrition across communities, including broad household food insecurity, low use of breastfeeding among South Sudanese refugee women, gaps in host community nutrition programmes and low vaccination coverage. Widespread open defecation, limited access to safe water supply and unhygienic conditions, particularly during the rainy season, as well as a lack of access to menstrual hygiene materials are compounding health and nutrition vulnerabilities across all nine sites.
