Project Summary

The project aims to reduce food insecurity through capacity building, skills acquisition and livelihood support of 20,060,200 beneficiaries across Borno and Yobe State. The project will focus on empowering vulnerable girls, boys, women and men with various livelihoods options by building their skills on vocational activities, enterprise formation and empowerment, CFW activities, market and financial linkages and will integrate VSLAs across all interventions to promote financial inclusion and ensure that beneficiaries build a sustainable means of livelihoods.

1 - Needs

High levels of conflict across the Northeast is driving limited engagement in agricultural activities, declines in humanitarian access, and displacement, with many populations being displaced multiple times. This, coupled with the significantly above-average staple food prices, constrains household purchasing power and food access. As a result, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes are widespread across much of the Northeast. The harvest is expected to somewhat improve outcomes in areas of the region for a short period of time; however, Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes are expected to persist throughout the scenario period in hard-to-reach areas. A risk of Famine (IPC Phase 5) persists in hard-to-reach areas of the Northeast. While it is not FEWS NET's most likely scenario, Famine could occur in a worst-case scenario if there is a dramatic uptick or shift in conflict that limits access to typical food and income sources and humanitarian assistance for a prolonged period. As humanitarian access has declined in recent months with increased conflict and continued displacement, many households in hard-to-reach areas are experiencing large food consumption gaps indicative of high levels of acute malnutrition and excess mortality. As these populations face severe difficulty meeting their food needs and are only expected to engage in the ongoing agricultural season at minimal levels, the Risk of Famine persists through at least January 2022

2 - Activities

Cash for Work – 440 HHs (Yobe) Targeting at least 70% Women to work for 15 days a month for a minimum of 4 hours/day on identified community activity. Train and support IGA: CARE will identify 20,060 HHs (Borno – 9990, Yobe – 10,070) and will support them with IGA materials. VSLA Group: From the existing participants identified, CARE will leverage on its expertise on VSLAs to integrate savings groups into all the components to ensure that participants are able to save resources and have access to loans when required. 2121 HHs Borno 1196 and Yobe 925 will be targeted and trained on savings and loans schemes. A total of 84 groups will be formed. CARE will identify new skills trainers and leverage on existing ones (trained and mentored in other projects) to provide vocational skills trainings to participants the trainings that will be conducted across board for all participants include Business Management Training and entrepreneurship skills

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CARE will ensure that all beneficiaries who include avoidance of further harm, ensuring impartial access to assistance, avoidance of causing psychological or physical harm and assist in the claiming of rights of beneficiaries. These principles will be mainstreamed into all project activities. All project activities will be carried out with a protective lens and from a protection perspective following the principles of protection mainstreaming. Specifically, CARE will take the following approaches to ensure protection mainstreaming throughout the process, conducting protection risk assessments, ensure safety, dignity and Do No Harm Principle is upheld, capacity building of stakeholders on protection mainstreaming principles, advocacy on gender & protection considerations and adapting interventions to COVID-19. Finally, CARE will continue to maintain awareness of levels of transmission in all our operating contexts and act in accordance with public health measures called for in global recommendations and/or national guidance. In all our programming, CARE will follow and continue to prioritize a Do No Harm approach

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

A functional and contextually appropriate feedback and Accountability mechanism will be put in place in such a way that the beneficiaries can confidentially and safely relay their complaints, dissatisfaction and suggestions of project implementation. Due to the context of where CARE works, suggestion boxes will not be efficient hence the use of complain help desk and a toll-free line by which the beneficiaries may relay their complaints comfortably and confidentiality will be prioritized. This feedback mechanism will ensure to provide the beneficiaries timely and appropriate responses while maintaining the principle of confidentiality
