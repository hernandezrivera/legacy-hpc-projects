Project Summary

During displacement, families separate, children boys and girls are greatly affected psychologically as they flee for safety, their daily routine are greatly disrupted, environment changed and missing out on exposing them to many child protection issues such as child abuse, violence, neglect child labour and violence due to their low ability to protect themselves. This project aims at providing life-saving child protection services to conflict affected children boys and girls in Koch and Maiwut Counties. CRI Child protection staff will work closely with the community networks and identify and document separated unaccompanied and missing. The identify children will receive FTR packages, where most vulnerable unaccompanied minors , child protection team together with community based child protection Network,CBCPN will identify suitable foster care families, while coordinating with other actors to initiate adult tracing to facilitate reunification. The UASC will be monitored (Temporary care Management), and their concerns addressed basing on case to case needs. The ongoing conflict in Koch has a devastating effect on children’s lives. Frequent displacement in Koch and Maiwut counties have led to family separation on mass scale, leading to acute child protection issues. For those children without family care, cases of child headed households are on the rise as is exposure to exploitation, violence, sexual assault, child labor and prostitution. In addition, as the war continues, forcibly recruitment of children has been occurring on a massive scale across territory in Unity and Upper Nile. Recent attacks in Mimir have also highlighted more serious abuses against children such as killing, rape and abductions, which necessitates urgent access to the population and responsive programming. Prevention and response case management, which aims to divert children away from harmful activities and recruitment, is necessary, as is psycho-social Support for many who have experienced significant trauma due to the war. Across all sites the lack of education facilities, access to services, safe play areas and social infrastructure is an issue, which has compounded children’s vulnerability to different forms of violence and exploitation. With the current humanitarian catastrophe in Unity and Upper Nile and declaration of famine, and the displacement of communities is a major concern that cause trauma to whole community. In this condition children are not left out, they too are distressed and need immediate attention. CRI child protection will provide psycho-social support to conflict distressed children through structured gender dis-aggregated games and activities at child Friendly Spaces both in static and mobile team.
Lifesaving message to prevent separation, mine risks, will be integrated in child protection activities to minimize cases of mine injuries and UASC through community gathering, churches and organized awareness campaign.
This project aim at scaling up the delivery of child protection services in Koch, Leer and Maiwut through establishing child help desk in distribution sites, providing psycho-social support to reach out children at the community and distribution sites. The CP team will identify children with specific need and referring vulnerable children including cases of children in need of family tracing and reunification. The project intend to reach 8,500 direct beneficiaries, men, women boys and girls in Koch, and Maiwut Counties in Unity and Upper Nile

Needs

Koch, and Maiwut Counties have been flagged by the Child Protection Sub-cluster in South Sudan as a priority locations after fighting in the area took place in April of this year. According to the South Sudan Key IPC Findings published on September 28, 2018 Koch, Maiwut Counties is in IPC 4 (Emergency Phase), and can be expected to worsen IPC 5 (Catastrophe Phase) if no humanitarian assistance is not provided. Due to consistent insecurity in the area in the past months, the latest assessment is from May 2018, and highlights 17 children separated from their parents, family, or relatives, and 21 children reported as unaccompanied and vulnerable. Thousands of civilians have been trapped in various states of insecurity for months with little to no humanitarian access. As parts of Unity and Upper Nile states opens up, the need for humanitarian intervention and services is dire. There are no child protection services in the targeted Counties and the existing structures address are unable to support the child protection issues. Conflicts and natural disasters put children and adolescent at risk, when academic activities are discontinued children engage in search for food and water which increases the exposure to protection risks such as sexual violence child labor, recruitment in armed groups and early marriages. This project aims at providing CP concerns and lifesaving child protection services to conflict affected children boys and girls in Padeah, Gwaar and Dhorwang in Koch, and Urieng, Jekou and Turu in Maiwut. CRI child protection staff will work closely with the community based network and identify and document separated, unaccompanied and missing. The identified children will receive a full FTR packages, where most vulnerable unaccompanied minors, child protection team together with community based child protection network will identify suitable foster care families, while coordinating with other actors to initiate adult tracing to facilitate the reunification. The UASC will be monitored (temporary care management), and their concerns addressed basing on case to case needs. CRI Child protection team will provide Psycho social Support to conflict distressed children through structured gender dis-aggregated games and activities at 6 Child Friendly Spaces.

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The organization ’s senior CP Officer in the field will monitor daily CP activities being carried out at the project site then provide the CP Manager with weekly and monthly reports to ensure the project is in line, and consistent, with the stipulated work plan. The M&E Officer will maintain contact field staff to monitor the progress of indicators against the target to ensure compliance with projections. The CP Manager will provide the necessary technical and capacity support and coordination to ensure timely and quality implementation of activities, as well as send the monthly 5W report to CPSC in Juba. The Executive Director and Monitoring and Evaluation officer will also visit the area in turns to provide technical assistance and also support in developing tools for monitoring purposes such as feedback, interview questions, focus group discussion prompts, observation-check lists, and so on. The project’s logical framework will be a critical tool for monitoring and evaluation. CRI will provide interim and final reports (narrative and financial) based on the indicators to ensure reporting relevance. Daily supervision of the project staff member will be done by CP Officer and FTR Supervisor in all the project targeted location. CRI has an internal monitoring checklist that the M&E Officer will use to track the achievement of the project. The M&E Officer will be supported by Database Officer, who will work to support field team to establish a detailed monitoring plan to guide in collecting appropriate and timely data. Monitoring tools (indicator tracking templates include: daily CFS attendance sheet, weekly new caseload offline sheet, internal indicator performance tracking table, internal monthly reporting template and FTR forms) will be used by all staff to accurately compile project data. Measuring success: At the end of project implementation, a survey will be completed to evaluate assess and measure success of this project. Key individuals within the community, key amongst them primary and secondary beneficiaries will confer and come to consensus around the various inventory rating. The survey will be conducted among direct and indirect beneficiaries and will include sections representing child protection actors, community leaders and lawmakers in the communities.
