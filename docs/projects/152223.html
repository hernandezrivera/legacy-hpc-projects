Project Summary

SC will rehabilitate and upgrade Child-Friendly Spaces in existing locations. SC will include protection mainstreaming elements to identify and mitigate risks to children in all capacity building and community mobilization activities.
Trained Case Workers will provide step by step support to at-risk children and their families to ensure appropriate services are delivered to targeted groups including, Separated and Unaccompanied minors, Children Associated with Fighting Forces/Armed Groups (CAAFAGs) and survivors of sexual Gender-based violence (SGBV). We will use Case management intervention as an entry point for identification and referral to other services, including health, nutrition, FSL, WASH, and educational or vocational training, as well as referral for other child protection care and services. Non- specialized staff will receive basic communication skills and Psychosocial First Aid training that provides staff with the definition of counseling and psychosocial support. For specialized mental health issues, children will be referred to a NEEM Foundation and IOM using the interagency referral pathway.
This project will provide immediate and life-saving humanitarian assistance to address the alarming rates of malnutrition and protection risks experienced by the most vulnerable population-malnourished boys and girls, pregnant and lactating women and girls (PLW/G) survivors of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), children formerly Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups (CAFFAG), and Female and male adolescents heads of households (AHH) affected by the conflict in Borno State, Northeast, Nigeria.
Additionally, Unaccompanied and separated children will be placed in alternative care arrangements while family tracing and reunification takes placed through referrals to appropriate agencies. These Caregivers of separated and unaccompanied children will be trained in positive parenting, child protection, and child safeguarding.
Girls and boys (50% girls) coping mechanism and resilience are strengthened and severely affected children receive appropriate Psychosocial support through one to one support from their caseworker and through group activities in the child and youth friendly spaces.

Needs

Across the six affected states of Borno, Adamawa, Yobe, Bauchi, Gombe and Taraba, an estimated 10.2 million people are in need of protection assistance of whom 52 percent are women and girls, and 48 percent are men and boys. Children constitute 63 percent of those needing assistance. The most acute humanitarian needs are clearly concentrated in Borno State where the crisis shows no sign of abating. In the absence of an integrated and well-coordinated response, the impact of the armed conflict may lead to immediate and long-term devastating consequences on the life and wellbeing of children, their parents, and the entire community. The Child Protection working group estimates that there are still at least 20,000 UASC in Borno, Yobe, and Adamawa (HRP 2017). The protection needs of children in Nigeria are well beyond the available capacity of the government to support all children who desperately need assistance. It is imperative to rapidly scale up child protection interventions that enhance the wellbeing of children, adolescents, and caregivers recognizing the different ways in which each group has been affected by the conflict. In addition, more needs to be done to improve children’s psychosocial wellbeing of vulnerable children including survivors of Sexual Gender-based violence and ensure their safety and protection. A 2018 assessment conducted by SC revealed the need for increased Psychological Support services for children affected by armed conflict including Children Associated with Fighting Forces or Armed groups.Sc seeks to advance gender equality in all its humanitarian response, guided by SCI gender equality policy which requires that all programs are gender-responsive at minimum and gender transformative wherever possible. In October 2017, a rapid gender assessment was conducted in Borno through focus group discussions (FGDs) (including women, men, girls and boys in separate groups) and key informant interviews (KIIs) (including with officials from the MWASD, The Ministry of Education and Health, UNFPA, community leaders and NGOs to inform the design of this project. The assessment provided information about the different situations, needs, capacities, and coping strategies that women, men, girls, and boys are adopting within the Northeast, Nigeria as the result of the crisis. A more in-depth gender analysis will be conducted during the first quarter to ensure gender different needs have been meaningfully and accurately identified and incorporated.Current estimates indicate that 28% of IDPs, 34% of people living in host communities, and 32% of returnees do not have sufficient access to safe water for drinking, cooking, as well as washing and bathing.
