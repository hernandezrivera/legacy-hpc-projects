Project Summary

The project focuses on increasing utilization of gender based violence(GBV) prevention and response services for children,women,men,boys and girls as well as people living with disabilities in Yambio and Nzara counties improving their protection. The intervention will provide immediate lifesaving assistance to address priority GBV needs, strengthen prevention, mitigation and response to GBV issues as well as strengthening quality of care.
To ensure GBV survivors receive survivor centered life-saving assistance in accordance to the GBV guiding principles, comprehensive GBV case management, psycho-social support and safe referrals will be supported targeting 500 survivors(350 women,100 girls,40 men and 10 boys) and 50 beneficiaries that include 20 women,20 girls,5 men and 5 boys who will receive targeted cash based protection assistance.2 women and girls friendly spaces with 300 women,300 girls,50men and 50boys will be supported to offer psycho-social support and case management services to GBV survivors enhancing their coping mechanisms. To enhance basic life skills for survivors and other community members, 300 women,100 girls,50 men and 50 boys will benefit from basic livelihood skills e.g. knitting, weaving and bakery at the center that will also offer small gardening activities empowering them to provide basic needs for their families. To ensure competent and qualified staff offer services to beneficiaries, 2 trainings will be provided to survivors targeting 30 participants(13 men and 17 women) in case management and 34 community volunteers,24 women and 10 men will be identified and trained at the community level to strengthen protection risk mitigation, early warning among others. Protection accompaniments will be provided for 20 vulnerable GBV survivors to enable them safely access life-saving services. In addition to training 300 women and 200 girls on how to make reusable sanitary pad supplies to cater for their menstruation, a further 700 women and 300 adolescent girls will be provided with dignity kits to mitigate prevailing GBV risks as well as hygiene needs.8000 community members(4000 women,1250 girls,1500 men and 1250 boys) will be reached with key GBV awareness messages to boost prevention, offer more support to survivors and empower targeted beneficiaries with the right knowledge and information. Another 4424 community members(2000 women,1415 girls,1260 men and 849 boys) will be reached through training of 7 groups on communities care program that focuses on dealing with negative social norms to enhance prevention of sexual violence against women and girls. Protection monitoring will help in identifying GBV risk factors working with affected communities and design appropriate response mechanisms, increase advocacy and inform humanitarian interventions.
Since the targeted communities have low regard for girls and women,300 adolescent girls and young women often victims of significant amount of negative cultural norms will be empowered to stand up and support each other face within WGFS.

1 - Needs

Since the beginning of the crisis in Western Equatoria,South Sudan,CMMB through its program office in Yambio has been directly providing psycho-social support and emergency GBV case management services in partnership with Unicef and local authority.CMMB provides specialized support to survivors in safe spaces in Asanza and Nzara women friendly space(WGFS) and medical support in established CMMB supported health system.Survivors are referred to health,legal,safe shelter options and other services.In collaboration with partners,CMMB implements prevention activities such as women empowerment through WGFS and community awareness raising.Because of insecurity,GBV incidents are reported to be on the increase including rape,robbery targeting female headed households,harassment of women and girls,abduction and physical assault.Many families are not able to work on farms and economic opportunities remain limited.In this context, the risk of harmful coping mechanisms such as survival sex and child marriage increases.Frustrations of youth and men as a result of lack of economic opportunities remains high as most married men are unable to cover basic needs of their families contributing to increased risks of domestic violence.Displacements have further fragmented protection related community support mechanisms for women,girls,boys,children,elderly and people living with disabilities.Interagency assessments conducted,affirmed the situation described above e.g. Gbudue State IRNA(5th to 14 March 2019) identified that women and girls were most affected by the conflict supporting their families in the absence of the men coupled with increased GBV particularly in Nzara.Nadiangere joint assessment(11th-14th April 2019) noted rampant rape of women and girls,forced recruitment of underage children and youth by armed groups,displacement and abduction of elderly persons as key protection concerns. Discussions with women and girls under CAAFAG and those who escape from armed groups indicate that they were forcefully married and used as sex slaves and threatened to be killed.They remain traumatized following long stay in captivity when remembering indiscriminate incidents which calls for urgent psychosocial support.Non Violent Peace Force mission to Ri-rangu and Sue cantonment (June 25th-26th 2019) noted that GBV was common but hardly reported with early marriages/early pregnancies especially those from female headed households easily accepted by the community.During a Protection assessment in Yambio and Nzara (29 July-1st August 2019) women reported experiencing domestic violence and lack of representation in the community decision making structures. For instance,in order to access PHCU services in Diatoro,a GBV survivor should provide a letter from executive chief indicating whether medical care should be provided as well as police services.In Rimenze abduction of adolescent girls’ on their way to the garden by unknown armed men was reported. With a limited no of GBV actors,survivors especially women and adolescent girls lack appropriate access to women friendly support services key in delivering psychosocial support and other case management services.Limited livelihood opportunities pre-disposes women to domestic violence with negative cultural norms e.g. early/forced marriages denying girls access to education and exposing them to health risks.Use of form 8 as a pre-requisite to access life-saving services continues to be an obstacle to women seeking medical support in certain health facilities.CMMB has identified GBV as lifesaving and requires urgent attention and prioritization beyond current response.Proposed action has the potential to minimize risks of and vulnerability to GBV/SEA and provision of quality care for GBV survivors.There are challenges/major gaps in service delivery eg in Lirangu,the most affected Payam by conflict has no GBV services.CMMB prioritizes current response scale upand strive to meet key gaps in GBV service provision

2 - Activities

Through establishment of 2 WGFS targeting 600 beneficiaries(300 girls,300 women);CMMB will offer individualized/group case management services, psychosocial support, basic livelihood and recreational services tailored to beneficiary needs. Since men and boys have previously shown interest in taking part in some activities a provision of 100 participants ie 50boys and 50 men will be accommodated within the WGFS. Basic livelihood skills e.g. bread baking, embroidery, literacy and basic gardening will be given priority at the WGFS to enable participants earn a livelihood and support their families. These activities will target 300 women,100 girls,50 men and 50 boys from the total WGFS targets. Recreational activities engaging adolescent girls, boys, women and other interested youths to connect with each other offering peer to peer support will be encouraged.200 girls and 300 women will be trained on making reusable sanitary pads to better handle their menstrual cycle within the WGFS.1000 dignity kits will be requested from the GBV sub cluster and distributed to 700 women and 300 adolescent girls in Yambio and Nzara. In addition to the WGFS, case management services and referrals will be provided to 300 women,30 men,20 boys and 150 girls with protection concerns through 2 CMMB supported health facilities that will include clinical management of rape(CMR) services and treatment for injuries. Safe referrals for survivors will be facilitated through strengthening of the GBV referral pathways in the targeted locations and training of service providers. Cash based individual Protection Assistance will be provided to 50 beneficiaries(20 adolescent girls,20 women,5 boys and 5 men) selected through established criteria. Child survivors will receive child protection services through CMMB’s CP program while the team will visit WGFS on specified days to support children who might accompany their mothers/caregivers.30 staff, community volunteers and frontline workers(13 men,17 women) will be trained on GBV case management to improve quality of services delivered including individual/group specialized PSS and other related services. To strengthen community-based protection and enhance awareness raising on key GBV issues,4000 women,1250 girls,1250 boys and 1500 men will be sensitized on available services,referrals,women rights etc. through community outreach/campaigns in schools, safe spaces, churches etc. Targeted monthly community discussions to identify high risk areas and factors exposing women and girls to GBV and develop strategies to address these risks will be a significant focus. Using the UNICEF communities’ care GBV prevention program, guided weekly community discussions will be conducted that will empower 7 groups to raise awareness and promote positive norms and spark community-led action to prevent it. 6,000 community members(2000 women,1500men,1500 girls,1000 boys) will be targeted for awareness raising by trained groups. To prepare communities to mitigate protection risks and enhance preparedness,34 community volunteers(10 men and 24 women) will be trained.CMMB will actively participate in coordination forums e.g. GBV working group, protection cluster etc.CMMB co-chairs CPWG,an essential platform to champion GBV issues affecting children. All data collected will be disaggregated based on age,sex and type of service and reports submitted to the GBV sub cluster, protection cluster etc.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Given reports on sexual exploitation and abuse within communities, CMMB will develop and disseminate advocacy notes with recommendations to relevant sector on steps to mitigate SEA. Advocacy campaigns with various sectors will be conducted and Information Education and Communication (Campaigns) materials on PSEA from the cluster shared. CMMB will disseminate information to IDPs, returnees and host communities. Existing CMMB complaints and feedback mechanism in different sectors will be strengthened through awareness creation. With funding from HRP, CMMB will establish a PSEA task force/network consisting of agency and sector focal persons to spearhead dissemination of PSEA information and advocacy issues. Assessment and mapping out potential risk areas for children,women,girls,boys will be carried out to ensure the beneficiaries are aware and jointly recommend protective actions to combat SEA. Adolescent girls and women peer groups ‘will encourage reporting and ensure confidentiality.WGFS are within safe and central locations to reduce exposure to unsafe locations. Approved information sharing protocols e.g. GBVIMS tools will minimize exposure of sensitive information. Limited access to project sites by children and adults living with disabilities will be addressed through engaging community volunteers who might identify and refer them appropriately.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

CMMB approach to feedback and complaints handling is part of a broader organizational commitment to accountability, which promotes information sharing, transparency, participation and learning with project participants. Under the proposed Feedback, complaints mechanisms will be based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use. Steps involved in processing and responding to complaints, reports will be clearly explained to beneficiaries including how confidentiality will be maintained. For those who cannot read and write other ways of getting feedback e.g. using pictures and expressions will be explored to ensure their contributions are captured. Key messages will be translated into local languages to ensure that they are well understood. All feedback will be recorded, responses will be given to community members or specific groups on monthly basis. Monthly report of the feedback received will be shared within CMMB management team. CMMB program has a staff who is responsibility is to support complain and feedback mechanisms within the projects implemented by CMMB. CMMB well established accountability and complain and feedback mechanism system in project locations in Yambio, Nzara, Ezo and Mvolo where CMMB uses the experiences across those locations to address accountability and feedback issues by sharing challenges encountered and suggesting recommendations. In additional to complain and feedback mechanism, the project Community Mobilizers will organize a flow of feedback from communities and complaints about targeting and project design which will be used as a means to request further services.
