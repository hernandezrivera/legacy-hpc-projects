Project Summary

Islamic Relief (IR) Syria aims to enhance protection response systems in Northwest Syria (NWS) through collaborative efforts with vulnerable groups, addressing the urgent needs exacerbated by the 2023 earthquake. Focused on girls, boys, women, and men, the project encompasses multifaceted activities:
1. GBV Awareness and Support:
- Raising awareness through safe spaces and support groups targeting 2880 individuals.
- Prioritizing vulnerable groups like widows, disabled individuals, and elderly-headed households.
- Case management targeting 468 individuals.
- Capacity building training on GBV issues targeting 100 human workers.
- Vocational training targeting 360 individuals.
- Reaching 3,120 women & girls with targeted support.
- Dignity kits distribution targeting 1500 individuals.
2. Child Protection:
- Awareness among 2,430 caregivers of child protection issues.
- Parenting programs targeting 1000 individuals.
- Providing child-friendly spaces and case funds for at-risk children.
- Reaching 3,120 children with targeted support.
- Case management targeting 330 individuals.
3. General Protection (GP):
- Targeting 3120 individuals with age-appropriate psychosocial activities.
- Offering sustainable income-generation activities for financial independence.
- Skills training in areas such as handicrafts, income management, and market integration.
- Legal awareness-raising sessions targeting 240 individuals.
The project responds to identified needs post-earthquake, including increased GBV risks, child protection concerns, and economic vulnerabilities. Protection Risk Analysis acknowledges the impact on service points, emphasizing the importance of gender-sensitive programming in shelters. The PSEA strategy ensures robust measures, training, and community awareness to prevent exploitation and abuse.
Mitigating identified risks involves coordination with local authorities, data protection measures, accessibility solutions for vulnerable groups, and security protocols. Assumptions include cooperation with partners, effective training, and community acceptance.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

IR Syria's comprehensive approach aims to address the escalating protection needs post-earthquake in NWS. The project is intricately designed to combat the multidimensional challenges faced by vulnerable populations, emphasizing collaborative and inclusive interventions. 1. GBV Awareness and Support: The project recognizes the heightened vulnerability of women and girls to GBV post-earthquake. Awareness-raising activities, facilitated through safe spaces and support groups, target 6,240 individuals. Special attention is given to widows, disabled individuals, and elderly-headed households. The establishment or support of Women's Networks (WNs) amplifies outreach efforts, fostering community engagement and dialogue. 2. Child Protection: Child protection is a core focus, acknowledging the increased risks faced by children in the aftermath of the earthquake. Awareness sessions for caregivers aim to reduce child protection risks, complemented by child-friendly spaces and case funds for at-risk children. The project aims to reach 3,120 children, providing specialized support and addressing individual needs. 3. Income Generation Activities (IGA): Economic vulnerabilities, exacerbated by the earthquake, are tackled through income-generation activities. Eighteen rights-holders, including vulnerable women and men, benefit from skills training, promoting financial independence. Handicrafts, income management, and market integration training empower individuals, contributing to long-term resilience. 4. Protection Risk Analysis: The earthquake significantly impacted service points, affecting the delivery of specialized GBV services. Gender-sensitive programming in shelters and IDP sites is crucial to mitigate risks. The Protection Risk Analysis emphasizes the need for GBV mainstreaming in various sectors, including shelter and WASH facilities. 5. PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) Strategy: IR Syria adopts a robust PSEA strategy, ensuring accountability and prevention. Key components include designated points of contact, staff training, community awareness, and a community-based complaints mechanism. The strategy reflects a commitment to zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse, safeguarding the dignity and well-being of beneficiaries. 6. Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures: Identified risks include obstacles from local authorities, data leaks, accessibility challenges for remote populations, and potential violence escalation. Mitigating measures involve securing local approvals, implementing data protection protocols, ensuring accessibility solutions, and prioritizing staff safety. Assumptions revolve around effective coordination, successful training, and community acceptance, forming the foundation for project success.
