Project Summary

Operating in Libya remains challenging, with numerous security, logistical and access constraints. Thus, a strong and effective GBV sector coordination mechanism is required to ensure, humanitarian response is timely, effective and meets the needs of the most vulnerable people. Collaboration with national and international Humanitarian actors and Human Rights organizations are critical to ensure that assistance is provided safely and respectspeople’s dignity. Functional sectors and inter sector coordination group support humanitarian actors withcoordination, information management, needs assessments, advocacy for access, safetyand protection. It will benefit the entire humanitarian community to effectively and timely reach the most vulnerable people in Libya, as well as to sustain and extend their members’operational reach and impact. The annual review of existing common coordination mechanisms and structures includes the review of the sectors and the Inter-Sector Coordination Group, ensuring that the system is fit per purpose and able to deliver as expected. Information management products, Periodic Monitoring Reports, dashboards and snapshots are key elements well functioning sectors and ISCGs should deliver as they constitute the primary source of information for planning and decision-making. INGO co-coordination is crucial to ensure a balanced view on on programming and realities eperienced by implementing partners.
A strong GBV working group coordination is needed to ensure GBV sector response is coordinated, principled and information on GBV is collected safely and ethically in line with guiding principles and standards.

1 - Needs

The huge gaps in GBV services means that health consequences, including sexually transmitted infections, unplanned pregnancies, unsafe abortions, and other reproductive and possibly life-threatening health complications remain unaddressed. Thus, it is crucial to immediately scale up and establish holistic and comprehensive GBV prevention and response services, especially in geographic locations that lack any specialized GBV services, affected by increased conflict and displacement such as Al Kufra, Murzuq, Sirte and its immediate surrounding areas. Inclusive access to remote service delivery needs to be ensured in terms of language barriers; geographical reach and different abilities, in parallel women and girls need to be provided with resources, knowledge and monetary assistance to safely access changed service modalities. Shelter and independent safe houses for GBV survivors (in particular migrants and refugees) are urgently needed. The MSNA findings are also evident of the gap of information about rights, safe access opportunities and service availability among all population groups. Sexual and reproductive health and social protection services need to be capacitated with resources, including translators and reproductive health kits to provide safe and lifesaving assistance and care for survivors while abiding to GBV Guiding Principles. Continuing advocacy on positioning GBV prevention and response at the core of the humanitarian interventions while prioritizing safe access to GBV and sexual and reproductive health services amidst the protracted crisis and COVID-19 pandemic is key along with capacity building and advocacy for safer access to health services, respecting GBV Guiding Principles and CMR guidelines.Furthermore, advocacy on protective legal mechanisms for women and girls, to end impunity for perpetrators through the legal loopholes and unlimited humanitarian access to detention facilities needs to be strengthened. GBV prevention and response capacity needs to be mainstreamed across sectors and with national stakeholders.The GBV Sub-Sector Coordination will closely engage and coordinate GBV service providers in Libya to ensure above needs are addressed with comprehensive and quality GBV Prevention and Response services.

2 - Activities

Global inter-agency minimum standards for GBV prevention and response were developed by the GBV Area of Responsibility (AoR) and have been endorsed in 2019/2020, which includes partners in the United Nations, non-governmental organizations (NGOs) and the relevant inter-governmental organizations in humanitarian action. Included is a minimum standard on coordination that provides an overview of expectations for the GBV coordination lead agency, the coordination group as a whole and its members. It is important to use these standards as a mechanism to establish agreement and common approaches to GBV interventions when fulfilling tasks and roles. Coordination ensures a more predictable, accountable and effective response to GBV in emergencies. Coordination requires a collective, inter-agency and multi-sector effort for an effective process of engaging all relevant actors including States, UN agencies, international and local non-governmental organizations and communities to achieve a common goal. Only through collective and sustained action can the protection and safety of women and girls in emergencies be achieved. At the country level, this means working in partnership with national and local authorities and humanitarian actors to lead GBV coordination mechanisms, establish and strengthen national systems and ensure accessible, confidential and appropriate services for survivors. It also means consistently underscoring that prevention and response to GBV is everyone’s responsibility. Coordination can promote a common understanding of GBV issues amongst key humanitarian actors, uphold GBV minimum standards, monitor adherence to standards. The GBV Sub-Sector Coordination in Libya will ensure that all training and acapcity building is in line with GBViE minimum standards and specificall supports GBV mainstreaming, organising interagency trainings and workshops and facilitates engagement with key national stakeholders.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

Barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanisms will be addressed by working closely with the CFM and ensuring that the information on the respective reporting mechnaisms will be widely circulated. Lack of access to/or awareness of legal pathways to seeks justice, remedy, redress including barriers in accessing justice due to discriminatory laws and cultural practice - Partners and communities will be engaged to address lack of information on legal services and referral pathways will be strenghtend. Disclosure of sensitive personal data of affected persons, including safety and confidentiality of case management conducted in detention centres, leading to increased protection risks and further harm - GBVSS partners will be trained on data protection in line with GBV guiding principles and standards Barriers for beneficiaries to report sexual exploitation and/or abuse in a safe and confidential manner - Active contribution to re-activating the PSEA network to ensure communities and staff are aware of PSEA mechanisms. Support trainings on PSEA for GBV partners. All elements of GBV prevention, response, coordination and advocacy should adhere to,at a minimum, the following guiding principles: 1. Safety: The safety and security of thesurvivor and others, such as her/his children and people who have assisted her/him,must be the number one priority for all actors. 2. Confidentiality: People have the right to choose to whom they will, or will not, tell their story. Maintaining confidentiality ensuresthe survivors, witnesses and information sources are protected, and informed consent isobtained before action is taken. 3. Respect: All actions taken should be guided by respectfor the choices, wishes, rights and dignity of the survivor, and be guided by the best interests of the child. 4. Non-discrimination: Survivors of violence should receive equal and fair treatment regardless of their age, gender, race, religion, nationality, ethnicity, sexualorientation or any other characteristic.
