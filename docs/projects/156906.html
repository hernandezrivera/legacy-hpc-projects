Project Summary

In response, the proposed action will provide a set of early recovery support to vulnerable households in communities worst affected by chronic cycles of conflict. The planned action will support 12,040 vulnerable, conflict-affected individuals to meet their priority basic needs through the Economic Recovery and Market Systems, and New Livelihoods Development notably with vocational and business training, small business grants and apprenticeship placement. The action aims at keeping beneficiaries away from resorting to severe negative coping strategies and launch new viable livelihoods strategies where previous livelihoods have been destroyed by the sustained impact of the conflict.
The project will seek to deliver a market-driven response in line with local-level demands, resources, and gaps. As per PIN’s strategy, cash-based responses will be favored wherever possible, since they contribute to building resilience and revitalizing markets; although where security/access/market functionality precludes cash delivery, in-kind modalities will be utilized (NFIs, livelihoods start-up kits).
Likewise, this emergency response is complemented by other sectors notably covering Food Security, WASH and Education in host communities receiving IDPs, in order to maximize “do no harm’”considerations and ensure that vulnerable host populations also receive appropriate assistance. In addition, the complementary of implemented sectors permits, when possible to direct beneficiaries from emergency to regular food assistance and early recovery through an efficient combination of monthly cash, cash-for-work, vocational trainings and small business grant support, which will both allow individual beneficiary households to avoid recourse to negative coping strategies, as well as providing priority basic services and goods that are most in demand in the most heavily conflict-affected communities on the other.
Constant M&E monitoring act as activities backbone through beneficiary selection and verification, need assessment, market monitoring, post-implementation (such as distribution) monitoring, routine monitoring, Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM).
It should be noted that activities are designed to be in line with the relevant coordination system such as Sphere standards and meet FSL cluster recommendations (length of assistance, contents of kits, value of cash/vouchers etc.).
Lastly, all activities will be carried out in coordination with local councils (LC), ensuring to share information on needs and response gaps at the village level as well as involved in beneficiary selection process; local associations, and IDP representatives at village level will be considered, if any.

Needs

Needs in Northern Syria remain high across the humanitarian spectrum, with the Humanitarian Needs Overview for 2018 (HNO) estimating a total of 13.1 million people in need of assistance, of which 5.6 million facing acute needs and 2.98 million living in hard to reach areas. Access to services and livelihood opportunities remains scarce as needs touch virtually all sectors across conflict-affected areas. Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL), Non-Food Items (NFI), and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) are amongst the most urgent of needs. Needs in PIN's operational areas are amongst the highest in the country, with Aleppo, Idlib and Raqqa hosting close to 40% of the total estimated people in need in each of the above sectors across the whole country. 2018 HNO highlights that main early recovery and livelihoods needs include: better access to markets; livelihoods; emergency employment; business revival; and sustainable income generation opportunities. Rehabilitation of infrastructure such as quick repairs of electricity and water, jobs and employment opportunities, are also urgently required in many areas. Rapid onset emergencies are expected to continue over the course of 2019, eroding livelihoods options and increasing the need for life-saving humanitarian aid, necessitating coordinated and holistic response planning. Changing dynamics across Northern Syria are likely to occur with active hotspot and further leading in humanitarian needs growing in scale and severity. A flexible and responsive approach to the provision of assistance is required in order to best meet the evolving needs of vulnerable, conflict-affected communities.

Implementation of activities

Objective: to contribute enhancing market systems recovery, and empower vulnerable, conflict-affected households to launch viable income generating activities to meet their basic needs.Output: livelihoods opportunities for vulnerable conflict-affected populations are enhanced.Activities: support to early recovery and market systems.Sub-activities: •business training.•market and labor assessment.•provision of small business grants.Description: PIN aims at providing diverse individual employment opportunities and launch new viable livelihoods in the absence of formerly prevalent employment opportunities with large-scale employers, factories, and the civil service. Primary motivation for the distribution of small business grants is to ensure the sustainability of the effects of vocational trainings and ensure new livelihoods are viable for beneficiaries to achieve autonomy. Secondly, the provision of the business grants should multiply the effect of the training and help to promote the accessibility of the product or service to broader consumers and beneficiaries. PIN will prioritize the business grants that thematically match the vocational trainings provided. Activities: Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET).Sub-activities: •training development.•individual training.Description: the focus of PIN TVET program is providing long-term support to the most vulnerable beneficiaries by teaching them skills which will enable them to start generating income for their families and stop being dependent on short term assistance such as food baskets, food vouchers or cash grantsThe vocational trainees who present the highest levels of success in their training, as well as planning and motivation to launch their own small businesses will be supported through provision of equipment and small business grants to sustain their new livelihoods independently. The trainers will be selected primarily among the local practitioners, who either run the respective business in past or have a running businesses and are willing to participate in the program.Activities: apprenticeship placement for graduates.Sub-activities: •identification of potential employers.•training of trainers.•support of ongoing apprenticeships.Description: this activity is designed to foster employment opportunities of beneficiaries who were previously supported by emergency modalities and have good potential to secure and stabilize either their income generating activity or their employment. PIN will focus on linking newly skilled workers with potential employers while providing apprenticeship stipends to fund these work placements in order to overcome the initial investment barrier for the employment. To facilitate this process, in the initial phase of the project PIN will identify potential employers in respective vocations targeted by activities under this sub-sector. Following a vetting process, employers and employees with relevant skillsets will be linked-up and employers will receive training on how to provide meaningful on-the-job training/mentorship.

Protection Risk Analysis

People in Need (PIN) approaches to programming in Northern Syria are inherently driven by protection considerations.Some of the protection risks are as follow:1. High needs and vulnerability rate can lead targeting criteria to be the focus of tensions between beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries 2. Beneficiaries more exposed to hazard when travelling-long distance.3. Cash diverted by influencing stakeholders-LCs, non-beneficiaries. The core tenets of PIN's approach to protection mainstreaming include:1. Rigorous communication and feedback loops to ensure that the most vulnerable are able to voice their preferences and concerns at all phases of programme cycle management;2. Protection Mainstreaming initiatives to enhance accessibility to assistance of those most vulnerable, including development of new dedicated protection strategy for Northern Syria3. Capacity Building of staff to enhance protection awareness and ensure policies and strategy are fully assimilated into daily work4. Continuous M&E activities carried out throughout all project

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks and mitigating measures: 1. Inaccessibility to target areas due to insecurity, large scale military offensives, or change in territorialcontrol.PIN would suspend, relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; shift program resources to otherareas; and/or switch to remote management, potentially through pre-identified local partners. 2. Safety and security of staff and beneficiaries is compromised.PIN uses rigorous security monitoring mechanism carried out by PIN staff and triangulated with information available through other sources,including the International NGO Safety Organisation (INSO). 3. Borders are temporarily closed. PIN would suspend activities; advocate with UNOCHA and local authorities to facilitate access and operations; purchase locally if possible; eventually resort to remote Cash-based programming in extreme cases. 4. Legal/administrative constraints – including refusal of required operational permissions and visas – delay or prevent aid delivery in countries from where cross-border operations are conducted adopt a phased approaches to withdrawal 5. Inflation of the Syrian pound / Exchange rate fluctuations / Local markets are not able to meet the increased demand for goods use available in-kind commodities through food basket/bread/RTER provisions; switch allocations to increase in-kind assistance provision, reshape programming by revising targets in coordination with ECHO, switch to food basket distributions with items procured in neighbouring countries. 6. Restricted CTA liquidity inside Syria/International Bank Transfers rejections.Cash modalities would be suspended whilst the finance department assess and monitor market situation.If the situation persisted, as a final option, PIN would explore switching to in-kind modalities. PIN would engage with back up CTA. 7. Syria offices/warehouses/assets are raided PIN would temporarily suspend activities; carry out internal/external investigations; advocate for dialogue with relevant actors; inform relevant counter-terrorism stakeholders; seek to relocate the intervention to other areas. 8. Local Councils or other relevant local stakeholders stop collaborating with partners or fail to conduct targeting according to agreed methodologyPIN would temporarily suspend activities; engage dialogue to re-sensitize on humanitarian principles of engagement/PIN policies, relocate resources to other target areas. 9. Aid is perceived as politically motivated and/or PIN are subject to intimidation from military groups or local authorities.Activities would be suspended; re-sensitization/dialogue, reallocate funding to alternative target areas.11. Aid is diverted for personal or political gains. PIN mitigates aid diversion through a comprehensive set of rigorous management policiesPIN would suspend activities; carry out investigations, advocate for dialogue for return/replacement of diverted aid; re-sensitize actors; reallocate funding to alternative target areas.
