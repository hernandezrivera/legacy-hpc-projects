Project Summary

According to UNOCHA situation report in October 2023, the outbreak of fighting in Sudan on 15 April 2023 led to an influx of people fleeing the country. This crisis has affected South Sudan significantly, with more than 312,958 people fleeing from Sudan into South Sudan, about 49% of the new arrivals are women and girls, 46% are below the age of 18 and 4% are over the age of 60 years; 89% of those arriving are South Sudanese. The core driver of movement of people, particularly women and girls, from Sudan to South Sudan is to seek protection. Women and girls fleeing conflict in Sudan continue to face high risks of GBV including rape, sexual exploitation and abuse, intimate partner violence and physical violence when they arrive in South Sudan. Adolescent girls are even more vulnerable with child marriage increasing in many locations across South Sudan. Incidents of conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV) have been documented in the Sudan crisis and the impacts of this are compounded by gaps in lifesaving GBV services in South Sudan including clinical management of rape (CMR) and GBV case management and psychosocial support (PSS). Overall, the capacity and coverage of multi-sectoral GBV responses including health, GBV case management and PSS, higher-level mental health and other services remain scarce in areas experiencing elevated levels of returnees and refugees in Unity, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Jonglei and Central Equatoria. Protection concerns remained high, with people impacted by violence having limited access to justice and the rule of law. In 2024, the GBV- sub cluster estimated that 2.7 million people will need GBV support and 546K will be targeted for support in 2024. Influx of refugees and returnees from neighbouring countries resulted into strain in available services. At least 278,533 (89%) of Sudan influx are returnees in addition to the already existing refugees and returnee’s caseload will exacerbate protection risks if no actions are taken. According to UNOCHA, in 2023, there were 2.3 million IDPs in the country as compared to 2 million in 2021.
Alight has operational presence; in Aweil West, Aweil Centre (project ended in September 2023), Ulang, and Nasir Counties of South Sudan. Alight runs GBV response and prevention interventions in all but one, Aweil West County. In Aweil West County, Alight don't have active GBV services but have on-going nutrition interventions and a camp management for Wedwil Refugee settlement. HRP funds will be used to complement ongoing projects in; Ulang, Nasir and Aweil Centre and integrate GBV into our nutrition activities in Aweil West. To ensure that Alight and other partners do not duplicate services, Alight will undertake GBV service mapping on “who is doing what and where’’ in all the locations targeted. This will allow Alight to only target areas not served by humanitarian interventions. The cumulative effects of these emerging gaps in GBV call for scaling up GBV service provisions to locations and populations that have not been benefiting from our current GBV programs. In Aweil West, GBV services have been non-existent as identified by our nutrition staff providing nutrition services in Aweil West and even partners responding to Sudan influx have activities limited to the refugees without extending the same to returnees and hosting communities. To ensure inclusivity of interventions especially for persons with disability, Alight normally conduct assessment prior to launch of project to ensure that disability information is captured for planning purposes. This information usually helps Alight to put in place deliberate measures that address access and inclusivity of special interest groups in our projects.

1. Needs

According to UNOCHA situational Report issue 15 on response to Sudan crisis, the outbreak of fighting in Sudan on mid-April 2023 led to an influx of people fleeing the country. This crisis has affected South Sudan significantly, with more than 312,958 fleeing from Sudan into South Sudan, about 49% of the new arrivals are women and girls, 46% are below the age of 18 and 4% are over the age of 60 years; 89% of those arriving are South Sudanese . The core driver of movement of people, particularly women and girls, from Sudan to South Sudan is to seek protection. Women and girls fleeing conflict from Sudan continue to face high risks of GBV including rape, sexual exploitation and abuse, intimate partner violence and physical violence when they arrive in South Sudan. Adolescent girls are even more vulnerable with child marriage increasing in many locations across South Sudan. Incidents of conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV) have been documented in the Sudan crisis and the impacts of this are compounded by gaps in lifesaving GBV services in South Sudan including clinical management of rape (CMR) and GBV case management and psychosocial support (PSS). Overall, GBV responses including health, GBV case management and PSS, and other services remain scarce in areas experiencing elevated levels of returnees and refugees in Unity, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Jonglei and Central Equatoria. Aweil Centre and Aweil West: According to the UNOCHA update on crisis situation report of 13th October 2023, the Sudan crisis has added about 90,000 returnees and above 9,000 refugees in Northern Bahr El Ghazal State alone straining already meagre resources available to respond to the situation. The most urgent needs for returnees include food, shelter, non-food items and health. The resident communities have exhausted their food items, increasing their vulnerability. According to the HRP 2024 inter-cluster Prioritization, put Aweil Centre and Aweil West in priority level 3 (severe). Chances of relapse to extreme and catastrophic are high if the situation remains unaddressed. Alight’s HRP 2024 therefore aimed to address the gaps as well as expanding GBV services to Aweil West County where GBV services have been unavailable. Ulang County and Nasir: Ulang and Nasir Counties are found in Upper Nile State along Sobat River Corridor. It is perennially affected by flooding and inter-communal violence (ICV) and now refugee returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia. This year, while flooding was not experienced, ICV increased displacement and thus protection risks for women and girls that place women and girls at disadvantaged position. According to the Spontaneous Refugee Return (SRR) bulletin released by UNHCR in September 2023, there are 22,201 spontaneous refugee returns from Ethiopia that entered Upper Nile State and most of these settled in Ulang, Nasir and neighbouring Counties of Maiwut, Longechuk and Jonglei areas. The HRP inter-cluster prioritization 2024 has put Nasir under catastrophic category and Ulang under extreme in terms of needs. The HRP 2024 funding will be used to upscale services to these areas so that persons of concern in these areas are provided with services and their access challenges addressed.

2. Project activities

Cluster Objective 1: Ensure mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability. presence/missions to field and deep field locations. Activity 1.1 Strengthening specialized GBV response services (GBV case management, referrals, legal aid, safe houses & WGFS programming) as well as capacity building, field monitoring, and linking GBV survivors through referral pathways. Survivor-centered and confidential case management services to GBV survivors will be undertaken as well as consent-based referrals. To this end, Alight will collaborate with partners to localize and update referral pathways regularly. Activity 1.2: Strengthening Psychosocial Support service provision through capacity building, field monitoring visit and collaboration with MHPSS working group. WGFS will be an entry for women and girls to access PSS and other services. Alight will identify a fully dedicated WGFS facilitator from the beneficiaries to coordinate skill building and PSS activities so that caseworkers can fully focus on case management. This will also foster sustainability and ownership of WGFS activities by the community. Activity 1.3 Strengthening the reach and coverage of dignity kits distribution and programming. Alight will request dignity kit from cluster pipeline, and these will be distributed to women and girls of reproductive age as a GBV risk mitigation measure. 5,571 dignity kits will be distributed to vulnerable women and girls in all the project areas. Activity 1.4: Strengthening GBV referral pathways through conducting GBV service mapping and establishing / updating GBV SoP. This will help to ensure that survivors of GBV enjoy all the available services provided by partners operating in a particular location so that their multiple needs are addressed through complementary service provision. Cluster objective 2: Ensuring that human rights are upheld and promoted for the persons of concern by strengthening coordination, advancing centrality of protection among humanitarian actors to reduce protection risks, and addressing coordination gaps/issues through close dialogue with partners and effective protection monitoring to inform programming, advocacy, and response. Activity 2.1: Strengthening GBV awareness through house-to-house visit, focused group interventions, campaigns, and mass media. Alight will use the mentioned approaches to pass GBV key messages to the community. 14,327 women, girls, boys, and men will be reached with GBV messages through awareness raising. Activity 2.2: Strengthening the rollout and implementation of primary GBV preventions interventions. Activity 2.3: Integrating Cash and Voucher Assistance into GBV programming by working closely with Cash Working Group. Alight will introduce assistance to people affected by disasters as an effective, efficient, and transparent way of providing humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable. This ensures people have the freedom, dignity, and independence to decide on their own recovery and ensure that affected people are served in dignity through the cash-based programming. Activity 2.4: Scaling up the reach of fuel-efficient stoves, in collaboration with FSL and WFP, adopting innovative approaches and best practices. Cluster objective 3: Promoting nexus to development and solutions as the transition between the delivery of emergency humanitarian assistance, systems building and the provision of long-term development assistance to meet peoples’ needs, mitigate risks and vulnerabilities, exacerbated by systemic and socially reinforced exclusion of women and girls and persons with disabilities. Activity 3.1. Provision of need based capacity building trainings to non-GBV humanitarian actors on integration of GBV in their sectoral responses.
