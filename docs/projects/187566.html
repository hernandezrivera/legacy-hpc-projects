Project Summary

In this project, NGO X is submitting an integrated approach to Shelter and Winter cash. This integrated project will address the multiple vulnerabilities that households and communities are facing after ten years of conflict. At the household level, the program design creates links between its multi-donor funded emergency response program and its food security, winterization and shelter programming, and complements food assistance programming with basic needs assistance, Shelter activities target beneficiaries on this level with repairs and rehabilitation of shelter units in communities and IDP sites to ensure that beneficiaries are living in dignified conditions. On the settlement level, the program will focus on flood prone areas providing preventive measures to alleviate the effects of flooding and prepare for winter.
NGO X will enable Highly vulnerable IDPs and resident households to meet their basic winter needs through coordinated and timely winter cash. The priority locations for the winterization response are typically selected according to the following criteria: mountainous areas, areas with snow coverage and areas with historic cold weather patterns. A fuel market assessment with fuel vendors and Winter Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) with affected people will then be conducted to ascertain the availability of fuel, market access and functionality.

NGO X will aim to complete winter cash response by the end of November each year. Cash will be the main and the default response mechanism as long as minimum preconditions for the use of cash exist. The cash transfer value per HH is $ 150 which is informed by SNFI cluster winterization guidance.

NGO X plans to utilize and e-card platform to deliver cash where feasible to reach a selected number of particularly vulnerable beneficiaries under this activity, priority will be given to child headed households, single/widowed women, the elderly and people with disabilities.
For the Winter cash component, a total of 48,000 households (24,000 per year) living in particularly cold and mountainous areas, including camps and informal settlements will be targeted under this project.
NGO X will rehabilitate shelter units in communities using self-help modality providing CASH grants to HHs to carry out the required work themselves and will target in-camps shelters with rehabilitation activities using contractor modality, the shelter rehabilitation activity will target 759 HHS. In Camps and IDP sites NGOx will carry out IDP site improvement activities including roads gravelling, construction and rehabilitation of rainwater drainage systems, tents levelling, and installation of solar lights targeting 5,074 HHs.
In total the unique target for 2022-2023 is 53,833 HHs and 269,165 individuals

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 SNFI Cluster Syria MSNA in Idleb and Aleppo 53% mentioned shelter assistance as one of their top priorities, while 42% mentioned rehabilitation of infrastructure. According to the 2021 HNAP, Shelter Situation, IDP Report Series, Nationwide 1 in 4 IDP households face some sort of shelter hazard – such as wind, sandstorms, flooding, and fire. However, in-camp IDP households are nearly 7 times as likely to face a hazard compared to out-of-camp households. In NWS. As reported by HSOS, 79% of key informants mentioned shelter and repair materials being too expensive was the most reported challenge for HHs to repair their shelter. IDPs are the most financially vulnerable group and within this group female IDPs, female headed IDP households, including widows, heads of IDP households with disabilities and recently displaced IDPs are the most financially constrained and often facing higher barriers for accessing income opportunities (HNO 2021). Due to poor shelter conditions and limited financial capacity to meet increased needs (heating fuel, clothing) during the winter period, affected populations face exposure to harsh winter conditions. NGO X Fuel Market and Winter MSNA in Sept 2021, found that fuel for heating/cooking was the top priority need for 49% of interviewees during the winter season. Although the fuel market was functional, the assessed populations didn't have the financial capacity to access it. 99% of interviewees preferred cash modality.

5 - Implementation of activities

- NGO X will carry out ‘self-help modality’ shelter response, for 50 HHs. The approach combines conditional cash grants with on-site technical support and skill transfer. Each household will receive an average of $1,000 USD per shelter unit. In addition to contractor-led repairs in IDP location.- NGO X will target tented settlements and IDP camps with site and access improvements including rehabilitation of access roads to ensure uninterrupted access to camps during winter months, installation of solar lighting, including around WASH facilities, installation of sewage system and rainwater drainage improvements. These activities will reduce protection risks, especially for women and children, and improve access for persons with reduced mobility (e.g., older persons and persons with disabilities). For winter cash, NGO X will:- Conduct Fuel Market assessment and Winter MSNA of households,- Identify the initial locations most vulnerable to cold weather. Then will carry out 100% HHs selection and verification process. - Provide winter cash to vulnerable households. The cash value is budgeted at $150 in line with S/NFI cluster winterization. - Conduct Distribution Monitoring (DM) during the distributions and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) surveys following distributions.
