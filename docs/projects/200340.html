Project Summary

In collaboration with UN OCHA and through the mandate of the UN Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), REACH proposes to conduct a Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA), consisting of a multi-sectoral household (HH) survey employed among a relevant sample nationwide for Lebanese, PRL (Palestinian Refugees in Lebanon) and Migrant households. REACH will lead the overall technical implementation of the MSNA and provide oversight to each phase of the MSNA research cycle; joint inter-sectoral analysis of MSNA data; and the production of the key outputs. The MSNA will therefore provide data that will directly inform the aid communities prioritisation and planning in 2023 and beyond, fulfilling Core Commitment 5 of the Grand Bargain through the conduct of a joint, impartial, and collaborative needs assessment. In addition, REACH will pilot a qualitative data component focusing on protection and Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) needs in a select number of the most vulnerable districts to fill important data gaps not able to be filled by a quantitative survey. Again, REACH will lead the overall technical implementation of the qualitative and provide oversight to each phase of the research cycle, though close collaboration with protection sectors will be ensured throughout each phase of the research cycle as well. ]

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

REACH’s data collection is undertaken within the framework of a do-no-harm approach, and in accordance with REACH policy on the collection, storage and dissemination of PII. Sensitive conflict-related information, or information that could potentially pose a protection risk to the respondents will not be collected. Any information that could potentially violate respondent anonymity is removed from the dataset before disseminated. Respondent contact information is not shared. REACH will clearly identify age, gender and disability related information in all its data collection activities, and REACH will share its data in an age and gender disaggregated manner. In addition, REACH is committed to managing the expectations of the vulnerable populations visited in relation to a potential response. It will be made clear at the beginning of each survey that REACH cannot provide assistance or influence another actor to do so

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be firstly ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). All enumerators will be informed and trained on CRM in order to share information with respondents and encourage respondents to report any issues or concerns during the MSNA’s data collection process. The involvement of affected populations will occur in two key ways: 1) The MSNA will collect core AAP indicators, such as self-reported priority needs, preferred assistance modalities, and preferred communications modalities, to support people-centred and inclusive humanitarian needs analysis; and 2) In collaboration with OCHA and other MSNA partners, representatives from affected population groups can be invited to participate in either the joint analysis process or validation of findings (in the case of the latter, through dissemination of Arabic-translated key findings from the assessment).
