Project Summary

Concern intends to address the food security, health and nutrition needs of the affected populations. These needs are chronic due to the fact that the displacement crisis started in 2015, with several waves of displacement due to violence in the intervening years and the very small number of people returning home. International agencies operational in Lac Province have been focusing largely on humanitarian needs of the IDPs and refugees but needs are clearly exceeding the resources available, due also to the chronic underfunding of the Humanitarian Response Plan. The health system is extremely weak in Lac Province and with the growing numbers of IDPs, humanitarian assistance is essential to address the health and nutrition needs. Concern will build on the experience of previous and ongoing projects to contribute to meeting those needs.
Overall Programme Goal: Communities and systems are resilient to security threats and the adverse impact of climate change
Outcome 1: IDPs and host communities have improved food security
Outcome 2: IDPs and host communities have the required skills to diversify their livelihoods options and to demonstrate nutrition sensitive behaviours

1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

Chad, ranked 187 out of 189 on the Human Development Index, is one of the poorest countries in the world, facing multifaceted crisis in various areas of its territory. According to the United Nations HRP 2020, an estimated 5.3 million people are in need of assistance. The needs are particularly acute in Lake Chad province. According OCHA’S humanitarian overview report in november 2021, 80% of the population of the Lac province (i.e., 549,000 people) require humanitarian assistance. The state-led SMART survey 2020 showed alarming data: children in the Lake Province have the highest rate of Severe Acute Malnutrition in the country: at 3,9%, this is significantly above the alert threshold. Furthermore, in 2021, Concern observed a considerable increase of almost 70% in admissions of SAM (Severe Acute Malnutrition) to health centers supported in Bagasola. The Harmonized Framework predicted that food security in Lac Province will be at a “crisis level” from June to August 2022 with around 30% of the population in phases 3 and above. According to the Food Security Cluster in Chad, there are first indications that the situation might even deteriorate.However, the Lake Chad area is historically known to be a relatively fertile and wealthy area. The main livelihood is agro pastoral and fishing. The Lake, despite having shrunken in size since the 60s, still provides enough for irrigation and fishing waters. The seasonally flooded polders and wadis along the lake are fertile and allow up to three agricultural seasons. The pasture is usually good and feed large quantities of livestock. The Lac Province is part of the transhumance migration corridor and provides essential pasture and water when resources are drying up further north.The concerning food situation is mainly due to conflicts between the Chadian armed forces and non-state armed groups (GANEs) such as Boko Haram which have caused since 2015 the displacement of nearly 450,000 people in the Lake Chad province. They have been located in around 171 IDP sites and 57 host communities/ villages welcoming displaced in the Province. Forced movements of population are currently still ongoing as insecurity persists (rise of attacks and kidnapping in villages and IDP’s camps in march 2022). Most of the time the displaced population is settled on land with harsher environment (less access to water and less fertile soils), has very limited shelters and infrastructures, and suffer from a lack of access to basic services (Water, Health and Education). They also have a lack of access to food and economic activities as people have left their villages in a rush leaving behind all their resources. Women and girls are specifically vulnerable,they are often targeted by the frequent kidnapping of BH armed group, andas they live in high precarity, they are more exposed to gender based violence. They have limited access to healthcare infrastructure for maternal, reproductive health and for their children. The effects of climate change further deteriorate this already fragile situation. Lac Province is experiencing droughts and floods, with the most recent flood in August 2020 displacing around 7,000 households. Climate uncertainty, extreme weather and changing rain/drough pattern affect sowing circle, soil fertility and damage crops and land, enhance stress in livestock and transhumance movement and conflict around land ownership. This affects people’s crucial economic activities, their understanding of natural environment, their resilience capacities, and more generally food production and food security. In this context, prices, especially for maize, rose sharply, standing more than 20% above the average in provincial markets. Livestock prices are already showing an upward trend. Markets are functional in the province but they need strengthening and diversification.In this complex context, there is a pressing need to restore people livelihoods, to/ develop high potential activities and to enhance access to basic services. Supporting people to implement a climate smart and diversified agriculture is fundamental. Concern intends to address the food security, health and nutrition needs of the affected populations. These needs are chronic due to the fact that the displacement crisis started in 2015, with several waves of displacement due to violence in the intervening years and the very small number of people returning home. International agencies operational in Lac Province have been focusing largely on humanitarian needs of the IDPs and refugees but needs are clearly exceeding the resources available, due also to the chronic underfunding of the Humanitarian Response Plan. The health system is extremely weak in Lac Province and with the growing numbers of IDPs, humanitarian assistance is essential to address the health and nutrition needs. Concern will build on the experience of previous and ongoing projects to contribute to meeting those needs. Programme logic: Outcome 1 : IDPs and host communities have improved food securityOutput 1.1 : Increase agricultural productivities in wadis and output from rain fed agriculture (largely millet)Activities: Provide inputs to beneficiaries (tools, seeds) ; Training on improved techniques (inter-cropping, furrow irrigation,...); Farmers trainings and sensitization sessions on the use of organic fertilizer and herbicides; Training farmers on Climate Smart Agriculture techniques (zai pits, demi lunes, mulching, spacing); Build farmers organization capacities (strengthen agriculture extension services, lead farmer approach)Output 1.2 : Increase dietary diversity through vegetable production and consumptionActivities: Training households to develop kitchen gardening; Training and giving technical support to households to produce quality organic fertiliser (compost with cow dung) for their market gardening; Donation of equipment and materials increasing access to water and water management (e.g solar/treadle pumps, grey water use, irrigation, mulching) Outcome 2: IDPs and host communities have the required skills to diversify their livelihoods options and to demonstrate nutrition sensitive behaviours Output 2.1: Increase profits of small businesses (especially business aiming to develop new goods and services)Activities: Coaching/mentoring targeted groups on business concepts/option available in their communities; Train (groups or individuals) on business plan adapted to context Output 2.2: Improve fishery production for 1620 people (824 female, 796 male)Activities: Provide to targeted individuals quality fishery equipment (supply&demand); Train business owners to reduce waste in transportation, delivery of the product and application of better practices fish processing, transport and end products (hygiene, quality, diversity); Train and sensitize fishermen on environmental law, use of conventional nets and conventional fishing techniques Outcome 3: Reduced prevalence, mortality and morbidity associated with acute malnutritionOutput 3.1: Improved hygiene practices at community/ household levelActivities: Training and equipment for community health workers (IYFC, danger signs, culinary demonstrations…); Conduct community screening and referrals for acute malnutrition cases; Hygiene and nutrition sensitisation/ awareness campaignsOutput 3.2: To improve the quality and access to health and nutrition services through support to two existing health centersActivities: Training and capacity reinforcement for health facility staff (National protocols PECIMA/PECIME) and community volunteers (COSAN, COGES, community relays); Stock health centres with medicines, therapeutic foods and medical equipment; Establish and implement mobile clinics in remote IDP sites
