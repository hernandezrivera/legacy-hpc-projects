Project Summary

Economic surveys of families in Iraq shows that 21% of Iraqi girls are married before the age of 19, compared to 15% in 1997 and 2004. In 2011, The Central Organization for Statistics conducted a survey that indicated 5% of Iraqi girls under the age of 15 were married, and around 22% of those under 18 were married. Early marriages have increased at an alarming rate, although in 2010 the ministry of women’s affairs warned that “girls between 15 and 18 are twice as likely to die during pregnancy and while giving birth than women between the ages of 20 and 24.” Such surveys shed light on the rights of female teenagers, who are most likely to die during pregnancy and childbirth. According to the studies results and our own observations from our work on managing the WCC’s, we think the main reasons of the increase in children marriage in the IDP’s communities in Iraq are:
1. The critical economic situation of these families because most of the head of the families lost their jobs or even lost their lives.
2. The religious nature of the Iraqi society encourages the early marriage.
3. The displacement causes many children school dropouts. Education is a powerful way to prevent child marriage, especially keeping girls in school through secondary grades. Schooling is an essential part of adolescent lives for both girls and boys.
4. The families are not aware of the children underage marriage on their children especially the girls.
In order to achieve the project objectives, the project designed to adopt three techniques: -
A. Social technique.
B. Economical technique.
C. Awareness
Accordingly, the project work plan is consisting of (3) parallel stages, each stage has its own planned achievements. These stages are classified according different suggested solutions. The first stage will work on helping return children get back to school. The second stage will work on empowering the children families economically. The third stage will work on conducting awareness meetings to raise the awareness between children families, teachers, and host community about the bad effects of the early marriage on community. The project main activities are: -
1. Conducting a quick assessment to decide the most vulnerable areas .
2. Identify (300) child, (200) girls and (100) boys and work closely with their parents and the education department in Mosul to help them get back to school.
3. Identify (3) schools in the target area and establish a (School Club) in each of them. The clubs membership will be for (parents, school teachers, educational supervisors from DOE, and community leaders). The clubs will work on discussing the problem of the children who are dropping from school and the best suggestions to get them back to school.
4. Choose (10) and give them a (1000$) grant as an economic empowerment for them. The criteria to choose the right families will be according to the highest number get back to school per family.
Sustainability Plan
1. In parallel with the above activities, TAJDID will conduct at least (10) public meetings in different areas of Mosul aiming to raise awareness about early marriage danger. TAJDID will invite (parents, school teachers and principals, educational experts, government officials, and community leaders) to these meetings. In these meeting, social and education experts will explain the risks of schools dropping children, its effect on the society and family. In addition, suggestions for solutions will be discussed. A report about each of these meetings will be written and shared different stakeholders with all the information discussed.
2. The results of these sessions will be used as awareness topics that will help in convincing the families of the importance of schools for their daughter’s future and will work on changing their behavior.
3. Train 70 Young females on Peer education technique to enhance their capacity over peer to peer education as advocacy.

How is your project accountable to affected population

The program will involve the targeted population in all the program stages as follows: -1- The design stage where the beneficiaries were assessed through our on ground team in Mosul.2- The implementation stage where the M&E teams will meet and collect the beneficiaries feedback on the implementation. In addition, TAJDID will initiate a complaints mechanism through a phone number which will be announced and disseminated in all the working area.3- An Evaluation process will be conducted after the project ended to summarize the lessons learned.4- Continuous meetings with local authorities and stakeholders will be conducted to exchange opinions.
