Project Summary

This humanitarian initiative will provide emergency food security and livelihoods (FSL) programming to address the immediate needs of internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and vulnerable Non-Displaced in conflict-affected areas, including Quissanga, Metuge, and Montepuez
Key activities include distributing agricultural inputs (seeds, tools, fertilizers) to enhance staple and vegetable crop production, training farmers in good agricultural and post-harvest handling practices, and supporting livestock production through vaccination campaigns, animal feed provision, and related training. The project also supports small-scale fishermen with equipment and training to improve productivity and market access. Additionally, vocational training and income-generating activities are promoted to diversify livelihoods and build resilience.
This humanitarian Food Security and Livelihoods project aims to improve food access, dietary diversity, and income generation for internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and vulnerable non-displaced population.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project will employ a robust risk assessment and safeguarding approach tailored to humanitarian Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) programming, ensuring interventions are safe, inclusive, and effective for the most vulnerable populations. Key elements include:The project begins with an in-depth assessment of security, environmental, cultural, and economic factors. This includes identifying risks to agricultural, livestock, and income-generating activities and addressing challenges faced by displaced or conflict-affected populations. Regular context updates help anticipate and mitigate potential risks to livelihoods, such as climate shocks or market disruptions.The project will ensure active engagement with community members, including women, youth, and marginalized groups, ensures local knowledge informs risk identification. Feedback mechanisms, such as regular consultations and anonymous reporting channels, empower beneficiaries to highlight concerns, enabling prompt responses to emerging challenges.All interventions are designed to respect cultural contexts, promote gender equity, and safeguard vulnerable groups under Do-No-Harm farmwork. For instance, agricultural input distribution and training on sustainable practices are conducted equitably, ensuring fairness while avoiding exacerbation of existing inequalities.The project will integrate regular monitoring of FSL activities, such as crop production, livestock health, and income generation, to identify and address risks proactively. Adaptive strategies ensure the safe and timely delivery of critical services, protecting livelihoods and fostering resilience.By embedding these measures, the project ensures that FSL interventions strengthen food security and economic stability without compromising the safety or dignity of target communities.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The proposed project promotes inclusive access to assistance and actively empowers women and elderly persons through tailored, sector-specific strategies within the Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) framework:From the project’s inception, women, elderly persons, and local leaders will be engaged in planning and decision-making processes. This ensures that the interventions meet their specific needs and priorities, reflecting their unique challenges. Involving women’s groups and elderly representatives ensures that food security activities, such as seed distribution or livestock support, align with their capacities and needs.The project will deliver targeted, gender-sensitive training for women and elderly community members. This includes providing skills-based training in agricultural practices, post-harvest handling, small-scale business development, and income generation. Women are also trained to become community promoters of nutrition, health, and sustainable food practices, empowering them to take leadership roles within their households and communities.The project will ensure that services are inclusive of the elderly, with considerations for mobility and health needs, such as providing easy access to distribution points and nutrition services. Women receive specialized hygiene kits, which include menstrual health items, and maternal health sessions are integrated into the project to address specific health and nutrition concerns.The project will also ensure that there are regular feedback sessions with women and elderly groups provide a platform for their voices to be heard, ensuring that project activities are responsive to their concerns. These sessions encourage community ownership and allow for continuous improvements in project implementation.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will take a proactive and inclusive approach to ensure individuals with disabilities have equal access to food security and livelihoods (FSL) services:The project will collaborate with community leaders, health workers, and local organizations to identify individuals with disabilities within the target areas. This will involve household assessments, direct outreach, and consultations to ensure that people with disabilities are included in all project activities, including food distribution, agricultural training, and income generation.A comprehensive needs assessment will be conducted to identify the specific challenges faced by individuals with disabilities in accessing food, agriculture, and livelihoods support. The assessment will focus on physical, informational, and social barriers, addressing both environmental (such as lack of accessibility to farming tools or markets) and attitudinal challenges (such as stigma around disability).FSL project facilities will be designed or adapted to ensure accessibility for people with disabilities. This includes modifying distribution points, training centers, and income generation facilities with clear signage to ensure they can fully participate in all activities.Project staff and volunteers will receive training on disability inclusion, rights, and accessibility. The project will also involve community sensitization efforts to reduce stigma, encourage a more inclusive attitude, and ensure individuals with disabilities are integrated into community decision-making and project activities.These actions will ensure full participation and equitable access for individuals with disabilities, enabling them to actively engage in and benefit from the project’s food security and livelihoods interventions. Regular monitoring and feedback mechanisms will be implemented to address emerging barriers and adapt strategies accordingly.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed project recognizes its potential environmental impact and incorporates comprehensive mitigation measures and actions to minimize adverse effects: Before implementation, the project will conduct a detailed environmental assessment to identify potential risks from activities such as land preparation, construction of water and sanitation facilities, and the introduction of irrigation systems. This assessment will also examine the environmental impact of livestock and fishing activities, guiding the development of targeted mitigation strategies to address any concerns.The project will promote sustainable agricultural techniques aimed at reducing soil degradation, conserving water, and minimizing the use of chemical inputs. Farmers will be trained in organic farming practices, crop rotation, and the use of natural fertilizers. These practices aim to preserve soil fertility and prevent contamination from pesticides.To combat deforestation and soil erosion, the project will encourage the use of water-efficient irrigation methods, as well as mulching and organic matter recycling. These practices will help retain soil moisture, reduce water usage, and prevent erosion caused by heavy rainfall.A waste management system will be implemented to address organic waste from agricultural activities and ensure safe handling of hazardous materials such as pesticides. Plans for managing solid waste will also be put in place to prevent environmental pollution from agricultural and household waste.Environmental education will be central to the project, focusing on sustainable practices and the environmental impact of human activities. Workshops on waste segregation, composting, and non-chemical pest control will empower communities to make informed decisions, fostering environmental stewardship.Regular monitoring will be conducted to assess the environmental impact of the project, with particular attention to soil health, water availability, and ecosystem integrity. Adjustments will be made as needed to ensure that the environmental impact is minimized, and mitigation measures are effective.Through these strategies, the project aims to minimize its environmental footprint, foster sustainable development, and enhance resilience among vulnerable populations in conflict-affected areas. This approach aligns with best practices in environmental management for humanitarian projects, balancing immediate needs with long-term environmental sustainability.
