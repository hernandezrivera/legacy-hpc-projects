Project Summary

In partnership with the Food Security Cluster in 2019, REACH proposes to bolster the availability of information in the realm of livelihoods regarding the restoration of fragile livelihoods of returning and vulnerable families with an emphasis on agriculturally focused interventions to support the FSC in making evidence-based decisions regarding the impact of livelihoods and resilience interventions.
With the primary objective of informing nationwide agricultural livelihoods and resilience programming that targets conflict-affected areas and returning populations in Iraq, REACH will develop three new assessment activities in partnership with the Food Security Cluster, namely: a) Returnee case studies tracing types of costs and amounts incurred during the return process; b) Third party monitoring of the Agricultural livelihoods interventions; c) Pilot Village Level Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments (MSNA) assessing the impact of livelihoods interventions implemented at the village level and remaining needs and gaps.
Under these activities, the following outputs may be developed, according to need:
a) Short Returnee case study report with maps and infographics;
b) Final comparative report of the third-party monitoring of the Agricultural livelihoods interventions by partners together with baseline and midline presentations/workshops with FSC and partners;
c) Pilot Village level Multi Sectorial Needs Assessment (MSNA) factsheets comparative report;
d) Presentations as needed;
e) Clean, anonymized datasets of the MSNA and Monitoring exercise;
f) Village Level MSNA Online dashboard.
Due to the nature of REACH activities which are focused on informing the humanitarian community and coordination structures no direct target beneficiaries were specified under the “Clusters” section as the exact catchment areas and populations will be specified based on the coordination with FS cluster and/or stakeholders. It is however expected that the intervention will directly benefit the humanitarian community at large, thanks to the production of outputs which will be available to all clusters and partners. Internally displaced persons living in both informal and formal sites / camps will indirectly benefit from improved information management enabling them to access information regarding the protection concerns, types of costs and amounts incurred during the return process, and average costs after returning in their areas of origin, in the long-term enabling the voluntary, safe, and dignified returns. Similarly, returnees will indirectly benefit from improved humanitarian interventions’ monitoring and referral system between Livelihoods, Food Security and Protection sectors/clusters.
Findings will be triangulated with complimentary assessments, such as host community assessments and market surveys conducted by REACH and others in order to contribute to humanitarian strategic planning. Moreover, REACH will make sure that all assessment activities are coordinated and complementary to other initiatives, to avoid any duplication of resources or activities through consultation with Sector/Cluster (Food Security and Emergency Livelihoods) coordination mechanisms and humanitarian partners. In particular, the proposed assessment will feed into the Livelihoods and Cash for Work programming, informing the 2019/2010 strategies through comprehensive needs assessment and interventions monitoring.

Needs

2019 will see the humanitarian context shifting from previous years, as active conflict against ISIL has largely ended. However, 6.5 million are still in need of assistance, according to the estimates of 2019 HNO. Out of these, 2 million remain living in IDP camps and informal settlements across Iraq. Significantly, 2018 have seen slowdown in the returns to areas of origins which is likely to continue in 2019 with the barriers to return not easily fixable, including lack of financial means to return, damage to housing or property, and security related concerns.Although intentions’ surveys highlight regional variances, several themes can be highlighted across IDP populations in and out of camp as barriers to return persist and these include (in different weighing depending on areas of origin and in/out of camp displacement): 1) security related concerns (lack of security forces, fear of discrimination), 2) damaged or destroyed homes, 3) lack of livelihood opportunities and in particular in the Mosul Response camps 4) lack of finance. Other factors include not sufficient basic services in areas of origin, the presence of explosive hazards and challenges related to the social cohesion. Among these, the challenges with access to food appear as a key barrier to return mainly related to limited economic resources, physical/logistical constraints and low quality of food, as per 2018 CCCM-REACH Intentions Surveys. Even though nearly 4 million individuals have returned to their homes in eight governorates, including in the city Mosul retaken by Iraqi forces in 2017 throughout 2018, and despite major efforts being made by the Government of Iraq and the Kurdistan Regional Government, many returning populations and vulnerable households not covered by the food based social protection systems, continue to be in need of support in ensuring access to food and basic services. As the Food Security Cluster underscores, these populations are often returning to agriculturally rich and active areas without sufficient means as tools, seeds, support, and access to credit/rehabilitation schemes. If the recovering livelihoods are not supported there is a risk that returnees will adopt negative coping strategies to remain in their area of origin. In light of the impact that these populations have undergone, analysis over time is required of standards of living and access to basic services and livelihoods opportunities. Particular effort is therefore needed to expand direct and remote data collection in newly accessible areas to provide more robust assessments of the needs and gaps for mixed population groups.In light of the significant information gaps related to the lack of/limited knowledge on the costs associated with returning to areas of origin (AOO) – from the point of departure until arrival to the AOO as well as the extent to which households rely on debt or borrowing to meet these costs, a clearer understanding of returns costs and means can help humanitarian actors in the provision of cash-based assistance and livelihoods programming. Analysis country-wide and over time is critical to better understand the needs of different crisis-affected population groups to support a principled, effective and inclusive strategic coordination and evidence-based humanitarian action at the national, regional, governorate, and local level.

Activities

In support of the one of the Food Security Cluster’s 2019 objectives - to improve quality of the response based on evidence, capacity building, and strong coordination with national stockholders, REACH will provide critical information management support to the cluster with the following activities. Data collection will be conducted by experienced REACH field teams, with the support of the Cluster to facilitate access and technical guidance in the design of research questions and indicators. 1: Returnee case studies: Tracing the cost of returnREACH proposes to conduct a series of case studies, following IDP families from camp and non-camp settings to their Areas of origin, initially targeting peri-urban and rural setting which are traditionally agriculture reliant communities. These qualitative studies will provide indicative, yet nuanced information regarding the types of costs and amounts incurred during the return process, and average costs after returning (e.g. debris removal, shelter repair, basic needs like food and utilities, or educational costs). It will also attempt to gather information on protection issues faced along the journey, such as issues related to documentation and reintegration into the community upon return.The assessment will employ a qualitative approach through remote, phone-based interviews with selected households (10-15 HHs for the initial pilot) at multiple stages between departure and return. Enumerators will conduct interviews using a semi-structured questionnaire, and will debrief the assessment team at the end of each data collection day. Under this activity, following outputs will be delivered:a) Short case study report with maps and infographics;b) Presentations as needed.2: Agricultural livelihoods interventions monitoringAs one of the Cluster’s objectives for 2019 is to help restore fragile livelihoods through provision of agricultural inputs and services, REACH proposes to conduct third party monitoring of these interventions to evaluate their impact and contribute to improving programming. Building on prior REACH experience with post-distribution and cash assistance monitoring activities, the proposed monitoring of livelihoods interventions – such as cash for work, in-kind support, and cash grants to restart small businesses – will employ a mixed methods and longitudinal approach consisting of a baseline, midline, and endline assessment. The exercise will apply a mixed methodology of representative survey and Focus Group Discussions administered to beneficiaries of livelihoods assistance within 30 days, 120 days, and 180 days of receiving assistance, stratified by type of assistance received. Baseline and FGD data collection will be conducted in person, while midline and endline surveys will be conducted by phone. Under this activity, following outputs will be delivered:a) Baseline and midline presentations/workshops with FSC and partners;b) Final comparative report;c) Clean, anonymized datasets.Activity 3: Village Level Multi-Sectoral Needs AssessmentsUsing the multi-sectoral, Cluster-validated indicators from the MCNA, REACH will adapt and scale this nationwide assessment down to a granular level, targeting a diverse array of villages across Iraq with predominant pre-conflict agricultural sectors. This will be a pilot activity, focusing on several villages selected in consultation with the Cluster, with diverse populations. Objectives will be two-fold: firstly, to assess the extent to which livelihoods interventions implemented at the village level are effectively responding to multi-sectoral needs of conflict-affected populations, and second, to help FSC and partners implement locally tailored, context-sensitive interventions. The assessments will employ a quantitative approach, based on a structured questionnaire administered to a random sample of households in each village. Findings will be statistically representative at the village level with a 95% confidence level and 5% margin of error. Following the pilot, these assessments will be scaled up through the inclusion of FSC partners in the implementation of data collection, wherein REACH will train partners on the tool and methodology, and support coordination of data collection and processing. Data will then be consolidated and shared through an online, inter-agency dashboard. Under this activity, following outputs will be delivered:a) Village level factsheets;b) Comparative report covering all pilot villages;c) Presentations as needed;d) Clean, anonymized datasets;e) Online dashboard.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation Measure 1: It is assumed that REACH will be able to access each location. Authorization from local authorities and local leaders will be sought for each location prior to data collection. In case the access challenges occur in certain parts of the country, for example Anbar, the data collection will include combination of direct, in-person interviews and remote, phone-based interviews.Mitigation Measure 2: Should security deteriorate, project areas would be switched to those still accessible and/or trainees would be encouraged and subsidized to travel to accessible areas, if feasible. ACTED security protocol shall be applied.Mitigation Measure 3: Regular security context follow-up to accurately assess any changes to the risk level in areas of operation. Close collaboration with relevant stakeholders to ensure access to targeted sites.Mitigation Measure 4: ACTED and REACH enforces the use of track 24 with speed limits and daily incident logs, combined with driver training to mitigate this risk. Pre-planning of missions, including security clearance for movements of the program teams, take into consideration the types and quality of the roads.Mitigation Measure 5: Regular security context follow-up to accurately assess any changes to the risk level in areas of operation. Security / evacuation plans established.Mitigation Measure 6: For locations where the risk of security incidents is high, ACTED establishes movements in convoy with multiple communication mechanisms, tracking capabilities based on GPS and Thuraya Satellite phone systems as redundant communications, as well as hourly communication checks in order to allow an on-time monitoring of the teams. ACTED has developed the Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) which are the individual safety and security rules to be followed when a specific incident occurs.Mitigation Measure 7: All staff are trained on Mine Risk Education and will closely follow on reports of UXO incidents to get better understanding which areas are mostly contaminated. Same knowledge will be obtained from host communities who usually have good understanding which areas are safe and which are not.Mitigation Measure 8: Regular security context follow-up to accurately assess any changes to the risk level in areas of operation.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Ensuring accountability to affected populations is at the core of REACH’s programming. All activities proposed are beneficiary-facing exercises which ensure that the needs of affected populations in formal and informal sites and areas of return are involved in the different stages of partner programmes. Their core purpose is to create site and sector specific understanding of the situation as reported by the affected population in order to better inform the humanitarian community and enable effective prioritization of assistance. All assessment activities are designed with consideration for protection concerns and sensitivity towards the population of interest. Indicators and questions will be designed with these factors in mind, including ensuring minimal collection of personal identifiers and the removal of any such data prior to dissemination of datasets. With respect to qualitative data collection, focus group discussions will be sex segregated in accordance with cultural norms and to ensure female participants are able to vocalize their opinions comfortably. Personal identifiable information will not be publicly disseminated in order to minimise/eliminate protection concerns for the assessed population. All data will be aggregated to a location and therefore no household identifiers will be publicly visible from the reports and maps. Where relevant, sensitive protection information will be passed on to the protection Cluster, in order to support interventions and planning. If agencies request the raw data containing household identifiers, then the sensitive name/contact details will be removed and replaced with a unique key so that the identifier information can be reconnected at a later date, based on the respect of protection standards. If there is a further request for the names, then REACH will contact OCHA as the coordination body to examine whether the requesting agency can receive the identifier data or not and under which conditions.REACH collects personally identifiable data only for specific purposes. REACH will include a request for consent within assessments to share personally identifiable information collected, which will only occur in the following circumstances and with the following parties:a) For REACH internal use for the purposes of data management (i.e. avoiding duplicate data collection);b) With donor / partner organisation where data-sharing agreement exists, and only for humanitarian purposes;c) With partner organisation and/ or agency where specific and urgent need occurs, for example urgent medical needs;d) With the owner (i.e. the assessed party) of the information if requested. REACH has designed and will implement the current proposal using an approach which contributes significantly to gender equality in partner programming. The research activity specifically allows the disaggregation of all data by gender and vulnerable sub-sets in order to inform programming that addresses the needs of unaccompanied women and households with particularly vulnerable individuals such as young girls, pregnant or lactating women, and elderly and disabled persons. Based on the data provided by REACH, partners will be able to tailor gender specific activities to the specific needs of these groups by providing such services as: gender-segregated information sessions, gender-segregated distribution lines, and gender-segregated toilet and shower facilities. Due to the limits of characters more information on the accountability to affected population can be received upon request.
