Project Summary

Through the proposed intervention, SIF will directly contribute to Objective 2 of the Shelter Sector’s Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Syria by supporting the sustainable rehabilitation of housing and related public infrastructures in Duma, Nashabiyeh and Arbin (Rural Damascus), Quneitra and Khan Arnaba (Quneitra), Karnaz, Suran and Muhardeh (Hama Governorate) and Eastern Kwaires and Jabel Saman (Aleppo Governorate). These localities were selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and SIF ﬁeld assessments, with all the localities being rated more than 3 out of 6 on the 2020 HNO severity scale.
SIF has a long past experience in rehabilitation of damaged houses and public facilities with OCHA and UNHCR in Syria. In 2019, SIF rehabilitated a total of 1,300 apartments and 16 schools, and provided a further 1,630 “doors and windows” shelter packages throughout the country. Approvals necessary for the intervention are now under discussion for 2020.
After consultations with local authorities and municipalities of each targeted sub-district, SIF decided to rehabilitate 1,300 damaged houses in Adra Al-Balad (100), Nashabiyeh (100), Zamalka (100), Arbin (100), Hamidieh (50), Mashara (150), Qahtanyeh (50), Karnaz (200), Helfaya (150) and Jabel Saman (300).

SIF is used to adopting a protection mainstreaming approach in the design and implementation of its projects. As part of the proposed action, SIF will consider threat of physical attacks and landmines in the selection of the locations, ensure clarity on HLP ownership of private houses in order to avoid eviction as well as involve local authorities in site planning and selection so as to avoid problems and ensure consent and participation from municipalities, governorates and the MoLAE. Speciﬁc needs will be addressed through including door locks and lighting, especially in WASH facilities, to better protect women and girls, particularly single women and female-headed households. SIF will also ensure that bathrooms are accessible to persons with disabilities and older persons, and safe for small children. Housing will be secured for unaccompanied children and, whenever possible, SIF will proceed to referrals to ensure the monitoring of children’s situation by specialized agencies. Finally, SIF will take into account local capacities and encourage the contractors in recruiting workers from local communities.

4 - Needs

SIF conducted needs assessment from December 2019 to January 2020 i n coordination with SARC, governorates and municipalities. SIF identiﬁed a high number of damaged schools with 4 in Shafuniyeh, 2 in Qahtanieh, 3 in As-Saan, 11 in Mourek, 2 in Eastern Kwaires and 2 in Jebel Saman. As a result, remaining functional schools are overcrowded and many children have dropped out of school, with 2 to 20 percent of the children being reported as dropouts depending on the localities. Moreover, private houses have been severely damaged by the hostilities with 250 to 1,500 apartments being damaged depending on the localities. Unavailable housing is further limiting returnees’ movement and early recovery in the targeted communities. For those populations living in urban or rural areas with buildings or houses severely impacted by the conﬂict, protection risks are important especially for vulnerable groups such as women, children, elderly and persons with disabilities (PwDs). Women and girls face risks of sexual exploitation and abuse as appropriate security standards cannot be ensured inside the buildings (no or broken doors and windows, lack of lockable doors, etc.) while PwDs and elderly are not granted suitable access to housing facilities.

5 - Implementation of activities

SIF will rely on a contractor to achieve the rehabilitation of the schools and damaged houses in the targeted areas of intervention. Contractor selection will be based on SIF Standards of Procedures (SoPs) in order to ensure transparency and accountability of the selection process. As part of this proposed intervention, SIF will coordinate with municipalities, Ministry of Local Administration and Environment, Ministry of Education and Syrian Arab Red Crescent to conduct the assessments, select beneﬁciaries, obtain the necessary approvals, achieve the works and issue oﬃcial acceptance certiﬁcates for hand over. A period of guarantee will also be provided to each owner and public facility in case some repairs would be needed after rehabilitation works are carried out. The project will be complementary to WASH, Education and Protection interventions in the targeted areas which will aim at providing the communities with functional sewage systems, non- formal education, psychosocial support, gender-based violence awareness sessions and risk education in order to foster communities’ early recovery and resilience. The activities will be monitored on a daily basis by SIF Shelter technical team through ﬁeld visits, data collection and reporting to SIF coordination, HQ and donors to report progress, so as to highlight areas of concerns and ensure good completion of the works. Through monthly project and budget follow-up, SIF will ensure to abide by standards of project implementation and fund resources are being effectively used. Participatory approach, through regular structured focus group discussions with individuals of different ages, gender and backgrounds, particularly those with speciﬁc needs on shelter issues (e.g. persons with disabilities and elderly), will ensure that any protection concerns highlighted are discussed and mitigated. MEAL team will also set up a mechanism for complaints and appeals, ensuring that men and women are both comfortable with it.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from property owner, and/or exposing beneﬁciaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitated. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: SIF will not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless an agreement is secured with the owner, and based on a proof of registered HLP ownership and veriﬁed ownership claims. 2. Risk of conducting rehabilitation of building with poor structural integrity and/or not fully cleared from land- mines and ERWs. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: A thorough shelter assessment and infrastructural technical assessment of the buildings that are aimed to be rehabilitated will be conducted; interventions with local authorities that might be aware of security issues in the neighborhood will be coordinated; ensure that ERWs clearance is conducted if needs be; check with other actors working in the same area to understand what kind of issues they encountered. 3. Risk of increased community tension due to unclear beneﬁciary selection criteria, or inﬂuence by local authorities leading to a feeling of discrimination and the most vulnerable being left out. Likelihood: high. Impact: medium. Mitigation: standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners; ensure beneﬁciary selection criteria is uniform/agreed among sector members and with the government and is clearly communicated widely within a community and amongst different groups; engage with community leaders, to raise awareness of selection criteria in the community; establish complaint mechanism at community level; deliver training to humanitarian staffs.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Risk of increased inﬂation rate. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigation: SIF has already included the inﬂation rate in the budget; in case of continuous inﬂation SIF will adjust the BoQs accordingly. 2. Security situation worsens and prevents the conduction of the works. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: regular monitoring of the security situation by the Logistics and Security Manager; contingency plan in place at ﬁeld and coordination level; preparedness of SIF team. 3. Works achieved by the contractor are not of good quality and are not sustainable. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: selection of contractors based on SIF previous experience; capitalization of NGOs over their past experience with contractors in the areas; coordination with local authorities (i.e. MoLAE, Governorate and municipalities); provision of a guarantee period; inspection of the works by provisional and ﬁnal acceptance committees (composed of certiﬁed engineers) to validate the good quality of the achieved works. 4. The activities have a negative environmental impact. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: all executed activities will comply with the global safety and protection measures and SIF will make sure that environment-friendly materials and products are included in the BoQs.
