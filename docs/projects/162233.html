Project Summary

According to the nutrition cluster 18 provinces out of 23 have high prevalence of SAM and bear more than 80% of the national burden which is estimated at 462,166 children under 5. ong which UNICEF targets to reach 345,100 (75%) in the Sahel belt of Chad. As part of UNICEF overall nutrition response plan to the food insecurity and nutrition crisis in the Sahel belt of Chad, the project is planned to improve quality of SAM treatment while supporting preventive actions at both facility and community level with equity in access, and service delivery. UNICEF lifesaving interventions will focus on scaling up treatment of severe acute malnutrition on the 18 priority provinces with high prevalence of global acute malnutrition (above 15%) or severe acute malnutrition (above 2%) identified by the Nutrition Cluster . This will includes provision of much needed supplies and equipment, capacity building of frontline health staff and technical support within the health system. At the community level, the actions will integrate early detection of growth faltering and referral while building the capacity of mothers and caregivers on essential nutrition actions including adequate young child feeding practices to prevent all forms of malnutrition.This action will also contribute to the measles response in seven (7) provinces.

Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

NeedsAccording to the most recent national nutrition SMART survey carried out in August 2019, Chad is facing an alarming prevalence of wasting, with the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate at 12.9% [12.1 – 13.7], Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) rate of 2.9% [2.6 – 3.4], underweight at 24.0% [22.8- 25.2] and stunting at 32.0% [30.6 - 33.4].12 out of 23 provinces have 30% or higher chronic malnutrition prevalence. In total, 18 provinces, mainly located in the Sahel belt have GAM rate above 15% and/or SAM rate above 2% ( Barh El Ghazel, Batha, Borkou, Chari Baguirmi, Ennedi East, Ennedi West, Guéra, Hadjer Lamis, Kanem, Lake, N'Djamena, ouaddai, Salamat, Sila, Tibesti, Logone occidentale, Tandjile and Wadi ﬁra).The GAM rate showed significally different between boys (14.8%) and girls (10.9%) while SAM rate is at 3.5% f and 2.3% for boys and girls respectively. Wasting (19.5%) and underweight (32.5%) are higher among children 12-23 months in comparison to other age group. Children under five month are the least affected by chronic malnutrition and underweight. With regard to micronutrient deficiency, the SMART results showed that over 44.7% of childbearing women are anemic while 40.6% of pregnant women are anemic. In Chad, the key drivers of malnutrition include inadequate Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices and poor hygiene and access to water. These two factors, compounded with recurrent food and nutrition insecurity, leave households in a perennially precarious situation. As a consequence, in addition to the numbers of children already malnourished, many are near the borderline and risk falling into malnutrition.Based on all of the above data, it is estimated that over 462,166 children will be severely malnourished this year. Taking into consideration the alarming level of food insecurity, the nutrition sector does not anticipate any improvement on the malnutrition rate or may even further deteriorate during the hunger gap season in the Sahel belt, possibly leading to higher mortality rates. There is therefore an urgent need to scale up life-saving nutrition interventions in order to address unmet needs and prevent associated mortality and morbidity."ActivitiesProcurement and timely distribution of ready to Use Therapeutic Foods and essential medicine; Training or refresher training of health professionals on the national protocol for the treatment of acute malnutrition and supply management system and on IYCF counselling; Conduct mass screenings of MUAC through community health workers and caregivers for early detection and treatment; Support monthly coordination meetings as a forum to review program implementation and identify issues and challenges at national and provincial levels; Organize quarterly joint monitoring and supervision visits to the nutrition units in collaboration with local health authorities; Provide communication materials and tools to the supported health facilities (anthropometric equipment, training materials, stock management tools); Train 250 CHWs on interpersonal communication and sensitization.Indicators- 460,406 Children under five are screened for malnutrition- 345,100 SAM children admitted and received quality therapeutic treatment- 991 Health centers have improved capacity to detect and treat SAM cases - 212,850 caregivers are improved knowledge on Essential nutrition action- 90% person of children affected by measles supplemented by Vitamin A
