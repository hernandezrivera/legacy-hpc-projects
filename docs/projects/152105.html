Project Summary

The aim of the project is to improve access and quality of preventive and curative services with static and mobile comprehensive primary health services for affected IDPs and host communities, women and men, girls and boys, in Bama (Bama town), Gamboru-Ngala (Gamboru host community and Ngala IDPs ) , Dikwa (Bulabulin IDPs camp) and Magumeri (Magumeri ward) LGAs. INTERSOS intends to maintain his current intervention in the locations above mentioned , running 10 PHC and supporting 2 department of a Genaral Hospital. The intervention is targeting both IDPs and Host-communities, and its mobile and outreach approaches intend to provide services to newly arrived IDPs in the existing camps and to hard-to-reach populations.

Needs

The current population of Magumeri LGA is estimated to be 213,975 of which 42,795 are children under the age of five years. INTERSOS catchment population from the seven accessible wards is 123,100 and more than 15% of them are hosted in Gajiganna ward. INTERSOS has continued to respond to the health needs of the Magumeri population since August 2017 and is one of the partners providing health care services in 6 out of the 7 accessible wards. In Gajiganna, INTERSOS is one of the primary partners providing primary health care services. Other partners in the area are WHO, IRC and AAH. In May 2018, the same HIS showed that among children <5, Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD) and Malaria were the leading causes of morbidity at 37% and 31.6% respectively while Acute Respiratory Infections (ARI) followed closely at 22% and bloody diarrhea was at 5%. A lot of information dissemination, behavior change, health promotion activity as well as ensuring the availability of these services is required to improve the uptake of sexual and reproductive health services. The population of Dikwa is 64,030 individuals (DTM XXIV) and INTERSOS is one of the 5 humanitarian actors that operates primary health care in Dikwa. In addition, INTERSOS is also one of the two partners operating within the host community in Bulabulin, targeting 17,526 individuals. Dikwa has been experiencing an influx of new arrivals (in July 2,597) putting extra burden on the existing health services. INTERSOS was also carrying out mobile clinics within the reception center, adapting the frequency of service provision and have set an outreach point in the new transit center set by IOM. The activities done by the mobile team are general consultations, ANC, minor dressing, nutritional screening, hygiene promotion. From INTERSOS data and reporting, the average number of consultations per week is 400 cases, and leading causes of morbidity in Dikwa are malaria (33%), AWD (12%) and ARI (31%). In Bama , a part from the relocation of more than 75,000 individuals in the host community, the IDPs population rose significantly from 15,272 in January, 2018 to the current 26,598 persons (IOM CCCM) with 6,873 persons living in the open without shelter (as at 17th August, 2018) . This is mainly due to high influx on new arrivals arriving mostly from inaccessible nearby villages.This situation had overstreched the capacity of health actors , and due to the dire humanitarian situation aggravated by high number of new arrivals and returnees in Bama, there has been an uproar about high malnutrition and mortality rates in Bama. As the rest of Borno, Bama IDP camp has been affected by cholera outbreak. Military counter‐insurgency operations continue to trigger huge population displacements in Borno State and recurrent insecurity incidents in LGAs such as Ngala led to increase of population movement. Some returness from Cameroun are settling in Gamboru Host Community and Ngala. INTERSOS is one of the 2 Health partners runing health services in Gamboru. More than 3,000 consultations are conducted monthly and more and more severe cases requiring further management have been seen , mostly severe malaria cases. 2,578 cases of Malaria have been reported in in the HF from April to September. Ngala ISS Camp has experienced a huge number of new arrivals, more than 3,000 as per August update. The population of the camp is estimated 40,697 (DTM XXIV), and cholera outbreak is ongoing in the camp, 1,010 cases admitted and 18 deaths reported as per 23 October 2018 update. MSF Switzerland is the main health actor providing PHC service along with FHI360 and UNICEF. The influx of new arrivals, the ongoing cholera outbreak have a huge impact in the capacity of providing quality services. With regards to the GBV, the main issues highlighted were the lack of awarness among women regarding GBV, the lack of preparedness of facilities in regards of GBV leading to the poor quality of the services offered to GBV cases.

Activities

To provide comprehensive primary health care services (Communicable diseases, MCH, including minimum package of SRH, EPI services, SGBV, NCD services, MHPSS), there is need rehabilitate and maintain health facilities , to ensure and maintai the correct staffing. INTERSOS intends to organize refresher training for Health staff in Magumeri, Bama, Ngala and Dikwa LGA on IMCI, ANC, PNC, BEmONC, and SGBV case management. Through a combination of formal trainings and on-job mentoring, INTERSOS protection and medical teams will enhance capacity on SGBV case management and use of the referral pathway. In addition, on-job training on GBV guiding principles and basic protection concepts for working with survivors and referral will be conducted in both health facility, mobile clinics and outreach sites. The training will be aimed at increasing the accessibility and strengthening the protection measures put in place for children, women, elderly and in particular GBV survivors with community engagement. INTERSOS will continue provision of primary health services via static and existing health facilities , parallell self-standing emergency health facilities and mobile/outreach services which will include outpatient consultation of children under 5 and above 5, and adults, ANC and PNC in all the locations if needed. INTERSOS will scale up the provision of health care to the beneficiaries living in newly accessible or hard to reach areas in Magumeri LGA or new arrivals newly located in the camps with main focus on the vulnerable group of children under 5 and an integrated SRH outreach services for pregnant and Lactating women. INTERSOS will focus on strengthening of the referral system from community to Health Facilities or to Secondary Health facilities in the respective LGAs he will operate.Through the proposed action, INTERSOS will ensure that this referral pathway is strengthened. In Magumeri, the pediatric and SRH departments of the General Hospital is currently being supported by INTERSOS, which is focused on stabilization center care as well as inpatient care for children under-5 and this is being done in collaboration with the BHMB. This would be in anticipation of the increased referrals for SRH services that would be expected from the various outreach sites to be supported by the proposed intervention. INTERSOS will emphasize the community sensitization on prevention of communicable diseases (e.g. diarrhea, measles and Malaria), GBV and the importance of vaccination. Community hygiene and health promotion outreach sessions will be conducted in all the locations through the distribution of information, with culturally accepted IEC materials. Sensitization campaigns and health education sessions will focus on health and hygiene key messages, GBV related prevention messages,to raise awareness among community member. For a effective provision of comprehensive primary health care services (Communicable diseases, MCH, EPI services, NCD services) in all the locatoins, INTERSOS will ensure provison of adequate support in medicines, medical material and equipment according to a standard list to be adapted to the target to enable management of common diseases and SRH cases. INTERSOS will maintain the monitoring and reporting through surveillance network system (EWARS), the disease pattern in INTERSOS catchment area, to identify major endemic and potential epidemic diseases for interventions in collaboration with the state ministry of health and other actors in the event of an outbreak, and will use the existing SMOH reporting tools. INTERSOS will ensure that its reporting mechanism is gender-responsive, and will analyze data dis-aggregate by sex and age to identify any risks associated with service access. The clinical staff will continue to be given refresher training on how to use the tools and submit the weekly and monthly report to the INTERSOS M&E department accordingly. INTERSOS will continue to submit EWARS reports via online platform.
