Project Summary

Provision of information, legal counselling and legal assistance on civil documentation and housing, land and property rights to people affected by crisis and displacement inside Syria. NRC will respond to the diverse problems and needs faced by vulnerable populations throughout Syria through the delivery of a multi-faceted response. The different components of NRC’s Shelter project are intended to contribute to HRP Protection Sector Objectives 1 and 2.
NRC will deliver this project through a combination of direct, partner-led and mixed modalities. Partner names have not been included in this submission for reasons of confidentiality
Geographic areas will be targeted based on the severity and scale of needs, inter-agency coordination at hub-level, and access. NRC will provide assistance to vulnerable crisis and displacement-affected populations, including IDPs, refugees, members of host communities, IDP and refugee returnees, and non-displaced households suffering the effects of the crisis. All interventions will seek to prioritize the most vulnerable households. Where appropriate, multi-sectoral vulnerability scoring techniques will be used that integrate vulnerability indicators

Needs

Legal identity, including civil documentation (CD): As a result of the long-term crisis in Syria, access to civil documentation has been severely curtailed, leaving an entire generation potentially undocumented or under-documented and unable to effectively enjoy their right to legal identity as well as other basic rights. The loss and lack of CD has consistently been identified as a major protection concern in the Syria HNOs of 2017 – 2019. This is in line with the findings of the NRC ICLA assessments carried out in non-government-controlled areas of Northwest Syria, where only 29% of surveyed respondents stated that it was possible to obtain GoS-issued documents. In these areas, access to documentation has been very limited; has not been static; and varies by location and over time, particularly given the frequency of changes in territorial control. In both South and Northwest Syria births go undocumented. In Kurdish controlled areas, IDPs face substantial challenges due to their inability to prove their legal identity or produce recognised civil documentation. The vast majority of IDPs currently present in the camps in Northeast Syria are unable to register births, marriages and deaths and confiscation of civil documentation is also a pressing issue . In government-controlled areas, official documents are available; however, procedures for acquiring such documents are complex and service providers are overwhelmed by requests as many civil registries are still non functional. Security concerns—including confidence in approaching the authorities and the need for security clearance— are barriers to accessing documentation. A huge number of people are missing civil documentation, whether because it was destroyed, lost, confiscated, were never able to access it or for other reasons. Causes may vary according to the areas. The scale of the problem is however vast and the impact on those who lack legal identity documents is profound and constitutes a hindrance to accessing humanitarian aid, education and to exercising freedom of movement. In addition, the long-term risk is that missing, expired or incomplete documentation could become a significant obstacle to return and other durable solutions. Housing, land property (HLP): HLP issues were identified as a problem by respondents in 57% of the communities surveyed for the 2018 HNO. Research conducted by NRC with IDPs paints a challenging picture of lost property documents, uncertain property ownership, informal and coerced HLP transactions weak security of tenure, property disputes and the presence of explosive remnants of war which creates further presume on land. Waves of short and long-term displacement often involve different claimants and occupants for the same parcel of land and HLP disputes, including inheritance and rental/ownership disputes, have been increasing. IDPs reported very weak security of tenure, with only 5% of respondents in the South having a written tenancy agreement (NRC 2018). The exploitation of IDPs who are renting accommodation without proper lease agreements in place and the use of negative coping strategies was widely reported during NRC consultations, including an increased risk of sexual and gender-based exploitation and abuse. The challenges identified by NRC in opposition-controlled areas can be extended to areas controlled by the Government of Syria. In addition, in these areas, the Government of Syria requires administrative and security approvals and reviews of HLP transactions, including for lease agreements, which is creating burdens on the population. NRC research and the experience of the ICLA counselling and legal assistance cases shows that women face additional barriers when trying to claim their HLP rights especially in the case of women experiencing divorce and widowhood as, for example, women are unlikely to have property documentation issued in their names.

Implementation of activities

NRC will implement activities through a two pronged approach in line with national legislation: 1. Direct assistance: Through the ICLA programme, NRC will provide target groups with information, counselling and legal assistance on legal identity, including CD and HLP, and information and counselling on essential services, employment and immigrations laws, and return. Through static and mobile information sessions, the ICLA teams will raise awareness on accessing rights and procedural requirements in respect of legal identity, HLP, essential services, employment and immigration laws, and return. Activities will be implemented by licensed lawyers. Activities in central Syria are on hold pending approval from the Government. As an added value, the ICLA Programme will also share information on return related issues and coordinate/collaborate on activities with ICLA programmes in Syrian refugees hosting countries to better support the increasing population that is spontaneously returning in their process of reintegration once back in Syria. NRC will also provide mobile and static legal counselling tailored to the particular circumstances of beneficiaries. Counselling activities may involve home visits to reach beneficiaries who are otherwise inaccessible. Legal assistance will be provided in respect of legal identity, including CD and HLP, as well as employment law to those PACD who are unable to claim and realize their rights themselves. The ICLA Programme will continue to carry out document verification to support shelter/WASH, education and livelihood interventions. 2. Increased Knowledge and Capacity to Respond: this component comprises of four result areas: Legal research and analysis: in 2019, NRC will continue to undertake legal research and studies, including desk research, using quantitative and qualitative methods, on legal identity, HLP, national employment laws and return-related topics. The research plan for 2019 includes the following: a whole of Syria women’s HLP research and research aimed at preparing for return. Such research and studies will expand knowledge, enable targeted programmatic and advocacy response to the challenges faced by displaced individuals and prepare for ICLA activities for returnees. Capacity building: The ICLA teams will provide technical assistance to duty-bearers, local and international humanitarian partners, and community leaders, through the production of in-depth fact sheets and trainings on CD and HLP-related topics, including relevant international and national laws. HLP and civil documentation coordination: The coordination of humanitarian planning and response with respect to HLP matters in Syria will be strengthened through a Damascus-based HLP Technical Working Group (TWG) (co-chaired by NRC, UNHCR and HABITAT) and a Gaziantep-based HLP TWG (co-chaired by NRC and UNHCR). The coordination of civil documentation activities is enabled by a Damascus-based TWG (co-chaired by NRC and UNHCR). These coordination hubs are tasked with the development and dissemination of technical tools and with the strengthening of reporting on HLP and civil documentation related challenges. Preparation for return: As of October 2018, the conditions in Syria were not conducive for large scale facilitated voluntary repatriation in safety and dignity. However, increasing spontaneous returns have started both in terms of IDPs (1.2 million only in 2018) as well as refugees. In 2019, the ICLA programme will strengthen preparatory work on return in coordination and collaboration with ICLA Jordan, Lebanon and Iraq. Activities will build on on-going and planned research, legal analysis and development of tools and can be an added value in supporting the reintegration of Syrian citizens once in Syria.

Protection Risk Analysis

NRC will work to minimise protection concerns and do-no-harm during activities through the following proactive measures: • Ensure that HLP and civil documentation assistance programming is based on thorough assessment and mapping of needs, where possible, and that communication with communities about solutions to civil documentation problems is as comprehensive as possible and also advises on possible risks. • Ensure personal data and confidential information of beneficiaries is strictly restricted to concerned actors and kept safe from unintended use. • Monitor increases in political tensions and work through advocacy to reduce risks to beneficiaries who secure civil documentation provided by non GoS sources in the event of reassertion of GoS control in these areas. • Advise on risks to those travelling cross-line to renew or secure civil documentation from functioning GoS services or at risk of exploitation of those paying third parties to secure civil documentation on their behalf • Provide ICLA services in times and locations that take into account physical safety needs of beneficiaries and the particular needs of women, including access to female advisors wherever possible and of persons with disabilities.
