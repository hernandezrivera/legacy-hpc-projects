Project Summary

Our programme aims to reduce the burden of malaria in highly in the highly vulnerable IDP population by delivering malaria testing and treatment to those living in Benitu and Malakal PoC with the aim of improving preparedness and response especially after an increase in transmission (usually after the rains). Malakal has a population of 24,402 residents and there are 114,683 residents in Bentiu and there are 4 and 5 sectors in each camp respectively. Through this programme, 139,085 IDPs will receive access to life saving treatment. Malaria agents will work in each sector of the camp providing malaria testing. All uncomplicated cases will be treated in the first instance and more severe cases will be referred to the health facility in the camp. All pregnant women will also be offered preventative therapy. In additional, comprehensive IEC messages will be delivered to all residents. These will focus on the promotion of LLIN use, ownership, and maintenance, messages on regularly emptying, and cleaning unsealed water containers on a weekly basis campaigns to emphasise the importance of ANC attendance to improve IPTp uptake, promotion of early treatment seeking behaviours and drug adherence.

Needs

Malaria continues to be the leading cause of illness and death in South Sudan accounting for 61% of all reported illnesses and 70% of reported deaths in 20173. Every week, approximately 77,500 people are newly infected with malaria with 64% of deaths from malaria being in children under the age of 5 years. The protracted conflict has led to mass internal displacement and the cramp living conditions in two of the largest IDP camps in the country has left those living in the camps vulnerable to infectious diseases. Here malaria is the most common cause of morbidity and mortality in all age groups, especially in children <5 years and pregnant women and previous years have witnessed unprecedented levels of epidemic proportions. There are continued threats of malaria epidemics in the camps, which have continued to increase since 2015, especially during and after the rainy season when there is often a sharp rise in transmission. MENTOR Initiative in partnership with UNICEF has implemented prevention activities in both the PoCs for the past few years, this has reducded transmission and avoided malaria epidemics. MENTOR has developed and established very good relations with the community and other key stakeholders on the ground. MENTOR is presently operating in Malakal, and can mobilise resources immediately, ensuring the feasibility of the implementation of activities, in line with the cluster strategies. MENTOR aims to save lives in emergencies through tropical disease control by working side-by-side with communities. MENTOR maximizes the impact of its programs through strong cooperation with corresponding clusters (WASH, Health and Nutrition) and capacity building of other operational NGOs and UN agencies alongside local health partners and affected communities. MENTOR has been operating in South Sudan since 2012, and is one of the leading experts in Vector Borne Diseases (VBDs) control, providing technical advice on both disease prevention and case management to the MoH, NMCP and implementing partners. MENTOR places the highest level of importance on ensuring close coordination and collaboration with all international and national stakeholders and partners at local and at Juba level. MENTOR encourages continuous information-sharing and joint activity planning with relevant partners to ensure maximum use of combined resources and respond to epidemiological data or changes in the operational context. Despite best efforts to reduce malaria burden through health facilities’ diagnosis and treatment, health facilities are often overwhelmed with high caseloads. Health facilities often experience stockouts or shortages of essential medicines and diagnostics- especially during peak transmission season. Additionally, as witnessed on previous occasions health facilities have been forced to close for months at a time as was the case in Bentiu in 2018. The proposed project is consistent with the cluster objectives: “Improve access and scale-up responsiveness to integrated essential healthcare needs of the vulnerable populations focusing on major causes of morbidity and mortality”. MENTOR addresses this objective through the active diagnosis and treatment of malaria and therefore increasing access and scale up of healthcare services. This will reinforcing and enhance the surveillance system of any disease and offer community based surveillance. There is also the integration of IEC messages across sectors on malaria prevention (risk communication).

Activities

Malaria diagnosis- Malaria agents will be locally recruited, properly trained to diagnose and if necessary treat malaria among the IDP population in the camp. Severe malaria cases will be referred to the local health facility. There will be 40 trained malaria agents in Bentiu and 20 in Malakal. They will also be trained to spot other high priority illnesses such as AWD and cases will be referred to the local health facility. Pregnant women will be offered IPTp. IEC - IEC will focus on: the promotion of LLIN use, ownership, and maintenance, messages on regularly emptying, and cleaning unsealed water containers on a weekly basis campaigns, avoiding stagnant water and pools and also to emphasise the importance of ANC attendance to improve IPTp uptake, promotion of early treatment seeking behaviours and drug adherence.
