Project Summary

In 2024, Cox’s Bazar hosts over 1 million Rohingya refugees, with children comprising 52% (4,98,855 nos.) of the population and facing heightened risks of violence and exploitation. Within the camps, vulnerable groups including unaccompanied and separated children, female-headed households, and children with disabilities of all gender are particularly susceptible to trafficking, child marriage, child labour, and neglect. These dangers are compounded by restrictive camp conditions and frequent natural hazards, such as monsoons and cyclones, which increase stress and limit access to safe spaces and essential services. The proposed program in Cox’s Bazar aims to address the urgent child protection (CP) issues affecting Rohingya refugees and the surrounding host communities. By adhering to global standards, this initiative is focused on creating and strengthening protective environments for children, adolescents, and women, specifically within Camps 1E, 1W, 4, 4ext, 5, and 13 as well as in the neighboring Jaliapalong unions. Over the next two years, the program is expected to directly benefit approximately 18400 children (8100 boys and 10,300 girls) and 6420 adults (3,010 men and 3,410 women) including 797 person with disabilities. The indirect benefits will extend to the wider camp and host populations as community awareness and protective practices are enhanced. A crucial element of the program is the expansion of specialized case management services for at-risk children, including unaccompanied minors and those targeted by armed groups. This targeted intervention aims to mitigate the severe protection risks faced by these vulnerable groups. In addition, comprehensive capacity-building sessions will be conducted to train a variety of stakeholders, including community-based child protection volunteers, adolescents, child protection actors, camp management officials, government representatives, and law enforcement agencies. This training will deepen their understanding of child protection, and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), fostering a standardized approach to protection services across all involved agencies. The program will also integrate protection mainstreaming, focusing on inclusion and environmental considerations. ECD for the 3-5 years old child and positive parenting for the caregivers, group and individual psycho-social support, recreational and life skill secttions for adolescents and youth is tailored as project activities.
To further engage the community, the initiative will employ the integrated Girl Shine approach, which specifically targets girls, alongside the establishment of community-based child protection committees. These platforms will host awareness-raising sessions that address , sexual and reproductive health (SRH), and positive parenting through interactive activities. Digital tools such as Child Protection Information Management System (CPIMS), KoBo, and ComCare will be utilized for efficient information management, while playful life skills development for adolescents will be facilitated through interactive digital boxes. Service providers will receive ongoing mentoring and coaching to ensure the quality-of-service delivery. Child protection interventions will be carried out in 10 Multipurpose Child Centers (MPCs). By employing evidence-based tools like the Girl Shine program and adolescent's forum, the initiative aims to promote positive behavior change regarding gender norms, risk mitigation and prevention. Ultimately, this will contribute to fostering sustainable, community-driven changes and enhancing the safety and well-being of children and women in these vulnerable settings. For system strenthening CB will actively participate in Child protection monitoring assessment.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The J-PMR, 2024 (Jan-Sep) shows that 101 cases reported related to the child rights monitoring, comprising the serious child rights violations perpetrated by the armed groups. Both boys and girls are at risk of being trafficked or recruited by armed groups. Community tensions, violence, and the presence of armed groups further expose children to harm. Parents and caregivers ongoing fears for their children's safety, reflect a deep-seated need for improved security and protection mechanisms across the camps. There is a heightened risk in Child labor , child marriages, neglect and psychological distress. Due to poverty, lack of vocational training and access to education center, Child labor (7.58%), Neglect (31%), Psychological distress (11.57%) , child marriages (4,54%), reported in the 2024 CPIMS+ from the period of January-September. In the CPSM 2024 shows that due to poverty, lack of vocational training opportunities, strict time in education center and carrying over caregiver responsivities because of large family member size are behind child labor. For boys this includes carrying loads, working as labor and for girls this is generally looking after the household chores. Whereas the reason behind the child marriages are cultural norms (18.69%) poverty (17.89%), romantic relationships (17.34%), food scarcity (7.28%) family pressure and heightened risk (13.13%) , forced marriage by armed group (5.6%) are associated. It affected boys and girls almost equally, with 15.94% of boys and 15.92% of girls impacted. The report also shows that domestic violence manifests in various forms, affecting both boys and girls and leads to severe long-term impacts on children's well-being. Physical and emotional maltreatment accounted for 7.84% of total reported cases, with 4.24% affecting girls and 3.60% affecting boys. According to CPSM KIIs, 69.15% of adults reported incidents of child neglect, while 60.57% indicated cases of domestic physical violence against children. The leading causes of neglect included lack of awareness (20.4%), domestic violence (19.5%), caregiver joblessness (18.0%), lack of resources (15.3%), cultural beliefs (13.0%), and inadequate housing and food shortages. Regarding the psychological distress there is a clear service gap. The CPIMS+ data from January to September 2024, reported psychological distress in 11.57% of all cases, with 6.20% of cases impacting girls and 5.36% impacting boys. Regarding the selection of the camp and host areas, it shows that, camp 1E, 1W , 5 has critical shortage in service gaps, whereas camp 4 , and jaliapalong has moderate concern and 4 extension has adequate service. As caritas is already implementing in 1E, 1W, 4, 4ext, 5 and Jaliapalong area and existing project will be ongoing and rest of the areas will be expand with the service. However, this project will be integrating with the other sectoral projects of caritas Bangladesh. Such as at 1E and 1W with GBV and education, at 4 with WaSH and Education service, at camp 4 extension with GBV and community based protection, at camp 5 with Education service at camp 13 with Shelter projects and at Jaliapalong union with DRR and livelihood projects of Caritas Bangladesh.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

CB is deeply committed to addressing the unique and substantial safety and dignity risks faced by Rohingya individuals, particularly those with heightened vulnerabilities such as children, adolescent girls, boys, and persons with disabilities will be engaged by non-discriminated way. The location selection for the facility based services focused on the accessibility of the all gender, age and diverstity group of adolescent and children. The establishment of Multipurpose Child and Adolescent Centers prioritizes safety, strategically located away from dark footpaths, risky slopes, and isolated areas. Moreover, these centers feature separate, clearly labeled latrines for girls and boys, ensuring a secure and dignified environment. Confidentaility and safety of the child will be maintained appropriately aligining the do no harm approach. During the project activity implemnettaion CB consider meaningful participatory approach consisting of dceecion making, risk mitigation , involve in project designing and monitoring of the project activity. A comprehensive approach such as mainstream with WaSH, shelter and education sector that guarantee equitable access to services. Caritas strives to ensure active participation, empowerment, and the strengthening of local communities. The community-based planning approach actively diminishes risks and hazards while fostering resilience. This proactive approach acknowledges the dynamic nature of the situation, allowing for agile adjustments that enhance the effectiveness and relevance of the project over time. Caritas remains dedicated to mitigating risks, upholding dignity, and ensuring the well-being of Rohingya individuals through a comprehensive and adaptive humanitarian response.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project incorporates a comprehensive child friendly regular MEAL mechanism, integrating Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles. Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), and regular stakeholder consultations. Stakeholder engagement is prioritized to enhance the overall quality of protection initiatives. CB will participate in the peace and security campaign for building the awareness on child violence reduction perpetrated by armed groups. Communication materials will be developed and circulated with the consultation of Protection sectors. AAP is a core responsibility and central to Caritas Bangladesh’s (CB) mandate. Refugees participate in deciding the priorities for activities implemented by CB and lead their own initiatives through community-based mechanisms throughout the operational management cycle. CB conducts regular community consultations in all camps to identify priority issues and obtain feedback in order to adapt its protection programming as needed. CB also operates community feedback and complaints boxes, email service, and a helpline in order to provide information to and elicit feedback and complaints from refugees. CB ensuring that individual complaints are responded to and that communities are provided with collective feedback. A fundamental part of CB’s work on AAP includes its commitment to the Code of Conduct (CoC) and a zero-tolerance policy towards sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and harassment for its staff by utilizing Child safeguarding policy.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Harassment (PSEA) is a top priority for Caritas Bangladesh. As an active member of the PSEA Network, focal and alternate focal cooridnate on preventing, mitigating, and harmonizing responses to SEA/SH incidents. Caritas Bangladesh is committed to upholding the highest standards of safety and respect. The organization maintains a dedicated team for the Rohingya response and an additional team based in Dhaka. Caritas Bangladesh conducts regular PSEA training for staff, volunteers, vendors, and third parties and organizes awareness sessions for beneficiaries. These sessions are complemented by the development and distribution of PSEA materials and community consultations on Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanisms (CFRM), SEA risks, PSEA maintreaming and communication channels. Staff, volunteers, and Community-Based Child Protection Committee (CBCPC) members receive annual and as-needed training on the Code of Conduct and PSEA/SH standards and guidance note. Caritas Bangladesh emphasizes accessible, non-verbal communication in complaint and reporting systems, ensuring that the importance of reporting is understood along with the protection and reputational risks associated with SEA incidents. The PSEA and safeguarding policy, child safeguarding policy and complaint investigation guidelines of Caritas Bangladesh aims to continue using these resources to empower the community and foster a safer environment within the camps.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

To identify specific CP risks, the hazard and vulnerability mapping will be done at the beginning of the project. As part of early warning communication adoption, the climate resilient multipurpose child center will be established and used as disaster related message sharing, awareness raising on climate and environmental issues and safe space during any emergency. Also girls shine and boys committee will be trained to disseminate the early warning into their communities. Considering the environmental aspects, plastic free item will be used. For instances, no play materials will be used made with plastic. Children will be encouraged to plant trees for greenery aspects. For adolescent girls, the reusable dignity kit will be distributed, and also emergency stock will be arrange. Coordination with WaSH programs for the camp cleaning campaign. Psychological First aid (PFA) will be delivered for right after any crisis. Community volunteer, adolescent groups ad parents will be trained on trauma focused psychological support after any disaster. Also to understand the impact of climate change in CP risks, a indicator will be developed to monitor based on local and national adaptation.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

CB will conduct a vulnerability assessment through child protection monitoring system and identify the potential hazards. With the collaboration of local communities a child friendly emergency plan will be outline with clear role and responsibilities and actions to protection children in emergency. CB is part of the PERU team, so collaboration with sectors and other multiple partner will be done in the meantime. CB will arrange training on CP in emergencies (CPiE) for the staff, volunteer to ensure the smooth PFA, Case management and referral pathways. With the help of DRR team of Caritas Bangladesh a simulation and drills will be arrange at both communities and engage the community people to practice. As per the contingency plan the 4% of the total budget will be useto in kind support such as dry food items, hygiene kits, NFI (Blankets, cloths etc.) will be rapidly deployed. To reduce trafficking, exploitation in the emergency situation CB will arrange awareness session for children, caregivers and community leaders. Complain & Feedback mechanism will be function and monitored timely and appropriate manner.

But as volunteer or care givers between 17-24 years old will be targeted for essential life skills including conflict resolution, communication, decision making, empowering youth to navigating challenge will be arranged. For the ever-increasing psychological distress among the youth regarding the conflict and expolitation issues sessions on stress management, trauma awareness and emotional resilience will arrange to help to build the inner strength and cope with psychological impact of crisis situations. Interested youth will be selected at the girl shine groups and and boys engagement forum to discuss about SRH information and be the part of vocational training and led the MPC’s to access the information , support and engage in recreational activities. Activities will be arrange to equip them with the skills of raising voice for their needs and participate in community decision making.
