Project Summary

As a result of the Great Return March events, the burden on the already overstretched health system has increased to unbearable level leaving it on the verge of collapse where more than 10,000 of injuries needed hospitalization and non-trauma patients suffer from interrupted treatment and health care. However, few organizations like MAP were able to respond immediately, and the level of needs far more exceeds the provided response. Health Cluster indicated that the total funding required for interventions from 30 March until the end of 2018 is US$43.8 million, of which $22.7 million has been contributed to date, leaving a shortfall of $21.1 million. Therefore, MOH has been calling for immediate response from the health organizations working inside Gaza to assist with the provision of urgently needed drugs and disposables in order to be able to save the life of Palestinian patients in Gaza. MAP is planning to purchase a list of essential drugs and disposables to be delivered to Central Drug Stores to ensure the access of trauma and non-trauma patients to the treatment and medical care they need.

Needs

Since 30 March 2018, Palestinian casualties in the Gaza Strip have increased significantly due to the mass demonstrations taking place along the perimeter fence with Israel and, to a lesser extent, hostilities and other incidents. From the start of the ‘Great March of Return’ demonstrations on 30 March up to 6TH of September, a total of 179 Palestinians, including 29 children, have been killed; over 19,014 others have been injured while around 52 per cent of them required hospitalization.To respond to this crisis, humanitarian organizations have prioritized three areas of intervention: emergency healthcare; protection monitoring; and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). MAP simultaneously with the other Health cluster partners have responded to meet the increased emergency healthcare needs arising from the magnitude of injuries. MAP among very few organizations, was able to respond immediately by releasing all of its prepositioned drugs and disposables on 30 and 31 March 2018 to the MoH and Al Awdah hospital in addition to limb reconstruction surgical instruments. Additionally, in the past few months, MAP was able to procure 124 items of essential drugs and 134 items of consumables and to keep as prepositioned items; 18 drugs and 15 consumables. The items were prioritized by the Palestinian Ministry of Health, at a cost of $2,533,266. However, Health Cluster indicated that the total funding required for the whole interventions from 30 March until the end of 2018 is US$43.8 million, of which $22.7 million has been contributed to date, leaving a shortfall of $21.1 million.Furthermore; the current surge in humanitarian needs takes place against a backdrop of an already precarious humanitarian situation in the Gaza Strip; the health system is on the verge of collapse and there is widespread despair as conditions deteriorate and hope for a sustainable solution fade. Moreover, Gaza’s health sector is collapsing in the wake of the now 11-year blockade, the deepening intra-Palestinian political divide, the energy crisis, inconsistent and reduced payment of public sector staff, and growing shortages in medicines and disposables.On the other hands, non-trauma patients suffer because treatment and healthcare for urgent medical conditions is interrupted as the wider health system responds to the casualties from the demonstrations. Consequently, by end of September 2018, 47% of the essential drugs were at less than one month’s supply; particularly those needed for oncology, Immunity& Genetic diseases, Primary health care, and kidney dialysis in addition to those used for emergency, ICU and lifesaving surgeries leading to increased morbidity and mortality as well. Additionally, 30% of the medical supplies were at less than one month’s supply. (CDS monthly report). Subsequently, and following the continuation of the demonstrations beyond the initially expected period, as well as the volume and gravity of injuries; MAP is seeking to procure more items of essential drugs and disposables to continue supporting MOH and to ensure the availability of drugs and medical supplies.
