Project Summary

The project is designed to support the most vulnerable people with WASH services by providing sustainable solutions for water and sanitation, emphasize prevention measure to COVID 19 and reduce huge reliance on costly non-sustainable direct provision water trucking, desludging, etc. to improve living conditions of vulnerable conflict-affected HHs.
MISK IS planning to respond to 4633 HHs, 24516 individuals within 2 communities (Beeret Kaftin and Maaret Elekhwan) and 7 camps (Jabal Kelly, Ataa Alkhair3, Ataa Alkhair, Ataa Alkhair2, Al Ainaa, Dar Alkiram(Kelly) and Al Baidaa) to ensure improved, sustainable access to WASH services and supplies.
The project aims to improve sustainable solutions to provide water for 2538 HHs, 13016 individuals, 2168 Men, 2583 Women, 4076 Boys, 4189 Girls) within camps, as there is a water network within camps and is connected to a reservoir, which is fed from a fully-equipped public well that works by fuel. Through the implementation of this project, a hybrid system (solar energy, fuel) will be installed, as this will reduce operating expenses. Operating costs of the station will be supported for 7 months (35 L/per/d) and 40L/per/d within summer months(Jul, Aug Sep)
To enhance the ability of beneficiaries to hygiene, especially with the condition of COVID19, distributing 2 HKs for each family in the 3rd month and in 7thmonth and contents according to cluster guidelines. Also, HP sessions will be conducted in targeted camps, through a trained mobile team (M, F) about hygiene, COVID-19 preventions and IEC, taking into account the prevention procedures, social distancing and sterilization.
Through the implementation of project 2095 HHs, 11500 individuals, 2432 Men, 3050 Women, 2998 Boys, 3020 Girls) will be supported:
Beeret Kaftin well will be equipped, and water station will be fully maintained
Installing a hybrid operating system (solar energy + fuel), which contributes to reducing operational costs
Maintenance and expansion of water network and high reservoirs within both towns
Maintenance and expansion of sewage network within both towns
Support 100% of operation cost of water station in 1st month, decreased by 20 %, 40%, 60%. 80% in following 4 months, where the local council will cover the operation cost gap from collected fees.
After completing all rehabilitation activities for the water system, and starting operating, fees will be collected monthly from beneficiaries under the supervision of LC to secure operation and maintenance costs required to sustain the project. In order to implement proper cost recovery and tariff system, MISK will work closely with LC and beneficiaries to include them in the design and planning phase. Proper capacity building will take place for stakeholders involved in the tariff system to ensure better managing of resources and water system. HKs for each family in the 3rd and 7thmonth in conjunction with awareness campaigns of HP and COVID-19 preventions.

4 - Needs

MISK field staff conducted a rapid needs assessment to assess the WASH needs and identify potential locations to ensure a better quality response through improved, sustainable access to WASH services and supplies. Analysis of the findings shows the following:1- Identified targeted sites included 4633 HHs (24516 individuals) within 2 communities (Beeret Kaftin and Maaret Elekhwan) and 7 camps (Jabal Kelly, Ataa Alkhair3, Ataa Alkhair, Ataa Alkhair2, Al Ainaa, Dar Alkiram(Kelly), and Al Baidaa).2- There is a water network within the aforementioned camps and is connected to a high reservoir, which is fed from a fully-equipped public well that works on fuel.3- The community of Beeret Kaftin relies on water trucking, as there is no water station within the town; previously, water was pumped to the town through the Maaret Elekhwan water station, where there is a well designated for the town. Since the well was out of service, it has not been targeted by any organization as for Maaret Elekhwan wells run on fuel and need maintenance. 4- Due to the expansion of the communities of (Beeret Kaftin & Maaret Elekhwan), the overcrowding of the population and the increase in displacement to it, which overwhelmed the sanitation in the two communities, as the people complained about the unpleasant odours in some locations and the blockages in some houses and streets. Therefore, the residents demanded the restoration, maintenance, and expansion of the sewage network.5- 90% of the assessed locations lack sufficient garbage bins, which resulting in compiling solid waste on the premises and near the tents of the camp. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Inequality in distribution; Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigating Measure: Promote Establishment of adequate management plan; Promote involvement /engagement with communities in distribution planning and feedback from different groups on the package for improvement; training of staff and enforcement of code of PSEA code of conduct among partners staff.Risk: Risk of theft of NFIs. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: high. Mitigating Measure: Agencies provide feedback and accountability mechanisms to different groups of users; Work closely with all partners on the ground; Promote distribution stock between multiple warehouses in the target regions (for large quantities of stock).Risk: Unequal distribution of services inadvertently empower or strengthens the position of armed groups or other actors. Likelihood: Impact: high. Mitigating Measure: Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time; Organise joint planning culture and agree on a minimum standard; Include community and authorities in targeting; Ensure sensitive information is handled with care.Risk: Potential exposure to explosive hazard (IED/UXO/ERW) during assessment/ rehabilitation of wastewater infrastructure (WWTP, sewer networks).Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigating Measure: Work closely with Mine Action actors in the area and conduct assessments/rehabilitation works only in areas declared as cleared; Conduct safety training with staff and contractors; Add explosive hazard awareness material to your programming.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Loss of cash or assets: - mitigation measures: daily inventory, control treasury better, involve many people in this process to extend circle of suspicion.Risk 2: Unforeseen Changes in Conflict. Conflict is such that an organization cannot safely access locations, staff gets besieged in one area of control or operational assets are lost. Unlikely Moderate Stable Local information shows an increase in conflict levels, as we as direct reports from staff on the ground. Mitigation measures: Continue to gather information from a range of sources. Develop a contingency plan to work in an alternative location if the conflict continues. Continuous efforts by the access teams to gain new assurances and connections to secure humanitarian space.Risk 3: Exposure of sensitive information. Communications with anyone inside of Syria are very likely compromised and we must assume that leakage of electronic data will occur. Results will include a Threat to the detention of individuals, reputational harm, and compromise of community members or partner safety. Mitigation measures: strict procedures on staff in case there was information exposed. - Risk: COVID-19 Pandemic /Probability: highResponse: Postponing the services if there is no pandemic for a short time until safety is back or relocation if the project's activities can't be provided in the same locations.
