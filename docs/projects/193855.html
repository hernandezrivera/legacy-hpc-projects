Project Summary

In this project, AAH plans to support most vulnerable women, men, boys and girls, targeting a total of 200 household, to face the difficult socio-economic situation they are in through Multipurpose Cash Assistance (MPCA - second line response) for an estimated period of 6 months (for a transfer value of 1,081 ILS per beneficiary). The project will focus on three governorates of the Gaza Strip, namely Middle Area, Rafah and Khan Younis.
The intervention aims at supporting vulnerable households who are facing difficulties in meeting their basic needs because of insufficient income and because of their vulnerability conditions, targeting in particular individuals who are most at risk of resorting to negative coping strategies. In particular, the intervention aims to support women and female-headed households, who are in dire need according to the MSNA findings and are more at risk of resorting to coping mechanisms.
Overall, the project is aligned to the second strategic objective of the HRP and will contribute to a dignifying and multi-sectoral assistance to affected population in Gaza by offering flexibility through the use of the MPCA. This is, in line with HRP MPCA chapter objective “Vulnerable HHs in oPt affected by shocks and stresses due to conflict and natural disaster supported with MPCA are able to meet their immediate essential needs and access to lifesaving services, decreases the incidence of negative coping strategies”.
The selection of households will be based on socio-economic and vulnerability criteria using existing mechanism, in particular UVAT and MoSD data base in order to ensure coherence and coordination of the response.
To enhance the multi-sectoral nature of the project, AAH team will ensure referrals of needs not covered through this project, as needed, to relevant clusters.
AAH will ensure that the intervention complies with the CWG, PSEA and gender standards and guidelines for quality assurance and accountability. Close coordination with relevant working groups and stakeholders will be maintained throughout the project to this end.
The intervention modality has been designed according to recommendations of the CWG and to beneficiaries’ preference, as 70% of the population in Gaza has reported physical cash as a preferred modality to receive assistance (MSNA 2022).

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip is extremely susceptible to external factors. The HNO 2023 estimates that1.32 million people in Gaza in need of humanitarian assistance, which accounts for more than a half of Gaza residents. In 2021 and 2022, Gaza experienced the effect of global events and an escalation in hostilities that further exacerbated living conditions and the quality of life. As a result of these events, the Gaza population has been forced to resort negative coping strategies to respond to the consequences, thereby impacting the cycle of poverty and increasing food insecurity levels, thus further eroding resilience. In 2021, between May 10th and 21st, the escalation of the conflict resulted in a significant destruction of homes, businesses, and infrastructure, damaging the economy and degrading public services delivery. According to the Gaza Rapid Damage and Needs Assessment (RDNA) (June 2021), the damages are estimated at about $290-380 million USD. The Social Sectors (i.e Housing, Health, Education, and Social Protection and Jobs), are the most affected; with housing sector representing about 93% of the total damages. Economic losses are estimated between US$ 105 – 190 million, with the social sectors ranging between US$ 60 – 80 million. Two million Palestinians, out of which 1.4 million are in Gaza, were estimated to be moderately to severely food insecure following the May escalation, with the root cause remaining the volatile and coercive situation (i.e blockades) producing a detrimental impact on food production, availability, and economic access. The current crisis is also tied to the economic and political instability at international level, namely the financial impact of the pandemic, of which vulnerable Palestinian are still facing consequences, as well as the Ukraine war, that have recently produced a sharp increase of basic commodities prices, remarkably affecting households’ purchasing power and reduced income. 57.3% of HH reported a decrease of income in the past year according to the MSNA; the percentage is higher for women, at 62%.. In this scenario, unemployment rates are extremely high. In the MSNA, 60% of households reported that a member is unemployed and looking for a job and according to the PCBS, the unemployment rate for women in the second quarter of 2022 stands at 65.5%, while 44% is the average.. The situation gets worse in the southern and middle areas, with 70% in Deir el Balah and 65.5% in Rafah governorates. The described situation is ultimately preventing households to meet basic needs. In fact, 81% of people reported not to be able to meet their basic needs (MSNA 2022), thus impacting food security levels as 63% of the Gaza population present “moderate” to “severe” food insecurity levels (FIES indicator, MSNA 2022). Consequently, people are resorting more and more to negative coping mechanisms. According to the MSNA, 56% of people in Gaza are adopting negative coping strategy classified as “crisis” (women present higher level with 63%), while 12% as “Emergency” (LCSI), thus consistently raising protection risks.Suffering already from high levels of marginalization, discrimination and exclusion, women in Gaza have been further severely affected by the recent developments, in particular the pandemic and May escalation. In fact, the May 2021 escalation of hostilities in Gaza and the COVID-19 pandemic have reinforced pressures facing women and girls, exacerbating inequalities, inequities and discrimination, with women and girls disproportionately impacted in a multi-dimensional way. Domestic violence has registered an increase along with increased domestic responsibilities and alarming rates of psychological distress. Child marriage has been more and more used as a coping mechanism to face the economic effects of the crises. Lockdowns and consequent schools’ closure imposed a largen burden of childcare with weighted mostly on women, whose domestic tasks sharply jumped.The worsening of the conditions is not limited to the domestic sphere, but also to the economic one. A study conducted by UN Women revealed that 95% of Palestinian women in women-led micro, small, and medium enterprises reported that their businesses had been negatively impacted by the pandemic compared to prior to the pandemic (UN Women, 2021). The crises have led to an even lower female participation in the labor market; in Gaza, only 2 out of 10 women in working age are employed.In general, in the Gazan society, the work sector rights and implications historically result in women subjugation. An example is the agricultural sector, in which despite women actively contribute as workforce, less than 5% actually own agricultural property.Despite significant improvements over the past 20 years regarding women’s educational status, women, especially the young ones, remain largely excluded from a stagnant men-dominant labor market.Due to the important role of “caregivers” that has been attributed to women across all families in Gaza, women’s resilience and coping strategies are essential to supporting households. In this scenario, in Gaza women are disproportionately affected by current crisis effects, including climate change and the more recent economic impact of Ukraine war. Based on a baseline conducted by AAH in September 2022, in Middle and South governorates, over 60% of HH have a monthly income lower than 600 NIS (less than the updated MEB), and only 14% have an income higher than 1,000 NIS . The MSNA reports 16% female having a high tendency in reducing the food consumption to cope with lack of food or of income to buy it. Most households in the Gaza Strip are now buying food on credit with many families reducing the quantity of meals and the food quality, cutting out on expensive foods such as fruit and poultry meat, which are crucial for a healthy diet. 22% of women reported having more than NIS 5,000 of debts.

2 - Activities

AAH proposes to conduct the intervention through direct implementation as only one activity is envisioned. Additionally, AAH has direct contact with the community’s representatives and has already conducted successfully direct implementation of cash interventions in the past. Community leaders, groups representatives (including members of marginalized groups) will be consulted and involved prior the implementation and throughout the project through FGDs, informal interviews and meetings. AAH’s staff and social workers have extensive experience on this and knowledge of the targeted areas and its communities. Activity 1: Selection of households. The selection of households will be based on socio-economic and vulnerability criteria using existing mechanism, in particular UVAT and MoSD data base, or alternative similar criteria (which includes prioritization according to the following vulnerability criteria in line with the CWG recommendations: individuals with no other assistance from other organizations or social protection schemes, HH whose house was damaged and livelihood during recent escalations or conflict, child and female headed households, unemployed head of HH, HH with monthly income below poverty line, no access to land). To avoid duplication, AAH will cross check the eligible applicants with MoSD, Cash Working Group and Food Security Sector to ensure avoiding duplications in the response. This will ensure to avoid duplication with other organizations implementing similar activities. Additionally, by reporting to the CWG, AAH will ensure appropriateness of the implementation, avoid gap and duplications, and comply with harmonization and coordination processes.In the targeting process, prioritization will be done towards female headed HH, presence of girls and youths in the HH, including people with disabilities. To ensure gender and protection mainstreaming, appropriate consultations with relevant groups and committees will be held to ensure accessibility of the assistance to these vulnerable groups avoiding do no harm or exclusion. At the same time, the assistance will be designed and monitored by taking into account gender and protection mainstreaming and related risks. AAH Gender and Protection Focal Point will be supervising the activities in this regard in collaboration with the MEAL team. Ad hoc field visits will be conducted by AAH gender and protection focal point to ensure gender and protection checklists, protocols and policies are adequately observed by the staff. The focal point will induct staff and social workers working in the intervention to ensure they are aware of gender and protection SoPs and protocols FCM and PSEA tools and channels are designed in a gender sensitive way in order to allow women and other vulnerable groups to access them with no risk of do no harm. In all activities, AAH ensure gender balanced team on the ground to comply with social norms and to ensure women feel safe and comfortable while receiving assistance. Distribution of MPCA (Second Line Response). MPCA will be delivered to the 200 targeted households (264 men, 262 women, 286 boys and 288 girls) for an estimated 6 months, reaching 1,100 individuals. AAH will follow the recommendations of the Cash Working Group and use a transfer value of 1,081 ILS. The cash will be delivered through the service provider PalPay (Bank of Palestine) with which AAH has a framework agreement already in place and that was used in previous grants. This activity is justified as cash is the preferred modality for receiving assistance according to MSNA 2022 findings. The modality is particularly preferred by women, as 74% of them stated physical cash as preferred assistance type. The transfer mechanisms is organized in a way that avoids fraud cases and doesn’t put at risk beneficiaries, in particular women, as only the recipient will be entitled to withdraw the cash in a safe way. Monitoring visits will be conducted in order to ensure this and to prevent arising of protection concerns and adjust the activity accordingly. PalPay will provide each beneficiaries with a PIN through SMS along with a list of approved cashier present in every locality. Beneficiaries will be able to withdraw cash from the cashier present in the list. Information sessions and leaflet will be distributed to the beneficiaries to further explain disbursement process and make sure also marginalized categories are all aware. A hotline will be available to provide timely assistance to any beneficiaries who might be facing challenges.The service provider has been already tested and positive feedback have been registered from the beneficiaries. Activity 3: Follow up with individual cases: Following the distribution of the MPCA, AAH will a) ensure the functionality of the Feedback and Complaints and beneficiaries’ access to it with a particular focus on women and other groups at risk;b) follow up on the possible issues faced by beneficiaries to access and use the service b) make referrals to relevant clusters as needed, prioritizing protection and GBV referrals c) monitor the effect of the cash injection and its use through PDM and endline exercises.Design, implementation and monitoring will ensure that assistance is accessible by all groups, with a focus on gender and age, with no discrimination, that the intervention is not increasing GBV risks and no one is left behind. AAH will ensure appropriateness, inclusivity and do no harm in all phases of the project cycle, from the programming to the monitoring and evaluation. AAH has been working directly with communities in its interventions and will conduct extensive consultations with community committees and leaders at all stages to ensure appropriateness, community acceptance and accountability with affected populations.

3 - Indicators

The interventions will measure its achievements through the below indicator: # of highly vulnerable HHs supported with multipurpose cash assistance (1,081 ILS) for 6 months. are able to meet their essential needs and access to life saving services . The MEAL unit will establish the MEAL plan and be responsible for the data quality of MEAL activities throughout the intervention. The M&E framework will measure key indicators defined within the Logical Framework at output and outcome level. The MEAL system will foster learning and the generation of timely and relevant information to adjust project implementation. The MEAL team will conduct frequent monitoring of all activities to ensure the harmonized use of monitoring tools, confirm record keeping and maximize program quality. The Project Tracking Tool that will be developed for the intervention will allow the MEAL team and technical team to conduct regular progress review meetings allowing to capture indicators progress and learnings due to the complexity of social change and the dynamic context in the Gaza Strip. The MEAL framework will be gender sensitive, as data will provide relevant gender-disaggregated and differentiated information that will capture women and men’s different experiences and perceptions of risks, shocks and stresses, and their different resilience capacities, accounting for other intersecting factors such as age or location.The following will be the key MEAL activities to allow the MEAL team to measure the success of the project: - Application selection analysis, the survey will measure the pre- status of the vulnerable household’s income, rCSI, access to basic needs and main protection concerns. - Post distribution monitoring survey will be conducted with vulnerable households benefiting from MPCA to measure the improvement in their ability to meet their basic needs as a result of MPCA assistance provided and monitor reduction in their negative coping strategies. - Onsite monitoring visits will be implemented by both MEAL and project team aiming at monitoring quality of implementation (consists of interviews with BNFs), monitoring field risks and assumptions, and collect community feedback. The MEAL data from all above activities will be systematically and timely analysed to identify potential improvements, gaps, and ways forward.Monitoring visits will be conducted in order to ensure that the assistance is not putting at risk any group, with a particular focus on gender for negatively impacting on the HH dynamics or creating protection concerns or other unintended effects. Monitoring will ensure that assistance is accessible by all groups, with a focus on gender and age, with no discrimination, that the intervention is not increasing GBV risks and no one is left behind.Regarding PSEA mainstreaming, the following indicator will be also measured:- # of Focal Points trained on PSEA, including how to report allegations in a safe and confidential manner- # of sites reached with communications materials on FCM including PSEA, how to report on SEA and how to access survivor centered assistance According to AAH referral SoPs, the identified PSEA cased will be directed to the PSEA focal point, in respect of confidentiality, and will be then referred to focal points designated by the protection cluster.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) is managed by the AAH’s MEAL unit. Following the FCM standard operating procedures it will be introduced to the targeted communities through information materials like brochures and information leaflets inside the communities where WASH interventions are taking place, to provide communities and beneficiaries with different channels to access AAH, prior, during and after implementation. The FCM will be active from the beginning of the project through an active hotline number managed by MEAL staff. Complaints, feedback or suggestions are recorded on a protected sheet, and MEAL staff uses the agreed FCM procedures to handle the complaint based on its level and sensitivity. AAH management and the program team jointly discuss the complaint (depending on its nature and sensitivity) and formulate responses on a case-by-case basis. On average, within 2 weeks the response usually is provided to the complainant or community members by contacting him/her back through phone or emails. If the response includes actions these will be considered by the program team, they will fill in the learning log sheet with actions planned to allow the MEAL team to follow up the execution of these actions. On a semi-annual basis, a FCM report will be developed and shared with AAH management and program team to document learning captured from FCM findings, suggestions and feedback

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Throughout the whole project cycle AAH ensures protection mainstreaming and Do No Harm. AAH considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. AAH has mechanisms in place to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. AAH has in place focal points at base and mission levels, as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. All AAH staff have the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct and our held accountable if they do not respect it. AAH has made use of cluster specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Tip Sheets, along with learnings from various interventions and external evaluations. Overall AAH’s aim is to take decisive humanitarian action with attention to risk mitigation thereby avoiding doing harm to the greatest extent possible. Risks assessments and analysis of the protection context are undertaken for all the projects, and even more so when implemented with at risk groups (e.g. minors) or in areas where risk of doing harm is exacerbated (e.g. Area C in the West Bank or inside health facilities). In order to inform its assessments and implementation AAH also continuously works on understanding gender roles and how its interventions might impact them without aggravating existing power relations between genders. By understanding gender roles and household dynamics, AAH believes that the organization mitigates the risks of doing harm. In 2019, AAH conducted a masculinity study in both Gaza and the West Bank, which focused on identities/roles of men in order to fully understand men, women, girls and boys and gender dynamics. As part of AAH accountability to affected populations, a feedback mechanism emphasizing fluid and transparent communication will be in place. AAH has a policy of zero tolerance with regards to PSEA both in the workplace and towards beneficiaries; and requires adherence to standards from all its employees, volunteers, daily workers, collaborators and partners. AAH has a PSEA focal point at mission level as well as two focal points in the Gaza base to ensure PSEA in mainstreamed according to AAH policies as instructed and supervised by the HQ PSEA focal person and committee created in case of policy and protocol breach. Focal points receive regular internal and external trainings in line with PSEA IASC regulations and AAH policies. All employees and volunteers receive a PSEA induction training in the beginning of their collaboration with AAH and are briefed on the PSEA guideline and protocol of which they receive a copy as well as reporting channels.During informative sessions, conducted prior the start of any activity, beneficiaries are properly inducted on PSEA main elements and reporting channels, which are a dedicated email address and a hotline in respect of confidentiality. The existing PSEA and FCM mechanisms were designed in consultations with communities AAH is working with in the proposed areas of interventions to ensure accessibility and community acceptance. Two indicators were added in the proposal to ensure PSEA mainstreaming, namely:- # of Focal Points trained on PSEA, including how to report allegations in a safe and confidential manner- # of sites reached with communications materials on FCM including PSEA, how to report on SEA and how to access survivor centered assistance

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AAH upholds the humanitarian principles of Humanity, Impartiality, Neutrality, andIndependence. During the course of the project, AAH will ensure all projects are easily accessible to those most at need, avoiding risks to the beneficiaries. AAH is committed to operate in an ethical manner and on a programmatic risk mitigation strategy for risk of fraud, waste, and abuse within the proposed activities. At a global level, AAH relies on the organizational Policy on Anti-Bribery, Corruption and Abuse of Power and Code of Conduct. Organizational whistleblowing mechanisms and procedure are in place, `ZERO TOLERANCE ́ towards fraud, bribery or corruption is applied. Reporting mechanisms have also been implemented to report these behaviours to headquarters.Ethical Risks related to any kind of abuse (sexual, power, etc...) are mitigated byproviding compulsory briefing to each new staff member on ethical policies. AAH Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. AAH has a Policy for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and in 2019, AAH conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation for which the consultant spent March in oPt. Particular attention was paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. AAH is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age; though pending the reporting format, AAH usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. Random checks will be carried out by the MEAL team members to ensure transparency, neutrality and objectivity and spot potential bias or incongruency.
