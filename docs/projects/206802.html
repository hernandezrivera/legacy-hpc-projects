Project Summary

The integrated emergency nutrition response is aimed at reducing mortality and morbidity linked to life-threatening forms of malnutrition in Konye, Mbonge and ekondo titi Health districts in the South West Regions. This will support the nutrition cluster HRP 2024 strategy and response framework hence creating a significant impact on the nutrition response plan for 2024.
For 2024, Caritas kumba will implement a well defined preventive and curative nutrition program that seeks to manage malnutrition in crisis affected communities Mbonge, Konye and Ekondo titi health districts. To achieve this goal, a preventive supplementary feeding program that targets children within vulnerable households and children diagnosed with moderate acute malnutrition will be implemented. This program will be implemented in collaboration with the World Food Program and shall not be budgeted in the present budget. Caritas Kumba will undertake both passive and exhaustive screening of children under-5 for acute malnutrition, appropriate treatment of uncomplicated SAM cases and referral of complicated SAM cases. Family MUAC approach will be scaled up to enhance early identification of SAM and MAM cases. Preventive actions that will include promotion of infant and young child feeding in emergencies (IYCF-E), deworming prophylaxis and micronutrient supplementation - with the aim of addressing micronutrient deficiencies shall be incorporated. For 2024, Caritas shall improve on the equitable access of iron/folic acid supplementation and deworming prophylaxis by pregnant women and breastfeeding mothers - with special attention to pregnant adolescent girls.
To reinforce partners and the community’s capacity to deliver basic nutrition services to affected communities, the need for refresher courses to revisit the IMAM protocol by Community health volunteers is necessary. Caritas Kumba will continuously liaise with the Regional delegation of Public health in implementing appropriate nutrition interventions without affecting health system. To enhance collaboration, endeavors to participate and support nutrition cluster activities and enhance multisectoral collaboration with other nutrition sensitive clusters (Health, Food Security, WASH and protection) to realize optimal nutritional outcomes shall be emphasized .
The “do no harm” principle shall be implemented through the training of all staff on core humanitarian principles mainstream aspects related to gender, protection centrality, age, gender, disability and Accountability to Affected Population (AAP). Interventions will also be adapted in the context of the a dynamic crisis and adjusted according to the diversification on the needs and field realities.
The outstanding performance in collaboration with UNICEF and WFP over the years has given Caritas Kumba a high level of access and acceptance within Mbonge, Konye and Ekondo titi due to her over 4 years experience in the implementation of General Food distribution as well as WASH and Child Protection Interventions, within these communities. Intervention will use static health structures for inpatient care, rapid response mechanism (RRM) and mobile clinics to access the affected population both in accessible and those in hard to reach remote areas.
The target groups will include boys and girls (irrespective of their disabilities/abilities) under-five for screening, referral, children with SAM, Vitamin A supplementation and deworming prophylaxis. Adolescent, pregnant and breastfeeding will be targeted for the iron and folic acid and deworming prophylaxis. Konye, Mbonge and Ekondo Titi have recorded very low access to health care services with most caregivers and mothers resorting to the use of traditional methods in managing malnutrition. It is extremely important to introduce an integrated approach in the management of acute malnutrition within these communities.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The situation in the North west and South west regions of Cameroon is dire with continues displacement in communities within Rural/peri-urban settings. The populations living in these remote areas, IDPs in urban/periurban areas, and households/communities hosting IDPs are vulnerable to malnutrition since they are highly exposed to aggravating factors (food insecurity, limited access to water, poor sanitary conditions, sanitation and hygiene services, and morbidity due to the overstretching of basic social services). According to the Food Assistance Fact sheet (USAID, 2021), the NW and SW regions continue to face Crisis and Stressed levels of acute food insecurity due to persistent conflict and insecurity. According to the Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWSNET, February 2022), dietary diversity is generally poor among households facing severe food insecurity especially among IDP households which is more evident in the Meme divisions. More than 65% of severely food insecure IDP households in these division display a low dietary diversity score (DDS) indicating that these households consume less than or equal to three food group from the 12 food groups as per the household dietary diversity score. This reveals that, the severely food insecure households do not have physical and economic access to a minimum of seven food groups from the 12 food groups of the dietary diversity score, hence emphatically indicating the degree of food insecurity within these communities. This is also due to the distinctive disruption the food system which has significantly affected the nutritional status of the affected populations as they continue to face challenges accessing balance nutritional foods. According to OCHA on the humanitarian needs overview (March, 2023), it is estimated that more than 230,700 people were in need of nutrition services in the North West and South-West regions. The situation is estimated to have worsen due to suspension of activities and low coverage as a result of limited funds (<9%) recorded in 2023. Due to the absence of a comprehensive SMART nutrition survey, the nutrition situation in the North-West and South West regions remains unclear. However, proxy data from rapid emergency food security assessments (EFSA) conducted in October 2019 revealed a precarious situation with a proxy GAM of 7.1% in the South-West regions. This is in line with the recent door to door MUAC screening reports conducted by Caritas Kumba and WFP in Meme division in October, 2023, a proxy GAM of 6.8% was recorded in Mbonge, Kumba and Konye health districts hence indicating a need for an emergency response in communities within these health districts. This situation may be worsen in Ekondo titi health district which has been void of humanitarian assistance since the beginning of this crises as a result of poor access and hence little or no coverage to support nutrition activities. The highly limited life-saving nutrition services in the South West region is more accessible within the hospitals in the urban setting than in the rural areas. This is glaring as over 60% of SAM cases identified and referred during door to door screening were not able to arrive the treatment centers due to insecurity, no means of transportation and long distances from their homes to the treatment centers. Hence the need for a more proactive community based approach through the use of community health workers and mobile clinics in the management of malnutrition within these hard-to-reach areas will be absolutely necessary. In a bit to reduce the cost of treatment, there is need to implement a preventive supplementary feeding program along side the therapeutic program to prevent and treat MAM cases as well as SAM cases hence arresting malnutrition to acceptable levels.
