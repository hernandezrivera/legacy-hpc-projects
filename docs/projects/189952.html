Project Summary

This project will enhance the resilience of children and caregivers by establishing two Child Friendly Spaces (CFS), serving the host and IDP communities. IsraAID will provide capacity-building training for local Child Protection (CP) service providers; and the CFSs will host CP and recreational activities, and other services meeting communities’ needs.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

IsraAID will establish and run two CFSs, which will target children and teenagers, and serve the wider community in the after-school hours. The centers will operate in host communities, in close proximity to IDP camps, for children from the host and IDP communities. The project will target 500 female and male children and teenagers both enrolled and not enrolled in the education system. This will promote the social inclusion and integration of internally displaced children into the community and school system. Additionally, 400 caregivers will be engaged in training, home visits, and other complementary activities.. We 100 community leaders, 50 community volunteers and 100 CBO members and employees will be identified and recruited to provide activities that will contribute to strengthening the MHPSS knowledge and capacities within the targeted communities. The project will use a holistic, multi-sectoral approach which focuses on the child and the community structures and mechanisms that surround them, as well as respond to the needs of vulnerable populations (e.g people with disabilities, children and youth). Activities within the CFSs will include (1) training to build the capacity of local service providers to provide Child Protection assistance services within the community; and (2) a range of Child Protection individual assistance, recreational and other services - such as direct psychosocial (PSS support) and WASH and public health activities, to meet the local communities’ needs.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Geographical areas: Ímpire -Metuge and Silva Macua - Ancuabe Activity 1: Establish two holistic CFSs in collaboration with local community actors. Activity 2: Implement capacity building training programs for local CP actors within the CFS setting, to strengthen the community’s resilience to prevent and respond to CP risks within the community (training topics to include, PSS, PFA, stress management, ethics and guidelines of interventions and referrals, etc). Activity 3: Provide individual CP assistance services, operated by community actors. Community CP actors, service providers and community leaders will be trained and accompanied by IsraAID teams, to facilitate the activities within the center. This will include; recreational activities for children (in the after school hours), women and girls groups; nutrition and WASH activities; and other activities that will serve the needs of the community. The flexibility of the project and the close collaboration with local actors will allow the CFS to become a center serving the community - IDP and host - as a whole, changing and adapting according to the specific needs expressed by the community. The activities will be aimed for IDP and host community populations as a whole, providing a space to promote inclusion and integration.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Designing, tailoring and implementing the program is done in collaboration and led by the community. The scope is to develop a program that is as adaptable and related as possible to the local context, needs, and assets. This will contribute to identifying and mitigating potential risks.Training and specific workshops on best practices and ethics. Service providers, community volunteers, community workers. Feedback mechanism. Data collection mechanisms in collaboration with the community. and ongogin monitoring and evaluation

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

Identifying an accessible location (both referring to physical and social access) determined with community partners. Implementing outreach activities e.g.: home visits both to bring PSS services to the homes and also to identify gaps in accessibility and help mitigate them. Working with and through community volunteers to reach out and bridge the gaps. Use of interpreters and translators to overcome language barriers.We are aiming to focus on local ownership and local management by involving the community and including its formal structures (community leader and CBOs) and also informal structures (activists, management committees, etc).

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

IsraAID will train key people from the communities to identify, address and monitor environmental, attitudinal, and institutional barriers and enablers to participation for people with disabilities and will dedicate a budget for the removal of these barriers. IsraAID will then include these people in the project's activities and/or recruit them as staff members and enhance their participation in community management structures (program management committees, program steering committees, etc). The needs assessment will allow IsraAID to adapt accessibility, meet specific food-related needs (i.e. type of food packaging, outreach to households without means to reach distribution), and increase participation of people with disabilities who are interested in taking part. People with disabilities will be consulted during the design, implementation, and monitoring of the project.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

In all locations, the project’s CO2 footprint will be minimised by refraining from car usage as much as possible, and awareness will be raised amongst IsraAID staff members of the importance of water conservation. To reduce plastic waste during training and workshops, water dispensers and non-plastic reusable individual cups will be given to each staff member for reuse. Both CFSs will use recycled, environmentally friendly and locally sourced materials. A dedicated waste management team will ensure safe disposal of waste and prevent access to hazardous substances. IsraAID takes a Disaster Risk Reduction approach to all of its projects. Given the growing hazards of climate change for the communities of Mozambique, this project will cultivate a culture of disaster preparedness and risk mitigation amongst local government, community based organizations, and individuals.
