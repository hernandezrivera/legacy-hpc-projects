Project Summary

IMC plans to establish a Women and Girls and Safe Space (WGSS) in Babella sub district, Rural Damascus. The center will work as entry point for identification of GBV related concerns, representing a safe space for beneficiaries to access confidential support through case management, psychosocial and referrals services. At the same time, the center will be offering a range of structured and non-structured evidence based activities, aimed at enhancing awareness, prevention and response about GBV concerns. Women and girls will be able to express and exchange concerns and experiences with their peers and qualified counselors, which will be crucial in enhancing prevention mechanisms while ensuring confidential and appropriate responses are provided when needed. Beneficiaries will be able to determine their interests in terms of PSS activities that the center will offer, which in turn will enhance their active participation. Women and girls and the community overall will be consulted in an effort to better understand the main risks as well as the types of community support systems existing before the crisis and how they have changed over the years.
IMC has been working in Babella and Yalda over the past months through a mobile protection team in spaces offered by the communities, mostly targeting children, youth and women. The team offers PSS structured, non-formal education and recreational activities in addition to case management services. Internal referrals are made for medical services offered by IMC medical mobile team also working in the same areas.
Based on its experience, IMC recognize that the establishment of a WGSS would further encourage and promote active participation of girls and women, offering a safe space for them to interact and discuss about topics of their interest. Furthermore, IMC team will work to raise information and awareness on GBV related topics, to strengthen community protection and support mechanisms. To this end, IMC also plans to organize and conduct capacity-building activities to community members and volunteers (these will be involved in the implementation of some activities at the center).
Aside from the WGSS, IMC plans to continue its case management and awareness and information activities within IMC primary health care facilities, both static and mobiles.

4 - Needs

According to the HNO 2021, 13.1 million people estimated to be in need of protection assistance. The impact of the prolonged crisis, of COVID 19 and the worsening of the economic situation are further contributing to other underlying causes of GBV, exposing women and girls to higher risks of violence, including sexual exploitation, abuse, child/forced marriage, domestic violence. Women have been increasingly engaged in livelihood and economic activities and domestic violence is reported to have increased because of the existing patriarchal culture that defines what is culturally acceptable. While the lack and/or loss of civil documentation impacts all population groups, it adds specific vulnerabilities for women, especially widowed and divorced, limiting their access to basic services and rights. Cases of forced and/or early marriage are reported to continue increasing, often ending in divorce, leaving adolescent girls and women alone with their children and being stigmatized by families and communities and increasing the risk for sexual exploitation. Based on IMC’s analysis of GBV case management services provided in 2020, out of the total GBV cases, 87% refer to domestic violence, followed by 7% of sexual abuse cases (although this is under-reported or reported after a long time since it occurred); psychological and emotional abuse (4%) and forced marriage (2%). These data also reflects the low reporting on specific concerns due to fear of further insecurity and stigma. Many clients seek case management services for depression and anxiety, and while working with them, the Case Managers realize the MH concern is often triggered by one or more GBV incident(s).

5 - Implementation of activities

1) GBV case management and PSS in the WGSS and IMC PHC clinics, including strengthening of internal and external referral pathways and distribution of dignity kits as needed.2) Structured and non-structured PSS activities in the WGSS: recreational and non-formal educational, life skills and empowerment programs.3) Vocational training: Provided through certified institutions, on different topics. Upon completion of the program, participants receive a certificate recognized by Syrian employers and in some cases, based on the specific of the course, a start-up kit to support them in setting up an income generating activity. 4) Information, awareness and sensitization activities to support girls and women in better understanding GBV concerns, strengthen prevention and encourage seeking services. These sessions also proved to contribute in increasing acceptance and trust in IMC services and to identify community members with specific needs for further referral to appropriate services. 5) Training of volunteers and community members on GBV core concepts, GBV mainstreaming, referral pathways, PFA and other topics as chosen by the community members. Trainings will be conducted in IMC Recreational Activity Center, located in Jaramana and WGSS. IMC will also support the conduction of FGDs to enhance active participation and promote feedback mechanism and thus allow beneficiaries to express their satisfaction on the services and suggest any improvement. IMC will monitor the quality of its activities by tracking project outputs, measuring the effectiveness of the program and the satisfaction of the beneficiaries as well as through monitoring and supportive supervisions to ensure staff is applying relevant technical standards

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: low quality of services and referrals expose survivors to further harm. Likelihood: medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1) conduct case management with survivors by trained staff only 2) Supportive technical supervision conducted regularly by qualified staff. 3) Service mapping including only providers implementing ensuring survivor centered approach, updated on regular basis. Risk 2: Low participation and stigmatization of beneficiaries, resistance from the target community. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: high. Mitigation measures: 1) Community and local leaders’ engagement in all phases of project cycle, including joint discussions on activities that are considered priority for the target beneficiaries. 2) Adaptation of messaging to the context and culture, and based on feedback and inputs from targeted communities and beneficiaries; 3) only female staff working in the safe space 4) Community based feedback and complaint mechanism in place. Risk3: Compromised quality of training due to lack of face to face training and lack of proper supervision. Livelihood: high, impact: high. Mitigation measures; 1) Provision of training for IMC service providers within the center. 2) Build the capacity of the other service providers and the community members in the targeted community to support preventing and responding to GBV. 3) provide face-to-face and remote supervision and on-job training as needed.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

RISKS: 1) Sexual abuse and exploitation. 2) Sexual activity with beneficiaries, including with children below 18 years of age. 3) Sexual harassment. 4) Sexual violence. 5) Physical/Emotional Abuse. 6) Child marriage (IMC staff engaging in marriage with a child below 18 years of age)Mitigation and prevention measures: The above-mentioned acts are prohibited and a violation of IMC Code of Conduct which all IMC employees must sign, and as such, subject to disciplinary actions including termination. Mandatory trainings and refreshers on PSEA and safeguarding issues for all IMC employees and volunteers. Awareness and information sessions to beneficiaries regarding PSEA and reporting mechanism. Availability of effective, safe and confidential reporting mechanisms. Internal mechanisms and procedures to investigate suspected violations.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISK 1 – Delayed approval to establish the center or for staff to be deployed. MITIGATION 1 Regular follow-up on approval process, submission of approval letters ahead of time; remote management system in place.RISK 2: Resistance and non-acceptance of IMC activities from the community. MITIGATION 2: Community engagement throughout all phases of implementation; involvement of beneficiaries on IEC materials and eventual adaptation. RISK 3: Sanctions affecting/delaying bank transfers to Syria. MITIGATION 3: Maintain bank accounts with at least two banks; continue to advocate through INGO forum on waiving sanctions for humanitarian funds transfer; maintain information sharing with other INGOs to identify alternative solutions.RISK 4: COVID 19 causing activities to be suspended. MITIGATION 4: establishment of remote management plans for case management services to ensure uninterrupted support; adherence by staff and beneficiaries to preventive measures; maintaining communication channels with community members to inform on disruption of services.Assumptions: 1) Continued and uninterrupted access to the areas of intervention for implementation, monitoring and supervision. 2) Acceptance of IMC activities and services from the targeted population. 3) Adequate financial resources. 4) Security situation remain stable. Mitigation strategy; establish a remote supervision plan in case of temporary adoption of remote management systems. Informing and sensitize communities on IMC activities and services. Continuous monitoring of market prices and budget realignments submission to donors. Continuous monitoring of the security situation, liaising with UNDSS and other humanitarian partners for exchange of information.
