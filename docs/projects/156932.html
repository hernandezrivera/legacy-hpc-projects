Project Summary

Syria Relief will provide critically needed emergency shelter support to 200 vulnerable IDP households in Idleb Governorate, Northern Syria. We will prioritize vulnerable urban and rural Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) who have fled the recent fighting in Idleb for assistance. The proposed project contributes to strengthening the resilience of the population of, Idleb as the measures will decrease their vulnerability and invest in strengthening structures that will help the population after project end to meet their needs.
The shelter repairs are substantive, for example insulation through new windows and doors, running water or room dividers for gender-separated sleeping areas. These measures will directly decrease people´s vulnerability by contributing to an improved protection against cold, diseases and protection risks. These benefits will continue to be there after project end insofar not undermined by attacks, theft or similar.
As part of this action, SR will support the capacities of 4,000 most vulnerable households living in Idleb and Aleppo, to heat their shelter during the winter season 2019-2020.
Using a restricted value voucher modality , SR will organize three shopping periods during the project implementation as part of which identified beneficiaries will be able to access roughly 360 liters of diesel (upon to the cluster’s recommendation) in total value of 198 USD commodity voucher as 4 liters/day per family for heating during three harsh winter months. The voucher value will be provided as three batches (66 USD per batch, the unit price for liter is $0.55).

Needs

Idleb Governorates are now home to lots of camps, collective shelters, ITS (informal tented settlements), and numerous households are hosting IDPs. Living without shelter, in damaged or unfinished buildings (including collective shelters and ITS), IDPs face critical issues such as the lack of access to tap water, gender segregated bathing facilities, cooking facilities, and energy. IDPs are at a higher risk to any other threat (e.g not being able to meet their livelihood, education, or WASH needs). Young, elderly, disabled and female headed households are most severely affected.According to shelter assessments conducted by REACH reports in northern Syria, 54% of households reported shelter damage, notably broken or cracked windows, cracks in walls, and doors unable to shut properly. Of the 54% of households reporting shelter damage, 67% were unable to conduct repairs to shelter, due to their inability to pay for repair materials or for a professional for repair. In addition, 55% of households reported shelter adequacy issues (e.g. leaking during rain, lack of insulation from the cold, inability to lock the home securely, lack of lighting, lack of space…) Field visits by SR’s program team in Jisr Alshogor this area have confirmed that private homes have suffered extensive damage from the years of conflict. According to shelter assessments, local consultations and field reports by SR, the need for shelter rehabilitation in Jisr Alshgor continues to increase as structures become increasingly limited and declining of quality, according to the local council and field team visit 20% of the houses in this city with small damage, and 45% of houses with medium damage and 35% of houses with high rate of damage, so SR in collaboration with QRC and LC will develop a criteria to target the most vulnerable people in this area. SR as agreed with its donor QRC will prioritize vulnerable urban and rural Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) being hosted by other families, as well as IDPs sheltering in collective centers. Beneficiaries will be prioritized in accordance with established vulnerability criteria, including prioritization of female-headed households, families with disabled or elderly family members, and families with more than six family members According to the HNO 2019, NFI needs remain substantial, both in terms of humanitarian life-saving needs, particularly those among the high numbers of newly displaced IDPs requiring core, supplementary and seasonal assistance, as well as longer-term needs, among those communities requiring resilience-oriented support to reduce dependence on external assistance and enhance the ability of families to withstand future shocks and stresses. Relatedly, findings show a decreased need for standard/core NFI items and a significant increase in requests for items specific to the situation faced by certain communities or population groups. In 2019, these needs will continue to compromise the ability of NFI partners to tailor responses based on the intersection of individual needs and vulnerabilities withthe localized capacity of markets and supply chains.Overall 300,000 more people now living in severity 4 and 5 areas over the last year with an overall increase in the number of people in need of 570,000. These increases have been concentrated in more densely populated urban areas. Populations in areas which experience cold conditions during winter continue to need seasonal support, with communities surveyed reporting heating-related shelter deficiencies (lack of heating and insulation) as the most widespread issue.

Implementation of activities

SR will implement the proposed shelter assistance program over a six-month period beginning in January 2019. Implementation includes working to finalize beneficiary registration, rehabilitation; provide technical installation support to particularly vulnerable households; and monitor the rehabilitation works. The final beneficiary list will note which families are living in unfinished buildings, which families will require assistance in installing the shelter assistances, and which households are able to utilize the materials without additional technical or labor support from SR. Simultaneously, SR will initiate tendering of shelter rehabilitation after finishing the assessment and selecting the targeted houses, SR will continue to monitor households’ rehabilitation throughout the program. Moreover, as the rehabilitation works in local Syrian markets in Idleb are good, the contracts will be cone through an open, transparent, and competitive process, in accordance SR’ policies. Prior to implementation of NFIs intervention, SR will inform relevant authorities, community leaders and other stakeholders of the intended activities. By engaging these actors, SR will inform the larger community of the project and processes to be undertaken, appropriate communication methods will be used to ensure the widest coverage. Preliminary activities-SR will recruit a gender mixed field staff to ensure access to all targeted beneficiaries. All of them will be oriented to SR PSEA Policy then they will sign it.-A training will be organized gathering the program teams to explain the technical aspects of the market based approach.-Context and security analysis will be conducted by SR logistics and security team to determine the risk profile and permissibility of programing in this environment- Conduct Market assessment in Idleb and Aleppo, Syria Relief will conduct a rapid market assessment through several interviews with wholesalers, vendors, key market informants, and likely beneficiaries, and direct observations in order to (1) gather more information on the market system, (2) determine the viability of a voucher program in the area, (3) analyze accessibility of women to the market and (4) ensure the project will not create a negative impact on the market. -SR logistic team will contract with a company to print vouchers for fuel with required security features. They will be delivered to SR office in Syria.The activities will be:- Identification of the beneficiaries and community mobilisation - Identification of the vendors- Distribution of vouchers to the beneficiaries- Shopping / redemption period- Post-distribution/redemption monitoring:- Payment of vendors with the reconciliation of the supporting documents. Finally, the reporting to about the program deliverables. SR will report the S/NFI activities to the S/NFI cluster on monthly basis using the 4Ws matrix formats.
