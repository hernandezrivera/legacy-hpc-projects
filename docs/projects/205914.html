Project Summary

The current education situation in South Sudan is deeply concerning, with a multitude of challenges that hinder access to quality education. According to the HNO 2023 an estimate of 3.3 million people needs education services in South Sudan. United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) estimates 2.8 million children in South Sudan are currently out of school for the academic year 2023. This situation is exacerbated by the ongoing armed conflict and environmental crisis which has resulted in the overall need for 8.5 million people, the existing educational infrastructure is inadequate, with only 27% of schools having basic facilities, as reported by the World Bank. Additionally, there is a severe shortage of qualified teachers. A comprehensive approach is required to address these challenges.
The proposed education project seeks to overcome these limitations by leveraging innovative teaching methodologies and the power of technology to create inclusive and sustainable educational initiatives. By harnessing the potential of technology, we can bridge the gap in education provision, deliver high-quality instruction, and empower the children and youth of South Sudan to acquire the necessary skills for future success. Through this project, we aim to create a transformative learning environment that not only addresses immediate educational needs but also contributes to long-term resilience and development in South Sudan as well as informed program approaches through the availability of rigorous evidence and information. The project will target a total of 3,821 individuals including 100 (25F ,80M) teachers who will approximately support 3699 (1850F, 1849M) and 22(4F,18M) SMC and PTA members in Central Equatoria from Juba County as a priority severity 3 county by education cluster by and support responding to education cluster goal 1 and 2. The project will ensure all groups are reached including girls, boys, men, and women as well as children with disabilities (CwDs).

1. Needs

The intervention will be responding to the children in need of education service while addressing the key gaps highlighted in the Education Needs Assessment (ENA) 2022 which focusing its operations on Juba County which is classified as severity 3 by education cluster. Despite the positive intentions to drive quality education for schools and learning centers, there needs to be teachers who are well-trained and can do their jobs well. According to ENA 2022 findings, only 22.4% of the engaged teachers in the assessment have been trained in pedagogical training, 34.2% for child protection in emergencies, and 34.5% in psychosocial support (PSS). According to General Education Sector Plan (2022-2027), against national teaching practice entry requirements, more than 80% of teachers in pre-primary, 70% in primary, less than half of teachers in secondary, and nearly 75% in alternative education meet the requirements. Moreover, only 11% of teachers in ECE, 8% in primary, and 10% in the AES have short-cycle tertiary education training. Apart from a lack of qualified teachers, the general education system suffers an acute shortage of teachers, which is evident from the high Pupil Teacher Ratios (PTR), averaging 90:1 in ECE, 93:1 in primary, 25:1 in secondary and 69:1 in AES. The system has had to turn to volunteer teachers to address this challenge, and up to one-third of teachers in general education are volunteers. There is an acute shortage of qualified teachers in general education, which is considered to affect the quality of instruction in classrooms. The IRC will respond to the needs and train 100(20F, 80M) on Classroom Social Emotional and Learning activities that can better inform teachers teaching techniques and students engagement in classrooms. The training will be facilitated with the use of technology where teachers will be able to meet remote and share their experience using the AprendIA Platform. This will better support teachers on continuous engagement through virtual Teachers Learning Circles (TLCs). The trained teachers are expected to support an approximate of 3699 (1850Girls, 1849 Boys) in classroom supported. The project will also engage 22(4Female, 18Male) PTA and SMC Members on school governance and climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR).To address the barriers to educational access, quality, and continuity in fragile conflict-affected settings like South Sudan, there is a need for increased rigorous and comprehensive studies. These studies would provide insights into the specific challenges faced by the education system in conflict-affected areas and inform evidence-based interventions (Birch, 2019; Save the Children, 2020; World Bank, 2021). By identifying the root causes of barriers and exploring effective strategies, stakeholders can make informed decisions and design targeted interventions that promote education in these settings. This project will be achieved through the 2 approved project within IRC education sector one being the Education Research in Conflict and protracted Crisis (ERICC) which will support on generation of evidence for education stakeholder and the second being the project that focus on continuous teachers’ professional development expected to start in January 2024.

2. Project activities

In collaboration with the National, state, and County Ministry of General Education and Instruction (MoGEI), IRC will support efforts to improve access, quality, continuity, and coherence of education programs in Central Equatorial State. The goal is to benefit 3699 students (1850girls and 1849 boys) by 2025. The proposed intervention aligns with the core functions of the Education in Emergency (EiE) service delivery and knowledge generation to inform strategic decision-making by the Humanitarian Coordinator/Humanitarian Country Team (HC/HCT) and education sector objectives outlined in the Education Cluster Strategy South Sudan 2023-2025.To enhance the safety and quality of education, the project will provide pedagogical training to 100 (20F,80M) primary school teachers from Juba County including teachers with disabilities and women. The training will focus on teaching methodologies that incorporate Social Emotional and Learning (SEL)Skills through Classroom play-based activities. The aim is to equip teachers with skills to effectively engage distressed children and create a secure and nurturing learning environment. This training will be supplemented by Virtual Teachers Learning Circles (vTLCs) conducted through the AI-based last-mile education content delivery platform, AprendIA. The vTLC will serve as a virtual platform for teachers from different schools to interact and share their experiences.The importance of improving attendance and data collection in schools has been emphasized in the 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and the 2022 Education Needs Assessment (ENA). By providing teachers with training on engaging children through play-based learning techniques, we anticipate that a total of 3699 students (1850 girls and 1849 boys) including children with disabilities will benefit from these play interventions in their schools. These interventions are designed to enhance student attendance and promote continued learning, making the school environment more attractive and conducive to their educational journey. The students researched will include a range of children from host communities and IDP community. Evidence Generation: Further the project will be working towards fulfilling the education cluster's second core function, which involves generating evidence through its ongoing research project called Education Research in Conflict and Protracted Crisis (ERICC). The intervention will focus to transform education policy and practice in conflict-affected and protracted crisis situations globally. By identifying effective approaches to improve access, quality, and continuity of education, The intervention will focus to support sustainable and coherent education systems that foster the holistic learning and development of children in conflict and crisis contexts within a specific country. The intervention aims to bridge the gap between research, practice, and policy by producing accessible and actionable knowledge that can inform local and national decision-making processes. Through collaborative partnerships and the co-construction of research, the process will develop a research agenda that can guide various stakeholders in the Education in Emergencies (EiE) field to focus on priority areas that require more evidence. The project will engage 22(4Female, 18Male) PTA and SCM members through awareness sessions to best remind them of their role and responsibilities in school governance as well as delivering messages on the impact of climate change and the strategies for mitigating its impact in education. This will ultimately contribute to building a more resilient and sustainable educational environment for students and communities. The teachers and project staff to be engaged will be oriented on safeguarding policy which including protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) to ensure everyone engaged is safe and learning environment are friendly for everyone.
