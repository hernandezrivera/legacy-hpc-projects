Project Summary

According to South Sudan WASH Cluster HNRP 2025, a total of 5.01m are considered PIN while 2.25m are targeted with WASH services. This project targets Fangak, Nyirol and Ayod which are among those categorized as being under severity 5 as per the South Sudan HNO Intersectoral Pin and People targeted Ratio Map 2024. These counties are characterized as those which are common with Malnutrition and also having multiple hazards namely the Sudan Crisis, flooding and heightened risk of disease outbreaks especially cholera. For purposes of inclusivity, this project will target the returnees, IDPs and host communities.
These three counties are prioritized by the WASH Cluster in South Sudan as having multiple WASH related challenges. Therefore, the emergency that this project seeks to address include high prevalence of malnutrition and high WASH related morbidity and mortality, lack of access to basic WASH services in underserved locations affected by disaster and lack of inclusivity of disability and mitigating GBV at WASH facility as well as inability to provide durable access to services and communities chronically affected by disaster.
These targeted counties have some of the worst flood affected payams namely Manajang, Pulita, Phom and Mareang in Fangak, Chuil and Pading in Nyirol, Mogok and Pagil in Ayod. The critical drivers of this WASH emergency are widespread conflicts, insecurity and perennial flooding which has caused displacement, damaged facilities, service disruptions and access barriers. With WASH infrastructure having been destroyed with serious public health risk implications, failing to respond will result in increased mortality and morbidity of malnourished children and PLW, and poor outcome performance of nutrition interventions.
The implementation of this project will be guided by the four WASH cluster objectives which seek to reduce WASH- related morbidity and mortality through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition, provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties, coordinate GBV mitigation activities and also reduce the community members’ exposure to environmental hazards through the construction of disaster- resistant structures and infrastructure.
A total of 5 Assistance packages will be delivered as prescribed by the WASH – HRP guideline of 2025 and target U5 with SAM, their caretakers and PLW. Key strategies will involve: 1) Integration with nutrition at both community and facility levels through provision of WASH NFI, hygiene promotion and emergency sanitation and water supply to break disease transmission 2) Integration with health at both community and facility level (technical support) 3) Deliver services through static partners and mobile emergency response mechanism (EPR) 4) The cross-cutting package consisting of do-no-harm conflict sensitivity practices, AAP, food resilience; gender appropriate rehabilitation of facilities, water-points and latrines and measures to mitigate SGBV in relation to program delivery with emphasis on safety audits and disability inclusion. 5) Sustainable access to water in communities living in Priority 1 or 2 where feasible and those regularly affected by flooding. For successful integration, CMA will engage all co-located partners to fill in respective sector gaps.
Whereas the WASH cluster target for the three counties stands at 169,759 beneficiaries, this project will target a total of 48,096 spread across the three counties of Ayod, Fangak and Nyirol. The key activities include: 1) Rehabilitation of both facility and community-based water points. 2) Provision of NFIs and aqua tabs for chlorination to secure water safety 3) Rehabilitation of both facility and community-based latrines with gender safety measures 4) Building the capacity of stakeholders on hand washing and borehole operation and maintenance 5) Sensitization of community on sanitation and hygiene behavior change 6) Distribution of soap and hand washing NFIs.

1. Needs

South Sudan faces enormous WASH challenges, which cause poor health, including malnutrition, school absenteeism, loss of household income, and reduced workforce productivity. According to UNICEF, 59% of the population lacks access to safe drinking water with just 10% having improved sanitation. The majority are exposed to contaminated water, leading to waterborne diseases such as cholera and other diseases. Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) are linked in many ways. Poor hygiene, inadequate access to clean drinking water, and lack of sanitation facilities cause millions of the people to die from preventable diseases each year where children plus the aged are particularly affected with 33% d of U5 children suffering from diarrhoea. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites. Women and girls who are traditionally responsible for water collection face an increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water far from their homes. High open defecation rates also expose women, children and PWDs at higher risk of violence and discrimination, particularly in IDP sites and other crowded settlements. Although access to adequate WASH is a human right, in South Sudan, these services continue to be a luxury for the majority both in rural and urban setups. The 2nd Joint WASH Sector Review (March 2024), revealed that access to basic water supply had fallen from 41% in 2010 to 39%, basic sanitation fell slightly from 16% to 14%, Open Defecation rose from 60% to 63% while only 22% of the population had handwashing facilities with soap and water in their homes (WHO/UNICEF JMP reporting on the progress of the SDG for WASH). The situation is worse in counties such as Fangak, Ayod and Nyirol which have suffered cyclical effects of perennial insecurity and severe floods resulting in displacement, loss of food crops and damage to facilities causing service disruptions and access barrier for all those seeking WASH services. According to the ISNA (IoM 2023), boreholes were found to be the predominant source of drinking water relied on by 55.8% of the households, followed by rivers or streams at 24.4%. A concerning revelation is that over 70% of households do not treat their water. Ironically, the 24.4% of respondents whose primary water source is rivers and streams (considered unimproved water sources), 75% do not treat their water before drinking, increasing their vulnerability to several potential health risks. In terms of sanitation facilities, 56.1% of the households still practice open defecation which underscores the urgent requirement for enhanced sanitation infrastructure and awareness initiatives. Indicatively, of those who practice open defecation, 66% reported an unprotected water source as their primary source of drinking water. Additional reported sanitation facilities include, open hole (21.4%), pit latrine without slab (10.4%), pit latrine with slab (7.4%) and others (4.7%). Overall, the problems that this response plan will address include 1) Nonexistent or insufficient water supply, sanitation and handwashing facilities 2) Toilets or latrines which are not adapted to the needs of children, women and girls 3) Broken dirty and unsafe water supply, sanitation and hand washing facilities 4) Children with poor hygiene and hand washing practices 5) Lack of infrastructure for adequate fecal sludge management 6) Lack of provision of adequate consumable WASH supplies e.g., soap, menstrual hygiene kits etc.) 6) Lack of awareness creation on positive hygiene practices through the adoption of WASH messaging through RECCE Actors 8) Lack of water treatment practices which underscores the need for interventions to promote safe water practices and mitigate health risks associated with water consumption.

2. Project activities

This project, just like any ideal WASH project, is premised on the need to model implementation by innovatively providing a set of evidence-based practices for the sustained provision of safe water, dignified sanitation and good hygiene practices. This project is, therefore, designed to contribute to the reduction of WASH related morbidity and mortality in the context of conflict affected and flood damaged WASH infrastructure and also in the context of WASH Cluster classification which identified priority counties as characterized by multiple hazards including the influx of refugees from Sudan. Being aligned to the WASH Cluster, similarly, the goal of this project will be achieved through four main objectives and the several concomitant activities summarized below. Objective 1: Contribute to reducing WASH- related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with NutritionActivities: 1.1 Distribution of standard WASH kits to SAM children and PLW/caretakers with hygiene promotion; 1.2 Rehabilitate facility-based water points to provide secure water safety for nutrition facilities and community users; 1.3 Provide NFI and chlorination with aquatabs maintaining a residual chlorine concentration ≤ 0.5 mg/L; 1.4 Rehabilitate facility-based latrines for use of attendees for nutrition services; 1.5 Train facility nutrition personnel on hand washing and train pump mechanics on the operation and maintenance of facility-based boreholes for sustainability Objective 2.0: Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties.The activities which will help in achieving this include the following: 2.1 Promote sanitation and hygiene behavior change with training of community members through gender-balanced teams of frontline health and nutrition personnel and community-based hygiene promoter; 2.2 Distribution of soap and hand washing NFIs like bucket, Jerrican with tap etc. for community members; 2.3 Provide soap and hand washing buckets at the facility level; 2.4 Rehabilitation of water point and provide NFIs and aquatabs for chlorination to secure water safety for community users; 2.5 Rehabilitation of latrines for community use with gender safety measures included and promote safe practices for human excrement disposal. Objective 3. WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation.The focus here will be on the WASH specific activities for GBV (or mitigating WASH GBV) Activities will include the following: 3.1 Conduct safety audits of community water-points and latrines, develop the safety plan for construction/rehabilitation; 3.2 Construct/rehabilitate gender-appropriate pit-latrines at community centers like schools, churches and market centers; 3.3 Provide menstrual hygiene management kits for the most vulnerable women and girls of adolescence age and train them how to make menstrual hygiene management using the local material for sustainability. Objective 4. Community members exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster- resistant structures and infrastructure. Proposed activities will include the following: 4.1 Applying elevated flood-resilient designs to rehabilitation of gender-appropriate institutional water-points to serve health and nutrition facilities, schools, churches and markets centers; 4.2 Applying elevated flood-resilient design to rehabilitation of gender- appropriate institutional pit latrines to serve health and nutrition facilities, schools, churches and markets centers.
