Project Summary

The increased access to basic quality education with Integrated and synergized quality Education response programme with focus to Uror, Nyirol, Akobo, Maiwut, Pibor and Twic Counties, South Sudan. The programme will seek to improve the access to quality education amongst the crisis affected children and youths as an effort to promote sustainable quality education with incentivised enrolment and attendance to learning, reduce risks of cognitive underdevelopment and malnutrition, and provide access to safe and protective learning environments. The project will target a total of 45 schools with 15,661 total beneficiaries desegregated into 14,761 children and youths (8,119 B & 6,642 G), 450 teachers (250 M, & 200 W), 450 PTAs/SMCs (250 M, & 200 W); the response aims at provision of scholastic materials, establishment and renovation of TLSs, capacity building of teachers & PTAs/SMCs, providing school feeding, restoring and providing safe and protective education among boys and girls while integrating with already existing projects in intra-sectors (especially Health, Nutrition, FSL, WASH, and Protection). The project equally hopes to enhance psychosocial development of learners besides improving hygiene and sanitation. The project will use its existing M&E system to monitor report, feedback, document, and share evident lessons. The project is also designed to ensure maximum impact is achieved through the proposed interventions besides ensuring accountability to the affected population. The project will also sort to address the protection and SGBV challenges in schools besides incorporating comprehensive sexuality education into the integrated programme while linking and strengthening into the existing community based referral pathways.

1 - Needs

According to the education cluster assessment findings, it was evident that nationally, the biggest support required by primary schools is teaching and learning supplies (46.2%), this is followed by school feeding (44.5%), teacher salaries (43.7%), rehabilitation of infrastructure (35.2%) and teacher training (23.2%) (ECA, 2018). Recommendations to break through the supply-side barriers to education therefore include: Implement the food-for-work/school feeding policy, encouraging active PTAs, community constructed classrooms, school feeding, and school garden interventions for primary and secondary schools – particularly those located in remote and conflict-affected areas. Since 2011 HHs in the project target locations have experienced multiple shocks that have compounded the population’s vulnerability and increased food consumption gaps. Increased food consumption gaps have led to a very severe HHS. As schools can play an important role in feeding children and promoting good nutrition, hunger and nutrition are included as a supply-side barrier (Global Initiative on Out of School Children in South Sudan Report, May 2018). Over the past four years, the conflict and widespread insecurity have severely worsened the state of education across the country, only 41 per cent of the schools listed in EMIS 2013/2015 were non-functional at the time of the assessment and insecurity was overwhelmingly reported as the main cause of school closures in 2017. Compared to 2016, schools were able to reopen in some areas, while schools closed in others. In the wake of persistent fragility and conflict, at least 2.2 million school-aged children are estimated to be out of school with thousands more at risk of dropping out. The number of out-of-school children (OOSC) in South Sudan has increased in recent years and this trend is projected to continue, reaching over 2.4 million in the next two years, if present circumstances are maintained (OOSC Report, 2018). In the project target locations, fighting, displacement and emergency levels of food insecurity have interrupted education for children. Disease outbreaks such as cholera have also interrupted access to education as many schools have to close and children are not able to fully participate. In the areas where education continued, the quality of teaching deteriorated due to missed opportunities to train teachers and delays in payment of salaries. This interruption of education will have immediate, medium and long-term consequences for the stability and development of South Sudan. Apart from missed development, the longer children stay out of school, the more vulnerable they are to forced recruitment by armed actors. Children, parents and the communities consider education a critical priority (Situation Overview Report, 2018). According to the IPC classification report, if the current drivers of food insecurity worsen through the end of 2018, and in the absence of humanitarian assistance, there is a heightened risk of Famine in areas where large populations are already experiencing severe food insecurity including Uror, Nyirol and Pibor Counties. To prevent populations falling into Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5), as schools can play an important role in feeding children and promoting good nutrition, hunger and nutrition are included as a supply-side barrier. In light of the above analysis, most children in various learning centers of the project target locations have been majorly affected hence therefore there is dare need to develop an integrated intervention that will ensure that the educations system is restored by increasing access and quality of learning, hunger amongst the school age children is mitigated so as to improve their retention rates in schools, while ensuring that they risk of new cholera infections are significantly reduced by developing both short term and long term interventions.

2 - Activities

1) Support EiE enrollment and learning to 45 Primary Schools/ learning centers composed of 14,761 Learners (8,119 Boys & 6,642 Girls) in crisis affected areas; 2) Establish and rehabilitate 20 temporary learning spaces for out of school children and youths who have been affected by emergencies; 3) Provide psycho-social and recreation activities to create enabling environment for 14,761 Learners (8,119 Boys & 6,642 Girls) out of school children and youth for joining education interventions; 4) Provide teaching and learning materials including ECD kits to 14,761 Learners (8,119 Boys & 6,642 Girls) youths, children and 450 Teachers (250 M & 100 F) who have been enrolled into EiE programme; 5) Provide incentives to 200 teachers/ volunteers (120 M & 80 F) to offer teaching services to children and youths who have been affected by emergencies; 6) Train 450 Teachers (250 M & 100 F) teachers on: a) Life skills curriculum and materials, b) Basic pedagogy with focus on participatory and active methods, c) classroom management and lesson delivery; 7) Provide genders segregated WASH facilities (Latrines, Hand washing Points & Water drinking Points) and hygiene services to 45 school or learning centres/ spaces to improve the hygiene standards of learners and the learning environment; 8) Provide Integrated Emergency School Feeding Programme to 20 schools targeting 8,000 Learners (5,000 Boys & 3,000 Girls) in order to improve incentivized and sustainability enrollment of children and youths affected by emergencies to school/ learning centres; 9) Establish referral mechanisms links between 45 schools and health, nutrition, protection service providers to promote to promote good health to the children and youths affected by emergencies; 10) Provide trainings on PSS and school gender based violence (SGBV) prevention and management in 45 learning centers/ schools.11) Provide emergency-affected children with quality education and recreational material.12) Provide and distribute hygiene kits/ sanitary towels to 3,000 girls in the 45 targeted schools.

Yes, the target population to this project will be will be oriented on environment protection measures which will be integrated with WASH and Health aspects of the project; the teachers will be enlightened on how to ensure that learners are environmental sensitive and the modes that can be practically be applied to conserve it besides enhancing its purpose in human existence.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, during the project launch, the project team will clearly elaborate the exit and sustainability strategy to the stakeholders through the planned stakeholders’ workshop, their comprehensive involvement and participation of the local community to the programme from the onset will be very essential in regard to sustainability. The community will be encouraged to possibly develop a culture of cultivation and fractional contribution to the schools programme through the PTA arrangements as a best practice. The community through its stakeholders and the local authority will be briefed in details about the exit/ project end; they will be presented with the summary of the progress periodically since the onset of the project as agreed during the project launch stakeholders’ workshop.
