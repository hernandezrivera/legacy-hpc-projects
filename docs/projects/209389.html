Project Summary

The conflict between SAF and RCF has been one of the most violent conflicts in Sudan’s modern history. The conflict had led to one of the highest global displacements with over 6.1 million people forced to leave their homes, out of which 4.85 million have suffered internal displacement. In areas such as Darfur, especially West Darfur which has witnessed some of the most violent clashes, especially intertribal clashes, ADRA’s assessment has indicated a high need for NFI and Shelter as many households had to flee for their lives without the ability to carry of their essential items. According to ADRA’s assessment (Rapid Protection Assessment in Kerenk, Genina and Kulbus), had indicated that the majority of households being displaced internally are female led households, and without access to adequate Shelter items they facing increased risks of SGBV. Furthermore; many of the households report looting enroute to the three assessed localities, making those households unable to purchase the most basic of supplies.
In El-Gezira, Khartoum and White Nile, ADRA has reported mixed needs as well as the need for more tailored assistance. The majority of the households in those States are from Khartoum and other urban settings, around 76.3% of the IDPs report seeking shelter in rented accommodation. According to the livelihood coping strategy in El-Gezira, many have exhausted all their savings and are now asked to evacuate without a place to go to.
The current crisis has mandated a more flexible and tailored responses towards the growing needs. ADRA in 2023 had piloted several response modalities and through this has explored what would be suitable for the different beneficiary groups. In 2024 ADRA aims at continuing with emergency responses to serve a total of 15600 HH (9360000 individuals) in West Darfur, Khartoum, White and Blue Nile and El-Gezeira States. Assistance packages will include rental subsidies, NFI (both vouchers and in kind), cash for NFI and Emergency Shelter Kits.
ADRA aims at mixed modalities that are tailored to meet the needs of the different groups, as well as support the livelihood of the host communities in the area. In areas with active markets and where beneficiaries are able to procure their needs such as White Nile, Madani, Khartoum as well as some localities in Blue Nile, ADRA would opt for a voucher/cash provision to support the households meet their Shelter/NFI needs. This is supported with a thorough and comprehensive market assessment that takes into account needs, risks, market functionality and inclusive access. ADRA will also upscale the rental subsidies program that was successfully implemented in El-Gezira and White Nile state in 2023. ADRA will provide support through in-kind distribution in areas such as the Darfurs, and some area of Blue Nile to support displaced households. This will be done through the procurement centers that has been set up by ADRA in Chad as well as in White Nile.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection is not only mainstreamed into all activities but it's also takes a focus in the project. ADRA will ensure that communities are involved across the different stages of the project from development to closing. Working through local actors and grassroot community structures such as “Emergency Rooms” and “tribal committees” helps ADRA to adequately calibrate the programs according to the distinct need of each community. ADRA also ensures that a comprehensive conflict analysis is developed and regularly updated in each of the States that ADRA currently intervenes in. This comprises of the mapping of actors and players as well as the different ethnic, religious and political motivators. This can guide ADRA in it’s implementation and ensuring that targeting is not biased towards any political/religious/ethnic group especially in the current polarized environment. For Shelter, ADRA is deliberate on ensuring full and proper coordination with the different actors, groups, and the UN to ensure that provision of esk do not lead to a set-up of camps/settlements in land that might led to further conflict. Information on distributions and new displacement is regularly shared with the government/de-facto authorities. For cash programming, ADRA ensures a double layered approach to ensure accountability. This includes verification of all households and ensuring that lists are inclusive of different members and backgrounds. A PDM is always conducted and several modalities for complaints and feedback are shared with the community members. ADRA will monitor and evaluate both unintended and intended impacts of the program on the conflict, tensions, as well as power dynamics. Particular attention will be given to monitoring potential backlashes related to tensions over new individuals and groups gaining new authority and independence and others fearing to lose their role and authority. Disaggregation of data on indicators by ethnical/tribal belonging as well as other factors such as age, gender, and disability status will help to ensure that all groups are represented and heard and that specific conflict sensitivity concerns are detected timely. Techniques and methodologies for data collection will also be conflict sensitive.

ADRA's position of monitioring evaluation, learning and accountability MEAL, enhances the interlinkages between the four elements of programming. The complaint and feedback mechanisms have been designed to allow for quicker and more effective integration of feedback and complaint. The MEAL manager shares with the project manager feedback as is recorded in the CFRM register (described in 4.2 below). The feedback is then followed up on on a weekly basis, and integrated either through pipeline planning or in new projects.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

The Beneficiary Complaints Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) allows program participants to provide feedback on the design, implementation, systems, and staff of the program, allowing them to influence the decisions related to the program and motivate corrective actions if needed. Depending on the already existing and/or new modes of feedback, the communities will determine to use what best works for them and ADRA will support accordingly and work with the community to provide feedback and corrective action. One of the existing mechanisms that has been successful is the set-up of complaint boxes in centers and health facilities. ADRA also has established hotlines to ensure that complaints that come in through the hotlines are addressed adequately and confidentially. Traditionally, complaints also come through the local leaders, emergency rooms, and community elders, and in this proposed Action, complaints will also come through the CPBNs. ADRA appreciates that the CFRM must be community centered so that community members, especially women and PSNs will have the opportunity to determine any other modalities that suit them best. ADRA’s Protection Assistant (trained by ADRA International Protection Advisor) receives the inputs and logs them into the CFRM log sheet. The sheet follows a traffic categorization system, where red indicates serious and sensitive allegations or feedback (PSEA, Fraud, Corruption..etc), and these are sent directly to the Country Director. The Yellow allegations are also serious but not sensitive and can be addressed by the Project Manager, meanwhile green are for minor complaints or feedback that could be addressed directly by the Protection assistant or involve the field teams. Serious PSEA allegations are often reported to the donor within 48 hours of receiving them and an investigative committee is set up by ADRA Board of Directors and ADCOM (ADRA Sudan administrative Committee).
