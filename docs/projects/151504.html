Project Summary

Continuation and expansion of current PUI projects in eastern Libya, including Greater Benghzi and nearby cities such as Ajdabiya and Derna, and also Al Kufra. Focus areas of intervention include rehabilitation (or essential maintenance) of public and WASH infrastructure in conflict-affected communitied, WASH facilities in detention centres, as well as NFI and hygiene item support for displaced communities and detainees (for example bedding and winter items for detainees, shelter materials for IDPs). The target population of 30000 which includes 2500 migrants/asylum seekers/refugees (the estimated population in eastern Libya, based on what PUI is currently seeing in their activities), and 27500 IDPs, returnees and vulnerable/conflict-affected host communities.

Needs

In all Eastern Libya, deteriorating security situation resulted in damage on a significant part of public and community infrastructure and facilities (such as schools and health centres), while the secondary impacts of conflict on the supply chains, economy and institutions resulted in a failure to maintain basic services and infrastructure. Thus, in the case of the WASH Sector (according to the 2018 HRP), damaged and ageing water networks and equipment now result in the loss of around 35% of water, while pumping stations and treatment plants are in poor technical condition. This led to the fact that only 64 per cent of the affected population has access to adequate drinking water sources and 43 per cent of the population is currently relying on water trucking as the main sources of drinking water. The deterioration of these systems is also due to the unstable supply of electric power, including power cuts and surges. WASH activities are essential in PUI's integrated approach and naturally complements all related health activities. Substandard WASH conditions will contribute to a high burden of diarrhoeal and skin diseases that disproportionally affect the most vulnerable, as well as other conditions such as malnutrition.Generally speaking, the rehabilitation and reactivation of non-functional public infrastructure (WASH, health, education…) will restore the access to basic services for the most vulnerable, including host communities IDPs and returnees. This is also a significant factor in promoting peaceful co-existence between IDPs and host communities. In addition, many of the IDPs and returnees are in need of many items necessary for daily needs, including hygiene products, clothes, blankets (pertinent as winter is coming), and cooking items. As these are generally covered by the distribution of NFIs, PUI will work in the distribution of core relief items, including hygiene kits and some items which will improve the basic living conditions of the most vulnerable persons including their hygienic and health situation..In south-eastern Libya in particular, including al Kufra, local conflicts, isolation and lack of humanitarian presence, has deterred meaningful assistance, and also resulted in ongoing deterioration of services, especially in marginalised communities. Access issues (i.e. for certain ethnicities) also restrict the amount of support that has historically been given to these areas. This results in resentment and tension, which are a barrier to stabilisation in these areas.Regarding detention centres, the different actors intervening in these institutions (PUI, MSF, IMC, IOM…) agree on the poor WASH conditions. The access to clean water and hygiene facilities remains very limited. Skin diseases and other pathologies related to substandard WASH conditions are consequently very common among migrant populations.

Activities

• Rehabilitate Public/community structures (WASH infrastructure, schools...) in high priority areas to improve basic living conditions and reactivate essential services. Also rehabilitate WASH facilities in detention centres (water trucking as an immediate intervention until work to restore water supply can be completed)• Distribute NFI related to basic living conditions (hygiene kits, clothes, basic cooking items, winter items, waste items, etc) according to their needs - for example the distribution of clothing and winter items to detainees; distribution of cooking items or light shelter materials to families suddenly displaced.• Develop health and hygiene promotion activities focusing on hygiene practices to prevent disease complimentary to the use of hygiene items and NFIs.• Support waste management and implement vector control related to the prevention of diseases.
