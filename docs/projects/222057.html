Project Summary

South Sudan is facing a complex protection crisis, with 5.34 million people in need of protection services in 2025. This humanitarian situation is driven by severe climatic shocks, economic decline, the influx of returnees and refugees, and frequent intercommunal conflicts, all leading to increased displacement. Many displaced populations, particularly returnees from Sudan, are unable to access food, clean water, livelihood opportunities, and protection services, which leaves them far from recovery and rebuilding their lives, further increasing their dependence on humanitarian aid.
Additionally, recurrent subnational and localized violence between community-based militias and civil defense groups has displaced civilians, putting vulnerable groups—especially children, women, persons with disabilities, the elderly, and others—at heightened risk of violence and exploitation. These risks include killing, injury, family separation, GBV/CRSV, sexual exploitation and abuse, targeting of civilians, revenge killings, cattle rustling, property destruction, restricted freedom of movement, forced recruitment, abductions, and limited access to basic services, housing, and land. These threats, coupled with negative coping mechanisms, have exacerbated the protection crisis. Persons with disabilities are especially vulnerable, facing physical and psychosocial dangers such as drowning, abandonment during conflict, sexual exploitation, and lack of food, shelter, medical supplies, and assistive devices.
In response, CINA aims to assist 25,527 individuals (8,580 men, 10,462 women, 3,409 girls, and 3,076 boys), including 2400 persons with disabilities, with timely lifesaving protection services. These will include regular protection risk monitoring, conducted through tools like Key Informant Interviews, Focus Group Discussions, and Observation assessments. CINA will track the arrival of people from Sudan to South Sudan, raise awareness of available services, and provide information-sharing to ensure proper referrals. Protection support will also target returnees and internally displaced persons (IDPs) seeking reintegration, offering psychosocial support, access to documentation, and messages on prevention and peacebuilding. Community-based structures will be strengthened to support local protection efforts.
CINA will use a mix of static programming and roving responses, including cash assistance for comprehensive case management in line with protection principles. Protection analysis will be carried out in collaboration with the Protection Cluster to ensure conflict-sensitive, need-centered programming across sectors. Community engagement will be integral, with regular consultations, feedback mechanisms, and integration with communication efforts. CINA will regularly conduct protection risks monitoring using the Protection Cluster Protection Rapid Monitoring System (PRMS) guidelines. Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and protection mainstreaming, including SEA prevention, survivor support, accountability, and investigations, will be upheld with technical support from the PSEA Task Force.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan is unprecedented, with over 2.7 million people in need of protection assistance due to severe climatic shocks, economic decline, the influx of returnees and refugees, and frequent intercommunal conflicts leading to increased displacement. This crisis is worsened by the lack of local government capacity to ensure safety, security, and access to basic services, leaving vulnerable populations exposed to heightened protection risks. These groups, particularly women, girls, adolescents, the elderly, persons with disabilities, and other vulnerable individuals, continue to endure violence, loss of life, psychosocial distress, GBV, SEA, and other threats linked to negative coping mechanisms. Lakes, Upper Nile, and Jonglei are the most affected states, where women and girls report conflict and displacement as their primary protection concerns, which hinder their access to markets and violate their rights. Despite fragile stability from recent peace efforts by the state government, revenge attacks, road ambushes, and cattle-related violence remain prevalent. The October 2024 OCHA Flash Flood Updates for Jonglei and Upper Nile states highlight immense protection needs due to flash flood displacement, with about 158,069 new arrivals recorded since August 2024. The absence of political intervention and support for recovery has weakened the protective environment, leaving over 60% of IDPs, returnees, and refugees unwilling to return to their areas of origin until adequate security and settlement mechanisms are provided. During such crises, the most vulnerable, including children, women, adolescents, persons with disabilities, the elderly, and pregnant or lactating mothers, face even greater risks and vulnerabilities. Those with specific needs often lack access to protection information, livelihood opportunities, food, medical care, legal aid, shelter, NFIs, and psychosocial support. Tensions over land and community resources exacerbate existing vulnerabilities, particularly for women whose housing, land, and property (HLP) rights are often violated. GBV against women, girls, and persons with disabilities has become rampant, and survivors lack appropriate responsive services, as well as preventative and resilience-building support. The needs of persons with disabilities are often overlooked, and they bear the brunt of the crisis. Urgent action is needed to provide the essential protection services in line with the Protection Cluster's objectives for the 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan in the identified priority locations.

2. Project activities

1. Protection Rapid monitoring system (PRMS) will be conducted to inform protection and overall humanitarian response. This will be done through static programming in the state and field-based roving response in hard-to-reach areas. Protection Rapid Monitoring System (PRMS) submissions through (KII,Observation, and/or FGD tools will be strengthened within the community. Findings and recommendations will be shared with protection cluster for dissemination. 2. Border Monitoring for people upon arrival to South Sudan. CINA will conduct regular border monitoring to document new arrival of people from Sudan to South Sudan. 3.Conduct awareness-raising and information sharing. To support improvements of the general protection environment, CINA will conduct protection awareness campaigns at community level targeting 19,000 people (9,990 women, 9,910 men). The Awareness-raising and information sharing sessions will include topics on protection of the most vulnerable persons with specific needs including elderly, widows, orphans, pregnant and lactating women, persons with disabilities, prevention of family separation, abduction of children, recruitment of and use of children by armed groups/forces, harmful cultural practices, gender roles, prevention of GBV and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). 4. Identify and provide vulnerable people with protection concerns or specific needs with referral through static and mobile presence. CINA will map service providers as part of a holistic protection response in order to establish or improve referral pathways available to the communities during static and/or mobile response. After identifying the referral pathway, CINA will utilize the Community Management Committee (CMC) and Community Based Protection Networks who will help to identify individuals from the community with protection needs and referred them to the nearest service providers. 5. Identify and provide individuals with non-cash assistance through protection case management. CINA will establish CBPNs and train them on South Sudan vulnerability criteria for identification of people with specific needs (PSNs). The targeted PSNs will include persons with disabilities, elderly, widows, pregnant and lactating women, in need of general protection assistance and support. Once identified, CINA will conduct an individualized assessment to determine whether they indeed meet the vulnerability criteria and those with moderate needs and require referral will be put under a care plan and referred for case management in the areas of concerns.6. Identify and provide individuals with cash assistance through protection case management. To increase access to protection assistance for the most vulnerable people, CINA will establish CBPNs and train them on South Sudan vulnerability criteria for identification of people with specific needs (PSNs). The targeted PSNs will include persons with disabilities, elderly, widows, pregnant and lactating women, in need of general protection assistance and support. Once identified, CINA will conduct an individualized assessment to determine whether they indeed meet the vulnerability criteria and those with critical needs will be put under a care plan through individual protection assistance (IPA) and case management. 7.Community-based protection structures established or enhanced. CINA will be ensuring the establishment, or strengthening of community-based structures in support of community protection activities.8. Community-based protection activities implemented including resilience and PSS activities, PSEA, peace-building activities and training through AGD approaches through conducting peacebuilding awareness raising activities and training of community based protection networks (CBPN) and Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) committees on conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation and peace building. Particularly, youth will be involved as guardians for peace building in the community.
