Project Summary

Enhancing the integration and inclusion of children with disabilities in any sector requires a special approach facilitated by technical specialists, appropriate educational programs, individual assessments, and an educational plan that includes supporting services such as a family guidance plan, community sensitizing and awareness raising, capacity building for the
education personnel, infrastructure development, and activating the case management and referral system along with improving the quality and access of Education in NWS. To achieve such aims, Sened focuses on supporting children and youth with mild and moderate, mobility and mental, disabilities, through several levels.
Sened's interventions will be implemented in existing schools that provide formal and non-formal education (remedial classes) along with protection services to support children with and without disabilities with tailored activities that focus on removing the barriers and the negative attitudes along with raising the awareness of their families about their needs given that such steps need to be implemented gradually through the room of resources which provides Children with disabilities with initial assessments that identify the type and severity of their disabilities and their educational skills which contribute to including them in formal or non-formal classes while children without disabilities will be assessed after attending ASER test
which measures their educational level as a prior step towards referring them to formal or non-formal classes along with training them on implementing joint activities with their peers with disabilities at school under close supervision of Sened's technical staff. The 1st step that Sened takes into consideration is identifying different types of barriers through conducting a needs assessment that covers the project's proposed areas. The project will be implemented in 3 locations as follows: A’zaz Camps, Marret Tamasrin, and Harem taking into consideration the severity scales of those areas which are in order: 4/ 5/5.
Sened will implement the planned interventions through the following 8 components as follows:
1. Provision of formal & non-formal education services for Children with and without disabilities.
2. Capacity development for Teachers and Education Personnel:
3. Rehabilitation of learning spaces & WASH Facilities.
4. Providing schools with furniture and solar system.
5. Providing teacher kits & teacher incentives for those providing Formal & non-formal Education in Idleb and non-Formal Education in A’zaz.
6. Carrying out BTL campaigns.
7. Safety and security equipment.
8. Winterization kits and fuel for heating.
9. Awareness sessions about Cholera Pandemic.
Moreover, Sened will also provide protection intervention through the following activities: PSS, Case management & Referral including IPA, Awareness sessions, and parenting sessions.
The implementation of those components will take place by Sened's PSS & Special Education teams who will be trained on dealing with persons with different disabilities to enhance the quality and access of IDPs and HC to Education.
Regarding the protection interventions, they will be provided through a protection project implemented by Sened in the same locations.
Sened will make sure to share the targeted beneficiaries by filling the EDU 4W on monthly basis.
The total target of those interventions is 10200 BNs: the activities will target 500 children with and 2050 without disabilities (boys & Girls) aged 5 to 17 as well as their 1500 caregivers and 750 education personnel (Men & Women) aged >18.

3 - Needs

According to HNAP 2022, the rate of disabilities is likely to be higher amongst older individuals, regardless of sex. Findings are concerning as older individuals, especially those with disabilities are less likely to access sufficient employment and more likely to increase their dependency on household members. Likewise, households with a member with disabilities are half as likely to report having sufficient income to meet their needs. Capacity to survive a humanitarian crisis is correlated with both health, including mental health and financial security, both of which become increasingly less likely as an individual ages. Chronically high rates of children with disabilities is also concerning. Children with disabilities are some of the most marginalized in times of conflict, in-part because of the lack of reliable data regarding their needs, but also due to the significant disruptions in their education. Such developmental disruption may contribute to, or compound, additional health complications that cannot be reversed due to the lack of access to health services that support child development. Indeed, children with disabilities are significantly less likely than their peers to be engaged in education. According to HNO 2022, Education and child wellbeing are a long-term investment that necessitates a holistic approach. If learning is expected to take place and wellbeing expected to be fostered, education services need to be available, accessible and predictable.

4 - Implementation of activities

Sened is proposing to include children and youth with mild and moderate disabilities who are out of school and with learning difficulties. This service will be provided by conducting activities already mentioned in the project summary that aim at enhancing access and quality of Edu in addition to providing specialized protection services. As PSEA is considered one the most serious breach of accountability towards affected populations, PSEA is seen as a critical component of Sened accountability mechanisms. All Sened staff working for the Sened Syria mission are trained on the Code of Conduct, PSEA, and CP Policies and complaint response mechanisms. The training on PSEA aims to ensure that all staff adhere to the Sened Code of Conduct and are aware of their obligation to report any allegation or suspicion of PSEA and the sanctions taken for any breach of the Code of Conduct. As part of Sened’s accountability mechanism, the CRM enable Sened’s stakeholders to safely raise a concern or make a complaint in respect of its work and to be assured that appropriate action is undertaken. The CRM channels have to do with complaint phone number, Sened’s FB page, complaint box...etc. Sened shares with beneficiaries a brochure that includes all details about the complaint mechanism. Sened’s MEAL Officer will monitor the implementation of activities and provide weekly and monthly reports about the implementation including any barriers faced by beneficiaries.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Protection Risk/Threat Learning facilities could be attacked. Attacks may include: airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff / students or military use of the school. Mitigating Measures Threats and associated risks may be multiple and varied. They may be influenced by sex, age, geographic area, disability, time of day, time of year, events and other factors. These should be identified and to the extent possible measures taken to reduce the risks for each population group. Depending on the threats and risks, Sened will take the measures below : 1. Reducing time/distance travelled by decentralizing learning facilities. 2. Considering sunrise and sunset times when setting learning hours. 3. Providing students, their caregivers and school personnel information on safer routes/times to travel. 4. Providing students, their caregivers and school personnel information on what to do and what not to do if they face different types of threats. 2.Protection Risk/Threat Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect students, students participate in bullying or act violently towards other students and/or to teachers. Mitigating Measure Learning facilities should have an enforced code of conduct against all forms of violence and unacceptable conduct, including bullying....etc.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sened protects the privacy of beneficiaries and vulnerable groups by adapting and securing their data, adhering to the "Do-No-Harm" concept, and maintaining their dignity. Beneficiaries are informed on how to use effective feedback and complaint method. This mechanism comprises established reporting procedures and channels for sexual exploitation and abuse. The PSEA idea is also considered in the awareness-raising themes provided for children and adults in order to raise their awareness of their rights and safety, as well as the safety measures to report such misconduct. Risk: Affected populations and Sened staff, are placed at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, including harassment. Mitigating measures: Training will be provided to Sened staff PSEA Policy and Code of Conduct. which would be provided by our specialized staff in protection to other staff in Sened 1. SEA by humanitarian workers male-dominated, gatekeepers, and potential other beneficiaries. 2. Risk of violence from beneficiaries among themselves/with humanitarian workers when huge onset occurring (crowd control) Likelihood: High. Impact: High in terms of reputation, trust. mitigation measures: 1. Establish a feedback mechanism 2. Recruit female staff and train them. 3. Train staff on code of conduct 4. All staff contracts to include PSEA 5. Community committee with equitable representation of women 6. Train staff on the Inter-Agency Coordination in Community-Based Complaint Mechanisms.
