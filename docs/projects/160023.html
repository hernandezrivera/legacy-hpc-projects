Project Summary

The current drought, caused by weak 2018 autumn (Deyr) rains coupled with delayed spring (Gu) rains and poor regeneration of pasture, availability water and crop production affects the whole country. Post-Gu assessment results indicate that most of the 14 major Internally Displaced Person (IDP) settlements are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Stressed (IPC Phase 2) in the presence of humanitarian assistance. In most urban areas, mostly stable food prices and employment opportunities have helped to sustain food security outcomes in Stressed (IPC Phase 2) or Minimal (IPC Phase 1). However, households in urban areas in Sool, Sanaag and Hiraan regions are facing food consumption gaps and are classified as Crisis (IPC Phase 3), driven by the high cost of living & limited income-earning opportunities.
Without sustained humanitarian assistance, 2.1 million people will face Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or higher) outcomes. An additional 4.2 million people are expected to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people facing acute food insecurity to 6.3 million. Current levels of humanitarian assistance must be sustained through December in order to prevent anticipated food security Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes. Livelihoods support is also required for people that are Stressed or worse (IPC Phase 2 or higher. The project addresses the crises facing more than 6.3 million vulnerable households in Somalia.
The Action aims to prevent further depletion of assets, short term support, promotion of income generation activities and livestock related interventions to stem food insecurity and livelihoods to mitigate costlier interventions in the future. The Action is therefore designed in line with the needs & priorities identified by the food security cluster to prevent further deterioration of food security and livelihoods of vulnerable households & thus directly contributes to the HRP food security cluster objectives which are:
Objective 1: To improve immediate access to food, priority activities include unconditional transfers (e.g. food assistance and cash), conditional transfers (e.g. cash-for-work for small-scale infrastructure repairs). Approximately 1.6 million people facing severe levels of food insecurity (IPC Phases 3, 4 and 5) will be supported under this objective.
Objective 2: To protect and restore livelihoods related to food and income sources. Priority activities include provision of: (i) seasonally appropriate agricultural inputs (e.g. certified quality seed, farm tools, training, land preparation & irrigation support); (ii) emergency livestock assistance (e.g. supportive treatment, vaccination, feed, fodder production); (iii) livelihood diversification (e.g. fishing, backyard poultry and vocational programmes); (v) improved post-harvest practices
Objective 3: To support resilience of vulnerable populations against shocks. This objective will contribute to protecting & conserving natural resources on whose lives & livelihoods depend, such as water, soil, land & forest resources.
VSF Germany is proposing an integrated humanitarian response for food security & livelihoods intervention response to address immediate food access for households in IPC3&4 through unconditional cash transfer, protect and restore livelihoods through animal health services, provision of supplementary livestock feed & unconditional & conditional cash transfer(CfW), livelihood recovery; capacity building through training of CAHWs. The Action targets 3300hh in IPC 3&4 (including IDPs) with unconditional cash transfer for immediate food access; 3750hh with income opportunities through Cfw in IPC2 with livelihood support; 9700hhs with provision of supplementary livestock feed, community capacity building on zoonotic diseases (one health) handling, detection, control & animal health services for asset & livelihood protection; training-for capacity building(160 CAHWS).

Project Activities

Activities or outputs:The major activities or intervention proposed: • Conditional and unconditional cash transfer, • Cash voucher for water access for human and livestock consumption• Provision of supplementary livestock feed, • Animal health treatment and services through voucher system, • Capacity building -training for CAHWs • Community awareness on zoonotic diseases prevention and control measures.

Project Indicators

Indicators and targets:• 80% of beneficiaries increased their income.The activity will support women involved in income generating activities as a means strengthening efforts for durable solutions to the recurrent shocks. The targets are women groups involved in income generating activities supported by the project • 12,750 of livestock treated:This activity will focus on treatment and /or supporting vaccination of livestock against endemic diseases which affect livelihood. The aim is to protect productive livelihood assets and enable the pastoralists withstand shock and establish durable solutions. The targets households in stress conditions (IPC 2).• 2550 households benefited from provision of Supplementary livestock feed. This activity will focus on protecting livestock affected by shortage of feeds during drought periods. Supplementary feeding will enable lactating animals get nutrition and sustain milk production and also enable the young ones to survive through the drought period and provide a durable solution to pastoralists after the stressful period. Targets are households with lactating animals which remain at home when other animals migrate to far grazing areas. • 2550 households receiving animal health services. • 2550 households receiving animal health services; targets are households in stressful situation (IPC2) who own livestock and are at risk of decimation due to livestock diseases.• 1200 Households will receive cash voucher for water access. Thais activity focuses on emergency support to poor households for immediate access to safe water for domestic use. The targets are poor households who cannot access safe water from commercial suppliers for domestic use due to lack of cash. Cash voucher will be preferred as it will condition to use of the voucher for a specific item needed by the household and avoid possibility of using the cash for other needs at expense of water access. Also private water suppliers also exist in the areas during drought. • 4600 Households trained on zoonotic diseases prevention and control measures.The activity will focus on creating awareness among pastoral and agro pastoral communities on common zoonotic diseases, their causes, transmission, and control methods. The aim is to control and prevent spread of zoonotic diseases within the communities and bring in durable solutions to common diseases linked to animals and their products. The targets are livestock herders and general community who interact with animals. • 1600 Community Animal Health Worker (CAHWs] trained and linked with private pharmacies and public veterinary system.The targets are community based animal health service providers who are the main animal health service providers in target locations. This cadre of people are well trained in many areas by NGOs and UN Agencies and provide durable animal health services to pastoralists. • 90% of children and women with improvement on nutritional status.The targets are households with malnourished children, breast feeding, and pregnant mothers.

Mitigation Measures

2a. Exclusion and marginalization: Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistanceMitigation plans:• Collect and use data disaggregated by age, gender and diversity to effectively inform programming. • Apply a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries – otherwise vulnerable persons, such as older people, may easily be excluded as other more mobile and vocal population groups are registered. • Ensure lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing services. • Communicate information through various means to reach the broader community and to account for different literacy levels (eg. door-to-door, poster, radio, social media, and use of pictograms). • Identify the power dynamics in the area. Who has access to food, assets and inputs? Who is able to influence decisions on food, agriculture and livelihoods? This information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access, discrimination against particular groups or whether assistance is being diverted. 2b. Exclusion and marginalization: Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processesMitigation plans:• Ensure the location, distance and access routes to the distribution points is accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. • Separate male and female queuing areas and ensure female staff presence at distributions so that they are accessible to women and girls. • Introduce special arrangements for persons who have difficulty accessing the distribution point or unable to carry the rations (such as persons with restricted mobility or marginalized groups) - community mobilization, home/mobile distributions, youth groups assisting older or chronically ill persons. It should not be assumed that friends and family will do it. 3b. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance.Mitigation plans:• Ensure the location of and access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly distribution. • Ensure the mode and frequency of distribution minimizes safety risk – be aware that beneficiaries may face theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion whilst taking their rations home. • Where feasible, consider door-to-door distribution • Consider the frequency of distributions – the greater the time between distributions the larger the quantities distributed, which puts beneficiaries at risk of attacks and pillage. • Consult both displaced and host communities about food security and livelihoods needs and assess whether assistance could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities.4d. Protection mainstreaming: Barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanismsMitigation plans:• Be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures and processes that affect them. • Set up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary exposing themselves to project staff. • RESPOND to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place.• Organize awareness raising sessions so that people know how it works. • Consider a joint feedback mechanism with other sectors to minimize confusion. • They can play a key role in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and be proactive in ensuring the voice of marginalized groups is represented.

In Somalia in particular, the FSC will aim to improve the timeliness of responses in order to provide a more effective and cost-efficient response.
