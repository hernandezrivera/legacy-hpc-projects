Project Summary

TADO’s overall programme strategy in South Sudan is to serve the affected population through the five of its ‘core thematic areas: Shelter/NFI, Health and Nutrition, Education, Food Security, Peacebuilding and protection programming. The overall aim of TADO’s education strategy in South Sudan is to provide vulnerable children and youth affected by conflict and displacement with access to equitable and quality education in a safe and protective environment. The project will adopt child-friendly and community focused approaches in messaging strategies especially in BTL and Girl Child campaigns, ensuring that maximum outreach and impact is achieved. three thousand nine hundred and eighty five 3985 ( 1952 F, 2035 M) will be targeted by the project for both primary and ECD schools(classes).3000 (1470 Girls and 1530 boys ) in Primary schools 985(485 girls and 500 boys) in ECD centers in targeted 10 schools under this project which include internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, host communities’ children and youth with disabilities, who have limited access to education services in Gogrial west, Gogrial East County of Warrap state and Ulang and Nasir counties of Upper Nile State. This intervention will support safe reopening of schools and improve coping mechanism by pupils and teachers in class through establishment/ construction of Semi-permanent/temporary learning spaces(TLS) to the schools ,Provision of WASH facilities fitted with hand washing facilities, Training of teachers in numeracy and pedagogy, Training of school management bodies, improving copying mechanisms among children including distribution of learning and teaching materials in line with the five strategic goals of the Government's General Education Strategic Plan (GESP the 2017- 2022), to increase access to general education and promote equity and improve quality of general education which includes expansion and rehabilitation of schools, elimination of barriers to girls' education and promotion of gender equality, provision of alternative and accelerated learning opportunities for out-of-school children and youth, increase enrolment in schools, build institutional and human capacity and promote early childhood education. It is also in line with the education component of the Humanitarian Need Response Plan (HNRP 2025) which focuses on front line life-saving and age-appropriate education services including early childhood development, primary education, accelerated learning programme, vocational training and life skills. This Action will also contribute to the achievement of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development Goals (SDG2030) specifically to Goal #4 - Ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all. TADO will adhere to all the technical specifications of Ministry of Education construction guidelines and also within from the support TADO construction manager for the construction of the semi-permanent/TLS establishment, WASH facilities and the handwashing facilities including the necessary visibility guidance

1. Needs

Sub national conflict, Food insecurity, economic crisis and Flooding have contributed to a worsening crisis in the education system in South Sudan . In 2024, both Upper Nile and Warrap States were hit by above average rainfall that has caused flooding in most of the counties of the 2 states. An IRNA assessment carried out in September 2024 in Warrap State by partners found an unprecedented suffering of the people displaced by flooding in the counties of Gogrial East and Gogrial west with a total of 12,341 HH displaced translating to about 74,044 individuals in the 15 payams. Frequent arms conflict between civilians and army witnesses this year in Nasir and Ulang couples with flooding has exacerbated conditions in both counties that is already dire. An IRNA conducted by partners in Nasir in September 2024 and another in Ulang counties in November 2024 has over 8,214HH displaced both conflict and flooding translating to 49,284. Damage of farmland and food crops, collapse of shelters including schools, threat from waterborne diseases including malaria, occupation of schools by the displaced persons and lack of safe drinking water are some of the needs that the displaced and the host community are facing including protection and GBV issues. Flooding has led to exposure of populations of both IDPs and Host to diseases noticed but high rate of Malaria and malnutrition, bites from vectors including snake bites have been reported and are causing death in all these four counties affected. An estimated 7.980 children are affected in Gogrial East, Gogrial West, Ulang and Nasir counties and some schools were closed. Out of school Boys and girls continue to be exposed to dangers of protection issues including early marriages/pregnancies among young girls and boys engaging in gangs/drugs or join cattle raiding groups or are forces to join armed gangs for the case of Nasir. Food insecurity has caused rise to domestic violence among women seen as breadwinners as they could not provide enough food. Based recent released IPC projections for September-November 2024, 3 counites of Ulang Nasir and Gogrial West are ranked at Critical/Emergency levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4) and further both Gogrial East and Nasir populations wll be experiencing this effect again December 2024 to March 2025 and the 4 counties again April and July 2025. Many women and girls continue to be exposed to sexual exploitation or GBV issues. Displacement has eroded the schools with trained personnel i.e., teachers, SMC/PTA, as many have fled their homes to other areas. These has left untrained teachers and local SMC/PTAs to manage schools. The current food insecurity will lead to lack of coping in classes with the re-opening of the schools. Psychosocial Issues among children is evidence due to them being exposed to displacement through fighting that leads to losing love ones. Waterborne illness have become rampant due to breakdown proper waste disposal which is predicted to worsen and there is need for robust awareness and mitigation through fixing of more WASH facilities. Many schools have been looted or damaged by flood thus needs rehabilitations and provision of educational supplies for both learners and teachers. More dignified and protective wash facilities needed for both boys and girls. Teachers and school authorities will need capacity building to support management of schools in emergency context and delivery safe and protective services. Protection and GBV issues will be mainstream throughout the project implementation.

2. Project activities

This initiative will leverage TADO's past experiences in Education in Emergencies (EiE) across Ulang, Nasir, Gogrial East, and Gogrial West counties. It seeks to build on TADO's former EiE work to attain the anticipated outcomes. The planned activities include:• Conducting "Back to Learning" campaigns, advocacy, and community mobilization in the four counties to motivate children, parents, and community leaders to enroll their children in school.• Constructing eight semi-permanent temporary learning spaces (TLS) totaling 16 classrooms, with three in Nasir County of Upper Nile and five TLS in Gogrial West and Gogrial East Counties of Warrap State.• Establishing gender-segregated WASH facilities in semi-permanent TLS, with five in Gogrial East and West Counties of Warrap and three in Nasir County of Upper Nile, following INEE guidelines and ensuring the needs of children with disabilities are addressed.• Enrolling and monitoring out-of-school children from IDPs, returnees/refugees, including host communities, with special attention to girls and children with disabilities.• Providing training in numeracy and pedagogy to teachers in all 8 targeted schools, reaching 83 teachers (21 females, 62 males).• Offering training to school inspectors and county education officials on education in emergency contexts, benefiting 10 school inspectors (1 female, 9 males).• Training Parents and Teachers Association (PTA) members and County Education Officials in school management, Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), and climate change.• Implementing training in life skills, including psychosocial support, mine risk education (MRE), peacebuilding, and environmental protection within the education community.• Distributing educational materials to students and teachers.• Setting up a feedback mechanism desk to ensure the project adheres to the Accountability to Affected Populations framework.• Developing tailored educational programs for girls.
