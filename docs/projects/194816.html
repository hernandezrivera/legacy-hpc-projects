Project Summary

Objective: Reducing food insecurity and inability to meet basic immediate needs, through monthly food security assistance, emergency ready-to-eat rations
The objective will be achieved through providing food security assistance, and emergency food assistance. The program will achieve its purpose by improving food security, reducing reliance on risky coping mechanisms, and enabling current and newly displaced HHs and vulnerable communities to simultaneously meet their immediate multifaceted food needs with dignity and flexibility. This will further support the overall program goal by addressing lifesaving needs and promoting self-resilience leading toward early recovery.
The project aim to target 50K HHs with monthly cash for food distribution in Aleppo ( Azaz, Al Bab, Jarablous, Jabel Saman) and Idleb ( Idleb, Ariha, Harim, Jisr-Ash-Shugur)
The proposed modality is e-voucher, where each BNFs will get 80 USD per round for 8 rounds
The selection criteria are:
Families with no regular income / limited income (24 USD per member) (Total of regular income from assets & business & remittances)
2 or more family members unless one elderly living alone (above 60)
Families are living in the targeted area/community/camp
Targeted families are not from the contracted vendors (for vouchers)

3 - Needs

according to HNO 2023, Worsening socio-economic conditions are expected to exacerbate needs in 2023. Income insufficiency and degradation of livelihoods, diminished purchasing power, the contraction of the economy, lack of basic services, inflationary trends in key goods and services, widespread low productivity of inputs, and the impact of sanctions on availability and prices of basic commodities, specifically fuel, will continue to affect people’s ability to meet their most basic needs. Children, female-headed households, women and adolescent girls, people with disability and the elderly will continue to be disproportionally affected. An increased number of people are likely to be displaced due to economic reasons or poor conditions in places of displacement. The food security situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated since last year. In 2023, an estimated15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68 per cent ofthe total population. At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure, including all 2 millionpeople living in camps who are deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure, and 2.9 million people areestimated to be at risk of food insecurity, in addition to the cholera outbreak which spreading fast and as of 5th November 2022, there were 33,567 suspected cases and 2,002 confirmed cholera cases,158 including 92 associated deaths.

4 - Implementation of activities

Activity 1. Monthly food security assistance: CARE will enable 250,000 vulnerable individuals (137,500 female and 112,500 male; 50,000 HHs) to meet their basic food needs through providing monthly Cash distribution (cash in hand) over a period of 8 months. Each HH will receive approximately $80 per month, reflecting a total planned cash distribution (after 8 months). Due to the deteriorating status for both IDPs and host communities. Activity 2. Emergency Food Assistance: As part of CARE’s RRM, CARE will distribute one-off in-kind Emergency Ready-to-Eat (RTE) rations (value $50) to 50,000 newly displaced individuals (10,000 HHs) that are not yet settled and cannot access cooking utensils. The NWS FS Cluster continues to consider emergency food assistance as a frontline IDP response. These RTE kits provide 2,100 Kcal per person/day for a family of 5 for a duration of 5 days. 2,100 Kcal is the Sphere and FSC standard for providing basic nutritional daily necessities.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Food Assistance protection risks include continued negative coping strategies among women and girls (as noted above). Distribution sites may lack gender-sensitive planning in terms of distribution locations, routes, and times or maybe without the presence of female staff. Cash assistance has been shown to impact gender dynamics in both positive and negative ways. As part of CARE's do-no-harm approach, we routinely collect and analyze multiple data points during & after distribution to ensure a safe and effective response that maximizes the positive and minimizes the potentially negative outcomes associated with the cash modality. As a part of CARE’s Post distribution monitoring process, participants will be interviewed about how food is shared within the HH, who makes decisions on the assistance is being used, and tpositive and negative impacts of the assistance, among other areas. These questions aim to capture the gender-related dynamics, assessing changes or exploring opportunities for change towards more equitable practices and norms that will inform CARE strategies. CARE has a vast amount of experience in the area of cash and gender globally and relies on its global technical expertise as well as contextual experience to understand the gendered impact of cash transfers and address the potential risks as noted above. CARE is currently completing a study under COMRES I to increase its understanding of the gender dynamics and impacts related to its cash intervention

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Renewal of cross-border resolution: The current UN Security Council Resolution (UNSCR) 2585 expires on 10th Jan 2023. Non-renewal would have a significant impact on our activities and other essential services being run by UN agencies. CARE has developed a draft UNSCR 2585 non-renewal contingency plan and action plan which provides details on CARE’s planning efforts, scenario forecasting, and options to immediately adapt and scale-up programming to address related gaps and needs.• Security monitoring: Security levels do not prevent implementation. CARE continuously monitors the NWS security situation through Cluster partners and the UN Access WG and has proposed a flexible approach to shift locations, activities, and modalities, as required.• Humanitarian access: CARE and its IPs will be able to operate in the targeted areas. CARE’s coordination with stakeholders, participation in NWS Clusters, and its neutrality, will enable CARE to monitor any context changes and ensure to continue to reach these communities.• Population movements: Displacement may continue during the program. Activities will therefore have maximum flexibility across sectors and modalities to ensure vulnerable individuals are reached and that our response takes these conditions into account.• Local markets and liquidity: Local markets remain open, functional, and accessible, with sufficient availability and liquidity. CARE will use alternative modalities, such as RTE kits distribution
