Project Summary

The project will provide life-saving child protection prevention and response services through provision of Comprehensive case management services and referrals for children with critical protection concerns; Psychosocial Support (PSS) activities; Child protection awareness raisings or campaigns and Strengthening of community-based child protection mechanisms to prevent and respond to child protection concerns targeting 35,617 most vulnerable individuals (12,657 girls,10,783 boys, 6,777 women and 5,400 men). The project will be implemented Plan’s existing field locations of Eastern Equatoria (Kapoeta East and Torit); Lakes (Rumbek Centre, Wulu, Yirol East, Awerial); Central Equatoria (Lainya and Yei) and in Jonglei (Pibor). The project will target IDPs as well as host communities. Plan International will coordinate closely with the Child Protection Sub-Cluster and UNICEF in implementing this project. Further, in the different field locations, Plan will work closely with other child protection partners (Save the Children, DRDA, TDH, GREDO and Caritas Torit) to avoid overlapping and duplication of efforts. For instance, will work in different payams or bomas from other CP actors in the target locations. Plan will also coordinate with CP partners through referrals of CP cases.

Needs

According to OCHA over 6 million people are estimated to be in need of humanitarian protection (56.4% female and 61% children), including IDPs, host communities, communities affected by violence and refugees. There are wide-scale and severe violations of International Humanitarian Law and Human Rights Law, including grave violations against children. At least 2,700 verified incidents of grave violations against children affecting over 81,000 children have been reported (UNICEF). Furthermore, UNICEF estimates that about 19,000 children are currently recruited into armed forces/groups. The ongoing fighting and the resulting displacement continue to separate families, with an estimated 12 000 children in need of family tracing and reunification. Gaps in psychosocial support structures for children and caregivers persist due to break down of family structures and the local protective mechanisms. There are an unmet needs regarding the social and emotional wellbeing of children and caregivers. Gender Based Violence (GBV) is widespread. Although the full extent of gender based violence (GBV) is unknown, there is a large amount of evidence showing that GBV is an ongoing risk for women and girls throughout the country. According to UNICEF, nearly 25% of documented incidents of conflict-related sexual violence affect children. Landmines and unexploded ordnance pose constant threats to the civilian population. In Pibor (Jonglei), intermittent inter-ethnic fighting (the recent one being April 2018) between the Murle and the Lou Nuer has exposed the Pibor communities to multiple vulnerabilities with limited access to basic services. Age-set fighting is common place. Girls face the risk of child marriage and early booking as cultural practice and adolescent boys and male youths continue being at risk of recruitment into the armed groups and forces to protect their communities. 75% of FGDs (Plan, March 2018) held reported the existence of children engaged in paid work. Community members have limited knowledge and capacity to provide a protective environment for children. In Awerial, partner mapping conducted by Plan staff reveals that there are no partners implementing child protection in Site zero and in host population locations such as Dor, Bunagok, Alel and Abuyung payams. However, CP issues such as, psychosocial distress, child marriage, lack of parental care, sexual exploitation, child labour and harmful cultural practices persist. According to the IRNA (June 2018) IDPs from Rumbek Centre living in Yirol East continue to face multiple vulnerabilities associated with displacement.The assessment found out that 6 children (all boys) were reported missing on their way to Pagarau; 30 unaccompanied and separated children were documented of age categories (0-5; 6-10 and 11-17 years), and 3 orphans (1 girl and 2 boys). Additionally 3 more children drowned in river Muony. Two rape cases were reported (one involving a girl while another involving a pregnant woman). Many of the assessed persons said that women and girls risk sexual violence/exploitation due to poor housing/shelter and as they must walk longer distances to collect water and firewood. If not addressed GBV cases will be on the rampant. Psychosocial needs persist due to displacement and fear of attacks. In Yei, there is potential for other emerging emergency shocks. Yei borders Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and there is a high chance that if not properly contained Ebola may spread to South Sudan. Further, if the peace process translate into something meaningful, there is a high chance of sustained returns. This in turn will increase the protection needs of the host communities and IDPs living in Yei. Also, in some pockets of Yei such as Kupera and Mukaya localized conflicts between the Government forces and IO are common place.

Activities

1. Identification and documentation of children with critical protection concerns2. Provision of case management services and referrals to boys and girls with critical protection concerns (UASC, child marriage, CAFAAG, sexual violence)3. Provision focused and non-focused PSS activities for children in schools and communities4. Training of caseworkers and CP staff on Child Protection case management and child protection5. Training of caregivers on positive parenting skills and PFA; establish peer to peer support groups for the caregivers. The groups will be tasked to conduct home visits to reduce child maltreatment and abuse.6. Establish or strengthen CBCPMs (community based child protection committees, youth groups) and train them on child protection7. Establish peer to peer adolescent clubs in the communities 8. Establish or strengthen child rights clubs in schools and in CFSs9. Conduct child protection awareness raisings or campaigns on prevention and response to child protection issues and on referral mechanisms.10. Capacity building of government officers on CP11. Training of non-child protection staff on CP12. Mapping of the existing child protection systems including service providers13. Conduct child protection risks assessments in target locations to identify child protection risks and concerns, regular monitoring visits and provide reports that will assist in informing child protection response and advocacy

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Plan International will conduct project kick off meeting with representatives of the community (women, men, boys and girls). During this meeting information on the assistance to be provided by Plan will be shared. Community representatives will be given an opportunity to seek clarification on what to expect from Plan. Additionally, Plan will conduct awareness raising with community members through awareness sessions, radio, IEC materials (t-shirts, caps). Meeting with beneficiaries will be held regularly and on a needs basis. Plan will also work through child rights clubs and CBCPNs whose role is to increase awareness on CP issues to pass information on Plan activities.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Plan will provide context specific messages that are age and gender sensitive. For instance, messages on danger of child marriage will be designed together with the community members in order to reach boys, girls, men and women separately. Further, messages will also be tailored according to the specific CP issues happening in each context taking into account age and gender sensitivity. Messages will be designed according to the different audiences targeted by the project.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Plan has a Code of conduct that staff, volunteers and contractors sign on and is bound by it.. beneficiaries will also be trained and oriented on how to report alleged PSEA issues/ complains

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Plan has held consultations with children in the child friendly spaces through FGDs (separate groups for boys and girls) in order to understand their different needs, meetings with members of community based child protection networks (consisting of different representation from the community) and youth groups have been held. Household interviews have been conducted with head of households. One on one interviews with beneficiaries from target locations have been held to understand the main needs of the community.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Plan International has a community feedback mechanism where beneficiaries are informed on how to channel their issues/ complains to the organisation. they are allowed to choose the most convenient option for them, for example suggestion boxes or feedback meetings depending on the nature of the complaint and other factors such as literacy, confidentiality etc and the complaints are addressed or referred upwards depending on severity. with children FGD are normally used. the feedback is used to improve project implementation and quality.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Community will be engaged during the identification of the needs (needs assessment). Hence it’s critical for them to be involved in the monitoring of the project to assess whether the project meets the identified needs (of men, women, boys and girls). For instance, Plan will hold FGDs with children in order to get their views on how the CFS activities are being implemented and possible areas of improvement. In addition, the selected CBCPNs/youth groups will be tasked to monitor and evaluate Plan’s activities as the designated locations and provide feedback. A separate reporting template will be provided to them which they will use to evaluate our activities. Moreover, the local authorities including church leaders will be informed of their role to monitor and provide feedback on Plan’s activities.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will identify the different protection risks of the community and develop mitigation measures which in turn will inform our decision making and strategic operation. For each activity to be conducted, Plan will conduct a risk assessment using an internal risk assessment template which outlines possible risks in implementing the activity and mitigation measures. This will be done jointly with the beneficiaries. A ‘Do no Harm’ approach will be adopted in the implementation of activities in order to ensure the safety and dignity of beneficiaries. The needs of women, girls, men and boys will be addressed separately based on their needs. Plan will engage with the local authorities in the designated locations to ensure that our activities are context appropriate

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will identify the different needs of men, women, boys and girls taking into account age, diversity and inclusion. For instance, the selection criteria of beneficiaries will be developed together with community representatives to ensure that different populations groups are considered based on their vulnerability. Disabled children will also have access to the CFSs and CRCs. In the target locations, Plan has already established good working relationship with the local authorities and it’s anticipated that the relationship will continue limiting any challenges to access locations.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will address protection threats faced by beneficiaries by age, gender, diversity and according to context. For instance, child marriage among adolescent girls will be addressed through awareness sessions with adolescent children, local authorities and caregivers. Gender inequality which is rampant in South Sudan will be addressed through engaging men and boys in awareness sessions in order to encourage positive masculinity and discourage negative masculinity. Women will also be encouraged to take an active role in decision making process. Child neglect and violence will also be addressed through mass awareness campaigns and trainings of CBCPNs, youth groups and key stakeholders.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Plan will ensure that there is equal and non-discriminatory access of assistance and services by the targeted beneficiaries (age, gender and diversity) with specific needs. The project will measure access of beneficiaries through participants’ lists of beneficiaries attending awareness sessions, trainings and meetings. Also, through the CFS attendance sheet which will determine the age and gender of children participating in the CFS activities. Plan will work closely with local actors at targeted locations to ensure increased access to targeted beneficiaries and limited constraints. The ability of the CBCPNs/Youth groups to conduct their activities within the targeted locations without access restrictions will also inform accessibility to beneficiaries. Plan will conduct FGDs with target beneficiaries (women, men, boys and girls) which reveal whether or not they were reached by our activities.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

This will be measured by the number of risk assessments that done prior to conducting activities. The principle of do no harm will be used while conducting activities. The safety will be measured by beneficiaries’ willingness to provide, share or report any information with Plan without feeling threatened, intimidated or afraid. Also, if the beneficiaries approach Plan staff on their own without any form of coercion. If beneficiaries say/tell Plan that they feel safe.Also, FGDs with community members or community representatives. Through the number of security incidents reported that interfered with project activity or affected beneficiaries

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project activities will take into account the dignity of men, women, boys and girls by addressing their specific needs. The dignity of beneficiaries will be measured through FDGs with beneficiaries to find out how they feel about Plan activities in relation to their dignity and also the conduct of Plan staff towards them.
