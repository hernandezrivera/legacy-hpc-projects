Project Summary

WFP, as the lead agency for Logistics Sector, aims to oversee all the strategic objectives from the sector, namely: 1. provide logistics services (inclusive of surface transportation, transhipment, and warehousing) to humanitarian organisations responding to the Syria crisis; 2. maintain regional WoS interagency logistics coordination and information management in order to support the overall humanitarian response; 3. enhance the response capacity of humanitarian actors via dedicated logistics trainings and the procurement of necessary equipment; and 4. establish the United Nations Humanitarian Air Service (UNHAS) to provide safe, reliable and sustainable air access to beneficiaries for the humanitarian community, medical and security evacuations and facilitating the movement of light cargo where required.

4 - Needs

Key needs identified in consultation with partners include common warehousing, common transport and inter-agency convoys in order to ensure a predictable and secure logistics supply chain in the midst of access and security constraints. In addition, the increasing trend of cross-border operations into Syria necessitates the extension of coordination activities and expansion of logistics hubs where required. In line with partners’ needs to enhance logistics capacity, dedicated trainings will continue to be organised in 2020, focusing on more diverse areas within logistics. The Logistics Sector also remains on standby to facilitate emergency access to fuel as a last resort, in case availability and humanitarian needs become critical. Finally, a needs survey conducted in December 2019, indicated that the vast distances and limited access to commercial airlines in Syria posed an access challenge to humanitarian organisations. Responding actors showed a need for domestic passenger and cargo flights operating from Damascus to Qamishli and Deir-Ez-Zor as well as other regions in Syria. The sector maintains a flexible approach that allows it to adapt its services, taking into account the situational needs, and augment or reduce capacities as required.

5 - Implementation of activities

The Logistics Sector will maintain common logistics services by ensuring that warehouse contracts for different locations are in place for 2020 in order to maintain sufficient storage capacity inside Syria, and coordinating inter-agency humanitarian deliveries to newly accessible areas by communicating with the different actors. The Logistics Sector will also coordinate cross-border transshipment operations by setting contracts with the hub owners and taking all the necessary security measures so that the operations will not be interrupted. In addition, the Logistics Sector will continue to organise meetings in Damascus and other field locations to better respond to the different needs of organisations and provide partners with operational updates and logistics information on regular basis. Regarding UNHAS flights, WFP wil charter a medium sized aircraft from the short-list of registered operators, liaise with the authorities for necessary facilitation and ensure that the operations will comply with UNAVSTADS.. UNHAS Steering Committee will provide overall guidance on the smooth running of UNHAS and the User Group will promote interest of users in the movement of passengers and cargo in order to fulfil needs of the humanitarian community. While remaining focused on filling logistics gaps faced by the programmatic sectors in the Syria response, the Logistics Sector will also bring subject matter experts to conduct trainings across Whole of Syria in order to enhance the capacities of humanitarian actors, enabling them to better implement sectoral response plans.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

WFP ensures that the service providers contracted abide by labour rules and regulations in-country. WFP will maintain the already implemented reporting mechanism for harassment cases in Logistics Sector warehouses, and furthermore, organisations will be encouraged to nominate female candidates to attend trainings organized. Gender sensitive and gender appropriate facilities will also be made available during a training.Finally, agency GBV focal points will be invited to meetings with contracted service providers and humanitarian organisations to give guidance on issues related to harassment, abuse and gender based violence.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Logistics coordination remains pivotal in ensuring needs continue to be met while simultaneously maintaining capacity and resources to address any emerging issues. The main risk the Logistics sector might face is losing access. In order to mitigate that risk, the sector will continue to advocate for better access through both OCHA and SARC, explore different routes and assess the available options to reach different locations. In addition, WFP as provider of last resort has long term agreements and contracts with different transport companies which allow flexibility and adaptability in case of any sudden change in the situation on the ground. Safety and security risks that may affect implementation of UNHAS operations will be continuously monitored and relevant mitigation measures put in place to ensure implementation of safe operations. A lack of sufficient funds is also an important risk to take into consideration. Funds need to be made available to adequately respond to emergency developments, while also ensuring the continued implementation of other ongoing sector activities. In such a case, the Logistics Sector will work in consultation with partners and HCT to ensure that the most critical logistics services are prioritized and remain uninterrupted to ensure the most vital needs continue to be met. In addition, regular monitoring of funding and reporting to donors will allow for best future planning and timely fund raising.
