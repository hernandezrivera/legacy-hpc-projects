Project Summary

The proposed action, in line with such priorities and taking advantage of the field experience of the four actors, aims to target in-camp and out-of-camp high-need/neglected areas with a comprehensive package of primary health care services, specialized assistance for PWDs, health waste management, and community outreach. A coordinated and integrated approach will enhance efficiency and effectiveness in reaching vulnerable population, ensuring continuity of service to key facilities already supported by the ongoing CDCS phase three consortium project. Additionally, looking at continuing with quality response through better awareness in the community, augmented accessibility, and the offer of a wider portfolio of inclusive PHC, HWM, referrals and protection services that ensures a holistic response to high priority needs.

4 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in NES continued to worsen, with nearly 11.1 million PIN in early 2021. The deterioration of the health system continues to represent a key challenge for the population, and the impact of covid-19 has further eroded both the economic condition of communities already fragile and local authorities’ capacity to support public basic health services. From August 2021, 20,611 COVID-19 cases were reported across NES, representing the second highest monthly caseload since December 2020. The increase in COVID-19 cases deeply affected essential services and humanitarian response. In Nov 2021, 58% of the resident communities interviewed in NES and 53% of IDP communities reported health care as a priority need. The communities reported the high cost of health services as a common challenge. Financial barriers, especially when coupled with breaks in supply chains for essential medicines, are barriers to health access. Furthermore, with ratios of functional health facilities to population far below emergency standards in many areas of NES, patients face transport costs and often-insecure travel routes to reach care. The ability to access essential health services, including maternal health, is an urgent concern, with KIs in 49% of communities reporting lack of medicines or health equipment at health facilities as a barrier to healthcare access. The Lack of water continues to be a public health threat, and cases on malnutrition continue to be on the increase.

5 - Implementation of activities

Activity 1.1: Comprehensive financial support to targeted HCFs for the provision of Life-saving and Primary Health Care assistanceThe proposed action will include full support to As-Suwar PHCC, Shaddadi PHCC, Al Hol Camp Phase 4 and Washokani. UPP and KRC will ensure the provision of the following services: Reproductive Health care, Paediatric care and Internal, Level 1 Emergency health care, Community Health Outreach Activities, Life-assistance through ambulances for urgent delivery and maternal care, Referral and transport to hospitals for for stabilized cases, EWARS for epidemiological surveillance and response, Integrated health waste management system and Laboratory Activity 1.2: Provision of pharmaceutical and medical commoditiesUPP will procure essential, lifesaving pharmaceuticals and medical commodities for the PHCCs in as-Suwar and al-Hol Camp ph 4, Shaddady and Washokani and for all UPP/KRC supported health care facilities in NES. Activity 1.3: Capacity Building and of the job coaching UPP will continue the capacity building efforts delivered during the previous programs to ensure the needed transferable skills and technical capacity is in place to continue services even with a gradual and guided exit strategy.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Limited access to services, especially for vulnerable groups identified (women, children, persons with specific needs, PWD, foreign families). Mitigating measures: Carry out safety audits and community consultations for safe beneficiaries’ access to site locations, including appropriate gender separate areas, confidential spaces, lack of physical barriers; Anti discrimination measures include staff training and sensitization, coordination with PwG and advocacy on a local level; Staff recruitment/composition will be culturally and gender sensitive; Staff training in outreach and prevention will increase access to services for women and girls; Staff training and dedicated trained staff for referral to protection specialized staff for case management; Outreach activities and awareness campaigns free and available to all services, including outreach and consultation with most vulnerable groups. 2: Risk of Sexual Exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers in activities including in clinics, mobile units and in the community. Mitigating measures: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism; Train staff on code of conduct and PSEA mechanisms; Increase awareness among communities about PSEA and the complaints mechanism; Deploy Protection specialized staff to monitor protection standards including do-no-harm, ensuring gender representation amongst staff; Carry out safety audits for safe beneficiaries’ access to site locations, including appropriate gender separate areas.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All UPP and implementing partner staff are trained on Humanitarian Principles and all staff members have signed the CoC. Moreover, trainings on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) are conducted on a regular basis.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The assumption behind the Action is that context situation allows supported HFs and ambulances to be operative 24/7 to provide life-saving, PHC and emergency referrals to the war-affected population. Main risks are: - Further escalation of violence between SDF and GoT, and increased waves of population displacement (high risk); - ISIS resurgence and regained control over areas or infiltrates carrying out attacks (high risk); - Lack of access and difficulties to provide services and supplies for implementing partner; difficulty to monitor the activities in case of remote management (medium risk); - Cross-border INGOs evacuate expat staff and therefore are more limited in their capacity to respond while active hostilities are ongoing (high risk); - Presence of Covid-19 cases in NES, inducing loss of humanitarian access, loss of part of the health workforce, evacuation or quarantine of UPP and impl. partner's non-medical staff; shortages of medical supplies; shortages of pharmaceuticals; - Turkish forces expand “Peace Spring” operational theater eastward and westward to counter Kurdish presence in NES Mitigation strategies include flexible health structures to be relocated according to conflict scenarios. Strengthening of all COVID-19 measures already in place with particular focus on IPC. maintaining access also unstable areas and negotiate access to accessible western territories with relevant authorities and military councils. Move to remote management modalities.
