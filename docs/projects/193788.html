Project Summary

Palestinians in the Gaza strip are experiencing the compounding effects of a diminishing economy, limited access to employment, and an overall decline in socioeconomic development opportunities. The COVID-19 pandemic and the escalation of hostilities in the past two years, with peaks in May 2021 and August 2022, have exacerbated the current economic recession and ongoing humanitarian needs, whilst hindering any growth opportunities in the long term. In August2022 Needs Assessment, conducted by the AEI in close collaboration with WeWorld-GVC (WW-GVC), aims to understand the Health and WASH sector-specific needs, as well as the main vulnerabilities of households in these sectors across the entirety of the Gaza strip. Although recent MSNA assessments conducted in the oPt have informed these sectors, a gap remains with respect to multi-sectoral data highlighting linkages in sectoral needs which could enable an inter-sectoral analysis that reveals underlying vulnerability drivers. Therefore, the current assessment is designed to support humanitarian actors in their development of the 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), in addition to supporting humanitarian, development, and peace actors in their development and implementation of a nexus approach for the oPt.
This project is aligned with HRP 2023 Strategic Objectives 2, and complements Health Cluster Objective and complementing WASH cluster objectives 2. The needs and interventions were coordinated with and endorsed by the Ministry of Health (MoH), Health Cluster and WASH Cluster.
This project will be implemented by Ard El Insan (AEI) and WW-GVC and will target families of children aged 6-59 months old via household interventions. A total of 1407 children will be referred to AEI centers for treatments of moderate to severe wasting as well as iron deficiency anemia.
The main goal of this project is to contribute to the provision of lifesaving health and nutrition services for children under 5 years old and their families, targeting the most vulnerable households in the Gaza strip. The project will target children whose morbidity and mortality can be associated with moderate to severe malnutrition or anemia, and that could be significantly magnified when exposed to virulent micro- organism epidemics.
AEI and its partner will provide health and nutrition care services to children < 5 years old. The project will ensure access to essential lifesaving health interventions, including nutrition, hygiene and sanitation services, for communities of vulnerable areas, aiming towards achieving incremental changes in their situation as reflected in the project outcome .The project will address the problem of environmental enteropathy and more severe diarrheal diseases that are associated with poor child growth through a robust set of activities focused on the
The project will target 1407 children (direct beneficiaries) selected based on criteria that include: location classified as vulnerable areas, health status, exposure to protection risks and needs severity. Beneficiaries will be selected from the most affected locations in Gaza (Wadi Salqa and Mughraqa), Khan Younis (Khuza and Fuhkari) and Rafah (Shoka and Nasser).
The project will apply a rights-based approach and respect the “do no harm” principle. The project will comprise a screening of 2743 HHs from vulnerable areas and admission of 1407 children with moderate to severe wasted health status and/or moderate to severe anemia. They will be provided with proper medical and nutritional treatments, including micronutrient supplements, follow up visits and individual counseling sessions. the project will encompass the provision of WASH essential services to 299 HH, including the provision of cash and voucher assistance (CVA) for 985 families of them (86 women headed HH).
The project will use participatory approaches and focus on AGD inclusion, with emphasis on women and children

1 - Needs

Across the oPt, a total of 1.5 million Palestinians, two-thirds of whom are in the Gaza Strip, have been identified as requiring health-related humanitarian assistance in 2022. Among the households accessing health services, 23 percent reported facing access barriers, predominantly related to the high cost of services and medicines. Regarding the nutrition situation in the oPt, this is characterized by high rates of malnutrition, driven by poverty, food insecurity, and poor dietary habits. Micronutrient deficiencies and obesity pose significant challenges to health and well-being. Extremely vulnerable groups including female headed households, pregnant and lactating women and children are especially affected. The MSNA found that eight percent of female-headed households, and five per cent of male headed households have a poor and borderline food consumption score, and as much as 20% of pregnant and lactating women are malnourished. According to the WASH Cluster partner’s assessments, approximately 1.35 million Palestinians across the oPt are affected by public health risks associated with insufficient access to WASH services and will be in immediate need of humanitarian assistance in 2022. The MSNA 2022 found as of July 2022 that, although 95.1% of Gaza households reported having access to water on their premises, the majority of households (82.6%) were reliant on unimproved water sources for their drinking water. While 94.7% of Gaza households were able to use piped water into a compound as their main water source for domestic purposes, only 3.2% of households were able to use piped water into the compound for drinking, confirming that tap water in private homes generally not potable or safe for consumption. Reliance on water trucking (82.5%) and piped water connected to a public tap (13%) were the most commonly reported main sources of drinking water for Gaza households. While 92.2% of households reported having access to sufficient quantities of water for drinking and domestic purposes, high rates (84.9%) of households employing coping mechanisms in order to cope with a lack of water were also observed - with many of these being negative coping mechanisms. The most commonly reported negative coping mechanism identified was receiving water on credit (48.9%). Moreover,5.7% of households reported drinking water intended for domestic use - potentially increasing the risk for the spread of waterborne diseases or exposure to hazardous chemical substances. These households are also adopting various coping mechanisms related to sanitation, including sharing latrines with other families and using shelter spaces, rather than bathrooms, for handwashing or bathing. Such practices generate privacy and protection concerns, particularly for women and girls.In light of this information, AEI and WeWorld-GVC (WW-GVC) conducted a complementary field survey targeting 1407 HHs last August 2022. for Malnourished children (moderate and severe wasting and Anemic children) in vulnerable areas to assess the WASH conditions in particular (in terms of water, sanitation, hygiene, and general shelter conditions), considering the different needs and expectations of women, men, girls, boys, the elderly and people with disabilities. The assessment identified 299 HHs in needs to intervene in the rehabilitation/upgrading of the WASH facilities (bathroom and kitchen).. The proposed intervention aims to enhance the resilience of these vulnerable women, men, girls and boys, considered to live in some of the most vulnerable areas across the whole Gaza strip, by improving their hygiene awareness and conditions. Further, it also aims to develop and adapt a suitable environment for people with disabilities to comply with “inclusivity” principles. Women will especially benefit from this project since, according to local culture, they spend more time inside the house as they are in charge of all housework. Moreover, the HNO 2023 identified that 40% of children under 5 years old are anemic, 73% of children have Vitamin A deficits, 64% of children have Vitamin D deficits. These conditions can result in unfavorable health outcomes including irreversible brain damage and a high risk of disability, including motor and mental disabilities. Moreover, poor nutrition among children under 5 years old, which can be due to poverty, lack of access to nutritional foods and poor feeding practices, can lead to a rise in cases of wasting, stunting and anemia in children, as well as recurrent infection such as gastroenteritis, diarrhea and respiratory tract infections. Water, sanitation and hygiene conditions, such as quantity and quality of drinking water available and the presence of waste disposal facilities, are vital to ensure that all health and nutrition interventions integrate key elements of water, sanitation and hygiene good practices, including the promotion of handwashing and provision of soap and latrines. Due to the increase in poverty levels, many families cannot afford the cost of food or basic needs including clean water and hygienic materials, which increases health risk exposure, particularly among children. The situation is particularly severe in the Gaza Strip, where the health system has been undermined by the longstanding Israeli blockade, the internal Palestinian division, a chronic power deficit, and shortages in specialized health staff, drugs and equipment.

2 - Activities

Activities to be conducted by AEI: AEI will provide access to healthcare and nutrition to prevent excessive maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality by ensuring essential newborn care services are available at all times. Proper coordination was performed with MoH, Local Authorities, and the Health Cluster, and major stakeholders active in the Health sector in GS on the target communities to avoid overlapping. At the project’s start, AEI and WW-GVC and will carry out a detailed needs assessment, be conducted in cooperation with MoH and health cluster to prioritize and confirm the needs and specifications. The proposed intervention will be delivered through service modality (80%) and Cash modality (20%) and proposes following activities:Activity 1: Provision of basic nutritional services - micronutrient supplements and therapeutic treatment of moderate and severe wasting, will be procured to AEI nutrition centers that will join and support the project. These will be used to provide full support for the 1,407 targeted children identified with specific nutritional needs (quantities are estimated based on AEI database and are subject to change upon actual admission of cases). The needed materials for this activity include • High Nutrient Powder, (952 tins). • Multivitamin Syrup 200 ml, (5,677 Bottles). • Vitamin (A&D) Drops 10 ml, (5,814 Dropper). • Iron drops 15ml (50mg elemental iron per ml) supplements as prophylactic, (5600 Bottles). • Anti-biotic, (513 Bottles). • Anti protozoal and Anti helminthic (855 Bottles). • Anti-fungal (1,026 Tubes). • Anti-pyritic (684 Bottles). • Folic acid, (56,760 tab). Activity 2: Management of malnutrition cases in children with moderate to severe wasting and anemia. Children aged 6 to 59 months old with signs and/or symptoms of moderate to severe wasting and/or anemia will be identified through screening and assessments of the selected HH. The beneficiaries selected will be referred to AEI Health Nutrition care centers to start malnutrition management and treatment processes. Activity 3: Cash Voucher assistance will be provided for 985H.H over three months . Each family will receive 45 $/month , to allow targeted beneficiaries to purchase goods and access services that can have a positive impact on their maternal and child nutrition. These include nutritious foods, items to prepare food, hygiene items, safe water, transportation, and productive inputs. every family will have pre identified list of basic needs and monitored by project team with coordination with qualified suppliers This activity will be conducted in collaboration with WW-GVC. The activities to be conducted by WW-GVC intend to improve the essential services of WASH facilities at 299 HHs, including at least 86 women-headed HHs. This will be done through the following activities: Activity 4; Pre/post needs assessments that will comprise: (4.1) Conduction of an in-depth assessment to verify and monitor the exact needs, including a technical survey for planning and design of the required activities; (4.2) Individual field visits to identify the 299 HHs that will be selected as beneficiaries; (4.3) Conduction of10 gender-separated focus groups (at least three FGs for each selected locality based on the vulnerable areas assessment mentioned above) to collect feedback and incorporate it in the action. The results will be consulted with HH members, especially women, to collect their feedback and prioritize the rehabilitation methodology, ensuring the beneficiaries’ comfort and privacy. Activity 5; Rehabilitation works of WASH facilities for 299 HHs facing a severe lack of WASH conditions, including 5.1 Bills of Quantities, technical specifications, and tendering processes, to meet basic humanitarian principles and a signed commitment to comply with Special Measures for Protection from any kind of abuse and harassment; 5.2 rehabilitation/upgrading of bathrooms (toilets, hand washing/ shower facilities, partitions, windows and doors, tiling, plastering, and painting works, solar water heater, access for disabled people); 5.3 rehabilitation/ upgrading of kitchens (dishwashing sinks, counters, tiling, plastering, and painting works); (5.4) rehabilitation/upgrading of the access ramps for PWDs; 5.5 basic intervention aiming to improve the water storage. The WASH interventions will ensure women’s and men’s physical and climate protection, privacy, and dignity; improve access to essential HH WASH services, safety and security, and reflect gender and protection approaches.Activity 6: Hygiene promotion and environmental health activities The activities will be implemented through a participatory community approach, tailored to the specific needs and behaviors of women, men, girls and boys, to raise awareness on sanitation, hygiene and environmental health, as well as the adoption of life-saving hygiene behaviors. 6.1 At least 98 Hygiene awareness promotion sessions targeting women, men, girls, boys, the elderly and PWDs. A pre-intervention knowledge, attitude and practices (KAP) survey will set up the baseline, while a post KAP survey will measure the impact and effectiveness of the campaign to increase awareness in IPC protocols. 6.2. At least 16 Public environmental health campaigns will be implemented in collaboration with AEI using the Behavioral Change Communication (BCC) approach to enhance the protection and safety of the surrounding environment.. The partners will play an important role in supporting all phases of the campaigns, mainstreaming gender, with the purpose of avoiding the perpetuation of stereotypes and socially imposed roles for women and men.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The targeted areas of this project show multiple health-specific needs, especially related to insufficient access to healthcare services and high out-of-pocket payments. Expanding the capacity of the health system through the cash/voucher modalities will allow beneficiaries to use the private sector, which will support accelerating the rise of health coverage in these areas. Moreover, another objective of this specific modality, is that it will improve the collaboration between AEI-WW-GVC and the private sector. The voucher model is expected to improve collaboration by establishing models for HHs. essential health and nutrition services delivery through public-private partnerships, in addition to addressing the health gaps of their urgent needs. On the other hand, the service delivery modality reflects the project rationale and its effectiveness in achieving the objectives of the programmes. It will be used throughout the project cycle from needs assessment, response analysis and design, to implementation and evaluation. The project will allow working in physical presence with the direct beneficiaries, creating opportunities for active engagement, immediate feedback gathering, and establishment of lessons learned for all parties involved. an estimated 985 families will benefited from CVA specially those children referred for admission in the health center suffering moderate to severe malnutrition , anemia and other common childhood illness (according to AEI protocols and guideline of four months period before discharge . Children's families will be targeted for CVA support of 164 $ / family.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

AEI and WW-GVC have a clear Complaints Response Mechanism The system is announced through social media and the website of each organization. Partners use the complaints and response system so that affected target groups can give feedback on their treatment and quality of services to ensure accountability in programs implemented by AEI, WW-GVC and other stakeholders. All project implementers will be encouraged to establish feedback mechanisms on their own. Their role will be to listen and facilitate discussions and document and address complaints from the beneficiaries and community members. Complaints can beverbal or written (by email, SMS or hotline, as well as complaints and suggestion boxes managed by a designated person). Afterwards a certain committee will evaluate, investigate and address the complaints received and report back to the affected beneficiaries and to designated field office. This committee will be responsible for handling issues such as exclusion of the project activities, problems in assessment and provision of relief items. In the case of complicated or sensitive complaints including complaints against staff, the committee will forward the complaint to a designated Human resource manager or to a higher-level organizational accountability committee.
