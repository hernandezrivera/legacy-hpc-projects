Project Summary

The proposed integrated life-saving and essential emergency health project is aligned with the South Sudan Health Cluster’s Objectives CO1, CO2 and CO3 for the period 2024 which are aligned with the 2024 HRP South Sudan Strategic Objectives SO1, SO2, and SO3. The project will address critical humanitarian needs in selected priority payams and villages of Mayom County currently in HNO 2023 level 4 in Unity State by ensuring effective coordination, complementarity and integration of development and humanitarian nexus in all areas in collaboration with the State Ministry of Health (SMoH), Mayom County CHD, partners, and other stakeholders on the ground ensuring that proactive involvement of local communities and authorities is central to our programing – design, implementation, monitoring, and joint accountability.
Yo’ Care will achieve this by adopting a range of differentiated service delivery modalities such as the provision of static and mobile health services. This approach is expected to help overcome the accessibility issues affecting current healthcare coverage at all health system levels. Several factors including emergency events like conflicts and flooding may exacerbate accessibility. Implementation of static and mobile services in Mayom County will help address the essential needs of the target population, especially those people whose peculiar conditions (disabilities, mental health disorders and exposure to SGBV) make them more vulnerable, rendering their access to health services extremely challenging.
For this purpose, health-related activities will be integrated with other clusters such as nutrition, WASH, and protection clusters. This will be done through the opening and sustained reciprocal referral channels for the affected people to be offered both clinical care and psychosocial support.
The project is expected to reach 32,043 people in Mayom County with essential lifesaving health services. this includes at least 13,900 residents, 4,110 internally displaced persons (IDPs) and over 14,033 returnees mainly from neighbouring Sudan, requiring US$ 600,000 to achieve these planned targets and results.

1. Needs

The needs identified and their proposed solutions in this proposed project by Yo’ Care South Sudan are based on the 2023 South Sudan Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) originally issued on 25 November 2022 by UN OCHA and the Health Cluster Inputs for the Humanitarian Response Plan, 2024 issued by the South Sudan Health Cluster on 31 October 2023. Mayom County in Unity State is considered a priority humanitarian setting both in the 2023 IPC Food Security Classification and by Health Cluster at severity classification level 4, where an estimated 76,904 people (42% of the County’s population) are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and emergency (IPC Phase 4) between September and November 2023 according to the recently released IPC Report issued on 6 November 2023 by Integrated Food Security Phase Classification. This number is projected to increase to 82,000 people in need between December 2023 to March 2024. This is a significant increase in the level of the affected population compared to 7% (n=6,998) in the 2022 HNO. These people suffer from poor health, malnutrition, hunger, and violence with women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities being the most vulnerable groups (2023 HNO: HC HRP 2024 Inputs 31 Oct 2023). Mayom County is being heavily affected by conflicts and flooding in 2023. The persisting high internal and cross-border conflicts coupled with flooding have hampered access to and utilization of quality basic health services, particularly in Wangkei, Riak, Pub, Kueryiek, and Bieh Payams with no medical services provided at Wangkei PHCC since March 2023 when ICRC pulled out of the facility. Conflicts and flooding have resulted in the internal displacement of people as there are 9,864 IDPs and 33,678 returnees in Mayom according to Displacement Tracking Matrix and ISNA assessments reports (Mobility Tracking Report R14, IOM, May 2023). A matter of concern is the IDPs and returnees’ burden on host families whose resources are limited because of raids/lootings and environmental factors. This is even more concerning when considering that, due to the continuous inter-communal fighting and flooding, the large majority of households are headed by women, more exposed to violence and likely to be food insecure. Despite the IDPs' and returnees' vulnerability, they are predisposed to diseases in the face of inadequate and inequitable health facilities distribution, poor staffing conditions (staff number and skills, equipment, supplies), insufficient funding to sustain routine services such as vaccination and immunization, and failure to offer due preventive and curative functions. According to the 2021 Annual Health Sector Performance Review Report, the health indicators in Mayom County are generally low as ANC4+ stands at 41.7%, stillbirth attendance at 11.9%, and OPD utilization stands at 61.3%.

2. Project activities

Activity 1: Provision of comprehensive, integrated and quality preventive and curative primary health care services at the static facility level (PHCC and PHCU). This activity is aligned with the Health Cluster Objective (CO1), and Yo’ Care’s focus will be on the availability of basic life-saving services – such as for prompt management of common endemic infections like malaria, pneumonia, and diarrheal diseases. Activity 2: Provision of life-saving essential healthcare services including epidemic control and management of non-communicable diseases through static (Hospitals, PHCC, and PHCU) and mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of healthcare workers. This activity aligned with Health Cluster Objective 2 (CO2), and Yo’ Care will focus on outbreak prevention, detection, investigation, and integrated response. Activity 3: Data-driven decision-making and stakeholder engagement in Mayom County. Under this interventional approach, closely aligned with the Health Cluster Objective 3 (CO3), Yo’ Care will participate in weekly/bi-weekly data and performance review meetings with both the health cluster team, health authorities and prospective donor (s) and any identified challenges/gaps will be addressed in collaboration with these stakeholders and the Health Cluster team. Activity 4: Ensure continued provision of integrated Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services delivery. This activity is aligned with Cluster Objective CO1 and SPO 1.1. Activity 5: Providing integrated GBV care and prevention services: Under this intervention, Yo’ Care will provide knowledge to recognize, prevent and refer cases of IPV/SGBV management. Yo’ Care will also ensure continued community-based GBV education, screening, and psycho-social support.
