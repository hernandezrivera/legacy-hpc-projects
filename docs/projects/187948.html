Project Summary

INTERSOS Action tackles identified problems/unmet needs, through an integrated protection response (GP, CP, GBV, MA) targeting most vulnerable population in areas with high severity conditions (3-4 as per Protection Sector Severity & PIN_HRP 2022) in Hama, Southern Idleb, Rural Damascus and Dara. This Action aims to enhance the prevention/mitigation of protection risks and respond to protection needs through supporting the protective environment in Syria (HRP Strategic Objective 2). In 2022, INTERSOS will reach a total of 45,289 beneficiaries (G 12,454; B 10,190; W 12,454; M 10,190) across Hama, Southern Idleb, Rural Damascus and Dara. A similar projection of targeted beneficiaries is foreseen for 2023. Over 2 years, the Action aims at contributing to improving psycho-social wellbeing and self-reliance capacities of conflict-affected populations through stand-alone and integrated protection interventions. Protection activities will be implemented through a static-mobile approach and include: a) targeted protection at individual-group level for the most vulnerable children and adults: case management (CP, PWSN), in kind assistance (medical assistive devices, dignity/education/winter kits, transportation to access services) safe identification/referrals; structured and unstructured PSS activities for children and adults b) work at community level to build on pre-existing strengths and self-protection mechanisms: establishment/support of community groups, community-led initiatives, prevention/empowerment activities for women/girls; awareness raising, MRE, training and capacity building on key protection topics.
Integrated actions include Child Protection in Education: activities are conducted directly in schools to enhance the protective environment for children through training of education personnel & PTAs; school-based structured and unstructured PSS and case management; referral to specialized services; reduction of barriers to access education and school-dropouts’ rates in addition to targeted intervention for OOSC. Protection and gender considerations are mainstreamed throughout sectoral interventions (Health/NFIs) aiming at avoiding causing harm, ensuring meaningful access, accountability, equal participation/empowerment of all affected individuals. INTERSOS is currently working under the umbrella of the MoU with SARC jointly implementing protection activities and under the framework of MoUs with relevant Line Ministries. The MoU with MoE was signed in June 2021 and the MoU with MoSAL is at final negotiation stages as of Jan 2022. With the upcoming signature of MoU with MoSAL, INTERSOS aims at strengthening direct implementation of a full range of protection activities targeting most vulnerable groups (with a specific focus towards disability inclusion).

4 - Needs

2022 Protection HNO estimates growing number of PIN in 2022-23 (14.2M/14.7M) with 96% living under severe, extreme, catastrophic conditions. Among these, 10.7M live in areas reporting explosive contamination, 6.2M are children and 4M are living with a disability. Years of conflict have profoundly impacted people’s physical/mental wellbeing and resilience. INTERSOS Rapid Needs Assessments conducted between July-December 2021 have highlighted that psychosocial distress is reported in Hama, Rural Damascus (RD) and Idleb as highly prevalent among children, also affecting caregivers, teachers, women and girls. Women/Girls (particularly W&G with disabilities, divorced, widowed, single, older women) are frequently exposed to early marriage, harassment, domestic violence and other intersecting form of discrimination/social exclusion. Negative coping strategies (early marriage, child labour, family separation) are reported in Hama and RD as being commonly adopted. Particularly in rural areas, children (specifically adolescent boys) regularly drop out of school during the harvesting season to support their families, increasing the risk of being exposed to explosive accidents. Older persons and PwDs face specific challenges unique to their individual circumstances. PwDs and their families in RD, Hama, Idleb continue to face: increased economic hurdles; physical and environmental barriers limiting their access to services; discrimination, stigma, social isolation.

5 - Implementation of activities

Protection activities are conducted through mobile/static approaches at community and individual level. Protection programming includes stand-alone, integrated protection, protection mainstreaming. INTERSOS implements protection activities in partnership with SARC and directly under the framework of MoUs with Line Ministries (MoE active partnership/MoSAL final stages). Activities include: capacity building on core protection subjects (protection principles, GBV, CP, Safe Identification/Referral) for SARC volunteers, community members/groups, education personnel, PTAs, health workers; awareness raising on core protection topics including legal awareness (through SARC) and mine risk education; establishment of community groups and support of community-led initiatives; prevention/empowerment activities for women/girls; unstructured/structured PSS for children and adults, caregivers programs; CP/PWSN Case Management for individuals at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse and other protection violations; in kind assistance (assistive devices for PWDs, dignity/education/hygiene/winter kits). INTERSOS is part of the IA Referral Task Team and will contribute to strengthen referral mechanisms to specialized services. A M&E plan is developed with quantitative/qualitative indicators to measure achievement rate/impact of interventions. Needs assessment/community consultations (through FGDs & KII, PDM) are regularly conducted to foster communities active participation.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: Expectations of response/services in locations with limited services. Targeted population at risk due to location of the facility/activity/mismanagement of data/no confidentiality. Different vulnerable groups don't have the same access to assistance. Tension in community, for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff.Tension in the family, including risks to physical violence or safety and disruption of the previous family dynamic. Familiarization may cause civilian population to become overconfident or complacent to the threat of explosive hazards. Backlash from family/community on women/girls participating in activities. SEA by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services. Mitigation Measures: Use existing tools/mapping and coordinate at local level to ensure balanced coverage based on needs; avoid overlapping of activities; focus on underserved/rural areas as well as projected return areas. Establish adequate selection criteria based on vulnerabilities. Protection Mainstreaming Training. Target women/girls with socially accepted activities as safe entry points, conduct safety audits. Train staff on data protection/confidentiality. Ensure data protection protocols are in place. Digitize data collection and storage, and password protect. AAP, ensure consultation/information sharing with community/authorities. Design complaint mechanisms/follow up ensuring standards of data protection/confidentiality.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

R: INTERSOS/implementing partners (IP) staff, suppliers (etc.) have prior conviction for SEA related offenses; IP, staff member, suppliers, do not comply/accept PSEA policy; IP does not have a strong PSEA system in place; SEA perpetrated by staff/service providers; PSEA allegations are not timely/effectively addressed; survivors have limited access to safe assistance/remedies; lack of PSEA reporting mechanisms; communities not aware of PSEA risks/reporting channels. M: INTERSOS has a Global PSEA/CP Policy that applies to all personnel in addition to a country level Strategy to ensure SEA risks/mitigation strategies/procedures are tailored to the context. INTERSOS staff must sign a PSEA/CP Policy Declaration, attend a PSEA/CP Policy induction and one refresher training per year. One PSEA Referent and two PSEA Focal Points are appointed to receive complaints/reports about alleged acts of SEA and follow up in compliance with relevant guiding principles, ensuring safety of survivors. Complaints are referred to the in-country PSEA Referent and are subject to investigation. An assessment about IP commitment and capacity to prevent/respond to SEA is conducted; efforts are made with IP to harmonize PSEA strategies/reporting procedures to the best extent possible. Training is offered to partners to strengthen the identification of SEA risks/adoption of related SEA mitigation measures; when possible awareness raising on PSEA risks/reporting channels is conducted at community level.
