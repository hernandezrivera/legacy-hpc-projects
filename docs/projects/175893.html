Project Summary

The action will contribute to create a disability inclusive and accessible protection environment in order to prevent and mitigate protection risks within most vulnerable crisis-affected population in Cabo Delgado and Nampula Provinces. The action will enhance resilience capacity, involving community members and key local government authorities.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

It is not fully effective to approach protection risks prevention and mitigation using a mere top-down approach focused only on central government level without involving communities. In Moçambique there is a strong need for subsidiarity, empowering communities, CSOs and local authorities to deal with their risks exposure and mitigation.This action, covering some of the districts most affected by displacement of people in Cabo Delgado and Nampula Provinces, aims at enhancing community and civil society actors’ capacity for the protection of most vulnerable groups. This will contribute to create an enabling and inclusive environment which will promote resilience, self-community members protection, access to information and basic services accessibility. In parallel, the action will identify barriers preventing the universal access to life-saving and basic services, advocating for barriers removal and enhancing government proection actors capacity. Project over-arching approaches are: Community Based Inclusive Development, Rights Based Approach, Universal design. Main target groups will be people with disabilities, women and children, who are the most exposed to protection risks and experience multiple barriers to access life-saving services. The action will benefit from lessons learned and synergies with other AIFO projects in the same implementing area, taking into consideration Covid-19 pandemic context.The action is addressed to contribute overcoming protection risks whitin communities, with a specific focus on women, people with disability and children. Women with disabilities are one of the most vulnerable group, because they face double discrimination: the first related to gender and the second one related to disability. As a consequence, women and girls with disabilities are at risk of more than direct violence, they are often excluded from basic services, such as health and education, or do not have the right to work. During disaster situations women with disabilities are extremely vulnerable: not only are services poorly adapted to their specific needs, they are also at a heightened risk of violence. The action will put emphasis on gender and gender/disability specific needs and vulnerability, addressing them with a specific attention during all the training, sensitization and awareness activities, both at community and government level. In addition, gender and age groups needs will be taken into account during the accessibility audits and barriers removal.Following the Community Based Inclusive Development (CBID) approach, women will be active actors of the project and they will be involved throughout the whole project cycle.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Activities will take place in all districts involved.- Accessibility auditing to main district services, for the identification of main barriers (physical barriers, misconceptions, geographical availability, acceptability of services, informational barriers and communication difficulties, others)- Physical barrier removal to access main services such as health units and schools. - Community information and sensitization about basic services and humanitarian assistance – using accessible and inclusive IEC material.- Creation /strengthening of inclusive community-based protection structures- Community Sensitizations on human rights, disability, others – using accessible and inclusive communication material - Training of protection actors – government and non-government- Training of local authorities on protection legal frameworks, principles and standards, including Human Rights, and Humanitarian law.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Accessibility Auditing of main infrastructures and main public service providers of the Districts, identifying specific protection and accessibility needs of community members. The project will conduct an “Auditoria de Acessibilidade” using a monitoring and self-assessment tool to measure the inclusion of Health Units and other main public service providers: a simplified version of the "Disability Inclusion Score Card - DISC". This tool will provide the team with a score of how much they can already consider sufficiently inclusive different areas (finances / human resources / governance) in their service. In addition, it will encourage discussion and awareness of what “inclusion” means in practice and what needs to be changed to avoid discrimination and encourage meaningful access to assistance. The accessibility checklist will be drawn up in accordance with the national provisions governing the construction of Public Buildings (see in particular Decree No. 53/2008). After the auditing, the teams will agree on the strengths and weaknesses and will develop a final evaluation report which will be followed by the definition of a concrete Inclusion Action Plan.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

Following the Community Based Inclusive Development apparoch, AIFO works within and with communities and its staff is on a continiuing auscultation and dialogue process with them. Community members will be the main actors of the action as they will be equipped with tools and knowledge in order to establish their own protection environment, taking into consideration cultural setting and whithin this context, acting for pro-active behavioural change. Feed back mechanism: Focus Groups and periodic meetings with community members involved; sponsor the use of Linha Verde and Linha Fala a Crianca; the community-based protection structure itself will incorporate feedback mechanisms.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

People with disabilities (PWDs) are at risk of being most excluded from protection because they are unable to access vital information and services on equal basis with others, because of multiple barriers: physical, social and cultural. Despite the existence of specific disability laws and policies, line Ministries – at central, provincial and district level – are not aware on the rights of PWDs and they don’t have the tools to apply policies: this affects programme and planning design which, as as result, are not inclusive.In line with the United Nations Disability Inclusion Strategy and with the CBID approach, the action will identify PWDs using the Washington Group Short Set of Questions and it will collaborate with the Forum of Mozambican Association for Disabled People for the identification of gaps in access to services for PWDs in humanitarian respons and for the identification of barriers and their removal. The participation of PWDs themselves at the decision-making level and at all stages of the humanitarian response is considered a key issue by AIFO. AIFO has a long stangind collaboration with FAMOD and its Disabled People Organisations members and it has developed many projects with the aim of strengthening the disability movement in Mozambique.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

This action does not have a negative impact on the environment while mitigation and protection risks within communities will tackle community prepeardness to natural disasters – as the geographic area is exposed to this catastrophic natural events.
