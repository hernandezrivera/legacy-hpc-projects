1 - Needs

The humanitarian context of the WB, including EJ, remains directly tied to the impact of the Israeli occupation, which marked its 52st year in June 2019. Protracted socio-economic and protection difficulties continue. Representing one-third of the West Bank population including East Jerusalem, many of the 843,931 Palestine refugees registered with UNRWA experience challenges marked by continued violence, forced displacement, administrative and punitive demolitions as well as restrictions on access to services and markets, particularly for those living or owing in Area C and in the Seam Zone. In addition, the stagnant economy and lack of employment opportunities erodes the purchasing power of Palestine refugees thus classifying them as one of the poorest segments of Palestinian society. While 15 per cent of the Palestinians in the West Bank were unemployed during the first half of 2019, Palestine refugees’ unemployment rate reached 17 per cent, and 21 per cent for those living in camps. Food insecurity have, according to the 2018 SEFSec decreased in recent years. The number of food insecure refugees remains relatively high however (11 per cent, 113,000 individuals, of which 46,500 are severely food insecure) compared to non-refugees (9 per cent). Those in deep poverty among refugees remain vulnerable, and poverty contributes to their food insecurity levels. Females are furthermore considered vulnerable as their unemployment rate reached 32 per cent in in the first half of 2019 (15 per cent for male), as a result to several factors including availability of job opportunity and cultural norms inhibiting women’s participation on the labour market, further contributing to their vulnerability. Female headed households are thus especially vulnerable to poverty, furthermore affecting the food insecurity. In 2018, UNRWA faced a critical funding shortfall under its Emergency interventions which led to the discontinuation of most of its food insecurity interventions. This resulted in 90,000 refugees (48,000 which reside in camps) at risk of deepening poverty. This is also against a backdrop of the deterioration in the West Bank which has resulted in an increase of unemployment levels, particularly within the refugee community amongst other factors including regional insecurity, an increasing coercive environment by the occupying power and donor fatigue for Palestine support. Since UNRWA started discontinuing its emergency program, it has witnessed a significant increase in the number of emergency beneficiaries applying to enroll in its main relief programme, the E-Card program. The reassessment of beneficiaries’ poverty status revealed a significant number of refugees that are living in abject poverty, less than 2 dollars a day. This forced UNRWA to reconsider its approach in halting emergency services & decide to integrate this load into its E-Card programme. This decision was instigated by the dire financial situation of those families which will force them to resort to negative coping mechanisms that would have detrimental effect on their lives if they were left without assistance. The e-card modality, which provides cash to vulnerable refugees through bank debit cards contributes to empower beneficiaries, providing more freedom of choice and enabling greater flexibility in household spending. UNRWA plans to continue this important programme in 2020.

2 - Activities

In 2020, UNRWA will seek to provide unconditional and non-restricted cash assistance to a minimum of 3,573 abject poor refugee households (although the number of needy households is bigger than this) in the West Bank and its 19 refugee camps through its E-Card modality. This modality provides cash to extremely poor refugees through bank debit cards dedicated for this purpose. Beneficiaries are selected after UNRWAs social workers visit the households and assess their consumption. The poverty level of the households is then assessed using the (PMTF). In addition to consumption, this formula takes into consideration aspects of vulnerability, such as presence of chronic diseases, housing characteristics, accessibility to utilities, number of elderly and disabled (multi-disabilities) within a family, as well as female-headed households. Households with those aspects are prioritized for assistance. Families found to be extremely poor and vulnerable are then enrolled in the program. Heads of households will receive debit cards allowing them to benefit from basic assistance of NIS125 per person each quarter. Social workers will continuously maintain contact with beneficiaries; households consider the social worker as one of their major sources of information. Since the launching of the e-card, the Agency has been developing and improving this modality, for instance by introducing troubleshooting system and a hotline to assist beneficiaries. Based on their feedback, UNRWA modified its processes and signed a contract with the Bank of Palestine as the new service provider. This has improved the efficiency of the existing program. Furthermore, the Agency created a number of monitoring tools to monitor the implementation and targets set through the programme. Through monitoring in the past years, the programme was found to be contributing to food security and protecting people from falling into deeper poverty. Beneficiaries have reported that the new modality has improved their dignity and freedom of choice . The majority of respondents have been finding obtaining cash through this modality easy while more than 90 per cent agree that the e-card gives flexibility with regards to how to receive and use the money, when to spend it. The cash provided is mainly meant for food rather than non-food items. Generally female members of a household decide what to buy. The programme will go through two main phases. (1) The preparation phase: the identified beneficiaries by the end of 2019 will be informed of their eligibility. This will be done through the call center operated by UNRWA as well as social workers. In January and February, around 20 workshops will be conducted in the WB in which social workers will give new beneficiaries an orientation on the program. Arrangements will be put in place for the service provider, the Bank of Palestine (BoP), to accommodate the new beneficiaries. BoP will issue the new e-cards and distribute them to beneficiaries in its respective local branches, according to the lists provided by UNRWA, by the end of January 2020. (2) Implementation and monitoring Phase: by the end of February 2020, the first payment will be sent to beneficiaries’ accounts for Q1 2020. UNRWA will generate lists of beneficiaries on a quarterly basis and send them to the BoP. In its turn, the bank will send the payment to listed beneficiaries to withdraw the cash from the designated shops or BoP branches. Social workers will monitor the progress during monitoring visits as well as regular ones. They will address any concerns beneficiaries might have and assist beneficiaries in using the system if needed. Special attention will be given to vulnerable groups, particularly the elderly as well as persons with disabilities. Staff operating the troubleshooting system will monitor complaints, work to resolve them and report on trends to management to address any issues that might arise.

3 - Indicators

Outcome: abject poor refugee households living in West Bank (including the 19 camps) are receiving cash assistance to cover their basic food needs and restore their coping capacities. Indicators and targets: • 3,573 abject poor refugee households in the West Bank (in rural, urban & in the 19 camps) receive cash assistance through the e-card modality. • 25,330 abject poor refugee individuals in the West Bank (in rural, urban & in the 19 camps) receive cash assistance through the e-card modality. • US$3,546,200 in total value is disbursed as cash to abject poor refugee households. Monitoring & evaluation in 2020; UNRWA will ensure monitoring of progress and results at different levels through its Relief and Social Services (RSSP) staff (social workers and monitors). This will be done through the field visits. UNRWA will monitor first-hand the programme’s overall progress, on-spot visits will be conducted through RSSP and UNRWA Monitoring & Evaluation unit. UNRWA will monitor around communication and outreach, cash withdrawal, utilize of cash, technical issues with withdrawing cash as well as access to bank branches Furthermore, UNRWA has an additional internal monitoring tool which is the free toll hotline, in which UNRWA can receive complains or feedback from its beneficiaries. The hotline is also a tool to get feedback from beneficiaries about the program, any issues with the bank and the utilization of the cash.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In UNRWAs Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritisation of safety and dignity is explicitly addressed as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneficiaries, communities or staff. Further, UNRWAs Medium-Term Strategy 2016-2021 explicitly prioritises the "do-no-harm"-principles by stating that UNRWA will address protection challenges that arise from its programme service delivery in education, health, relief and social services, microfinance, and infrastructure and camp improvement. This will include focusing on instances when UNRWA, through its actions or policies, unintentionally undermines the realization of specific rights such as the socioeconomic rights to food, water, health and education. By addressing such concerns through ‘do-no-harm’ and other approaches, UNRWA service delivery promotes and respects the rights of beneficiaries and ensures the security and dignity of beneficiaries. Protection needs are addressed in all aspects of regular UNRWA programme and project design, policies, protocols and procedures, as well as in staff training. In all contexts, UNRWA will work to ensure that its assistance and service delivery is carried out in accordance with internationally agreed standards and its own minimum protection standards. UNRWA will regularly measure its level of compliance with these minimum standards and take necessary corrective action.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project targets vulnerable groups in the targeting stage through the vulnerability criteria: (1) families with members suffering from chronic disease; (2) large families with a majority of children; (3) female-headed households; (4) families with members with multiple special needs; (5) age.
