Project Summary

The proposed interventions aims to enhancing access to and provision of emergency life-sustaining gender-sensitive WASH services to affected hard-to reach rural communities of Pibor and Twic East counties in Jonglei State. Rural Health Services will foster gender-sensitive community-based WASH interventions in villages of Patiou, Panguet, Wernyiol, Kongor, Maar, Piliau and Raap of Twic East and Labrab, Maruwa, Nyawapuru, Gumuruk, Mewun and Verteth of Pibor County. The project intends to support a target population of 25,000 beneficiaries; 19,000 direct and 6,000 indirect beneficiaries in the aforementioned project villages, 62% (11,780 people) of beneficiaries come from Pibor and with 38% (7,220 people) from Twic East.
RHS proposed WASH project aims to create access and provide various WASH interventions that will be subsequently ascertain, and performance rated through the following indicators;
1. Number of water points rehabilitated/constructed with a flood-resilient design
2. Number of latrine stances rehabilitated/constructed with a flood-resilient design
3. Number of women and girls with improved access to water and/or sanitation after a safety audit
4. Number of people reached through household water treatment
5. Number of people reached with safe water other than household water treatment
6. Number of people with access to sanitation facilities
7. Number of people reached with hygiene promotion and soap
8. Number of SAM children/caretakers and PLW provided with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion
9. Number of nutrition facilities with improved access to sanitation
10. Number of nutrition facilities with improved access to safe water
11. Number of WSC Priority 1 and 2 counties with a decrease in severity by 1 class
The proposed WASH interventions target a population of 19,000 beneficiaries at an estimated project budget of 716,300 USD.

1 - Needs

Up to 6 million South Sudanese children (53%), women (24%), elderly persons (8%), and 15% disabled persons are considered to be dire needs for WASH humanitarian interventions in 2022 due to inadequate or limited access to clean safe water and improved sanitation. Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) conditions are most severed especially in the Nile Basin states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity of South Sudan. The 2021 Global Climate Risk Index rated the impacts of weather-related loss events (storms, floods, heat waves etc.) alarming, ranking the country the 8th most affected with expected increase in rainfall intensity next season. About 78% of the populations in Upper Nile region’s states of Unity, Upper Nile and Jonglei are considered in extreme humanitarian conditions with only 39% of the populations reported having enough water to meet their household needs countrywide and 34% in rural areas. (HNO, 2022)Jonglei state is flood hard-hit with 33% of households travel longer than 30 minutes to access water sources, a major risk affecting mainly persons with physical disabilities. Additionally, extremely low coverage of water infrastructure exposes up to 49% of households to rely on surface or unimproved water sources, Widespread practice of mass open defecation, creates a substantial public health risk, especially during floods, where latrine pits seep into waterways. Limited access to water is also compounded by only 14% of households reported having at least one jerrycan and/or bucket to safely store water. The exacerbating shocks include, atypical seasonal flooding, recurrent inter-communal conflicts, chronic insecurity, hyperinflation and scourge of Covid-19 on humanitarian personnel mobility and limited roads transport compounded the precarious conditions (HNO, 2022). Twic East and Pibor of Jonglei state are among the 15 WSC 5 (priority 1 counties) worst affected counties in the country with over 78% of the populations considered to in extreme needs for WASH humanitarian assistance. Unlike Like other affected counties, sanitation facilities in Pibor and Twic East are very rare as the few sanitary infrastructures got destroyed by flood waters, exposing communities to mass open defecation as over 80% of households don’t access a latrine resulting in AWDs and other water-born or related diseases. Flooding, insecurity and the economic downturn and resultant mass displacement have compounded the impact of historically low coverage of water infrastructure and exacerbated the disruption of water services, limiting resources available for the maintenance of wells and boreholes. floodwaters have not yet receded, people remain displaced and are not able to engage in livelihood activities, leading them to continue to rely on humanitarian assistance.Pibor and Twic East with population sizes of 227,854 (127,598 PIN) and 124,116 (79,434 PIN) are devastated by floodwaters, chronic insecurity due to spontaneous sub-national conflicts resulted into mass displacement to adjacent relative highlands which are now overcrowded and thus, resource constrained as the carry-capacity is extremely exceeded. The affected villages include; Marua, Kaseiongor, Labrab, Gumuruk and Verteth of Pibor County whereas, Pilau, Kongor, Wernyiol, Maar, Uchung, Panguet and Raap of Twic East are too, severed and extremely seculed from services due to inaccessibility and prone to water borne diseases and acute watery diarrhea in particular. Our field rapporteur in coordination with other partners and authorities reported that four (4) non-functioning boreholes were identified in Jebel Boma villages; Nawayapuru, Labarab and Maruwo IDPs camps. In most cases people are getting water directly from the swamp which are poorly protected and seasonally. Rampant open defecation worsened by poor hygiene among women, men, boys and girls, poor access to water and inadequate boreholes/water points, lack of WASH-NFIs requiring remedial WASH interventions.

2 - Project activities

Through this project, RHS will provide nutrition- and GBV -oriented WASH interventions via community-based and in-facility activities at Paliau, Panguet, Maar, Wernyiol, Kongor, Raap and Patiou villages of Twic Eastcounty and Labrab, Maruwa, Kasiengor, Nyawapuru, Gumuruk and Verteth vilages of Pibor county of Jonglei state.Our WASH interventions are mainly linked to, and will contribute to CO1 & SO1 (Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition), CO2 & SO2 (Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties and WASH specific activities for GBV (or mitigating WASH GBV), and CO3 & SO3 (Community members exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures and infrastructure)In summary, RHS shall contribute to CO1 & SO1, CO2 & SO2 and CO3 & SO3 through:1. Provision of WASH kits to unit “caretaker/SAM child” of nutrition facilities,2. Build, Repair and rehabilitate broken boreholes and dilapidated water points3. Provision of safe clean drinking water and constructing water treatment tanks in facilities and affected vulnerable communities of Twic East and Duk counties, 4. Repair and Construction of elevated water points and latrines/toilets and hygiene facilities with flood resilient designs5. Distribution of dignity kits to the reproductive age group 18-45 years6. Community engagement and hygiene promotion to prevent cholera, diarrhoea and other disease outbreaks7. Training of hygiene promoters on WASH-IPCs and hand pump mechanics8. Purchase and distribution of WASH-NFIs to vulnerable affected communities and nutrition facilities hosting affected U5 children, PLW and caretakers9. Risk communication engagement and Community social mobilization activities towards WASH best practices; proper hand-washing with soap and water, treatment of drinking water, proper disposal of human excreta and proper food handling hygiene as well as distribution of soap at household level to vulnerable families10. Public awareness campaigns at all public gathering and social centres; churches/mosques, schools, markets and health centres among others.11. Conduct WASH-Health integrated surveillance through active AWD case finding, stabilization, referral and reporting of complicated medical cases12. Distribution of ORS at household level and training on its use. Promotion of exclusive breastfeeding for at least six months shall target reproductive mothersThe proposed project activities will support and serves up to 20,000 beneficiaries and additional 6500 indirect beneficiaries; Host communities (87%), IDPs (9%) and Returnees 4%), with 62% and 38% from Pibor (11,780 people) and Twic East (7,220 people) counties respectively, with a budget estimate of 716,300 USD.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

RHS project will adapt the developed beneficiaries' feedback matrix to rate the performance of project responsive activities of which beneficiaries' regards inform and modify the course of various project activities. Taking into account beneficiaries' preferences induce a sense of inclusive participation, collective responsibility, stewardship and thus, satisfaction and project sustainability thereof.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

RHS project will ensure feedback from most vulnerable members such the disabled persons, illiterate, women and girls through the engaging and consulting with representative organizations of such most vulnerable groups to collectively appropriate preferable feedback and complaints mechanisms of information sharing network and relay pathways for inclusive participation and stake-holding. However, RHS has a community-based project tradition by which a community-anchored project committees are formed with proportionate composition of various vulnerable groups; women, girl, illiterate and persons with specific needs; disabled, elderly and others. They subsequently trained and allied bodies during and after the project implementation. The body acts as social-suggestion box for relaying information by the women, girls, disabled persons and illiterate members of beneficiaries. There will be suggestion boxes for the minority literate members to explicitly express their regards without fear or favour in regards to project performance. It should be noticed that RHS as a an organization has a professional code of communication that safeguard against breach of information and communication ethical principles; integrity, confidentiality, informed consent and benevolent.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

RHS shall flexibly update its PSEAH policy to commensurate the beneficiaries' protection needs in compliance with the donor's PSEA code of practice. The project will explain to the beneficiaries on the scope, context and PSEAH policy content through orientation workshops to sensitize them on the rights and duty of care of every project stakeholder including information sharing about the corporate protection "DOs" and "DON'Ts" as well as advocacy on Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). We have our organization code of practice of each employee is first oriented and precautioned to strictly adhere and regularly comply with diligently throughout the course of humanitarian services.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Through RHS project modified and informed M&E matrix, all aspects of accountability to affected populations including disability and old persons inclusions are properly engineered to capture various vulnerable groups complaints and compliments for appropriate informed decision-making thereof.Prior to the project initiation, all beneficiaries' representative organizations; women, men, disabled persons, Elderly, youth, faith groups, community leaders and local authorities in project locations are engaged, consulted and social mobilized towards the project goals. Their regards are considerably appropriated in to the project design, implementation, monitoring and Evaluation mechanisms as well as preferences in information sharing and relay among the partners. RHS shall make use of the suggestion boxes for the elite members and community-based WASH committees (CWCs) that act as community feedback and complaint channel for the illiterate members as well as a watch-dog and whistle-blower on unusual deviations from the original scope of work and context thereof.
