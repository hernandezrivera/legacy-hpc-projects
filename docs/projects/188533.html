Project Summary

The project aims to provide Multi-Purpose Cash Grants (MPGs) response for most vulnerable familise to help them cope with the harsh conditions and secure their needs in a dignified way, by distributing three rounds of cash grants with a total value of 100 USD per round per family (aligned with SMEB value). The project and the grant amount were designed to allow the beneficiaries to cover their needs of food, NFI and WASH since this cash modality will give them flexibility in choosing items they need and to give the project the ability to meet all changes in beneficiary needs.
Through the proposed project, GAN seek to target 3000 families of host and IDPs (1500 of them in 2022 and the others in 2023), the beneficiaries will be selected in close coordination with the local councils and civil authorities with 100 % verification from MEAL team in the targeted areas based on the vulnerability and eligibility criteria including displacement duration criteria since the newly IDPs are the most vulnerable groups to the shocks. Also, the project will prioritize the women-headed households as well as the households with children, disabilities, pregnant, and lactating women.
GAN will benefit from exchanging experiences through sharing the lessons learned and the solutions to overcome the challenges with SFNI, FSL clusters, and CWG.
GAN could cover as much as can of the geographical areas in Idleb and Aleppo governorate, such as Afrin, Harim districts that have overcrowded IDPs to reach 1500 vulnerable and newly HHs in urban, rural or even camps. Since GAN, through its ‘on the ground’ operation team and its prior experience in Idleb governorate will be able to access the locations and markets during the project implementation.
Due to the lack of the banking system in northern Syria, GAN will use MTA agent who will be responsible for delivering the MPG's amount to the beneficiaries in the different locations.
prior to the MPG distribution, a rapid market assessment in terms of availability of the food items, prices, and accessibility will be conducted to make sure the local markets are functioning and to ensure all beneficiaries have good and equal accessibility to the different goods and services.

4 - Needs

Throughout 2021, the macro-economic context continued to deteriorate sharply and is accelerating into 2022. The combined effects of currency depreciation, soaring prices, reduced fiscal revenue increasing domestic debt, widespread job loss. The long-standing needs of an estimated 6.9M IDPs remain staggering, particularly for over two million people in 1760 informal settlements and planned camps. With a disproportionate impact on female-headed households, older persons without family support, persons with disabilities, and children. An increasing number of women and adolescent girls are forced to work in order to provide for their families, either as sole breadwinners or contributing to the family income.The mounting food insecurity almost 60% of the population. Stunting increased by 4%, SAM increased by 29% and MAM increased by 45%.With average household expenditure now exceeding income by 50%, compared to 20% in August 2020, only 10% of households have an income above the cost of SMEB.The national average FBP has increased by 27% in the past 6months (May 2021) and by 115% in the last year (November 2020).Households are now reverting to negative coping mechanisms more frequently than before. This includes child labor, child marriage, and the sale of productive assets.IDP households also report negative coping strategies more frequently than other population groups, such as selecting less expensive or preferable food and purchasing food on credit.

5 - Implementation of activities

beneficiaries' selection will be finalized in coordination with local stakeholders, and communicated to LCs and community members. Potential beneficiaries will then be identified through specific outreach and referrals from local organizations and the relief coordination administration. a scoring system will be used to prioritize based on weighting assigned to the selection criteria. Based on past experience, GAN will develop an appropriate mechanism to verify from the registered HHs in close coordination with the relief administration and the LCs. - distribution: e-voucher cards will be distributed for beneficiaries and WhatsApp channels will be created for each group of beneficiaries to communicate with them on time, a dedicated female staff will be maintained to ensure female access and protection. At DP, each beneficiary will be required to present his/her ID card and e-card to receive 100 USD each round, then the team will capture the banknote with ID to monitor the FSP and link it with the e-system invoice. MEAL team will be existed to observe the process and raised the beneficiary awareness to the complaint mechanism. - Post-distribution monitoring to monitor receipt, use and impact of assistance provided Following every distribution round, GAN will be using the Harmonized PDM tool developed by the Cash Working Group.beneficiaries will be able to submit complaints and suggestions through the complaints boxes and hotline (WhatsApp), and through the M&E field teams.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members: Likelihood: high, Impact: medium. Mitigating Measure: - Use the recommended minimum standards of cash amount. 2-Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees Likelihood: high, Impact: high. 1.Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. 2.Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. 3. Make efforts to target % of the vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support IDPs. 4. Establish feedback mechanisms and conduct follow-up visits if needs be. 5. Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process. 3- physical harm at distribution points (security, safety): Likelihood: medium, Impact: high. - Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the distribution sites are planned to be set-up, postponed or canceled. -Well organized distribution to avoid overcrowding -Training to staff on establishing and managing distributions and crowd management. -Application of checklist at distribution sites. -Consider specific risks to women and seek community opinion on how to best avoid and mitigate risk. 4-corruption at the stage of beneficiary selection: Likelihood: medium, Impact: high. 1. Strengthen over-sight mechanism. 2. ensure that their staffs have read/areaware of the Code of Conduct. 3. Outreach to affected population.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Beneficiaries may be exposed to exploitation by project stakeholders, especially vulnerable women. It will ensure that the project activities are adequately explained and the community is aware of their rights and duties towards the project and towards some of them to mitigate these risks. In addition, the mechanism of feedback and permanent monitoring of the reactions of stakeholders will contribute to knowing any risk and determining the mechanism for dealing with it. GAN is a member of PSEA network attending a monthly meeting and following the guidelines, so to mainstream, the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse in the project GAN have developed a policy stating standards of conduct including acts of SEA and a work plan to implement the policy in place, GAN will ensure all staff members are signed and adhered to a code of conduct policy, PSEA and will support the members to access relevant pieces of training (PSEA), ensuring that non-humanitarian stakeholders who will be in touch with beneficiaries such as (suppliers) will be informed and adhere to the humanitarian principles and code of conduct, PSEA posters will be available.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A1:Markets are functioning properly in the targeted areas.The money transfer agent/Hawala services are functioning. -Access to Syria remains possible and safe through the neighboring countries Risk: 1- risk: Due to the unpredictable situation in NW Syria, there is a risk that markets are not functioning properly due to lack of access, or other unexpected issues. Likelihood: low, Impact: high. Mitigation strategy: GAN confirmed the markets are functioning and the security situation allow the implementation of the project. The situation of the market will be monitored on regular basis by the program team. In case the situation would change, GAN would consider relocating the areas of intervention where the required conditions are met. 2- Risk: Money transfer agent/Hawala services are no longer available: Mitigation strategy: Proactive identification of alternative cash transfer agents, Investigate further the possibilities of vouchers systems as an alternative. 3- Risks3: Refusal by the FSP to distribute the cash after payment upfront by the aid agency: Payment after distributions: The transfers to the FSP after the cash is distributed. any issues or robberies happening to the FSP must be covered by the FSP itself as stipulated in the contract. 4-Inflation – price increases for staple items due to lack of supply to meet demand causing harm to all affected people and other community members who use the market.GAN will use and adhere to CWG’s common Risk Mitigation Matrix.
