Project Summary

Children in Somalia continue to face acute humanitarian needs due to conflict and environmental hazards such as drought and flooding. COVID-19 pandemic has compounded the challenges exacerbating vulnerability of the crisis-affected populations across the country. These crises have been the drivers of vulnerabilities which have made access to basic social services extremely low in Somalia. According to Somalia Humanitarian Needs Overview, 7.7 million people need assistance in Somalia representing a half of the population of Somalia and 2.9 million people are IDPs. 593,000 people were displaced in Somalia between January and September 2021(PRMN).
The recurrent violence and climate change have negatively impacted the education delivery. The education sector continues to lack vital learning resources, trained teachers, adequate facilities, and access to safe learning environment is constrained for most girls and boys. Continued lack of financial support has also hindered the delivery of education services. As such the most affected are the vulnerable children, including children with disabilities who have no access to education services. The lack of educational opportunities coupled with an uncertainty due to the crisis leave many children in psychosocial distress and vulnerable to further violence, abuse, neglect, and exploitation.
3 million crisis affected children need education assistance in 2022 (Education Cluster). The increased needs of education assistance are as result of increasing population, COVID-19 pandemic, conflicts, and climate change. An estimated 237,200 (40%) school-aged children have been displaced in 2021. The impact of crisis has adversely affected the education system, which has resulted in many children growing up without the education, protection and support that schools should provide. Girls and boys face steep obstacles in accessing education services, as 41% of IDP settlements have routes to schools that are not perceived to be safe (CCCM Cluster 2021). Education services are inadequate with only 12 per cent of enrolled children in primary schools are from rural areas, while the rural population is over 50% of the national population (HNO). According to the government figures, 34% of Somali girls are married by the age of 18, and 16% before their 15th birthday.
Teachers, especially in rural areas work within an extremely challenging environment; inconsistent monthly incentives, lack of teaching and learning materials, and inadequate training and supervision, among others. This significantly affects the learning outcomes of the most vulnerable children in Somalia, as teachers are the main and critical component of any education system. The 2018/2019 EMIS shows the total number of qualified teachers is only 35%.
Access to educational opportunities for children with disabilities is a major challenge. The number of children with disabilities enrolled in primary school is significantly low with only 811 children (47% girls) enrolled in schools; this represents 0.3% of total enrolment (2018/2019 EMIS). The exact number of school-aged children with disabilities in Somalia is not yet known and education system is not yet developed and equipped to meet the varied needs of children with extreme cases of disability.
The 2021 COVID-19 closure of the schools disrupted learning exacerbating already existing disparities within the education system and children lives in Somalia. The impacts of COVID-19 exposed children to higher risks of abuse and increased psychosocial distress and deprived of opportunities for growth and development. The disadvantages are disproportionate for under-privileged learners who tend to have fewer educational opportunities.
The proposed project aims to improve access to quality education for the most vulnerable children including adolescents’ children with disabilities for both the displaced and non-displaced vulnerable communities. It aims to target 220,000 (110,000 girl) in Somalia.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Mitigation Measures Protection of displaced civilians: Centrality of protection will be the key area this response focuses on, to ensure children have access to inclusive learning opportunities in safe and protective environments. The interventions will be designed in a way that addresses protection risks related to the impacts of conflict and natural disasters. To ensure the protection of children, the project will include an integrated Education – Child Protection response, in line with the 2019 Education – Child Protection Response Framework. The interventions will consequently ensure improved safe and protective learning environments with access to protection services to reduce vulnerabilities and strengthen the safety of learners.In line with the Education Cluster Response Plan, the project’s strategy will have a two-pronged approach; a) prioritizing education support to children at the place where they primarily live, and b) support education to children at place where they have moved due to displacement, to ensure they have continued access to education and protection services.Mitigating risks related to exclusion from humanitarian services: UNICEF and the implementing partners will ensure community consultations and discussions take place during the assessment, planning, implementation, and monitoring phases of the project. This will ensure the views and needs of the communities are taken into consideration, as the stakeholders themselves are best placed to find the most suitable solutions to address their own problems.Key stakeholders, such as Community Education Committee (CECs), community leaders, parents, teachers, and all learners will be engaged, to raise awareness on child rights and influence behaviors and practices that enhance overall enabling learning environments for children.At the school level, CtCs will support peer to peer support, rights awareness and will take part in schools' decision making, to ensure children's voices are heard. The project will be designed to address the specific needs of girls, in recognition of the specific challenges they face, including the provision of sanitary kits to adolescent girls to ensure regular school attendance. This will enhance girls’ access to learning opportunities.Protection mainstreaming: The community will be engaged, to ensure improved safety at and on the way to and from schools. In addition, the communities will be involved in the establishment of safe and violence-free environments with improved infrastructure, such as gender responsive learning and sanitation facilities. Teachers will be trained in psychosocial support and provide referral pathways for children in need of specialized child protection services, such as counselling and case management. Schools will identify a male and female teacher to be the child protection focal point of the school, who will support learners in need of protection support.
