Project Summary

In line with the 2025 Child Protection Area of Responsibility (CPAoR) needs and humanitarian response plan, SC will provide child-centered interventions to 21,500 people, 17,000 children (8,500B & 8,500G), 4,500 adults (2,250W & 2,250M) such as comprehensive case management support, material support, mental health and psychosocial services and cash-based initiatives to 2,300 (1150B & 1150G) most vulnerable children that include survivors of sexual and gender-based violence who face heightened risks and limited access to services, unaccompanied and separated children (UASC),,children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAGs) and other children prone to abuse, violence and exploitation, 700 (350G & 350B) children will be supported through cash assistance for child protection. Community based psychosocial support interventions which include safe families programming to 18,000 people, 14,000 children (7,000B & 7,000G), 4,000 adults (2,00W & 2,000M) to ensure the engagement of children, families, community leadership and members in child protection interventions. In addition, capacity building of 500 (250W & 250M) child protection players at both national and subnational level which will include frontline workers, Government stakeholders and child protection agencies to ensure delivery of quality services to at risk children and their families. 384 awareness raising sessions will be conducted with children and caregivers on CP issues, protection risk, counselling and positive parenting.
The target group for the sector are vulnerable children at risk of abuse, violence and exploitation, children with identified protection concerns that include UASC, CAAFAG, survivors of sexual and gender-based violence and other categories of children affected by conflict and climate induced factors leading to displacements in IDP camps and host communities. Furthermore, families of affected children and community leaders that involves traditional leaders, Chiefs, religious leaders and community members are targeted by interventions. Child protection practitioners, case workers, social workers and child protection teams in Government, INGOs and NNGOs and multi sectoral stakeholders who work with children affected by abuse, violence, exploitation and neglect will be targeted.

1. Needs

The South Sudan HRPN 2025 indicated that South Sudan will continue to experience a protracted child protection crisis, particularly in conflict-affected and underserved areas across the country. Key child protection issues include family separation, recruitment into armed groups, physical and sexual violence, exploitation, and psychosocial distress, particularly among adolescent girls. Children, especially in urban areas experience high levels of exploitation and resort to enrollment into gangs as an extreme coping mechanism. Children with disabilities and child survivors of GBV, particularly girls, are disproportionately at risk with limited access to essential services such as health, psychosocial support and legal aidSC conducted a Children's Multi-sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) between July and August 2024, covering Abyei Renk, Akobo, and Bor, in Akobo and Bor, 47% of the surveyed population face key barriers to accessing protection services such as lack of information/knowledge of CP services available; safety and protection concerns, the surveyed households specifically identified physical violence, revenge killings, theft or robbery, forced labour, domestic violence, and sexual violence against women as the main protection risks faced in their communities. Other frequently seen risks include abduction, early and forced marriage and child labour. Major risks in Abyei include, child labour mentioned by 15.2% of the surveyed households also visible on the streets of Abyei, domestic violence, physical violence in the community, psychological violence at household and in the community, family separation, sexual violence, child marriage, negligence among others.15% of the sampled population in Renk reported the presence of UASC caused mainly by accidental separation during displacements and death or fleeing parents or caregivers. Communities in Renk county face challenges arising from chronic food insecurity, patriarchal gender norms, high levels of poverty, intra- and inter-community tensions and insecurity, the yearly cycle of extreme flooding, and the war in Sudan. For refugee children and youth, protection risks are exacerbated as their parents leave the transit centers and camp in search of employment opportunities to meet basic needs, leaving them alone for days at a time. That same economic and survival pressure has also increased incidences of child labor among adolescent boys and girls. Moreover, the MSNA highlighted theft and robbery, child marriages and family separation, physical violence, domestic violence, and sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) as other major protection risks, while access to basic protection services is limited. The presence of active combatants in some of the refugee camps means that both refugee and host community children and youth remain vulnerable to abduction and recruitment into armed groups.

2. Project activities

Vulnerable groups of children, victims of distress, abuse, neglect and exploitation as indicated in the vulnerability assessments will be supported with various interventions. Upon identification, they will be supported with the appropriate case management response, which includes Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR) and reintegration support and alternative care. The high-risk cases will include Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC), Children Associated with Armed Forces and Groups (CAAFAG), and child survivors of Sexual and Gender based Violence (SGBV) among other risks. A vulnerability assessment will be completed for each child, case plans drawn and implemented according to case management guidelines and the standard case management SoPs for South Sudan, which will be further contextualized with SC’s Steps to Protect Common Approach. Community based child protection networks will also be strengthened to support identification, monitoring and reporting of child protection incidents in the community and supported to conduct awareness raising events on child protection, sharing information on child protection services and identification of potential foster care givers for UASC. They will work closely with case workers to ensure case follow-ups are timely.Through child friendly spaces, SC will target age categories of 5-10years, 11-14years, 15-17 years offering a safe place for vulnerable children to spend time and engage in activities that help them learn new skills, overcome anxiety and regain a sense of normalcy and social connection to peers. The CFS will also be an entry point to children for the provision of MHPSS services inclusive of HEART programs and for formation of children’s groups for child-led initiatives. The project will apply child-led peer-to-peer initiatives and activities for messaging on child protection concerns in their communities during events such as International Day of the Girl Child, Day of the African Child and 16 days of activism against GBV.Child Protection formal and informal service providers will be equipped with knowledge and tools to deliver quality child protection and SGBV services.
