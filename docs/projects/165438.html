Project Summary

This unconditional multi-purpose cash assistance program will help vulnerable Syrian households (HHs) meet their survival basic needs, focusing on Dana sub district in Harim district of Idleb Government, where IRC is the current cash actor lead as determined by the cash working group. IRC has the ability and capacity to reach communities in other sub-districts depending on needs and coverage by other cash actors as coordinated through the Cash Working Group platform on a case-by-case bases. The IRC has been conducting cash assistance in northwest Syria (NWS) since 2016, and has a strong global technical enterprise in cash and voucher assistance. Based on experience with direct cash interventions, the IRC hopes to scale up the cash current responses to displaced HHs in Dana to meet their basic needs. Since January 2020, the IRC has already provided cash relief to over 4,400 HHs in Dana and Maarat Misrin. In this project, HHs will receive a one-time cash transfer in USD or SYP depending on market monitoring on exchange rates, currency availability, and coordination with other cash actors. The transfer value will be per Cash Working Group recommendation, and will cover the entire SMEB plus a 'float'.

4 - Needs

According to HNO, 2019 an estimated 2.7 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance. Of those, 9 million require emergency food assistance to meet their basic food needs, including 6.5 million people facing life-threatening food insecurity. Disruptions to income generating activities have contributed to lack of cash flow to HHs and diminished purchasing power leading to challenges to meet food and other basic needs. This especially negatively effects vulnerable HHs and IDPs that are often excluded from income generating activities without such disruptions. This leads to negative coping strategies with people resorting to increasingly exploitative and hazardous activities as their financial and material resources are exhausted. Such negative coping strategies are likely to disproportionately affect the most vulnerable individuals, including IDPs, female headed household, pregnant/lactating mothers, child-headed households, elderly people and persons with disabilities (PWDs). Women and girls are at particularly high risk of gender based violence and early or forced marriage, while young men and boys are at high risk of recruitment and child labor. A recent IRC assessment in Dana found HHs had an average CSI score was 36.94, and a recent Rapid Needs Assessment found that food was the number one need for HHs followed by fuel and tents, with 91% preferring cash over in-kind assistance. IRC’s recent market assessment in Dana found that while food is widely available, non-food items (NFI) such as mattresses, blankets and tents are more rare following increased IDP arrivals, insecurity, fluctuations in exchange rates and increase in commodity prices. Recent PDMs find that HHs spend cash assistance on debt repayment, then food, and then rent with 97% saying items they needed were available in the market.

5 - Implementation of activities

The IRC will provide multi purpose unconditional cash grants to vulnerable HHs, including recently displaced IDPs, HHs headed by women, the elderly, PWDs, and vulnerable host community members. The amount of cash transfer will be per Cash Working Group recommendation which is based on the Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket and is currently $120 USD per HH. Assistance will be likely single-round aimed at IDP displacement but will also include some vulnerable host HHs who are referred from Protection teams. The IRC's beneficiary selection process uses economic vulnerability scoring and ensures vulnerable groups, including people with disabilities, female headed HHs, and the elderly are automatically qualified for cash assistance. IRC conducts 100% HH-level verification of lists provided by the local councils, uses QR code technology as well as photo identification and security questions to ensure assistance goes to the correct individual, and has multi-levels of monitoring and a highly-functional feedback and complaint system to detect any issue. Pre and post distribution market assessments are conducted. Post distribution monitoring is conducted 2-3 weeks after distribution. To address possible exclusion due to the lack of civil status documentation, the IRC has developed Community Validation steps which allows the identification of those without civil status documentation to be confirmed and clears them for assistance, should they be eligible. In addition to registering HHs on the Local Council list, registration desks, wide project awareness during community engagement, and accountability and complaints system provide additional channels for HHs to self-identify themselves to the IRCs registration teams if they are not on the list, to mitigate against exclusion. As only distribution cash to women in the HH could lead to significant protection risks, cash will be distributed to the head of HH, regardless of gender, in order to not increase tensions in the HH.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The IRC is committed to making the concept of Do No Harm a practical reality in its cash programming by incorporating conflict sensitivity and protection principles within project design, implementation, access, security, monitoring, and engagement with beneficiaries. According to Protection Risk Analysis (PRA) guidance, key examples include risks related to heightened visibility for beneficiaries/exposure such as increased tensions with other beneficiaries in the community not selected for assistance; cash overly spent on non food items, thus negatively impact food security outcomes; cash not being equally shared/distributed within the HH such as increased tensions in a HH when financial decision making norms are disrupted. The IRC will seek to mitigate these risks by ensuring strong community buy in and support through community and local council engagement, market monitoring to ensure price and availability of food and commodities prior to distribution and repeated post-distribution to measure negative effects on markets; USD or SYP is considered; including community members in the project design; clear eligibility criteria and a transparent beneficiary selection process; clearly articulated expectations for HH and local councils regarding payment of any taxation in any form; and closely monitoring all activities by an independent team. By providing MPCA to vulnerable HHs, the IRC aims to reduce the need for negative coping strategies such as pulling youth out of school, youth joining armed groups or taking on exploitative employment in order to meet food security needs for themselves and their HHs, cutting down food consumption, as well as the specific risk to women and girls such as exploitation, expectations of sexual favors to reduce debt burdens, or coercion at check points if women/girls do not have sufficient money to cross and pay fees. Targeting FHHs helps to ensure that risks associated with negative coping strategies are reduced for women and girls.
