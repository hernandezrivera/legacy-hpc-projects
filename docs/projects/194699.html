Project Summary

ACRA proposes a 12 months Water Supply, Sanitation and Hygiene Promotion services to the returnees, host and IDPs communities in in Jonglei (Fangak, Ayod, Akobo and Uror Counties), Upper Nile (Maiwut and Longechuk Counties) and CES (Terekeka). The Program goal is to reduce and/or mitigate potential further escalation of WASH related diseases and to provide integrated services on nutrition, protection and WASH activities aimed at reduce risks relating to GBV of vulnerable populations affected by triple shocks of flooding, conflict and COVID-19 through timely, sustainable and equitable access to clean and safe water, sanitation and hygiene services. This is in line with the first WASH cluster sectoral objective integrates: WASH response with nutrition and health which aims to reduce the burden of WASH-related diseases as aggravating factors for malnutrition and the second WASH cluster sectoral objective: address life-saving needs in disaster contexts in restoring immediately access to basic WASH services either temporarily or permanently including flood response scenarios. The project will also contribute to cluster sectoral objective 3; resilience building in locations that were affected by flooding in Jonglei and Upper Nile. ACRA will however endeavor to mainstream protection and mitigate WASH Gender Based Violence to guarantee that beneficiaries of WASH services, in particular women and children can access facilities in a safe manner.
ACRA will integrate WASH activities with nutrition and health to maximise the impact and reduce excessive morbidity and mortality of children under five will also provide standard WASH NFI kits along with hygiene promotion to 8,000 SAM/MAM children and their family/caretakers at facilities and follow up visits will be conducted for households hosting the children. In addition, ACRA will provide access to safe water and sanitation facilities to 20 nutrition facilities. ACRA will also support communities that are showing high prevalence of GAM rate through improving access to safe water and promoting safe hygiene practices. WASH activities will also be integrated into health activities in the context of disease prevention and response. This aims at breaking the transmission cycle of water related diseases including diarrhoeal, cholera, vector borne diseases and COVID-19. This is intended to be achieved through multiple integrated approaches (improving access to safe water and hygiene promotion) and ensuring minimum IPC in health facilities supported. ACRA will work with protection partners, community leaders and religious leaders to identify people with disabilities, elders, marginalised and PLWHIV. Focus group discussions will be conducted with these groups of people or their representatives to identify their needs, barriers, and obstacles that limit them from accessing WASH services. During project design, these groups of people or their representatives will engage with ACRA and forward their views and opinion and design preference for the construction of sanitation facilities. In all the project locations, ACRA will assess protection concerns related to women and girls accessing WASH services and take robust action to mitigate the risk of exposing women and girls. Depending on the contexts, ACRA will install 10 new water points where there is low coverage, upgrade two existing handpumps with solar power system, rehabilitate 40 existing water points and/or distribute water filters or household water treatment products. ACRA will conduct general community hygiene promotion in the target locations with emphasising disease prevention (such as COVID-19, cholera and AWD), safe excreta management, safe water storage and handwashing with safe water and soap. Water user committees shall be formed and trained to ensure project sustainability. ACRA will construct institutional and communal toilets in crowded settings where their absence will create a public health risk.

1 - Needs

Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) conditions were found to be most severe in the Nile Basin states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap and Lakes, and Eastern Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal, (HNO, 2022). Between January and December 2023, an estimated 1.4 million children under five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition based on analysis and estimations from the results of the SMART nutrition surveys, Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) and program admission trends. Approximately 345,893 will need SAM treatment while another 1,057,703 will need MAM treatment. Acute malnutrition burden will be highly concentrated in the four states of Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap in about 61% of the total burden. Displacements associated with insecurity, severe flooding and the economic downturn have compounded the impact of historically low coverage of water infrastructure. The economic downturn has exacerbated the disruption of water services, limiting resources available for the maintenance of wells and boreholes in areas hosting displaced people, above-capacity demand results in water points being unable to supply enough drinking water to the people, with the situation worsening when additional people are displaced. According to the WASH cluster HRP 2022 severity mapping and targeting, the WASH sector needs and gaps in Fangak, Ayod, Uror and Akobo Counties of Jongolei State, Terekeka of CES and Longechuk and Maiwut Counties are highlighted as severity level 4 and at critical condition. This trend is expected to continue in 2023. According to IPC analysis, it projected that 62.7% of the population in South Sudan who will be facing severe food insecurity. According to the December 2022 to March 2023 IPC analysis, 703,000 people in Jonglei State are in Crisis (IPC3) while another 559,00 people are classified as emergency (IPC4). In Upper Nile 622,000 people are projected to be in IPC3 while 239,000 are classified as IPC4 (emergency). Jongolei and Upper Nile are some the state worst hit by the triple climatic shocks including flooding that led to mass displacement of populations and has caused a lot of strain and stress on existing WASH facilities. According to the WASH cluster severity mapping; only 39% households have sufficient access to safe water and only 11% have access to sanitation in Upper Nile and Jongolei States. Improvements in security Jongolei has resulted in mass influx of returnees placing considerable strain on resources and humanitarian partners’ ability to adequately meet the WASH needs of both returnees and hosting communities. The return of south Sudanese refugees from neighbouring Countries has further stretched the demand for WASH assistance. As per the WASH cluster mapping, the overall access to water quantity in Terekeka is between 21%-40% only with limited access to sanitation, WASH NFI and Handwashing stations. Floods in 2021 and 2022 had affected over one million people in 47 counties with an estimated 485,000 people being displaced from their homes. Given the congestion of flood-displaced populations in high ground areas and heavy contamination of the safe water sources in Jonglei and Upper Nile States, as mass displacement across the country affects the majority of the population, the WASH situation assessments gave alarming results. According to IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix, 89.7% of IDPs and 90.6% of returnees in host-community settings live in settlements without gender-separated latrines, compared to 30.8 per cent of IDPs living in camps and camp-like settings. According to October, 2022 IRNA report for Uror County, there are over 1109 household in Uror County which are affected by the flood. Therefore, immediate scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to save lives and avert total collapse is required especially in the ACRA target Counties in IPC4.

2 - Project activities

The following activities will be implemented;1.1. Installation of additional water points and rehabilitation of water point including hand pumps repairs targeting the Payams with lowest coverage; Safe and adequate drinking water will be provided at health facilities level for patients, caregivers, and staff. ACRA will also ensure all the nutrition facilities are implementing the basic IPC WASH services. ACRA will also supports communities that are showing high prevalence of GAM rates through improving access to safe water and promoting safe hygiene practices. Depending on the contexts, ACRA will install new water points where there is low coverage and rehabilitate existing non-functional water points. During rehabilitation in flood prone areas, the aprons of the water points will be raised to avoid inflow of contaminated water from floods. 1.2. Establishment and training of Water management committee (WMC). The training will include specific modules on operation and maintenance of water sources and material support the WMC with pump maintenance tool kits. ACRA will ensure that at least 50% of committee members are women.1.3. Construction of communal latrines that are gender and disability sensitive, using iron sheets and plastic slabs clearly marked with separate gender, lockable from inside, well-lit for women & men, boys & girls fitted with handwashing point. ACRA will also install handwashing facilities in crowded public places including communal latrines and markets. Institutional sanitation facilities will also be provided (in schools, health facilities and in nutrition sites) 1.4. Establish Solid Waste Management Committees (SWMCs) in the target villages supported with cleaning tools and training with active participation of both men and women. 1.5. Treatment of water sources at HH level through chlorination and distribution of household water treatment and storage (HHWTS) kit (including 1 bucket with lid with tap, 1 Jerican, soap for 3 months, 1 filter cloth).1.6. Provision of safety audits before and after any new construction and at every facility rehabilitated facility aimed at addressing protection and specifically GBV ACRAs in WASH services. The safety audits will also include the establishment/update of referral pathways in every location. ACRA conducts protection analysis and focus group discussions with women and girls before constructing both water points and communal latrines1.7. Provision of MHM kits for menstrual age women, including solar lamps and prioritizing GBV survivors or particularly vulnerable women and girls at risk (widows, child headed households, etc.)1.8. Hygiene promotion at community level with emphasis in the following topics: diseases prevention (Water-borne diseases and COVID-19), handwashing, safe excreta management, GBV messages and promotion of referral pathways. Training of health/hygiene promoters who will be 60% female will conduct hygiene promotion at household and community level, early detect and manage AWD/cholera cases in the areas targeted by ACRA and also identify and refer GBV cases to ACRA protection team for subsequent actions and guidance.1.9. Provide WASH and nutrition integrated responses: ACRA will continue supporting SAM/MAM children and caretakers by providing a standard WASH NFIs kits along with hygiene promotion at the facilities and follow up visits to the households depending on the COVID-19 situation. The WASH NFIs include household water treatment products, a bucket with lid, a bucket with tap and lid, soap, and filter cloth. Hygiene promotion targets caregivers or mothers at the facility level with priority hygiene messages such as hand hygiene, safe handling of food and drinking water, and safe excreta disposal1.10. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM): ACRA will carry out both mini (at end of each distribution) and comprehensive (after 1-2months of distribution). PDM to document feedback from beneficiaries on the items preferences

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

For the people with special needs, whose access to feedback and complaint mechanism is limited, ACRA will adjust the community-based complaints and feedback mechanism paths to provide a platform for beneficiaries with special needs to voice any issues of ACRAs and feedback on the project activities. ACRA will set up help desks and encourage vulnerable community members to share any feedback they have on the project through face to face meetings. The most vulnerable members will form part of the PDM sample to ensure that their voice is heard. Help desks will be erected in FSL and WASH sites to provide a good opportunity for two-way dialogue. In case of beneficiaries with disabilities with limited mobility, ACRA will visit households with the help of the hygiene promoters and food monitors/ enumerators to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate, community hygiene promoters, complaint committee and enumerators will be encouraged to document the complaints and feedbacks since they are selected from within the community and are familiar with the local language. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the complaints and feedback mechanism, as well having the opportunity to provide feedback directly to ACRA program staff or monitoring team. Feedback and complaints can also be shared through local chiefs, FGDs, hygiene promoters, food monitors/ enumerators, water user committee, FSL enumerators who are part of the community and are participating in all implementation activities. Depending on what people feel comfortable with, they can share the feedback personally or via trusted persons.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Through community mobilization and sensitization activities, ACRA will endeavour to provide accurate, timely and accessible information to beneficiaries about every aspect of the project including the planned activities, beneficiary entitlements, timelines and how to share feedback or report complains to ACRA. Community mobilization will take the form of regular community and bilateral meetings. ACRA will also place banners and signposts with project details at strategic points in the community. ACRA will actively involve beneficiaries in identifying their priorities and solutions to their challenges to ensure that the voices and ACRAs of all groups are considered during the project’s design, planning and implementation. Data for assessment, baseline and monitoring will be collected through institutions and HH survey, focus group discussions with representatives of various groups disaggregated by gender and age, people with disabilities, interviews with key informants, authorities and other NGOs present in the area. Each time ACRA and/or enumerators will inform about the scope and aim of data collection. Collected data will be a base for designing activities, adjusting to the needs or external circumstances. Special attention will be given to women and girls’ voices, PWD and members of minority groups. Regular meetings with the community representatives during intervention implementation will be organised to present plans of the activities and receive feedback on the implementation process, as well as suggestions for improvement. At all phases of the intervention, ACRA staff will always consult and engage with the beneficiaries and local authorities to ensure that the decisions are made jointly with their prior knowledge.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

ACRA staff will be responsible for championing good practices and maintaining an organizational culture that prioritizes and safeguards all against SEA. Managers at all levels (Both Juba and Field level) have responsibilities to support and develop systems that maintain an environment that facilitates implementation of this Policy and which prevents SEA. During the community mobilization and sensitization meetings, ACRA will provide beneficiaries with information on how to safely and confidentially report SGBV/SEA if and when it occurs. ACRA will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues of ACRAs and feedback on the project activities. In line with ACRA's Safeguarding Policy on PSEA, ACRA will inform the target communities about ACRA's staff responsibilities and the expected code of conduct. At the start of the project, ACRA will develop a plan on how information about PSEA will be disseminated. In all its communication activities, ACRA will re-emphasize that all the beneficiary entitlements are provided freely and that they should immediately report if and when they’re asked to do or provide anything in exchange of the project’s entitlements. ACRA will work closely with community leaders and stakeholders and will sensitize them on how to report any SEA cases that they come across. The PSEA information will be part of information campaign conducted at the beginning of the project implementation. The messages will be given by ACRA officers (including women) to groups of beneficiaries during FGD baseline studies, KII, education campaigns. PSEA will be important part of training of community members engaged in the project and volunteers.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To ensure effective records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, ACRA team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. ACRA will also organize project performance and quality review meetings with staff, beneficiaries, and partners to assess progress towards the set objectives and work plans, to monitor project performance and quality of activities based on monitoring and beneficiary feedback data, to identify risks and set risk management strategies during implementation. A project evaluation will be carried out at the end of the project and the process used to measure the achievement of project outputs and outcomes then documented for the record. ACRA will take immediate and appropriate action upon receipt of any report or complaint about SEA allegations, ACRAs or suspicions, or retaliation related to SEA. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and will be used to adjust project activities. The records of complaints and feedback is kept and regularly analysed by MEAL team in Juba, whose officers track each case and advise/support the programme team. The implementing programme team, under supervision of HoP will conduct regular review meetings and adjust the project implementation to according to constructive feedbacks received.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The risk of members of the marginalized community’s/minority groups not being included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance: The mitigation measure for this will be for ACRA to adopt participatory, vulnerability and rights-based approaches throughout the project life-cycle from the onset of the project, consultations, identification, verification and registration of target beneficiaries. Under the guidance of ACRA, the PMC’s will identify and register the WASH NFI beneficiaries according to ACRA’s vulnerability criteria for targeting women/child/chronically ill-headed HHs, People with disabilities, widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned, Persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS, Survivors of GBV etc. This will ensure the prioritization of assistance towards those who need it most. This risk will also be mitigated by ensuring that vulnerable groups are part of the targeted group right from the assessment. During the registration for distribution, ACRA will endure door to door registration to ensure that even those who are not able to come to the registration points are registered including their vulnerability status. Women and girls will be consulted separately on their needs. ACRA will ensure that decisions pertaining location of WASH facilities are made in consultation with women and girls. Separate focus group discussions will be carried out specifically for women and girls by a female staff or protection officer so that they can open up During registration/distribution, ACRA do registration from household to household and will ensure that order is maintained by involving community leaders, crowed controllers and creating separate ques for men and women, children and different categories of people. Efforts will be put to ensure WASH programs are not reinforcing exploitative gender stereotypes and inequalities but rather that all partners thrive to implement sensitive and transformative WASH interventions. ACRA safeguarding policies require all staff and any one engaged in program to comply with code of conduct and reporting of breaches. The beneficiaries will be sensitised on CRM and their right to share feedback and complaint. ACRA will ensure that the staff are trained on GBV, aware about referral pathways and sensitise the beneficiaries on reporting and use of GBV pathways. The protection risk assessment will be conducted before deciding the distribution site to ensure that distribution sites are safe and accessible to the beneficiaries. ACRA will conduct protection assessments and risk analysis, stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with clear age gender and disability (AGD) data to understand context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level for each location. ACRA will achieve it through focus group discussions, key informant interviews, meetings and HH surveys with women, men, children, institution workers, community leaders, within IDPs, the host community and returnee population. The project has clear risk analysis and mitigation related to access, including logistic and security challenges. The analysis is regularly repeated and the security situation is monitored. Ongoing coordination and communication with the log cluster are done to support the delivery of project items. ACRA will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues. ACRA will strengthen community communication to ensure beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project, beneficiary criteria and how to provide feedback or complains to ACRA. This will be done through regular meetings with the target communities and where possible use of IEC materials to disseminate information in the target communities about the CRM. ACRA will sensitize the local authorities on the beneficiary selection criteria. The beneficiary selection process will be monitored by ACRA to ensure impartiality.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The access to water, sanitation and hygiene is one of the basic services, allowing dignified and safe life. As described in the proposal, ACRA ensures the equitable access and inclusion of all community groups, by various means through provision of flood resilient WASH facilities i.e. aprons in flood prone areas will be raised to avoid flood water. ACRA works strongly to build future resilience and basis for the long-term development of the communities, minimizing exclusion, empowering the vulnerable groups and encouraging ownership of the project results.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

ACRA coordinates with protection partner and camp management for protection services and referral of GBV survivors. ACRA ensure that staff is trained and aware about protection risk and referral pathways. Similarly the beneficiaries will be informed about referral pathways for GBV cases. The WASH service provision will conduct protection mainstreaming using WASH cluster guidelines. WASH services, as planned in the proposed project has some, however not a direct and significant influence on the provision of protection services. Mainly, thanks to community mobilization, ACRA officers, water user committees, hygiene promoters can spread the protection messaging and encourage seeking for help. ACRA can provide a referral if there is protection partner in the area.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The access to adequate quality and quantity of water is essential for food production, health wellbeing and overall water born disease prevention. The income spent on water and health often taken from food budget thus the project will improve access to free water, sanitation and hygiene. Access to water will reduce community time spend on collection of water, thus the community particularly women can participate in income generating activities increasing households’ abilities to purchase nutritious foods. ACRA also promote kitchen gardening at nutrition sites and home gardening, thus the access to water is crucial for food security outcomes contributing to decreased portion of population facing IPC 2, 4 and 5.
