Project Summary

Although the prevalence of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) in Yobe and Borno relatively well (1.2%), stunting (25.2%) and under-weight (15.5%) remains problematic. Security situation in some areas of Borno is relatively calm. with pockets of Boko Haram attacks reported around Mafa, Kaga and Konduga LGA continues to attracts influx of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) from such conflict lingering areas. In spite of ongoing and existing life-saving interventions, the high prevalence of chronic poverty and food insecurity has as well exerted more pressure on the vulnerability of women and children in the entire area; thereby impacting on the high prevalence of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (10%). Children and women remain critical to suffer from the exerted pressure of poor socio-economic status, poverty, protracted conflict, food insecurity and childhood illnesses. These factors, if not addressed will continue to significantly pose serious threat to the lives of the vulnerable populations. A significant number of acute malnourished children live in Nigeria where cyclical food insecurity and protracted crisis further exacerbate their vulnerability. Since CMAM is the most desired evidence-based approach to treating SAM for majority children in need. A more practical guidance is now required at the country level to establish and continue scaling up community-based management of SAM using an approach that is established within existing systems and services to strengthen them. This focus on community mobilization and engagement which is one of the principles of CMAM and is essential to establishing maximum coverage and determinant of the effectiveness of a good CMAM programme. Therefore, this approach would significantly contribute to attain optimal nutritional status of the most vulnerable population which includes women and children. The progamme seeks to target all children age 0-59 months within host communities of Yobe and Borno state. This would be achieved through a community led participatory approach to ensure that every child within the given age range is routinely screened.
Local authorities, traditional and religious leaders, shall actively be involved in the entire process from advocacy, awareness creation, selection of mobilizers to the end of the project.

1 - Needs

Strengthening Community mobilization for improving integrated Nutrition programme in Yobe and Borno Nigeria (SCMI-INP) would help to address the problems of poor health seeking behaviors among community member; It shall increase awareness and knowledge of caregivers and community stakeholders on the importance of improving nutrition and livelihood of its members, especially of women and children for a better national development. The project seeks to train caregivers and mother on current lifesaving- IYCF-E practices that would help improve and sustain quality health, nutrition, and livelihood and food security situation in Southern Borno.

2 - Activities

Community mobilization as a component of CMAM involve raising the awareness and ensuring that members of the communities are directly involved in identification of new and recurring cases of children 6-59 months with all forms of malnutrition and health related challenges. Step1: The approach shall be achieved by recruiting Community Nutrition Mobilizers (CNMs) from within camps and host communities using community mobilization approach. The CNMs shall be selected strictly from every host community and must at least communicate fairly with the local language and must read and write. Selection shall be done along with recruiting few supervisors with some tertiary level of education. They shall serve as guide to the CNMs to ensure that screening, referrals and follow up as deemed fit for malnutrition is done through existing best practices. Step 2: CNMS and Nutrition supervisors shall be trained on current issues and practices in CMAM, IYCF an MNDC programme. The mid-upper arm circumference method shall be employed by the CNMs to actively find malnourished children 6-59months of age that will be referred to the nearest OTP/HF. Having met the required admission criteria, such children will be admitted and managed based on strict observation by a trained, qualified health worker until child is fully recovered. Step 3: Upon admission into the OTP programme, these sets of children shall be followed up down to their household to ensure that the right ratio of treatment is administered by the caregiver. The CNM shall as well follow- up on children who are absent for 2 visits and who have defaulted. Children wilt persistent stagnant weight would also be followed home to determine the source of slow recovery. Health seeking behaviour, personal and household hygiene practices shall be incorporated into the IYCF counselling guide. Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergency (IYCF-E) Programme In an attempt to complement the CMAM approach to reducing malnutrition in southern Borno, the IYCF-E programme shall be used to foster the process through the formation of Mother-to-Mother Support Groups (MMSG) and father to father support groups (FFSG) in camps and host communities. MMSG/FFSG meetings shall be facilitated on a monthly basis by CNMs, of which participant shall not be more than 15 persons per group. Member of these groups shall comprise of women who are Pregnant and lactating, young women of child bearing age, elderly women and men in the host community. Social and behavioural communication change approach shall be used as tool to inform and embrace current breastfeeding practices, hygiene, ANC, immunization and common household sanitary practices that could help improve the nutritional status of the children and their mothers. In addition, health facility based IYCF counselling sessions shall also be incorporated into the OTP program such that women with counselling needs shall be identified and given appropriate IYCF based information in a bid to improve basic nutritional demands of their children. Such session shall be held within a carved out space within the OTP/HF. Also, food demonstration sessions shall be held along with the counselling session. Micronutrient Deficiency Control Program (MNDC) Still in an attempt to solve the pressing nutritional challenges at the host communities, special attention shall be given to children who are moderately malnourished i.e children identified by MUAC (11.5cm-12.5cm). These shall be approached by administering Micronutrient powders to affected children for 60 days. Key messages on usage shall be duly explained by attending health personnel to the caregiver. We hope to target children within the range of 6- 23months of age.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

AHSF shall consider differentiated needs women, men, girls, and boys may have and give attention to persons with special needs, such as elderly, disabled or chronically ill people. Disaggregated data is key. Protection considerations, notably the principles of do no harm and non-discrimination will be adhered to during assessments, programme design, while implementing as well as when monitoring and evaluating the project. Thus, AHSF will prioritize, Safety & security, including land tenure security, and dignity; meaningful access, including for women, children, persons with disabilities or other persons with special needs. AHSF shall work consciously in compliance with the Sphere Humanitarian Core Standards and Imperatives. All approaches shall align with the principles of protection as we remain committed to ensuring that this project does not expose beneficiaries to more risk in compliance with the DO NO HARM Protection Principle. Also, AHSF shall be intentional about maintaining an environment where there is zero sexual exploitation and abuse. All staff, volunteers, contractors, and vendors engaged directly or indirectly to implement this project shall be trained on PSEA. They shall be familiarized with FSACI's policy on PSEA, and they must express their unwavering commitment by signing the AHSF’s PSEA code of conduct. Also, AHSF shall also lay down reporting protocols for the reporting of PSEA cases if unfortunately, they occur. AHSF shall also ensure that the identity of the reporters is kept very confidential. When convenient, AHSF shall encourage that reports are made on anonymous grounds.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

AHSF having worked with so many communities in the regions and have a strong base connect with all the needed leaders, will ensure the active participation and involvement of beneficiaries in all stages of the project. AHSF will ensure accountability to affected populations using various consultative processes (suggestion boxes, interviews, community meetings, FGD, CRM, etc.). AHSF shall also administer survivor centered surveys at the beginning of, during and at the end of the project to collect perceptions and feedback from beneficiaries that should guide programme activities. In addition, complaints boxes shall be kept at strategic positions within the project environment for beneficiaries and their caregivers to drop their complaints. AHSF shall provide feedback of complaints to the beneficiaries, parents, and other relevant community stakeholders as it is made available and well validated. Effective and timely actions shall be taken to address concerns received through our established feedback mechanism. The identity of complainants shall remain confidential except otherwise requested by the complainant or feedback provider
