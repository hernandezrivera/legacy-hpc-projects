Project Summary

Action Against Hunger provide Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) support to pastoralist, agro-pastoralist, peri-urban/urban and internally displaced (IDP) populations affected by persistent periods of armed conflict, political instability as well as environmental and economic shocks in Somalia. The 2021 FSL project target 81,873 individuals (21,615 male, 32,729 female, 16,047 boys and 16,702 girls) living in Bakool (Xudur, Elbarde, Wajid, Rabdhure), Sool (Xudun), Lower Shebelle ( Afgooye), Banadir (Khada), Hiraan (Beletweyne) and Nugaal (Eyl, Garowe) regions. The project plan to reach 65,304 individuals (displaced and non-displaced) in integrated phase classification (IPC) 3, 4 and beyond with immediate lifesaving interventions including unconditional, conditional cash assistance and fresh food voucher to meet their immediate acute food needs to mitigate shocks/stress. Further 16,570 individuals’ (IPC 2) will be supported with seasonal livelihood inputs support for agriculture and livestock activities to build and strengthen their resilience capacities to meet their immediate recovery needs, leverage on existing assets to withstand the effects of a shock and lay the foundation for future economic growth activities. In order to foster more sustainable stress mitigation mechanisms, ACF activities will focus on creating greater resilience for the beneficiary population by promoting economic growth activities. These activities capitalize on the foundation laid under strategies designed to enhance absorptive and adaptive capacity and promote a pathway to income generating opportunities while ensuring that the systems that support economic growth are appropriately developed and protected for future sustainability.

Project Activities

ACF will prioritize implementation of activities, which provide mechanisms to discourage negative coping strategies protecting productive assets and offset exposure for immediate recovery from shock. These interventions are implemented under the auspices includes: a) Unconditional Cash Transfer (UCT) target most vulnerable households in host and IDP populations who do not have the capacity to participate in CFW programs; the project will provide UCT to meet immediate households’ food and other basic needs. The UCT will prioritize female-headed HHs considered at risk (without productive assets, income or active labour resources, referrals from health facilities ); these include female-headed HHs already engaged in negative coping strategies, HHs with malnourished U5, PLW and HHs at risk of COVID-19 pandemic ( Elderly and chronically ill member). This will be implemented during rapid and slow onset crisis which spark massive displacement as this is when vulnerable HHs lacks vital safety net. The monthly transfer value depend on regional transfer value recommended by Somalia CWG based on regional MEB. ACF will support 5,717 households for atleast 2 transfer cycles for each target households during 2021.b) Fresh Food Voucher (FFV): ACF will provide 2,000 vulnerable with fresh food vouchers. The action target malnourished children U5 with severe acute malnutrition and PLW with moderate acute malnutrition. The priority will be given to those from extremely vulnerable households’ and are at risk of dying, influenced by severe drought and rapid onset disasters (cholera/AWD’s, flash floods, displacement) in IDP caseloads in IPC 4 zone. The voucher consist of pre-determined list of nutrition rich fresh food such as green leafy vegetables, fruits, meat, egg, milk and other iron and vitamin through restricted cash vouchers provision by local vendor. Each target household will receive food voucher value of USD 50/month for 4 months. This represent 75% percent of regional recommended monthly food transfer values of Somalia CWG. Markets assessment will be conducted at onset to determine its functionality and are commonly used as a major source of food purchase. It is envisioned that the ration will complement therapeutic supplements and households own food access strategies.c)Cash for Work (CfW) provides immediate life-saving cash relief to meet emergency basic needs while setting the foundations for medium term recovery by rebuilding both livelihoods and infrastructure such as rehabilitation of productive farmland, rangeland and water infrastructure critical to community survival ahead of the rainy seasons. The action target 3,000 households for a 2 cycles. The daily labour rate depend on recommendation by Somalia CWG. CfW intervention will provide short-term employment (income) during the lean season (dry) when HH purchasing powers are limited. ACF will employ the Inclusive Community Based Targeting (ICBT) methodology validated by HH vulnerability indicators in the design and delivery process of CFW engagement. Before the start of CFW activities, a complete technical feasibility assessment of assets will be conducted and approved for rehabilitation. MoUs will be signed with local communities on the modalities and final list of beneficiaries for CFW activities and adapted CFW tools provided to communities. d) Provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs to 2,762 most vulnerable households. This includes: i) Emergency agricultural inputs such as seeds, tools, irrigation pumps and fuel subsidy to assist farmers to immediately offset losses resulting from the emergency (desert locust, Floods or drought) and lay the foundation for immediate improvements in food security. ii) Emergency livestock inputs such as vet drugs and fodder during emergencies ( desert locust, drought) to support disease surveillance, vector control, deworming and treatments through CAHWs network and line ministries and feed supplements during crisis.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash distribution especially through individual mobile money transfer services are cost efficient and faster than commodity-based alternatives because transport and logistics costs are lower. Cash transfer programming allows recipient Choice to decide on how cash are spent enabling people to choose what they most need depending on specific households' needs and preference.Cash transfers has multiplier effects on boosting local economic in local markets and trade if the money is spent locally, and facilitate investment in agricultural production and other areas of livelihoods including VSLA.Dignity – cash can be better at maintaining the dignity of recipients. It may, for instance, be possible to avoid long, degrading queues.Fewer costs for recipients – food often costs recipients a significant amount to transport from the distribution site to their home. No transport expenses to and fro distribution sites.Avoids disincentive effects – unlike commodities (food, shelter) cash is unlikely to discourage local trade or production.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

ACF conducted organizational own monthly market assessments and participated in Somali REACH market survey. The information collected over time has confirmed the availability and affordable prices of basic needs in the local markets in ACF project target locations. During the assessment, it was also confirmed the vendors have capacity to restock the goods, and areas are accessible for transporters to supply goods. As such, ACF has a long-standing presence in the areas, hence better understanding of the local market infrastructure and services and how the targeted population interact with market even in times of crises. The market monitoring information will be used to inform the proposed project preparedness planning to mitigate shocks. The market functionality will be critical factor in providing immediate life-saving assistance to the affected populations.

Mitigation Measures

Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance: ACF have strong global policies in place to prevent Fraud, Conflict of Interest, and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), along with a strong Whistleblowing policy to receive serious complaints. ACF already maintains an active Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) in all project areas, including contact numbers. Each complaint or request for assistance is responded to in a timely manner, and serious complaints are raised to ACF regional office to ensure accountable management.Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc: ACF FSL services will target the same beneficiaries who are accessing nutrition and health services and will ensure that any beneficiary who reports or suspected cases of GBV and protection is referred to health staff who will then link the survivor to appropriate service provider through established linkages. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities: ACF will include marginalized groups at the design, implementation and monitoring of the projects. To ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable. ACF will identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and ensure they are also targeted. ACF will conduct cash transfer through mobile money transfer to ensure beneficiaries do not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access them.Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources: ACF will engage District local authorities and village development committees’ structures including opinion leaders at the kick planning stage and during implementation to allow them participate in project implementation decisions.Risk of explosive hazard contamination in project target area: ACF will avoid holding mobilization meetings near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area: ACF will offer WASH services in centres that are neutral in collaboration with local authorise i.e ministry of agriculture to allow access by all beneficiaries regardless of clan or origin. ACF will also work with local organizations if needs be who understands deep community land dynamics and are able to provide services in undisputed areas.

Please provide further explanation

ACF designs its FSL interventions in line with Somalia FSL cluster and SPHERE standard which are aligned to government quality standards to ensure that local capacity and government structures are engaged and incorporated in the programme design. ACF will engage village development committees and district authorities during beneficiaries’ selections. Selection of cash transfer amount is based on mini expenditure basket which is determined by Somalia Cash Working Group (CWG) value recommendations. Through community engagement people affected by conflicts and natural disasters are allowed are allowed to get directly involved in the project such as through village development committees. Subsequently ACF will partner with government in the delivery of services and offer capacity building to ensure that joint program implementation strategies are developed and linked to community actions. This will ensure that displaced people are integrated into the interventions and are able to access the services just like the host communities.
