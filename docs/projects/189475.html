Project Summary

As reported by the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for South Sudan 2022, it is estimated that 5,467,710 people in South Sudan will require humanitarian healthcare services in 2022. Despite the huge humanitarian health needs, the health cluster will target 3,424,096 people with various health care services in different parts of the country.
The health cluster and its partners intend to reach this target population using two main sectoral objectives among others:
1) Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations.
2) Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.
UNFPA will partner with health cluster partners and other SRH partners to identify SRH needs, planning response and providing MISP services for the population in need. The SRH services will be delivered by procurement and distribution of IARH Kits through SRH partners in the prioritized counties. UNFPA will work with partners to ensure that beneficiaries have access to quality SRH services and information in the facilities where the IARH Kits are distributed. UNFPA will help partners in providing capacity building.
UNFPA’s output on Crisis affected populations, particularly women and adolescent girls, have increased access to information and services for maternal health, family planning, gender- based violence and HIV prevention in emergency and fragile contexts and promoting humanitarian-development nexus will fit into the health cluster objectives.
Safe motherhood services including ANC visits and skilled delivery remain low with only 17% of pregnant women attending up to 4th visits and limited access to EmONC services and other factors explains why South Sudan has some of the worst health outcome indicators globally. The unmet need for family planning remains high at 24%. The needs for clinical management of rape and psychosocial services for survivors of sexual violence remains high.
UNFPA will serve 821,783 women of reproductive age (WRA) group (15-49 years) of whom we expect 119,162 deliveries (17,876 deliveries out of the 119,162 will take place in health facilities), 89,371 women are currently pregnant, 58,347 WRA will attempt to use modern contraceptives, 49,134 people seeking care for STI syndromes, 17,879 women who will experience complications and about 16,436 cases of sexual violence who will seek care. UNFPA will also seek to reach 787,542 girls (476,300) and boys (311,242) with HIV prevention services and information, adolescent sexual and reproductive health services and information. Overall, UNFPA seeks to reach 1,609,325 affected people with various forms of SRH services and information through a well-coordinated mechanism for SRH at the national, sub national and county levels by the SRH technical working groups. Overall, 1,080 maternal deaths will be averted.
The main interventions to achieve the project purpose include, among others:
a) Ensuring availability of skilled staff and quality infrastructure for the provision of integrated SRH/GBV, including emergency obstetric care services at static health facilities and through outreaches.
b) Conducting community dialogues and health education for women, men, families and the community to increase awareness on service availability, benefits of safe sex, HIV prevention, family planning, ANC, PNC and delivery in a health facility by skilled birth attendants.
c) Procuring, prepositioning, and distributing a full range of IARH Kits and Non-Kit RH/maternal medicines and sundries essential for delivering SRH services at the community and health facilities, including RH equipment for EmONC (delivery beds, examination couches, lighting, and others).
d)Training service providers in SRH and GBV related topics.

1 - Needs

The ailing health system of South Sudan has been crippled by recent conflicts and recurrent humanitarian crises especially Sub National Violence in other parts of South Sudan and the current flooding which has displaced many populations with limited access to SRH services. Only 43% of the limited health system infrastructure that existed before the conflict is functional, although most still lack equipment, supplies as well as limited health personnel, especially midwives. Those marginally functional health facilities have a crumbling infrastructure with a severe shortage of space, staff, essential medicines and equipment, and are mostly inaccessible due to inadequate transport systems and insecurity that contribute to frequent stock-outs of essential/life-saving medicines for maternal and newborn health services. The South Sudan maternal mortality ratio still ranks as one of the highest in the world (789 deaths per 100.000 live births). Motherhood should be a time of joy for a woman, her family, and her community. For women in South Sudan, however, the reality is often grim due to inaccessibility to comprehensive obstetric surgical care, the limited number of skilled birth attendants and the lack of ambulance services. Access to essential medicines and medical supplies are still critical in the IDPs and government facilities. A significant disparity in health status across socio-demographic factors and geographical location is well documented. The high maternal mortality ratio is mainly due to limited coverage and availability of quality services, confirmed by the extremely low skilled birth attendance rate of 19%, while health facility delivery is 12 percent and women attended ANC (4th or more visits) is 35% . The continued conflict and disasters have also disrupted social services leading to the destruction of health and social delivery facilities such as reproductive health clinics and equipment as well as health staff and social workers fleeing due to fear for their lives. GBV risks will be mitigated by ensuring that women, girls and boys have access to services through community awareness sessions and protection networks, mainly to engage them in HIV prevention and combating gender-based violence. Women and girls are at increased risk of rape and sexual violence; Early, child, and forced marriage; Human Trafficking; STI and HIV acquisition; Unintended pregnancy; Unsafe abortion; Complications during pregnancy and delivery; Unsafe childbirth; and Maternal death. Sexual and gender-based violence is still widespread due to armed conflict and economic hardship as women and girls resort to life survival coping mechanisms (selling sex in exchange for money or food). 4,714 incidents of GBV cases were recorded from January – June 2021 with some survivors lacking access to adequate CMR services. The health cluster estimated 5.47 million people in need of health services. However, the health cluster is targeting about 3.4 million people in need to be reached with health services and information. This target includes 821,783 women of reproductive age (15-49 years) and 787,542 adolescent girls (476,300) and boys (311,242) in need of health care in 2022. UNFPA estimates that only a third of these women and adolescents will access quality sexual and reproductive health. UNFPA will reach 273,928 women of reproductive health age group (15-49 years) and 262,514 adolescents (Girls -150,300 and Boys -112,214 ) with a total of 536,442 beneficiaries.Through both national and sub national (state) SRH coordination mechanisms, UNFPA will continue to coordinate SRH services throughout the country with other SRH partners to ensure quality care. SBA deliveries will be strengthened by providing RH Kit 2A and other Kits, building the capacity of service providers and enhancing EmONC referral systems to strengthen BEMoNC and CEMonC services. UNFPA will integrate GBV services into routine health services especially treatment of rape survivors.

2 - Project activities

This project is intended to realize one of the health cluster's core strategies of improving equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations. Therefore, the activities below are intended to ensure that RH/GBV services are available and accessible in all the affected population locations. These activities are:1) Provision of life-saving essential healthcare services including management of non-communicable diseases through static (Hospitals, PHCC, and PHCU) and mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers. 2) Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives.3) Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and accountability to the affected population.

UNFPA will contribute to following indicators of the health cluster. However, operationally, the following indicators will be used to measure the day-to-day delivery of the project. 7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries’ feedback on any project is vital as this provides an opportunity for beneficiaries to appraise the project and project team must address their concerns. This will enable the project to respond to the needs of the community. UNFPA will account to the affected population in the following ways: Active participation of affected people (women, girls, boys and men) of appropriate age groups by influencing decisions throughout the project; Conducting community consultative meetings to identify needs of affected communities; Evidence of using beneficiary experience to modify and plan responses; and prepare and submit monthly, quarterly project performance review meeting with stakeholders; quarterly and bi-annual project report to the stakeholders. Beneficiaries’ feedback information will be used to inform decision-making, re-programming and corrective actions for the identified problems.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Preferred communication channels for specific age and gender groups will be established by Implementing Partners (IPs) by having separate discussions with women and men and boys and girls in the project locations. UNFPA will also hold specific consultations with its IPs to engage people with disabilities and the elderly. Based on these discussions, communication and feedback channels will be tailored, with the information provided through community meetings and in separate age/gender/diversity groups. Avenues established in project sites include communicating directly through IPs staff/volunteers and proactive focus group discussions conducted by monitoring and evaluation teams. UNFPA will ensure that its IPs use pictorial information, education and communication (IEC) materials for low literacy populations and local staff/volunteers who speak native languages.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Protection of beneficiaries from sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) remains a core component within UNFPA programming. This is done through joint planning with protection actors, where they mainstream a PSEA within mitigation measures for barriers to integrated SRH services for SGBV survivors. Training on client-oriented service provision for health care workers on the needs and services of SGBV survivors will include PSEA messages basically focusing on prevention, risk mitigation and victim assistance through referral pathways to existing GBV and SRH service delivery points. The mechanism will record and handle SEA-related complaints, including aspects of confidentiality, accessibility and follow-up; UNFPA has a dedicated PSEA focal person who works with partners to raise awareness on duty to inform/notify/report SEA allegations. All allegations are reported in a safe, effective, and culturally appropriate manner. All received allegations are sent to the PSEA focal person, while the non-identifiable information is shared with the PSEA Task Force Chairs, who notify the Organization responsible for initiating investigations. If the allegation involves UNFPA staff, allegations are safely reported only to UNFPA OAIS through confidential means. Feedback is provided to the victims on progress so far made through the Co-chairs.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

All UNFPA projects or programmes have functional feedback mechanisms/complaint mechanisms (CBCMs) in place which the project will utilize to gather feedbacks from the beneficiaries including the community most vulnerable population. This will be done through conducting community consultative meetings, safety audits, focus group discussions and key informant interviews. The project will use complaints boxes for those who can read and write, telephone hotlines as well as rely on trained community leaders who will act as recipients of the information. The community leaders also provide feedback to the community on how complaints are being solved. The strength of the system is to ensure everyone involved is trained on confidentiality. The system is easily accessible to the community, and any information obtained through the CBCMs is handled and processed confidentially.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project will be implemented within a context where the risk of GBV and SEA is already high when accessing humanitarian assistance (Likelihood – High and impact - High). Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence and exploitation during access to humanitarian assistance would have a devastating impact on the project. The women and girls would be gravely affected physically and psychologically, among others, while the image of the humanitarian system would be tainted. The consequences of sexual violence and exploitation are life long and can severely impact the community.With that in mind, the project will scale up awareness raising sessions on prevention, mitigation and response for both community members and survivors of violence. Similarly, the project will coordinate with protection partners/GBV actors to facilitate training for other partners’ staff. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities presents the likelihood of exclusion to be High and similarly with High impact. Project consultations with communities will be broad and representative, beyond community leaders and usual constituencies. Women, men, boys and girls across all social setups will be engaged in the consultation. So far, UNFPA consulted women, men, boys and girls through focus group discussions and a key informant interview. The team will also engage community leaders and religious leaders including special groups in the community like women group, Youth group and people living with a disability so that the project will be designed to meet their specific needs. Therefore, not consulting women and girls would pause a significant risk to the project. Women and girls input into the project design would enhance ownership and improve service uptake. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. The likelihood that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms is high with a corresponding high impact if they do not access/use the complaint and feedback mechanism. UNFPA from experience often works together with beneficiaries to explore a range of options to cater for the varying needs. For example, some beneficiaries who can read and write may prefer complaint boxes, others will prefer telephone calls and others would just wish to confidentially report directly to a focal point who can receive their feedback. Either way the project will ensure all these mechanisms are in place to enhance access and use of the established complaint mechanism. Data management is an important part of project management, and all those involved in the implementation of the project must commit to managing data safely and ethically. Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries has (Likelihood - Medium and impact - High). Unsafe and unethical data management, especially in the provision of health services, can result in severe consequences. In some situations, health workers have been attacked and injured in the past due to it, while beneficiaries have shunned services due to such practices.
