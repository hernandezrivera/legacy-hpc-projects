Project Summary

This project delivers a strategic and integrated set oThis project delivers a strategic and integrated set of interventions to restore local economy in the aftermath of protracted conflict further aggravated by the impact of the COVID19 pandemic, and deteriorated economic situation. Its main objective is to create income generating opportunities for the most vulnerable IDPs, and host community members in the targeted locations. The project proposes an integrated protection and livelihood intervention where the most vulnerable households (HHs) (those being referred from child protection case management actors and GBV case management actors, as well as PwDs) will be directly targeted to enhance their resilience and promote their access to employment and entrepreneurship opportunities. The project formulation has been based in needs as identified in multi-cluster RNA/HNO/REACH/HNAP data.
By pursuing the referred main objective, the project aims to achieve the following outcomes: 1. Essential socioeconomic services are revived and restored through the creation of short-term work opportunities 2. Access to inclusive livelihoods and entrepreneurship support is enhanced through vocational training (VT) 3. Local small businesses and entrepreneurs are supported 4. Social cohesion among IDPs and host communities is enhanced For this purpose, a set of interlinked activities will be conducted in coordination with FSL, WASH, Shelter and Protection clusters.

The added value of SARD is anchored around their experience in both ERL and Protection activities, which will be a key to ensure the smooth transitioning between protection activities to livelihood activities for the most vulnerable
The project aligns itself and contributes to the objective of strengthening access to livelihoods by creating inclusive income generating opportunities and by improving access to production and market infrastructure to restore local economy recovery. Failing income, widespread unemployment and diminished purchasing power have been leading millions of Syrians to poverty over the past years. Structural changes in the labour market -caused by the fragile security situation- have increased challenges to accessing any income sources.
The growing dependence on more uncertain sources of income, such as through self-employment, remittances and humanitarian aid, combined with the deteriorating purchasing power of local currency has forced many households to develop different coping strategies to cover their basic needs. This situation has only been aggravated by the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic. For the individuals whose businesses were affected or damaged and based on each’s circumstances and evaluation, targeting will be focused on providing the main instruments/equipment/machinery to make them capable of revitalizing the business once again. For the HH who are in need of sustainable livelihood, assistance based on vocational training, cash for work support and the access to business grants will be provided. Moreover, the intervention will target the most vulnerable of HHs in NWS, those where cases of children vulnerability are being managed by child protection case management, as well as those where cases of GBV are being managed by GBV case management actors.
The amount of cash disbursed on a monthly basis is determined and adjusted based on prevailing labor rates, to ensure that CfW payments are lower than competitive market wages. We will keep close coordination with ER cluster, Cash working group and follows the Cash for Work (CfW) Minimum Standard guidance (final draft version) to ensure the harmonize and do-no harm within implementing the CFW activity.
SARD will conduct comprehensive labor market assessment to identify the needs, demands, and gaps of careers, jobs, and vocations in the targeted locations, especially in floods affected areas and nearby host communities in cooperation and coordination with the SEECC.

4 - Needs

Across north west Syria (NWS), an estimated 2.8m people need various forms of humanitarian assistance. In the backdrop of a decade of protracted crisis, the devaluation of the Syrian pound and economic turbulence over the past year has significantly eroded people’s ability to meet their basic needs, no longer being able to afford vital commodities. The ERL cluster estimates that 11.6 m people need ERL assistance in whole of Syria. In Idleb alone,an estimated 1.6m need ERL assistance, with certain population groups facing particularly high levels of vulnerability. According to HNAP Governorate Profiles report, in Idleb, 97% of individuals are in extreme poverty, and 57% are either critically below the survival minimum expenditure basket (SMEB) or below the SMEB. The three most important barriers in gaining employment or diversifying income sources are lack of employment opportunities (reported by 63% of HHs), lack of skills (57% ) and lack of financing (48%). Against this background, the most recent Humanitarian Situation Overview conducted by REACH in NWS, shows that the most reported need in 60% host communities is livelihoods, whereas this is true for 56% of the IDPs communities. However, presence of livelihoods assistance was only reported in 2% of host communities and 1% in IDP communities. In the specific case of Dana, 62% of residents and 66% of IDPs report livelihoods as a priority need (HNAP MNM March 2021).

5 - Implementation of activities

The main objective of the project is the restoration of socioeconomic infrastructures, services and businesses and the creation of livelihood opportunities among the most vulnerable IDPs and host community members in the targeted areas. The project will address the needs identified through an interconnected and integrated set of activities, as below. - Rehabilitate community and public infrastructures, such as destroyed markets, shops and parks as a means to help recover the local economy/businesses which also paves way for sustainable supply of livelihood opportunities for local communities. -Provide CfW activities for local population and IDPs, which will contribute to the rehabilitation of essential socioeconomic infrastructures and services, as well as to provide skilled labor to active local manufacturers, and thereby also promote social cohesion between IDP and local populations as a step towards recovery. - Provide for technical/entrepreneurial capacity-building and small grants to businesses, both startups and existing ones, to help them initiate and restore their livelihoods. As mentioned above, the lack of livelihoods is one of the biggest challenges in northwest Syria. In order to restore and create livelihood opportunities, the proposal aims at strengthening the purchasing power of the individuals in the targeted locations by a tailor-made set of activities defined by the needs and specificities of each population group, focusing on the most vulnerable.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Under the current context, the most vulnerable categories, such as women headed HH, PwD, may be excluded from accessing the service due to the socio-cultural barriers.Likelihood: medium, Impact: HighMitigation Measures. a) SARD increase visibility for self-referral to ensure inclusion of the most vulnerable individuals. b) SARD applies a scoring system to all selected beneficiaries in order to categories them according to their vulnerabilities. c) PWD friendly tools such as ramps and handrail will be provided, complaint response mechanism (CRM). PWD will be considered by designing tools and conducting focus group discussions FGDsProtection Risk: The high target of the project may lead in some cases to reduce the quality of the service.Likelihood: low, Impact: medium.Mitigation Measures: a) Prioritization of safety and dignity and avoid causing harm: in IDP sites/camps, the program and MEL teams constantly monitors the quality of the assistance delivered, ensuring that only quality activities are provided. b) SARD has a strong CRM in place that is regularly advertised to local communities.Protection Risk: the services provider contracted by SARD, may employ children to work on sites.Likelihood: medium, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: a) SARD’s MEL and Project team conduct spot checks and regular visits to work sites, b) SARD included the PSEA, CSG, and CoC in the signed contract, c) SARD clearly specify the discipline measures in case of any breach.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Due the nature of the project, household visits are required to assess the needs and deliver the services which may expose the most vulnerable individual to the sexual exploitation and abuse.Likelihood: low, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: Through SARD's a) MEL team, and established accountability and complaint mechanism. B) constantly check the efficiency of the reporting pathways; c) SARD run regular refreshing training on PSEA; d) awareness raising activities to the beneficiaries of the program.Risk: SARD works with contractors to deliver services, the latter may abuse their power or violate PSEA, CSG and CoC.Likelihood: medium, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: a) SARD included the PSEA, CSG, and CoC in the signed contract with the contractors, b) SARD clearly specify the discipline measures in case of any breach by the contractors of the policies mentioned above, c) awareness raising for beneficiaries on the mean to set a complaint.Risk: The socio-cultural context in the area may impact the relationship between SARD employees which may results in abuse of power or sexual harassment.Likelihood: low, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: a) SARD value a divers and transparent working environment, therefore, it ensure all staff are aware of the PSEA and CoC; b) review the CoC and PSEA on regular basis and updates them accordingly; c) review the complaint mechanism and ensure all staff are aware of them by running refreshing training on regular basis.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions & RisksAssumptions: - Identification and willingness by targeted individuals to participate in work activities - Other IDPs accepting selected individuals in conducting short-term work without exacerbating possible pre-existent social tensions. - Security situation is stable - Women are included in the activity - Local labor market and local communities will not be affected negatively because of the CFW activity. - Work environment secure against SEA and CSG issues. - Participants are well trained and equipped to perform their tasks appropriately. Risks -Persistency/return to negative coping mechanisms, such as the employment of children, during the short-term work opportunity or at the end of it. Mitigation: the project will purposefully target households at risk of child labor or where child labor cases are present, with vocational training, CfW and business support. Moreover, the child labor cases will benefit from protection support in the context of the project. -Local community discourages women participation in CFW. Mitigation: Selection criteria will be followed and well explained to local community , and the project will enroll women in culturally accepted tasks. Risk: Smaller USD denominations, such as 5, 10, and 20 USD banknotes, are unavailable or are poor quality. Mitigation: SARD will provide the TRY as equivalents of smaller USD denominations when the latter are not available.
