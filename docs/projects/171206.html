Project Summary

The project will be implemented in 7 districts in lower Shebelle and Banadir ( Afgoye, Barawe, Kutuwarey, Marko, Sablaale, Walanweyn and Daynile ) and it will cover the health needs of internally displaced people and host community targeting women, men, boys and girls.
The project will use an integrated approach to offer free curative and preventive primary health care services through the facility and community-based approaches to contribute towards the reduction of maternal and child morbidity and mortality.
This will include screening and registering at the point of entry or triage, outpatient consultations for under 5 children, antenatal and postnatal care for PLW with referring of those with complications to a secondary level which is CEmONCwhile most of such services in Banadir and Afgoye are managed by PAC facilities like Banadir Hospital, Martini, and Afgoye general hospital, Immunization against vaccine-preventable disease for both U5 and PLW.
This project will use an approach of establishing strategically located mother and child health clinics, mobile teams and referral of complicated cases into the secondary level like Banadir, Martini and Afgoye Hospitals for mother and child with complications and sexual assault survivors for further investigation and case management.
Integrated community case management approach will be used to treat U5 boys and girls at the household level.
A total of 411217( 70% of vulnerable people in target districts) persons including 94,580 (70% of WCBA in the target populations) women of childbearing age, 65,795 ( 15% of the total target population) U5, and 16,499 U1 ( 4% of the target population),120 community health workers and 185 staff will be targeted in 10 districts for a period of 12 months.

Project Activities

1. Integrated management of childhood illness through decentralized MCH and Mobile teams for 65,795 under-five children ( 29,937 boys and 36,187 girls). 2. provision of antenatal and postnatal care for 16499 pregnant and lactating women, including micronutrient supplementation and provision of safe delivery kits through decentralized MCH and mobile teams 3. refer all pregnancies with complications (with danger signs of pregnancy), under-five children with complications(with danger signs of U5 children)and sexual assault survivors to district hospitals like Banadir, Martini and Afgoye hospitals.4. provision of immunization services as per IPI calendar for U5 children and PLW attending MCH and mobile outreach sites and through periodic immunization campaigns as per district micro-plans. 5. establishment of district health management committees to oversight, feel ownership and provide support to the project. 6. EWARN system is supported at 14 MCH facilities weekly surveillance data will be submitted to MoH. 7. provide 14 mobile health teams in hard to reach and underserved areas in Lowe Shebelle and Banadir. 8. provide community case management of (iCCM) of common endemic diseases like Malaria, Pneumonia, and Diarrhoea to remote and hard to reach in lower Shebelle. 9. support health strengthens system in all federal states by supporting the HMIS team and district health officers. 10. provide all tools for demand creation at both community and facility levels through behaviour change communications at community networks to sensitize and mobilize individuals, families and communities to adopt targeted health-seeking behaviour which in turn will impact the health outcome for women and U5 girls and boys.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation Measures1. Exclusion and marginalization: this project mainly targets children under five of age and women actively in the reproductive stage regardless of their age. communities in hard to reach areas due to insecurity and poor infrastructure suffer from lack of access to health services. young, mostly first time mothers are likely to lack pre and post natal services which puts them at risk of birth related complications. immunization services are also likely to be difficult to access for these communities which is a risk to PLW and under 5 children to communicable diseases specially VPDs. the mobile teams will ensure that remote but accessible villages are reached with essential health services. this is included engagement of CHWs who represent various clan profiles, who will increase to reach more target communities, moreover, the project will engage with all major groups to ensure that they are aware about availability of the services and their entitlements. all male care givers have specific considerations in this project in order not to miss the services. data collection and analysis designed in a way that allows PAC to better understand age and gender needs to ensure inclusiveness. 2. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: the main target population for this project are IDPs and surrounding host communities. displaced populations, youth, women, and the elderly are the most notably in caps who lack of health services. in addition, poor access and long distance to these services expose them, especially women and young girls to SGBV.. access to basic services and opportunities is further compounded by the pervasive marginalization and exclusion of IDPs and certain sections of the host community families from weaker clan or minorities face extra vulnerabilities and protection threats including economic exploitation, trafficking and discrimination. to mitigate against risks identified, PAC will ensure protection sensitive data collection and analysis to identify the specific protection challenges of target population at implementation stage of the project cycle. selection of the project locations and beneficiaries will be guided by the need with specific attention to house holds that are marginalized because of family or clan related factors. at engagement level, all community groups will be represented at target locations. all PAC staff are already trained on PSEA and have signed core of conduct. this project will employ approaches that reduces distances to the service delivery which includes use of mobile teams that will visit villages where the most vulnerability is existed. at inception phase of the project, proper consultation will be done with all key stakeholders in the target districts to ensure that those who benefit from these interventions are not put at risk following the principle of do not harm. 3. Armed conflict and violence: PAC works in areas where there is active conflict between state and not-state armed actors or between clans. the key risks include service delivery being misappropriated by different sides to the conflict against the others, aid services being diverted to the most powerful groups, attack on beneficiaries on their way to service delivery facilities. PAC local team and well trained and experienced in handling sensitive community issues. PAC will implement a neutral as part of humanitarian principles. security SoPs will be implemented for logistical movement of both staff and supplies at field level
