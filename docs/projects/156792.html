Project Summary

The aim of project is to contribute to ensuring food security in Syria through the implementation of multiple actions that are needed to adequately address access, stable availability and utilization of food. Emergency response support will be provided to respond to the needs on newly arrived returnees (from inside and outside Syria) and newly accessible communities. At the same time, food and lifesaving emergency agricultural assistance will target the most vulnerable population to support them to meet their food and nutritional needs. Finally, the project will provide agro livelihood support to increase self resilience, strengthen resilience and reduce the dependency on aid of the most vulnerable Syrian population.
Lifesaving and life sustaining interventions will contribute to the achievement of the Sector Objective 1”Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life sustaining food assistance” through provision of short term food assistance, monthly food assistance and bread. Resilience building will be supported through distribution of agro inputs (seeds, fertilizer, irrigation kits for instance), provision of Protection Food rations with agro kits, training on farming techniques, provision of poultry kits (including fodder, vet drugs and assistance) and it will contribute to the Sector Objective 2 “Support self reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives”
Activities will be implemented to support the most vulnerable families across the districts of Dara’a, As-Sweida Governorates and in the districts of Rural Damascus and Duma of Rural Damascus Governorate and will reach a total of 2,250 HHs with short term food assistance response for a period of at least one month. Subsequently to emergency rations and based on their assessed food insecurity, IDPs and returnees HHs, along with food insecure or at risk of food insecurity families among the host communities will be supported with monthly food assistance for a period of at least 8 months, reaching a total of 3,175 HHs. To decrease the dependency on food assistance and to promote the self resilience and dignity of beneficiaries, the action will provide agro inputs (seeds and poultry) for family consumption to a total of 2,680 HHs, out of the 3,175 HHs. The livelihood activities will only target host community and returnees.
In the selection of beneficiaries specific focus will be given to IDPs and returnees, but also to those families who have no or limited financial ability to access to food, such as female, elderly and People with Disabilities headed Households and unaccompanied children and to those groups who have only limited access to income to cover food family needs for long period and are forced to adopt negative copying mechanisms (unskilled or seasonal workers for instance).
During the preparation and implementation of the activities, COOPI will ensure the coordination with local authorities and the participation of the communities, also thanks to the constant field presence ensured by the establishment of community kitchens and protection centres in project areas. Coordination will be ensured with the FSA Sector and its members but also with international and national actors active in the same villages in order to implement harmonized response, avoid overlapping and to promote synergies. Synergies will be also promoted by COOPI with other projects submitted to HRP 2019 in the governorates of this Action, such as Rural Damascus, Dara’a and As Sweida to promote an integrated approach to Education and Protection needs within the local communities

Needs

After almost 8 years of ongoing crisis, 6,5 million of people are reported to be food insecure and 2,5 million are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity inside Syria. Even if the number of People in Need is decreased from 37% to 33%, if compared to HNO 2017, still around 9 million individuals are in need of support to meet their food basic needs or to avoid to fall into the food insecurity. Prolonged displacement, limited economical and physical access to food, high prices and inflation, loss and lack of income generation opportunities and production capacity, deterioration of livelihood sources are affecting the access to food of the population, and particularly of the most vulnerable ones. Almost 40% of population has been reported to be displaced or returnees, and among them more than 70% has been displaced for more than one year. Returnees and IDPs are at greater degree vulnerability, incidence of food insecurity is high among these groups reaching an average of 33, 4% among IDPs and 41.2% within returnees. Unemployment has generally risen up to 55% and purchasing power has declined to up 1/10 if compared to the level of 2010. 83-89% (ESCWA) of the population is considered to live below the poverty line, and even there is a portion of poor population who can rely on income, such as unskilled workers or public service employees, these have to spend up to 80% of their monthly salaries to purchase the equivalent of a food ratio with staples. Although food items availability in the local markets is improved, vulnerable families, particularly IDPs, returnees, female headed and rural households, cannot often afford the costs to purchase food and commonly buy it on credit while most of food insecure HHs are forced to adopt crisis and emergency negative copying strategy. At greater degree of vulnerability there are also households who are headed by Person with Disabilities and elderly people due to their limited capacity to earn their living and afford their basic needs. The ongoing crisis along with the weather conditions have also negatively affected the agro production and farmers and breeders’ population. Drought, lack of quality inputs, damaged infrastructure (mainly irrigation) and high production costs have limited the production and availability of food, particularly of wheat and barley. 600,000 farmers HHs lack of agricultural inputs (seeds, fertilizer, irrigation kits) to support their own production (MAAR). High costs of fodder, lack of veterinary service and reduction of livestock numbers have negatively impacted the Livestock production and the availability of their products in the local markets. There is an overall urgent need to provide multiple actions to support with food and livelihood assistance the food insecure population and those to risk in order to alleviate the suffering of crisis affected people in Syria. According to NHO 2019 PiN Severity FAS in fact 45,5% of population in Syria is food insecure or at risk. In the project areas of Rural Damascus Governorate districts of Duma and Rural Damascus count still 64% and 43% respectively of population who is food insecure. In the Governorates of As-Sweida the average of food insecure population is around 45,5% with a peak in Salkhad district with 70% of food insecure households. In Dara’a districts the incidence of food insecurity is around 39%, considerably higher than the percentage at national level, that is around 32,5%.

Implementation of activities

The proposed action will provide emergency response through Food Baskets or Hot Meals for at least 2100 Kcal/person/day for a period of at least 1 month to ensure immediate access to life saving food to respond to the needs on newly arrived returnees (from inside and outside Syria) and newly accessible communities in the project areas. RTE rations or cooked meals will be provided for a longer period than one month if regular food assistance is not available. In case of sudden onset of population movements out of the areas of this project, for instance in Aleppo Governorate where COOPI is present with Protection activities and where IDPs could move in from NW and NE Syria, the project will support the new IDPs with Emergency food assistance, in coordination with the Sector and FS actors.Along with the emergency assistance, monthly food distribution will be provided in the form of Food Baskets or Hot Meals and bread only to food insecure or moderately food insecure households for a period of at least 8 months to ensure regular access to life saving and sustainable food rations However, the moderately food insecure Households will receive a minimum of 1500 Kcal/person/day; while severely food insecure and highly vulnerable Households will receive 2,100 kcal/person/day. The latter caseload will particularly include families with limited or no access to income, such as female, PwD or elderly headed households or big families among IDPs, returnees and host communities. For the provision of cooked meal in case of response with emergency assistance or monthly food ration, the present project is planning to rehabilitate community kitchens in the project areas to support directly beneficiaries nearby the kitchens. According to information collected by COOPI in the current areas of intervention, IDPs and returnees as well as poor and vulnerable families reported the they do not have cooking facilities or gas available to cook their meals. Community kitchen are also a tool to directly engage the communities with project activities and allow to establish a daily presence in the locations supported with the action.Appropriate agro inputs (seeds, fertilizer, irrigation kits but also poultry kits with chicken, vet drugs and assistance and fodder) will be provided to food insecure and very vulnerable returnee and Host community farming households to protect and restore their livelihoods and to allow them decrease gradually their dependency on food aid. Food insecure families will be supported with both protection food ratio and agro kits to allow them to protect their assets until the harvesting period. Before starting the distributions, a new round of assessments and beneficiaries selection cross check will be conducted in coordination with SARC. FGDs with target groups divided by gender and age Distribution of inputs and food assistance will be provided through distribution points in each village, beneficiaries will be informed in advance about the typology of items provided, timing and date of the distribution. Dedicated line will made available at distribution points for people with disabilities, female and elderly people. In each distribution point a complain mechanism will be in place to allow community to contact COOPI staff for any complain or suggestion. All the items provided to the targeted beneficiaries will be procured in the local market to support the local economy. COOPI will be present at the distribution to ensure the correct provision of aid to the selected households and COOPI M&E staff will monitor and evaluate the impact of the action during the project implementation. Before the distribution of agro kits field visits will be carried out to ensure the beneficiaries have access to not contaminated land and to water for irrigation and are willing and capable to participate to the this project activity. The project will provide also technical farming training for beneficiaries to ensure they will adopt proper techniques

Protection Risk Analysis

Key protection risks staff and beneficiaries could be exposed during the implementation of food assistance distribution are mainly related to: sex exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers male dominated, gate keepers, and potential other beneficiaries; violence from beneficiaries against aid workers or among themselves; interference in the beneficiaries targeting; beneficiaries list not updated by local authorities and aid diversion. To mitigate these risks, the project foresees the following measures: establishment of feedback mechanism; balanced staff between men and women and provision of training to them; training and follow up on code of conduct; well organised distributions; training to staff on humanitarian principles and targeting modalities and criteria; periodical verification of list of beneficiaries before and during the project implementation; FGDs before, during and after the distribution.Protection risks related to provision of agricultural inputs could be: tension among the community due to the beneficiaries selection and diversion of input/assets. Key mitigation measures that will be adopted are: community feedback mechanism, hotline, integration of food assistance and livelihood support; periodical monitoring and FGDs before, during and after the distribution. Potential protection risks may also result from the targeting specific groups, such as IDPs or returnees, among the host communities with food or livelihood assistance. In this case, inclusion and exclusion errors, which could lead to tension but also to sexual exploitation of IDPs/retunes females will be mitigated though a community feedback mechanism, hotline and also by providing effective communication to the communities on the criteria and beneficiaries selection and by engaging with local authorities and communities. Moreover, before the distribution of agro inputs, project staff will check with local authorities to verify the status and ownership of the land to mitigate the risk to secondary or no rights holders.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In case of direct access to the target areas and beneficiaries is not permitted or becomes unsafe, COOPI will maintain constant communication flows with agencies, government and in-country security monitoring networks including SARC’s. The presence of UXOs may hamper access to agricultural fields and rehabilitation of food production in this case COOPI will organise awareness raising sessions for staff, volunteers and communities in UXOs recognition and will coordinate with SARC for information on progress of UXOs clearance operations and obtain clearance over agricultural fields prior distribution of kits and repair of irrigation networks.
