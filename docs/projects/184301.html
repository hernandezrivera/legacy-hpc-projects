Project Summary

The impact of the ongoing flooding compounded by economic and social impacts of COVID-19, escalated conflict, and forecast of below average rainfall from May to November 2021 may result in further deterioration of humanitarian outcomes for affected communities through 2022. In a context like Somalia where teachers are under qualified, classrooms are overcrowded, and materials and facilities are scarce, teachers are often using verbal or physical punishment of children to maintain control of the classroom. This behavior affects children negatively and hampers their learning outcome and well-being. Additionally, barriers to access to education for children with disabilities are considerable. The low enrolment of children living with disabilities can be explained negative attitude towards children with disabilities and unavailability of favorable learning environment compounded by untrained teachers on inclusive education. Schooling provides essential learning and when schools close, children and youth are deprived opportunities for growth, development and cognitive. Those who are greatly affected include girls, IDPs and Rural children who tend to have fewer educational opportunities beyond school. According to JMCNA findings the regions with the highest proportion of school-aged children in need of humanitarian assistance are in Southern and Northeastern areas. In Bari region, 63% of school-aged children in Qardo, 62% in Qandala and 61% Calula. Despite the advantages female education brings (increased economic productivity and increased awareness of rights) girls face a unique set of barriers that prevents them from going to school, including many cultural and economic hurdles, some of the reasons cited by Somali parents and teachers for gender disparities in education are: the inadequate number of female teachers, lack of gender-segregated latrines, and inaccessible schools amidst conflict and heightened insecurity. The project will, through its community sensitization activities, will specifically promote the benefits and value of educating girls, to boost the enrollment and retention of girls in school.
TASS proposes to address this gap through an integrated approach by ensuring that 18,000 (9,450 boys and 8,550 girls) school-children and particularly girl child and children with disabilities are able to access quality education in schools through the provision of inclusive basic education package in a protective learning environment combined with life-saving activities such as: Rehabilitation of 25 Classrooms, Provision of protection activities as per the Integrated Education-Child Protection Response Framework including establishing community dialogues in both IDPs and host community settlements to raise awareness on mitigating risks and all kinds of child abuse practices to enhance children’s access and retention in education, Provision of emergency teaching and learning materials to 18,000 (9,450 boys and 8,550 girls) Provision of teachers incentives -240 (40% female), Provision of Clean drinking water- 18,000, Provision of school feeding- 18,000 (9,450 boys and 8,550 girls), Provision of Teacher training- 240 (40% Female), CECs on school management, child protection and school safety and Provide training to Education Cluster partners and MoE staff (10M and 10F) on emergency preparedness and response planning and coordination, additionally TASS in Collaboration with MOE department of special needs will coordinate with identification of needs specific to boys, girls particularly children with disability and children from IDPs in ensuring better protective environment to all learners. The project will be implemented in 7 Districts in bari and Sool regions including Bari (Bossaso, Caluula, Iskushuban, Qandala, Qardho) and Sool region (Taleex and Xudun) targeting 18,000 (9,450 boys and 8,550 girls) including Children with disability, IDPs and Vulnerable host communities specially in hard-to-reach areas.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

To provide holistic support for the learners, the project will be mainstreamed with the protection projects implemented by TASS and other partners in the targeted regions. The project will renovate learning spaces and Latrines into Gender/Disability friendly through creating rams to enable them access both classrooms and latrines in order to provide adequate protection and safety in Schools. TASS in collaboration with the MOEHE Puntland and the community will conduct regular sensitization on the abolish of child negative disciplinary methods including child beating. All teachers will be trained on alternative and positive disciplinary approaches. In addition, teachers, CEC members and children (child clubs) will be trained on child protection and referral mechanisms. All teachers will be trained in the provision of psychosocial and psychological first aid support. Proper feedback mechanisms will be set in place to continuously improve on the quality of programming and ensure that TASS is responsive to the community needs. Additionally, TASS will establish complaints and feedback boxes in all schools to ensure feedback and complains from the children are captured hence Complaints Response and Feedback mechanism with a hotline is already available for use. The use of this system will be enhanced by making beneficiaries more aware of its existence in the proposed project locations.
