Project Summary

This project aims to provide protection support for extremely vulnerable IDPs and host communities in North Darfur, White Nile, River Nile and Khartoum. The project will be implemented through an integrated approach through strengthening community based prevention mechanisms and improving protection response systems. Currently, NADA has established and trained 10 community based protection network (CBPNs). Under this project, 10 CBPNs and 10 complain and response committees will be established and trained. NADA plans to construct 6 multipurpose community /women centers (W/CC) while rehabilitate 6 operation the W/CC. Women and girls will enjoy a safe space which will serve as entry points for GBV activities including identification of GBV survivors, disseminate GBV information, provide PSS including counseling and recreational activities. In consultation and collaboration with NADA social workers, CBPNs members will conduct regular PSS sessions among targeted communities through individual and group sessions, using survivor to survivor and child to child approaches. Enhancement of community capacity on protection will improve the community coping mechanism and wellbeing. Project staff will be provide by 2 days workshop on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), code of conduct, protection monitoring and reporting.
NADA has already set platform for identification of PSNs through training of the CBPNs and social workers on the conducting vulnerability assessment for persons with special needs (PSN).
The project will strengthen referral mechanism by identifying 2000 PSNs. The intervention will assist 1000 individuals among PSN through provision of direct assistance including case management, tangible support, based on actual needs, for identified GBV survivors. Project team will organize monthly meetings with relevant partners and services providers to ensure better access of the PSNs including GBV survivors. Provide psychic social support (PSS) for 8,000 individuals including counseling and recreational activities. Recreation activates which will be conducted by professional experts are considered as methodology for concealing and means to improve psychological ell being. In consultation and collaboration with Nada social workers and CBPNS members will conduct regular PSS session among targeted community’s through individuals and group session survivors to survivors, child to child approaches 5000 women and girls will receive direct support through provision of personal dignity Kits (PDKs). To enhance the community capacity and ensure wider dissemination of protection, GBV and CP information, the project will conduct 28 community based psycho-social support campaigns which will target a total number of 29,000 community members in project locations with protection, CP and GBV prevention and response, awareness messages on PSEA as well as messages on disabilities, impact of COVID on protection, environmental concern, risks and prevention methods.. This will allow NADA team to conduct structured awareness raising sessions within the campaigns as well as addressing some PSS community needs. Sharing of information and wider dissemination of protection, CP and GBV messages will be ensured through distribution of IEC materials which contained clear messages on PSS.

1 - Needs

This project aims to provide protection support for extremely vulnerable IDPs and host communities in White Nile, River Nile and Khartoum. In White Nile, the project will focus on IDPs in Umrumta locality and surrounding Kosti According to the 4 assessments and interactions carried out by NADA in have revealed that multiplicity of protection , Child Protection(CP) and GBV risks and hazards continue to affect daily lives of IDPs, returnees and hosting communities in proposed project's areas. The focus group discussions during rapid assessments conducted by NADA in the four states during 2021 highlighted that existing community support networks and coping mechanism needs additional capacity to respond to the community protection . NADA has identified the need for strengthening the capacity of targeted community including CBPNs, community volunteers, local authorities, community leaders to be able to roll out planned project activities. NADA assessments have revealed that, elderly, children especially unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), single female household and people with disability (PWDs) are considered among the most vulnerable groups as they are socially excluded and face major difficulties in accessing humanitarian services. There are no any specialized support mechanisms or social networks for supporting elderly and disability in project areas. Thus, they are left with very limited support. The assessment has showed that the elderly and PWDs represent at least 10% of the total population among IDPs. Meanwhile, sexual GBV, domestic violence and prevalent cultural barriers limit access of women and girls to education, life-skill training & livelihood opportunities. Early marriage and FGM/C are widely practiced in River Nile, White Nile and North Darfur. A total number of 32 out of 40 women & girls respondents mentioned that they have been exposed to some forms of GBV. More than 50% of GBV survivors are children. However, only 5% of incidents have been reported due to the stigma and social norms. The problems faced by victims of GBV are exacerbated for women living in IDPs gatherings, remote areas in Khartoum as they do not have access to various forms of assistance. Services for GBV survivors are extremely limited in these settings. In addition, a significant number of survivors do not seek GBV support where services exist due to cultural and social stigma, and fear of retaliation. Other major concerns are the risk of child soldier recruitment, especially in some areas in North Darfur such as Sortony and Kutum and child labor, sex slavery in Khartoum. In some cases, vulnerable families consent or even encourage child soldier recruitment as it offers them some degree of financial stability. CP needs are critical and the area is underserved. Children are exposed to different forms of abuse, negligence and violations as the militia forces are actively campaigning for child recruitment among the rebel forces in the project areas. In addition, lack of sustainable income and jobs pushes many children to work in markets, sell various commodities in the local markets or work in farms. This present significant protection risks for children, especially girls. NADA assessment reports estimate that (61%) of children in Sortony and Kutum are considered child labors. This becomes even more significant in the context of broken traditional community-based value and protection systems, which leaves minors unprotected & extremely vulnerable when they are exposed to abuse. Additional child abuse occurs when parents send their children for labor, causing disruption of their schooling. Displacement has also left considerable number of UASC unverified. There are more thousands of traumatized and stressed women and children left without psycho-social support or recreation spaces. There are no or limited number of operational Child friendly space or women or community centers in the targeted localities.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

NADA recruited and will continue to recruit project staff from targeted communities, staff will be involved and fully dedicated to implement and follow up project activities. Project staff will be provided by two days training on PSEA, GBV, protection monitoring and reporting ,code of conduct to avoid potential risks of violence, exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries. The integrated GBV, protection project will adopt community based approach that promotes active engagement and participation of all community segments in design and implementation phases to ensure community by-in and sense of ownership. NADA team confirmed that composition of CBPNs considers balanced representation of gender, age, social status and minority to improve t decision making and allow the voices for these groups. CBPNs already received training on different, GBV and protection risks, needs and concerns to be able them to contribute to sustainability of protection education and capacity among local community. This will further support self-protection capacity to be embedded deeply in the community coping mechanisms. Following participatory and community based approaches, targeted communities will be involved in discussion, consultations and decision regarding planned activities. Through this, project intends to ensure full participation and strengthen the ownership of communities. This will contribute to provide the targeted communities with concrete plate forum to identify their concerns, needs, challenges and needed actions to improve the protective environment. APP is core activity, additionally applying Do No Harm principle to clearly done in this project through involvement and inclusion of the communities and hosting communities with equal access to direct assistance based on vulnerability assessment .As part of the Do No Harm approach, NADA will provide training to all project team members and CBPNs on PSEA and code of conduct. This will help to mitigate risk of harassment, abuse and other violation to be committed by staff members against the beneficiaries. NADA will consider the dignity and confidentiality of beneficiaries, particularly when delivering CP and GBV services in particularly provision of case management. NADA will strongly prioritize the best interest of children/ GBV survivors/beneficiary by following protocols and standards of service delivery for the identified GBV cases especially child survivors. The information about these cases will be handled and managed anonymously and confidentially. Counseling for GBV survivors will be done in a designated private space with maximum confidentiality. In addition, the project team will conduct regular consultation with CBPNs, community leaders/ members and local authorities and receive their feedback about project delivered services through complaint and feedback mechanism to identify any negative consequences that might be cause as result of the project implementation.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Project will be implemented on participatory and community based approaches . Regular meetings and information sharing activities will be delivered to community to inform about project services. Targeted communities will continue to have equal access, participate and benefit from project services. Project will highly consider best interest of women and children when handling, sharing data and information about identified cases. Consultations with local community and line ministries will be integral part of this intervention. Project will hold consultations for full engagement of partners as an entry point for smooth implementation with clear exit strategy. Protection interventions have always been sensitive issue for authorities and communities. NADA has incorporated lessons learn from previous intervention in relation to AAP. it has been identified that extensive consultation with local authority and community as is instrumental for smooth and effective project implementation. This is considered as an area of strength for NADA, as all designed intervention have focused on this element as success factor for achieving intended project objectives. Project will also ensure constant feedback and open channels for receiving complaints. NADA will train 10 CRCs whom will receive and respond to complaints and feedback including SEA and abuse complaints .A simple written procedure will be developed, this will be coupled with a complaint box to enable submission of private, confidential or sensitive complaints. A specific tool (Beneficiary Satisfaction Form) has been designed for this purpose. Process, time for response etc should be clearly communicated to beneficiaries. Multi purpose community / Women center and NADA offices are equipped with complain box. Training of CBPNs on protection and GBV , PSEA and how to report its cases will highly contributed to APP. Community based PSS sessions will be conducted by committees and community members. This will ensure that beneficiaries’ opinion are incorporated in future intervention. Information sharing will be articulated in different ways such as readable, watchable IEC material .Awareness raising messages will be disseminated in accessible ways for PWDs. Message will be field tested among targeted groups prior to printing Project will apply Do No Harm approach through considering these sensitivities for successful project implementation without making any harm on communities. To achieve that, NADA has conducted analysis to identify any possible harm that to be caused by the intervention. The project team understands that many communities in the project areas are diversified and explored ways to unite the community; one of the ways is to promote participation of people from various ethnic backgrounds together as members of community networks, with aim to increase coherence and connectedness.
