Project Summary

This projects aims to reach 40,155 beneficiaries, 20,238 male and 19,917 female in 6 Counties of Juba, Bor South, Panyikan, Magwi, Baliet and Ikwoto in 2019. Health Link intents to achieve 20.9% of the Cluster target in the selected Counties (Bor South, Panyikan, Magwi, Ikwoto and Baliet and Juba Counties. In line with the Cluster Objectives, Health Link will implement specific activities under the service delivery areas below;
1. Improving Access and Scale -Up of Responsiveness
- HLSS will set up 4 Mobile Clinics that will be manage by 4 Mobile Teams– 1 in Juba, 1 in Bor South, 1 in Panyikan and 1 in Magwi. Each team will conduct integrated mobile outreach at least once every week.
- Health Link intents to Support 4 Primary Health Care Centres. 2 in Bor South (Bor PoC and Werkok PHCC), 1 in Juba (Rokon PHCC) and 1 in Magwi (Pajok PHCC). 8 PHCUs across the selected Counties and 2 Hospitals, Bor State Hospital and Al-Sabbah Children Hospital.
- Participate in 3 RRMs along with the HC Partners
2. Prevent, detect and response to epidemic prone diseases
- Health Link will support Weekly Reporting through IDSR for all the supported health facilities and EWARS for IDP Sites.
- Health Link has emergency team that will ensure Preparedness and Response to any outbreaks within the Country. Potential diseases of importance include Malaria, Measles, Cholera, Meningitis and Hepatitis E among others.
- With looming threats of EVD, Health Link with SMoH will enhance surveillance in 3 Counties of Juba, Magwi and Ikwoto considering potential entry points.
- Integration of WASH at all health care facilities will be central to the implementation of this project. WASH will be integrated at 4 PHCCs, 8 PHCUs and 2 Hospitals.
3. Improve Resilience among vulnerable population
- Health Link will conduct 1 public awareness creation/campaigns on Mental Health and Psychosocial Services per County per quarter. 6 Awareness Creation will be conducted every quarter.
- Daily awareness creation at 4 PHCC, 8 PHCUs and 1 Hospital will be conducted to increase demand for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services.
- Health Link intents to Map out disabled and vulnerable groups through facility based and mobile clinic services and share this information with Mental Health/Disability working groups to inform decision making at technical working group.
4. Integrated response for health/WASH/Nutrition/Food Security and Protection
- Health Link will manage SAM with Medical Complications at Bor PoC and Al-Sabbah Children Hospital Stabilization Centres (SC) and also conduct appropriate referrals to SC in other project locations
- Ensure adequate preparedness and response to potential outbreaks through an integrated approach involving WASH component

Needs

Accessibility to health care has remained a big challenged in the selected counties especially in Upper Nile and Jonglei States where only 211 health facilities out of 318 are functional (RRHP 2018). While there are no updated data, overall about 56% of the population in the country have no access to health care (SSHHS 2008). Displaced populations suffer more constraints in accessing lifesaving health care, with children under 5 most vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases as well as women of reproductive age facing high health risks of unskilled care during pregnancy and child births. Immunization coverage over 2 years (2017 & 2018) for all vaccine preventable diseases have persistently remained below 50%. This has created a big gap of 42% of children under 1 year at risk of measles (WHO EPI Report Sept 2018). Recurrent outbreaks, and or upsurge of epidemic prone diseases including acute watery diarrhoea/cholera, measles, malaria, meningitis and hepatitis E compounded by food insecurity presents increased susceptibility to morbidity and mortality especially to pregnant mothers and children under 5 years of age. Over stretching the health care system is the current looming threats of importation of viral heamorrhagic fever (Ebola Viral Disease) in the Country. With the ongoing peace process the anticipated returnees would even pose greater risk to the importation of EVD. This necessitates for early and adequate preparedness and response to potential outbreaks.Cross cutting issues like HIV/AIDS, TB, Mental health, disabilities and non-communicable diseases including lifesaving trauma care are being cut-off from life-saving services. This has been related to contextual disruption of services and unpredictable health care funding in conflicted affected locations with increasing IDP burden.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The project will provide plat forms for meetings with community leaders to identify and collective address bottlenecks. Weekly/Monthly reports will also be shared with participating health facility management committees, Boma Health Teams, and County Health Departments.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

General messages to the stake holders on the scope of this project and intended results will be given at State and County Levels. Specific Risk Communication will be adopted depending on the prevailing context. For example Cholera prevention messages, translated in different languages based on the beneficiary language to ensure adequate community level preparedness towards prevention. Attention will be paid to ensure Ebola prevention messages are incorporated in the daily health education packages at health facilities and also through the mobile clinic teams.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The implementation of this project will take in to account the protection of beneficiaries and Health Link Employees from sexual exploitation and abuses. Health Link has a clear PSEA policy within the human resource policy that provides for Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuses at all levels of service delivery. Over the project period, Health Link will take stringent measures to ensure PSEA policy is well adhered to.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Health Link provides feedback to beneficiaries through weekly and monthly meetings, share weekly and monthly reports with the authorities at community level whereas dissatisfaction by the beneficiaries are channeled through the County Health Department to the SMoH for appropriate corrective measures.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project implementation will ensure the community structures such as the Boma Health Teams, Health Unit Management Committee, Payam Health Administration, and County Health Departments have monthly meetings to reflect on project performance. These meetings will involve technical team from Health Link who will provide the members with key project activities done and those not done over a period of one month. Corrective measures are then agreed upon to ensure the project meets the intended results.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring team will conduct monthly monitoring to project sites to review data collection tools on curative consultations (OPD Registers), Admissions (IPD Registers), EPI Registers, ANC and Maternity Registers and all other primary data collection tools. Conduct meetings with beneficiaries in the project areas (Focus Group Discussions) and utilize all the findings from data reviews and focus group discussions to collectively make technical and expertise judgement of access to services by the beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

To ensure safety and quality of services to the beneficiaries, the project monitoring team will conduct personnel knowledge assessment at services delivery points, for example review of prescription notes and also ensure the availability and appropriate use of medicines, medical products and equipment. Findings from such assessment will inform collective decision on safety of services to the beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring will also focus on the process of triaging at health facilities, how patients/beneficiaries are attended to, without discrimination by gender/age/origin while ensuring zero tolerance to all forms of abuses, and attending to beneficiaries while considering privacy and confidentiality required for specific cases, for example survivors of SGBV. Attention will be paid to ascertain a clear referral pathways for survivors of SGBV and access to Services such as CMR and Psychosocial support.
