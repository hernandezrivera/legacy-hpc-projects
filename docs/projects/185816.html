Project Summary

Through the Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) programme and Site Maintenance Engineering Project (SMEP), WFP will work with Rohingya refugees and host communities to prevent and reduce risks from natural disasters, enhance accessibility and strengthen resilience. The project will provide targeted assistance for Rohingya refugees and enhance food security in host communities through asset creation (including Disaster Risk Reduction) by building and rehabilitating assets that will create a healthier natural environment, reduce the risks and impact of climate shocks and strengthen resilience to natural disasters over time. Such activities will ultimately address immediate SD needs through cash transfer modality. Also ensuring inclusive approach integrated with gender equality, women’s empowerment and protection principles including strong coordination at the camp-level (with SD focals and SMS) and to coordination at Cox’s Bazar with sector.

1 - Identified Needs

The arrival of almost 1 million refugees within a very short period of time in August 2017 has continued to place considerable pressure on an already impoverished local population while exacerbating poor living conditions of the refugees, especially those in the mega-camp. Due to large-scale clearing of trees and plants for settlement purposes, the entire area is continued to be susceptible to land degradation and exposed to eminent disasters such as landslides, mudslides and flash floods during the monsoon and cyclone seasons. Likewise, livelihoods opportunities in the camps are still extremely limited therefore a large number of dis-engaged youths are at greater risk of being radicalized or engaged in illegal activities. Ensuring community-led, sustainable and well-coordinated disaster risk reduction and alternative livelihood interventions is therefore crucial to ensure the continuation of resilience of both the refugee and host communities through the following two interventions: (1) Asset Creation (including Disaster Risk Reduction) – Cash-for-work (CFW)WFP's key activity is to implement disaster mitigation activities and alternative livelihoods through asset creation whereby refugees and host communities are engaged in community-based, labour intensive activities in exchange for cash. The proposed activities will (i) improve the safety and living conditions of the refugees through community-based public works, community services and disaster preparedness; (ii) engage youth-at-risk by employing them in productive activities hence preventing the potential for engagement in radicalization, drug abuse and trafficking; and (iii) reduce and reverse the ongoing environmental degradation through reforestation. The cash transfer amount will be in line with Transfers Working Group guidelines. (2) Site Maintenance Engineering Project (SMEP) In response to validation requirements by the SMEP Technical Working Group, SMEP “Heavy Engineering” will adopt a holistic approach for infrastructure construction, improvements and innovation with cost efficiency and environmentally friendly solutions. SMEP will coordinate closely with Site Management Site Development (SMSD) Sector, the Office of the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC), Camp-in-Charge (CiC), Shelter, Water Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) and other stakeholders. It should be noted that WFP’s asset creation and SMEP provides support in response to the requirements identified and validated through various competent authorities (e.g. SMEP Technical Working Group for SMEP which is comprised of WFP, IOM, and UNHCR), therefore it is not possible to provide breakdowns. The operational area of responsibility of the WFP for Asset creation/DRR and SMEP encompasses the entire refugee population. However, which specific subgroups of that population will end up being targeted, and their location/demographic attributes, is only known once a requirement is identified, validated and selected to include in the SMEP and asset creation workplan. The DRR and SMEP workforces engage vulnerable populations who have been particularly affected by the economic consequences of the COVID-19 pandemic. All project activities will integrate gender equality, women’s empowerment and protection principles. These elements will be mainstreamed throughout the project design, implementation as well as through its monitoring and evaluation. Concretely, WFP will ensure the maximum participation of women in CFW activities by ensuring an enabling environment for them (e.g. arranging childcare). In addition, WFP and its Cooperating Partners will strive to ensure that the projects are inclusive and cognizant of the needs of people with disability (for example, by ensuring that the outputs of projects such as evacuation shelters take into account their particular needs).

2 - Activities or outputs

1) DRR - Enhance accessibility, including renovation of bridges, footbridges, roads, footpaths, steps and stairs, handrails, flags and signs. 2) DRR - Community-based environmental protection, including promoting reforestation and restoration of the damaged environment, camp clean-up activities etc. including mitigate risks through slope stabilization, including terracing and perimeter fencing. 3) DRR - Construction/rehabilitation of drainage, including the rehabilitation of dams, cleaning drainage canals etc. 4) SMEP - Enhance accessibility: build new roads and bridges to connect camps and improve access around host communities. 5) SMEP - Maintain well-functioning drainage systems: carry out canal and drainage clearance work within the camps and ensures repair and maintenance related activities. 6) SMEP - Prevent landslide and soil erosion through land and slope stabilization work as well as the design and construction of riverbank protection works. 7) SMEP - Emergency Response and Cyclone Preparedness: ensure rapid response, including provision of fuel machinery and materials during monsoon and cyclone seasons. 8) SMEP - Strengthen the capacity of volunteers, men, & women through hands-on training within construction field.
