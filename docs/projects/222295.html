Project Summary

Safe and Protective Education in South Sudan is focusing on improving access to and quality of safe and protective education services for 18,462 emergency- affected IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable host community children in Lakes, Unity and Western Equatoria states. In response to the drastic lack of sufficient education across these states, our project will focus on ensuring that all children, including children living with disabilities, have a safe place to learn, access to proper sanitation and hygiene, and increased overall wellbeing. AET will work with local teachers and train them on education in emergencies pedagogical skills and child-centered methodologies, psychosocial support, and wider school governance. In school support will extend to school management committees (SMC) members, Parent Teacher Associations (PTA), and education authorities on school management and governance along with climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction. We will leverage our 25-year history in-country, our localised approach to strengthen community buy-in and ownership of the project, as well as our vast education and protection expertise.
The intervention would be guided by State Ministries of Education and the community through engagement and participation and would seek to build on already developed programmes and materials and infuse AET experience and expertise in rolling out programmes that would be of quality and bene fit to the marginalized learners in the target regions. AET will seek to build partnerships with other implementing partners and EiE cluster members working in the target regions to ensure project activities are not duplicated and to share lessons learnt during implementation. AET will be able to leverage its learnings, experience and relations from the Girls Education in South Sudan (GESS) and the Global Partnership for Education Learning Spaces project in partnership with UNICEF. Through these programmes AET already has current information on the communities that are in need of TLS’s and WASH facilities and will coordinate activities in line with these programmes. Networking through social media and face to face meetings will also be exploited to maximize efficiency, minimize the risk of duplication and reduce program transaction costs

Implementing Partners

AET has worked in South Sudan for more than 25 years, prioritising marginalised groups - including women and girls, displaced populations, and children living with disabilities. At AET we believe in the power of local organisations and we focus on leveraging local expertise to ensure effective, efficient interventions. This enables sustainable longer-term impact. By working in partnership with local level actors we are able to ensure that our programmes meet the needs of affected populations and that the solutions that we provide are appropriate for the given context. We will therefore implement this programme in partnership with three local organization, namely, Child Destiny and Development Organisation (CHIDDO) who have a strong existing presence in Lakes and Unity State, more than two decades of experience, and strong protection and humanitarian expertise to leverage; and Catholic Diocese of Tombura-Yambio (CDTY) who manage a network of over 50 schools in Western Equatoria State and are the only organization continuing to provide education and protection support in Tombura county

1. Needs

Out of an estimated population of 12.1 million, around 9 million people in South Sudan are currently in need of humanitarian assistance. There are an estimated 2.8 million children out-of-school children across the country and according to the UNESCO Institute for Statistics, South Sudan has the highest rate of primary school-aged children out of school in the world: a staggering 62%. The closure of schools due to Covid-19 lasted 14 months and resulted in an additional nearly 2 million children having their learning severely interrupted. The most marginalized children and youth, including girls and Children with Disabilities (CwDs), face multiple barriers to accessing education—which were only exacerbated by the coronavirus pandemic. The 2021 Education Needs Assessment (ENA) found that young mothers and married learners were the groups least likely to participate in education activities (74% and 72%, respectively), as well as children with disabilities (72%). The lack of enrolment and retention can be attributed to both psychosocial and physical barriers. For instance, traditional gender roles are rigid in South Sudan and practices such as early and forced marriages, coupled with economic pressures (in a country where 92% of the population live in multidimensional poverty) means families de-value the importance of education for their daughters and instead prefer their children to get married to relieve household economic stress through the ‘bride price’ payments. In fact, 52% of South Sudanese girls are married before age 18—which significantly decreases the potential of them finishing school. The expected years of schooling for girls is shockingly low too: just 3.5. If girls do enrol in school, a lack of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) supplies, dignity kits, and gender-sensitive washrooms discourages female learners from continuing to attend especially as they get older. Children living with disabilities also face myriad challenges in accessing and staying in school. Social stigmas and lack of awareness around disability, a lack of teachers trained in working with CWDs, and a lack of adaptive learning materials discourage them from attending. Not to mention the fact that the vast majority of schools are inaccessible. Widespread insecurity is also a significant barrier to education and active conflicts in Lakes, Unity and WES have forced schools to close. The 2021 ENA found that insecurity in and around schools was a significant reason for student absenteeism and drop-out. For those that are in school, poor school infrastructure, insufficient learning resources and a lack of trained teachers are compromising the quality of education. SAMS data shows that the highest proportion of schools (23.1%) have classes under trees/open air/ or a tent structure. These schools are easily damaged by wind and flooding and lessons are frequently cancelled due to rain and poor weather. For this reason school rehabilitation was cited as the priority intervention to re-open non- functional schools in the 2021 ENA. Just 1 in 3 schools reported having access to a functioning safe water source in the 2021 ENA, while a recent assessment of WASH facilities conducted by GESS in 2021 found that of 4,159 schools assessed, 2,478 (60%) have toilets and half of these have separate facilities for girls.

2. Project activities

Objective 1: Emergency-affected children and youth have access to safe, protective, inclusive education services that are appropriate to their age, learning level and need.. Objective 1 activities: 1.1: Construction of temporary learning spaces and classrooms in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards; 1.2: Rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces and classrooms; 1.3 Construction and rehabilitation of gender sensitive WASH facilities in line with MoGEI/Education Cluster standards; 1.4: Conduct community sensitization sessions so the community are sensitized on children's rights and the importance of education for all. Objective 2: Children and youth are provided with quality teaching and learning services that support retention and progress through the education cycle. Objective 2 activities: 2.1: Training of teachers on EiE pedagogical skills and child- centred methodologies; 2.2: Provision of teaching and learning materials for primary, and secondary school education; 2.3: Provision of ECD kits and training in their use; 2.4: Training of teachers and learners on psychosocial support (PSS), child protection (CP), and referral mechanisms including for CP/GBV, health and nutrition Objective 3: Coordination and crisis management capacity is strengthened to promote efficient, accountable, and timely education in emergencies response. Objective 3 activities: 3.1: Train SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR).
