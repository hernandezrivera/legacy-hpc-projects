Project Summary

It is vital that the suitable and timely Sexual and Reproductive Health SRH services are prioritized and made available in immediate as well as protracted crises, it is a mean for preventing morbidity and mortality and protecting the right to life with dignity.
UNFPA jointly with partners, will ensure the provision of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for reproductive health in emergencies and transition to comprehensive SRH services as the situation allows. MISP aims at reducing maternal and newborn mortality and morbidity, prevention and response to sexual violence, prevention of HIV transmission, reduction of HIV morbidity and prevention of unintended pregnancies, and sets the platform for comprehensive SRH services integrated in to primary health as soon as possible.
All intervention of the MISP will be implemented simultaneously through coordinated actions with all relevant partners. In addition, the planned interventions prioritize the timely provision of Emergency Obstetric care and save delivery, support referral system, availability of and accessibility for Family Planning services, Clinical management of SGBV survivor’s, HIV and STIs prevention and response program, as well as timely access to Antenatal and postnatal care, and demand creation through awareness raising and educational activities. Morbidity such as Obstetric fistula will be given an especial attention by UNFPA. The intervention primarily targets women, girls in reproductive age, youth and adolescents, men and boys are also addressed.

1 - Needs

Total people in need of humanitarian assistance and basic service is estimated at 8.6 million in Sudan as per the HRP 2020, of whom 2,064,000 are women at reproductive age. Reference made to MISP calculator 2019; total of 274,039 pregnant women are estimated to be in need for access to antenatal care, support for safe vaginal delivery, and postnatal care services. Around 82210 pregnant women are estimated to either experience obstetric complication or require caesarean section. It is also estimated that total number of 45,408 cases of sexual violence who will seek care during 2020. Sudan has a high maternal mortality rate (295 per 100,000 life birth as per 2017 UN estimates). Reference made to the 2018 Maternal death surveillance report, the major causes of maternal deaths were Obstetric hemorrhage 37.2%, Hypertensive disorders 16.4% and sepsis 12.8% . This report also confirmed the 72.3% of maternal deaths had no ANC before, and 74.6% of maternal deaths were due to limited accessibility (delay at home, or delay in reaching health facility). Based on Mapping and assessment of EmONC services 2018 and the HeRAMS 2018, significant gap in coverage with Basic emergency neonatal and obstetric care services is observed, with less than 37% of the need. States with severe gaps in EmONC availability against the recommended numbers: East Darfur, Gedarif , Kassala , North Kordofan , Blue Nile , Red sea , South Kordofan, North Darfur, Khartoum , Sinnar and Gazira. The health risks associated with pregnancy complications are also increased by the fact that majority of the deliveries occur at home It is reported that only 23% of all expected birth occur in health facilities, lowest observed rates were in East Darfur 1%, South Darfur and Blue Nile 2% each. There is also a gap in provision of CMR services across all Darfur states, where only 14-21% of health facilities provide CMR services. The regular mapping for CMR services confirms the significantly limited availability of services, including trained cadres. despite the frequent trainings and capacity building.

2 - Activities / Outputs

The planned activities include: 1. Support the SRH coordination mechanism at states and national levels to ensure the coordinated provision and expansion of MSP for RH 2. Strengthening national/ states contingency and preparedness planning focusing on Minimum Initial Service Package on RH in Crisis (MISP) and scaling up of MISP. 3. Capacity building of health care providers through formal training, supportive supervision, on-the-job training and mentorship on relevant SRH topics including EMONC, FP, STI management, Clinical Management of Rape survivors and psycho-social support, etc. 4. Support referral system through enhancement of locally initiated system to all emergency obstetric cases. 5. Rehabilitation and equipping of health facilities including delivery rooms, maternity wards,blood transfusion unit and theaters. 6. Support the establishment of Family Planning model centres within selected health facilities inside the camps. 7. Establishment of integrated SRH mobile teams at states with fragile context. 8. Procurement and delivery of emergency reproductive health kits, micronutrients, equipment and supplies. 9. Support Obstetric Fistula treatment in states with high humanitarian burden. 10. Support demand creation and awareness raising on reproductive health seeking behavior.11. Address Youth/ adolescent RH needs focusing on their specific needs and vulnerabilities in humanitarian settings. 12. Conduct evidence based researches and studies on SRH.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

UNFPA aims to make a positive contribution in the area of environmental impact mitigation. It’s work on sexual and reproductive health helps promote resilience among vulnerable populations. Access to voluntary family planning, maternal health care and quality education are key ways to empower vulnerable women and young people, making them better equipped to prepare for and respond to environmental -related crises. At the procurement, level the organization works with suppliers (International and national levels) to implement sound environmental impact provisions. Manufacturers working with UNFPA have active and documented waste-water programmes and have agreed to reduce raw material usage. UNFPA also produced and disseminates a guide for safe disposal and management of unused unwantedcommodities. Throughout the project, planning and implementation process UNFPA closely monitors and set the mitigation measures that needed to avoid, reduce or compensate for environmental impacts to enhance the environmental and community benefits of the proposal.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through all project steps, starting from planning, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation, the following principles will be adhered to1. Avoid causing harm: UNFPA and actors will ensure that service and assistance delivery preserves the physical integrity of individuals and communities, their dignity. These packages have already been adapted to the context of Sudan, and are culturally appropriate and minimizes any harmful and unintended negative consequences.2. Equality: UNFPA and the actors will ensure that assistance and services will be provided according to needs and not any other determinants such as ethnicity or nationality, in good quantity, within safe and easy to reach locations, are known by the affected individuals and are accessible by all various groups including disabled individuals.3. Accountability to beneficiaries: UNFPA and the actors will ensure that the affected individuals and communities are engaged in the measurement of the quality of interventions serving them, including mechanisms for suggestions and complaints from the population.4. Participation and Empowerment: Foster inclusive participation to decision-making processes, support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights and empower themselves

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

UNFPA has zero tolerance for all forms of sexual wrongdoing, whether perpetrated against a recipient of assistance or coworker. UNFPA conducts rigorous pre-employment checks of staff and personnel to prevent the rehiring of known offenders. All UNFPA personnel are required to undergo mandatory online training on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment. UNFPA does not partner with entities that fail to appropriately address sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment. UNFPA investigates all allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse, imposes disciplinary and/or administrative sanctions when allegations are proven, and may, through the UN Office of Legal Affairs, refer matters to national authorities for criminal prosecution. UNFPA Sudan CO is a member in PSEA Network which includes UN agencies, Donors, and International NGOs with possibility of expansion. All UNFPA partners both governmental and none-governmental, including vendors were updated on requirement of compliance to PSEA and agreements were updated accordingly.
