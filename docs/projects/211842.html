Project Summary

In Syria, various factors such as a 2023 earthquake, ongoing conflict, economic decline, power shortages, and climate change have severely hindered access to Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services. Damage to WASH infrastructure, compounded by economic challenges restricts community access to basic WASH services, affecting human health and dignity, particularly for the most vulnerable groups. PiN of WASH services in 2024-25 were estimated at 13.6M, including 5.69M considered critically in need. Among them, 2.9M are women and 3.6M are children. Access to improved water sources stands at only 54% nationwide, with significant differences between regions. Some areas lack sewage systems, posing environmental and health risks. In IDP sites, shared toilets elevate risks, especially for women and girls. Handwashing remains inadequate, with 17% lacking soap and water at facilities.
Children are particularly vulnerable to water-related diseases and malnutrition due to limited hygiene services. Schools and learning spaces are unconducive to learning and facilitating the spread of communicable diseases due to inadequate hygienic practices and lack of quality WASH facilities. Schools face substantial WASH challenges, with around 56% of school children dissatisfied due to inadequate clean water and insufficient toilets. Despite efforts from L/INGOs, many IDPs struggle to access hygiene items and effective handwashing, with consequent increased risk of transmission of communicable diseases. Syrian multiple crises disproportionally affect vulnerable HHs such as FHHs and HHs with PwDs, facing more water scarcity, heightening risks of GBV and hindering hygiene practices. Sanitation hurdles persist, with 29% of the population facing issues accessing functional toilets or proper wastewater disposal.
Against this context, COOPI is proposing a comprehensive WASH intervention as part of the WASH 2024 HRP, aimed at addressing the urgent needs of vulnerable displaced populations and host communities in Syria, with a particular focus on children in schools, in synergies with other interventions ongoing in 2024. Informed by the multi-sectoral assessment realized by COOPI in summer 2023 and in coordination with the other WASH cluster partners, COOPI will build on the experience gathered in 2023 on WASH rehabilitation work as part of the cash for work ILO-funded project, and will take advantage of the ongoing partnership with MoE, MOSAL and SARC.
COOPI will focus on the activities contributing to Objective 1 of the WASH Cluster LF of saving lives and reducing protection risks. Firstly, COOPI will distribute HKs to vulnerable HHs, with priority to high-risk groups such as FHH and HHs with PwDs. Consultation with at-risk groups, as well as coordination with other L/INGOs within the WASH cluster, will inform the procurement of HKs tailored to their specific needs. Additionally, specialized HKs catering to specific demographics such as babies, elderly individuals, and those requiring MHM will be provided. To ensure effective dissemination of hygiene practices, COOPI will capitalize its experience on conducting hygiene awareness sessions at the HH level and within schools by relying on schools FPs and COOPI volunteers. The awareness sessions will be facilitated by gender-balanced teams. Furthermore, COOPI will engage in light rehabilitation and construction of WASH facilities in schools, encompassing HWSs, gender segregated latrines, water tanks, and pipes.
COOPI will focus on 12 communities in Aleppo, Hama and Lattakia governorates in GoS area, where COOPI is already fully operational, has reliable and unfettered access, and a network of skilled staff with knowledge of local context. Moreover, targeting communities where COOPI is active will ensure an integrated multisector approach by creating synergies with ongoing FSA, Protection and Education interventions.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

COOPI will contribute to WASH Cluster objectives for 2024-25 by capitalizing the experience gathered since mid-2017, when COOPI started to provide humanitarian and early recovery programming to populations affected by the conflict. Based in Damascus and operating mainly in GoS, COOPI interventions encompassed protection, FSLA, livelihoods, education and CVA. In the wake of the February 2023 earthquake, COOPI responded providing emergency relief in affected areas through protection services and food assistance in the form of MPCA, in-kind distributions, PFA and PSS activities. COOPI is renowned at the global level for its WASH interventions and has gathered throughout the years a valuable technical expertise across its intervention countries. In 2023 COOPI operated on sewage maintainance as part of ILO-funded post-earthquake cash for work project, but a part for this until 2023 in Syria COOPI gave priority to intervention in other sectors (Protection, FSA-ERL, Education). In 2024 COOPI is going to strengthen its operations in WASH sector in Syria by harnessing its global expertise and through an expansion of its technical dedicated HRs. Being among the first operations in WASH sector, COOPI decided to focus in 2024 on communities where it is already active and proposing activities under Obj 1 such as distribution of HKs, awareness raising and light rehabilitation and construction in schools. COOPI’s clear ambition is to expand the intervention in the next years both geographically and programmatically. COOPI will take advantage of its international experience, its offices in Hama and Aleppo, its network of staffs in Damascus and in the target governorates, and the positive connections both with local communities and the political authorities. Also, COOPI will build upon the experience of a large-scale Education-WASH project currently being implemented in 43 schools of Damascus, aimed to enhance school attendance through the reduction of the spreading of lice infection. COOPI is distributing 24,700 hygiene kits inclusive of anti-lice products, conducting hygiene raising sessions in each class of the target schools, and sharing SBCC materials. This project is included in COOPI Education HRP 2024.
