Project Summary

UNHCR will continue shelter interventions to improve the living conditions of refugees considering Age, Gender, Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM) and individual specific needs to 431, 249 people across all camps in UNHCR's AoR. The project will be based on two sector strategic objectives; 1) to provide life-saving emergency Shelter/NFI support to households affected by disasters and other sudden onset events and 2) to provide safe and decent living conditions to reduce suffering.
Under specific objective 1)
Given the temporary nature of shelter material approved for use, coupled with extreme weather conditions in Bangladesh,
refugee shelters require regular maintenance throughout the year. UNHCR and its partners support refugee households to maintain their shelters with bamboo, tarpaulin, rope and wires. In addition, tie-down kits are considered for emergency distribution to reinforce refugee shelters in anticipation of strong winds during monsoon seasons. UNHCR will continue to preposition contingency stocks and conducts distribution based on needs assessments and validation. Regular NFI assistance including soaps and female hygiene kits will be distributed in coordination with WASH sector partners. The distribution and composition of non-food items (NFIs) will be planned and managed in a functional and effective manner in coordination with the Shelter/NFI Sector. To diversify access to NFIs, UNHCR plans to scale-up voucher distribution mechanisms so that refugees can select the items required. Refugees will be informed regarding the NFI distribution schedule with an appointment slip provided in advance. Distributions will be administered through the Global Distribution Tool (GDT) and manual master rolls. Lessons learned through the Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) and UNHCR's post-distribution monitoring (PDM), will be incorporated into the project and will comprise part of the participatory activity planning.
Under strategic objective 2,
UNHCR plans to conduct a detailed shelter needs assessment in it AoR to inform priorities and targeted response. The key interventions will incudes iter-alia; gender and inclusion activities, (inclusion of marganized groups in CFW activities), LGP distribution and refills, energy efficient cookware and support to extremely vulnerable households. Refugees will have access to cleaner fuel with limited exposure to protection incidents through the provision of LPG. UNHCR will expand user training to ensure that all refugees are trained and provided with a more efficient cooking technology and a cookbook outlining how to use and maintain a new more efficient stove and cooking technologies; refugees will benefit from more diversified cooking practices with the distribution of more efficient cooking technologies
Efforts will be in place to facilitate incremental improvement of shelters through reinforcing existing shelters with improved materials including HH level site improvement works. This will include construction of 400 Mid Term Shelters (MTS), and repairs of MTS in camps 4Ext, 21, 4, 2E and 2W. Shelter repair and maintenance, HLP activities and porter support to EVHHs will also be priortised.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Despite significant progress made by partners in the last years, the urgent need to provide shelter assistance remains, as well as more durable materials to improve the lifespan of the shelters. The ongoing use of temporary shelter materials such as untreated bamboo and tarpaulin requires regular material replacement and shelter reinforcement. A comprehensive shelter assessment will be conducted by UNHCR and partners to provide required baseline information for the shelter implementation to facilitate accurate planning of shelter program for those with limited resources. To date, an estimated 10% (9087 MTS built) out 91,500 households in UNHCR AoR camps translating to 90% of hhs who haven't received improved shelter materials (metal footing, treated bamboo, metal/concrete pillars, TSA-1/2 etc.) will require shelter assistance across UNHCR's AoR. For some non-food items (NFIs) and shelter items, 75% of refugee households expressed their preference for in-kind assistance as articulated in the post-distribution monitoring (PDM) of 2021. Overall, NFI distribution and prepositioning will cover regular NFI distribution, seasonal assistance, ad-hoc and emergency distributions based on rapid assessments, and through the voucher system modality or the ‘Pop-up Store’. distribution modalities will be based on different types of distribution in a dignified method to ensure equalized distribution covering all households in a blanket manner and targeted households or groups of PoCs as per specific needs to mitigate against risks of exclusion and exploitation. Post distribution monitoring (PDM) for NFIs will be conducted for distributed items to confirm acceptability and preferences, to confirm understanding of safe use of items, and to identify any issues with distribution mechanisms to improve future delivery of items. Continuous advocacy with the Government on predictable sustainable investment in infrastructure especially targeting the most vulnerable families will also be implemented. The Global Distribution Tool (GDT), rolled out in UNHCR-managed camps, will be further developed to facilitate assistance delivery, an activity that will require increased investment in data management support and monitoring. The GDT in turn will support comprehensive monitoring and evaluation of the distribution programme.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

As part of the programme, UNHCR conducts regular needs assessments to identify assistance required and prepare for the provision of support considering AGD and prioritization of persons with specific needs. It also ensures regular risk assessments and adjusts implementation as deemed feasible to obtain the desired outcome of the programme. UNHCR has a precise idea of who is doing what in the 16 UNHCR managed camps and will continue to strengthen inter-sectoral camp level coordination mechanisms through camp focal points and through the continued engagement of CiCs and other government authorities, as well as coordination at inter-agency and inter sectoral fora. With information sharing, mapping of partners' roles and responsibilities among organizations operating in the camps and camp level coordination the overall response will be more predictable with increased accountability helping to rationalize and provide better services to refugees and host communities. Projects will be implemented in complementarity with other operational partners working in the same areas and will ensure than no duplication or overlaps will occur. The UNHCR shelter programme will continue to explore a more innovative and integrated multi sectoral approach with other UNHCR units such as Protection, Education, Child Protection AoR, GBV AoR, Health, Nutrition, WASH, Energy and Environment and SMSD. UNHCR is considering rationalizing its partnership agreements with shelter and WASH partners aligning with the implementation of both programmes in the same camps. This will contribute to effective coordination at the camp level, efficient and integrated service delivery as well as strengthening strategic relationships with different stakeholders including the CiCs and refugees, which have been developed over the course of time. This integrated approach will comprise joint planning and partners review, field supportive supervision through existing MFTs.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Protection mainstreaming will be at the core of shelter and NFI interventions ensuring that the proposed response contributes to saving lives, ensuring safety and security, alleviating suffering. UNHCR ensures that maximum protection mainstreaming in the shelter and NFI response by prioritizing the most vulnerable groups and that referrals from protection actors are prioritized and upon the verifications to assist immediate. Also, during the identification of beneficiaries for planned shelter assistance, PSNs and EVIs are prioritized based on the list provided by UNHCR. e.g. EVIs and older persons will be prioritised for shelter and NFI assistance by mobilizing community groups to support the families with construction, repair and maintenance of shelters. As in 2022, shelter-NFI and partner staff will complete the annual PSEA full day training by the UNHCR protection team. A PSEA assessment has already been conducted for all UNHCR partners with satisfactory results

3.2 - Gender

The provision of safe and dignified shelter and NFI services to address the differentiated needs of refugees is one of the most fundamental principles of protection and GBV risk mitigation. Assessment of the needs of the various communities will focus on gendered needs to determine their specific requirements for shelter facilities and NFIs. Gendered needs and analysis will be considered for training, cash for work activities and monitoring services. UNHCR will ensure ongoing information sharing between site management, shelter, protection and GBV field staff in the targeted areas, and ensure appropriate training of the shelter partners and community volunteers on gender and age equality and GBV mainstreaming.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Children have the same right to shelter and NFIs as everyone else. At the same time, UNHCR recognizes that children may have specific needs. Improperly built and poorly maintained shelter facilities especially in crowded refugee camps may expose children to risks of sexual exploitation and abuse. During repair and maintenance activities, appropriate health and safety measures will be taken to ensure children (under 18 years) are are not engaged in CFW and porter activities. The project will also consider youth engagement and training to support the shelter upgrade and maintenance activities within their communities. Specifically, elderly persons will receive shelter support by mobilizing hhs to support repair, maintenance and construction works including the collection of NFIs from distribution points.

3.4 - Diversity

Focus on persons with special needs and risks associated with gender, age and diversity (AGD) will be assessed through participative and inclusive risk mapping focusing on the provision of basic services, as well as safety audits. Shelter activities will involve women, older people, PSNs and other forms of gender diverse population groups i.e., women and youth in the preparation of the different components of the shelter in the construction process including HLP issues.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Systematic and meaningful engagement of the affected population in the design of the projects are key steps in ensuring successful programmes and achieving quality output and outcome results. UNHCR will continue to promote compliance with AAP commitments on participation and partnership and information, feedback and action. Community consultations will be conducted during the planning, implementation and post-project phases through house visits, FGD and KII. Trainings will be provided to beneficiaries for all programmes to explain the programme so that eligibility and process are clarified, and beneficiaries are trained to utilize the materials provided properly. HH level PDM will be conducted. Through Common Feedback and Response Mechanism, the project will promote information sharing and community feedback mechanism among the community

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The strategy for DRR and DRM and climate resilient shelter and NFIs is a topic that UNHCR will discuss more in-depth in the coming months with the Shelter-NFI Sector. The project will have strong integration with DRR and climate change as the area is historically climate change threatened and disaster-prone. Relevant environmental aspects will be considered in terms of forestation, solid waste management issues and hh level shelter improvement. This project will engage community in risk and vulnerability assessment and planning for shelter construction and improvements. The provision of LPG will prevent use of fire word helping to avoid deforestation, promote use of jute bags, treated bamboo to prolong its life span and will minimize the use of plastic packaging for NFIs, and be coordinated with SMSD sector on site improvements works helping improve access and safety.
