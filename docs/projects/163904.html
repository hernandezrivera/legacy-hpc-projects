Project Summary

The project will provide a comprehensive WASH service for people in northwest Syria The project will focus on providing emergency and long-term WASH services to people who are living in formal/informal camps, collective centers and hosted communities. Shafak through this project will focus on rehabilitating damaged WASH facilities and infrastructure due to the absence of regular maintenance and unorganized extensions especially in main cities due to the displacement movements, and will cover the sanitation activities to enhance the environmental conditions for the people and avoid water-borne disease.
The project will comprise the following components:
a) Shafak will rehabilitate 9 water pumping stations including equipping the stations with all electrical equipment and water station facilities, and providing monthly running costs for the stations (fuel, chlorine, engine oil and incentives for workers)
b) Rehabilitation of 3 water tanks (high and ground tanks) and water networks.
c) Capacity building of the local operators and encourage the local communities to apply a cost recovery system.
d) providing rehabilitation of damaged sewer lines (3000 meters) and dislodging septic tanks (150 M3) in the locations that don’t have existing sewage networks.
e) supporting the local operators and municipalities to continue the garbage collection and disposal of the waste from 12 communities. This support will cover providing fuel for garbage vehicles, and maintain/repair of the broken ones in addition to support worker’s incentives.
f) The hygiene promotion will implement awareness sessions about the improvement of hygiene practices and behaviors.
g) 6000 hygiene kits will be distributed for the newly displaced people and children who don’t have the capacity to use hygiene materials.
h) Shafak will be ready to provide emergency WASH activities for 120,000 newly IDPs by providing emergency water trucks, distribution points, and construction emergency latrines.
In the case of emergency situations and new influxes of IDPs to targeted locations, Shafak will respond rapidly to the increased WASH need through scale up the activity of water trucking, as well as, provision of WASH emergency assistance such us latrines, hygiene kits, water tanks and hygiene promotion activities.
Shafak will ensuring innovative solutions as possible in its proposed interventions. In the informal camps that host the newly IDPs for a short time or for a period of up to three months, Shafak is working to apply a new mechanism for delivering WASH services especially for providing emergency latrines, as instead of building a concrete structure for latrines or install prefabricated latrines that need ensuring HLP and sometimes prevent the IDPs from the latrines if the landowner refused to construct the latrines on his land, Shafak will focus on preparing the new WASH emergency kit, which will include tools for constructing a private latrine (iron bars, plastic sheets, joints ...etc.) in the informal camps. The latrine will be for one family and linked with septic tanks construct by Shafak. This way will provide privacy for the family and can be portable if the family needs to move for another location.
Focusing on the rehabilitation of the infrastructure in the hosted communities will ensure the sustainability of access to WASH services for host communities and maintain the efficiency of the infrastructure to be able to receive more subscribers and avoid any damages which might be very difficult to the local authorities to fix it. As well as, the expected impact of the provided WASH services will be empowering the host communities to host more IDPs instead of sending to them to camps and subjecting them to the risk of deprivation of WASH services and increase suffering.

4 - Needs

After nine years of conflict, the WASH infrastructure and service provision in many parts of Syria require significant investments, repair and operational support. WASH systems have suffered from damages owing to years of functioning at high capacity, limited or no maintenance, destruction, displacement of technical staff and effects of climate change and poor water resources management. 42 per cent of the population is relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs. According to HRP 2020, vast WASH related needs continuous to exist in many parts of Syria, affecting various population groups. Access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies remain a challenge for newly displaced populations, IDPs living in IDP sites. Newly displaced populations require a timely and quality response based on comprehensive preparedness. IDPs in formal sites and informal settlements are highly dependent on continuous humanitarian assistance. Disruptions of services in IDP sites and their quality will have direct negative impact on people physical and mental well-being as well as protection consequences, specifically for women and girls. Communities not served with public water networks are in general at higher risk of unsafe water consumption, use less water than people well served via free/low cost water networks and spend significantly more of their incomes on purchasing water. At least 80 per cent of sewage is discharged untreated while at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional, 23 per cent of garbage is inappropriately disposed within communities . Such factors pose significant public health risks and have negative cascade effects on communities in terms of public health, inequality and poverty.

5 - Implementation of activities

The WASH response will reach 582,053 effected 35% of them are people recently displaced. Shafak will provide comprehensive WASH services through implementing the activities directly by their teams in the field. Shafak will contract with service providers to provide the following services.Act1.1: Water Infrastructures & Water Quality Assurance Shafak's intervention will cover the following aspects conducting rehabilitation of water network in the communities, water pumping stations, introduction of minimum cost-recovery for better support to operation and maintenance where possible; capacity building and financial support for staffing and ongoing operation and maintenance of water systems; improvements to water supply in schools, child-friendly spaces, and healthcare facilities. Moreover, Shafak will support the local operators through the provision of fuel, oil and electrical equipment to increase the capacity of the existing water stations. For emergency situation Shafak will provide water trucking for newly IDPs and install water tanks. Act1.2: Sanitation & Solid Waste ManagementShafak will conduct improvement/light rehabilitation to prevent the discharge of raw sewage and contamination of water bodies and soil. Additionally, capacity-building for staff and operations and maintenance of wastewater infrastructure and solid waste (municipal and medical waste) management systems will be supported, alongside the provision of required materials and technology to reduce overall public health risks and environmental pollution caused by the degradation of those systems. Responding to the people displacement. Shafak will construct emergency latrines and provide desludging of septic tanks in (in)formal camps and settlement. Act 1.3: Hygiene promotion:Shafak will distribute Hygiene kits to improve and encourage beneficiaries to strengthen hygienic behavior and practices and deploy the hygiene messages within camps and communities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The WASH sector will ensure a “Do No Harm” principle, and has carried out a Protection Risk Analysis as part of its sector strategy development. Potential protection risks that may arise from the implementation of the WASH sector strategy and related activities, along with a description of the relevant mitigation measures and resources required for monitoring those risks have been identified. The risks might include inequality in accessing services, work safety, gender-based violence (GBV) and exposure to explosive hazard . Regarding emergency services delivery, the sector will ensure that SPHERE/sector agreed standards are strictly followed and adapted to the context during each part of project implementation, especially during emergency sanitation infrastructure implementation phase when the risk of GBV is higher. WASH partners will take adequate measures to minimize exposure of the most vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls, children, people with disabilities, female-headed households) to greater protection risks, and particularly to GBV. WASH staff will be trained on key GBV concepts and referral pathways to properly support survivors. Women, adolescent girls and people with disabilities will be consulted during the design, implementation and monitoring phase of each project, with a need to put the necessary mechanisms in place to facilitate feedback from users.
