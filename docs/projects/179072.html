Project Summary

The aim of the project is to support self-resilience of HHs in Rural Damascus, Hama, Homs, Aleppo and Dar'a through distribution of food rations, agro-kits and poultry kits as well as organization of market driven income-generating activities. The Project will strengthen resilience of the affected HHS and reduce the dependency on aid of the most vulnerable Syrian population living in the proposed governorates. The project will contribute to the Sector Objective 2 “Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives” in order to reach 1.2 million HHs in 2021.
To decrease the dependency on food assistance and to promote the self-resilience and consequently the dignity of beneficiaries, the project is aiming to support 15,000 HHs. The main modality of implementation will be through distribution of in-kind assistance, while whenever possible this modality will be changed with restricted vouchers or cash-based assistance.
Support to small scale food production will be done through provision of agricultural inputs, poultry kits or food rations. Agricultural inputs will be delivered to those families who have an access to their land, ensuring that lands are free of UXOs, while in the areas still UXOs affected or for the families who do not possess land, poultry kits will be delivered. Food rations or food vouchers will be provided to the most vulnerable individuals that do not have access to a land and/or could not be involved into the rehabilitation of the agricultural or livestock activities. Market driven income-generating activities (IGAs) will target mostly women with proven interest in starting an IGA. The trainings will be organized for selected beneficiaries in cooperation with specialized institutions, the participants who attended the whole
training and passed the final exam will receive a small grant to start their own production.
In the process of beneficiary’s selection specific focus will be on returnees and local population (host communities). Special attention will be given to female headed HHs, elderly, HHs with People with Disabilities, unaccompanied children and to those groups who have only limited access to income to cover the family food needs and are forced to adopt negative copying mechanisms, unskilled or seasonal workers for instance.
During the preparation and implementation of the activities, COOPI will ensure the coordination with local authorities and robust participation of the communities giving them a voice to express desired modalities of implementation (the design of similar projects have been already successfully tested by COOPI in Dar’a and Nashabiyeh). Coordination will be ensured with the FSA Sector and its members but also with international and national actors active in the same villages in order to harmonized the response, avoid overlapping and to promote synergies. Synergies will also be promoted by COOPI through other projects submitted to HRP 2021 taking place in the governorates of this project.

4 - Needs

After 10 years of crisis,the food security situation in Syria has further deteriorated.According to HNO 2021 an estimated 14.2M people are in need representing 68.6% of the total population, 1,3M people are estimated to be severely food insecure, while 1.8M people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity, the highest record to date. The cost of living has escalated dramatically due to the depreciation of the national currency and the high dependency on food imports and deterioration of economic situation.The national average price of a standard reference food basket was SYP 136,398 in February 2021,increased by 241% in one year.According to the WFP mVAM, more than half of the surveyed households (51%) reported poor or borderline food consumption in March.Percent of HHs with inadequate FCS in the last 6 months by proposed governorates are:Hama 66%,Homs 59%,Aleppo 56%,Damascus 53% and Dara'a 46%.Between February and March 2021,the national average rCSI increased from 19.8 to 21.0, marking the highest national average level recorded since 2020.In targeted governorates situation is alarming: in Dar’a only 1% is producing food, while 24% of prople are dependent on food assistance. In RDamascus only 0.8% is producing, while 16.2% depends on food assistance.In Hama situation is slightly better, 1.5% is producing food and 7.5% depending on food assistance.In Aleppo 1.6% are producing food, while 7.6% depending on food assistance,while in Homs only 0.9% are producing food and 15.1% depend on food assistance. With this project we will support families to produce food and enable them to utilize their income for satisfying other basic needs, while decreasing the percentage of the families who are dependent on food assistance.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented in Rural Damascus, Dar'a, Homs, Hama and Aleppo governorates. In all governorates COOPI already implements activities related to food security or implementation will start in near future.The project will support 15.000 HHs providing food assistance, agro and poultry kits and support market driven IGAs.Agro related inputs or a poultry kits will be provided to food insecure and vulnerable families to restore their livelihoods and to allow them to decrease dependency on food assistance.HHs who has the land will receive agro kits,while families who do not have land will receive poultry kits.COOPI will provide food rations (or food vouchers) to the most vulnerable HHs that do not have access to a land and/or could not be involved into the rehabilitation of the agricultural activities.This refers mostly to food insecure women headed HHs, elderlies and PwD.Market driven income-generating activities (IGAs) will target mostly women with proven interest in starting an IGA. Before the distributions of agricultural inputs, the crosscheck of the beneficiaries will be conducted. Distribution points will be established,HHs will be informed in advance about the kit's composition,timing and date of the distribution.In each distribution point COOPI staff will be present.Where market is functioning and accessible, vouchers will be used to target food insecure HHs unable to participate in agriculture activities. Approaches and methodologies will be in line with recommendations from the CWG and FSL sector to ensure harmonization and develop best practices. Selection of beneficiaries for IGAs will be based on their interest, but also impact on the family income and local market will be taken into consideration.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

COOPI staff and beneficiaries could be exposed to protection risks during the implementation of the Action. The assessed risks are the following: sex exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers, violence from HHs against aid workers or among themselves, interference in the HHs targeting, HHs list not correctly updated by local authorities, aid diversion, hostilities in new accessible areas, tension among the community due to the HHs selection and diversion of input/assets. By prior experience in similar projects COOPI evaluate this risk as “low” and “medium”To mitigate these risks, the project foresees the following measures: establishment of feedback mechanism, balanced trained staff between men and women, training and follow up on code of conduct, distributions with strong planning and logistic attention dedicating special lines for vulnerable beneficiaries, training to staff on humanitarian principles and targeting modalities and criteria, regular verification of list of HHs before and during the project implementation, FGDs before, during and after the distribution, appropriate locations for distribution points in line with security situation in new accessible areas in consultation with relevant stakeholders, preparation of contingency plans, clear, public and transparent criteria for the selection of HHs, community based beneficiary selection. Moreover, before the distribution of agro inputs, project staff will check with local authorities to verify the status and ownership of the land to mitigate the risk to secondary or no rights holders.

Risk: Exploitation or abuse perpetrated by staff members or service providers when delivering assistance. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Establish formal and informal complaint and feedback mechanisms in each area of intervention; Provide staff with training on Code of Conduct and PSEA; Ensure the proper communication to the communities about the gratuity of the services; Established mechanisms and procedures for ensuring compliance with the PSEA zero-tolerance policy. Standard operating procedures are in place for submission and receipt of complaints, reporting, investigation and victim assistance. PSEA focal points have been established in COOPI country offices and headquarter and all staff members receive training and information on preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitation and abuse.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISK: Vouchers fraud and identity theft, vouchers sold or exchanged for non-authorized itemsASSUMPTION: Heightened visibility for beneficiaries/exposure and cash/voucher overly spent on non-food items, thus negatively impact food security outcomesMITIGATION MEASURES: Selection of many vendors at district and village level who are credible and acceptable by communities. Randomized quality controls of suppliers by technical staff (agronomist, veterinarian, and program staff).Voucher design with unique serial number, beneficiary codes already printed from central Database. Cross checking of the voucher supplied with the copy retained by COOPI (signatures/fingerprints, serial number, barcode, COOPI stamp across the two sections) as voucher receipt. Random monitoring at HH level of the quantity and quality of goods obtained being utilized (PDM questionnaires, PDM feedback meetings at village level). R:Direct access to the target areas and HHs is not permitted or becomes unsafe for humanitarian workers. A:Escalation of the hostilities in the targeted areas. MM:COOPI will maintain constant communication with agencies, government and in-country security monitoring networks including SARC. All staff will receive security on the field trainings in order to gain knowledge how to act if face problems on the field R: Selected beneficiaries cannot access their land. A: The presence of UXOs may hamper access to agricultural fields and rehabilitation of food production MM: in this case COOPI will organize awareness raising sessions for staff, volunteers and communities in UXOs recognition and will coordinate with SARC and UNMAS for information on progress of UXOs clearance operations.
