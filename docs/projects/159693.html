Project Summary

The project will target the most vulnerable drought affected IDPs and host communities especially women, men, girls, boys, elderly and PLWDs in Baidoa of Bay region ,the aim is to increase access to safe and appropriate water, sanitation services and hygiene practices by providing and improving access to safe water through rehabilitation/construction/extension of water sources through Installation and providing water piping system from the boreholes to the IDP camps.
Formation and training of water management committees to sustain the water infrastructure, Construction of disludegable segregated into sex sanitary facilities with privacy wall at IDPs and , Extension/construct of sanitation facilities at health facilities, distribution of hygiene kits to the vulnerable households specially to girls, select and training of community hygiene promoters on basic health and hygiene knowledge to create awareness sessions at community level by printing and distribution of IEC material that is written in the local language.
The project will do undertake solid waste management where the men and women will be provided with conditional cash transfer services on monthly basis after they successfully undertakes camp sanitation services and digging od=f waste management pits at the camp and also in the host communities specially the communities that is bordering the IDPs.
The project will support a total of 48,560 person which is segregated as Female at 29, ,530 and Male 19.030 while Children (under 18) 11,250 beneficiaries ,Adult (18 59): 32,164 ,Elderly (above 59): 5,146 persons and in summary the project will target a total of 32,600 IDPs and 15,960 Host communities.
The main purpose of the service provision is to improve access to safe water, reduce acute water diarrhea (AWD) cases, improve sanitation thus reducing sanitation related diseases that is transmitted through faecal oral route and promote health seeking behavior.

If there any unforseen challneges such as eviction, this will be done in a more provisional way where the infrastrucres constructed and other social ammenties will be wasted and destroyed.Islamic Relief Somalia has recently and partly ongoing cash transfer system for both conditional and unconditional cash to IDPs and poor host communities that were affected by drought in lughaya, Garowe .A total of 20,910 HHs from different donors specially Islamic relief partners such as Sweden, United kingdom, USA among others.IR experience of using cash transfers in humanitarian programming dates back to the early 2017 and has used cash transfers to respond to a range of acute and chronic needs for over 3 years.This has helped in meeting immediate needs whilst contributing to the longer-term economic recovery of disaster affected populations and increasing their resilience to future shocks.IR’s approach is market-based, incorporating delivery of humanitarian assistance through existing market actors and structures. In our efforts to respond to the needs of country program, IR East Africa Regional Office developed Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) guidelines in 2017 to country offices in conceptualizing and implementing CVA. The guideline is also inline with Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) and the Somalia Cash Working Group (CWG) criteria developed.IR Somalia used Telesom Mobile Company and its mobile money called ZAAD and Golis in Puntland which is more reliable and has more wide coverage in Somaliland and Puntland as well. Majority of the population including IR target beneficiaries in Somaliland and Puntland have mobile phones. Selected beneficiaries were given Telesom ZAAD enabled and registered sim-cards through which cash was transferred on a monthly basis from 3-8 months from one project to the other depending on the budget availability under each donor . Each beneficiary received between 60 -80 USD per month as per guidelines from the Somalia Cash Working Group (CWG), the cost of service charge was 1% of the total project budget and this cost was covered by IR Somalia.The use of unconditional cash transfers especially in Somalia led to a number of unexpected positive outcomes to the target beneficiaries. Some beneficiary households used cash received to start or restart a business, repay debts and keep savings. Cash transfers in Somalia also affected beneficiaries’ borrowing behavior/patterns and improved their access to local credit mechanisms, which can develop a pathway to longer-term economic resilience of the household. Using bank account in Ethiopia helped beneficiaries to feel secure with the cash as they can withdraw the amount they need at a time. Cash transfers also enabled to have bank accounts for the first time since they required to open bank account for payments. Based on the results of PDMs and FGDs conducted with targeted households after cash transfers, majority of the beneficiaries reported that they used the cash to purchase basic food and non-food items of their choice. Thy also reported that they were able to meet their basic food needs, meaning that cash transfers were timely and essential in securing their livelihoods. Therefore, use of cash transfers mitigated the risk of famine and thus saved lives in the context of protracted droughts and displacements. Therefore, the unconditional cash transfer that will be under taken under this project will be in line with the WASH cluster strategy of strengthening the sanitation services by under taken cleaning campaign in the IPDs camps and the neighboring host communities.Waste disposal site will be dug in a safer and designated areas after giving out sanitation tool to the beneficiaries that will be working in the sites sinece Sanitation services is important
