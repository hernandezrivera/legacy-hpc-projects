Project Summary

The project will overall target 10,000 HHs (60,000 individuals) that will comprise of vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and Host communities in Koch, Leer, Mayiendit, Panyijiar, Guit, Mayom, Pariang and Abiemnhom Counties in former Unity State. Of these 2300 will be women, 1900 will be men another 1800 are girls and 4000 boys. The project will provide emergency support to various vulnerable groups to meet their immediate needs while strengthening their resilience to cope with long-term shocks exacerbated by the conflict. The above target beneficiaries will be divided into the different counties as follows: Koch: 1250 HHs, Leer: 1250 HHs Mayendit: 1250 HHs, Panyijiar 1250 HHs, Guit: 1250 HHs, Mayom 1250 HHs, Pariang 1250 HHs and Abiemnhom 1250 HHs. The intervention will contribute to the Food Security and Livelihood Cluster objective as follows: Cluster objective 2 “Protect and rehabilitate livelihoods for the vulnerable population at risk of hunger and malnutrition”, directly linked to the Strategic Response Plan (SRP) objective “Improve self-reliance and coping capacities of people in need by protecting, restoring and promoting their livelihoods”, as well as the Cluster objective 4 corresponding to SRP3: “Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks”. The emergency life assistant will include supporting the households with seeds, inputs and other agricultural inputs in order to produce their own food and thereby safeguarding their livelihoods especially in areas of relative peace and return. It will also cater for short term lifesaving intervention to displaced or households living in collection sites with fast measuring vegetable kits and seeds to improve their nutritional and dietary needs with special focus to lactating women, elderly, sick and children under age of 5. It is envisaged that for the project to achieve its overall goals, CH shall integrate and mainstream across the various activities elements of GBV, general protection, Health, conflict transformation and WASH to have holistic results. CH has experience and work in those sectors before and willing to collaborate with others to complement its efforts. Where necessary, CH will work in close collaboration with other partners where we do not have experience and expertise in achieving the overall goal of mainstreaming and integration to maximize on resources as well as benefits. The project is expected to address urgent short and medium term support to most vulnerable members of the communities in concentrated IDPS sites such as in islands and other hard to reach areas as well as providing basic support for them as some start to return slowly to their homes. CH will provide agricultural inputs of vegetables and crop seed to the returning farmers. Given the potentially devastating risk of fall armyworm infestation, CH will support development of an Early Warning System (EWS) to ensure farmers are able to prepare and deal with this risk appropriately. Alongside this, CH will also provide transitional livelihood kits in the form of fishing rods, fast maturing vegetable crop seeds, and other agricultural inputs to enable them farmers cultivate food for their families and some for sale to the market. Considering the communities targeted by this project are pastoralist with livestock as one of the main lifeline, the project will also target about 50,000 cattle together with partners to provide treatment and information in order to increase productivity hence overall support to farmers.

1 - Needs

The situation in South Sudan generally since December 2013 has been perilous coupled by multiple crises and particularly in former Unity State where the conflict has been intensified. The results have devastated livelihoods, death, displacement and diseases. The humanitarian reports depicting this situation in the public domain are worrying to say the least. According to the recent IPC data released in September covering up to April 2020, the counties targeted here have most of their population in phase 4 and 5 of IPC. In 2020 6.35 million people will be in need of assistant up from 6.1 million in 2019. It is estimated that (55.4% of the total population) people being classified as severely food insecure. Therefore humanitarian assistance is critical in averting the emergency situation deteriorating to catastrophic levels. Access to affected population remains a challenge due to insecurity, with reported cases of access constraints increasing in 2019. IPC classifications already assume that a significant level of humanitarian assistance will occur according to plan, and any failure to provide this assistance would significantly increase the number of severely food insecure population. As IPC 2020 projection predicted 170,000 individuals will be at emergency stage by January and April. 515,000 will be in food crisis by next year that will estimate the total of 64.9% in unity state. The REACH reports and OCHA snapshot reports indicates that other protection concerns such sexual assaults, communal violence, cattle raiding, revenging killings and rowdy youth are on rise and impediments to slow recovery phase. Majority of the population in former Unity State are displaced to Protection of civilians sites (POCs), remote hard to reach islands as internally displaced, neighbouring countries such as Sudan, Uganda, Kenya and other eastern Africa countries. The few remaining host communities have had their assets limited resources exhausted, as they become host to huge number of returnees and IDPS. The counties that are targeted in this proposal are worst hit and in need of urgent support in term of humanitarian assistance. The protracted conflict has continued to cause increased insecurity along commercial supply corridors, fight of private sector actors, market fragmentation, food and fuel price inflation, risks of cattle raiding, limitations or changes on mobility for livestock, fishing and hunting migrations and conflict-related displacement. The conflict has led to the establishment of Internally Displaced People (IDP) camps or the absorption of IDPs within host communities, placing an additional burden on the already weakened livelihoods of local communities. This situation is expected to be worse if the current peace continues to hold and huge influx of IDPs and returnees come to their former homesteads. The other scenarios are that if the current peace agreement flop and new hostilities start again whether with the new groups or old is further going to push already dire situation to the extreme in those counties. Therefore to mitigate these risks, the proposal package is made in the context of the likelihood scenarios and in line with the food security cluster response Plan for South Sudan for 2020 and the prevailing humanitarian context

2 - Activities

Activities/Outcomes. This project will achieve the following activities as falling under CL4/CO2 “Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3)” and CL4/CO3 Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks” Activities Description: Activity 1.1.0. Carry out rapid needs assessment in the mentioned Counties to identify the existing community demands, with participation of 200 participants using the cluster tools such as FGD, KII and direct observations to identify the gaps. Activity 1.1.1. Community mobilization and sensitization of 9,000HHs in Leer, Mayiendit, Koch, Panyijiar,Guit,Mayom,Pariang and Abiemnhom Counties. Activity 1.1.2. Identification, registration verification and distribution of quality livelihood kits to 9,000 HHs in the targeted counties. Activity 1.1.3 225 farmer lead groups in in Leer, Mayiendit, Koch, Panyijiar,Guit,Mayom,Pariang and Abiemnhom Counties trained as (TOT) on identification and early warning system of fall armyworm surveillance and reporting. Activity 1.1.4 Train 450 Agricultural extension workers, 25 each per county on pest and crop disease control e.g. detection, prevention & eradication. Activity 1.1.5 A total of 225 farmers in Leer, Mayiendit, Koch, Panyijiar, Guit, Mayom, Pariang and Abiemnhom Counties provided with farm level support on improved production/modern farming methods. Activity 1.1.6 Provide 5,000 farmers with emergency livelihoods support such as fishing kits and fast maturing vegetable seeds (60% women and 40% men) in Leer, Mayiendit, Koch, Panyijiar,Guit, Mayom,Pariang and Abiemnhom Counties. Activity 1.1.7 Protection of livelihoods: provide famers on time with season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; provision of seed protection rations & seed multiplication sites; vegetable & crop kits. Activity 1.1.8 Training community animal health workers (CAHW) on livestock diseases, prevention & treatment especially quarantine measures on zoonotic diseases e.g. RVF, Anthrax, Hemorrhagic septicemia (HS), black quarter among others that are common in these counties. Activity 1.1.9. Mobilization and sensitization of communities and identification of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) Activity 2.1.0 Collection and distribution of Livestock Kits to the CAHWs and regular replenishment. Activity 2.1.1 Conducting monitoring, surveillance and reporting of diseases in the target areas. Activity 2.1.2 Strengthen the role of Community peace committees for indigenous conflict mitigation institutions to promote peaceful co-existence among IDPs, returnees and host communities to enable smooth farming.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

As a practice, our project staff carry out periodic focus group discussions and key informant interviews with separate groups(women,girls,boys and men) to find out how they feel about our services and how they would like to us to improve them. The sessions give them opportunity to air out concerns,complaints and compliments.A Separate FGDs for women and girls and one boys and women will be conducted. Some sensitive information where groups might not be comfortable to share will further be obtained through key informant interviews organized privately by our respective staff(males and females).In order to make everyone comfortable and free to speak, a thorough mobilization will be carried to explain the background of the exercise so that people will be free to speak and share their experiences without fear of being victimized. The beneficiaries might be illiterate in term of English but some might able to communicate in other languages such as Nuer,Dinka and Arabic.We would encourage those to provide feedback in the language they are comfortable with as we can understand or translate the feedback later on.
