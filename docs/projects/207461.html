Project Summary

AMAN's Livelihoods and Skills Development project, integral to the Joint Response Plan 2024, is a comprehensive initiative meticulously crafted to empower Rohingya refugees, with a particular emphasis on women, through robust non-formal technical and vocational training. The primary objective is to augment self-sufficiency by training in diverse vocational fields, mitigating physical and protection-related risks, and fostering resilience among refugees for a self-reliant future.
The project focuses on five key objectives: providing Livelihoods and Skills Development training based on recognized certification programs, empowering refugees—especially women—in various vocational fields, mitigating physical and protection-related risks for vulnerable populations, promoting peaceful coexistence, and enhancing resilience through additional capacity-building support.
Implemented in camps 10, 14, 15, and 16, including Extremely Vulnerable Households (EVHHs) and persons with disabilities, the project boasts a dedicated team comprising Community Mobilizers, Trainers, and Market Linkage Officers. Running concurrently with various activities, the project aligns with the Livelihood and Skills Development (LSD) Sector policy, adhering to the Multi-Year Operational Plan until December 31, 2025.
The training focuses on vocational areas such as Sewing Machine Operations and non-formal technical areas like Tailoring & Dress Making, Industrial Sewing Machine Operation, Tie Dye, Block Print & Handprint Operation, Boutique Management, Jute Ware Operation, and handicraft training. Targeting refugees, including women, older individuals, people with disabilities, and other gender-diverse populations, the program ensures a holistic approach.
The operational strategy encompasses 120 hours of non-formal technical training and 360 hours of vocational skills training across six centers, benefiting 1410 participants. The training includes Manual Sewing, Industrial Sewing, Boutique & Handicraft, and Social Awareness classes. Awareness sessions cover a spectrum of topics, fostering a comprehensive understanding.
Beneficiaries follow clear pathways post-training, utilizing their skills in self-employment and volunteer opportunities within the camps. Selected graduates undergo a four-month Entrepreneurship Program, receiving daily-based payments from product sales. AMAN facilitates market linkage and job placement for 180 Graduates.
The sustainability model outlines reinvestment of income for the first two years, with subsequent funding and operation of the production center by AMAN, supporting artisans and various social causes. Group formation occurs every three months, ensuring ongoing support and machinery provision for women's employment groups.
Regarding outcomes, 150 beneficiaries will receive 360 hours of Formal Competency Based Training adapted from Myanmar National Qualifications Framework (MNQF) and ASEAN Qualifications Reference Framework (AQRF), while 390 will go for non-formal technical training, including 120 hours of Jute Operation, Tailoring, Block Boutique, and Handicraft. The initiative aims to generate employment for 180 graduates at the production center, providing them with additional support and market connections. Additionally, 270 individuals will receive a machine along with market linkage assistance. There will be delayed support for 870 selected participants from 2021-2023 to address discrepancies between donor expectations and government restrictions.
In essence, AMAN's project establishes a robust linkage between non-formal technical and vocational training, offering diversified livelihood opportunities within the camps. The clear pathways, holistic support, and targeted interventions ensure the project's success in promoting self-reliance and comprehensive well-being among the Rohingya refugee community.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Livelihood Opportunities Discrepancy: One of the foremost identified needs in the region pertains to significant differences in livelihood opportunities between the Rohingya and host populations. Government restrictions prevent refugees from accessing formal employment, making humanitarian assistance their primary income source. While up to 80% of Rohingya households generate some income, most engage in casual labor with low earnings. In contrast, nearly all host community households have at least one adult involved in income-generating activities, with over half relying on casual labor as their primary income source. As a result, refugees resort to harmful coping mechanisms to meet their basic needs.Gender Disparities and Inclusion: The normative framework governing the lives of Rohingya refugees disproportionately affects women and girls, limiting their mobility, decision-making abilities, and access to life-saving services. It deprioritizes their needs compared to those of men. Women are excluded from decision-making processes and lack access to essential information, consultations, and feedback mechanisms. This reduces women's capacity to influence prevention and response plans. It is crucial to address these gender disparities and enhance the inclusion of women, women leaders, networks, and volunteers in planning, implementing, and monitoring response strategies to ensure inclusivity and effectiveness.Vocational Training and Literacy Gaps: Vocational training and literacy programs are vital to improving livelihoods, but they face challenges. The population aged 18+ needs access to suitable literacy programs, affecting enrollment in vocational training. Among refugee households, 71% reported barriers to income opportunities. Only 5% relied on volunteer work as a source of household income, and 14% generated income from small businesses and self-production. Informal income-generation activities pose challenges for refugees and the host community, impacting the region's economic stability.High Unemployment Rates: High unemployment rates persist in host communities, particularly among youth and women. Sustainable employment and entrepreneurship-oriented skills development are critical to addressing this issue.The identified needs underscore the importance of addressing economic disparities, enhancing livelihood opportunities, and promoting gender-inclusive approaches to uplift the livelihoods of the Rohingya and host communities in the region. These interventions will be essential in creating a more resilient and self-reliant population.
