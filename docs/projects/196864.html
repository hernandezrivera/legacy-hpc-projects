Project Summary

Following a decade of unfathomable violence and conflict, the humanitarian situation in Syria, specifically in Northeast Syria (NES) has resulted in the displacement of millions of Syrians and in worsening the socio-economic situation as well as handicapping the basic services in the region such as electricity, water, access to health and education.
The IDP camps in Northeast Syria are in a dire situation, especially in terms of education, majority of the camps don't provide formal schools, and due to their geographical placement, there are no schools available within a walking distance for the children inside the camp to attend. According to HNO22, 36% of the displaced school-aged children are less likely to attend any learning form compared to children outside the camp.
In Raqqa, Twahina camp is home to over 3500 IDPs, among them, 39% of the resident are school-aged children. Despite the absence of formal school, the one non-formal learning centre is not sufficient for the number of school-aged children inside the camp to provide the UNICEF Self Learning Program SLP, this has left many children without proper education, they are either dropping out or not registered in schools at all, which further exposes them to protection risks, such as early marriage and child labour.
AFH is aiming to target 3000 people with the back to school (BTL) campaign and enrol 400 students (200 M -200F) out of them 10% will be for PWD to provide a school for children to create a positive, stimulating learning environment, empowering the students to participate in building their society with awareness and positive behaviour.
Given the fact that most of these children did not get a chance to have an education due to the displacement of their families, they still need more educational support.
The school in Twahina camp is aiming to build and prepare students to learn and innovate by providing them with the intellectual and mental skills necessary for the self-learning process according to the UNICEF's SLP agendas, as well as consolidating the behavioural values in the students, revealing the students' aptitudes and skills and allowing the talented ones to refine their talents and develop their capabilities. Furthermore, the availability of schools inside the camps will motivate the caregivers and help the students to continue their education to achieve comprehensive development.
Action for Humanity will advocate for the students and help them to develop an appreciation of responsibility to make the students aware of their rights and duties. Identify the needs of society and prepare a generation that will contribute effectively to the advancement and development of society.

3 - Needs

Lack of education is one of the major gaps in NES, the continuous war for over a decade has resulted in destroying many schools' buildings, kept a generation out of education, and declined the quality of learning, and awareness among the population.The camp settlements inhabit the most vulnerable internally displaced people, they lack the basic means of living, and many camps don't provide formal education for school-aged children, According to HNO 2022, 36% of the displaced school-aged children are less likely to attend any learning form compared to children outside the camp. The non-formal education ( Self Learning Program by UNICEF) is the only form of education provided by humanitarian actors inside the camps for the camp residents and helps them with the right education and proper learning. Twahina camp is home to over 3500 IDPs, among them, 39% of the resident are school-aged children.This program is provided to children who are not attending school (out-of-school children) and do not have the right academic level for their age group and therefore fall behind compared to their peers. The intervention will also target children who dropped out of school for a long time to provide them with the necessary support to be readmitted to the school.The provision of education for children is a critical key in reforming a post-war society, since they are the foundation, and their deprivation from education will obstruct and lag the future of the community.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The main protection risks that might face the implementation of the project are as follows: 1- Child abuse, such as physical punishment, acting violently or mistreatment by teachers or educational personnel. In this regard, AFH will make sure to train all teachers and educational staff on safeguarding and child protection. AFH will make sure that all teachers and project staff will sign the Organization's Code of Conduct. 2- Acts of sexual harassment conducted by boys on young girls in latrines or unsafe places. AFH will ensure to rehabilitate of the sanitation facilities in a way that provides privacy and gender segregation to protect girls from any SG or harassment risk. 3- Services not matching the needs of people with disabilities and the difficulty of people with disabilities going to distant schools, as well as the lack of health facilities (bathrooms, wheelchairs...). in this regard, AFH will make sure that all the facilities are PWD-friendly including classrooms and sanitation facilities.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The main risks that might affect the execution of the project are the following: 1- Parents' resistance to children's education and especially girls as a result of negative coping mechanisms such as child labour for economic reasons or forced and early marriage as a cultural and traditional reason. In this regard, the AFH team will conduct several awareness-raising sessions for caregivers about the importance of education to their children. Furthermore, the team will engage the community leaders and the elderly in advocating for children's access to education. 2- Lack of qualified teachers in the area. This risk can be mitigated throughout the capacity building program that would be integrated into the project so that recruited teachers will undergo a package of training on PSEA, SEA and GBV cases. The teachers will also receive several training in active learning, child protection, methods of dealing with children, case management, as well as protection assessment. 3- Possible theft of schools' assets and equipment. In order to avoid this risk, AFH will hire two security guards to ensure the protection of the school. Furthermore, the team will engage the community leaders and the well-respected individuals in the camp to increase their sense of ownership. 4- Health risks such as cholera, and Covid-19 are mitigated through daily health awareness sessions, and focusing on spatial distancing and providing personal protective equipment such as sterilizers.
