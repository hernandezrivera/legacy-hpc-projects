Project Summary

GOAL will support the provision of comprenhensive health & nutrition services in 24 health facilities across South Kordofan and 17 clinics in North Darfur, in areas with high numbers of conflict-affected households (including long-term IDPs, vulnerable host communities, and some returnees. Nutrition interventions will be extended to Umbaro and Saraf Omra which are other localities in North Darfur. GOAL has identified areas where we have implemented in 2019 and still need support (6 health facilities in Dilling locality and 3 in Delami locality), as well as new areas with no partners currently working that are in high need of support (3 health centres and 1 Stabilization Center in Talodi; 4 health facilities in Rashad locality; 3 health facilities in Habila locality, and 5 health facilities in Abu Karshola locality). Services include Reproductive Health, EPI, early warning and outbreak response, support and training for health staff, clinic running costs, provision of drugs, medical supplies and equipment, and support for the Locality Health Department, including the Health Information System. GOAL also provides community health through Care Groups, community health promotion, defaulter tracing for RH and OTP services, and the Nutrition Impact and Positive Practice (NIPP) approach to prevent and treat Moderate Acute Malnutrition. Prioritization of new areas includes areas covered in GOAL's December 2019 needs assessment in Talodi, Rashad, and Habila localities, as well as provision of comprehensive support to Abu Karshola as part of a consortium in SHF 3rd allocation. GOAL will ensure comprehensive care in supported facilities, and GOAL will continue to closely coordinate through the Ministry of Health and the Health and Nutrition Sectors in Kadugli and Khartoum, and will also engage in ongoing coordination with other partners in each locality to ensure no overlap. Nutrition services aimed at preventing and treating MAM will also be expanded to Kassala & Gedarif states through the Nipp Technical Advisory Services (NTAS).
GOAL proposes to implement asset creation project that will sustain-ably improve household nutrition status and break the intergeneration malnutrition cycle of Umbaro and saraf Omra localities which are localities identified with high malnutrition rates.
GOAL Sudan will target both Kassala and Gedaref states of Eastern Sudan with the main objective of improving the living conditions of refugees, IDPs and host communities, by building capacity of State and Locality authorities as well as national NGOs to implement the multi-sectoral NIPP approach to address malnutrition, through the NIPP Technical Advisory Services (NTAS). In addition, GOAL proposes to disseminate this innovative intervention in new target communities through two national NGOs in the two states.

1 - Needs

South Kordofan is hosting a total of 48,000 South Sudanese Refugees and 179,266 Internally Displaced People (IDPs). This adds to the pressure on already inadequate health services. Infant mortality is estimated at 62 per 1,000 live births and the under-5 mortality rate at 81 per 1,000. Only 32.3% of women receive at least two doses during pregnancy and only 67% of children aged 12-23 months have received Penta3. The S3M survey from 2013 indicated that wasting (WHZ) in Dilling stood at 7.9%, Severe Wasting (WHZ) at 5.8% and stunting at 39.6%, while wasting (WHZ) rates in Delami stood at 9% and severe wasting (WHZ) at 2.7%. Dilling locality had the highest AWD in the 2017 outbreak with 294 cases and 33 deaths, a case fatality rate of 11.2%. In Talodi, the population of 48,862 people includes 13,968 IDPs concentrated in Talodi town itself. Talodi has 16 health facilities but only 6 (37.5%) are working, and all of these are critically short of staff, supplies, and the staff that are there are in need of training . In Rashad, which has 22,342 IDPs among a total population of 92,719 (1 in 4 residents), there is also a significant gap in health service provision. Of four facilities visited by GOAL’s assessment in Rashad, one was not functional and the other 3 were being run only by a nurse or CHW. None of these four facilities provided OTP or RH services. In Abu Karshola, similarly only 50% of its 13 health facilities have some functionality and nearly all are not submitting EWARN reports, leaving a large proportion of its 100,116 people lacking access to health services and at high risk of outbreaks. Use of health facilities is low, and 54% of women dropped out between ANC 1 to ANC 4, according to the HIS, reflecting a lack of access to and trust of health facility RH services. The maternal mortality rate is still equivalent to 222 out of 100,000 live births according to the Abu Karshola 2018 HIS, indicating a clear need for improved quality of and access to RH services in the locality. In North Darfur, Kutum locality had just 23 functional facilities of a possible 39 with GOAL directly supporting 13 of these (i.e. 57%). Alarmingly, the only facilities contributing to the Early Warning and Response Network system are those supported by GOAL giving just 21% surveillance abilities in Al Waha and 57% in Kutum. There is a high risk of disease outbreaks in Darfur, because of population displacement, high population density in camp settlements, and poor access to clean water sources. 2017 HIS data showed that more than 85% of the morbidities seen in the supported clinics are communicable diseases. GOAL reproductive health services surpassed targeted women for ANC services though TT vaccination remained below the national target of 80%. Existing data shows over 90% of deliveries are taking place at home. The November 2017 MUAC Mass Screening indicates GAM rates of 12.2% and SAM of 1.4% which fall within Serious WHO Crisis Classification (10 – 14 %). The Nutrition situation in Kassala and Gedaref is particularly alarming. According to 2013 Sudan S3M survey , high stunting rates are reported in Kassala and Gedaref (respectively 55% and 52%). Acute malnutrition rates in Kassala (15%) is among the highest in the country. An updated S3M is expected to be released by the Federal Ministry of Health soon based on nationwide data collected in late 2018, but rates in the eastern states are expected to still be high, as other secondary data continue to show high rates. In Kassala over 1,700 OTP and SC admissions were recorded in November 2018 alone. The food security and nutrition has been worsened further by the inflation and economic crisis that hit Sudan in 2018, significantly eroding purchasing power and families’ ability to purchase adequate food. Annual inflation is estimated at 73% since 2017, and the IPC analysis in October 2018 projects that by mid-2020, over 260,000 people in Gedaref and 390,000 in Kassala will be in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis).

- Comprehensive primary health service provision at 17 clinics in North Darfur and 24 in South Kordofan - Ensure provision of RH services at all supported clinics - Training and support for health staff in all supported - Support vaccination campaigns in all supported clinics - Ensure EWARN reports are submitted and that supported localities have emergency response plans - Provide drugs and medical supplies for supported health facilities - Conduct community health promotion and defaulter tracing for EPI, OTP and ANC - Implement Care Group and School Health Club approaches to improve community health-seeking behavior especially among families with young children, and reaching adolescent boys and girls with health messages. - Provide CMAM services by supporting OTPs in 17 facilities in North Darfur and 24 in South Kordofan for SAM cases, including covering staff costs as needed - Support rehabilitation, equipment, and supplies for OTP sites- Support the Stabiliation Center in Talodi town to be fully functional - Implement NIPP Circles to prevent and treat MAM as part of CMAM, integrating male and female circles to improve gender roles in CMAM - MUAC screenings and rolling out the "MUAC by Mothers" approach - Support referrals of SAM with Complications and EMoC (RH) to Kutum Hospital - Support LHD for monitoring and supervision visits to clinics and OTP sites - Training, equipment, and supplies for 9 localities in North Darfur to implement DHIS - Training of State Ministry of Health on NIPP approach and support for NIPP scale-up - GOAL also integrates WASH into its health & nutrition programme

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

GOAL's Nutrition Impact and Positive Practice (NIPP) approach is a community-based gender-sensitive approach to prevent and treat malnutrition by addressing the underlying behavioral causes of malnutrition. This includes training for participating households on production and use of improved cookstoves, and on microgardening. This is critical to the context in Kutum locality where deforestation and climate change are impacting the environment and people's ability to grow a variety of foods. In addition, all of GOAL's health activities support clean environment by integrating with WASH activities, both water supply and health facility sanitation and hygiene, and ensuring that proper waste disposal is practiced in health facilities (by training, installing incinerators. Due to lack of space elsewhere, this section also includes information on Protection and Gender mainstreaming. GOAL Sudan incorporates protection principles in every aspect of programing and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian action, including the following actions:  Vulnerable groups will be included in community activities and meetings, and consulted on their priority needs during needs analyses, using participatory methods, to ensure their opinion is heard, views are taken into consideration and they can input into program design, and convey any concerns or feedback.  At the community level as well as at the country level, the program has adequate female facilitators and staff with requisite skills and experience  Specific measures concerning older persons and person with disabilities are mainstreamed into programing  Elderly caregivers (such as grandmothers) are also prioritized for community health interventions and inclusion in NIPP circles.  GOAL is mapping other protection stakeholders active in the communities, to ensure complementarity, coordination of interventions and referral systems are in place.  Responses are coordinated across clusters (Health, WASH, Food & Nutrition, Protection etc.), and information is shared.  GOAL’s commitments to safeguarding is embedded in our policies (Code of Conduct, Child Protection Policy, PSEA Policy, whistleblowing policy). Promoting gender-equitable access to and utilization of Health, Nutrition services and remains a key aim for GOAL Sudan. GOAL has a Country Gender Focal Person, supported by a Field Gender Focal Person in each field site who is responsible to ensure gender is mainstreamed in all GOAL’s interventions in Sudan through  Collecting gender-disaggregated data  Conducting training on gender analysis as part of systematic gender mainstreaming both for GOAL staff as well as for Partners with whom GOAL Sudan is working  Ensuring that the community-based activities consider the cultural difference in the roles of men and women and use different approaches to equitably reach both genders.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

GOAL Sudan incorporates protection principles in every aspect of programming and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian action while making sure that response is participatory and that it is accountable to the people GOAL seeks to assist. For GOAL Sudan, protection mainstreaming is crucial both internally and externally to ensure that extremely vulnerable groups including children, women, people with disabilities and the elderly can access services and are protected from both intentional and unintentional harm. GOAL has ensured that all staff and partners are aware of protection issues through training, and protection mainstreaming tools have been used in programming and internally to ensure the safety of the extremely vulnerable groups. Throughout its intervention GOAL is promoting gender equality and promoting beliefs and norms that foster respectful, non-violent gender norms to help prevent and mitigate GBV. Safety, respect, confidentiality and non-discrimination in relation to survivors and those at risk are vital considerations for GOAL.
