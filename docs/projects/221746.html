Project Summary

High prevalence of wasting remains a public health issue taking a heavy toll on the survival, growth, and development of young children in South Sudan. According to the IPC-AMN 2024, the malnutrition crisis in South Sudan will continue to deteriorate in 2025 as a result of multiple, converging humanitarian crises. Approximately 4.3 million children under five, as well as pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, will need life-saving treatment and preventive care. This includes 0.65 million children under five will be at risk of severe acute malnutrition and requiring urgent treatment.
Results from the recently released IPC-AMN 2024 indicates that 49 counties have acute malnutrition rates that exceeds the emergency threshold of 15%, with one county in Upper Nile projected to reach IPC-AMN phase 5. One in two children are sick at any given time with fever, diarrhea, and respiratory infections, coupled with poor nutrition, water, and sanitation access (over 60% practice open defecation), added to the protracted crisis. The situation is further aggravated by economic challenges, climate impacts, ongoing conflicts, heightened GBV risks, and spillover effects from the Sudan crisis. With heightened food insecurity affecting almost 50% of the population, young children are not accessing adequate diet as reflected by Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD) of 2.2%.
Given the projected nutrition situation and in response to humanitarian needs in South Sudan, UNICEF and nutrition implementing partners have projected to reach 3,380,264 beneficiaries in 2025 (2,513,162 children U5 and 867,102 pregnant and breastfeeding women) with lifesaving and preventive nutrition interventions. In 2025, UNICEF will target 2,926,469 children and women with prevention of malnutrition services and 453,795 children at risk of severe wasting with treatment services. As the core pipeline manager, UNICEF is still confronted with a shortage of essential nutrition commodities, including Ready to Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF). The current gap of RUTF is 409,025 cartons, which will translate into approximately 409,025 children missing critical nutrition services. The estimated total cost of delivering the UNICEF nutrition services under this project is estimated USD 73.5M; this includes 43.5M for frontline services and UNICEF’s technical support while almost 30M is for the gap in procurement and distribution of lifesaving nutrition supplies.

1. Needs

According to the IPC-AMN 2024, the malnutrition crisis in South Sudan will continue to deteriorate in 2025 as a result of multiple, converging humanitarian crises. Approximately 4.3 million children under five, as well as pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, will need life-saving treatment and preventive care. This includes 0.65 million children under five at risk of severe acute malnutrition and requiring urgent treatment. Results from the recently released IPC-AMN 2024 indicates that 49 counties have acute malnutrition rates that exceeds the emergency threshold of 15%, with one county in Upper Nile projected to reach IPC-AMN phase 5. In 2025, eight children out of every ten children under five are projected to be at risk of acute malnutrition, IPC-AFI results suggest that 7.69 million people including 1.5 million children under five to be severely food insecure by July 2025. The situation is further aggravated by economic challenges, climate impacts, ongoing conflicts, heightened GBV risks, and spillover effects from the Sudan crisis.The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating because of the economic crisis, climatic shocks - primarily widespread flooding, conflict and insecurity, repeated outbreaks of fever, diarrhea, and respiratory infections, coupled with poor nutrition, water and sanitation and the inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan also exacerbates the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile country. Diseases in the flood prone areas such as diarrhea, ARI and malaria are likely to persist and with the inflation and low economic power, access to health services is expected to remain. Given the projected nutrition situation and in response to humanitarian needs in South Sudan, UNICEF and nutrition implementing partners have projected to reach 5,588,623(children and women) in 2025. 530,000 children U5 with severe wasting to be treated, 2.9M children U5 for preventive intervention including Vitamin A supplementation and deworming and 2,100,000 pregnant and lactating women with key Maternal, Infant and Young Child (MIYCN) counselling and messaging. However, for this HNRP project, UNICEF and nutrition implementing partners have projected to reach 3,380,264 beneficiaries across the country in 2025. This includes 453,795 children U5 with severe wasting to be treated, 2 M children U5 for preventive intervention including Vitamin A supplementation and deworming and 867 K pregnant and lactating women with key Maternal, Infant and Young Child (MIYCN) counselling and messaging. UNICEF will work in collaboration with sister UN agencies and implementing NGO partners to scale up high impact nutrition interventions and improve the enabling environment. Nutrition interventions will follow a multi-sectoral approach, coordinating with WASH, Health, FSL and Protection partners/clusters. To ensure coordination and efficiency in responds to the looming humanitarian need, a sub-national coordination support will be provided to ensure smooth implementation of an integrated service.UNICEF as the core pipeline manager is still confronted with shortage of essential nutrition commodities including Ready to Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF). The current gap of RUTF is estimated at 409,025 cartons which will translate into 409,025children missing critical nutrition. The estimated total cost of delivering the UNICEF nutrition services under this project is estimated at USD 73.5 M; this includes 43.5 M for frontline services and UNICEF’s technical support while almost 30M is for the gap in procurement and distribution of lifesaving nutrition supplies.

2. Project activities

UNICEF and its nutrition implementing partners will implement numerous interventions and activities to respond to the needs. The planned activities are:  Procurement and prepositioning of Nutrition supplies to UNICEF’s main hubs and distribution to partners. Train health and nutrition workers on Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) at national, state, county and community level.  Provide individual and group counselling on MIYCN to pregnant and caregivers of children aged below 24 months, including men.  Support establishment of kitchen gardens and cooking demonstration at the facilities and the communities.  Promote initiatives to improve dietary diversity with local food. Conduct two rounds of integrated Vitamin A supplementation, deworming and Mass MUAC screening for children 6-59 months campaigns. Malaria testing and treatment at the nutrition sites (OTP).  Distribution of mosquito-net and WASH non-food items (NFIs) and hygiene promotion at the OTP sites through WASH Cluster.  Training of the Health/Nutrition staff on how to provide psychosocial support (PSS) to beneficiaries and early childhood development (ECD) activities at the nutrition sites (OTP).  Train health and nutrition workers on Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) to improve care for children aged 6-59 months with wasting.  Community mobilization and active screening for early case detection of children with wasting SAM and referral. Provide integrated care for children with SAM in OTP/Stabilization Centers (SC) in health facilities, including WASH services and ECD at nutrition sites.  Train frontline nutrition staff on GBV basics, including how to respond to GBV disclosures safely and appropriately and provide gender-based violence (GBV) referral services at the OTP sites.  Monthly monitoring of the progress and quality of nutrition interventions and services at the static nutrition facilities and outreach sites.  Provide integrated on-the-job coaching and mentorship on SAM treatment, MIYCN, supplies management and end User monitoring. Track monthly stock utilization and supplies balances to be analyzed for decision making. Routine management of nutrition programme data and information.  Integrate social protection in nutrition with unconditional cash plus activities to pregnant and breastfeeding mothers Advocate/initiate activities for integration of nutrition in wash, food systems, education, etc.
