Project Summary

The Mobile Health Teams project seeks to improve accessibility to essential primary health services for
28 remote Palestine refugee communities in Area C who are facing access restrictions to health services
due to Israel's restrictive planning system.
Through the deployment of three Mobile Heath Teams, UNRWA will provide essential health services while
taking measures to ensure accessibility for people with disabilities and a strong emphasis on accessibility of SEA complaints mechanisms.

Implementing Partners

The United Nations Relief and Work Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East (UNRWA) has a fundamental humanitarian operational presence in the West Bank, both geographically and in terms of sectors of intervention. Next to the Palestinian Authority, UNRWA is the biggest service provider in the West Bank. In total, the Agency serves 1,111,064 Palestine refugees and other registered persons (UNRWA, Registered Population Dashboard, January 2022), which amounts to one-third of the population in the West
Bank, of whom more than half a million are women and girls. UNRWA was established by the General Assembly in 1949 and is mandated to assist and protect some 5 million registered Palestine refugees in the region. As the main service provider for Palestine refugees in the region, UNRWA delivers primary education, health care, relief and social services, infrastructure and camp improvement, microfinance, and emergency assistance. The Agency has extensive experience providing emergency assistance to Palestine
refugees during humanitarian crises to save lives, preserve human dignity, and prevent and respond to the impacts of such crises. In the West Bank, the Agency's programmes seek to reduce the impact of the protection crisis stemming from over 50 years of the Israeli occupation by addressing the critical needs of the most affected individuals. In terms of response, UNRWA provides critical services to those exposed to protection threats, including psycho-social counselling, medical assistance, cash-based interventions, and the provision of dedicated programming to specific vulnerable groups.

1 - Needs

Mobile health teams offer viable options to improve access to primary health care for vulnerable and remote populations. These communities include, primarily, those located in Area Cand the Seam Zone which are the closed areas behind the Barrier. In many of these areas, the journey to the nearest clinic has lengthened because of the need to travel circuitous ways around settlements and barriers. Access to healthcare can be hampered by the unavailability or high costs of transportation, and in some communities, access to emergency services is challenging as even ambulances face delays. Since the beginning of 2022, the security situation in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem has deteriorated dramatically. ISF violence against Palestinians are at record-high levels judging by most conflict metrics – such as injuries, fatalities and live ammunition use. Rising tensions have particularly impacted Jenin and the surrounding area, with ISF operations characterized by excessive use of force, and frequent exchanges of live fire with armed Palestinian factions. The intersection of increased numbers of conflict-related injuries, increased incidence and risk of settler violence and intimidation, and heightened / existing restrictions on movement for certain communities has increased the need for health services, while concurrently impacting the capacity for refugees in many communities to meaningfully access health services. According to the 2022 MSNA, 11.5 per cent of Area C refugee households with a health need in the three months prior to the MSNA data collection, experienced barriers due to movement restrictions by need of permits/ or checkpoints. Over 50 per cent of Area C refugee households with a health need, experienced barriers to accessing health services (10.9% reported that distance was too far, 14% that no medicine was available at health facility and 14.9% that no treatment was available while 11.5% reported movement restrictions). Under its core budget, UNRWA only provides health services through its network of 43 health centers and Qualqilia Hospital. The increased tensions in West Bank and East Jerusalem, have significantly and further reduced regular and routine access of Palestine refugees to urban and village centers where health services are offered. Many Palestine refugees in Area C have ceased access to any health service. Based on a vulnerability assessment by UNRWA's Protection team and through the mobile health clinics sub cluster, UNRWA has identified communities in need and plans to target thirteen remote communities in Area C with a total population estimated at 65,169 persons. The demographics of these communities also reflect the vulnerability categories identified in the 2023 HNO, namely Bedouin and Herders in Area C, refugees outside camps and people living below the poverty line. Provision of health services through mobile health teams will be prioritized based on additional categories of vulnerability within the target communities. In addition to the identification of the basic health needs for Bedouins and herders in Area C, additional vulnerability factors of gender, age and disability intersect to create additional and distinct health needs for sub-groups within these refugee communities. For example, multiple studies conducted in the oPt have highlighted additional barriers for elderly, pregnant and lactating women, members of female headed households, and persons with disabilities to access health care. According to the 2022 MSNA, 11.2 % of refugee households reported having at least one member withdisability. UNFPA has also highlighted additional barriers for women and girls to access basic health services, particularly in rural/remote areas, with elderly women and women and girls with disabilities facing higher levels of exposure to violence, discrimination and access to basic services (oPt Gender AlertAnalysis, 2020; UNFPA, Women and Girls with Disabilities, 2019; EducAid Italy, 2020; Al Marsad, 2020). Research also indicates that elderly persons are disproportionately affected, with almost one-third of elderly Palestinians reporting more than one disability (BMC Public Health, 2019). Moreover, the intermittent imposition of restrictions on movement during lockdowns has significantly impacted the capacityof women and girls experiencing GBV to receive essential services, including health services. As the provision of health care is often a primary entry point for GBV survivors, the offering of essential primary health care for these communities will offer at risk groups and survivors a vital means to obtain basic health services, information on available UNRWA GBV hotlines and support, and obtain appropriate referral services. The vulnerability of the target population to protection risks following rights violations and the relative power imbalance between humanitarian workers and refugees requires UNRWA to ensure that safe complaints and feedback channels based on consultations with beneficiaries, are accessible and known to the target population, including feedback and complaints on goods and services and more serious complaints related to misconduct and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Consultations undertaken by the PSEA Network for the oPt confirm the need for all organisations to strengthen SEA policies and processes. The MSNA 2022 data found that 8.5% of refugee HHs reporting awareness of how to access and use complaint or reporting mechanisms (compared to only 1.7% of non-refugee HH). Information gaps remain on the needs, preferences, safety and confidentiality concerns of refugee women and Information gaps remain on the needs, preferences, safety and confidentiality concerns of refugee women and adolescent girls, especially women with disabilities, single headed households, pregnant/lactating women and new mothers and elderly women. Information will be obtained through surveys,interviews and focus group discussions.

2 - Activities

With this intervention, UNRWA will 1) provide primary health care, including medical consultations and examinations, mental health assessment and services, dispensation of essential drugs and medication for chronic diseases and non-communicable diseases. The provision of health services to 28 remote communities is intended to respond to immediate needs, also for those who have longer term medical needs, including women during pregnancy and childbirth; sick children and infants; the elderly; and the chronically ill or persons with disabilities. These groups face significant mobility barriers and therefore have even lower access to mainstream health services. The knowledge that health access may be difficult and risky also acts as a psychological stressor that can directly affect health. UNRWA also protects the vulnerable populations by reducing their need to travel, which has been identified as a priority need by both Palestine refugees themselves and UNRWA West Bank, and reflects the Agency’s commitment to meet the needs of the most vulnerable and at risk individuals. Most of the proposed communities have significant numbers of Palestine refugee populations. Limited MoH and private health providers in proximity to some of these communities require additional payments for refugees to access the services posing additional hurdles for Palestine refugees. In some areas near Jerusalem, refugees are often unable to access UNRWA’s Al Zawiya (Indian Corner) health clinic in the Old City of Jerusalem. For refugees without permits, Jerusalem is inaccessible for UNRWA health services and residents must travel to Ramallah/Bethlehem. Transport costs are significant as are access delays when passing through checkpoints. For two locations that are covered by other partners, UNRWA's MHT will start after April 2023. Each MHT comprises : a medical doctor, an assistant pharmacist, a staff nurse, a midwife nurse, a psychosocial counselor, a clerk/driver, and two cleaners. Cleaners will be identified from the communities served. MHT will provide primary health care such as medical consultations, examinations, drug dispensary in a suitable space identified and secured by the communities themselves. MHT will strive to ensure readiness to interfere in case of need; capacity building (of the team and the community) on how to deal in case of any emergency as first aid, life support, infection control; sufficient equipment, material and drug supply; stockpiling lab supplies, drugs and disposables, enhanced coordination and communication (with all related health providers and the local community) and provision of health care services. In response to the distinct needs of the groups identified above, the provision of mobile health services will prioritize persons with disabilities, elderly, pregnant and lactating women, children and female headed households. 2. Psychosocial support. The addition of psychosocial counselors and midwives will allow UNRWA to identify vulnerable individuals, make referrals for GBV survivors, and conduct the community engagements aspects of the project. Health services will be provided to remote communities in consultation with the communities in order to identify safe, confidential and accessible health services to all members of the community, including monitoring and responding to feedback. 3. Health staff training. All health staff working within the project will complete PSEA training and are obligated to report all suspected cases in accordance with UNRWA's PSEA framework. 4. In cases of illiteracy, an UNRWA staff who is neutral from the project will assist in filling the survey in coordination with protection team. UNRWA’s protection team will also continue to monitor and communicate directly withcommunity leaders and representatives regarding their satisfaction with the services andto support remote health teams to address the health needs of the most vulnerable groupswithin the community. 7. 9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

The MHT project adopts a variety of mechanisms to facilitate the meaningful participation, feedback and complaints from all population groups. Finally, the COVID-19 emergency has increased demands on UNRWA and its services and presented challenges to establishing appropriate communication with communities, as well as to accountability mechanisms in the context of COVID-19 restrictions. The Protection Department has led the establishment of dedicated AAP lens to the COVID-19 response, including drafting key communications to the refugee community.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The MHT project adopts a do no harm and conflict sensitivity approach, including specific measures to ensure that identified risks are addressed within the action and unintended negative consequences are minimised. UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. UNRWA’s Case Management SOPs ensure that protection cases are identified and referred to specialised support services. SOPs cover compulsory reporting on SEA and support to survivors. A dedicated psychological consultation hotline is available to all refugees. UNRWA has existing guidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in this project. In UNRWA's Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritisation of safety and dignity is explicitly address as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneficiaries, communities or staff, including risk mitigation for SEA. On a biannual basis all of UNRWA's activities are subject to a protection mainstreaming audit, which assesses the integration of protection principles in UNRWA's work. This audit included extensive interviews and focus group discussions with beneficiaries, and sought to assess UNRWA's application of the four main protection principles, including the principle of Do No Harm. In 2019 UNRWA developed a new information management system for the collection of sensitive protection monitoring and documentation data; specifically the system now separates out biographical data from incident related data, with individual cases being only referred to through generated case code. For the purposes of ensuring that briefings involving affected persons, are carried out in a way that prioritises the safety and dignity of these persons, UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. This includes a checklist to guide all staff working in this capacity. UNRWA has adapted its case management in response to difficulty of physical access to concerned individuals/households. UNRWA has established a dedicated psychological consultation hotline available to all refugees. In addition, UNRWA has existing guidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in this project.
