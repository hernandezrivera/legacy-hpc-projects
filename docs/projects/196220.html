Project Summary

The project is designed to strengthen the self-reliance and resilience of the affected communities by improving their access to livelihood opportunities and basic services through the integration of ERL, FSL and Protection related activities. The intervention targets Jandairis and A’zaz cities along with four camps (Al Resala, Al Mukawamah, Deir Ballut and Al Muhamadiya), all locations classified as underserved according to the current OCHA allocation with identified needs aligned with Envelop 2 of 2022 SA-1. The project consists of 3 pillars: (1) delivery of vocational trainings; (2) provision of livelihood opportunities in the form of small-business grants and apprenticeships; and (3) provision of protection services to reduce the affected communities’ protection risks through the establishment of solidarity groups and delivery of case management.
Under the ERL component, 360 participants will benefit of vocational training in sewing, electricals, and baking and pastry, of which 160 will be selected to attend the apprenticeship program and 30 will receive small grants to start up businesses. Under the FSL component, 120 women participants will benefit of vocational training in food processing, of which 20 will be selected to receive small grants to start up businesses. During the vocational training, the pax will receive 4 USD/day to cover their transportation and meal costs, which will be transferred monthly. Moreover, the apprentices and their mentors will receive a daily pre-determined allowance for their consistent attendance and the SBGs will be worth 1000$.
Under Protection Component, GP and AR activities will be conducted by the Mobile Protection Desk (MPD) (consisting of outreach and case management staff). They will assess cases and conduct cross-component referrals when relevant and necessary. Upon assessment, identified cases will be provided with CM and IPA support. MPD will also conduct AR activities. All the staff members of MPD will be provided with coThe solidarity group structure aims to recognize, support and strengthen the protection capacities of the affected population and service providers. Group members (consisting of 5 beneficiaries and 3 local authority representatives such as camp managemnt staff, community leaders, etc) will act as one of the beneficiary identification sources for case management activities, and will also play a vital role in disseminating critical information about rights and services and increasing awareness of own community members. WHH’s committee model is disability inclusive and mainstreams age and gender. Moreover, WHH has closely coordinated with other actors on the ground to avoid overlapping with existing CBP mechanisms. 92 protection and non-protection actors will be trained on protection, mainstreaming and referral mechanisms and will be provided with extensive capacity building program CBP, CP, GBV, conflict sensitivity, CM and PFA. Lastly, through KABP surveys, different formats of surveys, FGDs, etc, WHH will closely monitor the protection environment to identify emerging/protracted needs and protection risks.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2023, NWS remains to be the most impacted area, with 5.6 M PiN. The inflation is around 90%, and the prices of food baskets have increased by 87%. According to REACH HSOS 02/2022, livelihoods is among the priority need both for residents and IDPs in NWS. Unemployment remains a major problem with only 58.9% of individuals aged 18+ working in the past 3 months (HNO 2022). Women represent only 7% of the working force, encountering many difficulties in accessing the labor market. The unemployment rates do not differ at the level of NWS more than 1-2% between residents and IDPs while the regions with high levels of displacement burden often face increased competition over limited resources leading to a rise in tension between the two communities (HNAP, Livelihood Situation Analysis in NWS). As inexperienced youth have the most difficulties in accessing jobs, apprenticeship programs are needed at the local level. In terms of starting new businesses, capital remains as the main challenge, especially for IDPs. Vocational training, small-business grants and cash for work were mostly demanded by youth.

4 - Implementation of activities

The activities will be implemented through self-implementation modaility. The activities will ba carried out through field team based in Syria and project team based in Türkiye through X-border. WHH strives to align and adjust its activities in accordance and coordination with Cluster recommendations as an active member of all relevant clusters. The activities will be introduced to all stakeholders prior to the implementation. The activities will be implemented under 3 clusters: 1)FSL (vocational trainings and SBGs) (for 120 women) 2)ERL (vocational trainings, SBGs and apprenticeships) (for 360 individuals) 3) Protection (Case Management, Awareness Raising) (for 3,480). Protections lens and involvement of affected people are incorporated in implementation by ensuring the most vulnerable and able to work beneficiaries will be referred by solidarity groups to the empowerment activities, specific tracks of SBGs and apprenticeships will be available for women, separate training for men and women will be delivered in safe and appropriate locations, PWDs will be integrated and supported, through proper and accessible venue selections and facilities to ensure active participation. PDM and other MEAL and programmatic exercises will take place. Under the protection component, case workers will work side by side with solidarity groups members to identify local protection risks and provide case management, including IPA, for the most in need ones, focusing on GBV, PWD and CP cases.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1)Turkish authorities prevent implementation partners from operating across the border into Syria. M1)WHH ensures good relations with the Government of Turkiye through legal compliance and appropriate coordination with the relevant authorities. R2)Border closed due to acts of war. M2) Measures are partly carried out by vouchers, so that in the event of a border closure, only in-kind measures with procurement outside Syria would be affected. Furthermore, with the existence of PTT and a procurement team in both Syria and Gazinatep, procurement inside SYR is an option in order to be able to continue the project. R3)Strong fluctuations of the SYP and TRY price in relation to EUR/USD. M3)Procurement within the TUR, including lines in the contract to allow price negotiations with the suppliers, and budget realignment when necessary. R4)Project funds are stolen from armed groups in Syria, or armed groups force the IP to hand over parts of the aid to them. M4) As an active member of AWG, coordinating on aid diversion, available robust partner monitoring framework and requesting the support of local authorities. R5) Military actions of the civil war parties force partners to cease their activities. M5) existence of appropriate protocols and shifting to other locations where programming is possible R6) Local authority members won't participate in the CBP committees because they will not receive per diems M6) Calls for members will emphasize the voluntary nature of their participation
