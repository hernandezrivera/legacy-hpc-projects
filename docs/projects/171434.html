Project Summary

Due to the combined effects of widespread and severe flooding, Desert Locust infestation, socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19 and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks, up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through December 2020 in the absence of humanitarian assistance. In addition, 849 900 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through August 2021-(FSNAU-Technical Release-Sep-2020). The 2020 Deyr (October-December) rainfall season is likely to be below average to average across the country, which could lead to drought and trigger a worsening of the humanitarian situation if the 2021 Gu (April-June) season rainfall is also delayed or performs poorly. Severe riverine and flash floods in April and early May caused significant population displacement and damage to property, infrastructure, farmland, and crops. Atypically heavy rain since July led to further riverine flooding and flood-related damage. The proposed project aims at life-sustaining interventions and other life savings which will include conditional and unconditional cash transfer. The project also proposes recovery initiatives such as skills development for women and youth, livelihood assets creation, provision of agricultural inputs for improved production. The food security project works in close collaboration with stakeholders from the humanitarian organizations, local authorities, and both the public and private sectors to promote the cash programming support. KAAH Relief and Development Organization works closely with the community leaders, local authorities, and the community-based organizations for smooth implementation in operational regions.
KAAH is in agreement with the telecom companies for money vending services to safely and transparently transfer cash to beneficiaries and ensure upholding their dignity. Improved agricultural production is to improve food security among the rural agro-pastoral communities while vocational skills development will help generate market-sensitive job opportunities. In addition, small farmers and agricultural practicing households will be provided with agricultural inputs including (Training, cereal/pulses / horticultural crops seeds, and Tractor tillage hours) for improved agricultural production and access to nutritious horticultural crops. KAAH adopted the localization approach program operational regions and will ensure complementing of programs supported by our donors and ensuring overlapping will be conducted in several villages. To ensure food security and other values added, the targeted households will benefit from the program in awareness-raising and sensitization on good nutrition practices and intervention, health food preparation, and access to diverse food supplements, good hygiene practices, and diversified livelihoods options. These integrated services in the program will ensure benefiting poor households has improved capacity, access to food, and income-generating sources. KAAH will make sure to implement cash transfer programs (CTPs) considering seasonality, agricultural calendar, and fluctuating market prices. Resilience building initiative will help beneficiaries to sustain shock, protecting, and conserving natural resources on which they bank and making an informed decision. KAAH will support targeted populations especially communities in hard to reach areas to meet their basic food and non-food. In the long run, this will help in development. KAAH organization constantly conducts assessments to identify poor households for recovery and resilience programs. The identified households are registered and supported by food security programs.

Project Activities

Activity 1: 400 households from Galgaduud and Lower-shabelle regions are selected and registered for unconditional cash transfer. Targeting includes poor households, minorities, women headed households, child-led households, persons with special needs, PLWs and marginalized groups. Activity 2:400 households from Galgaduud and Lower-Shabelle regions are selected and registered for conditional cash transfers in cash for work initiatives for asset creation including poor households, minorities, marginalized groups, women headed households. Youth and women are registered for training in employment skills. Activity 3: Provision of agricultural inputs (seeds, GAP training and tractor tillage hours)- Provision of Agricultural inputs, seeds distribution, GAP training and tractor hours for 400 households in Lower-Shabelle. Activity 4: Provision of fodder for pastoral communities in Galgaduud and Lower-shabelle.Fodder distribution support for 500 households.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash transfer is made to ensure that dignity of the beneficiaries are upheld and not subjected to harm. The cash transfer through telecom companies is safe and also as part of the health guidelines provided for social distancing to curb the spread of COVID-19.Cash transfer is encouraged since its used for multi-purpose, despite the primary objective is to increase food security, the families often prefers for buying nutritious and nourishing foods. The priorities of the families are upheld and most beneficiaries prefer the cash transfer compared to in-kind.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project is designed to increase the asset creation and improve production such as agricultural and skills development. The aim is to improve the production of agriculture and skills, to increase marketing of their products and to sustain themselves in the long-term.

Mitigation Measures

1. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services- KAAH will ensure that during registration and distribution access for the beneficiaries and reduce the distances of travel by ensuring CFW works and other related activities are close proximity to the settlement sites.- The organization will employ distribution security personnel to ensure no violence occurs during the distribution process. The organization will ensure that no beneficiary is subjected to violence by the staff or other affiliates of the organization and will minimize the turnout of beneficiaries to mitigate violence.- To reduce risk of exposure and spread of COVID-19, the organization will limit the number of people turnout during registration and distributions. - The organization upholds the ‘DO NO HARM’ policy and will practice to ensure beneficiaries are dignified and are treated humanely at any stage of the implementation. 2. Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance- To prevent any form of sexual exploitation and abuse, KAAH will enforce the organizational policies. - All personnel and affiliates of the organization are asked to sign the organizational PSEA policy and the code of conduct.
