Project Summary

The school meals programme aims to restore and stabilize access to education and to enhance health and nutrition status of children in targeted areas particularly areas hosting large number of affected populations, IDPs and returnees with low food security and education indicators. By encouraging regular attendance and enrollment, the programme contributes to providing protection to children from reverting to negative coping mechanism such as child labor and child marriage in addition to the education, health, and nutrition benefits. The programme meets the first objective of the education sector “Increased safe and equitable availability and access to in-person and home-based formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth”
The School meals activity includes:
- In kind food distribution including daily distribution of fresh meals or fortified snacks to children in formal education pre and primary schools during school days and to children enrolled in non-formal education, including children in Self learning centres in North East Syria camps. In addition the programme provides support to children coming from crossline and cross-border locations to sit for National exams.
- Provision of electronic vouchers to students who were out of school and have returned to education and are currently enrolled in curriculum B or nonformal education programmes. The provision of the electronic voucher is conditional to student’s attendance rate of 80%.
School meals activities will target boys and girls aged 5-15 years in formal and non-formal settings, including children with disability, who are attending the targeted schools and learning centres. The Programme is implemented in coordination with the Ministry of Education and other partners of the education sector in Syria including UN agencies and National NGOs. Considering the high needs and limited resources, the project will focus on areas with acute and immediate needs of humanitarian assistance. Targeted sub-districts will be prioritized based on food and education severity of 3 and above. In case of lower funding, priority will be given to locations where severity of food security and education of more than 4 and to newly accessible areas where education sector is picking up its activities.
The Activities are aligned with the timeframe of the school year, thus, beginning in September of the current year and ending by May of the next. Distribution of snacks may continue in the summer to support last chance camps.

4 - Needs

The education system, already fractured by 10 years of conflict, was rendered more vulnerable by COVID 19 as many schools lack the necessary infrastructure to provide safe learning environment. The Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021 shows that 6.4 million Syrian children are food insecure and 6.9 million need education humanitarian support, 94% of them live in areas with severe and catastrophic education conditions. The number of out of school children in 2020 was 2.45 million, however, this number has since increased due to a compounded effect of COVID 19, deteriorating economic situation and growing food insecurity which have driven many families to send their children to work or to marry their girls to support the household income. The key impediments to children accessing education included distance from schools and safety of roads to and from schools, poor facilities at schools and quality of education. This had negatively impacted enrolment and retention rates. On the other hand, insufficient food at home negatively affected children’s attentiveness and cognitive abilities. Severe poverty, lack of awareness, and poor education quality drove many parents and caregivers to no longer consider their child’s education a priority. Many children remain out of school to support their families. The education sector findings of 2020 show that 12% to 23% of children attending school come from families perceiving themselves as hungry while the percentage jumps to 26% to 49% among children who are not attending. This is aligned with observations from WFP field missions and visits to school where many children do not have breakfast at home or do not bring a snack with them to school. 5 - Implementation of activities

During 2021, The School Meals project will Target of 750,000 children in sub-districts with its activities with acute and immediate needs of humanitarian assistance which have score of 3 and above on food and education severity scale. The section below describes implementation for each activity:i) distribution of fortified date bars; Implemented directly by WFP and MoE schools and in non-formal education centres.-Prioritised subdistricts selected based on food security and education needs.-To minimise creating pull factor to schools, all primary schools/ centres in the sub-district will be targeted. -WFP sends monthly dispatches of snacks to target schools/ centres and then Schools will distribute 1 pack/day. ii) distribution of fresh meals-The activity will continue in eastern parts of Aleppo city, an area of high vulnerability-WFP supports cooperating partner to run healthy kitchens. The partner has daily and sustained access to areas of implementation-fresh meals are prepared and distributed daily-WFP monitors hygiene and safety measures in kitchens and distribution using monitoring checklistiii) Electronic Vouchers (cash transfer)-Vouchers provide incentive to OoSC enrolled in curriculum B or non-formal education-Families of targeted children are registered by cooperating partners -provision of voucher is conditional to achieving 80% attendance-Vouchers redeemed at retailers contracted by WFP with food and non-food items-monitoring through visits by WFP monitors WFP will support National Exams response through ready to eats, bread and date barsMonitoring of all activities process and outcomes is done by WFP monitors using data collection tools, post distribution surveys and FGD .
