Project Summary

IOM’s Protection Project for the 2024 JRP connects two strategic objectives with activities that aim to benefit the most vulnerable Rohingya and host community persons, specifically to reach older persons, widows, single-headed households, persons with disabilities, and persons with diverse sexual orientations and gender identity/expressions, and to target authorities and partners in a way that does no harm, assists based on needs, and prioritizes respect for human rights, safety, and dignity.
IOM will continue serving as the Protection Focal Point in seven camps (9,18,19,20,20 extension,24, and 25), directly implementing the activities. IOM’s protection monitoring activities will facilitate beneficiaries’ meaningful access to all humanitarian assistance, dignity, and human rights. IOM will strengthen community-based protection through community engagement, awareness raising, sensitization, case management, and specialized Protection services for individuals in need. IOM’s core counter-trafficking activities will be prevention, protection, capacity building and partnership, which will be implemented in 17 camps and 10 Union of host communities under Cox’s Bazar district. These activities are intended to reduce the trafficking incidents and risks in the camps and host communities through comprehensive activities that follow the governmental National Plan of Action. The Counter Trafficking activities will foster collaboration and increased capacity among stakeholders who respond to human trafficking cases. The aforementioned integrated approach to Protection strives to have sustainable impact and improve peaceful coexistence between various communities.
The research component within the IOM protection team is to help tailor our activities, institutionalize, and analyze the impact of our work and IOM’s general programming as a whole. The information that is gathered through the research team serves as a basis for our monitoring and evaluation to ensure that we do no harm, and our work remains relevant and culturally appropriate. As well as being aware of the constantly changing dynamics that need to be considered.
For disaster risk reduction, IOM will work with a range of partners, including other Sectors, community-based protection networks in the camps, and relevant authorities to identify Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVIs) or households needing specialized assistance and preparation before a disaster. IOM will verify the needs of EVIs, coordinate services, and support other partners to mitigate harm during disaster response. IOM will continue leading the Protection Emergency Response Units (PERU) in five IOM Protection-led camps (19,20,20extension, 24, and 25).
IOM’s protection mainstreaming activities will promote a holistic lens, incorporating the age, gender, and diversity needs of the affected population in Cox’s Bazar into programming through capacity building, training, as well as activity monitoring and modification. Finally, IOM will continue to co-chair the Sector’s Anti-Trafficking Working Group (ATWG) and be a proactive member of the Protection sector, the Protection Task Team, and other related working groups.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar continue to face formidable challenges, needing Protection and other life-saving humanitarian assistance. Among the 965,467 Rohingya population, 80% consists of girls, boys, women, and elderly men and women; 3.85% of individuals were identified with at least one specific need and 12% are persons with disabilities according to Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment - Rohingya Refugee Response (May 2021). Frequently, refugees approach protection actors to have their disability status updated in databases and to access specific support. Limited self-reliance activities render the refugees highly dependent on humanitarian assistance and with unmet needs. Violent incidents and conflict have occurred between and within Rohingya and host communities. Influential community leaders, who extort, physically punish, and psychologically coerce the refugees, grew more controlling of humanitarian services. Recent trends indicate that gang culture, petty theft, abduction and similar other crimes have risen. Gangs seemingly carried on without impunity, gaining influence and illicit profits and instilling wide-spread fear among the community. Noted by IOM protection monitoring, refugees who rented land from host community families struggled to pay increased rental fees. Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) issues were particularly vexing in Camps 19, 24 and 25, where IOM observed that families, who struggled to pay higher rents, started to send their children to work in houses, grocery shops and restaurants. Barbed wire fencing that encircles all camps restricted the unauthorized movement of refugees outside the camp, limiting the scope of work available to them. The fencing also impacted host community families, who lived inside the camps, restricting movement and fomenting dissatisfaction and evictions of refugees. IOM’s protection monitoring highlighted new needs caused by the government’s relocations of the refugees to Bhasan Char and the monsoon flooding across many camps. Affected refugees experienced family separation, disrupted humanitarian services, secondary displacement and upsetting moments. The research team builds on different research initiatives and works closely with other areas of programming to ensure that protection is mainstreamed and proper considerations are being made. The need is constantly evolving and is mainly dictated by the community. Through continuous interaction with the community and the Rohingya researchers, IOM is able to identify priority areas of intervention. There is a need to continue counter-trafficking efforts through awareness raising and capacity building of government authorities and humanitarian actors, and victim identification and assistance. Some trafficking victims identified by IOM said they left the camps because of limited livelihood opportunities. According to IOM’s case data, 44% of the caseload included Rohingya individuals and 56% of the cases were Bangladeshi individuals trafficked for mainly forced labour. Self-reliance through skills development and income-generation for Rohingya and Bangladeshi trafficked victims along with community awareness sessions and capacity building training for stakeholders are needed to address and prevent human trafficking. Community-based protection activities that promote inclusion, participation, and meaningful access, and measures to mitigate harm are necessary for Rohingya and host communities. The vulnerabilities and thus the various needs of Bangladeshi and Rohingya men, women, boys and girls require a comprehensive and fortified Protection approach while enhancing dignity and fulfillment of human rights and capacities at the individual, household and community level.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

IOM uses an integrated Protection approach to respond to the needs of vulnerable children and their families, GBV survivors, victims of trafficking, and other persons who experience harm or are at high risk. Specifically, to strengthen community-based approaches and increase meaningful access to information, protection services, and other humanitarian assistance for men, women, boys, and girls. IOM will continue to serve as the Protection focal point in Camps 9, 18, 19, 20, 20x, 24, and 25, coordinating protection activities to improve Rohingya refugees’ access to protection services and other humanitarian assistance, strengthening community-based protection mechanisms through a network of 130 Rohingya volunteers and dedicated Rohingya-led protection committees and by conducting risk mapping exercises; identifying and assisting Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVIs) and engaging with the community to raise awareness on protection risks and threats; and to enhance knowledge and skills to mitigate the risks and strengthen self-capacities. Protection monitoring activities (direct observations, key informant interviews, focus group discussions) and analysis of the ever-changing situation in the camps are the standard methods that IOM will use to collect data for protection monitoring analysis. The findings will be coordinated with the Protection Sector to compile an analysis for all camps. Documented counter trafficking cases indicate that the lack of economic opportunities, inflation and poor living conditions have exacerbated the situation of trafficking for both the host community and Rohingya community. It has led to individuals seeking opportunities outside the camp and outside Bangladesh, accepting poor work conditions, with little to no verification of the job prospect which put them at risk of trafficking. IOM counter trafficking unit aims to provide a comprehensive package to decrease the chances of re-trafficking and re-victimization.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

IOM places protection at the core of its humanitarian response. Our objective is to empower individuals with resources for resilience against future harm, while emphasizing the centrality of protection, safety, and dignity in all programming.IOM adopts gender mainstreaming as a core strategy, recognizing it as vital for accelerating progress in gender equality. All planned actions are assessed to better understand the implications for women and men and individuals from gender diverse populations. Gender mainstreaming at IOM involves explicitly addressing the priorities, needs, and contributions of individuals of all genders in all aspects of planning, implementation, and monitoring of development interventions. This approach aims to achieve gender equality, uphold women's rights and empowerment, and actively involve women and men and individuals from the SOGEISC (sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression and sex characteristics) communities in leadership and decision-making, ensuring equal influence on development directions and outcomes. Integrated protection specialists address needs for general protection, counter-trafficking, child protection, and GBV. Protection specialists also support protection mainstreaming in non-protection activities. IOM continues capacity-building sessions for protection and non-protection community volunteers, advocates, and women support groups, ensuring improved access to information and supporting informed decision-making. The protection team collaborates with non-protection components within IOM, ensuring mainstreaming in initiatives such as Cash for Work targeting vulnerable families. Additionally, efforts are made to create accessible shelters and safe spaces for gender-diverse individuals in livelihood programming. IOM is a member of the Age and Disability Technical Working Group and Gender Diverse Population Working Group. IOM is also on the SAG of the Protection Sector, GBV Sub-Sector, and CP Sub-Sector.

3.3 - Age & Disability

With Age and Diversity considerations IOM is aware of the various needs of different age groups and, thus as part of its protection monitoring activities, it seeks to discuss with elderly persons and persons with disabilities the unique challenges that they face, whether it is mobility issues, access to services, porter support, adequate specialized humanitarian agencies active in the area, involvement in cash for work/livelihood activities or being involved in the collective community decision making. To ensure child safeguarding, the IOM General Protection team works collaboratively with Child Protection Focal Points in the camps to promote a child-friendly and accessible environment for all. IOM seeks to hear a diverse range of voices from the community and is responsive to its needs. The interventions planned under this project are identified through a consultative process where different needs, capacities, and priorities of diverse people like person with disabilities, LGBTIQ community and others are considered, and the assistance is targeted to the persons and groups most in need. IOM actively participates in the Age and Disability Working Group (ADWG) and Gender Diverse Population Working Group (GDPWG) providing practical inputs and briefings on IOM’s engagement in projects and work to ensure inclusion of person with disabilities and gender diverse population.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

IOM's Protection programming prioritizes its community-based and participatory approaches, which aim to be responsive to a diversity of needs and ensure inclusive participation. IOM is actively engaged in seeking feedback from the affected population at all stages of the project implementation cycle to gain insights into emerging risks and threats and to shape how activities are developed and carried out. IOM maintains vigilant monitoring of the protection environment and activities within the camps to prevent any harm resulting from our initiatives and to ensure the safe implementation of multi-sectoral programs. Further, IOM will continue to conduct gender and age responsive Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) surveys and other types of response monitoring through structured questionnaires, Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) desks, case management feedback surveys, and regular focus group discussions (FGDs) and consultations at the community level, including with individuals with disabilities and those with diverse gender identities, including those with diverse sexual orientations and sex characteristics, are utilized to maintain a community-driven and ""do-no-harm"" approach. IOM has a designated and dedicated PSEA Officer to support and address the concerns of the beneficiaries. IOM has a well-established PSEA hotline, online platform, feedback boxes within all facilities for the community for providing feedback. IOM also has appointed PSEA champions from the staff in all IOM AoR camps who regularly provide capacity sessions to the volunteers, community-based support groups to ensure that are well informed about the reporting mechanism.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

IOM is committed to ongoing efforts related to combat PSEA. IOM has a designated and dedicated PSEA Officer to support and address the concerns of the beneficiaries. IOM will continue to roll out PSEA training packages and offer Training of Trainers (ToT) sessions for PSEA Champions in the coming year. This is intended to ensure that IOM staff, implementing partner staff, and volunteers adhere to the PSEA guiding principles and mandatory reporting systems. IOM has a robust reporting mechanism (hotline, online platform) in place and will continue to actively participate in the PSEA network while providing support to implementing partners (IPs) and the Inter Sector Coordination Group (ISCG) in terms of capacity building and advocacy. IOM provides PSEA training to partners and ensure that partners have a PSEA policy and established reporting mechanism in place. IOM regularly delivers adapted PSEA training packages for the community volunteers, women support groups, and other community-based groups. IOM also disseminates IEC materials on PSEA to the community to ensure their understanding and accessibility on reporting is in place. Finally, IOM and WFP – in partnership with Translators without Borders had launched PSEA at the Frontline, a campaign to further empower frontline humanitarian workers and partners on PSEA through a comprehensive package of communication and outreach materials.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

IOM plays a pivotal role in enhancing disaster preparedness and emergency response. IOM contributes to the inter-agency Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU). Further IOM Protection staff support the Mobile Medical Teams (MMTs). Prior to emergencies, such as cyclones, IOM protection units identify EVIs in need of additional support or relocation because of disaster-related risks and conduct outreach to develop and support individual and household preparedness and response plans. Following a disaster, IOM Protection staff are experienced in, and trained to, facilitate the restoration of beneficiary access to humanitarian services. Protection staff also vigilantly monitor protection trends, including shifts in the human trafficking landscape, to identify and address emerging threats and risks. IOM maintains close coordination with Site Management and the Camp-in-Charge (CiC) offices and provide support to Disaster Management Unit (DMU) volunteers, offering training on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and safe referrals for protection issues, such as gender-based violence (GBV). IOM also regularly disseminates Protection Key Messages, with the aim of helping the community mitigate the impact of disasters before, during, and after they occur. This effort includes house-to-house visits conducted by trained volunteers to ensure that individuals with disabilities and other vulnerable populations receive the necessary support. IOM endeavors to maintain an environmentally friendly approach, including by limiting paper and plastic waste. Institutionally the organization is committed to climate friendly programming, including the use of treated bamboo for shelters and offices as well as programming that prevents deforestation.
