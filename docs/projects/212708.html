Project Summary

The worsening economic situation in Syria, ongoing effects of conflict, and limited access to essential services are contributing to widespread humanitarian needs throughout the country. A significant portion of households are struggling to fulfil basic requirements such as housing, food, and healthcare, impacting more than 70% of families. As of September 2023, the average Minimum Expenditure Basket for a family of five has risen to SYP 2,198,000, representing a nearly two-fold increase over nine months and a quadrupling over two years. Factors such as the devaluation of the currency, lack of employment opportunities, inadequate services, and limited access to electricity are collectively exacerbating economic stress, diminishing urban activity, causing severe currency devaluation, high inflation rates, and escalating affordability challenges within the society.
The project is centered on Enhance skills and access to sustainable livelihood opportunities for the most affected and vulnerable people. This will be achieved by offering small grants to a group of women, men and youth to initiate new enterprises, as well as by creating temporary employment opportunities for the most vulnerable demographics including workers and supervisors through the removal and disposal of solid waste from cities. Also, by installing a solar power system at elderly care facility to enhance the quality of life for those individuals, who are grappling with diverse health issues and in-need to access electricity, lighting and hot water. In addition to project aims to support group of people by training them on life skills to be active and able to create an impact to individuals and society. ERL project prioritizes the rights and safety of vulnerable individuals, including women, girls, young boys, and the elderly. Gender equity is promoted, and measures are in place to protect against PSEA. A code of conduct and Complaint Response Mechanism are implemented to address these issues. Data collection tools consider gender and age, and community participation efforts incorporate gender considerations. EPDC aims to hire a gender-balanced team and collects protection-related data disaggregated by sex and age. Child protection and safeguarding standards are upheld, and a safe environment for children is ensured. A comprehensive risk assessment is conducted, and mitigation measures are implemented for different age groups. Monitoring, evaluation, and beneficiary feedback are utilized to assess services. The project follows the principle of 'Do no harm' and promotes inclusive participation. Measures are implemented during distributions to prevent PSEA, including gender-specific queues and priority lines. External risks encompass factors such as conflict, insecurity, international sanctions, ongoing instability, potential devaluation of the Syrian currency, and unforeseen events like the resurgence of COVID-19 and earthquakes. Mitigation strategies for these risks entail conducting security assessments, maintaining close communication with local authorities, identifying alternative routes, advocating for streamlined fund transfers, and closely monitoring prices and market conditions. Risks associated with interventions and the organization itself include heightened violence against women and girls, gender-based discrimination and marginalization, restricted access to information and services for women, obstacles in resource access, instances of extortion and embezzlement, inadequate accountability and complaints mechanisms, and exclusion of vulnerable groups. Recommended measures to reduce these risks involve designing inclusive responses, engaging in participatory activities, demonstrating cultural sensitivity, conducting gender analyses, providing staff training, implementing internal controls, establishing robust complaints mechanisms, applying transparent selection criteria, coordinating with relevant stakeholders, and enforcing accountability mechanisms.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

ERL project focuses at first, on distributing cash grants to affected and vulnerable women, men and youth in Shaghour Barani (Dwelaa) in Damascus, Big Hmeimeh in Aleppo and Khan Arnaba in Quneitra to support their business or start new ones. The initiative commences with the publication of an advertisement, followed by a registration process to identify and select the BNFs based on vulnerability and program criteria. Selected BNFs will undergo business development training courses, with final selection being made by a committee of trainers and specialists. The distribution phase will be initiated by notifying beneficiaries of the distribution schedule via text messages through transfer companies, and the distribution process will be supervised by EPDC program, MEAL team, and volunteers. Second, project will also focus under the CFW activity on providing temporary employment opportunities and short-term income for the most vulnerable population, such as IDPs, women and population in Jaramana in Rural Damascus specially after the crisis and displacement. Resulting in an increase in population density, therefore, inability of municipalities, lack of garbage collection mechanisms and janitors to cover the whole area, in addition to the spread of diseases. The project will start with the publication of an advertisement, registration by Kobo upon selection criteria, contracting with cleaning companies, purchasing and distributing the equipment and will include activities that contains sweeping and collecting waste spread on public and sub-roads and accumulated in and around garbage containers and transporting it from its locations to approved waste collection dumps. Third, providing electricity in the elderly home in Syriac Quarter in Aleppo by installing solar system, taking advantage of the existing roof, where solar panels will be installed on it and provided with batteries and inverters to secure the necessary power for lighting. This energy source not only offers numerous financial and long-term advantages, but it is also an environmentally responsible option. Fourth, activity will take place in Daraa, especially the country side, in Nawa, Al-Sanamayn and its neighboring areas. Work will focus on training local people on life skills and qualifying them to be the first who implement this funded initiative to create an impact on the individual and society. A full program will be implemented for work groups and youth ones, they will be trained through a focused courses that includes planning and follow-up, first and foremost, financial management, effective communication skills, etc. the project will emphasize on the presence and empowerment of women, gender discrimination, and GBV. Implementation of all projects follows established SOPs, including planning, reporting, procurement, and quality assurance. Continuous monitoring and evaluation are conducted to assess the effectiveness of interventions and gather feedback from stakeholders.
