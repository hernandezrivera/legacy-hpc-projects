Project Summary

Political situation like the current fighting and economic crisis, diseases outbreak, and communal conflicts have largely weighed down the resilience and social stability of many communities in Sudan, particularly women and children in Central Darfur and River Nile state faced day to day risk increment on Child Protection such as gender-based violence (GBV), UXOs/explosion and other child rights violations.
The project will focus on addressing multiple vulnerabilities during the crisis, strengthening local capacities for effective and inclusive basic services delivery systems and enhancing local government and promote community engagement through engaging community members in local child protection systems like CBCPN and SW. The project mainly target the locality of zalingei, Mukjar, and wadi salih (villages; Kordol, Amer Jedit, Baga, Damber, Korley and Kuturi), Kosti, Rabak- White Nile State, Shendi, Atbara, Al Damar and AL Matama -River Nile State.
To attain sustainability, the program will support interventions that are relevance to needs and context, promoting ownership by community and local government by leveraging community resources and structures.
JMCO aim to establish Community-Based Child Protection Networks (CBCPN) with more focuses in inclusive youth/adolescent groups; sustainability of the child protection interventions, enhancing local government and promoting community engagement for strengthening child protection; recruit and provide capacity building -through conducting and organizing of TOT training to social workers at each cluster to provide support on family tracing and reunification (FTR, Referral pathways, and PSS,GBV) process. Also directly improve water and sanitation hygiene in the three states (Central Darfur, White Nile and River Nile) by upgrading mini water yards, rehablitating hand pumps and hand dug wells to provide cleaning water for domestic and aquatic use. As well as emberk on community based HH hygiene messaging and campaigns through community mobilizers to enhance creation of health and sanitation awareness in the communities.

1 - Needs

The Children Call Quick Intervention Central Darfur State-CCQICDWNRNS project will address the urgent needs of Gender Based Violence, CFS, PSS, UXO/explosives among the communities of Kordol, Amer Jedit, Baga, Damber, Korley and Kuturi in Central Darfur kosti, Rabak and Atbara, Shendi, Ad damar and Al matama in River Nile state and work on strengthening the available Child Protection systems at grass root level

2 - Activities/Outputs

The CCQICDWNRNS project will provide; support the established Community-Based Child Protection Networks- CBCPN, enabling youth / adolescent group participation, provide TOT training to social workers at each target localities to support on family tracing and reunification (FTR, Referral pathways, and PSS, GBV), Train community-based child protection networks- CBCPN, including youth / adolescent groups on (FTR, Referral pathways, and PSS, GBV) by the trained social workers, Establishment, support and equip the State Family and Child Protection Unit – FCPU enabling provision of Child protection services, Establishment, support child right clubs and build their capacity and knowledge on child rights, Establishment and support of community-based organization for children with disabilities, provision of Public GBV/PSEA awareness in the target localities through loud speakers and comedy, Equipping the local CP networks by supporting Social workers with communications and data collection materials (smart phone) to easy and quicken children rights violation and incidence of UXO/explosion for quicker response by protection actors, Identification and supporting of HH with specific needs with cash assistance to respond to protection needs, Provision of professional mental health group-based health and PSS services, Provision of legal assistance to affected IDPs in the 10 locations, Issuing of legal document -birth registration certificates to infants through out the project locations, water, sanitation and hygiene
