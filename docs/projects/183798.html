Project Summary

This project is aimed to provide humanitarian life-saving child protection assistance for children and adolescents along with GBV response during 2022. The project targets five states including North Darfur (Melit, Tawilla, Daresalam, Kutum, Kabkabya, Saraf Omra localities); South Kordofan (Kadogli, Talodi and Abojubbha localities); Kassala (Reifi Shamal Ad Delta and Reifi Hamashkureib); White Nile (El Salam, Guli, Al Jabalain, AD Diwaim and localities) and in Khartoum in (Khartoum (Mayo) and Um Bada localities). The child protection response target 10,442 people out of which (2350 girls, 2350 boys, 2872 women, and 2870 men), and will focus on prevention and response to risks affecting girls and boys among IDPs, refugees, returnees and vulnerable affected people and enhance their access to life-saving services and systems in targeted areas. Interventions will include community education/ awareness raising, service provision e.g. case management and family tracing, and community group activities. These will be delivered in line with Minimum Standards of Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (CPMS). Linkages will be made between formal and informal systems and structures to enhance coordinated response and operationalize community feedback and response mechanisms and accountabilities among actors. Children with special needs will be given special attention across the activities. Resilience building will be a key component during the interventions beside promotion of life skills, peace building conflict transformation. Ultimately, the response will be in close coordination with service providers and child protection actors, as well as with child protection subsector mechanisms.
The Gender Based Violence (GBV) response is planned in same localities, and is working to prevent and respond sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) that affect girls and boys within IDPs, refugees, returnees and vulnerable affected host communities, targeting 4,41 people 4(807 girls, 807 boys, 1555 women, and 1245 men), and enhance their access to life-saving GBV response services and systems including clinical management of Rape (CMR) in targeted areas. Activities will include supporting running costs e.g. for women’s centre), trainings to strengthen capacity, empower children, and particularly young women with life skills and recreation activities, and empower groups of girls and women with income generating activities and vocational skills. This will be delivered based on survival centered approach, and the minimum standards for GBV in emergency programing. Plan will work to enhance linkages among GBV actors improve coordination, collaboration, and systems strengthening. The same CFRM will be used report and respond SEA issues and ensuring accountability to affected population through different and workable means based on areas of intervention. The intervention will promote gender equality and inclusion to challenge discriminations against women then reduce exploitation and violence among women and girls and support them to voice out their issue and call for their rights, this will be aligned with life skills and vocational training and peace building conflict transformation programing to maximize the impact and ensure developmental nexus to the intervention.

1 - Needs

According to 2022 HNO projection, about 3.2 million girls and boys are in need humanitarian assistance, this because of the impact of ongoing conflicts, displacement, floods, sexual violence, recruitment, disease outbreaks, grinding poverty, repeated displacement, inflicting high levels of life-altering traumatic injuries and stress, and limited capacity of response among stakeholders. In North Darfur there is continued clashes between SLA/AW and Sudan Jameel resulted in continued insecurity in areas around Sortony close to Tawilla locality; the intercommunal violence in Tawilla and Shangli Tobaya, has resulted into incidents of GBV reported during the attacks. South Kordofan is more similar to ND due to conflict; no it is started community movement between the government and SPLA controlled area, but stills diverse needs occur; during Plan’s visit to Talodi before the raining season there are about 5 basic schools founded (1 boys, 1 girls & 3 mixed) accommodating 1853 students 919 boys and 934 girls, the situations in these schools, are poor education and child protection environment, class needs rehabilitation, no water sources no latrine particularly for girls. In both areas, adolescents are exposed to new forms of violence, boys are more exposed to drug abuse, recruited and life threating due to engagement honor killing or injured, drop out of school due to economic hardship or involved in child labor. Girls are more exposed to negative harmful coping mechanisms within families, married early and exposed to FGM; thus, both need awareness and support on appropriate ways of reporting SEA issues. Boys and girls in Kassala, White Nile, and Khartoum targeted localities are much impacted by the economic situation which hardly hits the poor families and push them to negative coping mechanisms; it was accumulated by existing gaps on services and capacity of local systems to respond to the needs including strengthening, linking and equip community-based child protection networks (CBCPNs) with service providers and government partners. New IDPs and returnees and newly appointed government actors need capacity building in child rights, protection/care, gender equality, peace building and reconciliations to help community coexistence. Adolescents find themselves in a very gloomy situation and in need of life skills, and support to access protection services, education, awareness and recreational activities. The formal and informal are working in isolation due to existing political and social fragmentation accumulated by the poverty and increase of livelihood costs, so they in need support to joined efforted and provide comprehensive support to vulnerable children, families and communities and work to complement each other in all areas. GBV is a key concern in targeted localities particularly among displaced communities around Tawilla, the cases are underreported due to a culture of denial, social stigma, fear and stigmatization. There is limited knowledge on referral pathway which is either weak or not functional, gaps of knowledge on the importance of reporting and support to GBV survivors. The law enforcement and justice system are week, social workers need training on cases management including linkages with clinics to ensure survivors access to CMR, women centers and CBCPN are managed in intermitted approach, with continues turnover among members, they need to be revived, trained and equipped to be more functional. Adolescent girls lacking life skills to deal with their body including knowledge to deal with mistrial hygiene, SRHR, recreational activities; so, they need continuation of life skills activities started in 2021; vulnerable girls need to access dignity kit to reduce the chance of exploitation. Some of young adolescents were engaged in early marriage and get divorced and they need opportunities for income generating to depend on themselves based on local opportunitie.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The CP activities in ND, SK, and KRT include: Establish/Strengthen and Equip 36 CBCPNs (180 female and 180 male) and train them on prevention and response to CP concerns, raise awareness in 50 communities targeting (1050 men, 1050 women, 700 boys and 700 girls) 70 per session. link CBCPN with social workers and SCCW and other service providers, Train 60 local CSO staff including (30 women, 30 men) on child protection risk’s mitigation, support peacebuilding and conflicts resolution; Train 50 duty bearers (local authorities) newly appointed government at local level including peace signatory group (25 female &25 male) on national and international legal formwork, CP/Rights The GBV activities in ND, SK, and KRT include: support provision of GBV, and case management of 50 children, capacitate partner on Plan’s approach on child survivors of SGBV support targeting 60 social workers including GBV actors to strengthen coordination GBV at sub-national level and survivors received appropriate support; train 150 (75 female &75 male) CSO and duty bearers (local authorities) on GBV prevention and referral mechanism, gender equality; support state level GBV meetings of 40 members and provide working aids. Peace building activities in ND and SK include: Conduct conflict analysis and joint community consultations on participatory formation of joint peace committees that is community managed with participation of CBCPN to perform conflict resolution with representation of adolescents, youth, and women in the main structure; train 150 committee members (75 women, 75 men) in conflict analysis skills, mediation & conflicts resolutions to promote peaceful co-existence; support quarterly intercommunal dialogues for community -to community peace engagement; Promote a call for youth creative peace building initiatives for application of 10 top competitive community engagement initiatives; Conduct 4 cultural and sport tournaments between refugees and IDPs & host communities The CP activities in Kassala and WN include; Provision of case management and FTR for 100 (50 boys, 50 girls); Training on case management for 45 social/case workers; ToT for 20 community volunteers; Conduct life skill sessions for 140 adolescents groups (430 male/430 female); Conduct training on PSS for 40 people (15 CFSs animators, 12 teachers, 13 community volunteers; 50% female); Establishment and train 4 CBCPN; 40 members (20 female, 20 male) of CBOs and; Conduct 36 awareness-raising sessions targeting (400 boys, 400 girls, 167 women, 167 men) on CP, prevention of separation, monitoring, reporting, and responding to at risk children (30 per session); initiate community consultation sessions on Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) design and implementing it, PSEA, train 70 CSO staff (35 men, 35 women) on the CFRM; conduct parenting sessions for 20 parent groups (30 male/30 female); train 38 people (19 male/ 19 female) including animators, teachers and community volunteers on PSS; update referral pathway with 20 stakeholders (10 men and 10 women). GBV activities in WN and Kassala include; Establishment of 8 women groups (20 per group) train and equip them on child protection, GBV, monitoring and response to local GBV/SEA cases; 84 awareness-raising sessions on GBV and SEA in 9 communities targeting 5880 people (2000 women, 2000 men, 940 boys and 940 girls ); Identify, train, and equip 150 girls and women who are GBV survivors, child mothers, and at risk of GBV and place them in vocational training skills; support locality level advocacy meeting targeting 50 government staff (25 male/ 25 female); Update referral pathway for GBV Survivors and service mapping; Train 90 social workers and CBO/CSO members (45 men & 45 women) on PSEA, PSS including CMR, GBV, safeguarding and Code of Conduct; Establish linkages informal and formal system i.e. (women’s groups, CBCPNs, and relevant actors).

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Safety and dignity will be prioritized to avoid causation of further harm and prevention and minimizing unintended negative effects or risks during the intervention; meaning access will be ensured through supporting people’s access to assistance and services in proportion to needy groups and without barriers of any discrimination; more attention will be given to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulties to access assistance and services; existing CRFM will utilized to have two way communication and acting upon concerns which could be measured by the affected population in regard to its adequacy and addressing raised concerns and complaints; diverse capacity building will be provided to communities to assist them claim their rights, including those related to other services. So, the project focuses on enabling factors to mainstream child protection through conduction of safety audit to ensure do no harm and participation of different groups of female and male and empower women to be seen and voice out their issues. In this project the most vulnerable children including disabled children will be prioritized and supported including vulnerable children and those with child protection concerns to reduce the associated risks and discrimination

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Plan International is a member of PSEA Network and already received leaflets and posters to disseminate knowledge and prevention of PSEA. in addition this will reinforce Plan's own safeguarding children and young people policy and prevention of Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse policy (SHPEA) through implementation and expansion of existing child-friendly feedback and complaints mechanism. As part of the GBV SAG, the CO will lead and actively participate in the PSEA Network to collaborate on advocacy and information sharing on high-risk areas. The organization will maintain consistent attendance and participation in the AAP/CEA Working Group. Plan International continues to support and will feed into the GBV hotline working with CVAW, and make use of the UNCT/ UNOPS collective call center to ensure issues of accountability to affected populations are dealt with. Within the project locations, Plan will ensure contribution to mainstreaming initiatives led by UNOCHA. Plan will collect information from beneficiaries through CFRM currently operational in some targeted communities and will continue strengthening the systems that ensure do-no-harm principals, thus including provision of capacity to staff, partners and community segments and structures and filling gaps on PSEA focal point in all operational area.
