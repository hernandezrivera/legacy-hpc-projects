Project Summary

The proposed response aims to address the immediate protection needs of the Beirut Lebanese, migrant and refugee population affected by the crisis, with a particular focus on women and girls, who are disproportionately affected by GBV risks at and other protection challenges in times of crisis. Emergency needs for specialized support have emerged in the aftermath of the disaster, along with continued support necessary to meet their mental health, sexual and reproductive health, protection and dignity/menstrual hygiene needs. ACTED will in particular focus on the following activities:
- Protection mainstreaming and community participation targeting via participatory gender analysis, GBV/PSEA awareness and communication;
- Update service mapping and referral pathways (covering also beneficiaries of other life-saving services under Shelter/WASH and Food Security assistance);
- Psychosocial first aid, GBV case management, and quality mental health and psychosocial support to SGBV survivors and women and girls at risk, with attention to a vulnerable population (adolescent girls, people with disabilities, older people, LGBTI people);
- Establish and support female-friendly spaces for vulnerable women and girls of all ages for integrated services and SGBV prevention and response;
This programme aims to provide comprehensive and area-based assistance, complementing other ACTED implemented/planned intervention across other sectors (such as Food Security, Shelter, WASH and Health) and will target zones 71-74 and 104-110 in Bourj Hammoud, zones 66-69 in Basta el-Tahta and zones 6-11 in Karantina where ACTED’s historical presence and long term programming in the target areas made it one of the first and key responders to provide immediate relief to the Beirut Port Explosion.
Moreover, ACTED will also ensure that the relevant referral pathways are also shared with beneficiaries of another type of assistance (shelter rehabilitation, cash-based assistance etc.) which might bring the total number of beneficiaries above the activity target and will overlap with other sectors. ACTED will also closely coordinate with other protection actors such as ABAAD, UNWomen etc. to make sure that their services and information (such as planned PSEA hotline and awareness-raising) are also streamlined across ACTED’s programming and disseminated via community networks.
Based on current ongoing programming in partnership with the National Mental Health Program under the Ministry of Public Health, ACTED has already developed material to provide a community-based non-clinical mental health and psychosocial support to distressed individuals, including modules related to Psychological First Aid. Since 2019, ACTED has been conducting GBV awareness and PSS sessions with vulnerable women and adolescent girls in Tripoli and Beirut in partnership with UN Women and UNFPA, reaching a total of 350 beneficiaries in less than 10 months. Moreover, ACTED’s trained social workers are experienced in mainstreaming community-based protection mechanisms through the establishment of women support groups and promotion of participatory gender analysis/advocacy activities led by women and girls. With an established presence in the underserved neighbourhoods of Greater Beirut since 2013, ACTED has conducted several protection interventions in these areas which have granted the organization an extensive knowledge of key stakeholders in this complex urban environment. This allowed ACTED to immediately start-up operations and deliver on-the-spot emergency relief in the aftermath of the disaster while ensuring ongoing coordination with local and international actors active in the response.

1 - Needs

The Beirut Port explosions had significant consequences on the most vulnerable Lebanese and non-Lebanese communities, already struggling to cope with existing challenges, including the COVID-19 pandemic and the socio-economic crisis. As a result of the blast, several sectors of Greater Beirut are affected, some of which are home to vulnerable, poor, migrant workers or refugee communities, all of whom had already been experiencing a mounting economic contraction in recent years.An estimated 300,000 people are displaced as a result of the explosion, approximately 81,000 of whom are women of reproductive age (15 - 49 years), including 24,000 adolescent girls. The destruction of homes is expected to increase the risks of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) for women and girls. Thousands of women and girls are residing in damaged houses, temporary relocation sites or shared shelters with multiple unrelated families. Privacy and dignity may be undermined, and SGBV risks are expected to increase due to inadequate and insecure living conditions. These risks of GBV were already on the rise before the incident due in large part to the COVID-19 pandemic and its resultant restrictions on movement, which in turn caused a notable rise in reported GBV incidence, notably domestic violence, and a growing sense of insecurity among women and girls. A recent survey showed that 54 per cent of women and girls interviewed across the country observed an increase in harassment, violence, or abuse, with 44 per cent reporting feeling less safe in their homes and 57 per cent less safe in their communities since the start of the pandemic (UNFPA, GBV Advocacy Brief, August 2020).The current crisis will probably exacerbate pre-existing risks and result in emergency needs for specialised support for women and girls. Moreover, initial reports indicate that mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) needs are acute among vulnerable groups impacted by the blast. The explosion has reignited buried memories of previous traumatic events, whether related to previous episodes of violence (civil war for older residents, the July 2006 war, the Syrian war for Syrian refugees) or personal trauma. According to a Rapid Assessment conducted by HelpAge/DORCAS in the aftermath of the disaster, 26% of respondents felt that their mental health was a major health concern, with a higher percentage of Syrian respondents being affected (32%). Women, the elderly and people with specific needs (chronically ill, persons with a disability, elderly headed households) will remain particularly in need of protection assistance across all affected locations.

2 - Activities

ACTED’s Protection Response to the Beirut Explosions Emergency will contribute to both objectives of the Protection Sector, namely:a) Ensure protection mainstreaming and community participation, via:1. Protection mainstreaming and community participation 1.1. ACTED will facilitate participatory gender analysis with the community members, through assessment of gender and age-segregated groups. At the same time, community focal points (women who are historic residents in the neighbourhood and have good outreach among community members) will be supported in the delivery of community-based protection activities. 1.2. ACTED will establish WhatsApp communication tree groups, managed by community focal points, to spread GBV and PSEA key messages and service maps, and disseminate information available on SGBV services, as well as on COVID-19 specific SGBV risks and mitigation measures (including IEC material). 1.3. ACTED will establish GBV watch groups (helping other women and girls in the neighbourhood, through peer to peer support in identifying and referring cases in need of protection services) to offer immediate support to GBV survivors and refer them to relevant service providers, to reinforce or create a strong sense of community in the area, amongst affected populations. 2. Update service mapping and referral pathways (covering also beneficiaries of other life-saving services under Shelter/WASH and Food Security assistance – see ACTED appeal for Shelter, FS and WASH for reference)2.1 ACTED’s social workers will regularly ensure to update relevant inter-coordination tools including service mapping and referral pathways. All tools will be disseminated among the target population either via WhatsApp or distribution of printed materials.2.2 ACTED will conduct ToT for community focal points and front liners of local organizations that are responding to the crisis across all sectors, to streamline basic GBV concepts, and train them on PFA as well as on the identification of GBV cases and referral pathways. b) Provide appropriate and efficient protection services:2. Provide psychosocial first aid, GBV case management, and quality mental health and psychosocial support to SGBV survivors and women and girls at risk, with attention to a vulnerable population (adolescent girls, people with disabilities, older people, LGBTI people) 2.1 ACTED will conduct remote MHPSS sessions in the target areas (PFA, MHPSS GBV prevention-focused sessions dedicated to increasing the capacity for resilience and identification of positive coping mechanisms for the women and girls affected by the Beirut blast). 2.2. ACTED will offer PFA and individual counselling sessions to any women and girls who are referred through the community support groups (Activity 1.3) and/or who will visit the ACTED tent in the target locations. 3. Establish and support female-friendly spaces for vulnerable women and girls of all ages for integrated services and SGBV prevention and response ACTED will support an identified local partner in Karantina or/and Bourj Hammoud that has an established WGGS to either 1) refurbish/conduct repairs of the space following the explosion, or 2) contribute to the running costs of the space.
