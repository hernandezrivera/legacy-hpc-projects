Project Summary

This project focuses on needs-based activities that support the health of Rohingya Refugees and vulnerable Bangladeshis in Cox’s Bazar. MedGlobal will provide direct health services at the Health Post-040 in camp 4, capacity building for local health care providers, and community health worker services including case follow-up and public health education within camp 4. The outputs of activities will be quantified through a data collection tool, with reports made available to the Health Sector. The project also plans to equip other facilities in the camps with critical medical supplies and equipment, and to advocate for local needs in local and global forums. All activities will be planned in collaboration with the Health Sector in order to ensure that they align with Health Sector priorities and other health activities in camp 4. The following measures will be undertaken to ensure accountability: (1) a logic model planning process (ongoing), which was based on needs assessment and baseline surveys that aim to include the community; (2) thorough audits and budgets to ensure financial accountability; (3) monitoring and evaluation to ensure program quality. This proposal considers gender and age equity. MedGlobal’s work at the health post focuses on 60% women and includes nutrition and maternity care for young mothers plus family planning. Gender and age are included in data reporting. In 2021, MedGlobal will be conducting needs assessments with consideration of women and different age groups, and we will work to integrate these results into our next logic model cycle. In 2020 we implemented several COVID 19 capacity projects to help the Upazila Health Complex with Central Oxygen System and other medical equipments and supplies to health facilities at FDMN community and we implemented project in partnership with local partners around 175,000 USD. We included the all the activities for host and FDMN together in the budget line of 2021 including the annual Helping Babies Breath (HBB) and point of care ultrasound (POCUS) training course in collaboration with the Health Sector.

1 - Identified Needs

Improved access to health services for Rohingya refugees including: general primary care, NCD services, well-child care, SRHR and ANC services, nursing emergency care and referral services, and mental health services. Continuing medical education for local health care providers tailored to the local context including disease burden, social and cultural considerations, and available resources. Education for Rohingya refugee community members to improve health knowledge and practices and health-seeking behavior. Data collection to quantify outcomes of implemented activities and to guide quality improvement efforts. Advocacy for challenges faced by Rohingya refugee and vulnerable Bangladeshi communities in Cox’s Bazar.
