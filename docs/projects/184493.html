Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Somalia remains critical due to the ongoing impacts of drought, conflict, locust infestation and the COVID 19 pandemic. In the recent months of 2021, most parts of the country have been experiencing severe drought conditions. This drought conditions were triggered by consecutive seasons of below-average rainfall including the below average October to December 2020 Deyr rainfall season, the above average temperatures during the January to March 2021 harsh Jilaal/dry season as well as the significantly below average April to June Gu/long rains. FSNAU and FEWS NET Post-Gu Assessment in September 2021 projected that nearly 3.5 million people in Somalia would be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) through at least December 2021, in the absence of humanitarian assistance. This includes 1.1 million Internally Displaced Persons who will face moderate to large food consumption gaps. In addition, 3.7 million people are classified in IPC phase 2 (Stressed) engaging in negative coping strategies to meet their food and non-food needs, bringing the total number of people in Somalia experiencing acute food insecurity to 7.2 million by late 2021.
The worst affected region is the Jubaland state particularly Afmadow district which is currently on alarm phase of drought that has led to depletion of pasture and water. Water pans and dams in the district have dried up thus increasing pressure on the few existing boreholes, crop have failed and substantial livestock mortality have been reported in the recent months. Prices of basic food commodities have also been on the rise. A large proportion of the populations in the drought affected district are currently dependent on small scale emergency water trucking interventions initiated by the local community, diaspora community and the humanitarian organizations operating in the region. The food security situation in the district is expected to further deteriorate due to the impacts of the drought. Currently, an estimated 46,640 people including 780 internally displaced persons are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Emergency (IPC Phase 4) across the target district according to the Food Security Cluster overall targets for 2022 by district.
It is against this background that WASDA is proposing to undertake integrated food security and livelihood support project whose main objective is to improve the immediate food security needs 12,000 drought and conflict affected pastoralist and agro-pastoralist households in Afmadow district, Lower Juba. The specific activities to be undertaken under this project will be multipurpose un-conditional cash grants to 800 households (4,800 individuals) and cash for work (CFW) to 400 vulnerable households (2,400 individuals) affected by drought. The cash will be disbursed for two cycles of three months each and will follow the Cash Working Group recommendation on the minimum expenditure basket for vulnerable households in the Lower Juba. The will UCT will target 70% women with specific consideration to female-headed households, those with children under five and elderly women, 20% persons with disabilities (PWDs) as well as 10 % vulnerable men. Since the CFW is labour intensive, the activity will target 70% men and 30% women from vulnerable households. The households participating in the CFW will be expected to work for 3 to 4 hours a day, for 18 days a month. With full consultation of the local communities, the Cash for work (CFW) will be used to rehabilitate community owned infrastructure such as water pans, small irrigation canals, shallow wells and feeder roads. To protect and safeguard livelihoods assets, the proposed project will support 500 pastoralist households (3,000 individuals) who have lost their livestock assets as a result of the drought with restocking and 300 agro-pastoral households (1,800 individuals) practicing micro-irrigation with training on Good Agricultural practices and basic farming inputs.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1.1: Project launch - WASDA will organize inception meeting with key stakeholders to sensitize about the project. WASDA will share project information including project objectives, expected results, activities and target population to build rapport with the community and key stakeholders.Activity 1.2: Selection and registration of households targeted for UCT, CFW, restocking and basic agricultural inputs – WASDA team in collaboration with Village Relief committees (VRCs) will use pre-defined beneficiary selection criteria to conduct selection and registration of vulnerable households. Through this, 800 households (4,800 individuals) will be targeted for un-conditional cash grants, 400 households (2,400 individuals) for cash for work, 500 households (3,000 individuals) who have lost their livestock assets with restocking and 300 agro-pastoral households (1,800 individuals) with training on Good Agricultural practices and basic farming inputs. Registration will be done using Kobo Collect software.Activity 1.3: Provision of un-conditional cash grants (UCG) to 800 drought affected households - WASDA will provide un-conditional cash grants to 800 vulnerable households (4,800 individuals). The cash will be disbursed for two cycles of three months where targeted households will receive a cash value of USD 90 per month as per the CWG recommendation on the MEB for vulnerable households in Lower Juba. The will cash grant will target 70% women with specific consideration to female-headed households, those with children under five and elderly women, 20% PWDs as well as 10 % vulnerable men. The cash transfer will be facilitated by Hormuud communications limited through beneficiaries' Electronic Virtual Cash (EVC). Activity 1.4: Provision of Cash for work to 400 (2,400 individuals) drought affected households - WASDA will provide cash for work to 400 households (2,400 individuals). The cash will be disbursed for two cycles of three months where households will receive cash value of US$75 per month for 3 months. Considering CFW is labour intensive, the activity will target 70% men and 30% women. The households participating in CFW will be expected to work for 3 to 4 hours a day for 18 days a month. The Cash for work (CFW) will be used to rehabilitate community owned infrastructure such as water pans, small irrigation canals as well as feeder roads. WASDA will provide hand tools and protective gears to the CFW participants.Activity 1.5: Livestock restocking to 500 vulnerable households - To protect livestock assets, the proposed project will support 500 pastoralist households (3,000 individuals) who have lost their livestock assets through devastating drought with healthy and productive livestock (10 female goats). Prior to distribution, WASDA with support of Jubaland ministry of Agriculture will vaccinate the animals. The animals will be procured from the local markets through local livestock traders. Activity 1.6: Provision of basic agricultural inputs to 300 (1,800 individuals) farming households - To improve agricultural production, the project will provide one-off basic agricultural inputs to 300 farming households practicing small scale farming through irrigation. The inputs will be in line with the FAO and cluster farm input standards. The agricultural inputs that will be provided to households will include certified seeds, irrigation equipment such as water pumps, watering cans, hose pipes as well as farm tools such as spade, shovel, hoe, pruner and rake. Activity 1.7: Training of 300 farming households on Good Agricultural practices – Through this project, WASDA in collaboration with Ministry of agriculture will train 300 farming households (1,800 individuals) on Good Agricultural practices including cultivation, management of crops, storage and preservation of agricultural produce as well as farming as a business. The trainings will build the capacity of farmers thus increasing crop production.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The cash transfer program will give target beneficiaries the flexibility and complete freedom to set their own priorities and needs by purchasing what good and services their household needs. The availability of cash also improves the purchasing power of the target households. In the case of the un-conditional cash grants which mostly target women, it acts as a source of income for women, gives them power and influence within their households decision making process thus ultimately empowering women who are generally the most disadvantaged members of the community. The cash is also expected to stimulate the local markets. Injection of large sums of cash into the local economy not only benefits the recipients of those cash but also revitalize local trade including service providers, transporters of goods, suppliers and shops. Providing cash to beneficiaries is also cost effective for humanitarian agencies compared to in-kind. By providing cash, humanitarian agencies also eliminate logistical costs that would have been spent on providing in kind to beneficiaries. When goods are available and accessible locally, cash orvoucher-based interventions have the potential to bemore rapid than bringing supply from out of country.When beneficiaries use local markets, agencies alsoeliminate logistical and import costs. 2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Markets in Somalia play a critical role in the economy affecting both the food and livelihood security of the population. Markets and trade in general have proven to be dynamic and resilient. Pastoralists, agro-pastoralists and agriculturalists all depend on markets to varying degrees in gaining access to income and food. WASDA has been conducting quick market assessment by collecting data on prices of key local commodities (white maize, red sorghum and white sorghum) and imported food commodities as well as exchange rates between Somali shilling and US dollar in major markets of Afmadow district. The data reveal that the exchange rates between the Somali Shilling (Som.Sh) and the United States Dollar (USD) have remained stable while the prices of key commodities have slightly increased. The data further show that most market in the district are functioning well and there is free flow and sufficient supply of goods and services.
