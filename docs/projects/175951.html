Project Summary

Established in 2006, South Sudan Development Agency (SSUDA) is an active partner in education Cluster since 2011 having been a State Focal Point for education in Upper Nile between 2010 – 2014. With the opening of school planned after nearly a year of closure SSUDA is planning to support back to learning for 3,600 girls, boys and adolescent of school going age affected by conflict, flood and COVID-19 (IPC Phase 4 and Phase 5) in 4 counties of Manyo and Fashoda, (Upper Nile) and Kapoeta East (Eastern Equatoria States). SSUDA is already implementing education activities in the targeted locations and have static presence. To achieve this, SSUDA plans to establish new TLs and rehabilitates new safe and protective learning spaces and damaged classrooms with gender segregated WASH facilities (using locally available materials) for girls, boys and teachers; support and train teachers on pedagogy, life skills, peace building and psychosocial support; distribute teaching and learning, essential education supplies such as school¬-in-a ¬box kits, ECD kits, recreation kits, movable blackboards (procured from the Core-Pipeline). Additionally, SSUDA will carry out training of PTA and SMCs (men, women and PWD) to ensure proper management and maintenance of school infrastructure in their respective schools. Sustainably engage the communities through social mobilizations and awareness raising on the importance of education with emphasis on education of girls and children with special needs including pastoralist communities. The PTAs and SMCs will equally play important role in developing respective school development plans using and dissemination of information on COVID-19 pandemic using Ministry of Health and WHO Standard Guidelines. School development plans will include school sustainability strategies, governance, gender mainstreaming, environment, child protection, child-teacher support. As a static partner implementing multisector activities in the target counties and with collaboration with other cluster partners (WASH, FSL, Protection, Nutrition and Health), SSUDA will ensure an integrated approach that supports complete safety and well-being of learners and teachers in classrooms and school environment. With comparative advantage of its presence in the locations, SSUDA will work with community to monitor school progress with an aim to identify and address any challenges including dropout especially among girls and children with disabilities. Through community outreach activities, SSUDA will work with community leaders and PTA to strengthen Community-Based Child protection networks that will go a long way to identify vulnerable areas with high number of out-of- school girls, boys and adolescents and target them with key education messages on the importance of education. SSUDA will lobby and work with PTAs in various target schools to set up the prerequisite for WFP School Feeding programme. As FSL implementing partner, SSUDA will also pilot School Environment Club and School gardening as part of extra-curriculum alongside tree planting campaigns by learners and teachers to inculcate a culture of self-reliance and build young environmentalists. Distribution of sanitary towels will be carried out to girls of 12 years and above and female teachers who will equally benefit from reproductive Health information through peer-to-peer arrangement. Appropriate and simple to read hygiene promotion and COVID-19 prevention IEC materials with illustrations will be printed and disseminated to learners, teachers and PTA members. As an exit strategy, the capacity of community based organizations, local authority, education officials and PTAs will be strengthened to actively support the interventions at the school level.

1 - Needs

Education is both a human right and an indispensable means of realizing other human rights. It has a vital role in empowering men, women, boys and girls and can lift economically and socially marginalized adults and children out of poverty and provide them with the means to participate fully in their communities. For too long, those affected by humanitarian emergencies in South Sudan, especially girls and young women, have been deprived of education (UNESCO, 2020). The consequences of conflict in South Sudan compounded by floods, economic meltdown and COVID-19 pandemic has taken a tremendous toll on the children’s access to educational services and protection (HNO, 2021). Even before closure of schools in March 2020 following the outbreak of COVID-19, half of the country’s 4 million school-age children in South Sudan majority in Upper Nile region were not in school and already suffered protracted and multiple displacement, exposure to violence, family separation, chronic psychosocial distress, recruitment into armed groups and economic exploitation. (UNICEF, January 2020). Grave child-rights violations are widely documented, including the killing and maiming of children, forced and early marriages and attacks on education (UNICEF, 2020). Anecdotal evidence suggests that rural girls are likely to be used to cushion families’ income, which further exposes girls to sexual exploitation and gender-based violence (Sexual Violence in South Sudan, March 2020, UNMISS). This places girls at especially high risk of health and reproductive crises, including early and forced marriage, which puts girls at high risk of dropping out when schools reopen. Decreased mobility from quarantines and curfews restricts girls and women from essential protection services and support networks, further diminishing their autonomy. The closure of schools also poses a huge barrier to the normal support of learners struggling with mental health issues. The impact of COVID-19 on children and adolescents in South Sudan is adding on to high rates of stress, depression and anxiety as cases as reported in IDP camps in Upper Nile (SSUDA, Community Watch Group Assessment Report 2020). Isolating children and adolescents from their friends, peers, mentors, and teachers have only compounded their mental-health challenges. Opening schools will provide children to benefit from School Feeding Program. The school feeding programme is one of the largest anti-hunger initiative in the country. Upper Nile and Eastern Equatoria are some of the most food insecure States with high levels of Malnutrition at IPC Classification Phase 4 and 5. The school feeding programme through WFP is critical infrastructure that keeps more than 500,000 in South Sudan fed and nourished without which children are exposed to high malnutrition, hunger and destitution (WFP, 2019 Plan). In some countries since schools closed, education stakeholders are exploiting every window to keep learners continue with education including technology-mediated learning. For South Sudan, only small percentage of population have access to radios, televisions and smartphones to support online or remote education while the entire country especially in rural areas have no broadband coverage. SSUDA primary aim is to ensure boys, girls and adolescents (aged 3-17 years) have access to, and learn in, safe, inclusive and quality formal and non-formal certified learning opportunities. In particular, the objective is to reach 17,000 children (boys, girls, children with disability). Specific attention will be given to basic education opportunities that address psychosocial and social cohesion in the four target counties of (Manyo and Fashoda) of Upper Nile and (Kapoeta East) Eastern Equatoria States.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SSUDA operates under zero-tolerance on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and all staff members, Board of Directors and venders are expected to sign a commitment with the organization to avoid, prevent and report any SEA case at all times. SSUDA will be vigilant among its staff to detect any issue regarding SGBV such as Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Further, while implementing the project, SSUDA will have all teachers, volunteers and PTAs are well informed about the SGBV and sign ethical contract to commit them to adhere SEA policy. SSUDA will collaborate with Protection Cluster partners to ensure no child or female teachers are sexually exploited or abuse. All teacher and PTA members will be thoroughly vetted to ensure they don’t have any background of abuses. In addition, SSUDA will hire and train volunteers and temporary workers (men and women) who will work daily in collaboration with PTAs and School management committee members to help with deliver the project.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SSUDA recognizes that women, girls, boys and men all have very different education needs, interests and vulnerabilities. Vulnerable groups such as women, girls, people with disabilities and elderly can be taken advantage of and stigmatized. SSUDA will target and ensure that perspectives from these vulnerable groups (women and girls) are included in the consultation and decision making process as well as implementing a system for women and girls to submit complaints and issues that arise without any risk of reciprocation from perpetrators. This will allow the participation of women, girls and other vulnerable groups to get prioritized and ensure their access to quality education and other humanitarian assistance. Through needs assessment, registration, monitoring and evaluation and Post distribution monitoring, SSUDA will be able to collect sex and age disaggregated data and monitor whether these vulnerable groups are receiving adequate assistance.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

SSUDA will ensure that the target community actively participate in assess, planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluating of the project. During the assessment members of the community including women, men, children, people and children with disability and education officials at county and State level will be included to ensure the education needs of every child and youth are considered. Respective schools will be allowed to select properly constituted PTAs with 50% - 50% gender representation. The PTAs will be responsible for planning, implementation and monitoring of education activities in their schools. The committees will ensure the local community resources are identified and used to supplement the implementation of the project. the committees will be trained to ensure they are well informed and knowledgeable while handling and contextualizing education issues at their levels. In particular, the human resource and construction materials will be sourced from within the communities. The committee will be the link between the SSUDA and the schools and any decision making, feedback or communications will be as transparent and participatory as possible.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

As part of the Education Cluster, SSUDA will have designated officers to regularly attend national and State Level Cluster and Inter-Cluster meetings to obtain and share important information about the education programme, progress of our intervention as well as the general humanitarian situation in the country. This will enable better coordination with local authorities, Education Cluster, Cluster partners, the UN agencies and other NGOs to prevent duplication of efforts and address gaps in relief response and unmet needs by using Standard reporting formats. Monitoring of our activities will be regularly done and reports shared with recommendations.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Education in emergencies is a critical, life-saving response that works to protect children in conflict and natural disasters, and preserve their right to education. SSUDA’s education programmes in emergencies respond to the needs of children, parents, communities and education officials. Prior to a disaster, SSUDA works with schools and families through Parents, Teachers Associations and education officials to strengthen community based protection mechanisms through pathways referral, preparedness and planning, conflict education mitigation and peacebuilding.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

According to UN Sustainable Development Goals, there is link between Education and food security and nutrition. promoting education access for all, especially the rural poor and members of vulnerable and disadvantaged groups to basic education strengthens their capacity for self-reliance. It is evident that investing in primary education for rural people is fundamental in order to fight household food insecurity in low-income communities. Concentrating larger in pre-primary primary and secondary education will allow significantly progress, simultaneously, towards reduction of poverty, illiteracy, inequality and hunger and to contribute to the country's effort to achieving of three Sustainable Development Goals: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

SSUDA education in emergency works to ensure access to inclusive, quality learning and emotional support for pre-primary, primary and secondary school learners; provide support and safe spaces for caregivers and boys and girls in ECD so they can receive nutritional support, parenting counselling and mitigate the effects of the conflict on the developing child; helps build the capacity of teachers and caregivers to support children’s continued learning regardless of where learning takes place – in a classroom, living room or under a tree; provide teaching and learning supplies, as well as adequate spaces for learning; provide Child-Friendly Temporary Learning Spaces – these safe places are for children to learn, play, and receive the emotional support they need to begin the recovery process and partner with schools and communities to build back better, and promote stronger schools that foster peace building and conflict resolution.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

A three-pillar approach in addressing malnutrition among the school going children and adolescent is recommended is proposed in this proposal: i) regular provision of meals every school day throughout the school year: SSUDA will lobby for the provision of school meals in all he target schools through WFP’s School feeding programe ii) Acknowledgement of nutrition and nutrition education as core components of school meals; iii) Through school development plans, school feeding will be linked with smallholder farmers with the demand for school meals by procuring food directly from farmers where possible to cushion the WFP support. SSUDA is proposing school gardening as part promoting food security through young farmers club in school as a way of inculcating a culture of self-reliance. Many studies in different low-income countries showed that mother’s schooling has a significant positive impact on child nutrition.
