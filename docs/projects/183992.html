Project Summary

HOPE is planning to work on improving access to PHC services for the Vulnerable population in localities of Um Dukhun (CD), Kereneik (WD), Al Firdous (ED), Al Fao (Gadaref), and Abu Hujar (Sennar) that will contribute to 2 HRP Health sector objectives for 2022.
In this project, HOPE will (1) Provide essential primary and secondary health care services, including trauma care, EmONC and referrals mostly disaggregated to 15% children, 65% women, 5% elderly and 15% People with disability, including conduction occupational visits to provide essential primary and secondary health care services for 105,000 individuals, support trauma consultation for 3,000 individuals, do mental health consultations for 300 individuals, do physical rehabilitation sessions for 90 individuals, do antenatal care sessions for 12,000 adult females, attend (skilled attendant) vaginal deliveries for 3,000 adult females, conduct caesarian deliveries for 300 individuals, conduct postnatal sessions for 3,000 adult females, refer cases for specialized treatment (between levels of care across Sudan) for 3000 individuals, and do inpatient treatment for 600 individuals.
Also, HOPE will (2) Expand the reporting capacity of the early warning systems and disease surveillance system in 15 health facilities to submitting weekly surveillance reports in the 5 targeted localities, (3) Strengthen capacity to investigate and detect disease outbreaks in Sennar and Gadaref states, (4) Support health authorities to carry out timely response to emergencies and disease outbreaks in the 5 targeted localities, (5) Increase access to health services by supporting 15 Health Facilities to conduct mobile medical units and referral pathways for 10 months, (6) Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services targeted 300 SMOH staff, (7) Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services targeted 600 community health promoters, and (8) Increase access to health services by rehabilitating and refurbishing of 3 health facilities with the provision of medical equipment.
HOPE will focus on target Locality based on poor indicators with regard to PHC, Sanitation, Hygiene, RH service provision, patient/service provider ratio, the total beneficiaries for this project are 142,890 individuals.
HOPE will consider accountability to affected people through full community participation, strengthen the current health community mechanism/ committee as well as do follow-up for all benefits targeted separately to assess the project, identify challenges and provide give recommendations to improve the project outcomes.

1 - Needs

According to HNO of Health Sector, the Health severity for 5 localities is 4 or 5, the People In Need (PIN) are 46,619 in Um Dukhun (CD), 105,123 in Kereneik (WD), 34,295 in Al Firdous (ED), 96,622 in Al Fao (Gadaref), and 34,569 in Abu Hujar (Sennar). The PIN/ target disaggregated to 15% children, 65% women, 5% elderly and 15% People with disability.HOPE will focus on 5 localities based on poor indicators with regard to PHC, RH service provision, patient/service provider ratio by current service providers. Many health facilities have been destroyed, effectively leaving these places without government health services. Inadequate provision of and access to PHC services especially for women/ girls and children across target locality remains the main constraint as these same areas have high mortality rates for infants and mothers as results from pregnancy convergent, the reasons for this deterioration of health services are lack of equitable access to life-saving services and non-accessibility during rainy seasons. The main communicable diseases and outbreaks in the target localities are Malaria, AWD, Diphtheria, Fever, and Schistosomiasis as well as all localities at risk for COVID-19 due to bad health system, lack of communities practices in term of handwashing and wearing face-masks in a crowded area such as market or when visit health facilities. Also, there is a high risk to expand the outbreaks of Chikungunya, and Dengue Fever due to victor is living in the surrounding localities.The availability of qualified health cadres and healthcare workers is still a challenge in Sudan hindering the capacity and efforts to scale up the response. According to figures by the federal and state ministries of health, there are only 5,457 doctors, 12,601 nurses, and 17,343 midwives working in the public health system (0.76 doctors + nurses+ midwives per 1,000 population, against the 4.45 per one thousand population WHO target as recommended in the WHO Health workforce requirements for universal health coverage). The lowest ratios of medical professionals per 1,000 people, were reported in East Darfur, and Central Darfur. Across Sudan, one and a half million pregnant women of reproductive age lack access to both Essential obstetric care, Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care services (BEmONC) and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care services (CEmONC).According to the 2021 multi sectorial needs assessment MSNA, only 13.4 per cent of women across the country had their deliveries in health facilities during 2021 with 40 per cent of the home deliveries were not attended by a qualified healthcare person.In Darfur, the inter-communal, inter-tribal conflicts, and violence that is happened in the last years which are affected the safety and security of people exacerbating the risk of sexual violence especially when displaced. Women and girls continue to face the risk of gender-based violence including sexual violence when they leave the villages. Fear of reprisals from attackers often prevents rape survivors from seeking medical help. The conflict in Darfur has also increased the number of women who are the sole providers for their families. El Faw of Gadaref is suffering from poor health services and a deterioration in services due to the floods that happened this year. Also, in October 2020 the arrival of Ethiopian refugees fleeing the fighting in Ethiopia began, as well as Gadaref state hosted more than 64,180 refugees (UNHCR - Refugees dashboard 30 Sep 2021) and expected increasing the new refugee's arrival, so there is an urgent need to improve the WASH services of the host communities. Abu Hujar of Sennar is also suffering from poor health services, in last years registered the highest cases of Cholera and AWD due to a deterioration of health services, lack of sanitation, and hygiene practices by community people.Due to the above situation, HOPE looks for improving access to health services.

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

During design, HOPE assessed the safety and security for target areas as well as types of GBV that facing women. During implementation, HOPE will support the capacities of committee members and assist to know their rights to health and nutrition. All essential drugs at the pharmacy will be handled with the utmost care and storage - warehouses will be clean to avoid contamination. This project will include psychosocial stimulation activities for infants and young children will also be provided with psychosocial support to help mothers deal with their own distress and improve care for their children. Child protection messages on prevention and response as well as referral mechanisms will be included in activities related to health and nutrition outreach and awareness-raising. All health staff will be trained on referral procedures and mechanisms linking patients directly to experts from other agencies and thegovernment is already supporting the health sector at a nearby facility and other secondary facilities. Adequate shelter and waiting areas of all the health centers will be provided with all basic furniture and clean water to be accessed by all seeking services. Children and women will be given priorities. Privacy during medical history taking and examination, keeping medical data without unnecessary disclosure.Awareness-raising during community mobilization on health and nutrition, education a key component that HOPE has put in place for better understanding among the beneficiaries for self-protection on various programmatic matters. HOPE also will develop a system to collect feedback from beneficiaries to measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.
