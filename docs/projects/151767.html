Needs

Poor access to safe drinking water, a lack of adequate sanitation facilities, especially in Internally Displaced Person (IDP) settlements, and poor hygiene practices all combine to threaten the lives of vulnerable groups, especially children and the elderly. An estimated 4.6 million people are in need, where 1.5 million are in crises (IPC phase 3 and 4) and needs life-saving intervention. IDPs continue to be the most affected by acute food insecurity, accounting for 58 percent of people in crisis or emergency. It is 29,400 acute malnutrition children under 5 years, where 55,000 are sever malnourished (FSNAU Post Gu report). A Joint assessment conducted by the WASH cluster partners including IOM in July 2018 in Baidoa, indicated that 75% of the IDP camps had access to unsafe water from unprotected shallow wells or and buy water directly from the donkey carts that they are unsure of the water they use if safe or unsafe, thus exposing them to waterborne diseases. Storage capacities for the families to store water is very limited at both IDPs. An integrated approach to safe water supply, sanitation and the promotion of good hygiene practices is necessary to change trend. This WASH project will target about 150,000 living in crises or emergency (IPC 3 and4) to provide life-saving WASH services while 160,000 living in high risk area for AWD/Cholera, drought and flood prone communities will be supported through sustainable WASH service including schools, health and nutrition centers. The project is aligned with protection mainstreaming to ensure safety and dignity affected populations without causing harm and meaningful access, accountability and participation of beneficiaries in the provision of WASH assistance. The project will be a part of an IOM integrated emergency response program and resilience, where possible, efforts will be made to integrate IOM health, shelter, CCCM and protection activities.

Activities

Activity 1.1: Provide emergency temporary access to safe water to 100,000 people (35,000 girls; 30,000 boys, 20,000 women and 15,000 men) Activity 1.2: Construct and rehabilitate 10 strategic water sources for multi-use to provide reliable and sustainable safe water to 80,000 people (28,000 girls; ,24000 boys; 16,000 women and 12,000 men) Activity 1.3: Construct 30 water treatment systems to provide safe water to 40,000 people (14,000 girls; 12,000 boys; 8,000 women and 6,000 men) in riverine areas. Activity 2.1 Construct 1,000 emergency and temporary latrines with lockable doors ensuring privacy and security to 30,000 people (10,500 girls; 9,000 boys; 6,000 women and 4,500 men) affected by forced eviction, drought, floods and conflict for immediate provision of basic sanitation and hygiene.. Activity 2.2: Support 10 villages to become open defecation-free through the Community Led Total Solution approach by training 200 community mobilisers and natural leaders (100 men and 100 women, 20 per location) in hotspot villages for AWD/Cholera and have high malnutrition rate to conduct their own appraisal and analysis for open defecation. Activity 2.3: Rehabilitate and desludge 1,000 filled temporary latrines to improve access for 5,000 HHs (1759 girls, 1500 boys, 1000 women and 750 men) Activity 3.1: Recruit and train 300 (150 women, 150 men) community members and hygiene promoters. Activity 3.2: Disseminate key hygiene messages through house to house visits and single-sex and mixed-sex group discussions conducted by trained hygiene promoters and mass media communications to benefit 150,000 people . Activity 4.1: Provide capacity building for 250 (125 men and 125 women) local technicians, community leaders and local authorities. Activity 4.2: Provide capacity building on solar water solution, water quality testing and water quality and monitoring to 60 officials of the Federal Government of Somalia, Members States, regional and district level.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

In spite of recurring of droughts, flooding and AWD/Cholera hotspot in the country, there are a multitude of opportunities to build upon existing Somali structures and innovations to increase the overall resilience of the population. The project design and implementation will be taken into account environmental protection to avoid over exploitation and contamination of the environment and sustainability. This includes design and installation of the water pumping system based on pumping test to avoid depletion of aquifer. Constructed and rehabilitated boreholes will be equipped with elevated water tanks, distribution systems with household connections when possible and hybrid energization systems. On latrine constructed/rehabilitated will used standard designs to avoid contamination of groundwater and surface water. The project will be linked and build on ongoing resilience WASH program in close collaboration with government institution, local communities to ensure stable and safe environment. In 2019 HRP IOM is planning to strengthen the capacity of staff from Federal Government and Regional State. WASH committees will be established and a cost-recovery system to ensure proper operation and maintenance, community ownership and ease of access for all population groups.Private enterprise has thrived in many areas of the Somali economy, and the private sector accounts for a large proportion of WASH service providers, the project will promote private Public partnership (PPP) approach to ensure sustainable and affordable WASH services

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Some risks are foreseen with the project implementation specially exclusion based on displacement. The following measures to mitigate the risks will be implemented. Project sites areas will be well chosen based on the vulnerability criteria of displacment and duration of displacment. the new displace families with limited resources will be prioritise WASH emeregncy assistant Where security risk is higher the project will work through local NGOs/CBOs, who have experience in project implementation and have access. The project will also put in place community feedback of the WASH services and dispute resolution mechanisms are used to resolve any conflicts. Project design and implementation will also ensure an inclusive, participatory approach involving all key stakeholders including women and youth, thereby emphasizing stakeholder participation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The risk of exclusion based on gender is foreseen minimum as the project is designed to ensure gender equality across all ages in the project implementation. However in order to ensure to prevent the risk of gender and age, IOM will established feedback and complain mechanism to ensure accountability and transparent of the project implementation. This includes involving beneficiaries representative in all ages and sex, community leaders and local authority during designing, implementation and evaluation of the project. the project will use systematic outreach process in registering of the beneficiaries to ensure most vulnerable are included specially female, elders and disable people. During designing of the project, IOM will ensure location, distance and access routes to the water points, latrines and distribution sites are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Some risks are foreseen of armed conflict and violence during implementation project period. The following measures to mitigate the risks will be implemented. Project sites areas will be well chosen based on the criteria of having a stable security situation and prepred SOP for security for staff . Where security risk is higher the project will work through local NGOs/CBOs, who have experience in project implementation and have access. The project will also put in place dispute resolution mechanisms are used to resolve any conflicts. Project design and implementation will also ensure an inclusive, participatory approach involving all key stakeholders including women and youth, thereby minimizing conflict
