Project Summary

Project aims to meet the immediate, medium and long-term food security needs of the target communities of IPC level 3,4 and 5, also having the focus on restoring livelihood, enhancing food production, building resilience and strengthening capacity of the targeted population.
This will be achieved by various means. One of them is access to cereal and/or vegetable seeds’ kits and tools, accompanied by agricultural extension activities, focusing on good agronomic practices. This intervention will be delivered using lead farmers and farmer field school approaches with demonstration plots. Lead farmers and extension workers will be trained as trainers for community farmers. This methodology ensures ownership and long-lasting effects, as the capacity of trainers stays within the community. PAH will introduce different irrigation methods including drip irrigation systems together with community training on their adequate and sustainable use to enhance the copying mechanism during the dry season.
Additionally, to traditional plots, PAH will encourage setting up small-scale vegetable gardens, which can be established next to the houses. This facilitates planting and irrigation, allows higher diet diversity and minimize the protection risks in case of women walking long distances to access the plots.
Access to fishing kits and learning of fishery practices is another means to improve food security and livelihood. Fishing has the potential to immediately address food security needs. Conditional general food distribution may be considered within the project in case the targeted population is strongly affected by food insecurity. Food distribution will target the most vulnerable food insecure population with limited access to markets and having a poor road network. Contrary, in the communities with access to market and availability cash programming, will be considered. The decision will be based on initial market and community assessments, considering cash vouchers or cash for work. Food/cash for asset will be conducted in the areas where there are needs and local markets can support the project targeting the establishment of community structures and facilities like schools and infrastructures like community road projects and communal farms establishment.
PAH implements strong resilience and coping mechanisms in terms of supporting crop diversification and cultivating, using natural pesticides, introducing preservation methods, empowering the local traders, raising stability of crop & reducing post-harvest losses.
Capacity building component will support sustainability by providing various training allowing beneficiaries to implement gained knowledge in the next years.
For a more holistic approach, building resilience and long-lasting effects, PAH will integrate interventions with sectors of WASH, nutrition, education, protection and S/NFI through combined hygiene and nutrition promotion in addition to screening and referral to nutrition partners. Protection will be mainstreamed across all sectors and additional protection team will be supporting the communities.
As floods affects the country for the last consecutive years, to support the sustainability PAH plans the granaries design and construction with elevation above the flood levels and introduction of different crops that are resistant to flooding.
Concerning the beneficiaries breakdown, while planning the responses PAH will follow OCHA population aggregation.

1 - Needs

Based on the food security analysis following the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) of December 2021 and the Food Security and the Nutrition Monitoring system (FSNMS) reports of February 2022, more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people including refugees are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May-July). The magnitude and severity of food insecurity is anticipated to increase and worsen in 2021/2022 compared to 2020/2021. This is due to the current economic crisis impacting people’s ability to access markets, while conflict, flooding and seasonal effects are limiting the flow of both commercial and humanitarian goods and services. Counties of extreme concern found in Jonglei Lakes, Unity and Warrap (specifically Greater Tonj) with the greatest priority and Fangak county, comparable to Pibor county in 2021 coupled with the expected decrease ion food assistance by 10 to 20 per cent from the already reduced levels in 2021. It is expected that the projected resources available for 2022 will only be sufficient to cover eight months of needs as livelihood's support was funded at less than 35 per cent in 2021.Additionally, the cause of food insecurity is due to the floods, dry spells, continued violence, high prices and continued disruption of livelihood. Households in counties of extreme and severe concern will require immediate emergency food assistance, complemented by emergency livelihood and livestock support across farming, agricultural and pastoral communities in the medium- and longer-term to build both absorptive and adaptive capacity to mitigate such extreme levels of food insecurity with the exhaustion of coping strategies. Those most affected are children, people with disabilities, older persons, and women-headed households, identified as the most vulnerable to increases in food insecurity. Some of the main factors affecting food security are food availability, access, utilization, stability, and seasonality. These are also influenced by several other sectors. Nutrition has a significant effect on mental and physical growth, educational attainment, and later livelihood opportunities. The health of a person affects their ability to carry out manual livelihood tasks and to cover the long distances necessary to herd livestock or collect wild foods and firewood. Ease of access to markets and health care facilities impacts people’s productivity and vulnerability to disease or illness. Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) influences food preparation and safety. Protection concerns such as conflict, displacement, and gender-based violence disrupt people's livelihoods and have a very negative effect on food security. Poverty and vulnerability due to the protracted nature of the crisis in South Sudan have resulted in significantly greater levels of food insecurity and lack of livelihood opportunities, forcing many families to resort to negative coping strategies, some of which are harmful to children, such as early/forced marriage and child Labor. To address the food security gaps, Polish Humanitarian Action (PAH) will target some of the locations which are identified as food insecurity hotspots according to the December 2021 HNO as well as the Food Security classification of February 2022. PAH will target Central Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria in the Equatorias, while in the Greater Upper Nile, PAH will target Jonglei which has some of the locations with the expected food crisis spots including Pibor and Canal Pigi. In Bahr El Ghazel, PAH will target Warrap and Lakes states.

2 - Project activities

To meet SO1 - CO21. Fishing Production as an immediate source of food and improve livelihood. a) Fishing kits' distribution and demonstration on proper use. In case of access to market and availability CVA modality will be used. b) Distribution of vegetables kits (depending on preference), cereal kits and culturally acceptable tools. CVA modality may be used. c) Irrigation systems and adequate training on their use. PAH plans to provide vegetable farmers with 10 to 15-litre watering cans during the off season.To meet SO3 - CO32. Resilience and coping Mechanisms:a) Crop diversification-cultivating different types of crops can be an example that increases a household’s chance of sustaining its livelihood in the face of a localized shock such as pests or diseases that affect particular crops. For environmental and climate change impact, introduction of different crops that are resistant to flooding such as roots and tubes e.g. sweet potatoes and yams which will be an alternative as a copying strategy.b) Access to markets and Infrastructure - supporting the functionality of local markets through empowering the local traders to ensure there is stock available during the lean season which in turn enables the copying strategy of borrowing/buying on credit.c) Raising stability of crop & reducing post-harvest losses - PAH aims to introduce various technologies (e.g. silos, granary from local materials) and processing equipment training in rural areas especially in locations prone to flooding where the stored excess produce may be exposed to damage/wastage. The granaries design and construction will include elevation above the flood levels as flood resilient structures. PAH will support identified Artisan groups of farmers both women and men through vocational skills training on the production and maintenance of appropriate farm implements and storages equipment, making local stoves and baskets for transporting the agricultural produce to the local markets. d) Encouragement of establishing the vegetable gardens near the water points during off season.3. Capacity building:a) Training for lead farmer’s/extension workers and setting up Lead farmer field Schools: b) Farmers to farmer’s exchange visits to empower the farmers and gain new skills and knowledge. c) Support in capacity on households’ level – training in groups or on the household level on preparing nutritious meals with fish, vegetable, crop, and preservation of food. d) Training on fishing techniques, environment preservation, risks mitigation while fishing. The trainees will be linked up with traders to encourage market linkages between fisher folk and the supply fisher men.e) Training on different irrigation methods using local material/reusable bottles on their adequate and sustainable use to enhance the copying mechanism during the dry season.f) Training on the production of quality seed for preservation.g) Establishment of the demo kitchen garden at the Nutrition sites where the caregivers and CNV’s will be train on agronomic practice.h) PAH will collaborate with the State Ministry of Animal Resources and offer trainings to women groups on cheese making, leak salting, Milk hygiene, silage, hay formulation.PAH will have an integrated approach with FSL, WASH, Protection and Nutrition. PAH will pay attention to community hygiene and nutrition promotion on food hygiene and safe water management, to break the chain of contamination at the household level, nutrition screening and referral of SAM/MAM cases to nutrition centres, protection of most vulnerable and reduce the risks of GBV cases.PAH will mainstream cross-cutting issues across all activities focusing on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, accountability to the affected population and committing to improving the effectiveness of cooperation with communities.

MEAL plan will be developed for the project to determine the type of data that need to be collected, frequency of data collection, tools used for data collections, responsible person and track the progress against key results and output indicators and the information captured in different databases. PAH MEAL team will support the development of common data collection methodologies and it always takes part in joint needs assessments to ensure common standards. Periodic data analysis and interpretation will be made at different levels (at project implementation, location and country program level) and shared with relevant stakeholders to inform about the project progress. A mix of quantitative, qualitative, participatory and observatory data collection methods will be employed for collecting data against key indicators and the findings are incorporated into these reports. Data collection and analysis will be done separately for women, men, children and the elderly. PAH will utilise sex and age disaggregated data throughout the action. Baseline survey will be conducted to establish pre-project benchmarks for key project level indicators and will be used to measure the project progress towards the end of the project. Quantitative data collection methods will be used for this purpose. Post intervention monitoring (PIM) and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted in all target areas at the end of the interventions and/or after 6 to 8 weeks of each distribution to assess the short-term impact of the interventions and perception of people on the quality of items distributed, convenience of distribution location, safety of people traveling to and going back from distribution locations, etc. and inform subsequent distributions. HH Interviews, Focus Group Discussions (FGD), Key informant interviews (KII) and observations will be the major methodologies during PIM/PDM. 5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

CVA will be used only after careful assessment of the market functionality and availability of the products. Protection risk analysis will be conducted as well. Due to high inflation, the market assessment will give information on cost-effectiveness of the activity and determine delivery mechanism. CVA supports local economy and empowers the beneficiaries to purchase items of their choice e.g. tools, seeds and non-food items to ensure hygienic food preparation and storage, to start livelihood activities after receiving training and start-up kits. Cash for work will be considered as a delivery modality and a source of short term livelihood support. In frame of multi-sector responses PAH will consider multi-purpose cash assistance, according to Inter-agency Cash working group recommendation.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

In an effort to inform cash-based interventions and better understand market dynamics PAH will monthly conduct the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) in accessible locations and will use the data to inform the project activities. Additionally PAH will conduct monthly price monitoring using cluster unified tools. PAH will conduct protection risk analysis as part of market functionality assessment. PAH is a member of the Inter-agency Cash working group.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To ensure adequate records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and used to adjust project activities. The records of complaints and feedback are kept and regularly analysed by MEAL team in Juba, whose officers track each case and advise/support the programme team. Under the supervision of Senior Management, the implementing programme team conducts regular review meetings and adjust the project implementation to changed circumstances or according to constructive feedback.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

PAH will try to adjust mechanism paths. In case of people with disabilities, with limited mobility, PAH officers will visit them directly to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate persons, complaint committee and enumerators will be encouraged to document the complaints and feedbacks since they are selected from within the community and are familiar with the local language. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the complaints and feedback mechanism, as well having the opportunity to provide feedback directly to PAH program staff or monitoring team. Feedback and complaints can also be shared through local chiefs, FGDs, lead farmers, who are part of the community and are participating in all implementation activities. Depending on what people feel comfortable with, they can share the feedback personally or via trusted persons.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The PSEA information is part of information campaign conducted at the beginning of the project implementation. The messages will be given by PAH officers (including women) to groups of beneficiaries during FGD baseline studies, KII, education campaigns. PSEA will be important part of training of community members engaged in the project - volunteers.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

To ensure adequate records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, PAH team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. 8.11. Mitigation Measures.

PAH will conduct protection assessments and risk analysis, stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with clear AGD data to understand context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level for each location. The risk of members of the marginalized community’s/minority groups not being included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance will be mitigated by ensuring that they are part of the targeted group right from the assessment. Protection risks are most often higher for marginalized/minority groups’ members, so they will be the main focus in the project. All vulnerable groups will be targeted with attention to their needs and capacities. PAH has the flexibility to use innovative solutions to meet those needs, to ensure inclusion and empowerment. Complaint and feedback mechanisms are always in place to nd out about any gaps and address them. Women and girls will be consulted separately on their needs. Separate focus group discussions will be carried out specifically for women and girls by a female staff or protection officer so that they can open up. PAH works closely with local authorities, institutions and NGOs to clearly understand the cultural norms, inter-communal dynamics and potential risks. PAH will involve local actors in their mitigation. Thanks to comprehensive cooperation PAH can avoid aid diversion, ensure ownership of project results and involvement of the community in monitoring and control of the results. The project has clear risk analysis and mitigation related to access, inc. logistic and security challenges. The analysis is regularly repeated and the security situation is monitored continuously. Ongoing coordination and communication with the log cluster are done to support the delivery of project items. NGO forum and OCHA are focal points for all discussion regarding access and permission. Support from Access Working group discussed where needed. Thanks to close coordination with clusters, PAH has diversified sources of information, between others about explosive hazards, local history of cooperation with stakeholders, local regulations that may try to include taxation or influence beneficiary selections. Experienced team of PAH officers, supported by the humanitarian community, can deal with different stakeholders and solve complex problems without compromising humanitarian values and highest standards.
