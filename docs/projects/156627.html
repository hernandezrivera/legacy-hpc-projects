Project Summary

Due to the past 8 years of conflict vulnerable households often struggle to make ends meet. As husbands are either involved in conflict activities, fled the country or have passed away, women are left at home caring for their children. This project is especially targeting food insecure women-headed households or generally vulnerable households from among returnee populations with a 3-months food assistance intervention in Aleppo and Rural Damascus. Beneficiaries are selected from among households that were identified through community-based protection interventions or through civil society organisations in both locations, and intend to support the households with much-needed assistance to overcome food insecurity.
DAI intends to assist 2,498 of IDPs/returnees and 4,427 of host communities with a 3-months food provision. Close operational coordination with SARC ensures that benefiting households remain eligible to be supported by SARC's provisions before or after this project has been implemented.

Needs

The conflict in Syria grew out of what started as the civil unrest in 2011. Since then several million of civilians have been displaced, some have sought refuge in neighboring countries others have undertaken the journey to Europe in search for a newer, saver home. Specifically to Aleppo, around 30% of HHs miss at least one member (Humanitarian Needs Assessment Programme for Syria (HNAP) June 2018. Amongst the civilians that remained in Syria 6.1m have been displaced, some of them already several times due tochanging front lines (HNO2018). In 2018 1,250,956 Syrians returned, the top sub-district that welcomed returnees were Jebel Saman, Aleppo (MNM, Dec 2018). 89% of them returned to their own house, often needing to restart their lives there completely. In this fluent, uncertain situation children and women bear the burden of the consequences of the conflict. Many families live in dire needs and parents struggle to provide, because of lost livelihoods or unemployment. This significantly reduces parent's ability to provide for themselves and for their families. In the meantime, savings are depleted after several years of conflict and poverty is on the rise. In Jebel Saman there are an estimated 1,012,815 PiN, of which 65,720 count as food insecure. The severity level for the food security sector in the district is 4. In Qatana district, 120,912 PiN are estimated of which 105,280 are estimated to be food insecure. The severity level for the district is 3. In Az-Zabadani there are an estimated 24,045 PiN, of which 17,991 count as food insecure.

Implementation of activities

Food assistance through in-kind or vouchers for a period of three months is made available for vulnerable households selected mainly through outreach from community centers, or civil society actors. Select families are selected based on set criteria, such as women-headed households, returnees, households with PwD or PwSN. The project is fully funded for a 3-months food assistance provision. Of course DAI would prefer to extend the provision to the full 8 months cycle suggested for life-sustaining food provision interventions. However since the overall coordination advised to keep submissions conservative we opted to submit now for the 3-months provisions for which the funding is already secured, and then increase the submission during the half-year revision window, in case new funding possibilities appear on the horizon.The current planning would result in half of the assistance being provided through an in-kind modality and the other half of the assistance being distributed through vouchers. The decision to continue to implement some of the activities through the in-kind modality for half of the caseload has to do with the fact that no functioning market has been identified yet in the particular location of the operation.The collaboration and coordination activities are ongoing with the food assistance sector, SARC and the implementing partners, mainly faith-based CSOs and the community center in Hanano city. Learnings from past years show that good planning and coordination is needed with SARC and the food security sectors.

Consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to establish targeting criteria that are as harmonised as possible throughout protection service providers; 3. Consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to ensure that selected beneficiaries to which Dorcas just provides three months of assistance can be covered through other actors for the remaining months; 4. Ensure consultation with communities and local authorities on population needs, trends (including return), population dynamics in the area. 5. Protection Sector to support the harmonisation of targeting criteria, while steps have already been undertaken by the major protection agencies running these programs (e.g. UNHCR Guidelines) 6. Follow Guidelines adopted by the protection actors and ensure that they are properly applied in all project locations; 7. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and for whom they are intended. 8. Avoid the exclusion from assistance of persons with no identity documents by adopting alternative mechanisms for identification while referring the beneficiaries to legal aid service providers; Monitoring: > Periodical internal monitoring and feedback mechanisms, including through liaison with local partners, communities, authorities > Monitoring of the impact of the intervention through discussion with recipients and communities (where the facility is located but also in the outreach areas) to assess perceptions, meeting of needs for different segments of the population served, with attention to groups that may be excluded. > Consider dedicated/ external evaluations on the impact of the activities; Protection Risk: Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services. Likelihood: medium; Impact: high Mitigation Measures: 1.
