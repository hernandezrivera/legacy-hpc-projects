Project Summary

This project aims to address the increasing nutrition insecurity conditions in the country, in particular those affecting the most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant communities. The response of Action Against Hunger (AAH under the Nutrition Sector Response is divided into interventions that cover both prevention and management of malnutrition. In order to ensure that services aiming to prevent and manage malnutrition complement each other, the response entails improving promotion, protect and support for appropriate infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices to pregnant women and caregivers of children 0-2 years of age and improve timely detection of malnutrition and appropriate management of malnutrition, especially wasting, the most dangerous form of malnutrition. AAH will complement this support to the most vulnerable households, based on in-depth assessments, with multi-purpose cash assistance under its Food Security Response, especially households with women who are pregnant and breastfeeding and children under the age of 5 years. AAH will also dedicate efforts to ensure that multi-sectoral needs expressed by targeted communities are actively referred and followed up, especially that impact on nutrition outcomes are amplified when WASH, food security, protection, GBV, health, including mental health, are simultaneously addressed. Interventions under this program will target communities in Beirut, Bekaa, and South governorates, though it is important to note that not all interventions included here below will be implemented in all 3 governorates concomitantly, but depending on the needs, context, as well as AAH's capacity.
Overall, 2885 caregivers and 3875 children aged 0-59m will be targeted, inclusive of LHF activities. As AAH is mainly targeting migrants only in Beirut a maximum of 5% of the targets will be dedicated to support migrant communities.
To address malnutrition AAH implements interventions that it has experience with and are evidence-based, by prioritizing pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) and caregivers of young children with IYCF, health and hygiene promotion and counseling, both one-on-one and in groups, in health facilities and household level, including community mobilization. Another aspect of address malnutrition is timely detection of cases. For this reason, community mobilizers will be trained and supervised to conduct community level screening of wasting of children aged 6-59 months through MUAC (mid-upper arm circumference) screening. Caregivers with children aged 6-59 months will also be trained on the Family MUAC approach, so that they themselves can conduct screening on their own children on a regular basis. Included in this approach caregivers will also be supported to seek timely treatment in the nearest treatment center. Children aged 6-59 months will also receive micronutrient supplements to mitigate the risks associated with inadequate dietary intake. In parallel, AAH staff and stakeholders, including health facilities, will be supported to create and maintain enabling environments that protect, promote and support breastfeeding and optimal feeding practices. This includes training on the evidence based IYCF principles as recommended by WHO, MoPH, nutrition sector, IYCF committee etc. and oriented on the implementation of Law 47/2008 that adopts all the provisions of the International Code of Marketing of Breastmilk Substitutes. Health facilities will also be supported to create safe, private spaces to support breastfeeding.
All interventions prioritized by AAH are in line with the ERP's Nutrition Sector's response.
Additionally, AAH plans to conduct a nutrition survey at governorate level, in line with the SMART methodology to be able to update the specific nutrition needs in AAH areas of interventions, especially in Baalbek-Hermel, where this project is targeted.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

ACF works to ensure gender and protection mainstreaming at every stage of its project through a compliance with ACF’s Gender Policy and Do No Harm approach. Needs and vulnerabilities of women, men, boys and girls have been assessed through a participatory approach to develop meaningful activities in line with the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, neutrality and independence. Additionally, ACF also ensures two-way communication with beneficiaries through its dedicated feedback and complaints mechanism, allowing complaints to be directly collected and addressed. ACF shall ensure that all its employees and personnel comply with the provisions of ST/SGB/2003/13 entitled “Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Sexual Abuse”. ACF shall further ensure that none of its employees and personnel exposes any intended beneficiary, including children, to any form of discrimination, abuse or exploitation.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

ACF field staff engages with affected populations to clearly introduce them to program activities, expected impacts, and selection criteria in inception meetings planned in advance with local stakeholders and engagement and coaching of Community Mobilizers and Focal Points in diverse settings. The involved stakeholders, level of detail and level of engagement is project-specific, based on contextual factors such as access and acceptance in targeted locations. This engagement supports stakeholder trust and acceptance of project activities as well as providing a venue for stakeholder feedback on their relevance. ACF Lebanon is accountable to its beneficiaries, meaning it will acknowledge and act on all feedback and complaints from communities, local authorities, beneficiaries, contractors, service providers, and all other stakeholders throughout project implementation, especially through amplifying its Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM).
