Project Summary

This project aims at contributing towards the humanitarian community initiatives for ensuring food security for Rohingya and enhances the livelihoods and resilience of host communities. The project is highly focused on community needs, community engagement and women’s empowerment. Protection and Inclusion is considered as a cross cutting issue and addressed through a process of dialogue with the community and micro plans at community level.
Total number of participants is 3,960 from 2,400 households. Both man and women from 600 households will be the participants in 2 different events, 960 child beneficiaries would be participants for both livelihood support and portable skill training and coming from the same households that adults would be participating in cash programs. The participation allowance would be provided in a way that cash or kind goes to the household in consideration to MEB and sector standards. However sartup capital for IGAs is additional to support for subsistence. Intended outputs are At least 67% participants are with acceptable FCS; 1,6800 participants have received in kind or e-voucher support (About 41% is female and 10% of the participants coming from HH with disabilities); At least 1,320 people representing households are receiving livelihood support (75% of the participants are female and 10% coming from HH with disability); At least 960 people have received portable skill support (75% female, 50% 18+); Twenty four rehabilitation initiatives are undertaken in 24 sites. Activities are A. Selection of Beneficiaries considering inclusion of the most vulnerable section of people e.g. women headed families, people with special needs , socially excluded and minorities / B. Site Selection for Mitigation schemes following standard procedures / C. Implementation of Mitigation Schemes engaging communities and ensuring safety and security of the participants And Payments in kind or e- vouchers for consumable items/ D. Livelihood Support sack & vertical gardening portable skill training on selected trade with tailored modules and supporting the beneficiaries to start new IGAs. For the IGAs women participation and women’s empowerment is focused. Women’s access to productive resources are highly emphasized in the process of beneficiary selection, implementation and monitoring. Sector data and information collected by the program would be used for beneficiary selection. The process of beneficiary selection would be led by FIVDB MEAL unit and done in consultation with local authorities. A thorough implementation plan and monitoring framework would be prepared, which will be adjusted frequently on the basis of participants feedback and learning from implementation. There will be quarterly post activity monitoring and a final evaluation. SADD is used in beneficiary selection and monitoring.

Needs

According to the Refugee influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA II, May 2019), 88% of the Rohingya population is vulnerable. Almost none of the households can afford a nutritious diet, according to the Nutrient Gap Analysis of WFP October, 2019. Refugee Population remains 100% reliant on food assistance for subsistence and dietary diversification. Under existing condition, there are so many gaps in food security in Rohingya community like: lack of opportunity for food production, limited access to food or sources of income, movement restrictions, and protection issues. So sustaining regular and timely food assistance to population is paramount and any breakage in the food supply pipeline could deepen the existing emergency situation and threaten the survival of the vulnerable Rohingya Population. The food security of the poorest amongst the host community is a growing concern. Many have lost access to previously farmed lands (often government forestland) and have simultaneously lost work opportunities to the cheaper refugee labor market. Forest product on which they previously depended is no longer available. In addition, natural disaster and deforestation may prolong and worsen the food insecurity for both Rohingya and host communities. FIVDB is implementing an agricultural extension program at the moment, but finds many farmers uninterested in agriculture, due to lack of irrigation water and productivity of the land is not to the expected level due to absence of micro nutrients in the soil. For mitigating these gaps, Rohingya community needs in-kind support in camp as they cannot participate in food production and has no income source, as well as portable skill training so that they can ensure their required daily kilocalorie after repatriation. And host community needs support to agriculture, livestock and fisheries through financial literacy program. To implement this program, it needs training center, enough trained staff and other procurement and logistics support.Community consultations with women and men in camp -14 indicate that Rohingya women are interested to work in indoor activities and activities nearby their houses. Thus there is a need for creating more indoor activities like production of clothes and consumable items inside the camps. These indoor activities would also be able to include some of the peoples with special needs e.g. who are physically challenged to move. It needs to work closely with those people with other kind of special needs for undertaking innovative activities to engage them. Our database shows about 77% of the households are facing difficulty in sending their children in learning centers for different reasons. One of the major reasons behind this is engagement of children in collection of food and firewood. The main barrier behind girls not participating in different programs are lack of trust and protection issues along with cultural constrains. Some of the households (25 out of 3,303 surveyed in camp 21) do not have adult members eligible for sending to work because of disability or not having one. Considering child labor issue and child rights there is a need to create a special kind of activities for them e.g. theatre groups, skill training for providing some sort of activities for the minors to enable them participate in cash or kind support through e-vouchers.

Activities or outputs

Outputs: At least 67% participants are with acceptable FCS; 1,680 participants have received in kind or e-voucher support (About 41% is female and 10% from households with special needs) ;At least 1,320 people representing households are receiving livelihood support ( 75% of the participants are female and 10% from the households with special needs ) ; At least 960 people have received portable skill support (75% female, 50% 18+); Twenty four rehabilitation initiatives are undertaken in 24 sites. Activities: A. Selection of Beneficiaries: FIVDB has a database for the beneficiaries’ comprising of the information at household level (household size, literacy, consumption, disability, and SADD data). The database will be updated with information on services available to the household. For each activity, a draft participants list will be prepared on the basis of interest of the individuals and program priorities (that intends to include the most vulnerable section of beneficiaries) and will be finalized with the approval of the CiCs/ B. Site Selection: Mitigation schemes sites will be selected following a process of community consultation and authority’s advice. The database would be used for preparing and implementing the monitoring frame work / C. Mitigation Schemes: Will be on plantation, construction and maintenance of stair cases, terraces, roads etc. Beneficiaries will be divided in groups of 12 and working under supervision of FIVDB frontline workers. Protection gears will be supplied and safety and security will be ensured. Payments for the host community participants would be in cash, but the same for the FDMNs would be in kind i.e. vouchers. At the moment we are initiating a study to identify the appropriate set of consumable items for the beneficiaries’ through community consultation. We are now using printed vouchers and working for enhancing capacity and resources to move to e-vouchers. / D. Livelihood Support: The project is of 3 different kinds. First, 600 selected women would be provided 1 day orientation on sack & vertical gardening. They would be provided with seeds, manures and other materials for sack and vertical gardening and have fortnightly visit from the extension workers. These beneficiaries are supposed to come from the same household that the mitigation scheme beneficiaries are coming from. Second, 600 selected adolescent girls and boys would be provided portable skill training on trades they are interested, this aims at developing a supply chain of consumable items (e.g. clothes, dry fish, organic manures etc.) inside the camps with support from the host community. The beneficiaries would be also provided participation allowances in kind to enable them to participate in the course and ensuring their household level subsistence during the training. Third, there would be training on business planning and selected trade for 360 host community beneficiaries. The beneficiaries will come from the ultra-poor section of people. There would be provided subsistence allowance and startup capital for the business. Subsistence allowance would be according to the sector standards and startup capital would be worth $ 200. / E. Portable Skills: Portable skills would be selected considering beneficiaries’ interest and existing situation in the camps and aligned with the intended supply chain/ F. M&E: M&E would be based on a framework that aims at the engagement of the beneficiaries, and committable in terms of SADD standards.
