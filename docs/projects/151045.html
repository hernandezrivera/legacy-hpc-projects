Project Summary

The project will provide lifesaving health care services to the most drought and Conflict affected population (IDPs and host community) in Banadir,Bay, Lower Shabelle ,Mudug and Nugal regions. This project responds to the gender and age specific needs such as through lifesaving health services to vulnerable drought and conflict affected communities in target areas; The project will support fixed facilities and mobile outreach clinics in hard reach areas and Internally Displaced Persons [IDPs] at target areas.
The fixed centers and mobile clinics will provide antenatal and postnatal care, micro nutrient supplementation, nutrition screening of children under 5 years, immunization, treatment of common diseases, and health education. The Project shall collaborate with UNICEF to support the Maternal and Child Health Centers through the provision of vaccine supplies. The project aims to reach 120,000 beneficiaries comprising of female (60,840), male (59160), and children (71151) in target areas;. The project shall recruit health workers including (doctors, midwives, nurses, auxiliaries, hygiene promotors, and community health workers as per the need).
Capacity building of local health workers is one of the main avenues of providing quality health care to the community. The project shall provide training on Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC), Integrated Management Childhood Illnesses (IMCI), Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) and diarrhea case management and prevention. A great consideration shall be given gender dis aggregation
The project shall work to build the capacity of community through the provision of health education and awareness on Hygiene and sanitation, prevention of communicable disease, Infancy young child feeding, reproductive health and safe motherhood
The project aims immunize about2930 infants and 7000 WCBA; 10800 Pregnant and Lactating Women [PLW] shall be provided Antenatal Care and Postnatal Care services.
The proposed project areas are well known by HIJRA through the past and ongoing operation. The proposed project is intended to complement HIJRA’s existing emergency and resilience intervention. HIJRA will coordinate other actors in the area to avoid overlapping and will also be used referral for cases so as to be complementary each other and to maximize impact. Project will be monitored and report will be shared to cluster,unicefe,MOH and donor as per needed HIJRA engages the community in a sustainable and accountable manner to determine context and culturally appropriate need¬ based responses. The principle of Do-No-Harm approach will be use the entire project cycle. Community (men and women and youth) will be involved and will be fully participate in all project cycle and decision making processes . Hijra has Beneficiary protection desk aimed at increasing our accountability to our target beneficiaries. The beneficiary Protection desk will handle complaint and feedback mechanisms, the desk will receive complaints from beneficiaries, local authorities, internal displaced person (IDP) Leaders, other agencies and any other project stakeholder. Complaints will be channeled either through phone call or direct visit our offices located at KM5 Zobe In Mogadishu.

Needs

Due to decades of instability, Somalia’s health indicators continue to be extremely poor; One out of every 22 Somalia women is likely to die due to pregnancy or childbirth-related causes during her life course; the under U5MR of 137 per 1,000 live births is presently the third worst in the world. One in seven Somali children dies before their fifth birthdayOver 2.6 million IDPs in Somalia and majority hosted In the cities includes Banadir ,Baioda,Galkacyo and Garowe .The evictions of vulnerable people in Banadir region is continue.the IDPs still and remain the most vulnerable and desire of need of health assistance Over 1.5 million people are face (IPC 3&4) of which 32% are in Nugal,Mudug,Banadir,Lower shablle and Bay (FSNAU,2018).The 2018 Gu season has helped end drought conditions; however the adverse impacts of the 2016-2017 severe droughts are expected to persist and the risks of malnutrition and diseases outbreaks still exist. Since December 2018, a total of 6466 of AWD/Cholera cases reported including 43 deaths and 7200 cases of suspected measles. Transmission of AWD/cholera reported in Banandir and riverine parts. HIJRA’s health care facilities reported 1274 of AWD/cholera cases of which 59 female and 41% children as well as 126 of suspected measles since January 2018. Skin infections linked to water shortages have also been reported. The other common diseases reported health facility includes typhoid, malnutrition respiratory infections and anemia. Scale up of health assistant is needed to sustain to prevent further deterioration of health to the affected people in target areas. Therefore, under this HRP, HIJRA Will scale basic health services in priority to IDPs, host communities and other socially marginalized groups in target areas. Health cluster targeted to reach 2.4 million people; however, HIJRA is planning to provide health care services to 120,000 people, male (59160), female (60,840) and children (71151) in target areas.

Activities

Provide child health care services to male (12000) and female (12000) children under 5 years in the targeted districts; Provide antenatal, emergency obstetric and postnatal care services to expected Pregnant and lactates women (10800) in Nugal,Mudug,Banadir,Lower Shablle and Bay regions;Provide health care services to 120,000 vulnerable people, of which male (59160), female (60,840) and children under 18 years (71151) in target areas;Provide routine and campaign immunization to 2930 children < 1years (1465 boys and 1465 girls) and Tetanus toxoid to 7,000 Women of child bearing age (WCBA);Provide ambulance service to 250 complicated pregnancies and 380 very sick patients to referral health facilities in Mogadishu and Afgoye.Procure and distribute Maternal Newborn & Child care (MNC) equipment, drugs and supplies to cholera treatment center in Banadir hospital , 7 MCH clinics and 7 mobile clinic in target districts; Provide rehabilitation and maintenances for cholera treatment center and 4 health facilities in target areas if need be. Train 22 health care workers (of which 70% are females and 20% males) in basic emergency obstetric care (BemOC), focused antenatal care (FANC) and Essential newborn care (ENC);Train 22 health care workers in IMCI , EPI ,IDSR and communicable disease control and prevention of which 50% are women;Regular submission of epidemiological information/ data and information through disease and surveillance reports; Train 120 community health workers (CHWs ) of which 50% are females on IMCI,BEmOC,disaster preparedness and response including response to AWD/Cholera; in disease surveillance and Health information management systemTrain 90 community mobilizers (50 female and 40 male) for communicable disease prevention and control Print and distribute 130 job aids and 250 IEC materials with messaging on ANC a, safe delivery, IYCF and AWD/cholera prevention and treatment for target facilities;

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The project has seriously taken a note the risk facing displaced and vulnerable groups are excluded from the resources including humanitarian aid by the host communities. Marginalized groups including minority clans such as Somali Bantu, and other marginalized tribes at risk of being neglected will be given equal opportunity to participate as well as the disabled. During the selection of sites and training access to these groups will be considered. HIJRA will recruit some of its project staff from displaced populations especially for positions such as community health workers in order to ensure IDPs get inclusion in accessing and benefiting from all our projects and will also ask for their participation in project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.The project will note that making particular provision for persons with specific vulnerabilities – e.g., women headed-households, pregnant or lactating women, persons with disabilities (physical or mental), persons with HIV/AIDS or other chronic illnesses -- to ensure that everyone has equal opportunities, is not discrimination. this project for IDPs seeking for services must not be subject to discrimination for reasons related to their displacement

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

HIJRA Somalia has first taken account of the different needs, recognize the potential barriers faced and ensure women and men access health services equally. All data will be disaggregated by sex and age and apply a gender analysis. HIJRA will involve from the outset women, girls, boys and men, including those that belong to the vulnerable groups, in health assessments, priority setting, programme design, interventions and evaluation. All nutrition consultations, examination and care will be in privacy. Nutrition personnel (women and men) will be adequately represented in gender theme groups, Communication strategies will highlight specific nutrtion risks affecting women and men, as well as targeting adolescent girls and boys in local Somali language and include physical and mental health services and their locations and ensure all hard to reach vulnerable members are fully aware of the existing services

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

This project has seriously taken a note that women and girls are at increased risk of violence due to the on-going civil unrest in this project area and may be unable to access assistance or make their needs known. On the other side the men?s roles as protectors in this otherwise conflict affected area has placed them on greater responsibility for risk takingHIJRA works very closely with communities and local authorities, ensuring that local laws, customs and culture are adhered to, and avoiding action and advocacy that may be perceived as offensiveConflict sensitivity in the program; do-no-harm approachProtection of women against Gender Based Violence (GBV); The program provides a provision to mainstream GBV monitoring and prevention mechanisms including; provision of referral notes to victims of GBV, gathering data of GBV cases in the program target areas and providing regular reports to relevant stakeholders including protection cluster as well as specific agencies that thematic focus is to prevent GBV in Somalia.
