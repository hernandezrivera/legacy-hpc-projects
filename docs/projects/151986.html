Needs

While acute humanitarian needs remain present, particularly for new arrivals of IDPs, the root causes of the crisis in the North-East are multi-faceted and multi-dimensional and urgent early recovery needs have been emerging to address the multiple dimensions of the crisis comprehensively and simultaneously. Furthermore, support for the host communities to receive the increasing returns become an imminent requirement. Such needs cannot be achieved only by humanitarian response and UNDP aims to scale up its support in the North-East in order to ensure a comprehensive response package that reduces needs, risks and vulnerabilities and enables conflict-affected populations to cope, recover and transform their lives and livelihoods, focusing on the following needs： Basic Services and Local Governance: Whether IDPs are planning to integrate locally or to return, it is essential to boost access to basic services, both for them, as well as the host communities, in order to enable self-reliance. This includes ensuring the provision of essential basic services in local LGAs in areas of origin, in particular health, education and the provision of clean water. Providing basic services in areas of return requires a cascading set of conditions, starting from the return of LGA officials to their HQs, as well as massive investment into infrastructure and training of manpower. It requires development action to build upon resilience-based approaches of humanitarian action. Livelihoods: Supporting communities affected by Boko-Haram conflicts and promoting smooth returns will require an investment into providing meaningful and sustainable economic opportunities both in areas of displacement and IDPs’ areas of origin and the areas where IDPs have been concentrated, including in urban and peri-urban areas. Needs for livelihoods ranges from creation of immediate income-earning opportunities (e.g., Cash for Work) to promoting sustainable employment through vocational training and micro/small business creation. Social Cohesion: There is a need to restore the social fabric of the affected communities by promoting social cohesion, reconciliation, as well as a form of transitional justice, which will bring a level of confidence in the people to start trusting their neighbors again. The needs include promoting community mobilization, fostering dialogue with all social segments in the communities for participatory decision-making, and strengthening community solidarity through joint communal activities. Such support will lay a foundation for prevention of communal conflicts and violent extremism.

Activities

Priority Area 1) Restoration of basic services through the repair and reconstruction of infrastructure essential for the pursuit of a dignified life in communities that were devastated by conflict, and the strengthening of basic services provision. The restoration of basic services will be achieved through partnerships between the government and international actors for the provision of basic services. Priority needs will be confirmed through consultations with communities, security groups, and local governments (both traditional and formal representatives). Key activities include: - Reconstruction/ rehabilitation of housing and shelter for returnees- Construction/ rehabilitation of key infrastructure (e.g., water, schools, clinic, markets) - Promotion of renewable technologies such as solar power for basic services and environment protection, such as waste management- Coordination of service provision and capacity building, as well as the provision of effective maintenance systems Priority Area 2) Livelihoods Stabilization infused with opportunities for immediate employment and income generation including, but not limited to, cash for work (CfW) programmes. Interventions will focus on both emergency income generation measures (e.g, CfW, agri-input provision) and sustainable job creation. Key activities include: - Cash for work for construction/waste management labour- Provision of crop production inputs including tools, seeds, and fertilizers- Support for micro and small businesses both in agri- and non-agricultural areas- Re-establishment of market structures and community saving schemes- Grants for women and youth led small and micro-enterprise recovery.- Vocational training for vulnerable people, especially female-headed households. Priority Area 3) Rebuilding social cohesion and peaceful co-existence among IDPs in Host Communities, Returnees, and Host communities through systematic social engagement and community security initiatives. Restoring community stability, social cohesion and trust is the most critical precondition to recovery and peace building, given the deep impact of the conflict. The programme will strengthen community cohesion through building the sense of shared responsibility for and ownership of the reconstruction process and well as inclusive dialogues among different population groups in the communities Key activities include: - Strengthen the existing community mobilization mechanisms, in particular with traditional conflict resolution mechanisms, and access to the formal legal chain - Strengthening community-level peace-building activities, including cultural activities, peace dialogues, joint communal activities and trust-building between the community members and security/police actors. - Conduct stakeholder dialogue on counter narratives on radicalization- Conduct sensitization for religious and traditional leaders on radicalization- Research and knowledge development for prevention of violent extremism (PVE) Priority Area 4) Reinstating effective and accountable local governance systems through, community participation and capacity building activities.The programme will aim at building capacity of the local government to support for effective coordination and monitoring of existing Recovery Plans and Programs and gradually assume the responsibility of basic services provision and managing recovery efforts. Key activities include: - Mapping and profiling of local government structures and communities in places of both displacement and origin- Establishment of community platforms to serve as a forum to discuss community needs, grievances and security; and building a bottom-up planning model- Capacity building for state and local governments on budget planning and execution, and monitoring and reporting- Development of a transparency and accountability model through community participation (social accountability mechanisms)
