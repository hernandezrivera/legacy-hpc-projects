Project Summary

The Logistics Cluster as a service based cluster and provider of last resort aims to facilitate the timely delivery of humanitarian assistance to the vulnerable population through its logistics coordination support, information management and provision of common services to the humanitarian community in South Sudan

Needs

Given the scale and complexity of the humanitarian response in South Sudan, millions remain displaced and in dire need of humanitarian assistance. As of mid-2018, an estimated 6 million people have been in food crisis and are being targeted for humanitarian assistance. The population faces intersecting threats of violence and insecurity, large-scale population displacement, extreme food insecurity, declining economic conditions, infrastructural deficits, disease outbreaks and climatic shocks.South Sudan presents one of the most logistically challenging countries requiring a coordinated logistics response to ensure timely, effective and efficient delivery of relief items. The rainy season which lasts from May to November renders over 60% of the country inaccessible, meaning that road access to numerous areas of humanitarian intervention becomes impossible increasing the reliance on air and river transportation. Underdeveloped infrastructure, lack of road maintenance and long rainy season patterns in recent years have severely impacted the humanitarian response leaving major supply routes with acute humanitarian needs. In 2018, an average of 65 access incidents per month has been recorded - active violence on various routes continues to impede access via surface level transport (river and road) leaving communities isolated and many areas accessible only by air.

Activities

This project will facilitate the delivery of humanitarian assistance to affected persons by augmenting the capacities of the wider humanitarian community through logistics information management (IM), coordination support, and common services, where necessary, to respond to the crisis. In terms of Information Management, the Logistics Cluster will support by collecting operational information including providing GIS support to partners, mapping physical access constraints as well as convoy routes along primary corridors. Information gathered from partners will also feed into the Logistics Cluster preparedness measures and activities. IM products will be made available on the Logistics Cluster website and shared via the dedicated mailing list. At the national level and in strategic field locations, the Logistics Cluster will hold regular coordination meetings to address logistics gaps and challenges. Coordination meetings serve as a platform for organisations to discuss the integration of their response plans, enhance partnerships and share crucial information affecting the wider humanitarian community. Other activities including inter-agency road convoy coordination will form part of the Cluster's coordination mechanism and through priorities defined by the Inter Cluster Working Group (ICWG) support will be adapted and scaled up where necessary. As a last resort provider of services, the Logistics Cluster will avail common warehousing space and maintain a stock of Mobile Storage Units for deployment to the humanitarian community. Based on the demand from its users, the Logistics Cluster will airlift a monthly average of 530 mt (of relief assistance) to remote areas inaccessible by roads. As an alternative to air transport, the Logistics Cluster will continue its river transportation services with regular barge trips between Bor and Malakal and explore additional river destinations.
