Project Summary

The effect of years of conflict, displacement and lack of basic services has continued to be felt throughout the country with an estimated 8.5 million people in need of humanitarian assistance and protection services (According to Humanitarian Needs Overview 2024). The purpose of this project is to provide timely and critical lifesaving Camp Coordination and Camp Management targeting a total of 12,000 (2,000HHs) conflict affected IDPs, and returnees including people with disabilities and marginalized groups in County of Pibor GPAA. The CCCM condition is also demanding in this County with critical needs in access to Camp Coordination and Camp Management services for the IDPs and returnees. PCO intends to scale up CCCM services to this vulnerable group of people through an integrated approach in FSL, WASH, Protection (CP/GBV) NFIs, Nutrition and Health sectors to a population in IPC 3&4. The country was confronted by social and political instability due to violence and a series of interconnected shocks, including conflict, persistent and unprecedented flooding, inflation, and the impact and economic weight of COVID-19.
The Key activities: Establishment of the CCCM team per site/area, Provision of site management services including site/area level coordination, service monitoring, community engagement, Coordinate the operational entities, provision and access to services and protection in accordance with international standards aimed at guaranteeing the dignity of people and equitable access to basic services and fundamental rights, Establish a CCCM referral mechanism per site/area, Conducting joint safety audits at site level and new displacement sites, Developing site level multi-sectoral DRR informed contingency plans, site infrastructure care and maintenance, Set up of Transit Center for temporary accommodation and critical services which includes site planning, ground works and construction of basic site infrastructure and communal facilities. Establish complaints and feedback and complaint mechanisms at site level, Conduct bi-annual survey on complaint, feedback and referral mechanism, Conduct awareness-raising activities in collaboration with the site community. Establish and support representative and inclusive community committees and leadership structures ensuring inclusion of gender,PWD and minority groups are, Provision of capacity building to community committee members, site leadership and relevant authorities, to participate in, and eventual take-over of the management of the site, Mobilization and participation of IDP site population. Ensure care and maintenance of the site infrastructure by taking action to mitigate the impact of environmental degradation , Provision of CfW incentives for people involved in site maintenance activities, Coordinate the solutions for the IDPs in sites to include return, safe and dignified camp exit, community engagement and consultations, Identification and implementation of self-reliance activities in IDP sites, Collaborate with relevant humanitarian, peace and development actors, Quarterly IDP site data collection and update of site profiles, Promoting meaningful participation of local and national organizations.
PCO in partnership with IOM has just concluded implementation of CCCM in Pibor GPAA under SSHF SA1 funded by UNOCHA from 1st November 2022 to 30th October 2023, In addition, PCO is currently the CCCM Cluster Coordinator for Pibor GPAA. PCO is the current FSL Cluster Core Pipeline Manager in Pibor GPAA since 2022 to date. PCO has been implementing MYRP project intervening in 86 schools in Counties of Gogrial West, Tonj East and Tonj North funded by ECW Since 1st May 2020 up to 30th June 2026. Furthermore, PCO has been a long-term partner with UNOCHA in CCCM, Education, FSL, WASH and an active member of the relevant clusters. The Knowledge and experience gained during this period will enable her to successfully implement project of the same nature.

1. Needs

Humanitarian situation remains critical in South Sudan. According to Humanitarian Needs Overview 2024 estimated that more than two-thirds of south Sudan’s population, 8.5 million people, are in dire need of humanitarian assistance and protection services in South Sudan. The HNO-2024 estimated that 1.63 million people in need of emergency life-saving integrated Camp Coordination and Camp Management services. According to South Sudan Humanitarian Response Monitoring Report 2024, states that In 2023, 908,800 people remain in 142 formal and informal displacement sites across South Sudan, with around 327,215 people still living within displacement sites (PoC, IDP camps and camp-like settings) and did not receive CCCM support, resulting in limited access to coordinated services, community support, information, or site maintenance. South Sudan’s CCCM system has been negatively affected by protracted conflict and massive floods which causes displacement, economic crisis, and inadequate access to CCCM quality services for IDPs, returnees and limited sanitation facilities at PoC, IDPs sites and Camp like sites. The living conditions in camps, PoC and Camp like settings remains at critical levels across the country, however, there are very few people returning to their places of origin because of lack of basic services and insecurity. The continued influx of returnees from Sudan has continued to stretch the already dire resources. Additionally, over 400,000 projected returnees from Sudan in 2024 will need CCCM assistance in the transit and reception centers near the Sudan border. Based on monthly site verification, the CCCM Cluster anticipated that around 20 per cent of these returnees will settle in existing IDP sites throughout the country and to ensure that the needs of both IDPs and returnees access to life-saving assistance and protection is met. The integration of these additional returnees in existing IDP sites will require significant efforts to provide decent living conditions, protection, and humanitarian assistance. This prolonged nature of the displacement in South Sudan requires the provision of continuous assistance across a range of sectors, as facilities requires maintenance and replacement. CCCM support is also needed in ensuring displacement sites are safe and protected from the damage caused by increasing floodwaters and conflict. The effects of conflict, displacement and lack of basic services continue to be felt in the Counties. The purpose of this project is to provide timely and critical lifesaving CCCM service targeting a total of 12,000 beneficiaries’ conflict affected IDPs, and returnees Pibor GPAA. The target population has experienced a cycle of violence and flooding that has made it difficult to access for a long time. The same conflict/flood has displaced many of the target population to neighbouring settlement areas or neigh boring payams and are now returning to settle back in their original homes and is in need of immediate CCCM support and or resilience support. As a cross-cutting sector, CCCM is well-positioned to address the multidimensional needs of IDPs, promote comprehensive and durable solutions, and ensure that IDPs' needs and intentions are integrated into planning, implementation, and decision-making processes. The regular deployment of the comprehensive site profiling tool will provide site-specific information essential for solutions programming, benefiting a range of partners working through the nexus approach. The host communities in these areas are getting it hard to cope with the number of IDPs and returnees who are in need of their support and care with the little resources that they have. Several strategies will be used including joint interventions with other clusters such as Protection, Education, FSL, WASH, NFIs, and ensuring that our activities and the whole intervention complements other sectors and partners activities and no duplication.

2. Project activities

The overall objective of this proposed Humanitarian Response plan is to provide Camp Coordination and Camp Management services conflict/flood affected vulnerable IDPs and Returnees to 12,000 individuals (2,000HHs) through implementation of 4 Cluster Objectives with the following key activities: • Establishment of the CCCM team per site/area,• Provision of site management services including site/area level coordination, service monitoring, community engagement, • Coordinate the operational entities, provision and access to services and protection in accordance with international standards aimed at guaranteeing the dignity of people and equitable access to basic services and fundamental rights,• Establish a CCCM referral mechanism per site/area, • Conducting joint safety audits at site level and new displacement sites,• Developing site level multi-sectoral DRR informed contingency plans, site infrastructure care and maintenance, • Set up of Transit Center for temporary accommodation and critical services which includes site planning, ground works (e.g. clearance, levelling, etc.) and construction of basic site infrastructure and communal facilities. • Establish complaints and feedback and complaint mechanisms at site level, Conduct bi-annual survey on complaint, feedback and referral mechanism, • Conduct annual survey with the female committee members on their participation in the decision making process, • Conduct awareness-raising activities in collaboration with the site community.• Establish and support representative and inclusive community committees and leadership structures ensuring inclusion of gender , PWD and minority groups are represented and participate to the established representation structures, • Provide capacity building to community committee members, site leadership and relevant authorities, to enable their participation in, and eventual take-over of the management of the site,• Mobilization and participation of IDP site population, including through cash for work and Community-led quick impact projects to address immediate needs of both returnees and IDP communities, that are identified by the communities themselves• Ensure care and maintenance of the site infrastructure while preventing or taking action to mitigate the impact of environmental degradation (including through community-led projects and CfW).• Provision of CfW incentives for people involved in site maintenance activities, Coordinate the solutions for the IDPs in sites – this may include return, safe and dignified camp exit, go and see visits, community engagement and consultations,• Identification and implementation of self-reliance activities in IDP sites, • Collaborate with relevant humanitarian, peace and development actors, etc., • Data collection on intentions of IDPs, Quarterly IDP site data collection and update of site profiles, • Promote meaningful participation of local and national organizations.
