Project Summary

UTSA’s LEARN DSLR Project aims to cover Rohingya adolescent and youth girls and women raging from 11 to 24 years engaging them in community-based, community-led and community-owned; and female led and female-organized teaching-learning activities through basic education and further education under GIEP in the Rohingya camps in Bangladesh. The Rohingya facilitators and Bangladeshi facilitators both will be female and it will bring a positive change by providing life-enhancing educational services for Rohingya female adolescent and women in an inclusive model of education and further education. The chosen modalities are home-based learning centers, community based home work clubs, co-shared learning facilities (outreach facilities of other actors in their free times), and community engagement approaches.

1 - Identified Needs

UTSA is observing the Rohingya crisis from a different point of view through the lense of it’s strategic goal as per REHECDU approach. Our REHECDU lense finds us to know the Gender situation in the camps through our research initiatives. Over 1 million Rohingya refugees are living in Bangladesh. And half of them are women and girls. The difficulties of Rohingya women and girls is enormous. Uncountable forms of violence and discrimination is the major cause of their life under threat and discrimination. Education is the change maker but social structure is not supportive. Gender based violence, intimate partner violence, domestic violence, sexual violence and other forms of violence and discrimination is a regular or socialized one in the camps. Education Needs Assessment 2019 outlines a greater need of education for Rohingya women and girls. Joint Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment 2020 and Education Needs Assessment 2019 would help all to see the severe needs of education as tool of change and self-realization. UTSA’s research found that when support services are active still women and girls cannot enjoy it or cannot get the services as they are not educated. They don’t know what to do and how to do. This barrier makes them unable to find solution to their life problems. So UTSA believes that educational intervention must be taken in the accepted format and approach that is welcomed by the society and the women folk in the Rohingya communities. The approach presented by UTSA is not a supernatural one and hundred percent prefect one, but the approaches laid down by UTSA can be a tool to reach the illiterate women and girls and could give them a lantern of basic education and further education to know the basic things of literacy, numeracy, PSS, sex and reproductive health, services, gender based violence, social and life skills and other life-enhancing skills to identify their position and contribution in the society.
