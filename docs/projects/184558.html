Project Summary

Drought, recurring locust invasions and the socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19 – the so-called ‘Triple Threat’ - are continuously affecting the livelihoods of Somalia’s pastoralist and farming communities leading to displacement - on top of conflict/violence in many parts of the country, including on land/resources and between clans and government and non-state armed actors, adding to the 2.9 M people already displaced. In 2021, 5.9 M people (48% of the population) are in need of humanitarian assistance and over 2.7 M people are facing acute food insecurity (IPC levels 3-4). In addition, the Humanitarian Needs Overview for 2022 which is currently under finalisation already confirms that the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance will increase - from 5.9 M in 2021 to 7.7 M in 2022, including an increase of people living in IPC 3 and higher from 2.9 to 3.5 M. FSNAU and FEWS Net IPC and rain projections reach similar conclusions. Urgent and more sustainable mid- to longer-term investment in reducing risk and vulnerability is therefore required (Somalia HRP 2021). These high levels of vulnerability, risk exposure and lack of coping capacity have led to Somalia continuously ranking top on the INFORM Risk Index - also in 2022. This ongoing humanitarian crisis increases the burden on women and girls who are always disproportionately affected by emergencies in Somalia. Oxfam’s recent gender and protection analyses across Somalia confirm that the existing humanitarian situation has led to heightened protection risks that include gender-based violence, specifically rape or attempted rape, sexual assault, harassment from men, and even killings both within and outside their community.
Based on the projections outlined above, Oxfam with partners KAALO(KAALO Aid and Development), SADO(Social Life and Agricultural Development Organisation) and SSWC( Save Somali Women and Children) selected locations predicted to display some of the highest needs in Somalia throughout 2022, namely in Puntland and South central of Somalia with regions of Gedo, Banadir, Mudug and Bari regions . Focusing sectors such as: WASH, Protection, and Multi-Purpose Cash.
In Puntland, KAALO propose to support IDPs in Galkayo and Bosasso camps with integrated WASH and Protection services, and rural pastoral communities in Jariiban (Mudug) additionally with resilience activities in these sectors which focus on animal health and agriculture. KAALO therefore propose to work in IPC 3 and 4 locations, namely the Bosasso and Galkayo IDP camps.
Save Somali Women and Children (SSWC) will work in Banadir region in South Central Somalia, focusing on two districts (Daynile and Kahda), supporting IDPs and rural communities with integrated WASH and Protection services.
SADO will work in IDPs located at Bardera district in Gedo region, also covering the WASH and Protection sectors, with a particular focus on agricultural and resilience interventions.
