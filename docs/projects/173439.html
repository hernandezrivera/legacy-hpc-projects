Project Summary

Action Against Hunger operationalizes prevention and mitigation of sexual and gender-based violence through its integrated Nutrition, Mental Health, WASH, Livelihoods and DRR, CwC programming basis to the comprehensive gender analysis and sectoral assessments. ACF continues CwC, to promote a better understanding of SRHR needs and advocate for appropriate gender-responsive and youth-friendly programming.
ACF follows a holistic, inclusive approach across social ecology, including people with disabilities, women-headed households, PLWs, individuals facing protection needs. Among the services provided via Safe Spaces, awareness programs, MHPSS to vulnerable women, men, boys, girls have proven non-intrusive entry to identify survivors of violence, provide case-management including referral and follow-up services. ACF maintains good coordination with Local and Government partners through all stages of program implementation and actively collaborate for case management via referrals, follow-ups and capacity building.
ACF provides regular multilayered-training for internal staff and volunteers on themes on GBV and Protection, and with the gradual evolving process of integrating gender and protection to MHCP (MHCPGP), ACF will take part in GBVMIS, case management task team and gender/ protection specific thematic groups. Through baseline and end-line surveys, FGD, KII and feedback from the targeted beneficiaries; ACF continues to adapt its programming to enhance accountability.
Engagement of men and boys is considered a key priority within the GBV programming to strengthen gender equality and equity, increased accountability in the reduction of violence. ACF also focuses on capacity building of community women and reinforcing peer to peer support groups.
To promote better adaptation to the pandemic, awareness sessions are regularly conducted and facility-based interventions adhere to safety measures, along with the preparatory and sensitization programs focused on the seasonal calamities.
Safe-Spaces for Women and Girls and MH-Wellness Centers are consistently utilized for equitable, and accessible GBV prevention and protection services by trained Psycho-social Workers and Psychologists.
ACF adheres to Gender and PSEA policies, following complaints and feedback mechanism open to internal staff and community stakeholders. Gender and protection considerations are mainstreamed across all sectors. ACF is GBV Focal in Nutrition Sector and has an independent PSEA mechanism.

1 - Identified Needs

According to “Gender and Intersectionality Analysis Report 2020”, women and girls representing half of the total refugee population are at risk of SGBV, including domestic violence or IPV, child marriage, sexual abuse and exploitation and trafficking. IPV is the most common concern of the survivors of the SGBV. Adolescent girls are at particular risk of early marriage, sexual violence and trafficking; while they faced a challenge to access services due to safety and purdah. Apart from SGBV case management support, there is an acute need for MHPSS for all ages and social group of refugees with limited accessibility. The analysis highlighted how power within the household and community has impacted and has been reflected in the humanitarian responses. Men being the primary decision-maker in the household including accessing health care facilities for self as well as in the community. Lack of knowledge, limited and exclusively facility-based anti-natal care and socio-cultural acceptance of SRHR and family planning can combine in prevention from accessing SRHR services and family planning of women, youth, adolescents and men. It is also observed that in the refugee community, among the three major reasons for beneficiaries being reluctant to access complaint and feedback mechanism due to unaware about the process, shyness, limited mobility respectively. According to the recent analysis (In the shadows of the pandemic: the gendered impact of covid-19 on Rohingya and host communities, October 2020); women are likely to experience high levels of stress and mental health issues due to the increase in GBV, household work and financial problems. Increasing awareness among community people on protection and SRHR rights and services, CFMC and PSEA; coordination among different stakeholders and emphasising the importance of SADD; designing and adapting need-based services through community engagement with a focus on equal and equitable participation of male, female, boys and girls including people with disabilities can be supportive in mitigating and addressing the risks and needs.
