Project Summary

The project aims to provide timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene
for IDPs/host communities and returnees living in the hard-to-reach areas of South
Sudan. It will target newly displaced people, returnees and host communities affected by
conflict and flood. The project will focus on providing emergency WASH services to the displaced
persons including most vulnerable host communities and also support the areas of
returns. Focus will be on the repair and construction of Water points, training hand pumps
mechanics, provision of hygiene and dignity kits and water treatment materials as well as
hygiene promotion and messaging to increase awareness among the affected population
and reduce the incidence water borne diseases. The affected population will also be
supported in the construction of emergency latrines specifically targeting the most
vulnerable and newly displaced people. The project will be delivered through a rapid
response mechanism in project locations selected in line with the cluster prioritising
process.
The project will promote timely delivery of assistance through deployment of rapid response teams in line with humanitarian priorities for 2021 HRP's WASH Cluster SO1, SO2, SO3, SO4,SO5 & SO6. THESO’s mobile response teams will be ready for deployment within 7 days to conduct assessments and within 14 days for full-fledged delivery of WASH services and other activities per location. The project will also support returnees in settlement as a part of their local integration or returns to their areas of origin. This will be done in an integrated way with other projects being implemented by THESO so that the returnees don’t only receive WASH support but also a source of integrated health projects in the planned locations. Additionally, the project will be implemented together with provision for education in emergencies that will be made for the returnees where THESO is already implementing such projects e.g. by providing WASH services in schools or communities or return.
The WASH interventions will be implemented in close coordination with the Emergency Preparedness and Response (EP&R) WASH actors where implementation is based on the multi-sector assessments and team involving WASH, Health, Shelter, Nutrition, Food security and emergency Education with protection as a cross cutting theme. In some of the locations, the WASH projects link up with other emergency projects to ensure the beneficiaries receive a comprehensive response package. The project will be implemented in coordination with other players in the sector especially UNICEF for information sharing and use of the cluster core pipeline supplies when needed as well as other WASH cluster members for joint assessments and response when appropriate. The project will also be coordinated with the WASH and Logistics clusters especially in prioritisation of locations and delivery of supplies.

1 - Needs

Access to safe drinking water: Shallow/hand-dug wells, or surface water (rivers or streams) are the main sources of water in South Sudan. Access to safe drinking water continues to be a challenge, and water-borne diseases have spread due to lack of clean water and poor sanitation. It is estimated that only 7% of the South Sudanese people have access to improved sanitation and only 41% t to safe drinking water. The effect of conflict, flooding and evolving of inter-communal fighting to new areas in 2021 aggravated the situation. as boreholes were damaged or made dysfunctional due to lack of repairs or effects of flooding in 2020. In Jonglei State, the monthly REACH assessment reports shows that while boreholes are the main source of water, their operation and maintenance and contamination by flood water during the rainy season are the main issues. Similarly, in Central Equatoria State boreholes and protected wells are the main source of water while they are contaminated by floods during the rainy season aggravating the shortage of safe drinking water in the season. Due to distance to the few existing limited infrastructure, access to safe water during dry season drops drastically. With increased population in some areas due to displacement and reduced amount of water during dry season, it is expected that water induced conflicts will arise at the water collection points. In South Sudan lack of access to safe drinking water and adequate sanitation pose protection risks for women, with the search to find a safe place to defecate and the trek to collect water, women are exposed to the threat of violence and harassment. Unsafe excreta disposal: Only 16% of the urban population and 4% of the rural population in South Sudan have access to adequate sanitation facilities, which is one of the lowest statistical coverage in the world. 50% and 79% of South Sudanese practice open defecation in cities and rural areas, respectively. Open defecation is therefore common and is one of the biggest causes of diarrhoea, due to the faecal-oral transmission of diarrhoea-causing organisms. Lack of awareness regarding safe hygiene practices: A higher percentage of the affected population often have very low ability to effectively utilise improved water and sanitation facilities to prevent diseases. It was noted that this was a result of using dirt water containers for collection and storage. Poor hygiene and sanitation conditions, as well as a lack of access to safe water, have resulted in an ongoing high watery diarrhoea disease. There is a dire need to raise awareness through promotional activities and training to enable effectiveness of water and sanitation improvements. Community capacity building for the management of water sources: There is a need for capacity building and support to communities to empower them to take responsibility over their water facilities and actively participate in their Operation and Maintenance (O&M) in ways that are flexible and incorporate measures which deal with gender inequality and barriers to access.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Community have been engaged from need assessment to project design and they will be engaged in project implementation, monitoring and evaluation of project activities. Throughout these processes, community and beneficiaries are provided information about protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) at all levels.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Through use of it Community based and health facility based complaints and response mechanism focal points, THESO shall ensure that feedback from most vulnerable members(e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls) are received, recorded and feedback provided to the beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Complaint and feedback mechanisms will be put in place to attend to complaints of stakeholders (direct and indirect beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries, partners, local leaders, group of persons, organisations, project staff or any person can provide feedback about the THESO program, activities, staff, implementing partners and project suppliers) at all stages of the project. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the complaints and feedback mechanism, as well having the opportunity to provide feedback directly to THESO program staff, as well as invited to share their experiences and suggestions with THESO’s monitoring team or program staff. Feedback and complaints can also be shared through local chiefs, FGDs, hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, water user committee enumerators who are part of the community and are participating in all implementation activities. Depending on what people feel comfortable with, they can share the feedback directly with THESO staff, through complaint desks, community leaders, etc. information shared will be used for improving actions/activities, advocacy for further services from relevant actors in situation where THESO is unable to respond to the needs including referral to government body if the complaint requires government intervention. Positive feedback will be used for documenting success stories. For people living with impairment like the those are immobile, THESO team will consider visiting households with the help of the hygiene promoters to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate, community hygiene promoters, complaint committee and enumerators will be encouraged to document the complaints and feedbacks since they are selected from within the community and are familiar with the local language.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Through collection, collation and analysis of community/beneficiaries feedback using reports/information collected by THESO CRM focal points, THESO shall provide feedback to beneficiaries using it appointed CRM focal points persons in community and health facilities as the organisation uses the feedback to adapt project assistance.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Mitigation Measured for Risk 8.1 Mobilization of the community leaders on how to include the minority and marginalised groups during the need assessment and also during the response so that their views are captured, and their needs are identified, and they are aware on what support they will receive. Mitigation Measures for Risk 8.2: This risk will be mitigated through organising separate focus group discussion with women and girls. in the meetings consultation will be made with them on the needs as well as the proposed response. Their views will be recorded, and their concerns will be noted, and a proper gender friendly response will be organized. Mitigation for Risk 8.4: To arrange community awareness sessions focusing of GBV, Child protection and other protection issues guiding the community on what to do in a particular case. Also map out the service providers and let the community know where to refer in case of any protection case. Mitigation Measures for Risk 8.7: In order to avoid this risk, selection of the registration and distribution site will be carefully done, so that people feel safe to reach there. For registration efforts will be made that the project team visit the beneficiaries at their homes in case there is security risk for them to gather at an insecure place. Mitigation Measures of Risk 8.8: Sometimes the local authorities pressurizing the partners to pay extra illegal tax. For this case the issue will be referred to the country director and then share with NGO forum to advocate with the Government relevant department/RRC to avoid such things. Mitigation Measure for Risk 8.10: THESO staff has a strict code of conduct on beneficiaries for exploitation and sexual abuse. Action will be taken immediately by the management if such case is reported and confirmed. Complaint and feedback desk is arranged at the distribution/registration site where complaints and received at the spot and also responded.
