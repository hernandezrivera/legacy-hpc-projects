Project Summary

Please provide a short summary of the intervention, describing its purpose, targeting criteria and mechanisms being considered under this project.
World Vision in Lebanon and Tabitha will target the most vulnerable Lebanese, Palestinians and
Migrants girls and boys and their caregivers under ERP with essential Child Protection
(CP) and psychosocial interventions including Gender Based Violence (GBV) awareness
Raising and case management. The current situation in Lebanon has further exacerbated the compounding CP
needs and risks of violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation, child labor, family separation
and unsafe migration, GBV and Mental Health Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) concerns
among vulnerable populations. Moreover, the socio-economic crisis, uncertainty regarding
the future and breakdown of essential basic services in the country are taking a toll on
people living in Lebanon, especially persons with specific needs including children and
adults living with a disability. WVL will leverage its presence in the areas and its longstanding
partnerships in the communities and utilize previously implemented outreach
strategies that have proven effective in reaching the Most Vulnerable children and youth
among targeted population through local partners, door-to-door, media including social
media and phone calls. Tabitha and WVL will contribute increased gender equality by ending discrimination against boys and girls, eliminating forms of violence and harmful practices, restoring self-esteem, and creating equal access to services through a static intervention within already established centers or through a mobile intervention The selection criteria for children will be based on
vulnerability/risk factors studied and set by WVL’s CP technical team as highlighted
hereafter:
- Children identified through local community assessments with trained local partners.
- Experiencing abuse in any form (physical, emotional, sexual, exploitation, neglect and
other forms of violence)
- Children living or working on the street
- Children involved in labor affecting their school attendance and / or health and moral
development
- Trafficked children
- Children at risk or survivors of marriage
- Children living in child-headed households
- Children whose household is designated below poverty line
- Children deprived of parental care, because of their parents’ destructive behavior
(alcoholism or drug dependency etc.)
- Children separated from parents or regular caregiver due to abandonment, imprisonment,
trafficking or migration
- Children living with parent or caregiver with chronic illness in a household designated as
below poverty line or in extreme poverty
- Children living with older adults or caregivers
- Children living in contested / conflict prone areas
- Children left out of school because of disability
- Children living in isolated locations far from neighbors
- Children with disabilities living in deprived or underprivileged communities
- Out-of-school children / school drop-outs
- Children living with parents from marginalized groups (imprisoned, HIV-positive, ethnic
minorities, sex workers…)
- Children with no ID

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

WVL and Tabitha are committed to creating an inclusive and gender-responsive learning environment for all project participants. They will use participatory methods to ensure that both boys and girls, men and women have equal access and will identify and address barriers that may prevent participation. Sessions will be conducted by male and female facilitators, and gender-responsive questions will be incorporated into project monitoring and evaluation to better understand the benefits and remaining challenges for both genders. WVL also recognizes the importance of including children with disabilities in their programs. They use the Washington Group Questionnaire to identify children with disabilities and tailor activities to meet their needs.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

World Vision Lebanon (WVL) will implement a project that ensures a functional and accessible accountability system, enhancing beneficiary and community participation in project design and implementation. Beneficiaries will receive information packages via SMS, flyers, helplines and information sessions to improve their understanding of the project and community response mechanisms. A help desk will be available during major activities, and feedback will be gathered from beneficiaries to inform programmatic decisions. Complaints will be confidentially handled and closed within a set timeframe. Monthly reports will be produced to provide updates on activities and complaints, WVL and Tabitha staff will receive training on project participant accountability, participatory and disability-inclusive tools and methodologies, as well as Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Child Protection (CP) policies. Both organizations have a feedback and complaint mechanism.
