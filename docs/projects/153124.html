Project Summary

This program aims to provide life-saving child protection and youth resilience services to displaced girls and boys, their families and primary caregivers, and host communities in Kapoeta East county. The proposed child protection services include psycho-social first aid, psycho-social support, registration of unaccompanied and separated children followed by family tracing, youth resilience sessions for youth and parents/caregivers and holistic case management.
The project will focus on achieving Strategic Objective 2 ie To Prevent, mitigate and respond to protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience.
The target location is Kapoeta East County in the following payams: Nakalewat, Nawoyagule, Nawoyakali and Kasengor.
The funding sought is to provide support to an ongoing emergency program funded by the Rapid Response Fund in the same location but is coming to an end in December 2018.
GREDO is the only CP partner in these locations and will coordinate closely with CP working group in Torit. GREDO is also a member of PSS and UASC working groups and will update and closely coordinate with other CP partners.

Needs

This project proposal is seeking longer term funding support to supplement an emergency support provided by Rapid Response Fund that is coming to an end in December 2019. The target locations for this project Nakalewat, Nawoyagule, Nawoyakali and Kasengor Payams in Kapoeta North County, Eastern Equatoria State. Currently, GREDO is the only Child Protection partner in these remote locations.Violence erupted between the Jie community and Murle in Boma state in the morning of 6 July 2018 in clashes that lasted for hours between the two sides. It has an estimated total population of about ten thousand including internally displaced persons estimated to be about 3,000 as at the time of the assessment. The displaced Jie settled in three areas including Lorumute, Lowoyapuru and Koridid at the border of Eastern Equatoria and Jonglei. According to the interviewed IDPs and local authorities, the violence resulted to the loss of 45,000 heads of Jie cattle and the death of nine (9) men, three (3) women, and one soldier. Six (6) children were abducted and later killed. Twenty people, mainly women and children, were injured. The Jie community fled from the violence in Boma and returned to their ancestral home in Jie County. The displaced people traveled long distances through the bush and arrived in Kapoeta North firstly in Kasengor payam then later spread to other payams in small groups. By the time of the assessment in July 2018, the IDPs numbered 2000, mainly women and children, in Kasengor. More than 7,000 displaced persons are reported to be on their way to Kasengor. The total population of the internally displaced population that fled from the violence in Boma into Jie County is about 3000 individuals. A multi-agency assessment was conducted and identified various urgent needs including lack of shelter, food, NFIs, health services, WASH, nutrition and serious protection needs. The added stress on scarce resources due to the influx of IDPs has adversely affected the power relations of the host community members. Most IDP men feel dis-empowered since they have completely lost their means of providing for their families. Lack of appropriate shelter leaves the displaced families in an undignified state while lack of clean water and sanitation facilities increases chances of disease outbreaks. Child protection concerns were identified amongst the IDP children and include apparent psychosocial distress, presence of unaccompanied and separated children, lack of community child protection networks, low awareness on child protection issues, youth despondency as well as lack of adequate and appropriate spaces for structured play and educational activities for children. Furthermore, Kapoeta is adversely food insecure and is in an emergency IPC 4 with acute malnutrition being critical. Adolescent girls from the IDP community are especially at risk of sexual exploitation as they go looking for food and firewood with their mothers. According to the assessment, the IDPs and their families have indicated that they do not intend to leave the area to go elsewhere because they fear more Murle attacks in their previous homes and are content to remain with the fellow Jie in Kapoeta. This therefore calls for urgent, integrated and robust protection interventions that are scalable and sustainable for the long term.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is based on specific risk analysis that was done through key informant interviews and desktop research. In conducting risk analysis, GREDO has taken measures to identify who is at risk, how and why at the very outset of the crisis and thereafter, taking into account the specific vulnerabilities that underlie these risks, including those experienced by men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as internally displaced persons, older persons, persons with disabilities, and persons belonging to sexual and other minorities.This included an analysis of risks facing the children in the target payams for presence of armed groups hence risk of recruitment, cases of domestic violence, lack of essential services like education etc. Vulnerabilities assessed in Kapoeta East county include lack of food which exacerbates protection risks facing the children and especially girls who are prone to sexual exploitation in search of food. Kapoeta North county was also found to have very weak protective structures as there is no child protection partner especially in the targeted payams. GREDO will put in place measures to strengthen the collection, management andanalysis of information to inform and adjust early warning, preparedness, response, recovery andpolicy efforts.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

GREDO shall actively seek out the most vulnerable members of the population by use of IASC's vulnerability criteria and Child Protection Minimum Standards. The risk analysis conducted showed the extreme difficulties encountered especially by the IDPs in Kassengor payam which is the largest settlement of all the payams in Kapoeta east. GREDO will engage the IDP leadership and other local stakeholders to ensure that the services reach the IDP children and their families and not just the hosts.GREDo will track inclusion-monitoring indicators to reduce barriers to access for at risk persons.
