Project Summary

The project will support MSMEs affected by the explosion with a combination of cash grants, technical support and advice to help them become operational again as quickly as possible. A quick assessment of affected businesses will determine the extent of the damage done to both the physical infrastructure of MSMEs, as well as merchandise and inputs that are essential to their operations. Based on results of the quick assessment, cash grants will be provided to affected businesses to help them pay for necessary repairs, rebuild their shops and premises, and restock with merchandise and required inputs in order to relaunch operations rapidly. Additional to cash grants, MSMEs will benefit from counselling sessions with experienced experts that will accompany and guide MSMEs in their efforts to relaunch operations quickly. Technical advice and guidance will focus on the one hand on helping MSMEs to find the most cost-effective and rapid ways to rebuild and relaunch operations, and on the other hand on adjusting business plans and operations of MSMEs to help them reduce future risk and become more resilient, for instance by switching to digital technologies or operating more cost-efficiently.
Advice will be provided experts and consultants trained and certified in ILO’s business continuity and small enterprise development trainings.
As mentioned in the flash appeal, the ILO seeks to support damaged MSMEs with a combination of cash grants and technical assistance activities. Under other donor-funded programmes, namely Dutch-funded programme PROSPECTS, the ILO has developed, as an emergency response to COVID-19 and the financial crises, a technical assistance programme that aims to support business continuity, resilience and decent job retention in SMEs employing both Lebanese and Syrian refugee workers in Lebanon. The programme focuses on the provision of on-site coaching, training and advisory services to SME owners and workers to develop and implement business continuity plans, prepare for future shocks (cash flow management, finding new partnerships and new markets) and develop strategies to retain the workforce (support wages, reskilling of workers, alternative working-time arrangements). The ILO is currently building the capacity of a number of specialised business consultants and coaches to assist in the delivery of the programme, which will be adapted to the current context and will assist MSMEs located in most-affected geographic areas of Beirut and Greater Beirut in relaunching their operations, rebuilding premises, restocking inputs and merchandise, relocating if need be, developing and implementing business continuity plans, preparing for potential future disaster through proper disaster risk management and planning to ensure a minimum sustainability of operations and workforce protection. In addition to technical support, the ILO will provide cash grants to MSMEs to support re-operationalisation and decent job retention. Based on a targeted needs assessment, cash grants could cover reconstruction/renovation of workplaces, restocking inputs and merchandise, wage (and benefits) support for a period of at least 6 months to SME owners, workers and potentially their families. Cash grants will be provided through a local partner, BDS organisation or MFI, which will be trained and coached on the technical assistance programme. Provision of cash grants and technical assistance will be aligned with other ongoing initiatives that target MSMEs, and a monitoring system will be put in place to track grant provision, and in particular wage support, rebuilding costs, investment costs and so on together with all partners. Potential collaboration will be sought with partners of the food security sector such as ACTED to join forces in needs assessments, roll-out of technical activities and grant provision.

2 - Activities

Support MSMEs through a combination of cash grants and technical support: quick assessment to determine extent of damage; cash grants to cover necessary repairs, rebuild shops and premises, and restock with merchandise and required inputs; counselling sessions, coaching and technical assistance and advice by experts on business continuity to accompany and guide MSMEs in their efforts to relaunch operations quickly and become more resilient. -- Preliminary selection criteria of MSMEs include: - Size of MSMEs between 5 and 10 employees - Geographic areas: Beirut and Greater Beirut, namely, Port Area, Achrafieh (Karm El Zeitoun, Sassine), Rmeil (Geitawi, Mar Mikhael), Saifi (Gemmayzeh), Medawwar (Karantina), Bourj Hammoud, Bachoura area (khanda’ El Ghami’ and Basta).- MSMEs (e.g. restaurants, coffee shops, grocery shops, mechanic garages, “mini markets”, bakeries, clothing shops), with preference given to those employing both Lebanese and Syrian refugees. - Formal and informal - Across all sectors - Most affected small businesses. Some criteria can be considered here to assess the severity of the damage by quickly looking at damaged infrastructure, inputs and merchandise, SME owners/workers physically affected by the blast, homes of SME owners/workers affected by the blast…- Damaged MSMEs that have not benefitted from other sources of financial assistance or have benefitted from very little financial support. - Assess the vulnerability of SME owners/workers by evaluating the damage of their houses, the number of households they support, family members physically affected by the blast or who have also lost their jobs, income before the blast… - Willingness to engage in a technical assistance programme and implement activities such as resilience planning, disaster management planning or business continuity plans.
