Project Summary

• Needs.
At HNO 2024 The overall estimated Sector PIN is 15.4 million people (66 per cent of the population). Agricultural production, especially wheat, still does not meet the full national need (more than 35 per cent below the need), is well below the long-term and the pre-crisis production average. This also applies to the other crops production, especially vegetables, legumes, cash crops and fruits, as well as livestock production. The drivers of low agriculture production include a continuous deteriorating economic situation, a decline in both the exchange rate and purchasing power of most people involved in agriculture-based livelihoods, high prices of agricultural production inputs and low quality of available seeds, weather anomalies, water scarcity, destruction of irrigation systems and canals, scarcity of energy supplies, especially fuel, and electricity for various farm and off-farm activities, which impacts on supply chains and transportation costs, leading to an increase in production costs. In addition to the reduced purchasing power of people, which restrict their capacity to access good quality and reliable inputs, which may lead to a decrease in the production.
• Intervention, Implementation of activities.
Output 1: Access to agricultural inputs to facilitate production of cereals, pulses or vegetable for 115,000 households increased.
Activities:
1. Selection of beneficiaries of the most vulnerable individuals and ability to utilize the provided inputs to full potential.
2. Procurement and distribution of agricultural inputs (certified cereal, pulse seeds/or vegetable, fertilizers, etc.…). In the event of difficulty in sourcing the required amount of certified seed, engage with local groups to screen and test quality planting material)
3. Selection and contracting an appropriate implementing partner
4. Undertake post-distribution monitoring and impact evaluation.
Output 2: Skills for cereal and pulses production of 115,000 targeted producers enhanced.
Activities:
1. Design a training programme based on FAO materials, training will focus on good agronomic practices including context-specific climate smart agriculture (CSA) approaches.
2. Conduct a training of trainers programme for technical extension workers.
3. ensure provision of technical support throughout the cropping season. using social media such as WhatsApp is good option.
NB: All the activities under each outputs will address specific needs of different gender and age groups.
* Protection Risk Analysis
1)Beneficiaries being harassed due being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries’ selection. Mitigation: different categories of affected population based on their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.
2)Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation: Establishing local beneficiary selection committees with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; ensuring effective communication for guidance in local language.
3)Risk of diversion of inputs/assistance. Mitigation: Establish community-based beneficiary selection and monitoring.
PSEA:
FAO is committed to prevent (PSEA) by FAO personnel and implementing partners and put in place adequate response mechanisms and this is will be through: 1. Developing a framework for effectively addressing issues of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse within FAO, 2. standards set by the SGB by all FAO personnel. 3. Establishing responsibilities on the implementation of PSEA procedures and activities. 4. Raising awareness and building capacity of FAO personnel on PSEA. 5. Establishing PSEA complaints mechanisms for FAO personnel. 6. Ensuring investigation procedures on SEA are in place. 7. Raising awareness of community members on PSEA and FAO policies and procedures to deal
with it.
8. Establishing community based complaints mechanisms.
