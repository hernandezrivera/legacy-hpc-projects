Project Summary

The complex and dynamic humanitarian context in South Sudan requires accurate, triangulated, timely and readily accessible information. This project aims to provide the humanitarian community with well analysed and actionable data to fill existing and anticipated information gaps on displacement, returns, and general population movement across South Sudan and inform a fair and evidence-based humanitarian response delivery and early recovery programming. The main deliverables will include:
- A regularly updated IDPs and returnees baseline at location and area level. Information will include population presence, movement reasons, and needs of IDPs and returnees.
- Providing regular information on sudden displacement and return movements requiring humanitarian assistance through IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) event tracking mechanism.
- Biometric data management for accountable, effective, and targeted delivery of humanitarian assistance.
- Representative Inter-Sectoral Needs Assessment (ISNA) on the household level to provide information on needs, vulnerabilities, and intentions to feed into South Sudan’s Humanitarian Response Planning (HRP) and Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO).
- Regular updates on population mobility at border points and key internal transit hubs, including displacement sites, through DTM’s flow monitoring mechanism.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is complex specifically due to its unique mobility dynamics, based on spontaneous and organized population movement (displacement and returns). As a result, deploying a well-informed humanitarian response and early recovery programming in South Sudan, requires timely, well-triangulated, and readily accessible information. Data that would inform evidence-based humanitarian, peace-building, and recovery planning actions must also be accurate, actionable, and able to provide regular updates to humanitarian, development, and government partners. IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Mobility Tracking Round 12 data indicates a total displacement population of 2.2 million IDPs, including more than 700,000 persons displaced due to the floods in South Sudan and 1.9 million returnees from internal displacement and across the borders. Over 7.8 million people in the country are expected to experience high levels of acute food insecurity by July 2023 (South Sudan IPC Analysis, November 2022). Continuous sub-national violence, armed clashes, and imminent flooding further exacerbate preexisting vulnerabilities. The multi-layered emergency situation in the country increased the need for adequate and well-analyzed data in order to effectively plan and deliver humanitarian assistance with limited funding. With many years of being at the forefront of humanitarian data provision globally and in South Sudan, IOM’s DTM information management system has been well-positioned to support the population in need by providing real-time data on displacement, returns, spontaneous events, flow monitoring, biometric registration data, and related surveys to partners who respond through the various clusters across the country. IOM DTM has been working closely with the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) and the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) within the existing cooperation agreements. The RRC and NBS have endorsed DTM methodology and regularly support data collection, and have approved IDP and returnee baseline, as the official data for the country.

2 - Project activities

DTM will capitalize on the successes achieved since its inception by providing quality data to stakeholders, partners, and the government. DTM’s country-wide recognition as the country’s main source of displacement data among other essential data for humanitarian response and transition. DTM will conduct mobility tracking assessments with a presence in all 10 states (78 counties) and the Abyei Administrative Area (AAA) to provide regular estimated displaced and returning population figures, locations, reasons for movement, and priority of humanitarian needs for IDPs and Returnees. DTM will leverage its network of key informants and enumerators in over 3,600 locations to ensure the regular provision of IDP baseline data. DTM will undertake two rounds of the Mobility Tracking activity. The event tracking mechanism will build on its existing network of 350 enumerators across 500 sub-areas within the country to track new events in 2023- spontaneous displacements due to floods (new and recurrent), and sub-national and localized conflicts. Prompt tracking and reporting will contribute to early warning efforts, informing upon rapid responses and conflict prevention. Spontaneous updates will be provided for critical events or those requested by humanitarian partners and regularly shared with the Needs Analysis Working Group (NAWG) as the key inter-agency platform for discussion on geographical prioritisation of the response. Furthermore, bi-weekly updates will be shared with partners and available at an interactive public dashboard. In addition, IOM DTM will conduct a representative Inter-Sector Needs Assessment(ISNA) at the household level in coordination with OCHA and will continue to collaborate with partners on the design, implementation, and analysis through the Technical Working Group (TWG). In collaboration with the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), IOM will continue using enumeration areas as the basis for creating the sampling frame. Methodology and tools will be decided in collaboration with partners and relevant stakeholders to ensure information is collected and analyzed in a timely manner to feed into the Humanitarian Needs Overview and Humanitarian Response Plan for 2024. DTM will continue with daily data collection at over 20 strategic border points, internal transit hubs, and displacement sites through flow monitoring registry. The network of observation points will be maintained and adjusted through regular monitoring and evaluation process to ensure coverage over key mobility corridors. Data collected and analysed from flow monitoring actives will provide timely information on displacement, mixed migration and return routes which would improve knowledge of migrant profiles, movement intention and motivation. Further on, the data will be analysed and shared with partners to inform planning and response in case of any public health emergencies (e.g. Ebola, COVID-19). The Displacement Sites Flow Monitoring (DSFM) mechanism would focus on providing insights on movement dynamics in and out of selected displacement sites. With over one million uniquely registered individuals in DTM’s secure database, IOM will undertake biometric registration of new beneficiaries as requested by partners through the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) at both federal and state levels, among others. Biometric maintenance will be conducted to update the status and locations of beneficiaries in the database IdpReady particularly focusing on the main displacement sites in Juba (IDP Camp 1 & 3), Bentiu (Bentiu IDP Camp), Malakal (Malakal Protection of Civilian Site) and Wau (Naivasha IDP Camp, Masna Collective Center). Biometric data sharing will be provided as regulated through relevant data sharing agreements and in adherence to IOM’s data protection principles. IOM DTM will also support partners with regular population counts at major displacement sites (Juba, Wau, Malakal, Bentiu) and other areas upon request.

3 - Project indicators

DTM will monitor success of the project through its annual satisfaction survey mechanism. More frequently, DTM will share relevant data collected, validated and processed with partners through the Needs Analysis Working Group, Inter-Cluster Coordination Group, and respective clusters including Protection, WASH, NFI, CCCM clusters, among others, to inform direct prioritization of humanitarian response. Based on the discussion and updates shared by partners, the DTM team will be able to monitor if flagged needs were responded to by partners. Moreover, DTM will regularly disseminate information products in a timely manner with partners through its already established mechanisms to ensure data and information reach targeted audience and informs response planning.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DTM is guided by IOM’s Global Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) Framework . This framework provides for, among other things, a multi-layered feedback complaints mechanism during all IOM interventions including DTM. DTM uses this mechanism both in camp and outside camp settings. Furthermore, DTM works closely with IOM Protection as well as the Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) Units to effectively capture the voices of the most vulnerable. For instance, during Biometric Registration, the DTM team ensures that a complaints feedback mechanism desk is availed on site to the beneficiaries to bring forward their complaints and or feedback. During the same registration, the DTM team is supported by the Protection team who conduct fast tracking of the vulnerable groups within the queues. This ensures that complaints are received, recorded and followed upon. In addition, in camp settings such as Bentiu, IOM has complaints and feedback desks in each and every sector of the former POCs which are accessible to all beneficiaries. Outside camp settings, the DTM team normally operates on a mobile basis and thus it is not feasible to have a static complaints desk however, it liaises with local authorities and fellow humanitarian partners on ground to receive complaints and feedback on its behalf. Through these mechanisms, the DTM beneficiaries including the vulnerable and marginalized will be able to effectively channel their complaints and feedback

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The main beneficiaries of DTM’s activities are humanitarian organizations, including UN agencies, international and national NGOs and government actors such as the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) and the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS). With the objective of facilitating access to data related to displacement, returns and population movement by all humanitarian partners regardless of their size and location within South Sudan, DTM publishes all non-sensitive data and analysis on its website (https://displacement.iom.int/south-sudan). This includes reports, maps, datasets, and interactive analytics, which are also circulated through a public mailing list. Bi-weekly updates on displacement and return are also available through an interactive dashboard. The tailored analysis is presented and disseminated in bilateral engagements with partners and humanitarian clusters and through active participation in coordination bodies such as the Needs Analysis, Information Management Working Group, Population Movement Working Groups, the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group, Advisory Group on Solutions, State/National Level Task Force on Solutions, Partnership for Resilience and Recovery (PFRR) and other relevant technical groups (including coordination forums in events of public health emergency). DTM will also aim to provide presentations on request by planning groups for Transition, Resilience, and Recovery endeavors countrywide, including better migration management structures, urban planning advocacy, and population tracking systems establishment. DTM is also an active participant in local humanitarian coordination bodies in Malakal, Juba, Bentiu, Abyei, and Wau, where it has a static presence and liaises with local partners across the country during regular staff deployments. Regular engagements also take place with national authorities through the RRC and NBS, and with local authorities at the state and county level. Particularly, data collection operations are always coordinated with the local RRC/ROSS secretaries to ensure appropriate communication with local authorities and communities. DTM actively participates in the public health response to COVID-19 as required by providing relevant analysis to the Ministry of Health, United Nations set-up structures and other response partners through the National Steering Committee for COVID-19, and with county health authorities near key points of entry. At the local level, DTM enumerators are regularly trained to provide information on the project to key informants and community members during data collection, including Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) reflecting institutional zero tolerance policy to PSEA. IOM has an independent PSEA complaint mechanism, which permits reporting of SEA cases directly and securely to IOM’s senior management in the mission or to a dedicated unit at its headquarters. They are also responsible for escalating feedback and complaints from local communities to DTM field staff and the project management team in Juba. Beneficiaries provide feedback and complaints through the above interactions, as well as through a yearly user satisfaction survey disseminated through DTM’s mailing list. Biometric registration activities have embedded complaint filing mechanisms, designed and maintained in collaboration with protection partners and CCCM (for activities that are taking place within displacement and former protection of civilian (former PoC) sites).

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

DTM works closely with IOM Protection which renders supports in the form of PSEA trainings as well as Protection, Gender and Disability Inclusion mainstreaming. Thus, DTM staff and enumerators receive training on Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), IOM’s GBV in Crisis Framework and Accountability to Affected Population principles. In turn, the DTM team raises awareness and informs beneficiaries and local authorities on IOM’s Zero Tolerance policy on PSEA as well as the reporting channels which include details of IOM PSEA focal persons per location, IOM PSEA reporting toll free lines and email address. IOM Protection team conducts training and sessions on PSEA for staff and consultants on a quarterly basis.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

DTM regularly adjusts its data collection tools and information products to account for feedback from data users, including international, national and local humanitarian, development and government actors. DTM also promotes, and actively participates in, joint data collection and analysis initiatives that bring together multiple humanitarian actors, actively seeking thematic advice from humanitarian clusters, technical agencies and other data users. DTM also seeks to provide baseline data on spontaneous population movements in order to provide information on initial population estimates and needs prior to inter-agency coordinated visits. DTM’s biometric registration modalities are similarly reviewed on a regular basis, in consultation with assistance providers, CCCM and protection partners based on feedback received for continuous improvement and to ensure data effectiveness to serve population in need

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

DTM does not directly provide humanitarian services to South Sudanese populations in need, except for biometric data management. While the risk of safety incidents affecting beneficiaries during biometric registration, authentication or verification cannot be excluded, all related exercises are preceded by a safety assessment to mitigate the risk. The activities are undertaken in coordination with other humanitarian partners, community structures and where relevant local authorities / UNMISS to ensure appropriate risk mitigation and protection measures are in place according to the context of each activity including deep field activities. Vulnerable individuals enjoy facilitated access to biometric services in collaboration with protection partners, minimizing their exposure to risk. Lack of access / usage of complaints and feedback mechanisms by beneficiaries of biometric identity services, respondents and key informants participating in DTM assessments is rated as a medium risk due to some the remote locations in which some DTM activities take place, which limit the ability of respondents and communities to access feedback and complaints mechanisms. Moreover, these beneficiaries may lack knowledge of the complaints and feedback mechanisms in place. DTM attempts to mitigate this risk by mainstreaming basic protection training to its staff and enumerators, stressing the importance of informing communities about the existence of feedback and complaints mechanisms. By embedding its data collection network within local communities and closely cooperating with local authorities (RRC / ROSS) and humanitarian partners, DTM ensures that feedback and complaints can be voiced both internally and through independent actors reducing the risk. DTM pro-actively involves members of marginalized communities and vulnerable individuals when conducting registration activities and other assessments. To achieve this, DTM provides data collectors with appropriateprotection training and strives to improve the diversity of its staff and enumerators. The risk of vulnerable groups or individuals being left out is low and the impact would mean either a lack of registration or not being represented in the evidence base for various programming, rather than actual denial of assistance. In both cases, the impact would be moderated by the fact that data users triangulate DTM data with independent primary and secondary sources. Personal data gathered from biometric registration and household surveys is stored and handled according to IOM’s data protection principles. Mitigation measures include secure data storage infrastructure and software, limitations in data access rights within the team to sensitive and personal data, and risk analysis before the inclusion of sensitive indicators in datasets that qualify as containing personally identifiable information. Risk of interference from external stakeholders is possible as the ongoing conflict provides incentives for local and national political actors and power brokers to manipulate humanitarian aid for personal and group gain, including by influencing the informational basis for the response. DTM mitigates this risk through regular remote and field monitoring of data collection exercises by IOM national and international staff, rotating national staff to different areas of the country to ensure cross-monitoring and avoid the establishment of excessive links with local authorities. By operating across conflict lines and upholding humanitarian principles of impartiality and independence, IOM minimizes interference by excluding conflict actors in its humanitarian activities, including data collection. Data is validated against secondary sources and contextual information to flag and address possible anomalies before publication, and DTM works closely with IOM’s protection and conflict analysis teams to ensure that published data and analysis are appropriately contextualized.
