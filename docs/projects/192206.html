Project Summary

Due to the unprecedented culmination of crises that Lebanon is facing, and in-line with the ERP objectives, this project aims to provide direct support to the most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant school-aged children and their families. These objectives will be achieved through community support, improving access to formal education services and contributing to the capacity of schools to absorb the growing demand of families in enrolling their children into the public school system. This will be done by provisioning basic education materials for children, capacity and competency building for teachers and increasing engagement for parents and caregivers in their ongoing support of their children’s education. Thereby, contributing to strengthening the public education system.
The project is aligned with the ERP specific objectives 1. Direct Community Support Measures to most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant children and 2. Improving Formal Education of most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant children through schools.
Project will cover 4 unions and municipalities in Akkar and 3 municipalities in the North.
The program will directly reach 14,850 Lebanese and migrant public schools’ students and their caregivers, 125 teachers and school staff, 20 public schools in Akkar and 60 public schools in The North. Moreover, the project will have a much wider impact in the target communities as it will develop the competency of teachers and caregivers: it is estimated that another 50,000 children and adults will be reached as indirect beneficiaries. Lastly, the project will contribute to improved equitable access to education and a reduction in barriers to education.
Lebanese and migrant school-aged children will be provided with catch-up and learning support classes. In addition, children will be provided with quality learning materials together with access to remote learning through devices and internet connectivity. Special devices pertaining to inclusion will be provided to children with special needs and disabilities when required. Children will have access to safe learning environments through protection and psychosocial support sessions adhering to the minimum standards for Education in Emergencies (EiE).
Parents/Caregivers will be given the knowledge, skills and tools to effectively support their children in their learning. Parents/Caregiver groups will be formed to support children’s learning and increase their engagement with the schools; guidance and continuous follow up by Outreach Workers will be provided to these groups.
Taking into consideration the current context, families face dire conditions and barriers in meeting their basic needs. To address this and access to education, households of children will be provided with cash so that parents/caregivers can pay school-fees and other education-related costs. Food assistance will contribute to children having the basic nutritional status necessary for effective learning. These activities and direct support to families will enable enrolment of children into formal education, and ensure regular attendance throughout the academic year.
In addition to children and families, the activities under this project aim to ensure public schools have the basic conditions required for effective teaching and learning. Public school teachers will be remunerated for their additional, supplementary responsibilities to children entering the public school system for the first time. Public schools will be provided with funds to cover and support some operational costs in order to increase their enrollment capacity to cater for the current and emerging demands of quality education while adhering to national and international standards on Education in Emergencies and Child Protection.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Responds to children and community needs through integrated approach embedding protection, PSEA, gender and social inclusion. WCH staff, associates and partners adhere to the PSEA elements of WCH's Code of Conduct and its Child Safeguarding Policy; includes knowing their responsibilities in keeping people safe from sexual exploitation/abuse and ensuring no harm comes to participants by conduct of staff, associates and partners. Identification and referral of protection concerns will be on-going in project as in all our projects. Girls and boys, as necessary, including with disabilities will be referred to services of case management unit, and to external services, when appropriate. A set of sessions on PSEA, gender and social inclusion will be provided to the members of the project Parent/ Caregiver groups and teachers. We continuously increase the knowledge and skills of partners on these topics in all projects so they will be the trainers/facilitators with WCH supporting.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Through it’s community-based approach, WCH effectively accesses vulnerable hard-to-reach populations, in line with its commitment to strengthen local capacity through a mutual learning process. WCH is a member of the CHS alliance and is taking steps to address the participation and accountability as pillars in the CHS commitments and within the principles of protection mainstreaming. Ongoing feedback and reflection sessions are held between the implementation teams and partners by community facilitators/teachers/parent groups with project participants through feedback group exercises, FGDs or progress tools used to evaluate all aspects of the project during face to face & remote modalities. Drop-out assessments are conducted to assess the reason and respond to it. Feedback and lessons learned are documented, analyzed and shared among decision makers, field/technical staff and communities/partners through learning events for a more effective and efficient processes and results.
