Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to address unmet humanitarian needs, contribute to reduction of avoidable mortality and morbidity, and improve well-being of newly and older displaced persons and non-displaced people who are severely affected by crisis in selected target locations in three states . ADD proposes to support 15 PHC facilities to serve an estimated more than 341,000 conflict-affected persons and host community members in the selected sites, with particular focus on women of reproductive age, and children under 5 years.
ADD will provide a core, yet comprehensive, primary and reproductive health services, which includes both curative and preventive care. Services provided in the clinics includes; (i) General clinical services: outpatient consultation services for diagnosis and treatment for common illnesses, basic laboratory investigations, short stay admission, and emergency referral system to hospital for selected cases; (ii) Maternal and newborn health: full RH package that includes antenatal care and pregnant women vaccination, skilled care during childbirth, essential newborn care, basic emergency obstetric care, post-partum care, family planning, and access to urinary tract infections and sexual and transmitted infections treatments etc.; (iii) Child health: routine EPI vaccines by trained cadre, IMCI trained cadre, screening of malnutrition and referral; (iv) Communicable diseases control: sentinel sites of early warning system of epidemic prone diseases with established surveillance system, weekly timely EWARS reports, free-of-charge access to diagnosis and treatment of malaria; (v) Environmental health: procedures for proper safe waste disposal and management, functioning basic incinerator, sanitation facilities. These activities are complemented by community-based outreach and health promotion activities to multiply impact. They include production and distribution of IEC materials for behavior change communication (BCC) on subjects of malaria and self-referral, STI/HIV prevention, pregnancy and childbirth danger signs and where/when to seek help, breast feeding, child home care, hygiene promotion and water and sanitation.
This project is part of a multi-sectoral program run by ADD in the targeted areas that includes Health and Nutrition (fund requested from UNICEF & WFP) and WASH (ongoing funded by UNICEF). This approach ensures synergistic effect to maximize impact and hence achieve the aim of the project.

Needs

According to Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) 2019, the total number of people estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance is more than 6 million with an increase of 15% compared to 2018. This increase in number can be justified by several factors such as large scale population displacement, influx of refugees and climate and socioeconomic conditions across the country. Host communities and residents are estimated to be 1.94 million which represent 34% of the total people in need of humanitarian assistance in Sudan. Considering the type of the need, there are 5.4 million people are in need for basic health services; 51% of them are female, 17 % are children and 5% are older people. It is estimated that 1.3 million are women of reproductive age who are in need for comprehensive reproductive health services. 216,000 women will be pregnant during 2019 and will require comprehensive reproductive health services. According to HeRM, there are only 24% functional health facilities offering the main service components of the PHC package:The risk of dying of under-five children before the five birthday is very high in the states of South Kordofan (95.4), Blue Nile (83.9) and White Nile (61.3), High stunting prevalence among children in the states of Blue Nile( 46.7 percent), South Kordofan (40.6 percent) and white Nile (36 .6 percent), in disease episodes regarding Percentage of children age 0-59 severing from diarrhea, and acute respiratory infection (ARI) as following white Nile (36.2% &25.3%) Blue Nile (20.3 % & 12.9%) and South Kordofan (7.6% & 5.0%).MICS 2014 highlighted lower ANC coverage in rural areas especially in conflict affected state Blue Nile 43% & and the percentage of skill attendants at birth also remains low as in South Kordofan (48.7) and Blue Nile (61%). Natural and manmade disasters are still the main hazards affecting the health in addition to communicable and non communicable disease burdens. ADD health project will contribute to fill the gaps and relive the suffering through provision of comprehensive health services at the community and health facility levels to the vulnerable host communities in South Kordfuan, Blue Nile and White Nile states. Concerns will be given to ensure delivering of quality health services to the most vulnerable population including women, men, elderly and children without any discrimination. It is expected that more than 85,000 women on reproductive age, 68,000 children U5 yrs and 187,000 boys, girls, men and elderly will have access to comprehensive health services at the selected geographical area.

Activities

The proposed interventions are in line with Humanitarian Response Strategic and health cluster objectives: - In collaboration with UNICEF Implement community –based Health insurance initiatives in Blue Nile state through community financing/ community empowerment • Deliver minimum basic package of primary health care services including maternal and child health) and support referral services to secondary health facilities with ensuring beneficiaries dignity ad privacy. • Supply the health facilities with medical supplies, equipment and medicine including provision of essential and live saving medicines for communicable and non communicable diseases.• Expand and maintain the health surveillance system with early warning mechanisms.• Provide comprehensive reproductive health services including antenatal and postnatal services, STIs treatment, family planning and Obstetric, new born care according to MOH Standers. • Support the implementation of expand program of immunization: routine and outreach immunization activities • Support the referral system for selected emergency cases especially obstetrical complication.• Rapid response to diseases outbreaks in affected areas with close coordination with heath sector.• Perform essential rehabilitation and maintenance of health facilities. • Building the capacity of health facility staff through regular training and close supervision. • Involve the communities and raise their awareness through conducting several health campaigns about certain diseases control and prevention.

Impact on environment/natural resources

ADD will pay attention to the Integration of environmental issues during any Health assessments will ensure that environmental harm is reduced or eliminated and environmental benefits are maximized., In the Blue Nile in Sudan, deforestation and water scarcity are the two main environmental problems, and projects that could potentially affect forests and other land vegetation and water sources need to eliminate and/or mitigate negative impacts as much as possible. ADD in designing the response – key environmental concerns will be built into the project. To maximize negative impacts on the forestry resources ADD will be the introduction of alternative construction material such as Stabilised Soil Blocks (SSB) or Cement Stabilised Blocks (CSB) and iron bar to replace fired bricks and wooden for construction activities, in addition to that ADD will work with CBos to fence the health facilities by tress instate of brisk. ADD will monitor and evaluation of the outcomes of a response is important to ensure that the effects sought for were indeed delivered, and to help identify possible negative environmental effects in time to allow for an adjustment of activities. ADD will pay attention to Community consultation and participation which is a key part of environmental management, including working with the national and state governmental authorities. Civil society organisations including CBOs will be consulted, promoted and their capacities enhanced. People in these community structure and CBOs have invaluable knowledge and skill in the management of the local environment, and will be responsible for building the systems for equitable sustainable management in future
