Project Summary

The FSA/FSLA 2021 highlights that food insecurity across Syria has increased significantly as a consequence of knock-on effects from the Lebanese economic crisis, tightening of unilateral coercive measures and consequences of Covid19. These factors have triggered unprecedented inflation rates of basic commodities, including basic food items, pushing additional people into food insecurity. Overall, the food security sector estimates 12.4 million people food insecure, including of 1.3 Million severely food insecure and additional 1.8 million people at risk, for a total of 14.2 million People in Need. WFP’s general food assistance covers the critical food needs of Syrian families across all 14 governorates. Through GFA activities, WFP’s assistance ensures that basic food needs are being met through a food basket planned to have a minimum of 1,700 kcal for severely food insecure households and 1,300 kcal for moderately food insecure households. 4 - Needs

in 2018/19, the HNO indicated 6.5 million people facing food insecurity. Following the 2020 FSA/FSLA, the 2021 HNO presents a picture where 12.4 million people (60% of the Syrian population) in Syria are food insecure and an additional 1.8 million people are at risk of food insecurity. Food insecurity fuelled by conflict and mass population displacement was exacerbated by the impacts of the financial crisis in Lebanon and the COVID-19 pandemic as well as continued economic deterioration. The purchasing power of Syrian households was further eroded as the Syrian pound continued to lose its value, while food prices increased by 236 percent from December 2019 to December 2020. Furthermore, projections indicate that significant improvements in food security are unlikely in the near-to-medium term. As food insecurity soared, vulnerable segments of the Syrian population, unable to access diversified diets, have become at an increased risk of malnutrition. The national prevalence of chronic malnutrition stood at 12.6 percent in 2019 (SMART survey 2019), peaking at 22 percent in some governorates. While no nationwide nutrition assessment was conducted in Syria in 2020, available data from north-western Syria highlights that chronic malnutrition among children reached 33 percent in September 2020, an increase of 74 percent from May 2019. Similarly, the prevalence of acute malnutrition among pregnant and lactating women and girls (PLWG) reached 11 percent in September 2020.

5 - Implementation of activities

WFP’s general food assistance covers the critical food needs of Syrian families across all 14 governorates. Through GFA activities, WFP’s assistance ensures that basic food needs are being met through a regular food assistance. The basket provided to vulnerable families will be tailored to their needs in alignment with the Food Security Sector guidance. In areas of high food insecurity such as the IDP camps of North East Syria, WFP aims to provide a full 2,100-kcal basket. Resources permitting, WFP aims to provide a food ration to some 7.75 million people per month as in-kind food basket or through a combined cash-based and in kind commodity hybrid. Given the increased food insecurity needs, as resulted from the most recent FSA/FSLA, and the urgency to scale-up assistance, this proposal focuses on GFA. Part of the population identified as food insecure but showing the potential to recover its livelihoods will be assisted through livelihoods projects, through a revised livelihood strategy focusing on community asset rehabilitation, which will progressively reduce the dependency of entire communities on unconditional food assistance. Further, WFP seeks to catalyze the resilience of local markets, but increasing its cash-based transfer footprint, allow beneficiaries to access markets. Food value chains, especially bread, are also being supported at a more systemic level. Funding for such activities is being sought through different channels, although coordination within WFP across different programmes is ongoing. Finally, WFP is carrying out a nationwide Vulnerability Needs Assessment (VNR) exercise, which will allow accurate and efficient prioritization of food assistance to the households identified as most vulnerable.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The Syrian crisis continues to expose the affected population to a multitude of protection risks, including violence against civilians, sexual and gender-based violence, movement and access restrictions, and a lack of documentation, amongst others. These risks are often intertwined and rarely experienced in isolation. With an awareness that these factors can exacerbate food insecurity, restrict access to humanitarian assistance, and that food assistance can both mitigate or compound protection concerns, ensuring the safety, dignity and integrity of its beneficiaries is a key priority for WFP in Syria. These efforts are aligned with WFP’s corporate Policy on Humanitarian Protection. While WFP conducts protection risk assessments for its activities in Syria on an ongoing basis, WFP in June 2019 conducted a comprehensive protection risk analysis exercise across all programmes to inform its protection strategy by identifying actual and potential risks experienced by beneficiaries. These included a fear of violence, explosive remnants or war; movement restrictions; a lack of personal documentation; constraints related to the safe, consistent and dignified provision of assistance due to inadequate facilities, as well as challenges related to physical access and transportation. WFP supports partners in applying protection principles at distributions, including the physical enhancement of food distribution points (FDPs), raising awareness among staff and cooperating partners on the inclusion of people living with disabilities, PSEA protocols and AAP. In 2020 WFP has increased the staffing of its protection team, rolled out a Beneficiary Feedback Mechanism so that trends on protection concerns and be updated and addressed.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

WFP operates in an exceptionally complex and unpredictable environment characterized by active hostilities; shifting areas of territorial control; a deteriorating economy; as well as unpredictable food price volatility in local markets. Discriminatory socio- cultural norms continue to push households headed by women disproportionally deeper into food insecurity. WFP’s ability to fulfil its mission in the Syrian context presupposes a certain acceptance of and appetite for risk, so long that appropriate mitigations are in place. WFP Syria self identifies as highly risk averse when it comes to the health, safety and security of its staff, as well as incidents of fraud and corruption. In programme design, WFP Syria has committed to a higher appetite for risk, although some of the risk identified are progressively being addressed. Food procurement is done locally or regionally whenever feasible and cost convenient. To mitigate potential risks, WFP ensure adequate lead time, diversifies regional sourcing, especially in large contracts. WFP has put in place and regularly updates its Covid19 SOPs clearly outlining measures that must be followed to continue GFA Programmes without interruption, while at the same protecting WFP staff and beneficiaries. Lastly Cross lines access responses and prepositioning exercises are planned to mitigate a possible non-extension of the UNSCR for the XB operation and to ensure the continuity of the operation on the north west of Syria. In addition, to prepositioning efforts to prepare for a potential non-renewal of the UNSCR after July include piloting different transfer modalities; exploring scale up capacity of other partners and exploring alternative procurement options.
