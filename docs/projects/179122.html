Project Summary

An estimated 6.9 million people (97% children) need humanitarian education assistance based on HNO summary 2021. The sector is not able to provide a 2021 estimate of out of school children, however, in 2020 an estimated 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out3. Due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours due to COVID-19, worsening economic conditions, increased population numbers and displacement, the numbers of children out of school, and children who are at risk of dropping out, almost certain to increase.
HF works to strengthen the capacity of the education facilities in Northern Syrian and enhance the possibility of delivering better spaces for learning in schools to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children aged 5-17 years This designed project is to enhance the overall status of schools through repairing to return to their schools or enhancing the current condition of schools.
HF will rehabilitate 40 damaged schools in Afrin and Dana.
400 class room and 20000 beneficiaries will benefit from this.
The rehabilitation activities will include repairing damaged classrooms and WASH facilities; sanitation systems, bathrooms, and adequate water for personal hygiene and to clean toilets rely on INEE standards. After rehabilitation of the schools, the project will provide furniture; such as desks, chairs, blackboards.
HF has extensive experience in rehab works for damaged building and schools, more than 6000 Houses was rehabilitated through teams of HF funded by many donors, where SCHF is one of them. in addition, HF rehabilitated about 9 schools in the Syrian crises were damaged in Southern Syria.
HF will implement the rehabilitation work for those 40 schools in Afrin and Idleb in order to provide a better place for the students to have proper, safer, and more adequate education. Those schools are meant to be run by Syrian NGOs that are providing education services while they can not provide rehab work. In this context, HF will provide needed services as rehab work starting from construction works and WASH facilities, not to forget providing proper furniture for those schools.
special attention will be given to children with disabilities through providing needed facilities for children with disabilities regarding ramps that facilitate their entrance to the schools and classes or using bathrooms or any other services in schools.
This project is linked to HRP Strategic Objective 3
Increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities.
Specific Objective 3.1 Improved access to basic services, electricity, water, education and health.

4 - Needs

Based on HNO-2021, Schools have suffered immensely as a result of both targeted attacks and collateral damage. It is estimated that in Aleppo Governorate alone there are 42 - 55 million tons of rubble and as per OCHA reports.As per HNO 2021 an estimated 6.9 million people (97% children) need humanitarian education assistance. The sector is not able to provide a 2021 estimate of out of school children, however, in 2020 an estimated 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out. Children who are out of school face increased protection risks including child marriage and the worst forms of child labor. The escalation of violence has led to increasing war-related psychological trauma for children, their families, and education personnel. The high proportion of crisis-affected children and adolescents out of the public education systems is of high concern since they are at bigger protection risks situations, many children were forced to stay out of school or if managed to access schools, they were often exposed to unsafe and overcrowded classrooms. The destruction and damages of schools have led to an acute need for additional learning spaces for both host community children and internally displaced children residing in these areas. Teaching capacity remains overstretched with more than 150,000 teachers and education staff who have left their positions since the start of the crisis, the capacity and capability of available teachers are being severely overstretched. These shortages have limited the quality and provision of education.HF had conducted a RNA in which the targeted schools were assessed to be in need for rehabiliation.

5 - Implementation of activities

HF will rehabilitate 40 damaged schools. we will target the schools in (Aleppo/Afrin district- Idlib Dana) where its severity scale 5 (based on HNO2021).Schools will be repaired to targeted areas that promote equity and the physical safety of learners, teachers, and other education personnel. Schools will be designed giving careful thought to who uses the learning space. Spaces will be appropriate to sex, age, physical ability, and cultural considerations of all users. Entrances and exits will be repaired to allow students, teachers, and other education personnel to exit safely in an emergency. rehabilitation activities will include repairing damaged classrooms, roofs, doors, and WASH facilities; such as sanitation systems, bathrooms (Girls, boys, and CWD), solid waste disposals facilities, drainage facilities, and providing adequate water for personal hygiene and cleaning toilets rely on INEE standards. Sanitation facilities will be accessible for PWD and will maintain privacy, dignity, and safety. Toilet doors will lock from the inside.To prevent sexual harassment and abuse, separate toilets for boys/men and girls/women will be located in safe, convenient, and easily accessible places. After rehabilitation of the schools, the project will provide furniture; such as desks, chairs, blackboards, and laboratory equipment. Rehabilitation needs and related costs will be assessed and on the basis of which a targeted rehabilitation plan is designed and then implemented. During implementation of project, we will take into consideration gender-sensitive and disability-friendly that would ensure protection needs of boys and girls as well as the needs of children with disabilities allowing adequate access to all school facilities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served/cannot access or if information offered is not consistent between communities. Mitigating Measures:Continuously ensure service mapping, consultation with community on program interventions, regular communication with different communities on availability of services including host communities and returnee populations. Expansion of services to non-camp locations where needs are present, to avoid concentration of services in camps. Monitoring Mechanism: Regular consultations with communities, focus group discussion FGDs, feedback boxes Risk: Resistance from local authorities to accessing and implementing programming.Mitigating measures: Horan Foundation HF has established relationships with relevant local authorities and stakeholders, such as the local council, education directorate, ministries, community gatekeepers, and religious leaders. Monitoring Mechanism: Regular meetings with relevant authorities.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1: Access to targeted areas remains possible and safe. Risk1: Access to one (or more) of targeted areas becomes hindered or insecure. Likelihood: medium. Effect: medium. Mitigation: HF will advise staff on alternative routes to mitigate the risk. If the security situation changes in a certain area, we can wait or postpone activities for a few days or change targeted areas to another accessible area in need of support. Assumption 2: Skilled and unskilled workers can be hired from the local market to completerehabilitation works. Risk: It might be difficult to find enough qualified technicians and engineers to implement the rehabilitation works, Likelihood: low. Effect: low. Mitigation: contract and recruit high-quality workers from HF roster or hire from a wider geographicrange if needed. Assumption3: Rehabilitation items and materials are available to be procured locally. Risk 3: It might become difficult to locally secure one (or more) of the needed rehabilitation items/services of good quality or qualified workshops to implementthe rehabilitation works. Likelihood: medium. Effect: high. Mitigation3: through previous experience was able to directly purchase the materials from larger markets within Syriaand transport them to the project sites. Moreover, we will seek to contract high-capacity trusted vendors who can deliver the required rehabilitation works in Syria effectively.
