Project Summary

The project aims at preventing, mitigating and addressing the protection risks of affected populations in priority areas through three main activities: 1) community-based protection, including community empowerment, community outreach and community participation, 2) protection monitoring, analysis and advocacy, 3) provision of protection services for persons with specific needs and institutional support.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

UNHCR will undertake the following activities: 1) Community-based protection, including community empowerment of a network of Protection Focal Points (PFPs); 2) Protection monitoring, for the purpose of data analysis and advocacy; 3) Protection services for persons with specific needs, including case management, psychosocial support and legal assistance; and 4) Institutional support with a particular focus on the Ministry of Gender, Child Protection and Social Affairs, the National Institute for Disaster Management (INGC), the District Infrastructure and Planning Service (SDPI) and the National Directorate of Civil Identification.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The main objectives of the project is to prevent, mitigate and address the protection risks of affected populations in priority areas. To achieve this, UNHCR will undertake the following activities: 1) Community-based protection: i) Community empowerment: UNHCR will expand its network of Protection Focal Points (PFPs) who will be trained to identify and respond to protection challenges in their areas through a community-based approach. This includes: i) referring persons with specific needs to case management providers and specialized services; ii) disseminating information on human rights, SGBV prevention and response, child protection, protection of persons with specific needs and referral pathways; and iii) organizing community-led initiatives. ii) Community outreach: UNHCR will expand its mobile protection desks who will be managed by UNHCR or partners staff to provide individual counselling to affected populations and conduct identification and referrals of persons with specific needs. 2) Protection monitoring: UNHCR will collect data at household level with the aim of identifying protection gaps and incidents, undertaking analysis of protection and displacement trends, and conducting advocacy with Government or clusters. 3) Protection services for persons with specific needs: UNHCR will undertake case management of persons with specific needs, including GBV survivors and children at risk, and will provide specialized services such as psychosocial support, legal assistance (specially for legal documentation, birth registration and HLP). 4) Institutional support: UNHCR will strengthening cooperation with the Ministry of Gender, Child Protection and Social Affairs, the National Institute for Disaster Management (INGC), the District Infrastructure and Planning Service (SDPI), the National Directorate of Civil Identification and provincial and municipal authorities, in order to improve the protection of IDPs. UNHCR will provide trainings on international and regional legal framework for internal displacement, GBV prevention and response, child protection, protection of persons with specific needs and PSEA, among other topics. UNHCR will support the rehabilitation of infrastructure of Government offices and will donate office supplies and IT equipment to them. Finally, UNHCR will also support the domestication of the African Union Convention for the Protection and Assistance of Internally Displaced Persons in Africa (Kampala Convention). Cabo Delgado: Pemba, Metuge, Chiure, Montepuez, Namuno, Balama, Mecufi, Mueda, Ancuabe, Palma, Nangade and Ibo.Nampula: Meconta, Memba, Mecuburi, Rapale, Nacaroa, Nampula city, Erati, Monapo, Nacala-Porto, Ribale, Mossuril, Nacala-a-VelhaNiassa: Mecanhelas, Lichinga city, Marrupa, Ngauma, Cuamba, Sanga, Lago, Maua, MandimbaZambezia: Nicoadala, Alto Molucue, Namacurra, Quelimane.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

UNHCR staff and its partners are trained on humanitarian principles and the key elements of the centrality of protection: do not harm principle, safety and dignity, effective access to services and assistance, accountability of affected populations and community participation and empowerment. In Nampula UNHCR is already delivering, in coordination with INGC, trainings on protection of IDPs to local authorities in places where IDPs have arrived. Such trainings will be expanded into Niassa and Cabo Delgado Provinces. UNHCR has developed a protection monitoring tool for identifying risk profiles and collecting protection incidents, with indicators of age, gender and diversity. In the face of any immediate threat to security that is identified, an emergency protection or assistance service is activated. Protection interventions focus on saving lives and guaranteeing the rights to safety and physical integrity of affected populations. In addition, in order to guarantee the dignity of people, it is planned to provide timely and quality services taking into account the background of the individuals, the respect for their culture and religion and the survivor-centre approach, respecting their freedom of choice.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

UNHCR has developed SOP for humanitarian assistance and has adopted the Protection cluster vulnerability targeting criteria based on the specific needs of individuals. UNHCR also uses a global tool for classifying the level of risk of individuals (Heightened Risk Identification Tool) to assess the level of risks according to which protection and assistance services are provided.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

UNHCR plans to provide individual assistance based on needs, taking into account the targeting vulnerability criteria agreed by the Protection cluster and the standard specific needs criteria (and persons with disabilities are one of these criteria), prioritizing interventions based on the most common and urgent needs. UNHCR protection assessments include desegregated data on the specific needs considering age gender, diversity and disability approach. UNHCR also conducts specific consultations with persons with disabilities in order to assess their specific protection issues and provide personalized assistance and material support.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

UNHCR take environmental impact into account in all its operations, given that the environmental damage left by displaced populations could be a major concern. As part of its community-based protection strategy, UNHCR ensures that the communities are sensitized and engaged on the protection of the environment, emphasizing their responsibility for the local environment where they settle. As part of the community initiatives that will be supported in 2021, UNHCR will consider joint eco-friendly projects with IDPs and host communities.
