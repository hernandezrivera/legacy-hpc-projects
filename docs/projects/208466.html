Project Summary

According to IPC May 2023 report, It is estimated that nearly 4.4 million children aged 0 – 59 months are suffering and will likely continue suffering from acute malnutrition in the period of May 2023 – April 2024 in northwest and northeast Nigeria. This includes 1.04 million cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 3.37 million cases of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). In addition, 585,000 pregnant or lactating women are acutely malnourished and will likely continue to be malnourished and are in need of treatment.
According to Humanitarian Situation Update (September,2023) buletin, Several cycles of the Cadre Harmonisé (CH) analysis unveiled the problem situation of populations in some inaccessible areas. From the results of March2023 CH analysis in which 3,207,298 and 4,250,397 persons for the (Mar –May) and (June – August 2023) periods, respectively, were classified in phase3 – 5 of acute food and nutrition insecurity across the three states of Borno,Adamawa and Yobe, large proportion of this populations are located in both the totally and partially inaccessible areas of these BAY states. The final results from the Mar, 2023 CH round further reveal presence of 300,807 people in CH Emergency (phase 4) in Mar to May, 2023, with high risk of further deterioration to almost 522,366 in Emergency at the peak of the lean season next year .
To address the gap above, GHIV Africa will be responding to nutrition and food security need of the vulnerable group through Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) and In kind.

2 - Activities

For Nutrition:To Address the needs GHIV Africa will be carrying out the following activities; Screening and referral of children 0-59 months for acute malnutrition and feeding difficulties at community, - Establish SCs for the management and treatment of SAM with complication, -Capacity building of government health and nutrition staff -management of malnutrition with medical complications - Treatment of MAM under 5 with Tom brown supplement approach and supporting MAM PLW with cash to improve their nutrition status. - Training of mothers and caregivers on mother MUAC - Skilled IYCF counselling to PLW and caregivers. Assistance will be through CVA and in kind modality.For Food insecurity needs, GHIV Africa shall be addressing this needs through provision of Agricultural assistance to the vulnerable groups. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

GHIV Africa as a member of Network of Borno State women led organization (BONWOC) and also a member of Gender Technical Working Group (GTT) Northeast Nigeria, will ensures gender equality is fully integrated throughout the project, starting from targeted inclusive implementation processes to staff recruitment and the participation of both men and women in project structures and evaluation processes. Women and men will be given equal chances during project staff recruitment, with women highly encouraged to apply for positions. In addition, because of their important role in successful community mobilization, the project will deploy as many female workers as possible. While all program staff are accountable for project gender equality and safe programming goals, In collaboration with the government, awareness/capacity building of community nutrition and health workers participating in the project will be raised around the effects of gender inequality, power dynamics harmful traditional practices that have a direct impact or hinder on access to education, especially girls and children with disabilities, as well as the differential experiences and effects of child protection and safeguarding issues on girls, boys and CWD through training. Activities proposed are specifically designed to better meet the need of vulnerable group including disability status and gender groups. GHIV Africa follows the principle of “Do No Harm” which will be considered in all of its activities, which requires the organization to take specific steps and measures to protect and reduce the risk of harm from all intervention processes and activities , such as participatory beneficiary selection, involvement of community members throughout the implementation, informing them about the intervention’s aims and activities, informed consent collection, conducting a risk assessment prior to engagement , as well as ensuring the existence of a functioning and responsive feedback and complaint mechanism at community level that allows timely action to address issues of concern that may cause or put beneficiaries at risk of harm, reputational risk or create conflict in the beneficiary communities. Moreover, identifying gender-differentiated opportunities and constraints for improving access to education during the design phase of a project often leads to better interventions. In line with this, and building on our ongoing experience, sensitization on gender, child protection and safeguarding concerns is included in any training and awareness raising activity, especially during the inception workshop. Other sensitization measures include the mandatory onboarding signature of the Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct by GHIV Africa staffs and associates, and the gender responsive safeguarding risk assessments conducted prior to engagement to mitigate all concerns of risk of harm.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GHIV Africa is committed to the principles of participation and downward accountability. GHIV Africa is focal point for AAP and child safeguarding for EiE northeast Nigeria. GHIV Africa has established a Complaint and response mechanism (CRM) – a formalized process providing a safe confidential, accessible, transparent and effective channel for beneficiaries and other relevant stakeholders to raise concerns, register complaints, call for investigation and make suggestions. GHIV Africa will systematically investigate and follow up on complaints in a timely manner. The purpose of the CRM and relevant contact information (hotline,suggestion boxes, email, physical address etc.) is communicated to beneficiaries through complaints boxes wherever relevant, project activities as well as on distributed materials and / or at distribution sites. Staff will explain the purpose to beneficiaries. Received complaints are categorized according to level of sensitivity and severity and responded to in accordance to GHIV Africa’s Standard Operating Procedures for Programme-related Complaints Response and Feedback Mechanism. GHIV Africa will inform beneficiaries and the broader community on the selection criteria as well as on the distribution process in order to avoid the creation of tension. GHIV Africa’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) mechanisms provide a two-way communication channel through which beneficiaries and affected populations can give feedback and complaints, which will enable GHIV Africa to adjust its intervention accordingly and address issues directly in a time-effective manner. GHIV Africa will also ensure that its staff will comply with its code of conduct and serious measures will be taken if staff violates it during the course of the project.
