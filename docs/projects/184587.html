Project Summary

The proposed project aims to cover the WASH needs of the vulnerable host and IDPs community, the high humanitarian needs in Somalia are driven by seasonal floods, severe drought conditions, acute water diarrhea/ cholera outbreaks and protracted crises, exacerbated by COVID-19 outbreak. In May 2021, 50 of the 74 districts in Somalia faced severe water shortages and water prices increased and this is expected to crossover to 2022 with an already delayed Deyr(October-November) rains, Drought remains the main driver of WASH needs in Somalia leading to displacement and communal conflict over water points and the project intends to cover the needs & offer both emergency and long term sustainable solution to avert the potential risk of losing life’s through the multiple shocks, The WASH response will contribute to increased access to water and sanitation facilities for the vulnerable communities and prevention of a further increase in AWD/Cholera which is endemic in Somalia , as well as raise awareness to prevent the spread of COVID-19 through risk communication and community engagement, and support compliance to recommended measures through hygiene promotion, hygiene kits distribution and increased access to water and soap. To further improve the contribution of WASH to the achievement of health outcomes, the proposed WASH response will invest improvements of behavioral change through advocacy of attainment of community ownership of projects to prolong the lifespan & sustainability of projects for access to WASH facilities in health care facilities and other public institutions, the project intends to complement with Action Against Hunger Health and Nutrition projects in hard to reach areas to reach the elderly, disable and vulnerable person are included and targeted in activities, Overall, the project envisages to reach 70,000 individuals (19,809M, 19180 F,15,505 B and 15,506 G) including 7,111 people living with disabilities for a period of 12 months in Bakool, Bay, Lower Shabelle, Nugaal, Banadir, ACF WASH project will be gender sensitive through inclusion of women in WASH committee and are represented in crucial roles, are consulted in the design of the project especially during the planning,design and distribution of MHM kits to understand women and girls preference & functionality of the products in the local markets, The project will adopt protection mainstreaming in WASH and that latrines are gender segregated and with a barrier screen for privacy and WASH facilities are disable friendly through construction of ramps during distribution of WASH Kits triage sort of mechanisms will be adopted to give priority to those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with disability, unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating women, so that they wait for less time. Community hygiene volunteers and community health workers will be trained and work closely with the targeted communities and ensure that WASH minimum at health facilities are attained and the project linked with other interventions(FSL, Health & Nutrition) supported by Action Against Hunger for optimal impact of the project

1. - Projects activities

The proposed project will target the most vulnerable crises affected households without access to basic WASH services. ACF will provide both emergency and sustainable WASH services in the targeted locations prioritizing cholera/AWD prone areas, communities persistently affected by drought/floods, communities with high malnutrition caseloads and IDP settlements with low WASH coverage Emergency water supply will target the most vulnerable crises affected households without access to a sufficient amount of potable water and this will run for a period not exceeding two months. To support the speedy recovery of the WASH market environment in the crisis-affected areas, ACF will past experience in the use of cash and vouchers in WASH to provide drinking water using village water vouchers The targeted households will be supported to carry out household water treatment(HHWT) and safe storage and provided with containers for transport and storage of water. Further, ACF will carry out bucket chlorination at strategic water points in the targeted communities, monitor the quality of water by testing samples collected at different points in the water supply chain and at the same time invest in rehabilitation and upgrading of strategic water points to ensure basic water access for the targeted populations post the emergency water trucking phase. pipeline extensions and construction of water kiosks will be conducted at strategic locations to reduce women and girls exposure risk of gender based violence(GBV) when collecting water from too distant water sources, To ensure proper operation and maintenance of the new and rehabilitated water points. ACF will establish and trained gender balanced Water management committees, ACF will build the capacity of the committees through joint training with MoEWR staff and strengthen capacity of private sector players (fast moving spare parts). As part of disaster risk reduction, the aprons of the water points will be raised to protect the facilities from floods and drainage of wastewater included in the design to maintain the water point surroundings free of contaminants. For communities with poor or no access to latrines, ACF will rehabilitate the existing latrines and construct new ones that are flood proof to ensure basic and dignified sanitation access for targeted communities. Communal latrines and bathrooms in health facilities, schools and IDP settlements will be gender segregated and 10% of the facilities will be suitably adapted to make them age and disability friendly ACF will recruit and train community hygiene volunteers from the targeted communities to carry out robust hygiene promotion targeting behaviors identified through Barrier Analysis studies and conduct distribution of Hygiene & menstrual hygiene management kits and ACF will preposition WASH supplies in hotspot location with risks of disease outbreaks for quick response and to further contribute to sustainability of the hygiene promotion initiatives, ACF will utilize the increasing capacity of the government departments and conduct joint trainings for hygiene promoters with public health staff from MoH at district level. Detailed Activities include: Emergency water services in vulnerable settlements and communities reaching 12,600 people with 15l/p/d of potable water (45litres per household) for a period of 60 days. Emergency sanitation services will reach 10,000 people while 20,400 people will be reached with critical hygiene items and key hygiene messages. In total, 13,500 non-displaced people will be reached with sustainable access to safe water services in targeted settlements and communities including schools and healthcare facilities ,13,500 displaced people reached with sustainable access to safe water services in targeted settlements and communities, Project activities will be closely coordinated with other WASH partners and other clusters to add value to the overall response through integrated approach

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project will apply a mix of cash, voucher and in kind programming. In-kind distributions will continue as the dominant delivery model for items not available in the immediate market or are not available in the quality required. Such items include menstrual hygiene materials and water treatment tablets. Cash and vouchers will be prioritized for all other WASH kit items and water vouchers for communities to negotiate with water service providers and value for money (VFM)achieved as market assessments in the project areas have confirmed market functionality/flexibility and availability of the items at prices and of the quality demanded. The CTP approach will enable ACF to contribute to the promotion of a people centered approach as well as promote economic recovery in the target areas and by working directly with the market, ACF will reinforce the existing economic relationships between the targeted beneficiaries and the WASH market players. By strengthening this relationship, the proposed project will contribute to the sustainability of WASH services in the targeted areas,

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

ACF has conducted systematic market assessments with a robust logistic team who conduct market assessment routinely. The information collected over time has confirmed the availability of WASH goods on the market in addition to confirming re-stocking capacities of the retailers and wholesalers in the intervention areas. And ACF prepositions WASH supplies in markets that are deemed unpredictable & have accessibility concerns As such, ACF has a good understanding of the local WASH market infrastructure and services and how the targeted population interact with them during both ordinary times and times of crises. ACF will use the acquired market information to inform the proposed project preparednessplanning to ensure that should shocks occur in the project area, ACF will have capacity to immediately provide lifesaving assistance to the affected populations

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance: ACF have strong global policies in place to prevent Fraud, Conflict of Interest, and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), along with a strong whistleblowing policy toreceive serious complaints. ACF already maintains an active Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) in all project areas, including contact numbers. Each complaint or request for assistance is responded to in a timely manner, and serious complaints are raised to ACF regional office to ensure accountable management. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services: ACF will identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and ensure they are also targeted. ACF will identify central locations for delivery of NFI distribution to ensure beneficiaries do not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access them. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources: ACF will engage District local authorities and water management committees’ structures including opinion leaders at the kick planning stage and during implementation to allow them participate in project implementation decisions.
