Project Summary

The RHC project aims to restore access to quality health care for conflict affected populations in Syria through the rehabilitation of health care facilities across multiple governorates based on identified needs and prioritization of the most vulnerable locations.

Needs

Over eight years of ongoing conflict has left the Syrian population in severely affected, and with limited access to health care. There have been thousands of injuries to the civilian population, internal displacement, large scale migration, and emerging returnee population, and mounting humanitarian needs. Throughout 2018, many areas became accessible resulting in men, women, boys and girls to return to their areas of origin which limited access to basic services, including health care facilities, many of which are either closed or only partially functioning as they are severely disrupted or damaged. From January to September 2018, the Health Resources Availability Monitoring System (HeRAMS) reported that 46 per cent of health facilities across Syria were either non-functional or partially functional. Almost two thirds of health care workers have fled the country, leaving a drop in the number of available and skilled health professionals. This has caused major disruption and closure of services, limiting the ability of vulnerable people to access essential lifesaving medical assistance and critically affecting service sufficiency of remaining facilities and availability of staff to provide services. Based on ADRA’s Syria’s assessments of health facilities in Rural Homs, in addition to being only partially functional due to conflict related damages or limited maintenance for an extended period of time, lack simple facilities that would promote access for women, children and people with disabilities. This includes functional gender segregated WASH facilities, access ramps and suitable WASH facilities for those with age or mobility related challenges. Females make up 72 per cent of people in need of health assistance in Syria, out of which 5.2 million women of reproductive age are in need of Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child and Adolescence (RMNCH+A) services. During the past years, there has been an increase in the number of trauma cases among the population, especially among young and and middle aged adult males directly impacted by conflict, as well as men and women of all ages who have been indirectly injured as a result of conflict or accident. Witnessed first-hand by ADRA Syria staff conducting PSS support in Aleppo and Rural Damascus, there is a high need for PSS especially for those living in areas of intensified fighting such as East Ghouta and Deir Ez Zor including young children and children who have only experienced conflict, as well as adolescents and young adults who can remember life before the conflict. Middle aged and older adults are more likely to feel the stress of providing for their families. The World Health Organization has shown that assessments conducted in 2017, one in five Syrians is at risk of developing moderate mental health issues and in 1 in 30 is at risk of developing severe or acute mental health problems. A full 30% of trauma is reportedly linked with a Permanent Disability Outcome and the remaining survivors of trauma suffer temporary physical disability. The people at risk in the health sector include survivors of trauma, including the war wounded, people who live with chronic diseases; children who are not vaccinated; and those in need of mental health and psychosocial support. In newly accessible areas, there is a lack of basic utility services, including electricity, fuel, safe drinking water and basic sanitation services. This has led to an increase in disease outbreaks such as diarrheal diseases, typhoid and hepatitis; while overcrowding as a result of massive displacement increases the risk of outbreaks of disease, which can be prevented with the right health care and vaccinations.

Implementation of activities

ADRA will select areas in conjunction with the Governorate and field staff to rehabilitate health centers in vulnerable areas with limited humanitarian intervention. ADRA will only rehabilitate centers in areas where municipalities already have qualified staff and the necessary medical equipment to maintain the centers after implementation. A social assessment will take place to assess the potential impact of the project and will consult with a range of stakeholders including men, women, boys, girls, people with disabilities and the elderly to identify specific needs of the community and adapt the rehabilitation as required. A technical assessment will be undertaken in the potential centers and a contractor selected based on a competitive process. Necessary rehabilitation will be undertaken to ensure the full functionality of the center and will include the rehabilitation of doors, windows, consultation and waiting rooms, gender segregated WASH facilities and necessary modifications will be made to ensure access for people with a disability. Where possible, ADRA will intervene in related sectors (such as WASH, education, protection and early recovery) in the same areas to ensure full access to basic services and the best possible health outcomes for men, women, boys, girls, people with disabilities and the elderly. ADRA will coordinate closely with the Ministry of Health to ensure that a range of essential services, including Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child and Adolescence (RMNCH+A) services are available to the target population through professional and qualified male and female staff, including where possible appropriate PSS / mental health services for those affected by trauma and other mental health conditions.

Protection Risk Analysis

ADRA Syria will ensure that the RHC project works closely with all stakeholders, however, due to the nature of conflict, it is possible that some risks may occur. The main risk identified is that future rehabilitated health facilities and employed health care workers may be targeted. To mitigate this, ADRA Syria will monitor dangers in the area and constantly provide staff with training on safety and security measures. Areas selected will be carefully considered and selected based on a low possibility of active conflict in the area. In addition, project beneficiaries may feel that there is unbalanced access to health care, supplies, or services, which could increase tension within communities or towards the ADRA team. In order to mitigate this risk, ADRA will provide services based on need and a clear criteria selection developed which will be shared with all stakeholders. ADRA Syria will also encourage beneficiary feedback from men, women, boys, girls, people with disabilities and the elderly to ensure that the project implemented is meeting the needs of the people. Additionally, there is a risk of exposing patients to harm due to inexperienced health personnel who do not receive proper capacity building and training. The mitigation measure taken to avoid this will be to ensure strong coordination with the ministry of health and local municipalities and governorates to ensure that the staff assigned to the health facilities are properly trained and qualified. The health sector can also assist in the coordination of additional trainings as required. ADRA Syria has an approved protection policy that covers the protection of staff, beneficiaries and other stakeholders. The policy also includes a complaint mechanism in which beneficiaries can register feedback directly with male and female project staff, or anonymously through a hotline which is then recorded at the ADRA Syria main office and complaints followed-up and outcomes reported.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Several risks and constraints could impact on the success of the project. Key risks are summarized below. Contextual risk: Although the security situation in Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Deir Al Zour and Rural Homs is relatively stable, it is possible that conflict could reignite. ADRA will monitor the security situation of each area closely throughout the project lifecycle and be prepared to provide timely assistance based on the context and where possible. ADRA will also provide basic services in many newly-accessible and highly vulnerable areas which may place staff, beneficiaries or stakeholders at risk. ADRA will monitor the situation on the ground carefully though UNDSS, local staff and local stakeholders and will focus on community-based interventions to minimize the risk of harm to ADRA staff and beneficiaries. ADRA staff will be trained on explosive remnants of war and unexploded ordinances to help prevent incidents. Unequal distribution of services: Due to the unpredictable and often high numbers of spontaneous self-organised returns to newly-accessible and previously hard-to-reach areas, it is possible that ADRA Syria will not have access to accurate records of the most vulnerable communities. ADRA will work closely with local communities and other stakeholders in order to obtain the most accurate information possible. Risk of fraud or misuse of project funds: ADRA Syria has board approved financial management, Anti-Fraud and procurement policies in place, that comply with donor regulations and also include whistle-blowing, conflict of interest and anti-corruption policies. These policies are strictly adhered to and will be verified during regular visits by management and donors where possible to monitor project process. Project risks: The delay or difficulty in obtaining the necessary approvals needed to implement the project activities is a key project risk identified based on previous experience. ADRA will maintain regular contact with the relevant ministries and will encourage active involvement by all stakeholders in the implementation of each stage of the project which includes multiple locations, meaning that if one approval is delayed, it is possible to move ahead in another area if needed. ADRA will have a beneficiary feedback mechanism in place under this programme to ensure appropriate accountability and prevent harm to beneficiaries. Due to the targeting of vulnerable and HTR areas, it is possible that contractors are unavailable or unwilling to implement activities in these areas, or that there is an insufficiency of appropriate materials for the project. To mitigate this risk, ADRA already has an established presence in Aleppo, Homs and Rural Damascus with existing projects with reliable contractors available to undertake activities.
