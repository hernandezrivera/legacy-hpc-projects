Project Summary

Ayod, Twic, Maiwut, and Luakpiny/Nasir are high-priority counties facing acute vulnerabilities in WASH and malnutrition, compounded by high WASH-related GBV. The WASH, nutrition, and protection-related challenges affect an estimated 2 million people needing humanitarian aid. WASH services are highly required following the declaration from the MoH about an outbreak of HEV disease in Fangak. The WASH severity classification places these counties in phase 4.
The main findings highlight minimal access to clean drinking water with worse scenarios in Ayod, Fangak, and Nasir. People rely on rivers, swamps, or ponds as their primary source of drinking water, yet open defecation is a norm in the area. Despite all the efforts made by humanitarians, the response remains constrained due to access impediments and insecurity, including the delivery of humanitarian supplies with barge movements.
These counties display the lowest standards in WASH parameters according to the most recent WASH cluster severity mapping, which is likely to be exacerbated by unprecedented flood waters leading to the destruction of WASH infrastructure, food insecurity, logistical challenges, displacement of communities, deprivation of markets, and the increasing number of returnees from Sudan. The flooding has further increased WASH-related mortality and morbidity, affecting mainly under five children, pregnant and lactating women, persons with special needs, and those living with HIV/TB.
Providing adequate WASH services is critical to achieving sustainable solutions for communities in these targeted areas. Community Action Organization (CAO) is a national NGO working in Jonglei & Upper Nile States in South Sudan & plans a robust WASH response aimed at improving community well-being and improving the worst WASH indicators in the targeted counties.
The plan targets internally displaced persons, returnees, and vulnerable host communities with water, sanitation, and hygiene services integrated with nutrition and protection/GBV programming for one year. Following the Cluster response plans, CAO aims to respond to sudden-onset disasters and the needs of WASH Services, maintaining integration with the Health and Nutrition Clusters to prevent WASH-related epidemics.
The action prioritizes alleviating suffering through minimal hardware interventions, rehabilitation of existing systems, and promoting safe hygiene practices through sustainable services to address the vital needs in public health/nutrition and protection/GBV issues. It also aims to reduce levels of chronic malnutrition amongst children and pregnant and lactating women, mitigate WASH-related GBV by providing gender-appropriate sanitation and hygiene facilities, and tackle gender issues, safety, and WASH.
Persistent food insecurity, signaled by high GAM rates, with <5s and PLW mainly affected, will be mitigated by the provision of WASH in Nutrition services through targeted WASH minimum packages at OTP/SC, mainly for mother/caretaker and malnourished children. Water-related disease outbreaks have been traced to health facilities and community infrastructures/settings without adequate WASH facilities.
CAO will ensure that 33,485 individuals have timely and equitable access to WASH services to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. The design of the activities covers access to safe/clean water through digging boreholes, distribution of Water Treatment kits, distribution of WASH kits and MHM materials, and construction of latrines. In addition, adapted messages on hygiene good practices will complement all the other WASH services to provide the population with dignified means of hygiene and sanitation. CAO aims to have a long-lasting impact, even after the end of the project.

1. Needs

Targeted Counties face extremely critical needs regarding access to safe water, poor hygiene and sanitation practices, and lack of WASH items to ensure adequate hygiene (highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3, 4). Indeed, the current situation requires prompt action, and the CAO, as a static partner in the counties, has already identified pockets of vulnerability requiring immediate response. Less than 15% of households in these communities have access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes, with inadequate water available for domestic use. The deterioration of the economic situation reduced communities' ability to purchase required items, such as soap, purification tablets, NFIs, and hygiene items. Girls and women feel insecure in an environment without adequate sanitation and hygiene services. Girl child absenteeism in places of learning is linked to low uptake of menstrual hygiene management. At the same time, women spend a lot of time collecting water for household use rather than breastfeeding children and also focus on livelihoods and income-generating activities.Access to sanitation facilities is equally low. WASH severity maps indicate less than 30% of households have access to a restroom, yet most latrines are shared. Poor WASH conditions contribute to high levels of disease, malnutrition, and food insecurity, mainly affecting under-5s, pregnant and lactating women, the elderly, and PWDs.Open defecation is a norm, leading to cycles of water contamination, resulting in acute watery diarrhea (40%), cholera, eye infections, and skin diseases. Hand washing is equally low, with socio-cultural practices unfavorable to achieving acceptable WASH standards. Women are exposed to a series of protection risks due to distance and time taken at water points, lack of hygiene items and livelihood opportunities, as well as community attitudes towards women. Dysfunctional markets and the collapse of entire household livelihoods strain access to soap for household use, necessitating partners' support. Open defecation needs a field management approach - keeping in mind the protection risks for women and girls - which will decrease the chances of water contamination and disease outbreaks. CAO will carry out water quality monitoring for free residual chlorine (FRC) and domestic water, using the nationally approved Water testing kits throughout the implementation, to ensure that water remains safe for consumption by the beneficiaries. Needs related to flooding or rapid displacement causing loss of household items necessary for adequate personal hygiene or safe water collection and storage will be addressed through hygiene promotion and emergency kit distributions. This will allow the safe water cycle to be restored. Furthermore, the WASH kit distributions, including MHM kits, will be accompanied by hygiene promotion at the point of distribution to ensure proper hand washing and water storage practices. A combination of access to safe water, safe water storage, decreased open defecation, and appropriate hygiene practices will reduce moisture- and vector-borne diseases shortly after the emergency. It will contribute to the decrease of morbidity and mortality for shock-affected households. In this way, CAO will be able to contribute mainly to the clusters/HCT adopted a two-year vision and strategy for humanitarian action that ensures vulnerable crises-affected people of South Sudan are supported and empowered through innovative, inclusive, and solution-oriented responses that enable them to live with dignity in a self-reliant manner. All the actions CAO will undertake to contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH-integrated programming with Nutrition.

2. Project activities

Construction/rehabilitation of water points at community/ Nutrition facilities level.Under this activity, the CAO will construct/drill boreholes and rehabilitate the old borehole. CAO will first conduct a water survey, and during this process, all the water quality standards are checked by trained operators. The Boreholes and other water points in the community and Nutrition facilities will provide safe water in the community.Construction/rehabilitation of Sanitation and LatrineBefore implementing any sanitation response, all locations will be assessed using the cluster sanitation survey checklist to evaluate the Wash-related disease outbreak level. Rehabilitation/construction of latrines or management of the defecation field will be combined with additional hygiene promotion sessions for the target population. CAO plans mainly to rehabilitate existing and damaged facilities, but CAO will build new ones where the facilities are not there. CAO will prioritize rehabilitating latrines, comprising three stances (one for women and girls, one for men and boys, and one for persons with disabilities). CAO will also prioritize intervention at the institutional level, with the rehabilitation of restrooms located in Nutrition center levels (based on initial assessment and following the "WASH cluster WASH integrated response in IPC/Nutrition crises Preparedness and Contingency Plan"). CAO plans to rehabilitate over ten blocks of shared households' latrines, reach 480 beneficiaries, and construct ten new ones. As much as possible, restrooms selected for rehabilitation will be gender-segregated and designed to allow easy access and usage for people with special needs (women, children, and disabled.Safe water and hygiene promotion at the HH levelIn situations where surface water is the only viable source of drinking water, the CAO will distribute WASH Kits, including Household Water Treatment and Storage items (HWTS). The kits will contain one 18-inch filter cloth, 45 PUR sachets, 225 Aqua tabs, 2 water containers, and 3 800-gram bars of soap. CAO will request the kits from the WASH Cluster Core Pipeline and plan to align the kit content to the standardized one, including buckets with lids or collapsible jerry cans. CAO has estimated that distributing 2,500 kits per location will benefit about 8,000 individuals, and CAO will conduct post-distribution monitoring.Menstrual hygiene kits (MHM) are distributed at the community and institutional levels.The Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits ensure the needs of girls and women are covered. Distributions will be in-kind. CAO will request the gears from the WASH Cluster Core Pipeline. Each location will receive 3,000 kits to benefit 6,000 women and girls. The kits are designed for 2 women/girls; they comprise 6 pieces of underwear, six bathing soap, and six reusable sanitary pads. The distributions will be by locally hired workers. Training on how to use the items correctly will be given during the distributions. Trained female Community Hygiene Promoters will conduct the sessions with the support of the female CAO Hygiene Promoters at the demonstration corner.Community and institutional hygiene promotionCAO will conduct hygiene promotion through door-to-door household visits, with the support of locally recruited and trained Community Hygiene Promoters, who will be selected based on acceptance by the community, ability to communicate well and to pass on messages, and basic knowledge of good hygiene practices. We will aim to recruit 50% of CHPs to be women to facilitate sharing notes specific to women's concerns (menstrual management, safe access).CAO will also conduct 40 public sessions on key hygiene-related messages at water collection points and other concentrated settlements. These key messages will be delivered during the WASH and MHM NFI kits distribution, reaching 8,000 individuals.
