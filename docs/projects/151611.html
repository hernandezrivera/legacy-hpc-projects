Project Summary

Gender based violence (GBV) in Somalia is a pressing problem not only because it violates Human Rights but also because it poses a challenge to public health as well as economic and social development.
Woman and children fearing sexual or gender based violence are unlikely to be able to access effective protection from their regions. Customary laws are often used instead of a weak judiciary system that discriminates against women and girls. However, traditional approaches to dealing with rape tended to ignore the survivor’s situation and instead sought resolution or compensation for rape through a negotiation between members of the perpetrator’s and survivor’s clans. Some survivors were forced to marry perpetrators.
According to UNHCR reports, In May, 2017 nearly 2.4 million people including IDPs who were affected by the drought have protection concerns while 250,000 people were displaced due drought and famine, these people were at risk of Sexual and Gender Based Violence as well as other related human Violation.
The Somali Penal code criminalizes rape, providing penalties of 5 to 15 years in prison. The Judiciary did not effectively and consistently enforce this existing law. Due The weak judicial system and law enforcement agencies in the country many survivors of SGBV have lost trust in law enforcing sectors and opt for customary system.
Despite the ongoing efforts, a number of gaps still exist in achieving comprehensive package of care for survivors of GBV. Access to justice support for GBV survivors is extremely limited in areas like Bakool region. Therefore, the existing acute need for quality and timely support to GBV survivors is still weak to make an effective and durable solution of GBV prevention and protection.
On the Other Hand, the IDPs in Somalia face sequence of housing, land and property challenges relating to the conflicts and displacement that has taken place in many regions of the country. The lack of a central keeping documentation institution which weakens an already fragile judiciary system along with a lack of application of civil code and procedures tends to create many HLP cases concerning land disputes and conflicts.
Lack of tenure and long lasting solutions for IDPs in Somalia specially Bakool Region has created a situation in which entire communities flee. The major human rights violations done to internally displaced persons, since the Somali Government didn’t enforce the existing National IDP Policy and Guiding Principle of Internally Displaced Persons.
Wajid and Hudur were found to host the settlement of drought affected people, over 156,000 people reached Baidoa and estimated 55,000 people arrived in Wajid and Hudur. ARD identified and supported 837 GBV survivors in Hudur and Wajid from January to May 2017, 226 of these cases were sexual violated survivors. 341 of these survivors in Baidoa received medical, psychosocial support, and Dignity kit. While 496 cases also received legal counseling and legal representation
The aim of the project is to enhance community engagement for better prevention, capacity building for law enforcement to strengthen access to Justice and advocacy on Somali legal system that can offer justice for victims of sexual violence and other GBV as well as advocacy on including legislation that ensures the recognition and punishment of sexual violence in all its forms

Needs

Increasing number of GBV cases in Hudur and Wajid are constant for IDPs and Drought affected people, Many cases of GBV were underreported due to a culture of silence and fear of consequences towards the survivor from society such as stigma, isolation and revenge attacker is an issue in Gender Based Violence (GBV) which is a pressing problem, not only because it violates human rights but also it poses a challenge to public health, economic and social development. High rate of GBV are caused by the weakness and the lack of adequate capacity to prevent and respond, with situation of Wajid demanding an urgent intervention. Traditional leader are the main providers of justice in Wajid and Hudur Given the weaknesses in the formal justice system most of the population relies on customary laws and on traditional elders to adjudicate their problems. Traditional elders and Sheiks can be unsympathetic to GBV survivors. They often reach solutions that are problematic from a protection perspective. Traditional elders are generally older men, with deeply ingrained patriarchal views. They often seek restorative solutions, which promote community harmony, rather than solutions to hold perpetrators to account Despite the existing GBV prevention and response mechanisms in Hudur and Wajid are still needs stepping up for prevention, Response and coordination mechanisms. The little efforts are faced by a countless of obstacles which if not tacked might lead to erosion of the gains already made. As well as the existing challenges on the ground concerning prevention and response of GBV towards protection of IDPs, Returnees, drought affected people and Host Community. ARD intends to promote and protect the dignity of most vulnerable people in IDPs and Host Community through ensuring adequate prevention and mitigation of GBV cases as well as facilitating timely multi sectoral response to SGBV survivors and HLP issues. The project will serve with following activities:

Activities

- Increase provision of legal aid counseling and representation as well coordination mechanisms of Hanano Centre for GBV response.- Provision of legal assistance including representation and legal counseling to the GBV survivor, evicted people or those at risk of forced evictions.- Strengthen GBV response through established one-stop centre in Wajid and Hudur for survivors that can access complete package of service including basic psychosocial support, Medical, legal aid representation, Safe Shelter, Livelihood and Dignity kit.- Provision of Psychosocial counselling and Legal Advice for GBV survivors in Hudur and Wajid at established Reception and transit centres to identify person with concern and supported as well - Establishment Community conflict resolution mechanisms in Wajid so to strengthening and engaging community participation through community conflict resolution mechanisms in IDPs through media programs. - Improve Capacity of Wajid and Hudur Police officers on GBV case management so to enhance better protection and on timely response of GBV cases.- Promotion of access to justice for the land and property dispute Survivors particularly those whose properties are taken illegally due to their clan minority, social status, etc.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

ARD will engage project stakeholders at all stages particularly during the implementation stage of the project where concerned stakeholders including project beneficiaries, local authorities, justice institutions, law enforcement departments, relevant line ministries in both state and federal level will be involved to maximize the response toward the above mentioned problems. ARD is member of Protection Cluster Groups, Gender Based Violence Working Group (GBVWG), Child Protection Working Group (CPWG) and the network of legal aid providers in Somalia. And also ARD Participated monthly and quarterly meetings organized by different line ministries and UN agencies. These groups have various monthly meetings of which ARD is invited, in these meetings, ARD will table and share update information about the project and its impact as well as the challenges, particularly ARD will undertake various visits in police stations, central prisons and the attorney general office and will have meetings with the administrations of each of these institutions to address the problems that this project is intended to solve.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The major risk that might arise could include those beneficiaries especially survivors can face possible threats from the perpetrators or from the local authorities and if this happens, ARD will relocate the survivor to another safe place and will treat such case with utmost confidentiality. With regards to risks related to financial management, ARD has Policies and Procedures as well as computerized system in place that can produce authentic financial control and reporting of any funds received. Hence, during and after the completion of the project, financial and narrative reports will be shared with UNHCR on time with supporting documents, moreover ARD projects are annually audited by and external auditor in order to ensure that funds are spent according to the agreed contracts with our partners.
