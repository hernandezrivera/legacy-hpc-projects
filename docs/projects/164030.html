Project Summary

The project aims at developing a comprehensive Protection program in Aleppo (sub- districts of Jebel Saman, Al-Khafsa, Hadher, Maskana, Menbij, Tall Ed-daman) and Deir- Ez-Zor city, focusing on Risk Education (RE) and Child Protection (CP) as key pillars to carry out tailored community-based activities. Regarding RE, the extensive use of explosive weapons since the beginning of the crisis generated explosive hazards, posing a threat to kill or injure civilians, humanitarian response operators, and disrupting livelihood activities. As school is the most suitable environment to deliver RE to children, teachers need to be properly trained. For this, the Action includes training of 150 teachers in protection-related topics of RE, allowing them to raise children’s ability to deal with threats related to IEDs, landmines and ERW. Each trained teacher will be able to transfer the acquired knowledge to an estimated number of 50 children, reaching a total of 7,500 students, GVC will constantly monitor the training and implement focus group discussions with children to evaluate the impact of the information transferred by their educators. Standardized training materials and kits to deliver awareness sessions to children will be provided by UNICEF and approved by the Ministry of Education (MoE). A second component of the Protection program foresees community-based outreach activities focusing on a wide range of protection needs and tailored to different groups based on age, gender, social role and responsibilities. GVC, supported by Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC), will carry out events and outreach initiatives to raise awareness and stimulate critical thinking on: inclusive education; risks related to early marriage; threats linked with the presence of IEDs, landmines and ERW; psycho-social wellbeing of vulnerable children through PSS support activities. The events will be structured in a way to adapt to different target groups of beneﬁciaries and to the speciﬁc context (e.g. rural/urban) and social dynamics of the community where they take place.

4 - Needs

Based on data of HNO 2020 Protection section and need assessment conducted by GVC in Nov 2019 in the targeted areas, occurrence of multiple protection issues in communities is a fact: civilians continue to be directly exposed to effects of hostilities resulting in forced displacement, trauma, distress, violence and discrimination. Based on current population estimates, 11.5 million individuals live in areas reporting explosive hazard contamination in the last two years. Such hazards (landmines, to booby traps and unexploded remnants of war) have devastating consequences on the lives of children and their families. The crisis produced threats to mental and physical health, and overall development of children and youth: teachers and caregivers report violence, bullying and conflictual attitudes among children as widespread and worrying behavioural patterns. Disruption of traditional safety nets and livelihood opportunities, in addition to sudden displacement, put many women and girls at risk of various types of GBV that affect their lives in different ways, aggravating protection concerns such as psychosocial stress, limitation of movement in some areas of the country and potential for increased recourse to negative coping mechanisms, such as forced/early marriage. Besides girls and women, other vulnerable groups such as children with special needs are suffering from the impact of the crisis, being victims of exclusion and discrimination in a greater extent than it was in times of stability. This also occurs in the Education system, where referral mechanisms and access are not granted. A comprehensive response to complex and inter-connected protection challenges is needed in terms of community-based approach, which can maximize outreach capacities and resilience. Even though in targeted sub-districts Protection severity scale ranges from 2 to 5, they have been considered due to the integrated nature of the Action with Education (related severity scale from 4 to 6).

5 - Implementation of activities

Activity 4.1 (MA) Conduct risk education for at-risk groups. GVC will train 150 teachers in Risk Education using standardized UNICEF contents in rural Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor city. The trainees, selected by GVC and Directorates of Education (DoEs) in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor, will attend a 4-day course and increase their knowledge on how to deal with risks related to the presence of mines, IEDs and ERW. Teachers, as relevant public service providers, are considered the main messengers to spread information to their students in schools and to children enrolled in NFE programs. GVC esteems that each teacher will transfer RE to 50 students, reaching a total of 7,500 children and adolescents, relying on standardized guides and materials received during the training. Trainers delivering the course will be assisted by GVC staff who will give proper support and ensure good quality of information provided. Activity 5.1 (CP) Community-based child protection for girls and boys is available in targeted locations. GVC is planning to implement a community-based protection campaign in the targeted areas, relying on the support of 40 community outreach volunteers from SARC, who will be trained in basic protection principles, including PSS, RE, CP (with a focus on inclusive education for children with special needs and early marriage) and will acquire the right methodologies to deliver messages on the mentioned topics, namely: awareness sessions, entertainment events, focus groups discussion, etc. Around 30,000 individuals will improve their protection by raising awareness on: risks related with mines, IEDs and ERW; challenges linked with early marriage; the importance of inclusive education and psycho-social wellbeing for vulnerable children. At least 65% of the overall beneficiaries will be school-age children.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A.4.1 Risk:Creation of wrong expectations among beneficiaries, who could wrongly attribute to RE teams the ability of removing explosive ordinance;Familiarization with messages delivered at RE sessions may lead beneficiaries to become overconfident/engage in risky behaviours. Likelihood:High Impact:High Mitigation:Delivery of ToT to ensure large scale awareness/sustainability of efforts;Clear explanation of intervention and objectives to beneficiaries;Tailoring of messages/RE materials used in schools according to age/gender;Involvement of survivors of explosive hazards as role model for behaviour change;ToT of local liaison team to ensure self-sustainability of RE messaging within affected communities; linking RE with other interventions.A.5.1-R1.Protection risk faced by girls/boys may persist if community members do not play an active role in the delivery of protection services.Existing traditional/religious practices increasing due to limited community engagement/acknowledgement of certain practices as protection concern.Girls/boys exposure to protection risk due to limited initiatives to enhance children's participation in their own protection.Lack of sustainable protection intervention due to limited involvement of national stakeholder/community members in CP service delivery. Likelihood:Medium Impact:High Mitigation:Ensure service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation/communication with communities can be proactive/informed;Utilise sector agreed key messages to ensure consistency/inform sector of discrepancies/if new messaging is required.R2.Existing traditional practices/community structures conflict with CP principles. Likelihood:High Impact:High Mitigation:Training of staff as key resource persons to act as trainers/facilitators;Regular engagement/communication with communities;GVC will adopt complaint mechanism ensuring data protection/confidentiality (staff trained to follow up).

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS 1. Security context in Deir-Ez-Zor and Aleppo targeted area remains stable Risks: Security situation deteriorates to the point to prevent the implementation of some of the activities 2. Access to targeted areas is safe for staff to conduct activities Risks: Shrinking of the humanitarian and operational space in some intervention area due to military operation 3. Internal return of people increases because of improved political and safety situation Risks: Major change in demographics in the area of intervention requires modification in the strategy. 4. Collaboration of the Authorities to set up criteria for the selection of the beneficiaries, to monitor the activity, and approve it. RISKS: Major change in strategy of authorities and local partners (in particular SARC and MoE) toward the planned interventions and reject it, and/ or delay in approval and bureaucrat requests. MITIGATIONS: 1. GVC ensures continuous coordination with local partners SARC, Ministry of Education and international specialized agencies (UNDSS) in order to monitor the situation. GVC adopted a security plan and SOP in case of any major accident. 2. Policy environment is monitored through the attendance to Cluster, Working Groups and coordination mechanisms to stay updated on the reasons which may refrain teachers, educators and volunteers from attending training sessions and, generally, discuss any issue occurring in the implementation as it is planned, and/or adjust the training sessions in accordance with any Sector decision. 3. Thanks to dialogue and exchange with Sector and other agencies, GVC monitors the demography in the targeted areas and adjusts target/content/methodologies according to changes in the composition of population. 4. GVC ensures good relationship with local authorities, agreeing in advance the planned activities and changes.
