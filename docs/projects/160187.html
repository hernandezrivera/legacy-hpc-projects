Project Summary

An estimated 6,650,000 South Sudanese women, men and children will not have suﬃcient access to food and livelihoods in 2020. GBI seeks to deliver a 12 months’ emergency Food Security and Livelihoods activities to critically vulnerable Households through Provision of Food Assistance, supporting emergency food production with complementary kits and Livelihoods Resilience Programmes for the vulnerable people in South Sudan (Bor South, Twic East, Duk, Pibor, Aweil East and Aweil North of Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Juba and Terekeka in Central Equatoria and Kapoeta South and Kapoeta North Counties) as well as Awerial and Yirol East Counties of Lakes state.

In line with the FSL cluster strategy and the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), GBI is proposing to scale up response in these locations through distribution of emergency food production kits (vegetable and ﬁshing kits), livestock vaccination and training of CAHWs, cash transfers, assets creation and unconditional food) supporting ongoing or planned interventions.

The overall objective of this project is to improve the food security and livelihood status of vulnerable households (IDPs, returnees and host) in Lakes, Jonglei, Eastern and Central Equatoria and Northern Bahr El Ghazal states. This project is designed to reduce vulnerabilities and risk associated with the current food insecurity and poor economic conditions as a result of conﬂict, seasonal variability and displacement. A total of 117,515 beneﬁciaries comprising (returnees 20%, IDPs 15% and host 65% ) will be targeted directly with lifesaving livelihoods and food security interventions. 70% of total beneficiaries will be female while 30% will be male. The project will require $900,000 to provide the intended services and activities throughout the intervention period.

1 - Needs

According to August 2019 IPC, an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Yirol East of former Lakes State has an estimated 10,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) including 57.9% in IPC 3 and 4) while Former Jonglei State has the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 1.25 million people or 64.8% and Northern Bahr el Ghazal 59.3%. The high levels of acute food insecurity continue to be driven by the cumulative effects of national and localized conflicts, heavy reliance on unpredictable and poor rainfall performances, associated population displacements and prolonged years of asset depletion (FSNMS Round 23). The cumulative and compounding effects of widespread violence and sustained economic decline have exhausted the coping capacity and made many people remain on the move, continuing to leave everything behind in order to secure emergency access to basic consumables (Sida 2019). Additionally, conflict has disrupted livelihoods and impacted households’ access to other food sources, such as wild foods, fish, and livestock products. Furthermore, the on-going economic crisis has significantly reduced households’ purchasing power. Other significant drivers include the prolonged dry spells at critical stages of crop growth, flooding, crop pests and diseases, migration of cattle away from homesteads and growing destitution, humanitarian access challenges, chronic water borne diseases and malnutrition (FSNMS Round 23). Since June, heavy seasonal rains have triggered flooding in Jonglei State, prompting local authorities to request humanitarian assistance for affected populations (USAID July 2019). Floods caused by torrential rains have worsened the living conditions of South Sudanese people in many parts of the country in the past few months. Some of the areas affected include Lafon, Torit and Kapoeta South counties in Eastern Equatoria; Ayod, Akobo, Bor South, Duk, Twic East, Pibor, Pochalla and Uror counties in Jonglei; Aweil Center and Aweil North in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (Reliefweb/UNOCHA Oct 2019). According to (IPC August, 2019) food security has slightly improved during the 2019 lean season compared to the same time last year due to improved security conditions, which have permitted greater household movement and access to fish, wild foods, and markets. Despite the decline in conflict and anticipated improvement in food security, many households have lost key livelihood assets and coping strategies as a result of the protracted conflict. Consequently, high levels of acute food insecurity and a risk of Famine (IPC Phase 5) will persist (FEWSNET September 2019). Sustained, large-scale assistance to protect livelihoods and increase resilience to further shocks will be crucial to save lives, boost food production capacities and build self-sufficiency (FAO 2019). GBI is currently implementing Integrated WASH and Nutrition project targeting 1,600 SAM children and over 17,000 individuals in communities with safe water and sanitation in Twic East County, Jonglei state and an integrated FSL providing assistance to 6,667 households in 2019/2020 in Northern Bahr el Ghazal scaling up main season activities which targeted 5,956 HHs or 47000 individuals. With this call for proposals, GBI is planning to scale up in greater Jonglei state, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Central, Lakes and Equatoria where we have operational presence. Activities integrating FSL and Nutrition programming as well as other sectors at households level such GBV mitigation, reduction of malnutrition in children U5 and PLW through supporting of emergency food production and resilience activities of all vulnerable members of IDPs, Returnees and Host communities.

2 - Activities

1. In-kind food assistance through General Food Distribution (GFD), Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme (BSFP) and School Feeding Programme (SFP). These activities help provide IDPs/Returnees/Host communities with an in-kind food basket that meets all their basic food and nutritional needs.2. Provide livestock veterinary services to curtail livestock losses, improved meat quality and milk yield, thereby stabilizing and safeguarding rural and urban livelihoods through livestock vaccination and treatment on common diseases as well as surveillance of diseases.3. As a capacity building activity in livestock, Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) will be provided with drugs and trained on disease investigation, vaccination and treatment and best management practices to increase output and minimize livestock losses.4. Procurement, operation and maintenance of cold chain for safe storage of livestock drugs.5. Cash and voucher-based transfers – beneficiaries meet their immediate cash needs in locations with functional markets while ensuring that all households are receiving training in financial literacy to make informed and effective decisions relating to their financial resources, including on how to generate, invest, spend and save money. Trainings will be delivered jointly to male–and female–household members, as a means of promoting joint decision-making over household budgets.6. Distribution of seeds, tools, ﬁshing kits and quick maturing vegetable seeds to vulnerable and conflict affected households for emergency food and livelihoods support.7. Capacity building and training on demonstration ﬁelds on best management practices such as pest and disease management, post-harvest handling, value addition and marketing. 8. Formation of producers’ groups, village saving and loan schemes for diversification and restoration of livelihoods.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Through community mobilization and project design, beneficiaries will be contacted to give their inputs and a group representing them is always established at boma level. The project will be implemented with the help of local authority who makes the organization accountable to the beneficiaries. The beneficiaries have the right to their entitlements as they appear in the project, they have right to choose distribution locations, right to receive right information about the project and the right to take part in key decision making process.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

IDPs are always part of intervention and considered the most vulnerable people by the virtue of being displaced from their homes and having lost their belongings. The project allocates 15% to IDPs as a contingency plan and also target the already existing displaced populations in the target locations.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project works with local chiefs, payam administrators, Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC), Local government departments such as ministry of Agriculture and Fisheries, ministry of labor and human resources development, Community established AAP committees etc. Before project kick off, entry meetings are held with each available structures to share project information with relevant authorities so as to guard the beneficiaries and ensure accountability to affected populations.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The activities in the project are aligned with Country Programming Framework (CPF 2018-2021) which involves sustainable increase in production and productivity for food and nutrition security, support economic growth and income, increase resilience of livelihoods to threats and crisis. It will target IDPs % and % returnees as well as provision of social protection to vulnerable households by considering age groups and different gender categories.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Whilst overall access has improved in the context of the recently revitalized comprehensive peace agreement, insecurity in some areas still impedes humanitarian organizations from reaching people in need. Though skeptical, humanitarian community in South Sudan is optimistic that increased dialogue between warring parties will help enhance access and thus enable the humanitarian organizations to reach the most vulnerable population in the country in 2019. Lack or limited infrastructure may result in inadequate, irregular or delayed assistance due to limited access to people in need. The project will also observe the principle of “Do No Harm” in the project implementation by not causing drastic changes to the existing community setup as a result of the project activities. Beneficiaries and staff safety will be prioritized and strictly observed.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

To ensure the issue of sustainability and handover of activities to the communities, the project will prioritize capacity building, engage community structures and local government at all stages of the project life cycle. At all stages of the project implementation the communities are required to provide their contribution to cancel out over dependency on aid by providing inputs at their disposal for project implementation and takeover the ownership of the project outputs at close out.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Male and female, women and men or boys and girls are differently affected by humanitarian crisis, situation or disaster requiring an intervention. Community mobilization brings together all people of different sex, age group and status to decide for themselves as far as humanitarian assistance is concerned. The project will prioritize the needs of women and girls by involving them in key decision making processes and by being part of AAP. 70% of beneficiaries of target population are women and 30% men, 45% are children (31.5% girls and 13.5% boys). Special support will be given to women groups in form of cash transfers, training, loan and assets ownership. Forced taxation of Humanitarian Assistance and access restrictions is mitigated by seeking letter of clearance from Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) in Juba for humanitarian items moving from Juba to field locations. The risk is also transferred to third party by giving transportation work to logistics companies which take the responsibility of safe delivery of items. While delivering within the locality security reports or situations that might arise to forced taxation are analyzed before authorizing delivery. Movement of staff are planned to take place between 9:00am – 4pm when security of guaranteed – no voyages allowed. Normally, the local authority and local leaders try to corrupt the process of identification of beneficiaries by bringing their close relatives instead of vulnerable people in need of assistance or even having themselves registered. To mitigate targeting of wrong beneficiaries GBI conducts community mobilization before identification of beneficiaries to inform them about the selection criteria. We also work with Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) Committees established at Boma level to help in identification of beneficiaries. GBI also works in close coordination with relevant government ministries who are aware of the humanitarian work.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Before distribution, beneficiaries are informed about their entitlements, rights and contribution towards the project. The AAP committees formed are used as channel through which beneficiaries who can't present their own complaints can speak to the organization. Rapid Post Distribution Monitoring (RPDM) is conducted for beneficiaries to express their satisfaction or dissatisfaction about the project and through one on one or face-to-face discussion with beneficiaries. Beneficiaries are also provided with suggestion boxes through which they can share their complaints or feedback i.e. for those who know how to read and write.
