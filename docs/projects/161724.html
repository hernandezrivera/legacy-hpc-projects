Project Summary

The aim of proposed project is to provide lifesaving sexual and reproductive health care services to IDP, host community and new arrivals in kala-bale LGA and more particularly Rann.
The project aims to contribute to respond to the needs of the targeted communities in terms of reproductive health in Rann (Borno state) through two main components:
1. Support the community through Registered Midwives Practical Observational Skills Training of Traditional birth attendants (TBAs) and Community Health Extension Workers (CHEWs) to provide safe
clean delivery services and support for exclusive breastfeeding to women in a bid to reduce maternal and neonatal mortality and morbidity.
TBAs and Community Health Extension Workers (CHEWs) are lay health workers trained by the Ministry of health (MoH) to carry out healthcare delivery through a task shifting model. Both are usually in the center of delivering modern, traditional and cultural maternal health related interventions and are usually well respected in the community.
A massive gap in the delivery of maternal health care interventions and programs by trained health care professionals exists in Rann due du the insurgency in the first quarter of 2019. Registered midwives will conduct practical training to TBAs and CHEWS, whilst conducting basic antenatal and postnatal care, detection and recognition of danger signs of pregnancy and delivery. TBAs and CHEWs will be provided with clean delivery kits, registered and mentored on how to use the kits for optimum coverage of clean safe deliveries. Midwives will also mentor TBAs and CHEWs to take on case management of women during post-natal period and encourage exclusive breastfeeding for at least six months as a minimum standard. A doctor will visit the area once or twice a week to provide supportive supervision and treatment for complicated cases if appropriate medications are available or certify medical referrals depending on the security situation to the secondary health care in Cameroon
2. Support the community to prevent excessive amount of anticipated Measles disease outbreak cases during the harmattan season that may threaten and further destabilize health care system of the area.
his component seeks to enhance health promotion for disease prevention through awareness raising campaigns in the community.

1 - Needs

Critical gaps remain in functional primary and secondary health services, in community mobilization on key health issues and dedicated support to secondary health care facilities and general hospitals.Populations face significant obstacles to access secondary health care and referral services, especially in inaccessible areas, due to a combination of critical loss of ambulance services and lack of specialized health care providers. Only 39% of health facilities have a functional referral mechanism to a higher level of care and one out of two secondary health care facilities is damaged/destroyed and non-functional.The freedom of movement, for patients and healthcare providers, is often curtailed by general insecurity, as well as by specific restrictions, including for IDPs to move in and out of IDP camps, in Borno State in particular. Tdh highlighted the following needs, based on the analyses of the current critical gaps with regards to SRHR: 1. Need for safe clean delivery services and support for exclusive breastfeeding to women, to contribute to reducing maternal and neonatal mortality and morbidity. Humanitarian actors who were providing these services had to pull out of the area due to the January and February attacks. People who fled the area to neighboring Cameroon had returned to the area and currently, Tdh is the only health actor providing reproductive health services.2. Deliveries using Mama (clean delivery) Kits. TBAs are members of the communities and are therefore key actors in enabling safe delivery and safe health practices. There is a need to train, mentor and equip them with clean delivery kits to provide safe services.3. Referrals of complicated cases. The nearest accessible referral healthcare facility is in Bodo, a village in Cameroon, approximately 10 kilometers away from Rann and only accessible by donkey through difficult terrain. An assessment of this facility is needed to explore relevant solutions and or recognize a referral system since previous facilities were destroyed in the last attacks. However, children with common illnesses will be referred to as MSF. This assessment has been planned under the current grant, but implementation not feasible as the budget was underestimated. 4. Critical community sensitization and awareness-raising to mitigate a high magnitude of disease outbreak are lacking. Tdh’s WASH and health team will work collaboratively with other partners and donors to strengthen health promotion and awareness in the prevention of water and airborne diseases and provide disease surveillance, case finding, referrals and contribute to the response in the case of an outbreak

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

TDH will ensure accountability to affected populations through various activities. Firstly, in the present project, an accountability and quality improvement committee (AQIC) has been set up which is composed of community leaders, religious leaders, representative of women, youth and other social groups and a representative of all ethnic group present in the community. The AQIC plan community actions, implement and act as a feedback mechanism between TdH and the community. This committee has had monthly meetings since its inception and had discussed several issues affecting the community. During the meetings, problems affecting the community, even those not related to health were also aired and positive solutions sort from within the committee and in collaboration with other partners. The AQIC committee is currently acting as community sensitization agents for behavioral change on the need for visibly pregnant women to keep their clean delivery kit in a safe and hygienic environment.However, Information about the project will be shared using pictorial materials. The TDH team is gender-balanced and speaks the local languages to ensure the information about the project is disseminated through community leaders. By doing so the target population will receive clear, relevant and timely information regarding the project. The MEAL department will also ensure a feedback mechanism than can be received using various means, such as FGD, interviews and feedback boxes. Information on relevant services including referral pathway if available will be shared in coordination with the Protection team and WASH teams. TDH as a coordinator of the humanitarian actors in both Mafa and Rann will ensure the same is done by other actors working in Rann.
