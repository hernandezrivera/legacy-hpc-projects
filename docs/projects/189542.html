Project Summary

In South Sudan, WASH needs remain as one of the highest priorities for a sustainable response to the humanitarian crisis. Nowadays, only 36% of households report having access to an improved water source in under 30 mins without facing any protection or security constraints concerns. Safe access is even less for vulnerable groups constantly being disproportionally affected by the crisis as women and girls suffering significant risks of GBV at WASH services and people with disabilities, often unable to physical use them. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, inter-community violence and cyclical flooding in many parts of the country, particularly in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity state. WASH needs are particularly high among IDPs and returnees in non-camp setting and among already stretched host communities. Feuds for resources and “control” of WASH services, often exacerbate inter-community violence in already volatile areas suffering from constant revenge feuds. To ensure the most demanding needs are prioritized, INTERSOS proposes a tailored response for each county targeted, addressing the most urgent WASH and GBV needs. The overall response will seek to integrate as much as possible the provision of WASH services with GBV mitigation measures, especially in the counties where INTERSOS already has ongoing protection interventions including Pibor, Ayod, Malakal, Rubkona, Leer and Nyirol. The proposed intervention will reach around 60,000 people in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties. The first outcome aimed at sustainably increasing access to safe water and sanitation services through the rehabilitation/construction of additional water points, communal latrines, and provision of HHWTS kits. The second outcome will focus on GBV mitigation measures in the targeted counties which include conduction of GBV safety audits to enhance protection mitigation measures at the assessed WASH facilities including solar streetlamps and lockable door, among others, provision of MHM kits for menstrual age women, including solar lamps and prioritizing GBV survivors or particularly vulnerable women and girls at risk (widows, child headed households, etc and hygiene promotion/awareness at community level with emphasis on the following topics: diseases prevention (Water-borne diseases and COVID-19), handwashing, safe excreta management, safe methods to keep villages clean and free of waste materials, GBV messages and promotion of referral pathways. Finally, gender equality will be promoted in a sustainable way through the participation of women at WASH committees and the recruitment of female community mobilizers.

1 - Needs

An estimated 6 million people’s living standards and their wellbeing will be impacted in 2022 as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. Drivers compounding people's well-being are interlinked and include limited access to sufficient quantities of safe drinking water, poor hygiene practices, and poor sanitation. The findings from the 2021 FSNMS + analysis indicate that access to WASH facilities by women and girls, boys, persons with disabilities and elderly and services remains a challenge. Latrines were often overflowing also creating the fear of disease. Severe flooding in 2021 resulted in the destruction and damage of water facilities, reduced access to sanitation and loss of hygiene items which resulted in the urgent need for water, sanitation and hygiene services. The proportion of the population with access to latrines remains very low in the majority of the country, resulting in high open defecation levels. Overall, 80 per cent of households have no access to latrines with geographical variations in the statistics. In certain states such as Unity, Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei, the per centage of households accessing a latrine is consistently below ten per cent. Displacement, insecurity, and the economic downturn have compounded the impact of the low coverage of functional water infrastructure in rural areas. The economic downturn has exacerbated the disruption of water services, limiting resources available for the maintenance of wells and boreholes. In areas IDPs hosting displaced people, above-capacity demand results in water points unable to supply enough drinking water to the population. Protection concerns were flagged as people access water at water points, with 18 per cent of households, both in rural and in urban settings reporting that they felt unsafe while collecting water. Commonly reported issues in accessing WASH facilities include long lines and congestion at water collection points, leading to disputes and tensions. Women and girls frequently face physical and sexual assault and harassment at water collection points, bathing areas and latrines. Lack of latrines and/or overfull latrines lead to open defecation resulting in waterborne diseases.INTERSOS presence through protection projects in some of the most vulnerable counties including Malakal, Rubkona, Leer, Pibor, Ayod and Lankien has allowed the analysis of a direct link between poor WASH services and protection risks, mainly relating to GBV. According to INTERSOS needs assessment in January 2021, respondents reported an overall lack of safety in the water points where due to clan feuds harassment and abuse from rival groups is constant. As per the rapid needs assessment and the Safety Audits conducted by INTERSOS in Bentiu and Malakal after the flood that affected Unity and Upper Nile states in October 2021, it was reported by the women and girls participating that a vast cause of the GBV incidences taking place in those 2 counties is due to the lack of available night lighting in the PoCs. In Rubkona in particular, especially after the flood that took place at the end of 2021, protection became a rising issue as the entrances to the IDP were blocked causing a shortage of food and goods leading to a price surge. The floods have also ravaged Ayod, and until first quarter of 2022 it has remained largely under water with high influx of IDPs leaving flooded payams to Ayod town. According to protection monitoring reports of January 2022, over 13,000 people would have arrived to Ayod town in the last 2 months, posing an extra burden on already limited and functional WASH infrastructure in the area. Additionally, across locations where a lack of preventive GBV measures present at sanitation facilities, counties such as Ayod and Pibor, reported a higher degree of concern.

2 - Project activities

INTERSOS’s proposed project aims to implement activities in 6 locations: Malakal, Rubkona, Leer, Pibor, Ayod, & Lankien. Given the need of prioritization among locations and targets, the proposed project will contribute to HRP strategic objective 2 “ Vulnerable people are exposed to fewer protection threats and incidents, and those who are exposed have access to safe, tailored, timely and dignified access to appropriate services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action” and Sector Objective 2.1 “ Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties” and Sector objective 2.2: WASH specific activities for GBV (or mitigating WASH GBV):- aiming at addressing the most immediate needs in each of the locations.Activities:1- WASH Objective 2.1: Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties.1.1- Distribution of household water treatment and storage (HHWTS) kit (including bucket with lid and tap, jerrycan, soap supply for 3 months and filter cloth) paired with hygiene training to the receivers.1.2- Installation of additional water points, targeting the Payams with lowest water supply coverage. This include construction of new water points, installation of water tanker and construction of water distribution points;1.3- Rehabilitation of water point which include well cleaning, lining of water well and replacing of malfunctioning hand pumps with new one and spare part provision1.4- Construction of communal latrines gender and disability sensitive, using iron sheets and plastic slabs clearly marked, separate, lockable, well-lit latrines for women & men, boys & girls fitted with handwashing points2- WASH Objective 2.2: WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation.2.1- Provision of safety audits before and after any new construction and at every rehabilitated facility aimed at addressing GBV concerns in WASH facilities. The safety audits will also include the establishment/update of referral pathways in every location.2.2- Implementation of protection mitigation measures including stand-alone solar streetlights and other security measures in the facilities considered most dangerous by the results of the safety audit.2.3- Provision of MHM kits for menstrual age women, including solar lamps and prioritizing GBV survivors or particularly vulnerable women and girls at risk (widows, child headed households, etc.)2.4- Hygiene promotion/awareness at community level with emphasis on the following topics: diseases prevention (Water-borne diseases and COVID-19), handwashing, safe excreta management, safe methods to keep villages clean and free of waste materials, GBV messages and promotion of referral pathways. Health/hygiene promoters who will be 70% female will undergo a training first to be able to conduct hygiene promotion at household and community level and to also identify and refer GBV cases to INTERSOS protection team for subsequent actions and guidance.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

On a monthly basis, program staff will held information sessions with community members and community protection committees on the ground and add to a pre-established database all feedback received from community members and the beneficiaries will the measures and addressed.. The database will be shared with INTERSOS coordination that will do a strict follow up of the measures taken during field visits. For the PSEA and CP complaints, complaint will be collected through different channel including complaint boxes and PSEA focal points and will be share with the PSEA referent at coordination level. Each complaint and concern will be assessed and investigate if needed and a feedback will be provided to the person that raised the complaint.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Each CRM committee will have one representative of the most vulnerable groups of the community including among others PWD, elderly, youth, and women. The CRM committee mechanisms was put in place to ensure most vulnerable members had access to said mechanisms after other feedback mechanisms including complaint boxes showed their inadaptation to the context precisely by the important number of illiterate individuals. It is expected that through this inclusive mechanism feedback of the most vulnerable will be collected on a regular basis.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

INTERSOS is committed to prevent acts of sexual exploitation and abuse by INTERSOS employees and related personal. INTERSOS PSEA policy commits INTERSOS to achieve full implementation of the six core principles related to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse adopted in 2002. All INTERSOS staff and volunteers sign a Prevention of SEA Code of Conduct and undergo a PSEA mandatory training as part of the onboarding process. In addition, recurrent trainings are also conducted to refresh staff on their obligations to prevent SEA by timely reporting cases. INTERSOS will also ensure a confidential reporting and mechanisms of any cases regarding SEA. For complaints related to PSEA and CP policy issues, a focal point will be selected to collect complaints, he/she will receive specific sensitivity training and direct communication line (phone and airtime) with the PSEA referent in mission in Juba.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will use several channels to ensure proper communication with beneficiaries. First, the project will include a strong awareness and mobilization component where community mobilizers will oversee reaching all the community using door to door and information sessions and FGD to present the project, the selection criteria and the activities in general. Additionally, the project will work closely with local authorities to ensure they also promote the project within the community. To ensure beneficiary perception of the project is gathered on a regular basis, satisfaction quality survey questions will be included in the PDM exercises done after every distribution. INTERSOS will ensure that complaint and feedback mechanisms are in place throughout the implementation period, aiming at safeguarding proper accountability and program quality, For complaint and feedback on the implementation of activities (including for example delays of implementation, complaints about selection of beneficiaries, etc.), a CRM committee in each location will be established by ensuring all groups of the society - and particularly women, children and people with disabilities - have a representative. Each CRM committee will be trained about standard procedure for treating complaints and feedbacks. Additionally, complaint box will be established at every distribution point and training venue, and the CRM will also be in charge of collecting feedback provided by this mean.
