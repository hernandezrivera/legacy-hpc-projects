Project Summary

Kapoeta East is categorized as priority 4 by the 2024 IPCs analysis. The county is therefore recommended for an immediate scale up of food production to mitigate food insecurity by early 2025.
REDO South Sudan aims to promote and improve the farmer’s capacity to produce and harvest enough food for both consumption and increase access to the market where the beneficiaries will be able to sell their produces and maximize profit for sustainability.
To achieve the above aims, REDO through this proposed intervention targets 9500 HHs that will be directly involved in the agricultural project activities. To realize its objectives, the project envisage:
1. Provision of Environmentally Friendly Income generating activities (IGA) groups established;
2. Training of Trainers (TOT) refresher training on climate-smart agronomic practices,
3. Provision of business start-up grants to 1000 HHs followed by post-heaviest handling technologies,
4. Provide Farmers with high quality crops kits (maize, sorghum, and cowpea, provide Farmers/vegetable group kits with (Amaranth, collard, okra, tomato, onion, watermelon, pumpkin-each one sachet),
5. Provide Vegetable groups with agricultural tools/maloda, and irrigation treadle pumps,6.
6. Conduct awareness raising on community based early warning systems and disaster preparedness, training and or strengthened farmer’s association with refresher training, and support Target community committees to have contingency plans in place and are able to use it in case of emergency and training of partners on gender and protection mainstreaming into food security and livelihood intervention.

1. Needs

TThe September – November 2024 IPC 2024 report indicates an estimated 133,000 people (11.5 percent of the state’s population) are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity in Eastern Equatoria. The key drivers of food insecurity include unusually high food prices, a prolonged dry spell and persistent localized insecurity. Projection 1 (December 2024 – March 2025) indicates the food security situation will deteriorate marginally in some counties during the harvest/post-harvest projection period, with an estimated 168,000 people (14.5 percent of the state’s population) will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During this period, 4 counties (Budi, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North and Lafon) will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Key drivers of food insecurity during this period include significant cereal deficits caused by a prolonged dry spell, and the seasonal livestock migration in search of water and pasture which will negatively affect accessibility to livestock food and income sources. Cattle raids and counter raids will further disrupt some households’ access to food. Meanwhile during Projection 2 (April – July 2025): or the lean-season projection period, the food security situation will seasonally deteriorate, with an estimated 158,000 people (13.6 percent of the state’s population) will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The deterioration is linked to the exhaustion of household food stocks, high market dependence amid high staple food prices and low income-earning opportunities, limited availability of wild foods, and insecurity and conflict-related to cattle raiding. During the lean season projection period, 3 counties (Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North and Lafon) will be classified in Emergency (Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Food insecurity driven by high staple food prices linked to low production and limited supplies, and limited income from crop sales and other livelihood activities. Additionally, there is a likelihood of incidents of insecurity, including communal violence, looting, highway robberies and ambushes, and cattle raiding – all of which will contribute to the deteriorating food security situation through limiting household movement and access to food and income sourcesREDO South Sudan objective is to revive farming strategies in order to meet the demand of both production and supplies. REDO will work in close collaboration with Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) cluster and other partners implementing the agriculture projects in Kapoeta East. With support from the farmers’ association, women groups, community leaders and local authority will identify the target beneficiaries for this particular project. The economic activities will target farmers, women that are involved in agricultural work, those one doing small entrepreneurship to increase food basket for the family and sustain improved productivity. REDO will strive to achieve the objectives of the project through working with farmer’s associations, women’s group and local authorities to identify the beneficiaries for capacity building, and assess the nearby markets and connect the beneficiaries to those markets.
