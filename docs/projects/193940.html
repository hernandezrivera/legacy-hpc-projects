Project Summary

The project will focus on providing protective presence, that focuses on accompaniment, monitoring and documentation of International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL) violations in the South Hebron Hills and Jordan Valley (Area C), with focus on where Israel seeks to forcibly transfer Palestinian communities by creating coercive environment, particularly in the South Hebron Hills, the Jerusalem-Jericho corridor, the Jordan Valley, and areas suffering from rampant settler violence.
The protective presence and documentation of the violations will be conducted by both the Ecumenical Accompaniment Programme in Palestine and Israel (EAPPI) through their Ecumenical Accompaniers (EAs) presence in the field on a daily basis and through B’Tselem who will document violations through their field officers and their videographer volunteers. The primary source of information is first person testimonies from victims and eyewitnesses to human rights violations and for EAPPI, in certain cases, it is documenting what the EAs have witnessed themselves. The documentations and reporting of these violations from both parties will be used to raise awareness for the IHL and IHRL violations that are occurring in Area C, to increase respect for IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations as well as to provide protection by referring them to organizations that can offer support and assistance based on the needs.
Furthermore, in the aftermath of the absence of international protective presence, EAPPI will provide training and will work to mainstream protection with local and international partners on the ground. This will be done in cooperation with the Protection Cluster (PC). Focus will be on transferring expertise in the accompaniment presence methodology, Do No Harm approach, safety and security capacities, culture awareness and other related subjects for all those providing protective presence. In addition, DCA/NCA will facilitate access to communities in the South Hebron Hills where EAPPI will carry out consultation sessions with local communities on protective presence.
The project will be coordinated by DCA/NCA and will rely on a joint cooperation between EAPPI, B’Tselem that will contribute to a comprehensive and effective response in the targeted area to improve protection and thus resilience. EAPPI has established proven strategies which include the combination of protective presence, monitoring, documenting, and referral of needs to increase awareness about IHL and IHRL to the local and the international communities and advocate for upholding these laws.
EAPPI documents the violations, verifies them, and shares them with a list of organizations for their response. Furthermore, the documentation will be shared with diplomats, UN organizations and other duty bearers via their database and the EAs present the information in their advocacy initiatives that aim to address protection concerns and enhance accountability for violations of IHL and IHRL by all duty bearers. EAPPI will carry out a data analysis of these violations and provide information on the type of incidents, if there has been a raise in incidents and if there are any trends. After the data analysis, it will be forwarded to respective organizations.
B’Tselem’s field researchers will monitor and document human rights violations in Area C through primary sources of information; from victims and eyewitnesses to human rights violations while working closely with Palestinian volunteer videographers. The footage they capture is used to raise awareness of the impact of the Israeli occupation on Palestinians.
DCA/NCA will provide quality assurance of the program implementation, will formulate a steering committee with EAPPI and B’Tselem. In addition, DCA/NCA will monitor the implementation will be responsible for ensuring that accountability practices and measures are applied and that PSEA is mainstreamed across the project.

1 - Needs

Both EAPPI’s and B’Tselem’s data shed light on the coercive environment in Area C that leaves Palestinians with high levels of vulnerability facing violence and harassment from Israeli settlers and military. In addition to violations and restrictions that are occurring to Palestinians by the Israeli security forces, the situation has been escalating in the recent years due to the absence of international protective presence during Covid-19. As HR and IHL violations are only increasing and as access constraints are still ongoing, people are harassed and violently attacked in Area C. The need in providing protection to the Palestinians living in Area C is urgent and pressing, specifically after the Israeli Supreme Court approved the demolition of properties threatening to displace over 1,200 Palestinians including 500 children in Masafer Yatta’s. On May 11th demolished 19 homes, storage rooms, and animal sheds in the community. Total number of affected people in the area is 214 people, (282 M, 293 W, 299 B, 270 G) Additionally, the expansion of settlements and the increase in settlers’ attacks; means that families depending on agricultural and shepherding income face additional threats to their livelihood. Residents in Area C have been particularly vulnerable to this. Worthy to note that, women are deeply affected by the occupation, and checkpoints, house demolitions, and settler violence. Women are the ones who run their households without regular or sufficient water and are usually the ones at home during settler attacks and, thus, are the ones who must shield their children. Additionally, the traditional role of women in communities at risk places them in a position to deal with the trauma, emotional toll and the psycho-social impact of systemic settler violence on children. EAPPI has been gathering information and providing protective presence in the Jordan Valley since 2014 and the South Hebron Hills since 2010. Between the years 2016-2019 in the Jordan Valley, EAs recorded a total of 347 Incidents of violence, 245 of incidents of displacement and 185 incidents of access restrictions. Since the return of the first EAs post-Covid-19 restrictions, an increase of violent incidents by around 20% and access restrictions by 40% has been witnessed. In the South Hebron Hills, the situation is very concerning. Between 2016-2019, EAs recorded a total of 153 incidents of violence, 131 incidents of displacement and 103 incidents of access restrictions against Palestinians. Since the return of two EA teams post-Covid-19 restrictions, an increase of violent incidents by 55%, displacement by 33% and access restrictions by 36% has been recorded. According to the OHRCHR 76th session, these sharp increases correspond with the reported increase of attacks during the Covid-19 period of restrictions on international travel. During the absence of EA’s, the OCHA data base has recorded 638 demolished structures, displacing 806 people with the year 2021 being the heights in terms of demolitions. In addition, according to the Special Rapporteur on the situation of human rights in the oPt, 2021 saw a rising rate of violence directed by Israeli settlers towards Palestinians in the occupied Palestinian territory. “Settler violence has always been an extremely disturbing feature of the Israeli occupation, in 2021, we are witnessing the highest recorded levels of violence in recent years and more severe incidents” In addition, the absence of international protective presence forced concerned actors to consider alternative models of protective presence. International actors have reflected on how different types of activities indirectly provide protective presence, but there lacks an intentional approach to mainstreaming protection in other activities. Research done by the MIMAT consultancy and EAPPI, states that beneficiaries have noted serious concern about local accompaniment initiatives due to their lack of Do No Harm capacity.

2 - Activities

Through the EAs, EAPPI will monitor and document violations that occur either through directly witnessing these violations or interviewing a victim or an eyewitness. The incidents will be disaggregated by gender and age and are uploaded on the database, where the field officer vets and verifies the information. After that the EAPPI’s researcher will conduct a data analysis for the incidents that are received by the EA's in view of identifying any trends, type of incidents, increase or decrease in incidents, with focus of data collection on incidents occurring to women and people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups. Furthermore, the researcher will produce factsheets on a quarterly bases and share it with the PC which will be disaggregated by Gender and Age and People with disabilities. In these meetings, EAPPI will seek cooperation with other PC members to analyze this situation/ events in terms of long-term developments and responses. During the PC meetings, when new monitoring and documentation needs for Area C emerge, EAPPI will coordinate its efforts with other members of the PC and refer to other organizations in the PC for their support of the communities. The incidents will also be shared with the international community such as diplomats and members of parliaments (MPs). In addition, EAPPI will regularly provide protective presence to 8 communities (total 2620) in the Jordan Valley and South Hebron Hills. In addition, to presence and accompaniment to 52 communities living in the Jordan Valley area covering 4 governorates (Tubas, Nablus, Ramallah and Jericho) and 66 communities living in the South Hebron Hills area (southern part of the Hebron governorate) that are provided by EAPPI currently. In these communities EAPPI will engage with community leaders to see if there are people of disabilities in these communities and EAs groups will carry out meetings with them to see if they have access to protective presence, needs and include them in community consultations. . EAPPI will engage with the communities and discuss what type of protective presence and how often, this is based on the on the needs of the community and the vulnerability of that community in that time. If farmers are in need of them to join them during the different seasons (ploughing, planting, and harvesting) and they will communicate with EAPPI when they need to access their lands in some of the hot spots: for example land near the settlement fence, or when coordination with the Israeli authorities is needed. In addition, EAPPI will respond to communities need for sleepover by the local communities and provides protective presence overnight after getting the security clearance from the Security Department. In addition, provision of protective presence to children going to school for protection against settler violence. With regards to people with disabilities, for each of the 8 communities that EAPPI will work with, meetings with the heads of the community to see if there are people with disabilities to have meetings with them to see if they have specific needs when it comes to protective presence. EAPPI will also mainstream protection with other international and local humanitarians that provide protective presence indirectly and DCA/NCA will connect EAPPI to communities they work with through other projects in the Southern Hebron Hills to facilitate a discussion regarding protective presence, lessons to learn, safety and security, Do No Harm, what information is important for documenting violations. B’Tselem will monitor Palestinian communities which suffer settler violence supported by Israeli security forces or are at risk of displacement, and document violations through field research conducted by their 8 field researchers in the West Bank. B’Tselem’s field researchers will gather photo and video documentation and data from Palestinian communities. B’Tselem will publicize verified threats to Palestinian communities, including imposition of military orders, demolition orders, demolitions, equipment confiscations, and evacuations for ‘military training’ on its various platforms. The field researchers will work closely with Palestinian videographer volunteers who document their life under occupation and the human rights abuses they witness. The footage they capture will be distributed to Israeli and international audiences in order to raise awareness of the impact of the Israeli occupation on Palestinians.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Protection is the main focus under this application as it feeds into the protection cluster objectives and indicators. Interventions proposed in this response are protection focused with monitoring and documentation of violations, Protection mechanisms and consultations with communities as well as protective presence and accompaniment, that are aimed at addressing the needs arising from a coercive environment in communities at risk which is the core interventions under this application. Protection needs and priorities of the targeted communities are taken into consideration throughout the development phase with an emphasis on effect towards women, girls and people with disability.Since EAPPI has a well-established presence in and is highly appreciated by the community, the EAs have usually no difficulties reaching homes and connecting to female family members. Female EAs can pay follow up visits to the affected women and connect the women to specialized professionals and the broader society. As of June 2022, 29 meetings were held between female EAs and female family members in the South Hebron Hills and 22 meetings were held in the Jordan Valley. If cases of GBV were detected, the female EAs referred these cases to professional organizations working in the area, since EAPPI has an extended network of local partner organizations, in particular with partners that work with children and women issues. In addition, a number of the shepherding accompaniment activities focused on female shepherds. Furthermore, before stepping into the field the EAs are also trained in cultural sensitivity, gender in Palestine and child protection amongst other things. In addition, as B'Tselem monitors and documents the reality on the ground, it utilizes its presence, including field researchers, 140 local volunteers, many of whom are women, who provide local information, enhancing community engagement in B’Tselem’s work. This network of volunteers and communities informs B’Tselem’s work as it provides access/insights enhancing familiarity with communities at risk, and with victims and eyewitnesses of human rights violations. B’Tselem carefully considers its impact on communities while operating according to a ‘Do No Harm' approach in which the safety and dignity of targeted communities is considered above all. In addition, DCA/NCA will assist B’Tselem in developing an official Do No Harm policy that field researchers and volunteers will be trained on. B’Tselem staff and volunteers are trained to always prioritize their own safety above documentation needs and to withdraw from any threatening or dangerous situation. The field researchers and volunteers are instructed to never intervene in an unfolding confrontation between Palestinians and Israeli security forces or settlers. They do not attempt to stop demolitions, for example, only to document them. However, in case of arrest, B’Tselem provides legal support to its field researchers and volunteers. Furthermore, in this project, EAPPI and B’Tselem will refer the detected cases of GBV to relevant service providers. Addressing the GBV cases during the implementation will be dealt with confidentially and will be refereed to specialized agencies to protect the GBV survivals.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

DCA/NCA and partners adopt Anti-discrimination policy that criminalizes all aspects of discrimination and guides the implementation of interventions considering the humanitarian principles. All beneficiaries will be treated equally, impartially, and transparently, with attention to gender issues, considering the extra vulnerability inflicted on women in the society, and to people with disability. A principled humanitarian response will be implemented. EAPPI is guided by the notion of principled impartiality, meaning that it is not taking sides in the Israeli-Palestinian conflict. It supports groups from both communities working for peace and justice. Also, within the Palestinian communities, it does not engage in political action or preferential treatment of particular segments of the population. EAs are committed to hearing from as many perspectives as possible and having an open and honest conversation with the people living in the region. EAs value the work of deepening interfaith relations and are thus bridging the gaps among various communities. Additionally, EAPPI is committed to gender and age mainstreaming that takes place through guiding and strategy documents, e.g. plans, checklists, etc. at organizational and programmatic level. Specifically, the EAPPI Strategic Plan 2017- 2021 includes a sub chapter addressing the mainstreaming of gender: “Gender equality, justice and mainstreaming”. Organizational documents and tools, e.g., work plans and M&E tools are used specifically to disaggregate data. Gender data and needs are collected through female translators and higher involvement of female EAs, this is carried out in the woman to woman conversations that are held. Safeguarding mechanisms and policies are also used to support gender sensitivity, e.g code of conduct, sexual harassment and security policies. The disaggregation of data, safeguarding and gender sensitivity are included as part of EAs training in Jerusalem. With regards to people with disabilities; WCC-EAPPI operates within the overall vision of WCC, which as a fellowship of churches is committed to a vision of inclusion of people with disabilities. Operationally, people with disabilities have been included as local contact persons and have taken part in the design and strategy of the work. Disabled persons also have fed into the 2016 baseline study. The program prioritizes support and protection of children/people with disability at checkpoints and when attempting to access education. In addition, as EAs are present in the communities they are in direct contact with people of disabilities and offer their support in protective or to document any incidents that occurred and if there is a need for a referral. The program has informally adopted many aspects of the IASC guidelines in regard to information management and protection. With regards to B’tselem, B'Tselem works according to a human rights approach in which the Palestinian population participates in monitoring and documenting violations of their own human rights through collection of testimonies from people who witness or experience violations, which are used to understand and analyze Israeli policies in the oPt, and through our video camera project, which trains and equips Palestinians to document the reality of occupation they experience. B’Tselem’s field researchers and video staff represent B’Tselem towards the Palestinian public, and these relationships serve as open lines of communication between B’Tselem and its beneficiary communities. In addition, B’Tselem strives to amplify voices of women and girls, and to bring to light the unique ways in which they are impacted by the Israeli occupation and apartheid regime. B’Tselem’s video camera project builds the capacity of women to monitor and document human rights violations they witness and to assume prominent roles within their communities in advocating for human rights through video documentation of the reality around them.
