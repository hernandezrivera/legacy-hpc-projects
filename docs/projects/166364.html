Project Summary

Gender based violence remains a serious challenge within northeast Nigeria, causing more psychological imbalance for armed conflict-affected IDPs as well as returnees, especially women and girls most especially during this COVID 19 pandemic. The lockdown and restriction of movement measures may place women and girls at heightened risk of intimate partner and other forms of domestic violence. In 2019, 73 per cent of reported incidents of GBV in the BAY States occurred in the context of intimate partner violence, meaning that, of the married/cohabitating survivors who reported an incident of GBV, in at least 98 per cent of the incidents, the perpetrator was their partner (former or current) (GBVIMS Annual Report, 2019). Besides, data on GBV incidents within the context of service provision suggests that 54percent of the new incidents of conflict-related sexual violence in 2019 were perpetrated by security personnel (GBVIMS Annual Report, 2019). Hence, incidents of abuse and violence are likely to increase as the armed security personnel is required to enforce travel restrictions and lockdown measures. According to Humanitarian Response Plan, 2019, an estimated 1.3 million people have been identified to be in need of gender-based violence prevention and response across Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe states. Women and girls are often targeted while performing basic tasks such as travelling to water points, collecting firewood to cook food and going to communal latrines. They are often exposed to SEA, forced into prostitution, early marriages, and survival sex in exchange for food, their movement is restricted and basic needs are deprived. Women and girls may continue to experience violence from their homes and communities as a result of lockdowns from the COVID 19 outbreaks, where they are often raped, or beaten from the perpetrators. Despite the consequences, families are still forcibly marrying daughters as young as 12 years old to men or members of armed opposition groups, citing poverty as the main reason. Between October 2018 and September 2019, the IRC provided case management services for over 400 gender-based violence survivors in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe, indicating the high need for continued support. The majority of crisis affected people have experienced violence, repeated displacement, loss of or separation from family members, loss and destruction of property deterioration of living conditions, disruption of livelihoods. According to the HRP, 2019, most this vulnerable groups include, female headed households, particularly adolescent girls who are forced into a role as breadwinners. Pre- existing gender inequalities due to unequal access to opportunities have contributed to limited resources and skills among women and girls which have increased their vulnerability and exposure to abuse including sexual exploitation. The crisis has significantly affected the dignity of women and children. This further entrenches pre-existing gender disparities.

1 - Needs

Despite the Humanitarian has been an acute one in northern Adamawa (Michika and Madagali), Borno (Konduga, Magumeri, Mongonu, Gwoza and MMC/Jere) and Yobe (Damaturu and Gujba) where the crisis shows no sign of abating and there is an increase in violence including GBV, SEA, the COVID 19 pandemic has also increases the risk of most women and girls as well as other protection needs, especially for women and girls who are the most at risk and have limited access to services as well as protection or redress. These locations which are hard to reach and according to the Nigeria HRP 2019, are subjected to military and AOGs attacks, making it difficult for aid workers to provide needed services for the affected population. The IRC intends to scale up programming to improve access to quality lifesaving and well-coordinated GBV response services for survivors and individuals at risk through the provision of comprehensive services including appropriate health assistance, GBV case management, psychological first aid accompanied with referrals for appropriate services, other forms of psycho-social support, provision of critical material support, as well as enhancing access to multisectoral services through establishing and strengthening referral mechanisms most especially during the lock downs as a result of COVIA 19 outbreak in these locations, where women and girls are under lockdowns with their perpetrators and unable to access adequate services when necessary. The proposed program is designed to ensure the IRC is capable of delivering a comprehensive and GBV timely response and prevention activities in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. The proposed intervention is intended to contribute to women and girls’ safety and healing by ensuring that women and girls can access case management services that address the physical, psychological and social consequences of GBV to prevent further harm. The intervention will also engage community members and stakeholders in order to ensure women and girls’ safety and protection from physical and sexual violence and to promote a safe and supportive environment where women and girls can freely access services without being blamed, stigmatized, socially excluded or fair of going out as a result of the lockdowns... In communities where no safe space exists, the IRC in cooperation with local authorities and community members will identify a safe space for both distribution and CM service provision. In each safe space, confidentiality of survivors will be supported by well-trained GBV caseworkers who meet with GBV survivors in a private space to provide case intake and ongoing management support. The primary goal of social work case management is to optimize client functioning, wellbeing, emotional and physical safety. Existing Women’s Action Groups (WAGs) member’s capacity will be strengthened in each of the WGSS as they will act as local focal points for women and girls seeking access to GBV services. WAGs, will e trained on basic case management aimed at enhancing their knowledge on preventing and responding to GBV related issues. The IRC will utilize previously trained caseworkers who are providing case management, GBV prevention and response messaging on voluntary basis, to enhance the sustainability of the program, and all implementation will be linked back to exiting program after the COVID 19 response.

2 - Activities

IRC will continue to respond to the needs of women and girls in its establish women and girls safe spaces (WGSS) and at the comprehensive women centers (CWCs) to increase at-risk women and girls’ access to GBV services as well as increase social support and linkages for additional services in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe. 500 COVID 19 related GBV survivors (Michika 50, Mubi North and Mubi South 100, MMC/Jere 50, Konduga 50, Magumeri 50, Mongonu 50, Gwoza 50 Damaturu 50, and Gujba 50) have safe access to quality case management services and psychosocial support. Using a survivor centered approach to GBV case management. Psychosocial support activities will foster healing and recovery which will be done through counselling and adequate. The IRC will conduct the following trainings in Gujba, Michika, and Magumeri LGA to enhance understanding and promote GBV prevention and response through coordination and collaboration: Three 3 day GBV core concept trainings will be done for 60 community stakeholders stakeholders)). Three 5 day GBV basic case management training will be done for 60 participants including WAG volunteers. In order to mitigate their risk to intimate partner violence due to cash control, Women will be linked up to livelihood support most especially the GBV survivors. Weekly remote safety audits and strategies developed will be coordinated and implemented, outcome from the safety audits will inform our decisions in other program implementation and women and girls will be supported with risk reduction material. 2,500 at risk women and girls will benefit dignity kits a. Through coordination with the community structures such as the EMAP members, SASA! Activities and women action group, 8,500 community members will be reached with GBV awareness and also on the COVID 19 pandemic prevention and how their community members can keep safe to avoid been infected. The IRC will provide PPE for all the community structures.
