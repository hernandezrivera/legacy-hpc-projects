Project Summary

"In 2022, Action Against Hunger will continue to serve the information and communication needs through its CwC information hubs, address existing gaps and ensure that the voices of the refugees and host communities are taken into considerations in program design, implementations, and decision-making process. The information hubs will continue to raise awareness on COVID-19, nutrition, health, mental health, gender, gender based violence, protection and SRH issues, and cross-cutting issues, such as preventive measures to reduce disaster risks (eg. fire, flood, cyclone) to ensure overall wellbeing of the targeted populations. Action Against Hunger and its partner BBC Media Action will mainstream protection in its actions, including conducting risk analysis and mitigating measures, training of staff and volunteers on Do No Harm principles, CE and AAP. Gender-balanced staffs and volunteers will be ensured to provide with an enabling environment for refugee and host communities to actively seek services, participate in discussions and utilize feedback mechanisms. To implement an inclusive response, outreach teams will be mobilized to ensure that vulnerable groups (eg. women, persons with disabilities, and elderly individuals) have access to life-saving information services at their door-steps, including access to information provisions, mechanisms to share their voices/opinions/concerns and provide feedback or lodge complaints.
Action Against Hunger’s CwC program works in line with Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles and ensures active and meaningful two-way communication between humanitarian actors and communities of concern. To address tensions between refugee and host communities, Action Against Hunger will implement its community engagement activities including behavior change awareness sessions, trust-building, and feedback sessions to address low levels of social cohesion and promote peaceful coexistence within and between the Rohingya refugee and host communities. Close coordination and liaison will be maintained with government and local authorities (DC, RRRC, CiCs etc.) to ensure that project’s actions are aligned with wider humanitarian response and rules/regulations. Information Hubs’ staff will continue to participate in coordination meetings at a camp level to remain informed of the latest decisions and also to maintain liaison with government and local counterparts. As part of its programming for host community, Action Against Hunger will organize ICT trainings for youth and livelihood groups in target unions to build local capacity to access digital tools and platforms to utilize available public and private services on livelihoods. For its host community activities, Action Against Hunger will work closely with government line-departments (eg. DYD, DSW, DWA) and ensure that information on livelihood-related services (eg. skills building opportunities, social safety net programs) are available to target communities.
Action Against Hunger’s partner BBC Media Action will implement the Common Service component of this proposal, which will focus on community engagement and accountability through key areas of research and development of knowledge products, training and mentoring as well as the production of content and tools to support improved community engagement to the Rohingya and host communities. The common service will support community-based organizations with a focus on vulnerable populations including women, youth, older people and people living with disabilities, key influencers within the community who people go to for information including Imams, Majhis and government officials. Key themes will include improving people – especially women’s ability to provide feedback, health and nutrition, MHPSS, protection including GBV and child protection, rebuilding in and post COVID-19 pandemic and disaster preparedness (eg. cyclone, fire incidents, floods). "

1 - Identified Needs

"Since 2018, the information centers/hubs have been working as a catalyst in the humanitarian response to the Rohingya refugee crisis by ensuring that people had access to vital information and communication resources, enabling a space for people to share their inputs, give feedback, and lodge complaints. In MNSA (Oct, 2021) the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic and associated preventative measures appears to have partially reversed but there are still significant impacts on people’s ability to access information on a range of issues, ability to give feedback, and ability to provide input into how decisions are made about their lives in both Rohingya and host communities. 74% (Rohingya) and 99% (Host) households said they were unable to access clear information with 18% (Rohingya) and 36% (host) households faced problems when accessing information. While the MNSA 2021 reported 93% of Rohingya households said their opinion had been taken into account, deeper dives and research from IOM/ACAPS and the Common Service reports that people believe they are not truly listened to and involved in how decisions are made that impact on them. Access to information also varies from topic to topic but some groups – especially women, girls, the elderly, and people living with disabilities continue to be left behind. The Rapid Gender Analysis (May 2020) as well as “What Matters” reported that women have overall much less information than men, with their access to it highly dependent on men. And, accountability and feedback mechanisms in place in the camps are less accessible to women and girls. Overall, female headed households and those with disabilities are more likely to have unmet needs. COVID-19 has put additional stress on the disaster preparedness and response (MNSA 2021). Women and girls, both from host and Rohingya communities, face limited access to health services partly due to lack of trust and access to information about services.Considering the varying levels of literacy, comprehension skills and other barriers to access information, Action Against Hunger will use context-specific, user-friendly pre-tested audio-visual communication contents and disseminate vital messages through center-based and neighborhood-based interpersonal activities and sessions. Alongside, Action Against Hunger’s strategically placed Info Hubs positioned within Nutrition and other program integrated centers will allow both direct and indirect beneficiaries, particularly pregnant and lactating women, of other programs to access services from the info hubs. The J-MSNA found that 56% of households reported needing protection services and 65% reporting unavailability of services due to COVID19 as a reason for not accessing services. Many households headed by women were found to be at more risk of such protection issues. Furthermore, there was an overreliance on machines and CiCs for referral cases. Therefore, alongside strengthening its information provisions and accountability mechanisms, Action Against Hunger will also support the common feedback platform to improve its response according to the needs of target communities. "
