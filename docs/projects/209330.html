Project Summary

Since the start of the conflict on April 15th, 2023, protection needs in Sudan have dramatically increased amid continued intense fighting, physical violence, sexual and gender-based violence, and mass displacement. Sudan has been qualified as the worst displacement crisis in the world. Reports of physical violence committed against residents in conflict hotspots and IDPs as well as GBV committed against women and girls have increased since the eruption of the conflict. With schools being turned into emergency shelters for IDPs, 19 million Sudanese children in Sudan have lost access to education since April 2023 (UNICEF). The already fragile social protection system in Sudan has collapsed with the conflict due to the destruction of critical infrastructures. As a result, 19.9 million individuals are in need of general, GBV and CP protection assistance according to the HNRP 2024 for Sudan. The present project aims at continuing Acted's efforts to offer timely and quality General Protection, GBV-related and CP assistance to conflict-affected populations and IDPs. In all locations, Acted and its partners will map out available protection services and conduct safety audits to ensure all protection risks are identified and survivors or at-risk individuals get appropriate reliable information on possible assistance. In Gedaref, Sennar, Blue Nile, White Nile and Al Jazeera, Acted will establish CBPNs, taking into consideration gender, age and disability, as well as Male Agents of Change Groups (MACGs). Acted protection staff will provide capacity-building to the CBPNs, MAGCs and other humanitarian actors on general protection, CP and GBV issues. In non-conflict zones, Acted will train health care workers in Clinical Management of Rape (CMR). In IDP-hosting states, Acted and its partners will conduct awareness-raising sessions on protection issues and referral pathways to mitigate against protection risks in IDP sites. In Khartoum state, Acted will financially and technically support Women’s Groups, assisting displaced women, to provide PSS activities for children and support to GBV survivors. These activities across all locations will contribute to the creation of a more protective environment through community-led interventions to address protection issues. In addition, in all states except Khartoum, Acted will also support IDPs transiting to another location with transportation cost covered though IPA. Acted will also provide case management for adult and children IDPs remaining in their first location of displacement, which will comprise of in-kind assistance and cash for health referral. When feasible, CP services will be provided through PSS and case management to ensure all identified at-risk children receive assistance. For women and girls in reproductive age, Acted will distribute monthly dignity kits. Thus, Acted will be able to provide quality and timely protection assistance to at-risk individuals in hard-to-reach and other areas.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In addition to being a protection-specific project, the intervention envisages specific needs faced by different socio-demographic groups and encourages a gender-balanced participation of target communities– with a zero-tolerance policy towards discrimination– throughout the Action, which is embedded in the “Do No HarmPrinciple” and follows a conflict-sensitive approach. Preparation Phase: - Before and during the intervention, Acted will assess the security context and protection risks, and continue monitoring the situation throughout the project’s lifespan. - The whole community will be sensitised on the project’s scope and activities and informed on selection criteria, which were designed to address protection risks and cover sectoral needs of most vulnerable (e.g. single women, PLWs, children, the elderly, PwDs).Program Implementation Phase: - ACTED mainstreams gender-adapted and keyprotection messages during community sensitisation and trainings. - Waiting time and safety during traveling will be addressed through adhoc protection measures. - First aid kits and protective wear are available on distribution sites. Accountability: - Acted has an independent MEAL unit with experience in setting up Complaints andResponse Mechanisms (CRMs). A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage the CRM, through which beneficiaries can report complaints/feedback in a safe and confidential way. Besides its staff deployed in the field daily, Acted will also conduct regular spot checks. Acted will evaluate if a complaint was triggered due to flaws, shortcomings or gaps in procedures or implementation strategy. In addition to case-by-case handling, anonymized complaints reports will be shared internally to possibly reshape the proposed intervention if needed, and inform future projects’ design. Human resources: - Acted has a Code of Conduct that is consistent with the U.N. Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises. Acted also complies with The Code of Conduct (CoC) of The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and UNHCR’s CoC. Acted’s CoC is implemented in each program location and is under the direct supervision of the Program Manager. Any incident would be immediatelyreported to coordination through a standardized communication pathway and actions would be taken by the appropriate levels of coordination. - For the intervention, Acted will set up a gender-balanced implementation team to ensure a safer environment, improve protection mainstreaming and favour inclusion mechanisms. In all activities, Acted will promote an equal gender participation.

4 - AAP & PSEA
4.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback? Please select as many as relevant.
4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Acted will establish Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive response in a timely manner. A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage CRM permanently installed in Area Office as well as in all target locations throughout the project. At the beginning and during implementation, target communities will be sensitized on the mechanism, and on how to submit complaints. Complaints will be confidentially recorded and reported each month at country-level. CRM will ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between Acted and community, pay specific attention to gender-related concerns, and improve programs quality, moving from intention to prevent/mitigate any adverse social/environmental/health risks. In addition, established community-based structures (CBPNs, MACGs, etc.) will be able to voice their communities' concerns about activities and inform Acted about the priority needs of the community members. When appropriate, Acted will include community-based structures into decision-making regarding activity implementation which will be an additional avenue for the communities to voice their opinions about activity design and potential ways to adapt it. In addition, Acted will be in regular contact with community leaders to ensure the acceptance of the overall action. The regular communication with community leaders will also enable to get feedback from the community members which will, in turn, enable to adapt the activities based on the received feedback.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

As part of its accountability strategy, Acted will mainstream protection and PSEA elements during all activities, particularly through its CRM. Acted has appropriate and proportionate procedures and controls in place to prevent, report, detect, respond and report on safeguarding issues & allegations. Recruitment: - Acted prevents employment/deployment of unsuitable individuals through rigorous screening. People employed by Acted already have an obligation to read and sign the code of conduct and internal policies. Administration and employee cycle: - Signature of Code of Conduct for staff; - Monitoring of trial periods for cases detected - Disciplinary measures follow-up on cases detected in conjunction with country coordination. - Training program related to safeguarding issues. - Acted has an in-house training related to safeguarding issues: i) Annual training on the Code of Conduct and Policies in all countries and at HQ for 7,000 staff; ii) Pre-departure training for international employees. ii) Specific training on SEA & Child Protection in intervention countries. Acted Complaints and response mechanisms. - Acted will establish Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive response in a timely manner. A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage CRM permanently installed in Area Office as well as in all target locations throughout the project. At the beginning and during implementation, target communities will be sensitized on the mechanism, and on how to submit complaints. Complaints will be confidentially recorded and reported each month at country-level. CRM will ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between Acted and community, pay specific attention to gender-related concerns, and improve programs quality, moving from intention to prevent/mitigate any adverse social/environmental/health risks. Complaint’s investigation & disciplinary measures -Acted has capacity to investigate safeguarding issues in an independent and confidential way, while protecting both the alleged victim and the alleged perpetrator (Acted SEA Policy, Articles 19 to 21; Acted Child protection policy, Articles 20 to 22). Acted must always ensure that the author of a report does not suffer from any harm as a result. Acted also endeavours to protect victims of prohibited acts (Acted SEA policy, article 23). Acted has psychologists available to support individuals who would have been impacted as a result of a failure to comply with Acted's safeguarding policies. The HQ HR department will assess allegations to design an investigation plan and select an investigation team. Depending on investigation conclusions, different measures can be taken including disciplinary actions or reporting to prosecuting authority.
