Project Summary

The PP aims to empower CP system within local communities of NW Syria, by ensuring that CP concerns and considerations are taken into all other sectors of the humanitarian response to maximize the impact of the CP services in these communities and minimize the risks to children which may exacerbate from programming without children’s safety and will being taken into considerations. HRS will operate on many aspects to reach the goal of the project, such as: -Integrating CFS with school activities: Hurras is targeting to engage1,500 children(750M,750F) in structured, sustained CP programmers, within 60 schools’ libraries. And 300 children (150 M,150 F) through the community centres. - CM: HRS has will equipped and professional team in each office who can handle 100 cases(50M,50F) per time through CM system and children’s emergency fund. HRS will manage 500 children (250 M,250F) through this project. - CP training; to ensure adequate programming, 3 school staff of each of the 180 schools that HRS works with will receive training in regarding CP or CSG. In addition, as HRS is committed to raise the capacity of all partners who is working with HRS, HRS is planning to train all 20 suppliers on CP and CSG policies. - Provide microgrants attached to the 28 established CBCPC, to encourage local initiatives of raising the level of CP within their communities. - Awareness raising; HRS has developed multi- staged raising awareness program which targets 100 health paediatric clinics, 60 schools, and 28 communities. Health paediatric clinics would have a stand in the waiting room which will contain CP messages in brochures and awareness raising children magazine, this magazine (Tayara Warak) will also be distributed in 60 schools, to raise awareness of children about their rights and how to protect themselves in child friendly language. In addition, HRS will provide raising awareness campaigns through its 28-CP committees and the community centres for CP awareness raising and parenting programs to reach out for 5600 adults (2800 M,2800F) and 6000 children(3000M,3000F). - Establish and support the running of school-level child protection committees, HRS has established 168 committees in schools and could scale up to include another 162 schools. Each committee gender balanced representation of 3 parents, 3 school staff and the head teacher. Parents are elected during parent/teacher meetings.- Youths programs for 60 adolescents (30 M,30 F) between 16-21 years old, conduct cycles of training for youths and adolescents, through established 3 community centres about civil rights and engagement, life skills, and leadership along with vocational trainings, to support initiatives led by this age groups and provide them options which are limited for them. Each centre can hold up to 20 youths between 16-21 years old per cycle and Hurras would support 2 youth initiatives per centre.
HRS will establish 20 reception centres in 20 schools in NW Syria to provide a safe environment for 10,000 children(5000M,5000F) to overcome what they've been through in addition to providing services for 20,000 displaced families, where each centre will be equipped with a team of: PSS &CM workers, the centres will be also provided with 20,000 copy of service maps and 10,000 key protection messages and the new TW issued by HRS. The centres will allow affected people to access to information about services and humanitarian aid, access to key CP message, family separation and other risks identified by the community, identification of vulnerable children and link to CP CM, linkage to humanitarian assistance and immediate support to unaccompanied and separated children, access to PFA and PSS activities and conducting awareness sessions in various topics for children between 6-16 years old. In addition to a focal point to respond to cases of unaccompanied and separated children.

4 - Needs

According to OCHA, Since Dec-19, nearly 390K people have fled from their homes– around 80% of whom are women and children –, mainly from southern Idleb and western Aleppo governorates, mostly escaping hostilities by moving to urban centres and IDP camps in NW Idleb. Tens of thousands of others have reportedly moved to areas such as Afrin, Azaz and Al-Bab.Civilians living in northwest Syria have been enduring a drastically worsening humanitarian situation as hostilities continue to intensify since Nov-19. Many have been displaced multiple times, with each new displacement compounding experienced risks and vulnerabilities. With further mass displacement taking place since 27-Jan, this number is likely to continue increasing significantly. The large numbers of displacements are severely exacerbating protection shortages across northwest Syria, while HRS field team identified the following key issues:HRS can’t reach children on time: difficulties in reaching children in need of immediate PFA. The randomness of attacks led to the randomness of displacement and the population is scattered over 60 communities.The affected population has poor or lack of access to information related to available services in the host communities and access to humanitarian aid.Children have no access to play and PSS opportunities which could add a burden on their parents and the host families and they lose the opportunity to engage in safe, fun and child-friendly activities that are much needed after they were removed from their original social environment and experience the horrors of the last week indiscriminate attacks and the existence of early marriages within the communities, most of them are females between 12 and 17 years. Also, HRS identified child labour within their communities as problem preventing children from attending schools. All of the above-mentioned problem can be managed through CM system.

5 - Implementation of activities

CFS’s will offer 1PSS session per day for each class followed by reading sessions and will also offer after school extracurricular clubs for 1 hour. -PSS programs will be integrated into schools by providing PSS in school’s libraries. - 3 standalone CFS’s will act as a community centre and provide parenting programs for parents and ECD activities for children of 0-5 years old. -Awareness raising: CP key cluster messages will distribute in health clinics, schools and campaigns run by community-based committees. - Provide microgrants for the local CP committees. - Establish and support the running of school-level CP committees, HRS has established 168 committees in schools. -establish 20 reception centres for IDPs. - Conduct cycles of training for 60 youths and adolescents(30M,30F), through established CFS’s about civil rights and engagement, life skills, and leadership along with vocational trainings. Specialized CP services (CM) for girls and boys are available in targeted locations .- Strengthen human resources capacity to respond to CP concerns in Syria - training to frontline workers, education actors, health workers, suppliers, and key members of the communities, about CPIE, CPMS, SIR,CSG ,etc. HRS has been doing similar activities for the last 6 years and all these activities are already existing but HRS wants to extend them to cover more beneficiaries .the M&E tools are already developed with relevant technological solutions specifically designed for HRS such as salesforce software for CM, FORMERA for attendance and tracking of children. As for the documents that will be used: Monthly reports that includes reporting against the log frame, IPTT and other means of evaluation such as: pre-post Programming test and training reports, indicator trucking sheet, quality (qualitative and qualitative), assessment tools (specific to each activity),M&E plan. Then Means of varication will be photos, attendance sheets, interviews, videos, FGDs& questioners.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Establish reception centers - Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members - Likelihood: High. Impact: High -Mitigating measures: 1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. 2. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for COC and CSG policies . Specialized CP services (CM) for girls and boys are available in targeted locations - Protection risk: Risk of creating or perpetuating stigma for girls, boys and their families if staff are not appropriately trained or if client data is not managed appropriately. - Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High-Mitigating measures: 1. ensure training packages are in line CPMSs and consider remote managed context 2. Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules. 3. include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision. 4. Service mappings should only include services that have been determined to provide safe and quality services. 5. Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place. 6. CP messaging to non-Protection actors. Strengthen human resource capacity to respond to CP concerns in Syria - Protection risk: Challenges in measuring the impact of capacity development; compromised quality of trainings due to contextual constraints; high turnover of staff - Likelihood: Low. Impact: High - Mitigating measures: 1. Ensure training programs are designed to assess impact of the trainings .2. Link with other capacity building initiatives to ensure synergy 3. Support development of a cohort of core CP trainers in Syria 4. Encourage follow-up, mentoring and coaching initiatives.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: • A safe environment allows the staff to implement the activities (low airstrike bombing). • No delay in the provision of financial resources. • Engagement of local community with the committees. • Commitment of partner groups to HRS’s SOP. • Availability of services providers or services required as part of CM plan. Risks: Safeguarding incidents (Medium) HRS has a well-established. safeguarding allegation system that is accessible by children, families and community members. Each activity is assigned a safeguarding officer is made aware of how to report incidents through multiple ways: online system, verbal communication with safeguarding staff and complaint boxes. Community acceptance (Medium) Before engaging in any new community HRS teams establish formal relationships with independent civil entities. The risk to personal safety of field staff (Medium) HRS has a duty of care policy and all staff are trained on safety measures including emergency locators .Security incidents (Medium to High) HRS has a strong security department with qualified staff that monitors the security situation and put in place prevention, response and contingency plans .all staff receive S&S trainings. Poor staff capacity (Medium) HRS invests in 4 weeks training of staff when they join, and ongoing coaching and supervision processes are in place. HRS work closely with partners to identify the capacity building gaps and put in place training programs to fill the gaps. Poor evidence-based and knowledge management due to remote management (Low)HRS has a strong M&E team that is independent of the program team. HRS uses staff from different offices & projects to cross-check its programming, in addition, the area managers are empowered to manage the day to day implementation. A potential loss of money due to theft or airstrikes (Medium) HRS’s finance team limit the cash on hand to a certain ceiling to minimize the risk and rely on an established network of cash transfer offices.
