Project Summary

IOM will strengthen preparedness, community resilience and early action capacities for natural disasters and outbreaks of communicable diseases, through emergency stockpiling and prepositioning of medical supplies, availability and capacity of emergency medical teams, community engagement and community-based planning, risk communication and social and behaviour change communication, and bolstering public health surveillance capacity for early warning alert and response. Further, IOM will build government capacity for humanitarian interventions through training of healthcare workers and emergency infrastructure and equipment support, and ensure mainstreaming of SRHR, HIV and GBV interventions in emergency capacity building to ensure no one is left behind.
Ensure access to and continuity of essential primary health and MHPSS services, including capacity building, procurement of equipment and medicines, service delivery, emergency referrals, and outbreak preparedness, response and surveillance operations for vulnerable displaced people and host communities. This will be implemented through an integrated community-based approach in Niassa, Cabo Delgado and Nampula provinces and through the deployment of IOM-operated mobile clinics, temporary clinics and support to government mobile brigades.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Through this project IOM proposes to deliver community-based support, supplement primary health care service delivery to hard-to-reach populations and improve access to mental health care in Niassa, Cabo Delgado and Nampula provinces, which continue to be affected by pre-existing social issues, ongoing violence and displacement, recent impacts by natural disasters and outbreaks of communicable diseases such as cholera. IOM will provide primary health care services through the staffing and operation of mobile and temporary clinics in hard-to-reach areas, support integrated mobile brigades, and provide trainings and mentorship for healthcare workers, to meet the needs of displaced persons and host communities. IOM will ensure minimum capacities are in place to prevent, detect and respond to outbreaks of communicable diseases and investigate public health events with epidemic potential. IOM will aim to bolster infection prevention and control measures at the level of communities and primary healthcare service delivery points, engage communities on preventive behaviour and the risks associated with such diseases, capacity-building activities for case management and ensure the availability of rapid response medical teams, pre-positioning of supplies, and adapting and pre-positioning of IEC materials. Further, IOM integrated teams, in coordination with local authorities, will ensure community-based support for mental health and psychosocial support services, TB/HIV care and referral to specialized services. Community health workers will conduct community engagement, health promotion and community based surveillance activities in resettlements sites and among host communities to identify and respond to cases of communicable diseases and chronic conditions, including HIV, TB. This latter support will include capacity building of health authorities on public health surveillance activities. IOM will ensure coordination with DPS, SPS, SDSMAS, MISAU, INS and the health cluster partners at national, provincial and district levels.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

IOM has a mandatory instruction, which binds staff members to standards of behavior at all times and especially when working with beneficiaries of assistance, including during emergency response. Sexual exploitation and abuse of affected populations constitutes gross misconduct and is grounds for disciplinary action, including summary dismissal and referral for criminal prosecution, where appropriate. In 2019, IOM launched the We Are All In platform, which includes a publicly available reporting form and an internal space with awareness-raising and training materials. IOM has also made PSEA commitments that include inter-agency coordination to prevent and address SEA incidents, including active participation at cluster levels in Mozambique and engagement with the PSEA focal point. IOM provides humanitarian health services free of charge and all IOM staff attend an obligatory training on PSEA and health projects are required to outline necessary actions for the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. There will be regular reminders advocating for all health responders to undergo training on PSEA, and to facilitate training for their target populations. As much as possible, health teams will consider gender balance in staffing, so that women and girls feel safe when they access services.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

IOM has an integrated protection-MHPSS approach which ensures intersectional considerations of protection and MHPSS needs as well linkages with health programming in a whole-of-community approach. IOM Health Teams who support mobile clinics and community based activities and referrals are trained on protection principles. IOM identifies individual and structural-level protection risks by conducting the protection/safety audits in the districts. Also, the individual cases are identified and referred to specialized protection services in order not to cause further harm. IOM follows strict data protection rules and assistance standards in order to ensure meaningful access, not to cause further harm and empower individuals and communities assisted. IOM conducts community based rapid assessments in targeted communities and settlements, to map and assess populations main needs, vulnerabilities, resources, expectations and capacities, including key informant interviews and focus group discussions. This approach ensures that the project activities are tailored and respond to the needs of the communities and that community members can participate in project design and implementation. IOM also conducts satisfaction surveys and lessons learned workshops with affected populations to ensure accountability and for services to be appropriately tailored to meet needs. IOM’s community health workers are identified from the communities they serve, ensuring local involvement and accountability to affected populations, while enhancing the impact of interventions through their contextual knowledge and tailored approaches. This localized strategy has ensured community-level trust and holds benefits for public health-related social and behavior change communication. Moreover, IOM regularly assesses knowledge of community activists and health workers to ensure the quality of services. IOM has trained and deployed clinicians in coordination with provincial and district health authorities. These healthcare workers (HCWs) are presently employed by IOM, compensated at government salary scales and are supervised jointly by IOM medical staff and district medical chiefs. All HCWs have been identified and recruited from Cabo Delgado, including recent graduates and those waiting absorption in the health system, as part of IOM’s localization strategy. IOM has also actively worked with provincial and district authorities to integrate HCWs into public health system.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

IOM Mobile Teams identify people with special needs including individuals with physical and mental disabilities under the integrated health-protection-MHPSS activities. Also, IOM uses a diverse range of health information dissemination materials and channels (including door-to-door, community theatre groups, radio, and other community-based channels) to reach individuals with impairments. IOM also conducts accessibility audits of its health service delivery points for improvements to ensure greater accessibility and disability inclusion.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed project will include some construction work/upgrades on temporary clinics, with minimal environmental impact. IOM’s community-based health and MHPSS activities strive to use environmentally friendly, recycled or reusable materials, and respect for the environment is included in some awareness-raising sessions with communities. IOM also ensures appropriate waste management procedures are in place at all health service delivery points.
