Project Summary

The goal of this project is to provide lifesaving WASH services to rural communities in Pochalla and Pibor counties of Jonglei State. Pochalla and Jebel Boma areas are found in the eastern part of South Sudan bordering Ethiopia. The project will target 36,110 (including 3,122 IDPs) people in need in both Pochalla and Jebel Boma areas with key WASH services and will include repair and rehabilitation of 15 boreholes, identification and training of 72 hygiene promoters, training and orientation of 20 hand pump technicians, distribution of WASH NFIs and house to house community mobilization. The project will also include specific women friendly spaces according to identified needs among the IDP women and girls in addition to special attention to school going girls' WASH needs. It will employ sustained awareness of the mothers so as to uproot the problems at the household level. We shall use the CDC proven ways to prevent diarrhea such as provision of safe drinking water, adequate sanitation and human waste disposal, promotion of hand-washing with soap, promotion of breastfeeding to reduce exposure to contaminated water, treatment with oral re-hydration therapy and referral for antibiotics treatment, training of community health workers on diarrhea treatment.
Currently South Sudan’s health policy articulates community anchored health services where disease prevention and health promotion are placed at the centre of interventions. It is the desire of RHS to address issues at the most basic level of society. We believe doing this will lead to aggregation of the best practices at county and state levels and eventually the whole country.

Needs

Pochalla County and Jebel Boma Payam (Pibor County) are found in the eastern part of South Sudan, bordering Ethiopia. Due to problems of accessibility, the areas are extremely secluded from services and other parts of the country yet they are prone to water borne diseases and acute watery diarrhea in particular. Our recent rapid assessment found that 11 out of 14 boreholes in Pochalla North (Otalo, Ajawara, Alari and Nyium) and 7 out of eleven 11 boreholes in Pochalla South are not functioning. Four (4) non-functioning boreholes have also been identified in Jebel Boma Area. In most cases people are getting water directly from the swamp which are poorly protected and Seasonaly. To make matters worse open defecation was also evidently rampant in the area exacerbated by poor personal hygiene among women, men, boys and girls pointing to a great need for WASH intervention. In Jebel Boma area, an assessment carried out by REACH under the auspices of World Food Program found no WASH partner in the area despite critical WASH needs and reported 46.3% prevalence of acute watery diarrhea among children less than five years. The assessment singled out Nawayapuru as the locality with the greatest WASH needs; poor access to water and no boreholes, rampant open defecation, no recall of WASH nonfood items (NFIs) distribution in the area at the time of the assessment. RHS direct communication with the county authorities indicates more than 3,000 internally displaced people in Labarab and Maruwo in addition to the REACH estimated host and returnees population of 24,089 in the Jebel Boma area, who are in dire need of WASH services. These include women, men boys and girls.

Activities

Through this project RHS will repair and Rehabilitate 22 bore holes in Otalo 1&2, Ajwara, Alari, Nyium, Pochalla Town, Obodi and Okadi (Pochalla County) Itti, Kayiwa, Mewun, Nawayapuru, Maruwo, Kessingor and Labarab in Jebel Boma Payam (Pibor County) and will identify and train 60 hygiene promoters and 12 supervisors for Pochalla and Jebel Boma areas. The project will also include the identification and training of 20 hand pump mechanics for maintenance of the hand pumps in the areas. Purchase and distribution of water treatment NFIs shall also be undertaken and will be supported by communication and community mobilization activities to promote WASH best practices such as proper hand-washing with soap and water, treatment of drinking water, proper disposal of human excreta and proper handling of food as well as distribution of soap at household level to vulnerable families will be actively done throughout the project. Hygiene promoters shall also support solid waste management activities and will identify risk practices for AWD transmission in the communities. RHS trained hygiene promoters will disseminate AWD preventive and response messages in public places in addition to distribution of branded T-shirts and caps bearing critical WASH messaging. Public awareness campaigns in churches and mosques, schools, markets shall be taken periodically to back up house to house mobilization activities. We shall also undertake active AWD case finding, stabilization, referral and reporting of cases of AWD; simple cases shall be managed with ORS, zinc sulphate, aqua tablets in addition to distribution of ORS at household level and training on its use. Promotion of exclusive breastfeeding for at least six months shall target productive mothers. Provision of women friendly spaces shall be discussed with women groups and shall be provided according to agreed terms and dignitary pads shall be provided to school going girls among IDPs and host communities in order to keep them in schools. As a long term measure RHS will also undertake formation of health and environment clubs in schools to continue advocating for WASH best practices. Finally RHS M & E team shall undertake active evaluation and documentation of campaign results.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

RHS has a plan in place for handling complaints from the beneficiaries. The entire project is designed to provide information on WASH best practices among the host communities and IDPS. . RHS also works through community leaders to listen to community needs and addresses them through consensus and confidence building mechanisms.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes in deed messages are tailored to situations as they emerge. What is important is the specific respect given to each beneficiary to take part in the decision making process. Our information giving approach is gender sensitive; women are employed to handle sensitive information from women and girls and men handle issues related to their farewell. We also take special precautions to protect children especially unaccompanied minors in the IDP communities.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

RHS code of conduct prohibits the engagement of staff in sexual exploitation and abuse and all employees are trained on before contracting. This project will involve communities and households in particular. Therefore, it has been designed to protect the vulnerable household members especially women, girls and children from sexual exploitation and abuse.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

During the rapid needs assessment, the community gatekeepers (the chiefs, youth and women leaders and local government officials) were involved but the direct beneficiaries were also involved. For example, women were directly consulted during the water fetching activities at the swamp and the operational boreholes as well as the community protected wells.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

This project is designed to include a rigorous monitoring and evaluation mechanisms using RHS M & E framework. Complaints that will not reach through the normal channels i.e. from the supervisors and project officers shall be received directly by the M & E field visits. After proper investigation of the alleged issues, the head office shall provide feedback to both the beneficiaries and partners including the corrective measures taken where applicable.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project involves the community in M & E through their leaders and selected group representative. This includes direct stories from women, men, girls and boys on their take on the project. In fact for the sake of the continuity of the best practices, our plan is to train community leaders on continued monitoring and evaluation of such practices among different groups within their communities.
