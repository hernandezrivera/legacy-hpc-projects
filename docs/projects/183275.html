Project Summary

The years 2020 & 2021 have been exceptionally challenging; Lebanon’s economic situation continues to severely worsen with the LBP losing 90% of its value, spread of COVID-19 pandemic, prevalence of substantial stressors & lack in access to specialized services.
These crises had multiple and worsening impacts including increasing unemployment & underemployment, particularly for youth & women, reducing access to & quality of basic services; increasing multidimensional poverty & inequality; decrease in social stability & community cohesion. The most vulnerable groups, including children, are worse hit, particularly in the continued absence of a sustained social assistance programme to vulnerable households. Around 3.3 million people in Lebanon are categorized as being vulnerable, with an estimated 2.7 million categorized 'poor'.
Given the fragile context, short & long term effects on the well-being of children & caregivers were tackled:
On the short term children are at greater risk to fall victim to child labor, child marriage & other forms & abuse, caregivers are using negative coping mechanisms & women & girls are at risk of SEA.
On the long term, these conditions will be reflected on the overall development of children which can result enduring repercussions.
That said, RMF, focuses its intervention on strengthening access to life-saving services to vulnerable & at-risk cohorts from Lebanese & PRL populations residing in most vulnerable & hard to reach districts in North Lebanon where homes are crowded, substance abuse is at increase, limited access to services is exacerbating living conditions.
An integrated CP programming is followed stressing on the logic of equity for girls, boys, women & men seeking:
- More involvement of existing local entities such as scouts, youth clubs, social movements, municipality, mosques, SDCs, COBs;
- Increased access to an integrated package of quality services;
- Enhanced capacity of disadvantaged children, caregivers, & communities to promote practices that protect children and support in achieving inclusive interventions;
- Identification & outreach on new potential groups of hard to reach populations that may need support & referral to additional services due to the recent economic crisis.
Therefore, RMF socio-ecological approach focuses on the community as a whole entailing the preventive & responsive aspects of CP interventions.
- Prevention: This component aims to foster social connections & interactions through using existing community networks of children, youth & women, normalize daily life, promote a sense of competence & restoration of control over one’s life, build on and encourage children’s & community’s innate resilience to crisis. For this, RMF community based approach to PSS relies on existing structures as an entry point for making positive social and behavioral change & build their capacities for supporting in achieving inclusive interventions for preventing and responding to violence, neglect, abuse, & exploitation against girls & boys.
- Response: During crisis situations, children & adolescents encounter substantial stressors & in turn lack access to specialized services. For children anxiety, depression & other stress-related problems threaten their ability to grow up healthy and happy. Violence can take a lifelong toll on their emotional health, physical health & social development. If exposed to such breaches, the experience can even hamper their development. For adolescents, severe distress & exposure to traumatic events can lead to various harmful outcomes, including alcohol and drug abuse, low self-esteem, health issues, poor school performance, self-harm & suicide. For this, RMF conducts FPSS activities to children & caregivers at medium to high risk of CP violations or who have experienced a CP violation and their peers, in addition to case management services & referral to HADDI emergency cash assistance.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

RMF's intervention seeks achieving both objectives through an integrated CP approach. Under the first objective: 400 most vulnerable children will be benefiting from case management through ensuring the proper use of the SOPs and its standardized tools; 800 women, men, girls and boys identified at medium and high risk will be participating in FPSS activities and 1,800 most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys will be referred to HADDI emergency cash assistance program. Regarding the second objective of the ERP protection, 1,000 women, men, girls and boys will be engaged in CBPSS activities and conducting 10 initiatives that aim to mitigate child protection risks in the targeted communities in North Lebanon. To facilitate access to the foreseen activities participants will be provided by transportation, refreshment, internet recharge bundles if needed due to lockdown or road blockades and insurance in addition to ensuring precautions measures such as keep safe distance between participants, hand sanitizers are displayed at the entrance of the training venue, keeping face masks as much as possible and avoiding crowding during break time. In concrete terms, this means that RMF focuses on: (1) Outreaching local communities in North Lebanon and build their capacities. (2) Providing women, men, girls and boys with culturally and age appropriate activities focusing on CP key messages.(3) Mobilizing local authorities, key figures and caregivers to advocate for CP concerns.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

RMF calls front liners as well as service providers to take special measures to protect women and girls from GBV and other forms of abuse and exploitation. Similarly, promotion of social inclusion, in particular disability inclusion and working against all forms of discrimination based on gender, age, social position, ethnicity or other factors are fundamental principles of RMF's work. RMF counts on the community itself through building the capacity of core statutory service providers to increase their commitment and willingness to support and provide CP prevention and response services. Community dialogue engaging all population cohorts is initiated for identifying risks and suggesting solutions to address rights violations and issues related to CP risks especially those related to early marriage and child labour. Further, the project team will ensure prevention and response to SEA and the potential for other forms of misconduct are fully considered and integrated in the response.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

To ensure AAP, RMF seeks to ensure that communities are not only involved in decisions that directly impact their lives, but also able to raise feedback and complaints should we fail to fulfil our commitments to them. RMF is a right-based actor and puts vulnerable people at the center of its work, including children, youth, PwDs, and migrants.RMF will be using a combination of channels for collecting feedback and complaints; active channels where affected populations are deliberately and systematically solicited (such as focus groups); reactive channels, where affected populations have more control over when and how they share feedback/complaint (such as feedback/complaint box/hotline); and open channels where feedback is solicited in an ad hoc manner, through ongoing interaction during the implementation of the activities. This means that through implementing the Do No Harm principles and having complaint mechanisms partners ensure AAP.
