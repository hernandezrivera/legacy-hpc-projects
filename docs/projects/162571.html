Project Summary

"Mercy Corps proposes the Projet d’appui à la sécurité alimentaire, la réconciliation et la résilience with the goal of meeting the pressing food security and nutritional needs of conflict-affected populations as well as improving the well-being and economic resilience of communities through a holistic and conflict-sensitive response.
In Koro and Bankass circles, the project will provide food assistance over the course of 12 months, concentrating assistance during the lean season when food insecurity is predicted to spike. For the majority of households, assistance will be delivered through two interlinked modalities: unconditional food vouchers redeemed with local vendors (initial 2 months support), and Voucher/Cash for Work (CfW) payments (subsequent 2 months support), meaning that each target household will receive 4 months of support to cover food needs, either during the lean season or following displacement. In parallel, the most vulnerable households without an able-bodied member for participation in CfW will receive 4 months of unconditional transfer.
Based on successful approaches implemented by Mercy Corps in Mali over the course of the past seven years, the project will also meet the pressing needs of communities for humanitarian assistance in the sectors of Economic Recovery and Market Systems (ERMS), Agriculture and Food Security, Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance, Shelter and Settlements and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH). It will enhance the work of other humanitarian actors by filling gaps in geographic and sectoral coverage, and providing options for communities to meet their own needs even after the initial infusion of immediate humanitarian assistance. The program will pursue interventions under two primary outcomes:
1) Conflict-affected communities maintain and increase well-being despite ongoing shocks and stresses; and 2) Conflict-affected communities receive a timely and holistic package of humanitarian support in the immediate aftermath of conflict and displacement related shocks. In both Mopti and Timbuktu, the program will benefit from Mercy Corps’ prior presence through other, complementary programming for a rapid startup. Furthermore, underlying all of the program’s activities are conflict-sensitive approaches that seek to maintain humanitarian access and ensure beneficiary protection, and to lay a foundation for improved intra and inter-community relations.
The end result will be a more holistic and tailored response to the ongoing emergencies in central and northern Mali that meets the needs of 10,900 households (81,580 individuals)"
