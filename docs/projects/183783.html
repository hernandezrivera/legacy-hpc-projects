Project Summary

The proposed intervention links with and contributes to the Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) Sector strategic objectives of the 2022 humanitarian response plan Strategic Objective 1: Improved the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life sustaining food assistance, Strategic Objective 2: Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives, and Strategic Objective 3: Improve communities’ capacity to sustain households’ livelihoods through the rehabilitation/building of productive infrastructure as well as supporting services. It targets supporting displaced populations, returnees and host communities to meet their basic needs and access to essential basic services in West Darfur, West Kordofan, and South Kordofan States. As inadequate investment in agriculture, protracted displacements, frequent insecurity, impacts of the ever-changing climate, lack of investment in social services have condemned a large number of people to food insecurity and lack of livelihood opportunities, this intervention will also contribute directly to the rest of FSL sector sub-objectives by addressing life threatening and life sustaining needs. In addition, it will indirectly contribute to the sector’s third strategic objective by providing investment in rehabilitation and construction of productive agricultural infrastructures and supporting initiatives related to addressing the negative impact of climate change. The continued insecurity in the state means that access to agricultural land remains limited as farmers abandoned their farms. According to IPC analysis, during the first projection period (June–September 2021), which will coincide with the peak lean season, it is estimated that around 9.7 million people (21% of the population analysed) will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse. The project will support the people with agricultural production, livestock, IGA, MPCA, vocational training, and strengthening early warning early action system interventions to improve food security and provide opportunities to earn incomes. Hence, it enables food insecure households to access food, improve food production, and increase access to incomes, enable households meet their basic needs and provide livelihood opportunities and strengthen their resilience to further shocks and stresses.
This project will target 121,070 persons that include, IDPs, vulnerable host community members, and refugees at a total cost of $5,500,000. In the three states, the project will support vulnerable households affected by manmade and natural disasters, IDPs, returnee communities with limited access and availability of adequate, sufficient and nutritious food and small-scale farmers who face production and market constraints. There will be deliberate focus on women, beneficiaries that are marginalized and socially excluded and thus bear the greatest burden of food insecurity. Vulnerable individuals from the host, internally displaced, returnee and refugee communities who face food insecurity due to limited access to and availability of adequate and nutritious food, communities in high-risk areas affected by natural and manmade disasters, and small scale farmers who face major constraints in farming because of high cost of production, post-harvest losses, access to farmlands, markets and information will be the priority target groups under this project.

1 - Needs

Food insecurity and malnutrition remain alarmingly high in Sudan with an estimated number of 9.8 million people facing stress and crisis levels of acute food insecurity. The main contributing factors to this dire situation are protracted conflict, displacement since 2003, the current economic downturn, and higher inflation rates. The food price hikes further intensified by the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic. The prevailing humanitarian situation deteriorated rapidly during 2021, increasing the numbers of rising hunger across the country with an increased estimate of food assistance needs 50-60% above the five-year average (FEWSNET, Food Security Outlook, June 2021 – January 2022). In 2021, the Sudan Food Security & Livelihoods Cluster estimated 9.8 persons are in need of humanitarian assistance, that forms more than 20% of the population of Sudan., The highest prevalence of population in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse are observed in North Darfur (25%), followed by West Darfur (22%), North Kordofan (20%), South Kordofan (20%), Gedarif (19%) and Central, East and South Darfur states, ranging from 17-18%.. The humanitarian landscape in Sudan is complex due to long-standing protracted crises from repeated and ongoing displacements (due to conflicts, climate-related shocks, and stresses), poverty, the impact of Covid-19, deteriorating economic situation, and the influx of refugees. They require urgent action to save lives, protect livelihoods and reduce food consumption gaps. The most vulnerable people in Sudan include internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and vulnerable residents who are engaged in farming and pastoral livelihoods. Residents near urban centers are also severely affected by the soaring market prices of food and other basic services and the incidence of the COVID-19 pandemic. Women and girls are more likely to experience a worsening of existing inequalities and disproportionate secondary impacts of restrictions compared with men and boys. Even more at risk are other marginalized groups, including persons with disabilities and those in extreme poverty.Without external robust intervention, people in need will not be able to access food, basic services, and agricultural inputs to resume their main economic activity, agriculture. The main areas identified for intervention comprise agricultural inputs support such as drought-tolerant seeds and farm tools which are critical for both wet and winter season agriculture; training to increase knowledge and skills in improved agricultural practices; promoting climate change adaptation such as planting of trees and soil water management; vegetable gardening support for dietary diversity, provision of irrigation facilities both for wet and winter season cultivation; and small ruminants and poultry production support to increase asset holding and improve access to nutritious products such as eggs and milk. The project further focuses on protecting productive livelihoods assets such as vaccinations for livestock, and provision of cash-based assistance to meet the most basic household needs, especially for the most vulnerable households. Provision of vocational skills development support for employment and support of small businesses for women are among the main livelihoods interventions considered. In some of the localities in the states targeted, some of the people in need are already engaging in negative coping mechanisms to get food such as increased cutting down of trees for sale as firewood and burning of charcoal, borrowing money, and selling productive assets to access food. Levels of debt are escalating and affected populations will need to access cash as soon as possible to repay their debts and avoid increased food insecurity.

2 - Activities/Outputs

-Distribution 183 MT of short duration and drought tolerant assorted seeds of Millet, Sorghum, Sesame, vegetables, legumes and groundnuts to 6.800 farmers through seeds fairs and direct distribution based on the particular context. - Support of assorted hand tools (digging and weeding hoes, sickles etc.) for cultivation to 6,800 farmers through direct distribution. -Provision of MPCA to 20,210 to IDPs, People Living with Disability (PLWD), and the old and terminal ill and vulnerable women. The MPCA will be based on the MEB that will be revised in 2021 and will be preceded by a market assessment. -Vocational training to 300 vulnerable youths to acquire market-driven skills for employment. -Training and capacity building of 1380 lead farmers in improved agricultural practices, including climate smart agriculture, pest and disease control, soil fertility and soil moisture conservation & management, and post-harvest management. -Strengthening system of cascading project-promoted improved agricultural practices by the lead farmers to their targeted fellow farmers and carrying out 2 assessment to progresses of adoptions.- Support and follow operations of 8 demonstration plots and farmers field schools for improved farming practices to address 13,800 farmers through trained 1380 lead farmers.-Support strengthening of community seed system through support operations of 2 community managed seed banks.-Distribution of livestock (chicken and goats) to 2,700 households through livestock fairs using vouchers. -Vaccination of 23,000 livestock, focusing mainly small livestock, goats, sheep and chicken. The vaccination of livestock will be implemented in close partnership with the department of veterinary services in the Ministry of Production and Economic Resources (MoPER) -Livelihood skills training in small business and provision of supplies to 1,700 beneficiaries upscale & start small business for income generation in urban areas. -Provision of vegetable seeds and drip irrigation kits, and hand tools to 2,550 women in Mother Support Group (MSG) to engage in small-scale irrigation schemes during the dry season.-Supporting 7950 households of vulnerable groups and women to have access to financial services through locally owned and run saving and loan system.- Establishment, provision of an ongoing technical support, and integration of VSLA to all livelihoods intervention targets.-Rehabilitation of 2 livestock markets.-Facilitate community sensitization and demarcation of 50km pastoralist route, and support forage and pasture development for 9 community groups.-Facilitating establishment and strengthening of community-based disaster risk management groups and support disaster reduction plans -Support the early warning and early action system to have an anticipatory preparedness plan in three communities

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will not have direct significant impact on the environment. However, it is acknowledged that farming practices that the project seeks to support sometimes have negative impacts on the environment. For instance, in some of the localities where the project will be implemented, environmental degradation is a major concern. This is caused by cutting down of trees, poor agricultural practices and over-grazing, among other factors. The project seeks to improve agricultural techniques and practices such as soil and water management practices will be promoted. Moreover, environmental education will be mainstreamed in the training of farmers and is estimated to reach 1,950 farmers. Lack of viable alternative livelihood strategies also lead to wanton cutting down of trees for firewood and charcoal and by seeking to provide agriculture and livelihood opportunities, this project will directly lead to the cutting down of trees. Climate change adaptation practices will be prompted through different climate smart agriculture practices such as the use of certified short-cycle and drought tolerant seed varieties, tree planting and drip irrigation practices among others.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all reasonable feasible steps to protect programme participants. Concern’s approach to protection is based on key principles of analysis and risk management such as: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups identified within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create, with the imperative mandate to reduce vulnerabilities as a consequence. Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied to this intervention. Principles of protection mainstreaming will be exercised, including: meaningful access through consultation and involvement of communities about the best way to deliver services and consideration for vulnerable groups, and accountability mechanisms. Concern and partner will empower communities by training and capacity building and awareness raising about key protection concerns and risks, consulting them in decision-making process, identifying and training gender balanced project staff and volunteers. “Do No Harm” analysis will be conducted for each activity not to pose threats to beneficiaries especially women, girls and children. All project activities will be analysed and discussed with at risk groups to ensure beneficiaries are protected and services are easily accessible and safely utilized by all. Protection / (S)GBV risks faced by women and girls will be core focus of capacity building for staff and volunteers to ensure they are able to detect cases of abuse, recognize and refer to protection-mandated entities. Staff will also be oriented in order to make sure that programming does no harm, and they are able to respond safely, and appropriately, to protection concerns whenever they are reported using a confidential and survivor-centered approach to disclosure of violence. An internal mechanism for reporting complaint and ensuring that measures are in place will be promoted throughout the implementation period. Beneficiaries will be oriented about their rights regarding project objectives (e.g., access to information, access to services). Activities e.g., distribution of supplies will take place within the communities, awareness raising will be done, MPCA will involve both men and women. Lack of food in the household can sometimes lead to protection risks such as GBV and child labour and by increasing availability and access to food and livelihood opportunities, the project will indirectly be reducing these risks.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Concern WW is a Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) member and this interventionwill be implemented based on CHS principles. The targeted communities will be part ofthe entire project cycle through constant meetings, dialogues, focus group discussions(FGDs) and monitoring. At the beginning of the project, beneficiaries will be informedabout the project objectives, deliverables and their expected contribution andentitlements. They will also be part of the assessment process and theirrecommendations will be recorded and considered for the project implementation plan.All ES / NFI distribution rounds will be rolled out according to the criteria which will beagreed upon and developed with target groups within local communities. Verificationof intended beneficiaries will be conducted jointly with the afore-mentionedcommunity members. Distribution committees will be set up in each community wherethe project will intervene, in order to ensure full accountability and understanding ofselection criteria and procedures. Moreover, community members will activelyparticipate in the construction of emergency latrines and bathing units as a mean tofoster accountability and enhance their sense of ownership of the project deliverables.Volunteers and skilled labors will be selected among targeted communities, in order toincrease beneficiaries’ buy-in and acceptance of the proposed intervention. As per ES /NFI distribution rounds, selection criteria will be agreed upon and developed withpreviously appointed distribution committees who will be responsible of thedistribution of construction materials, overall coordination and supervision with thetechnical support of project staffContinuous information sharing through e.g. meetingwith field staff, monthly coordination meeting with key leaders in the targetedcommunities, and partners, will be part of the information management during theproject implementation. M&E activities include participants from the targeted activitiesto ensure appropriate feedback for improvement by way of HH visits and interviewsand community FGDs. Beneficiary targeting will be guided by needs observed on theground, humanitarian and “Do No Harm” principles, international methodologies andConcern’s specific policies targeting the poorest among affected population.Concern has an active Complaints Response Mechanisms (CRM) in place that will be utilizedfor the project. As per standard practice, a CRM table will be established during alldistributions; moreover, one female and one male focal point will be appointed foreach community in order to supervise the whole process and handle any potentialcomplaint in a confidential and culture sensitive manner. There will be various toolsapplied for getting information e.g., CRM hotlines, feedback boxes, focus groupdiscussions, one-to-one discussions to record the community/individual feedbackcoordinated by MEAL team.
