Project Summary

The majority of MSMEs selling food commodities within NGOX’s areas of operation survive on slim margins and are highly susceptible to market and currency instability, often resulting in an inability to restock on time due to a lack of financial capital. These businesses are often confronted with other issues such as the lack of appropriate infrastructure, equipment, and electricity. These challenges faced by vendors owning/managing food shops in NWS negatively impact the community they serve, resulting in reduced availability of affordable, diverse, and nutritious foods. The MSME support aims at strengthening markets in NWS through increasing vendors’ resilience to market shocks. This is done by supporting small vendors through a comprehensive approach involving the provision of business support technical assistance and cash grants. Technical assistance is offered in the form of group sessions and individual coaching on business-related topics such as record-keeping and marketing. This is complemented with the distribution of cash grants to the smallest and most vulnerable vendors in targeted markets and following the review of their application and business plan. Cash grants are used for a variety of business ideas, including small shop rehabilitation. Lastly, a small portion of the supported business owners will be offered to establish Village Saving Loans Associations (VSLA) with other owners/managers of shops selling food commodities in their market to support their financial inclusion and take loans to continue developing their business idea even after receiving NGOX’s MSME support. NGO X intends to provide support to 490 MSMEs between January 2023 and December 2023.
NGO X intends to implement Access Road rehabilitation to ensure physical access of NGO X beneficiaries to Markets supported under the food assistance and SME component, and to ensure facilitating access to basic needs and services including Food.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Risk of exacerbating real or perceived inequalities through unequal distribution of assistance, which further erodes social cohesion and generates intercommunity grievances. • Risk of excluding vulnerable communities from access to early recovery assistance by failing to factor in dynamics inhibiting their access (e.g., check points, lack of access to civil documentation etc)• Risk of exacerbating tensions between communities and local authorities or of losing credibility/ impartiality in the face of communities by failing to create mechanisms for greater community participation and responsiveness of authorities in the delivery of essential services.• Risk of female business owners/managers targeted by criminals returning from the financial service provider where cash grants are redeemed. As per SoPs, distribution points must be easily accessible to beneficiaries’ homes. Completed security assessments consider safety of roads to/from distribution sites, and safety of beneficiaries is monitored through all stages of distribution (way to and from distribution and at distribution). Where needed, LCs are engaged to enhance security measures for beneficiaries, otherwise NGOX would consider suspending assistance. To mitigate the above risks, the ERL sector is investing in increased local context sensitivity capacities of partner staff. It is also making available context analyses of prioritized areas and guidance tools to support the conduct of context-sensitive activities.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: Aid delivery and information sharing in NW Syria remain permissive and enabled.Risk: Disruption of cash flow into Syria via Iraq/Turkey, NGOX is unable to pay contracted FSP for Vendor cash grants. Mitigation: NGOX ongoing agreement with the PTT for fund transfers from Turkey to N Aleppo; NGOX has multiple bank accounts with different banks in Iraq for fund transfers from Iraq, NGOX seeks alternate banks to move money into Iraq, potential In-kind procurement of assets to distribute the grant to vendors in-kind instead of cashRisk: Fraud: Shop owners don't use cash grants for intended purposes/individuals pretend to manage/own a shop selling food commodities in order to receive the cash grant.Mitigation: Market assessments conducted prior to providing MSME support in markets; Market information confirmed by local authorities and randomly selected members of the community; Regular market monitoring and visits during the entire MSME support intervention; Market visits throughout the grants instalments; Documents/items verification and, disciplinary measures in SME agreement signed by business owners. Risk: Females at risk of exclusion due to gendered cultural and social norms/risk of harassment during their participation.Mitigation: Group female training sessions can be held; Enhance gender balance staffing for more fmales engagement in MSME activities; Integrate protection risk mitigation measures and conduct awareness sessions to reduce the risk of harassment.
