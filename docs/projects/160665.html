1 - Needs

Since August 2014, PIN has been responding to the humanitarian crisis in the Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts and is one of the largest humanitarian NGOs operating in Ukraine. Building on its 16-years activity in Ukraine, PIN established a significant presence and operational capacity in GCA and NGCA.The on-going conflict; many thousands caught in the conflict zone and large damages to housing, combined with a lack of means to cover essential costs have cultivated a significant humanitarian crisis. Food assistance remains a key need for 1.1 million people in eastern Ukraine. Poor market access, disrupted market routes and low incomes continue to threaten the ability of conflict-affected people to meet their basic food needs, with particularly vulnerable civilians living along the contact line. Approximately 558,000 are food insecure in NGCA in eastern Ukraine, including 103,000 severely food-insecure people. In GCA of the two oblasts, 13,000 people are severely food insecure and require immediate assistance to meet their food needs. The rural population is also characterized by a higher share of roots and vegetables in their food consumption pattern, which means that backyard farming continues to be a significant source of food for many. Since the humanitarian crisis is not expected to be over in the near future, various kinds of humanitarian assistance are needed.In this project, PIN proposes a Community-Based Approach to the provision of food assistance. In particular, the project will aim at ensuring access of the population to food security and basic livelihoods, including overall stabilization, recovery, vulnerability reduction and resilience. For this purpose, PIN will target one community/settlement or specific areas (cluster of a few neighboring villages) and provide an integrated solution to their humanitarian needs. The community will be mobilized and together identify their overall community goals/needs that can be addressed within the framework/priorities. As a result- PIN will design the project accordingly to assist the community priorities within the budget limitations.By applying protection mainstreaming standards through all humanitarian activities, PIN will strive to provide equal access to all populations, with special emphasis on women, children, elderly, people with alternative gender identity/orientation and disabled persons. Furthermore, women, elderly people, people of minority groups will be fully included in all stages of project design and implementation through consultations with local communities.Among possible areas of intervention are food voucher and agro voucher assistance, which will ensure the minimum household food security of vulnerable population and create opportunities for income generation. Alongside with this, the reconstruction of communal infrastructure objects that suffered war-related damages can also be an area of concern, thus making a “cash for work” intervention timely sensitive. Based on current experience of voucher implementation projects, PIN will select the beneficiaries based on previous assessment data and verification process, which includes community awareness sessions and information sharing by posters, leaflets, notices etc. During the final selection stage, the household composition, status, income, health and ability status, shelter and food needs will be consideredRegarding vulnerability criteria and beneficiary selection, generally, PIN will follow recommendations of the FSL Cluster. PIN will continue to prioritize individuals in vulnerable categories, like those with limited access to income opportunities. All activities described below are to be carried out in close coordination with the FSL Cluster, and other humanitarian organizations operating in eastern Ukraine. PIN is an active member of various coordination structures in Ukraine including several clusters, the Humanitarian Country Team.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Objective 1: Activity 1 Distribution of food vouchers to the conflict affected population to ensure their ability to cover basic needs. This modality will cover 6000 beneficiaries living along the contact line in both GCA (1000 ind.) and LNGCA (5000 ind.). A special attention will be paid to beneficiaries with disabilities to ensure their full access to the benefits of the voucher project. For this purpose, PIN will facilitate help to this category of beneficiaries whereas a group of local active community members will be involved to provide such beneficiaries with food items procured at their request and render other necessary assistance Activity 2 Cash for work to provide emergency cash injection to 700 beneficiaries in exchange for their engagement in basic works for the benefit of the local community. This activity will involve the work-age beneficiaries from vulnerable groups, falling out of standard humanitarian aid criteria. Selected beneficiaries will be organized in work teams and work on restoring the communal infrastructure objects that are of priority for the local communityObjective 2:Activity 1: Provision/rehabilitation or construction of agricultural assets. Provision of agricultural resources through the mechanism of vouchers for agricultural tools for 200 HHs (400 ind.) in GCA, and for 200 HHs (400 ind.) in NGCA. This intervention will cover the most vulnerable people who have the basic assets for home vegetable growing. The activities will be carried out through local suppliers selected by PIN with consideration of their transport accessibility for beneficiaries. The beneficiary selection process will be carried out with the involvement of active members of local communities, and will consider the composition and status of the family, as well as health status of the beneficiary.Activity 2: Agriculture-related trainings as well as larger events. To increase knowledge and skills on the use of drip irrigation equipment, PIN will conduct agricultural trainings for representatives of 200 vulnerable households (400 ind.) engaged in subsistence farming, who live in rural areasActivity 3: To increase economic efficiency and ability of small farmers to meet market needs, 200 HHs of small farmers (400 ind.) will receive technical expertise, capacity building and material support so that they could increase their families' incomes through collective actions. Assistance in conducting collective actions will be provided to farmers living along the contact line in the settlements of Donetsk region and Luhansk region.Objective 3.Activity 1: Self-employment or enterprise-level business grants. In order to improve the access of the affected population to popular services, PIN will hold a competition and support grants of 50 ind., (self-employed entrepreneurs/microenterprises)When selecting applications for winners, the PIN will consider the potential of the application (availability of assets, experience of the applicant), the importance of such a business in providing the community with products and services for a healthy and productive life of the population.Activity 2: Professional and one-time trainings on livelihood support. To increase knowledge and business skills, PIN will conduct short-term trainings for 350 women and youth living in the most affected areas of Donetsk and Lugansk region and interested in developing their business. The capacity building of these entrepreneurs will be supported by short-term mentoring and mentoring, which will continue for 6 months.Activity 3: Vocational Trainings (extensive professional trainings etc.) as well as one-off type of livelihood-related trainings on professional reorientation for 600 graduates of agricultural institutions in Donetsk region. This activity is aimed at increasing the ability of graduates to find the most paid work to meet the needs of small enterprises demanded by their employees, whose skills will be used in value chains.

3 - Indicators & targets

Output 1: # of vulnerable individuals receiving food vouchers (6000 ind.)Output 2: # of individuals participating in the cash for work scheme (700 ind.)Output 3: # of households benefiting from agricultural support (400HHs (800 ind.) attending agricultural training 200 HHs (400 ind.) participating in collective activities 200 HHs (400ind.)Output 4: # of individuals benefiting from livelihood and/or income generation support (50 ind. who have received grants for self-employment and micro-entrepreneurship), 350 ind. attending seminars and mentoring for self-employment).Output 5: # of individuals benefiting from livelihood and/or income generation support 600 ind.Outcome 1: % of individuals increasing Food Consumption (FCS) (80%)Outcome 2: % of rural households increasing income (80%)Outcome 3: % of supported microenterprises increase income (70%)Outcome 4: % of graduates trained with more job placement ability (70%) Monitoring and evaluation: Of all the activities PIN undertakes, community monitoring and evaluation are the most essential, since they identify shortcomings and provide for transparency and accountability. Monitoring and evaluation also refer back to participatory assessments for determining what is or is not working. Community-based monitoring focuses on the implementation of activities to ensure that they are running smoothly and helping to achieve immediate objectives.PIN M&E teams will ensure the applicability, accuracy and efficiency of the activities implemented over the course of the project and in the post intervention/post distribution period. The following M&E processes are sought:Beneficiary Verification: Beneficiaries will be cross-referenced with other humanitarian actors to avoid any risk of overlap. Monitoring: Regular monitoring activities to be conducted by both management and M&E staff with random beneficiary interviews. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM), baselines and endline CSI: M&E Units of PIN will carry out PDM surveys reviewing beneficiary experiences on the whole aid process.Accountability and Complaints Mechanism: Pre-distribution information sessions with beneficiaries will be organized in particular on how to use the dedicated hotline and e-mail address that have been established by PIN. A complaints database will be maintained, where all complaints will be categorized on the basis of urgency, relevance and sensitivity/seriousness.
