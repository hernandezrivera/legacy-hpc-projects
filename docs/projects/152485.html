Project Summary

This proposed project will address short- to-midterm WASH concerns of conflict and disease outbreak affected populations by providing integrated WASH services to vulnerable populations. This will improve water supply, sanitation and hygiene behaviour for the vulnerable IDPs and host families and reduce waterborne diseases. The project therefore intends to contribute to the reduction of avoidable mortality and morbidity through the provision of lifesaving integrated WASH services to vulnerable populations, especially women, girls, men and boys affected by armed- conflict and the host communities through a flexible, responsive and synergistic approach. This project will be targeting 39,574 individuals (Male: 4,630, Female: 5,120, Boys: 4,870 and Girls: 5,380) 20,000 in Tonj East, and (Male 3,579, Female 8,495, Boys 3,420 and Girls 4,080) total 39,574 in Yirol East and Yirol West.
Prior to project implementation, CCOSS will conduct, focus group discussions (FGD) with women, men, boys and girls at targeted community settlements will be conducted to get feedback on design, implementation, and monitoring of sanitation facilities, ensuring safety and mitigation of Protection / GBV risks, especially for females. These FGDs will also be utilized to trigger community action towards ending open defecation. A total of 20 two stance block latrines (40 latrines) will be constructed at key health facilities, market places, and crowded settlement areas. CCOSS will also rehabilitate non-functioning boreholes
The project will carry out clean- up campaigns to improve environmental sanitation at schools, health facilities, market centres and settlement areas since these are key areas where people gather and transmission of infectious diseases such as cholera is at a heightened risk. CCOSS shall conduct cholera awareness and prevention campaigns each month of implementation to improve hygiene practices and health-seeking behaviour for WASH-related infectious diseases. The awareness shall also provide knowledge on appropriate behaviours to prevent and mitigate WASH related diseases and encourage good hygiene practices.
CCOSS will train 40 community hygiene promoters (60% of them women) and 10 hygiene leaders on hygiene awareness and promotion. These hygiene promoters will be identified and recruited through participatory approach with the community members playing a central role. CCOSS will also assess the target population and identify at least 1618 most vulnerable households in Yirol East & Yirol West (Men: 2047, Women: 2741, Boys: 2397, Girl: 2523 and total 9,708 individuals) for distribution of WASH non-food items (NFIs) such as jerry cans, buckets, PUR tabs and soap. A needs assessment will be conducted at the beginning of the project implementation phase.

Needs

Yirol East County in Lakes State has also been experiencing an influx of IDPs since 2013. It is believed that political stability in Yirol East is said to have contributed significantly in influencing the decisions of the IDPs to settle in the area. In May 2018, more than 28574 individuals (4618 HHs) were displaced from the Pakam community in Rumbek North due to the government led disarmament program. The government forces are alleged to have burnt down all the houses and destroyed their household properties which forced the Pakam community to leave their area and relocate for permanent resettlement in Yirol East. Already, the State Government of Eastern Lakes has allocated the displaced persons land for settlement in Jiar, where they have settled. CCOSS through funding from RRF responded to the area targeting 1500 HHs (9000 individuals) with response in distributing WASH NFIs and S/NFIs to the vulnerable population. The WASH NFIs distributed included water buckets, Jerri cans, PUR sachets, soap and filter cloth. CCOSS also trained 55 Hygiene promoters who worked in the area for three months (mid July to Mid October 2018), by conducting hygiene promotion activities at community and house-to house level. However, with the completion of the project in mid October 2018, there still exist gaps in Yirol East that require to be addressed. These gaps include the following;• Lack of sanitation facilities such as pit latrines has been a bigger challenge in the camp. WASH interventions cannot be successful in locations where there are no pit latrines. Though the IDPs were involved in weekly clean up exercise, open defecation remains a bigger challenge. The IDPs don't have a single pit latrine in the camps.• There is only one borehole available for both IDPs and host communities. This has often resulted in tensions arising especially from host communities who feel that the IDPs are benefiting from WASH and S/NFIs interventions yet they are also putting pressure on existing facilities. The water results from the only borehole had the following results pH – 6.8 mg/l and Chlorine residual – 0.3 mg/l. There is need to drill some boreholes• There is no WASH partner responding to these IDPs with the exit of CCOSS. Without a continuous response, many WASH related challenges are likely to arise, especially issues to do with Acute Watery Diarrhoea and Cholera, due to lack of sanitation facilities and dirty water fetched directly from stagnant ponds. Tonj (North, East and South) broadly faces myriad of WASH related challenges emanating from accessibility challenges by partners, influx of IDPs as a result of forced displacement due to cattle raids from neighbouring Cuibet that spills over the Warrap State boarder into Manyangok Payam. Majority of Tonj residents drink water from unprotected sources, almost no existence of pit latrines thus open defecation, while Cholera and AWD are a main health challenge. Open defecation is a common practice of disposal of human excreta and the community knowledge on good hygiene practice is very low. This situation has exposed the populations to high level risk of vulnerability to water related illnesses. During rainy season Tonj is one of the locations in Warrap that always experience heavy floods, which always displace thousands of populations from their homes and this also exposes the populations to high level of vulnerability of contracting water related sicknesses, especially Cholera. Activities

a) Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations b) Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases.c) Enable vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and Hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate. sanitation services d) Provide a timely WASH minimum package of interventions on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the specificities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community e) Set-up of WASH services, training of health workers and pupils as ambassadors for appropriate hygiene practices within hand washing with soap at critical times at their household level

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

CCOSS has been working in Yirol for five years, and has been interacting and consulting with communities in many ways. CCOSS has established a working relationship with the local authorities in Yirol East and Yirol West, with whom project activities in the area must be reported to. CCOSS also works closely with the community leaders, and informs them of project plans throughout the implementation phase. In Tonj, CCOSS is currently implementing a WASH project in Tonj East, working with key gatekeepers in the community. For effective feedback, CCOSS works interacts and receives feedback from the community members directly through project planning and through feed-back mechanisms. Our staff regularly interacts and consults with the community members on a day to day basis. Prior to commencement of WASH interventions, community participation in the planning and implementation of this intervention will be sought . CCOSS will ensure its programme deliveryaccording to CHF and cluster guidelines. CCOSS always includes beneficiaries in project development through different surveys (with segregated data) and focused group discussions. CCOSS will have regular meetings with all stakeholders to share programme related information and also to respond to queries and complaints raised by beneficiaries.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Complaint /feedback mechanism will be agreed upon and established prior to commencement of project. Monitoring of the complaints and remedial action will be documented during the project implementation cycle. This will be done with the goal of promoting community engagement and accountability. The community participation in the planning implementation and monitoring of the hygiene interventions will also be solicited. For instance, the community will be involved in the identification and selection of hygiene promoters, water management committees among others. Also, after the completion of distribution of WASH NFIs, a monitoring team will be sent to visit selected samples of beneficiaries to confirm the actual quantity received and the quality of the relief commodities, as well as to collect further feedback from beneficiaries. Once complaints are received, CCOSS will look for the most appropriate way to respond to the complaints
