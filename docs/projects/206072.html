Project Summary

The "Improving access to safe, protective, and inclusive education services for emergency-affected children and youth (3-17 years old) in South Sudan" project will run for 12 months from 1st January 2024 to 31st December 2024 and will contribute to the Education Cluster strategic objectives one (1), two (2) and three (3). The project will increase access to safe, protective, and inclusive education in line with the first objective. Under the third objective, the project will strengthen the coordination and crisis management capacity of the authorities and communities (education authorities, SMCs, and PTAs) to promote efficient, accountable, and timely education in emergencies response . The geographical locations of the project are aligned to the Education Cluster priorities 1 and 2 counties with severity levels 4 and 3.
The project will focus on provision of safe and protective quality education services to emergency-affected boys and girls aged 3-17 years in South Sudan. At least 140,510 children, adolescents and youth (40% girls) aged 3 - 17 years will have access to quality education services. The project will establish 190 temporary learning spaces; rehabilitate 100 temporary learning spaces; establish 380 WASH facilities for 40,000 children (40% girls); rehabilitate WASH facilities for 80,000 children (40% girls); train 1,194 teachers (30% females) on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-centered methodologies; train 211 community education committees/PTA members and local education officials (30% females) on school management, PSS and DRR; and organize 100 school campaigns for back-to-learning.
The project will target a total of 141,915 emergency affected boys, girls, and youth, teachers, and PTA members in the priority 1 and 2 counties of the South Sudan Education Cluster Strategy 2023 - 2025. The project will ensure access to safe and protective quality education services for all children (boys and girls), particularly the most vulnerable, affected by emergencies including children with disabilities. Without the provision of education services, the future of children (boys and girls) in South Sudan is at stake.

1. Needs

The context of South Sudan is characterized by conflict, natural disasters, a high and growing proportion of the school-age population, and socioeconomic vulnerability. The education system, in particular, faces important challenges in providing access to safe, protective, and inclusive quality education for boys, girls, and youth in the country. Multiple crisis drivers continue to have a negative impact on education services, disrupting access to education and learning (South Sudan Education Cluster Strategy 2023 - 2025). According to the Annual Education Census (2021), the Gender Parity Index remains low at 0.95 for pre-primary, 0.89 for primary, and 0.77 for secondary level. The prevalent gender inequities worsen as children move from lower to higher levels of education. Male learners are the majority at all levels of education. Due to the unprecedented levels of flooding experienced in the country, the Global Climate Risk Index recorded South Sudan as one of the ten countries most affected by climate change globally in 2019 ​(Eckstein, 2021)​. In some counties, the flood water continued affecting the population to-date. Over one million people were reportedly affected by flooding in 36 counties across South Sudan in 2022 ​(OCHA, 2022)​. The impact of flooding on education delivery, not only restricts educational access in its resultant constrictions on mobility but it also physically damaged schools, with 877 schools reportedly destroyed by flooding in 2022, representing an increase by 20 from 2021 ​(OCHA, 2021)​. Limited access to education for children and youth with disabilities remains a challenge. According to the Annual Education Census (2021), only 29,098 children with different types of disabilities were enrolled in schools, which represents about 1.25 per cent of the total enrolment, yet the general education system in South Sudan is not adequately prepared to cater to their needs. Problems persist with accessibility, with many classrooms being barely accessible for physically impaired children. Once they are in the classroom, large class sizes and inconsistent teaching methodologies make learning difficult for those with disabilities to cope with their peers. The lack of adaptive materials and assistive devices, teachers with inclusive education knowledge and skills hinders children and adolescents with disabilities to receive learning that helps them to achieve their potential. A high number of internally displaced people (IDPs) and returnees in the formal education system, estimated at 620,000 school-aged children were forced to move from their communities because of hazards. (Internal Displacement Monitoring Centre, 2022), and the Annual Education Census (2021) reported 87,000 IDP children enrolled in the formal education system. A total of 2.8 million in South Sudan are out-of-school, representing 58.5 per cent of the population from 3 to 17 years (MoGEI OOSC Mapping, 2020). 53 per cent of the out-of-school children (OOSC) are in Jonglei, Eastern Equatoria, and Upper Nile within their school-age population, while the lowest shares are in Western and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. Another major challenge is the inadequate Government funding for Education. For example, domestic financing of education is persistently low. For the 2020-2021 national budget, education was allocated 11 per cent from 5 per cent in the 2019/2020 budget. However, the demand for education in South Sudan is very high and the needs are diverse, coupled with the high cost of delivering services. This negatively affects the quality of education services, and many children are excluded from the system. Critical services such a teacher enumeration, provision of learning spaces, and learning materials are highly compromised because of the limited financing.

2. Project activities

To address the main educational challenges described in the needs analysis and focus on the Education Cluster priorities identified in the strategy, the project will carry out the following activities:1. Establish 190 learning spaces in line with INEE's Minimum Standards.2.Rehabilitate 100 learning spaces in line with INEE's Minimum Standards.3. Establish 280 gender sensitive latrines in line with INEE and sphere standards.4. Rehabilitate 100 gender sensitive latrines in line with INEE and sphere standards.5. Mobilize children and youth (3-17 years) to enroll in pre-primary, primary, and secondary education. 6. Distribute dignity kits to adolescent girls in schools.7. Conduct awareness raising on key issues through Back-to-Learning campaigns.8. Distribute assistive devices to children with disabilities in schools.9. Distribute teaching and learning materials to children and teachers.10.Train 1,194 teachers on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centred methodologies.11. Conduct training for 211 SMC/PTA members and education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR).
