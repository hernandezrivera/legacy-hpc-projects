Project Summary

Up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps, high acute malnutrition, or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3)1 or worse outcomes through December 2019. Moreover, an estimated one million children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through mid-2020. Sustained and large-scale humanitarian assistance, which is planned and likely through September, is currently preventing more severe outcomes in many areas.
Current high levels of food insecurity are driven by drought conditions from late 2018 through mid-May 2019, exacerbated by below-average and erratically distributed rainfall during the 2019 Gu season (April-June), which began late and finished early. Many households are still recovering from the severe 2016/2017 drought or have been affected by conflict, and 2.6 million people remain displaced. Across Somalia, current estimates indicate that 1 in 10 children under the age of five is acutely malnourished with the median National GAM of 13.8 percent at Serious level, translating to 1.08M boys and girls of which 178,000 are affected by life threatening severely malnutrition while 830,000 are moderately malnourished. A further 832,000 pregnant and lactating women are estimated to be in need of nutrition assistance. Poor nutrition conditions amongst both these population groups are the result of both shock and underlying factors, with conflict, political instability, displacement and changing climate conditions continuing to put immense pressure on access to basic services and intensifying exposure to nutritional deprivations. This includes sub-optimal access to health services, poor childcare practices, seasonal food insecurity, limited access to potable water, and poor hygiene and sanitation practices.
In light of the persistent high rates of severe acute malnutrition (SAM), there is need to continue delivery of lifesaving nutrition interventions targeting in priority IDP camps and hard to reach communities. Mogadishu remains a priority since it is hosting larger of IDPs as well as, Lower Shabelle, Galgaduud and South Mudug regions where many pastoral communities lost their livelihoods and are vulnerable state.
The project targets a total of 49,024 children 6-59 months with lifesaving nutrition interventions; 18,228 severely malnourished children with appropriate treatment facilities in 15 Mobile, 12 Static OTP facilities with those manifesting complicated SAM admitted for treatment in either the Eldere or Harardere district stabilization centers (2) and 30,796 pregnant and lactating women.
In districts where CISP is the primary or the secondary IP, the intervention foreseen will be implemented using mobile OTPs facilities linked to existing static health facilities for better coverage. CISP is working with Education and Protection programmes in Lower Shabelle in the proposed areas.CISP will coordinate with the nutrition cluster to ensure that there is no duplication while ensuring maximum impact of interventions.

Mitigation Measures

The risk involved lack of gender equity and social inclusion: This will be mitigated CISP will prevent exclusion and inequality by ensuring equal participation of all people, including individuals at risk of exclusion and ensure they reach their full potential, both in terms of its economy and its level of governance. Social Inclusion will be promoted through:•When hiring incentive staff equal opportunities will be given to all irrespective of their social background, gender and disability • Equal representation in the Community Health Committees (CHC) and Community Development Committees (CDC0. • Training Health staff on do no harm The proposed project will equally target women, boys and girls, particularly those from vulnerable families affected by ill health and drought whose vulnerabilities are exacerbated by the worsening drought, water scarcity and displacement into IDP settlements where children live in extremely Precarious conditions with only limited community support. This project will support the MOH to identify the staff with optimum qualifications and experiences taking into account the different capacities and needs of men and women. As well as assigning equal opportunities, tasks and responsibilities to both men and women. Community Based workers selection and trainings will also consider gender sensitivity including both men and women. All this will ensure gender issues and equity is addressed. –CISP will prioritize safety &dignity, and avoid causing and any unintended negative effects of its intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and Psycho-social risks. CISP will ensure people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services.

Please provide further explanation

The community resilience approach will be overlaid with basic service provision of key health and nutrition, basic community-based public health services for prevention, promotion and referral and will be done by trained community based workers. The community based strategy will be channeled through service committees such as community development committees (CDCs). Community consultation and action planning will support the delivery of community based public health and nutrition. Linked with local governance structure, we will work with district and regional health authorities on delivering of prevention and promotion services through training and supportive supervision for stewarding community nutrition and public health interventions.
