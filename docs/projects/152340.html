Project Summary


Ukraine NGO Forum in accordance with the Sphere Project Mini mum Standards will aim to ensure that “humanitarian response is planned and implemented with the relevant authorities, humanitarian agencies and civil society organizations engaged in impartial humanitarian action, working together for maximum efficiency, coverage and effectiveness.”
Specific objectives:
• Coordination: To develop and strengthen effective mechanisms for active membership participation, cooperation, and NGO community representation [The Ukraine NGO Forum exists to complement existing coordination mechanisms and does not seek to either compete with or replace them.]
• Internal & External Information Sharing: To develop and enhance an accessible structure and encourage Ukraine NGO Forum members to share relevant information on a regular basis. To maintain a communication strategy highlighting the work of Ukraine NGO Forum members to increase the influence of and respect for the NGO community.
• Advocacy: To develop a strong, evidence-based advocacy voice for its membership and the NGO community working on behalf of IDPs and those affected by conflict, at both the national and international level.
• Capacity Building: To build the capacity of the members and NGO community to sustain and develop their activities in the future, and make them more trusted, effective, accountable, and result-oriented by distinguishing, assessing, and monitoring capacity needs for the NGO community.

Needs

According to the HRP (2018), keeping international attention on the humanitarian situation in Ukraine will likely be challenging, due to “mega emergencies” and growing humanitarian needs around the world. International support is uncertain and inconsistent, with donor funding for humanitarian programs drawing down, while recovery and development funding is still insufficient. In 2017, UNOCHA has received only 11.5% of the funds it needs to run its operation in Ukraine (HRP 2018). Today, almost one in 10 Ukrainians are suffering the direct and cumulative effects of this protracted crisis. Over the months and years since the crisis began, the Ukrainian government has worked with the international humanitarian community to adapt its systems and legislation, though by April 2017 a draft law on humanitarian assistance had been sitting in parliament for over 18 months, and legislative challenges had still not been fully addressed. [HPG Working Paper, “Humanitarian Access and local organizations in Ukraine”, September 2017]. Due to Unified State Register of Enterprises and Organizations of Ukraine of the State Statistic Service of Ukraine, there are 70 321 NGOs working in Ukraine (without taking into consideration temporary occupied territories of Autonomic Republic of Crimea and NGCA of Donbass). The bulk of the early response came from new groups composed of active citizens, mostly from the business sector. According to interviews for this study, in 2014 1,500 new NGOs were registered under the national charity law, and another 1,100 were registered in 2015. Often emerging out of the EuroMaidan demonstrations, these groups were generally loosely organized, and belonged to large networks of volunteers. Although their size and organizational structure varied, most comprised small cells of five to 20 volunteers linked to larger networks that could reach several hundred people. Some started informally before formalizing their structure. However, as the conflict became protracted, individual donations started drying up and volunteer groups struggled to find the funds they needed to respond. As a result, volunteer groups increasingly relied on institutional funding, usually through formal partnerships with international humanitarian actors. Many volunteers who could no longer support themselves without paid work joined international organisations as salaried staff. [HPG Working Paper, “Humanitarian Access and local organizations in Ukraine”, September 2017] Previous NGO Forum assessment of 30 NGOs [Conducted in August-Septembers 2017] operating at the east of Ukraine has shown that larger number of CSOs lack understanding of what organizational growth is and why it is important. Major challenges that organizations faced were: lack of understanding on how to manage the organization (shortlisted CSOs did not have any experience in strategic and operational planning), lack of beneficiaries involvement into a needs assessments and program activities development, as well as, absence of any monitoring & evaluation. As well, Ukraine NGO Forum in partnership with European Research Association have conducted research of NGOs’ capacity needs (finalized in Spring 2018) in which participated 152 heads of NGOs and authorities, and the following was identified:• NGO representatives lack professionalism and understanding of the real problems of the city or the community.• NGOs work chaotically• NGOs work with donors and do not know how to work with the authorities.• It is necessary to set up NGO reporting.• Only service NGOs work while there are many registered, so there are many "on paper" NGOs.• Some NGOs suffer from overestimating self-esteem and cannot solve problems.• The activities of some NGOs are politicized.

Activities & outcomes

The Ukraine NGO Forum will remain committed to enhancing and strengthening current efforts. Considering the fluid context in Ukraine, it will strive to continuously adjust its efforts to maintain relevance. Special efforts will be made in recognition that humanitarian, development, and peacebuilding efforts are complementary and need to reinforce each other to best respond to the crisis in Ukraine, including to enhance cooperation amongst response agencies.To be effective, the Ukraine NGO Forum must focus on activities which are based on priority needs and gaps in the operating context. The Ukraine NGO Forum will focus on the five aforementioned activity areas: advocacy, internal and external information sharing, coordination, capacity building, and organisational capacity and sustainability.The Ukraine NGO Forum membership has identified main strategic objectives:Coordination: Facilitating coordination meetings, working groups & Ukraine NGO Forums participation in existing coordination mechanismsThe Forum is committed to working with existing coordination mechanisms in Ukraine. NGO Forum’s Director plays an important role by representing NGO Forum members in front of Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), of which NGO Forum is the constant member and is engaged in various Working Groups. The Director is also co-chairing the Humanitarian Communications Sub-Group (HCSG). The Forum has an important role to play in minimizing gaps and duplication in humanitarian activities, including support and management functions performed. Internal & External Information Sharing: Development of the NGO Forum information channels into integrated information management systemUkraine NGO Forum has several information management platforms targeting various stakeholders: Ukraine NGO Forum web-site www.ngoforum.org.ua (approx. 3,500 unique visits per month), Ukraine NGO Forum mapping portal www.ngomap.org.ua (tool for NGOs coordination, cooperation and beneficiaries referral), Facebook group with 6,314 followers, Twitter account, YouTube channel, newsletters. In addition, NGO Forum plans further interconnect all this information channels into one effectively working system, which is properly integrated into existing humanitarian information management systems.Advocacy: Analysis of organisations and the environment in Ukraine informs that, while there are organisations effectively and efficiently advocating on a broad national and international level within the Ukrainian context, the Ukraine NGO Forum can nevertheless fill a highly important role, through its multi-faceted membership base (i.e. national-international, humanitarian-development-peacebuilding organisations). Advocacy strategies will be developed responding to the contextual challenges, target institutions, policy problems, and actors as defined by Forum members. The Ukraine NGO Forum will continue to engage with authorities on the international, national, and regional levels.Capacity Building: Emergency management/response capacity building program for national NGOsConsidering current humanitarian architecture transformation and decrease of donors’ funding connected with protracted nature of the conflict, NGO Forum sees capacity development and national NGO engagement in coordination mechanisms as a high priority to effectively plan, coordinate and implement emergency response. To address future emergency needs with readily available resources, increase the capacity of national NGOs and increase the coordination of national NGOs with Clusters and INGOs, NGO Forum plans to organize series of trainings related to work in emergency context. The trainings aim to increase capacity of national NGOs (especially the ones located in Eastern Ukraine).

Indicators & targets

Monitoring and evaluation (M&E) activities will engage member organisations and donors with better means to evaluate and learn from the work carried out by the Ukraine NGO Forum. M&E efforts will focus on overall results through improving programme delivery, planning, and allocation of resources, and will demonstrate results and accountability to members and key stakeholders. The Ukraine NGO Forum will be able to document what it does, why it does it, follow up on successes, and enable learning and informed decision-making. Regular, structured member feedback will be a key tool for further improvement. The Ukraine NGO Forum will adopt a systemic approach of regular evaluation of all activities to ensure services are relevant and high quality.
