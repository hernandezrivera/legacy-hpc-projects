Project Summary

For over 8 years now specifically from November 2016, the North-West and South-West Regions of Cameroon have been facing crises which have left many more vulnerable especially women, girls and children. The conflict which is on-going has resulted in many deaths, significant population displacement, slow flow in economic activity, significant decrease in school enrolment, increased incidents of gender-based violence (presently unreported), drug use, prostitution, rape, early pregnancy, unsafe delivery amongst others. The conflict situation has subjected tens of thousands of persons across the North West Region to various forms of suffering, violence and living in degrading sub-human conditions. The situation in this Region is further compounded by the patriarchal nature of communities where women and girls are subjected to cultural norms and values that violate their rights and dignity.
Therefore the main purpose of this project will be to identify vulnerable women and girls, men and boys facing GBV and providing them with informative interventions that will enable them build resilience and become more self-assertive while minimizing barriers to reporting. According to UNFPA reports of April 2024, intimate partner violence remains the most reported/occurrence. This therefore can be attributed to the reason why women, girls, men and boys shy away from reporting cases of GBV in the region. There is the noting of partners not wanting to disclose their spouses and this gives the impression that little GBV is happening where as it is not the case.
The need to address this GBV concern is in line with SDG 5 (to achieve Gender Equality and empower all women and girls). This will make for a more equitable society where the rights of everyone as a person are shared and valued.
The Objectives for these programme areas are based on:
- Prevention: Key Frontline actors are engaged in actions that contribute to GBV prevention and response.
- Response: Enhance quality service delivery and response to survivors of GBV.
The above are done through:
- The organization of community based outreach activities specifically targeted towards encouraging survivors to report.
- Conducting Safety Audits
- Sensitization Campaigns
- Provision of timely and holistic GBV/SRH including mental health related services to GBV survivors and other vulnerable persons including those with disabilities through Women and Girls Safe Spaces.
- Contribution in the reduction of GBV incidents among women and girls including those with disabilities that access GBV/SRH support services within respected communities.
- Increasing skill development and economic empowerment opportunities for youths and GBV survivors in 7 Division of the NWR by 2025.
Strategies envisioned:
- Capacity building for GBV prevention and response with service providers.
- Engaging men and boys, women led organizations and adolescent girls in the fight against GBV.
- Conducting of safety audits for GBV risk mitigation within targeted communities
- Awareness raising campaigns through sensitization and outreach activities for GBV/SRH and mental health prevention and response.
For response;
Set up and support Women and Girls Safe Spaces that provide psychosocial support services and respond to the needs of GBV survivors, most vulnerable women and girls including those with disabilities.
Carryout capacity building for Case managers, health workers, community mobilizes, mental health specialists and community stakeholders:
- To conduct effective referral services.
- To provide multi-sector support for GBV survivors and most vulnerable persons
- To carryout mobile clinic activities
- To provide airtime for remote GBV service delivery using the innovative Alert GBV tool.
- Distribute dignity kits during information sessions on GBV services to reduce GBV risk and promote safety.
- Provide Income generating activities support to GBV survivors

1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The 2024 MSNA reported widespread GBV incidents across the North West region, particularly in Mezam, Ngoketunjia, Momo, and Menchum divisions. Key findings include:Prevalence Rates: Over 703,000 people in the region require GBV services, with sexual violence, intimate partner violence (IPV), and forced marriages being the most common forms reported.Vulnerable Groups: Women and girls, especially those living in host communities face heightened risks. Persons with disabilities also experience increased vulnerabilities. Psychosocial and Mental Health NeedsSurvivors of GBV exhibit high levels of psychological trauma, depression, and anxiety, compounded by stigma and cultural taboos surrounding mental health.Access to mental health services is severely limited, with only 20% of health facilities offering basic psychosocial support.Barriers to ReportingFear of retaliation, social stigma, and mistrust of law enforcement discourage survivors from reporting GBV incidents.Many health facilities lack the resources to provide critical services such as post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) or emergency contraception.Education and Awareness DeficitsHealthcare System ChallengesOver 50% of health facilities in the North West region are non-functional, severely impacting service delivery.Health workers often lack the training and tools to provide survivor-centered GBV care.Activities to Address Assessed Needs Psychosocial and Mental Health Support1. Establish Safe Spaces:Create dedicated safe spaces in each division for women and girls to access psychosocial support, group counseling, and recreational activities.Equip these spaces with trained staff, including female counselors and caseworkers.2. Expand Mobile Mental Health Units:Deploy mobile teams to provide trauma counseling, mental health screenings, and follow-up care in hard-to-reach areas.3. Capacity Building for Healthcare Workers:Train healthcare providers, social workers, and counselors in trauma-informed care and mental health support.Provide refresher courses to ensure adherence to GBV response guidelines. Strengthening Reporting Mechanisms1. Introduce Confidential Reporting Systems:Set up toll-free hotlines and digital platforms for anonymous GBV reporting. 3. Community-Based Reporting Structures:Train community focal points to identify GBV cases and refer survivors to appropriate services.Conduct community dialogues to reduce stigma around reporting GBV. 2. Deploy Mobile Clinics:Bring integrated GBV/SRH services to remote and underserved areas through mobile health units.Offer family planning, antenatal care, and mental health services alongside GBV care. Community Awareness and Behavioral Change1. Conduct Awareness Campaigns:Use radio, social media, and community theater to raise awareness about GBV, its consequences, and available services.2. Implement School-Based Programs:Train teachers to identify and respond to GBV cases within school settings.e. Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E)1. Strengthen Data Collection:Develop a harmonized GBV data collection system to monitor trends and service delivery gaps.Ensure data confidentiality and ethical standards in all reporting mechanisms.2. Community Feedback Mechanisms:Establish feedback systems, such as suggestion boxes and community forums, to adapt services based on survivor needs.3. Regular Assessments:Conduct safety auditsConduct quarterly evaluations to measure the impact of interventions and identify areas for improvement.
