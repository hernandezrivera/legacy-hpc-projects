Project Summary

Mercy-USA for Aid and Development (Mercy-USA) aims to improve the quality of life and protect vulnerable communities from morbidity and mortality associated with water-related diseases through water, sanitation and hygiene activities. This 12 month WASH project will target vulnerable people affected by drought, floods and conflict in Hiiraan, Galgadud, and Mudug regions by improving access to safe water and sanitation facilities and facilitating the uptake of acceptable hygiene practices. The project activities are in line with relevant strategic plans, including the Somalia National Development Plan, UN Strategic Framework and WASH Cluster priorities and guidance.
The target populations in Hiraan, Galgadud and Mudug regions have been facing simultaneously multiple barriers and inequalities in accessing WASH services. These barriers include, geographical barriers, where some of the target communities live in rural settings, physical barriers, where persons with disabilities, children, older persons, pregnant women and others vulnerable groups are unable to access WASH services due to inappropriate design, and economic barriers.
Lack of access to safe water and sanitation increases the prevalence of preventable, water-borne diseases such as diarrhoea and dysentery, caused mainly by ingesting unclean water and contaminated food due to poor hygiene and poor human waste disposal.
Mercy-USA thus seeks to increase access to sustainable and inclusive WASH services for disadvantaged individuals and groups, including IDPs, people living in rural and remote areas, as well as people with disabilities and increase their resilience to emergencies, including current COVID-19 pandemic.
Mercy-USA's proposed inclusive WASH response is immediate and designed to control the spread of water-borne diseases and preserve the health of the target populations, focusing on the severely affected and hard-to-reach. Proposed interventions include the provision of free, safe and sustainable drinking water, routine chlorination of existing water sources, testing of communal water sources for chemical and microbiological hazards by use of a portable water testing kit, construction of gender-segregated VIP latrines for underserved public primary schools and public health facilities and hygiene promotion. Mercy-USA will directly implement project activities.
Mercy-USA will ensure stakeholder involvement in the implementation of project activities by first carrying out a stakeholder analysis to identify who has an interest or influence over WASH outcomes, at regional level, district level, institutional levels, community level, and private sector level. Mercy-USA will seek to incorporate their needs and ideas to implement inclusive, sustainable and climate-resilient WASH activities. Mercy-USA will ensure synergy with other Clusters to ensure adequate coordination of the activities through regular coordination with Health, Education, Shelter/NFIs, Protection, Nutrition, CWG and FSL Clusters to add value to the overall response through integrated response.

Shallow well designs will be flood-resistant, and all wells will be equipped with hand pumps and 1 animal watering trough per well. • Rehabilitation of 3 boreholes, including conversion of inefficient generator-powered boreholes to hybrid models by installing solar-powered pumping systems, construction of elevated tanks, piping extensions of water sources and construction of water kiosks in IDP settlements and primary schools in areas with high rates of malnutrition and AWD to improve access to safe drinking water. • Shallow well chlorination campaigns targeting 60 shallow wells.• Construct rainwater harvesting systems in 15 primary schools.• Training of 48 Water Management Committees (WMC) to ensure that targeted water sources (30 shallow wells, 3 boreholes and 15 rainwater harvesting systems) remain safe, effective and operational during and after disasters to provide safe and free drinking water for vulnerable IDPs. • Provision of water through water voucher system during periods of water shortage targeting 1,500 HHs in IDP camps for 3 months.• Provision of safe water through water tracking during emergencies to newly displaced IDPs - targeting 1,000 HHs for 1 month. 2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The voucher system allows beneficiaries to choose from a list of WASH items approved by the WASH Cluster. This increased autonomy enhances the dignity of beneficiaries and enables them to select items in line with household needs. Moreover, Mercy-USA favors the voucher transfer modality as it: • enhances beneficiary dignity: making choices for themselves empowers the beneficiaries and can help restore a sense of dignity that is often disrupted by forced displacement as HHs able to prioritize based on HH needs; • supports local markets through cash injection for small/medium size vendors and therefore create multiplier effects for the benefits of the humanitarian intervention; • increases the cost-effectiveness of Mercy-USA's response.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Through Mercy-USA's field offices, Mercy-USA has carried out an assessment of the local market and determined the proposed WASH items are available through vendors in the markets which remain functional and accessible. Mercy-USA will continuously conduct market surveys in the target areas to asses markets and vendors` capacities and to finalize the list of suppliers where the beneficiaries could redeem the vouchers. Mercy-USA will support markets through providing technical support or training, small grants/loans to WASH service providers and generating demand for the products/services among consumers.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

• Mercy-USA will carry out mapping exercises on a semi-annual basis to understand marginalized communities in the areas of operation and their needs. • Mercy-USA will ensure that community selection committees comprise at least 50% representation of various marginalized people, including IDPs, minority tribes, and people with disabilities. • Data collected for program implementation will be segregated in gender and disability, ensuring that the program reaches marginalized communities. • The design of WASH infrastructure will factor in the needs of the marginalized communities. For example, boreholes will be solar-powered to reduce the running cost hence providing free drinking water. Zero tariffs/costs on drinking water will ensure that no vulnerable members of the community are left without water. The WMC will charge a nominal fee for livestock owners. • Mercy-USA will ensure that formal confidential feedback and complaints mechanisms are accessible to all community members, especially marginalized communities.
