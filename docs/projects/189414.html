Project Summary

According to UNOCHA-HNO report for 2022, approximately 2.4 million people in South Sudan continue to be impacted by the destruction of housing and loss of essential goods caused by ongoing flooding and internal conflict. In addition, nearly 329,000 refugees and asylum seekers also require shelter and non-food item (NFI) support. At the end of 2021, nearly 1.9 million men, women, boys, girls, and older people live in inadequate shelter, while a total of 2.3million require NFIs. The number of people in need increased in 2022 due to the increased displacement of approximately 300,000 people in 2021 and the returns of nearly 270,000 from neighboring countries. The top three shelter/NFI-related needs are access to emergency shelter, NFIs and housing and land and property (HLP) rights to ensure security of tenure.
The Flooding and inter communal conflict have led to strained households leading to increased food insecurity, health risks, malnutrition, shelter and NFI needs, protection needs and high inflation rates lowering community resilience to meet their needs.
TADO proposes to carry out SNFI intervention through provision of in-kind reconstruction/ rehabilitation shelter, transitional shelter and provision of NFIs among the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities. The shelter and distribution of in kind NFIs will prevent the communities from health risks, reduce protection risks, improve the nutrition status through food diversity, improve food security situation and improve the community resilience.
TADO interventions targets 3 of the most affected states of South Sudan that includes Upper Nile, Warrap and Jonglei State with a total of 40,000 beneficiaries. The counties affected and caseloads to be targeted include; Upper Nile (Ulang 6000 and Nasir 7500 individuals), Jonglei State (Duk 10000, Twic East 3500 and Uror 4000 individuals) and Warrap State (Tonj East 6000 and Tonj South 3000 individuals).
The intervention will use the cluster set guidelines in selection and verification of beneficiaries that is inclusive of old people, PLWs, child headed HH, People with disabilities etc.
During intervention, TADO will mainstream protection through joint interventions with protection partners on ground during registration and verification of beneficiaries, distribution of S/NFIs and post distribution monitoring exercise. During restructuring/reconstruction of transitional settlements, TADO will follow HLP guidelines to ensure that the interventions do not cause conflict among the communities. This will be through pronged interaction with the community to understand the land dynamics. Together with HLP partners, TADO will train the communities on HLP access rights, referral to mitigate land cases, improve access to legal aid ad set up mechanisms for solving land disputes. This will be done in collaboration with local authorities and other partners.
The cluster AAP guidelines will be strictly followed throughout this intervention. This includes longer stay at project sites to study community dynamics and together set up community tailored AAP mechanisms. This will be through formation and training of community beneficiary accountability committees that will be inclusive to provide input on matters of concern to ensure all voices are heard and no one is left behind during the intervention. The feedback mechanisms will be followed throughout the project cycle. 1 - Needs

The number of internally displaced people across South Sudan increased from 1.7 million in November-December 2020 to 2 million in September 2021. Without political stability and peace, people may continue to be affected by violence in 2022 with subsequent displacement. Localized outbreaks of sub-national violence will likely continue to drive displacement and destruction of shelters and household items. People lost their houses and properties in the 2021 floods. Another year of sustained flooding will cause further destruction of shelters and depletion of household goods and may further drive temporary and permanent displacement. The lack of flood-resilient construction and continued depletion of natural resources for temporary and transitional shelter continues to drive vulnerability, placing households in a continuous pattern of emergency need without the opportunity for recovery. (HNO2022 and FSNMS, DTM). Further to flooding and conflict, the Revitalized Peace Agreement Signed by the government and opposition have triggered mass movement of Returnees from neighboring countries.In Duk County, Inter-communal violence and flooding happened to be real threats to human life and livelihoods of recent. Given the situation of insecurity, COVID-19 restrictions, heavy rainfall and flooding which led to shortage of food, displacement of people, loss of properties, and lack of access to basic services, among others. Accessibility for intervention becomes so challenging. Massive flooding and intercommunal fighting caused displacement of populations with the report indicating there were 27,006 people who had been displaced and forced to move to higher grounds. (Duk IRNA report December 2021). Uror County in Jonglei state is having similar situation as Duk and Twic East as massive flooding and intercommunal/inter-clan conflicts is gripping the county payams and their environs. Interclan conflict in Ulang between Nyangore and Ulang Centre in 2021 has caused displacement of populations in both payams leading to many children, women and elderly fleeing to neighboring villages for safety. During the conflict many shelters were burned with household items looted by youth as those who fled didn’t have time to flee with their items. The INRA report showed that shelters had been swept by floods and inhabitants had to run to highlands for safety in Neighboring Counties. Thus, straining the shelter and non-food item needs in the county. (Ulang IRNA report November 2021). Nasir County has been Impacted by the conflict since 2013, the Nassir County population has been displaced both internally and across borders into Ethiopia and Sudan. Both internally displaced and those in Ethiopia have faced challenges with lack of access to income generating activities, food, land, and housing as well as clashes between different ethnicities and clans in refugee camps in Ethiopia. According to REACH assessment of September 2021, in Tonj East and Tonj South, throughout 2021, insecurity and flooding have simultaneously driven large-scale displacement and prevented vulnerable populations, particularly in the Greater Luachjang region of Tonj East, from undertaking displacement journeys to access food and services. Greater Luachjang communities have reportedly been unable to displace to the north, east or south due to tensions with communities in the neighboring counties of Tonj North, Mayendit (Unity State), and Rumbek North (Lakes State). Barriers to movement in Tonj East have been further compounded by flooding, which has, according to focus group discussion (FGD) participants, prevented almost all movement out of Greater Luachjang. In the report, insecurity has reportedly prevented many households from cultivation. TADO thus intends to intervene in the Shelter and SNFIs intervention to 40000 vulnerable and affected individuals to the mostly affected and vulnerable communities in the target Counties.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will use all the information got from the beneficiaries through the feedback channels to improve service delivery among the vulnerable and affected populations. Any changes to the project design will be informed by the feedback from the beneficiaries.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

TADO will ensure that there is equal participation of all the beneficiaries including the most vulnerable in the community. This will be ensuring that they are well represented in the beneficiary accountability committees so that their views can be taken in to consideration during project implementation. TADO will have key informant interviews with the most vulnerable in the community and also focused group discussions with beneficiaries in local language to get information on the implementation of the project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

TADO has very strong PSEA policy that has non tolerance clause on sexual exploitation and abuse. TADO will put in mechanisms to present sexual exploitations and abuse by training the staffs on SEA and ensuring that the staffs sign code of conduct that strongly advocates against SEA and has guidelines on dispensary measures to be taken against the perpetrators.TADO will educate the community leaders and the beneficiaries about PSEA and give reporting channels and all the reports received will be fully investigated and acted upon.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

TADO will ensure that there is constant engagement with the vulnerable and affected populations. With strong community linkages TADO will engage the communities through out the project implementation setting up beneficiary accountability committees that will be responsible for feedback and complaints. TADO will have dedicated communication channels that includes a mobile telephone number and an email address for receiving complaints. TADO will have focal persons in the counties that its implementing the project to support communication between the community and the organization. Focused group discussions will be conducted regularly to get feedback on the implementation of the project.
