Project Summary

Nigeria is still facing a severe humanitarian crisis where armed conflict, insecurity, displacement, food insecurity, malnutrition and disease outbreaks have affected millions of people. Humanitarian needs are expected to remain high in 2024 as the conflict continues and the impact of climate change worsens.
In addition to the above, with the expansion of a government strategy for locating IDPs and decongesting camps, IOM will continue providing WASH support to displaced populations in need while reinforcing its humanitarian WASH activities with the idea of transition and recovery to support the influx of returnees to their places of origin. In direct response to the identified needs and as a significant contributor to the objectives of the WASH Sector in Nigeria, IOM is poised to expand its provision of WASH services, targeting internally displaced people and returnees in camp and camp-like settings, as well as the hosting communities in BAY (Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe) states.
To this end, IOM commits to continue:
(A) Safeguarding access to water facilities and services through comprehensive drilling, installation, maintenance, rehabilitation, water quality and groundwater monitoring of boreholes.
(B) Ensuring safe and equitable access to sanitation facilities (including latrines, showers, and laundry spaces) through the diligent operation and maintenance (O&M) of existing facilities and the construction of additional gender-segregated facilities and wastewater treatment systems.
(C) Fostering improved water and sanitation outcomes in targeted settings by systematically promoting personal and environmental hygiene messages aligned with national guidelines on water-related diseases. This includes incorporating Cholera preventive measures and supporting increased capacity within established gender-balanced WASH committees.
(D) Guaranteeing environmental sanitation (solid waste management) in the operational areas through the establishment of WASH Committees comprising both male and female community members. This involves procuring and distributing tools and equipment for effective waste management.
(E) Distributing WASH non-food items (NFIs) in adherence to national sector standards. IOM is committed to reinforcing the core pipeline strategy within the WASH sector, facilitating access to WASH NFIs for humanitarian actors implementing crucial actions.
(F) Contributing to bolstering capacity-building activities for local authorities, stakeholders, and key partners. This includes the integration of specialists in innovation and research in the fields of water and sanitation service management and environmental risk mitigation.
IOM is committed to ensuring that the planning and execution of all WASH activities are sensitive to the diverse needs of the project beneficiaries, incorporating considerations for age, gender, and disability. Special attention will be given to addressing the unique requirements of women and girls, including aspects of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM). IOM will rigorously uphold the "Do No Harm" principles throughout the entirety of WASH interventions. This will be particularly emphasized in the context of WASH infrastructure locations and construction, with mechanisms like consultations with vulnerable groups playing a pivotal role.
A comprehensive analysis of potential risks stemming from context-specific environmental degradation will guide IOM in prioritizing actions that effectively mitigate environmental threats. Notably, continues championing using solar-powered water supply whenever feasible, aiming to curtail reliance on fossil fuels generated by diesel pumping. In groundwater drilling, IOM will ensure a symbiotic link with monitoring initiatives to uphold sufficient aquifer recharge.
In the design of sanitation facilities rehabilitation, a preference will be given to materials that do not deplete local natural resources or contribute to long-term environmental damage.

1 - Needs

The imperative WASH needs in northeast Nigeria, encompassing access to clean, sufficient, and safe water for drinking, hygiene, and sanitation, have witnessed a notable escalation in scale and severity. This surge is attributed to evolving dynamics of population displacement and return, compounded by the impact of the climate crisis, marked by cycles of floods and droughts.The demand for access to safe water faces formidable constraints due to a combination of new and prolonged displacement and returns—whether self-organized or government-led. These dynamics pressure host communities significantly, diminishing their resilience as existing water points and sanitation facilities become increasingly strained and overburdened. The climate crisis further underscores the urgency of enhancing Nigeria's environmental risk management aspects of humanitarian interventions.Emergent displacements, coupled with challenges in groundwater potential in specific areas, have necessitated emergency water provision through water trucking. This has consequently resulted in escalated costs for conducting in-depth hydrogeological studies and optimizing water points to ensure efficient and sustainable use of water resources.Access to sanitation faces persistent challenges arising from the wear and tear of emergency-type constructions, ongoing displacements, and seasonal flooding, contributing to elevated instances of open defecation and diminished handwashing practices. Addressing sanitation needs, particularly for persons with special needs, women, and children, remains a critical gap within the sector. This necessitates additional resources, especially when considering factors such as proximity and the design of facilities.The collaborative efforts of the WASH and Health sectors in disease early warning and cholera prevention campaigns, coupled with a focus on environmental hygiene, aim to mitigate high Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) cases and cholera outbreaks. From 2021, the WASH sector has strategically streamlined partner operations to enhance accountability to affected populations and ensure better coverage of needs despite reduced funding, resulting in an improved quality of response.As an integral partner in the WASH sector, IOM remains steadfast in addressing the ongoing WASH needs in northeast Nigeria. Not confined solely to planned camps, IOM's WASH services extend to new arrival sites, transit and reception centers, spontaneous camps, camp-like settings, and host communities in the BAY States that currently have limited or no access to WASH services.

2 - Activities

IOM is dedicated to fortifying its humanitarian initiatives in alignment with the Nigerian WASH sector's objectives and guidelines and adhering to the global IOM's WASH principles. These principles underscore the importance of implementing immediate and rapid actions, innovative, compatible with evolving needs, scalable to address the dynamic context in Nigeria, and sustainable to tackle environmental challenges and the complexities of WASH resource and environmental risk management.To achieve these objectives, IOM will engage in the following key activities:1. Provision of Safe Water Access:- Keep drilling new boreholes and installation of hand pumps.- Rehabilitating and upgrading existing boreholes with solar-powered water systems.- Conducting water quality monitoring.- Capacity building for water system maintenance committees and WASH committees.- Groundwater monitoring initiatives and linkage with national / Sector Groundwater information system for sustainable resources management2. Delivery of Dignified Sanitation Facilities:- Construct new, adequate, and segregated sanitation facilities at household, institutional, and public levels.- Rehabilitating and upgrading existing sanitation facilities.- Operating and maintaining sanitation facilities.- Desludging of latrines.- Constructing wastewater treatment and disposal systems.- Providing handwashing stations with inclusive soap at public sanitation facilities and institutions.- Distributing solar lamps at blocks of latrines and showers in consultation with communities and beneficiaries.3. Provision of Safe and Dignified Environment:- Sustained environmental sanitation services, including waste bins, waste disposal pits, and sites.- Transportation of garbage to disposal sites.4. Community-Tailored Hygiene Promotion:- Disseminating gender and age-sensitive personal and environmental hygiene messages through sensitization sessions with community members and stakeholders.- Training Hygiene Promoters.- Conducting cholera preparedness and response activities.- Distributing hygiene items, WASH Non-Food Items (NFI), and Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits.- Producing and disseminating contextualized Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials.5. Capacity Building - Developing capacity-building activities for key stakeholders, authorities, and partners (to include partnerships for localization)- Initiating research and innovation programs on WASH resource management, environmental risk management, groundwater, flooding, and the impact of climate change on water resources.6. Resilience Strengthening and Nexus Initiatives:- Activities aimed at enhancing the resilience of conflict-affected populations.- Promoting the Humanitarian-Development-Peace (HDP) nexus.- Engaging in early recovery and durable solutions.- Integrating WASH into peacebuilding and fostering social cohesion.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The objective of IOM is to target populations and authorities who are encouraged to take ownership of WASH programs, promoting long-term resilience and sustainability while supporting the operationalization of the HDPN.The transition and recovery of IOM WASH Nigeria actions begin in humanitarian settings by applying development principles in such settings. The IOM WASH approach aims to gradually turn the dividends of humanitarian action into sustainable crisis recovery, resilience building, and development opportunities. Durable solutions, self-reliance, and community stabilization are key components of transition and recovery, as they can contribute to peacebuilding by addressing social tensions. These factors, therefore, lay the foundations for sustainable recovery and peace and reduce the likelihood of further displacement. IOM WASH interventions are uniquely placed to deliver on these objectives by providing tangible services as an entry point for delivering basic needs whilst delivering on additional aspects, such as governance, institutional capacity building, job creation, and conflict resolution simultaneously. IOM Nigeria will develop this strategy by supporting WASH beneficiaries, key stakeholders, and local governance to gradually hand over responsibilities for WASH management and finance to beneficiaries and regulatory authorities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

IOM, in seeking to ensure that affected communities' needs are addressed, human rights are upheld, and their needs fulfilled, subscribes to the concept of Protection Mainstreaming and Centrality of Protection. Protection Mainstreaming is the inclusion of humanitarian protection principles into the crisis response, ensuring that any response is provided in a way that avoids any unintended negative effects (do-no-harm), is delivered according to needs, prioritizes safety and dignity, is grounded on participation and empowerment of local capacities, and ultimately holds humanitarian actor’s accountable vis–à-vis affected individuals and communities. IOM ensures that it provides humanitarian assistance considering the specific needs of all population groups and conducts activities that lead to equitable access to assistance and services. In this regard, IOM will ensure GBV risk will be mitigated through the provision of gender-segregated sanitation facilities with appropriate locks, providing privacy screens, and ensuring sanitation facilities are located closer to the households in consultation with the beneficiaries. This will be followed by intensive sensitization and awareness campaigns (both door-to-door and mass media) in the form of community engagement to ensure full participation and involvement of the displaced population.In addition, through various Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) activities, women and girls will be engaged in discussions as they feel comfortable and open through the mentorship approaches and positive conversations to get their feedback which will be incorporated into IOM response to ensure that their concerns and complaints are addressed.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM will ensure accountability to the affected population and most vulnerable community throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the project’s design and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, the elderly, and persons with disabilities, among others. This will ensure, for example, that the physical abilities of the elderly and people with disabilities are factored into any forward planning regarding accessibility to services provided. IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, most notably through the community-based WASH committees. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the IOM Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. After the project has been completed, IOM will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure further accountability to affected populations and promote learning.
