Project Summary

The project for the resilience and wellbeing for single adolescent girls exiting conflict related sexual slavery and forced childbearing is a follow-up of GLOWA’s activities in the promotion and protection of the rights of children born of war in Cameroon. The project seeks to provide interim reparation measures (IRM) to single adolescent mothers who are victims of conflict related sexual slavery and the children born thereof in the Cameroon Anglophone conflict. The initiative is based on the recognition of the rights of survivors of conflict related forced child birth to reparation and the need to strengthen the care environment of children fathered by the belligerents of the Cameroon Anglophone armed conflict.
The project is dedicated to making a meaningful difference in the lives of the beneficiaries and the children they have through healing and empowerment measures that are determined by beneficiaries and tailored to their individual needs. The envisaged empowerment measures shall take into account the context and circumstances in which the beneficiary survivor lives. Individual survivors participating in the project shall determine the type and content of their empowerment package and directly participate in designing the indicators as well as the monitoring and evaluation process of the intervention.
The project shall integrate mental health and psychosocial support as well as advocacy activities aimed at shinning a spotlight and pushing for the protection and promotion of the rights of survivors of conflict related sexual slavery in the Cameroon Anglophone conflict in line with United Nations Security Council Resolution 1820 on sexual violence in conflict as well as the recommendations of the UN Special Representative of the Secretary General on Sexual Violence in Conflict.
As an interim reparation measure, it project shall offer mental health and psychosocial support together with economic empowerment through vocational training in a number of high demand trades in combination with a range of soft skills and social cohesion activities to help the adolescent mothers rebuild their lives while raising awareness to reduce stigmatization of survivors and children born fathered by combatants. The project targets 25 survivors as direct beneficiaries while the children fathered by combatants under their care, close relatives as well as other community members constitute the secondary beneficiaries. The implementation of the project shall seek to build self-awareness amongst both victims and contribute in building community participation in promoting and protecting the rights of adolescent girls trapped in conflict related sexual slavery.
As a survivor-led local non-profit working in the conflict area and having an understanding of the local realities, GLOWA shall contribute through this project to promote localization in humanitarian response. GLOWA is seeking for a grant support of Fifty Six Thousand Three Hundred and Seventy Canadian Dollars ($56,370) to enhance the livelihood of 25 survivors of conflict induced sex slavery and forced childbearing as well as promote the early education of 25 children aged 0-5 years who are fathered by the belligerent of the armed conflict in the North West Region of Cameroon.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Conflict related sexual violence is probably the most prevalent form of violence in the Anglophone Regions of Cameroon which unfortunately is also the most under reported. A number of reasons can be said to account for this trend amongst which is the inability of victims to self-identify as victims. In a humanitarian situation like the one in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon, self-identification is very vital for any intervention to follow. GLOWA estimates that there are more than five thousand armed men in the regions in conflict (both non-state armed group and state security agents away from their families) engaged in combat. These sexually active men most of whom consume varied forms of drugs regularly abuse adolescent girls and young women sexually resulting to the birth of a child. They equally initiate victims into drugs and other rituals of invincibility commonly believed to protect them in battle. The inability of those affected by conflict related sexual violence to self-identify as victims coupled by the targeting of victims by opposing sides of the conflict does not encourage disclosure even by family members and/or soliciting external support. Equally the regular occurrence of incidents of legal prosecution of survivors by state authorities for failure to report confirms the fact that both sides of the conflict consider victims as accomplices and also confirming limitations of combatants on international regulations relating to rules of engagement and UN Security Council Resolution 1820 on sexual violence in conflict as a war crime.Eight years since the outbreak of violence in the crisis that hit the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon in 2016, victims of conflict related sexual slavery continue to be erroneously considered by opposing sides as enemies and targeted for life threatening violence and sometimes extrajudicial killing. Victims and survivors including children born thereof constitute an undesirable social group because of the circumstance of their pregnancies and/or births. Children born from CRSV suffer rejection sometimes from their own mothers who consider them as dream killers resulting in limited mother-to-child bond. As recounted earlier, a considerable percentage of victims get initiated into drugs and other rituals for the invincibility of combatants. The consequences on victims and survivors gain intensity with the lack of adequate response from humanitarian actors at different levels. The need for urgent action is therefore very imperative. The UN Humanitarian response is organized around the cluster system. This cluster system does not adequately identify victims of conflict related sexual slavery as a group deserving attention. Instead, victims are bundled in the big basket of gender based violence area of responsibilities (GBV AoR). The risks they face from enemy combatants, also significantly contributes in eclipsing victims. Even though incidents of targeted violence are regularly reported in humanitarian fora within the conflict area, unfortunately, they are reported simply as happenings and little has been done to proactively protect potential victims.Once organized as a group, survivor participating in the project shall peer support each other and provide a platform for collaboration with humanitarian actors on varied domains including advocacy particularly targeting commanders of the armed combatants. This will break the prolonged silence amongst survivors. This project offers us a perfect opportunity to raise awareness amongst both community and humanitarian sector on their plight. Community member who suffered any form of abuse perpetrated by the father of these children have continued to nurse enmity with their mothers, families and even the innocent children.
