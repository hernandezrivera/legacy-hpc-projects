Project Summary

The proposed project is an integrated emergency project that will cover thematic areas of FSL, Education and WASH for 22,190 person (7056 women, 6258men, 4615 girl and 4260 boy) from Damazine, Kurmuk, Rosaries, Wad Almahi and Geissan localities in Blue Nile state. The project is aimed at providing the affected vulnerable communities with sustainable basic services to ensure well-being, improved food security at the HH level, improved enrolment and retention of school going children, provide gender for and culturally sensitive WASH and protection services while ensuring sustainable environmental practices. The proposed project will complement and linked to existing IRW projects in the proposed project sites and will include of construction and rehabilitation of WASH facilities, construction and rehabilitating school facilities and infrastructure including hygiene and sanitation facilities, providing small holder farmers with seeds and tools, capacity building of community members and healthcare staff on hygiene and sanitation while incorporating Covid-19 awareness and prevention. The proposed will thus contribute to improved and increased access to basic services by the affected vulnerable men, women, boys and girls while creating resilience and sustainability. IRW will coordinate the project activities with other partners and respective government departments in the project sites including UN agencies such as UNICEF, WFP, UNOCHA, WHO and UNHCR. Implementation strategy will be direct implementation strategy while IRW will also involve its implementation partners in community mobilization and engagement.

1 - Needs

FSL: According to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) platform, about 9.8 million people in Sudan are estimated to be acutely food insecure (IPC Phases 3 and 4) in September 2021. Despite favorable projections for this year’s farming season, soaring prices of agricultural supplies will likely affect planted areas and crop yields. This is due to sustained inflation and dwindling foreign currency reserves that are hampering imports. In addition, the full removal of fuel subsidies in June has constrained farmers’ access to fuel, affecting the semi-mechanized and irrigated sectors, which account on average for about half of the aggregate cereal output. According to the September issue of the Food Prices Monitoring and Analysis Bulletin of the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) Food prices are expected to rise and availability and access to food continues to be limited due to COVID-19 containment measures and the impact of the 2020 floods. This will worsen the food security of Sudanese farmers, particularly smallholders and vulnerable households who cannot afford to purchase food for their families or agricultural supplies needed to resume farming. Education sector is currently riddles with dilapidated infrastructure as a result of the protracted armed conflict in the targeted areas. Majority of the residents of these targeted areas are poor and cannot afford basic requirements such as school uniforms, books and school bags. Another challenge is lack of sitting desks in majority of schools and hygiene and sanitation facilities separate for both boys and girls. These challenges according to some are the major hindrance and or poor enrolment and retention of children in schools and more so the girl child hence the target areas report very enrolment rates, low retention rates, high rates of absenteeism, and high dropout rates. When children don’t go to school and more so girls it exposes to child labour, early, forced and planned marriages thus depriving these young children of a bright future while exposing girls to reproductive health completions such as prolonged labour, difficult births and even deaths. The latest need assessment and Ministry of Education records revealed that the Blue Nile ranks very low among the states of Sudan when it comes to availability, accessibility and quality of education. Attendance rate among the school age children is only 55% (51% for boys and 49% for girls respectively). The literacy rate among the population 15 years and over is 38%. Children’s attendance to pre-school education is as low as 47.7%. There are around 44.8% which is 118,773 school age children outside the education system in the Blue Nile. In Blue Nile, the enrolled children reach grade eight of basic education is low compared with other states in Sudan. The state has a high rate of child labour and this is another alarming indicator for the deteriorating situation of education. According to Inter Agency Rapid Needs Assessment Report of August 2020 in the target areas, Water facilities are deteriorating and needs rehabilitation and the existing HPs and 45% of hand wells are not functioning with the functioning hand dug wells being prone to contamination as a result of the current rains and flooding (July/August 2020). Open defecation is rampant amongst IDPs as they lack latrines with the existing ones in bad working conditions. The increasing numbers of IDPs as a result of conflict have led to increased pressure on the existing WASH and WASH facilities and there is need to construct new ones and rehabilitate the existing ones. The continued displacement and influx of IDPs has led to over population of existing schools thus contributing to poor learning environment with lack of WASH facilities within the schools which also have no school meals program that helps in increasing enrolment and retention of children in school while improving their nutrition status

2 - Activities/Outputs

Under FSL part IRW will provide of improved seeds of sorghum, sesame, certified vegetable seeds, simple agricultural environmentally friendly tools and Train extension agents on agricultural to small holder farmers as 3000 households will receive agricultural inputs and services this is include provision of improved seeds of sorghum and sesame for 3000 Farmers, provision of certified vegetable seeds also will provide set of simple agricultural environmentally friendly tools to 3000 small holder farmers, distribution of seeds and tools will be simultaneous with training of extension agents on agricultural techniques for Men and Women. Distribution of multipurpose tree seedlings targeting HHs, IRW also will distribute multipurpose tree seedlings after beneficiary selection through beneficiary selection committees. Conduct one trainings on beekeeping targeting for 30 beekeeper and support them with tools and equipment, 20 fishermen will receive structured trainings on efficient use of fishing tools and cost effective strategies for getting maximum yield from fishing and sustainable fishing practices. In education part IRW will construction 4 schools units and 4 preschool classrooms, IRW will contract services of a qualified and experienced contractor to undertake the construction works in the selected schools. The construction Bill of Quantities will be approved by the SMoE and works completion certificate will be provided at the end of the construction works. 4 school latrines will be constructed in schools in targeted areas along with appropriate sanitation/sewerage services, privacy and appropriate lightening, this will be done after a detailed assessment to identify the schools for construction. Provision of school seats, teacher office furniture and recreation materials, IRW will procure schools seats branded and distribute to children. Also recreation materials for the preschools classrooms, learning and teaching materials for 10 schools (for both teachers + students) and water tanks HHs.In WASH pert, in order to increased access to equitable and affordable access to sufficient quantity of safe water to meet their drinking and domestic needs. Water infrastructure often fall into disrepair due to conflicts, natural disasters or lack of functional maintenance systems. IRS will work against this through construction and rehabilitation of MWYs and hand pumps through consulting Water and Environmental Sanitation (WES) and community members and relevant stakeholders in identification of water facilities to be rehabilitated and selection of sites for new water facilities. The proposed intervention will work to increase access to improved hygiene and sanitation services, this will basically involve rehabilitation and provision of hand washing facilities and related supplies such as hand washing soap. These will be accompanied by sensitization on hand washing practices more so during the critical times. This will also go a long way in prevention and control of spread of Covid-19 amongst the beneficiaries.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project has been designed to respond to the different needs of groups at the activities, outputs, and outcomes levels. The project implementation team have vast experience on gender sensitive implementation and will practically implement project activities in a manner calculated to advance gender equality. The implementation teams will include both men and women. A significant proportion (52%) of the proposed beneficiaries are women. Girls and boys will be mobilized to benefit from hygiene sessions to reduce water borne diseases. Education, FSL and WASH Facilities will be constructed in safe locations that minimize risks and threats to beneficiaries and in particular women and girls for easy access. Fetching of water in most rural areas in Sudan is the responsibility of women, who spend a lot of time on this activity. However, this project will spare women an ample time and give them the opportunity to carry out other activities and participate in the development process of their communities, state and country through creating easy access and reducing the waiting time and distance when fetching water. School latrines will be gender and culturally sensitive and will be constructed separate for men and women, boys and girls thus promoting safety and privacy thus reducing dropout rates more so for the girl child. Hygiene promotion will be gender sensitive, with topics applicable for girls and for boys being delivered separately. Female hygiene will be sensitively addressed as per the norm of the community. Facilitators will ensure that issues addressed are gender balanced and engage both girls and boys> IRS has been working with schools in Blue Nile State for more than 20 years, frequently targeting IDPs, Returnees, Refugees and Host Community and has noted that girls tend to suffer disproportionally when there is a shortage of educational facilities. By providing safe learning spaces this project will encourage girls to enroll and attend school (and families to send their girls to school). Livelihood interventions will also ensure women participation and representation in community structures and capacity building opportunities. Some activities will directly target women and Female Headed Households in particular to enhance their self-reliance and empower them for sustainable livelihood. Nutrition education and awareness together with introduction of vegetables cultivation and fruits planting in the targeted villages will significantly improve the HHs diet with nutritious food items those are essential for children, pregnant women and lactating mothers’ health and wellbeing. Agro-processing training will equip women with the required skills and knowledge to access the local market and generate income for their families, jobs will be created as youths will benefited from the vocational training in electrical works and welding and will support them with tool kits to start their own Income Generating Activities (IGAs)

There is clear connection between our security and programme that are appropriate, timely, participative and transparent in the identification of beneficiaries and entitlements. Our programme always gives priority to saving lives, doing no harm and alleviating suffering and IRS allocates assistance on the impartiality basis of need, remain neutral within conflict and independent of governments foreign or domestic agenda. The relationship between programming and humanitarian identity is of utmost importance and plays a vital role in gaining acceptance and consent from the beneficiary’s community and other social power-breakers for our presence and programme delivery. IRS has well established beneficiary selection committees that are representative in terms of gender that ensures PWD and the marginalised rights and entitlements are equally given to them and that they are discriminated against at all times. IRS also prescribes and adheres to the 9 CHS standards that over the years has ensured beneficiaries are not discriminated against and provides beneficiaries with a platform for Complaints and Feedback and in all the cases IRS takes decisive actions and decisions that ensure non-discriminatory actions by her staff at all times. The project and activity design have considered the DNH principles thus will ensure that they are not a source of conflict, disagreements and discrimination but rather source of peaceful co-existence and harmony while fostering sustainable utilization and management of community resources. In addition, the project has been designed to minimise harm and support local capacities to build peace. Therefore, IRS will Cooperate and work with relevant partners to help promote social, environmental and economic well-being in a cohesive and inclusive manner and provide structures for handling feedback and complaints, Communities will be sensitized so that they know how to complain if project commitments are not met and finally, Appropriate awareness-raising tools and approaches are used with communities, which are relevant to age, language, social status and capacity. IRS and all partners will apply a conflict sensitive lens throughout project and implementation. As part of the baseline assessment, IRS will undertake a conflict sensitivity analysis that will assess issues of conflict and existing community structures that can contribute to peace-building and conflict resolution. The results of the analyses will serve as a guide for ensuring that IRS and project partners understand the complex dynamics within each beneficiary community. IRS will also ensure staff; partners, and communities involved in the project are aware of and take into consideration actions that have the potential to cause conflicts within and between community and social groups
