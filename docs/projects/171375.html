Project Summary

The project aims to mitigation the vulnerable farmer's struggles and suffering resulting
from Israel's occupation and settlers attacks to contribute to the sustainable income of
small scale women and men through increasing the strength of the most vulnerable
families resilience in East Jerusalem, and area C in Bethlehem by targeting vulnerable
farmers who highly vulnerable and lack access to basic services, the specific objective is
to increase the well being and income of women and men engaged in small-scale
agriculture in vulnerable communities through improved agricultural and access to land
and market. The project will support and enhance the resilience and protect the
agricultural livelihoods for 3834 individual beneficiaries (1919 M, 1915 F) of the
communities and families to build their capacity to absorb shocks, including access to
land through facilitating these essential services through these activities:
1- Rehabilitation of 400 dunums of agricultural land which serve 640 individual
beneficiaries and create 3200 days (cash for work) for 1024 unemployees.
2- Construct 10 kilos of agricultural roads that serve 350 farmers own 2000 dunums.
3- Rehabilitate 10 water cisterns that serve 100 farmers own 300 dunums.
in Al Kadr, Beit Tamir, Wadi Fukin in Bethlehem, and Al Eizariya, Al Isawiya, AlJudeira, Beit
Hanina al Balad, Hizma, Jaba, Mikhmas, Qaatanna at East Jerusalem to ensure threats to
food security, income, and life, in general, are minimized. This project address critical
issues for FSS under Strategic Objective #3 and MoA national strategy. More precisely,
this will contribute to:
1- Support vulnerable farmers’ resilience in the targeted locations to be able to withstand settlers' attacks and the occupation practices on their land's productivity.
2- Improve poor farmers' livelihood sources in targeted locations.
3- Protect farms, and trees from deers which mainly release up by settlers. which becomes an extra risk for trees.
4- Direct access to food by unemployed through working in agricultural land.
The entire areas are severely suffering from occupation threat and settlers attack like land
confiscations, so the entire targeted communities will have access to better and more

water, better access to their land through the rehabilitated roads, which will serve as well
as alternative and saver roads whenever the main roads are closed by the occupation and
its settlers.

1 - Needs

To understand the causes behind deteriorating livelihood conditions in Northeast Jerusalem, Northwest Jerusalem, and Bethlehem in area C, various economic, demographic, agricultural, nutritional, health, environmental and food security issues must be considered. AAA did a comprehensive needs assessment for the targeted villages in early Aug. We noticed, through group meetings, the extent of the suffering of farmers affected by the attacks of the occupation on their lands and their source of livelihood as they are prevented from using the bypass road 60 that separates the east from the west region, and these bypass roads have consumed a lot of agricultural lands, Israeli illegal settler's violence against farmers and their farms, consequently, the farmer's access to his land takes a lot of time and effort, as the infrastructure available for movement and mobility suffers from wear and tear due to the dredging and planned destruction of the occupation and by the weather factors, and the eastern regions are characterized by a low rate of rainwater, which burdens the farmer in summer in the process of irrigation of crops, confiscation notices against the possession of their land, the release of wild Deers (which become an extra risk for farmers' trees mainly when attending their lands), confiscation of lands and scraping of agricultural lands by the occupation, as happened in the village of Mikhmas, where 300 olive trees over 30 years old, opening the sewers of the settlements of Efrat and Beitar Illit on the lands of Al Khader and Wadi Fukin agricultural were bulldozed are attempts to force the farmer to leave his land, the farmers suffer from strangling and restrictions on movement by the separation wall, bypass roads that are not allowed to use, the continuous expansion of Israeli settlements which lead to confiscating of lands from the Palestinian people, continues violence that occurs by the settlers live in that area including destroying agricultural lands, roads, and infrastructure, limited access to water, and climatic change (less rainwater and high temperature), all these practices deteriorated life in the targeted locations, so the suggested interventions like agricultural road, land rehabilitation, and rehabilitation of cisterns wells, will facilitate/ease farmers’ movement, and work on their adherence to land and plant them. Also, they will use the harvested water in supplementary irrigation during hot summers, this will support farmers' livelihoods and resilience to stand in the face of land confiscation and therefore their survival in the profession of agriculture, which keeps their livelihood source secure, profitable, and sustainable. The idea falls in cluster objective (SO3) To support the resilience and the productive capacity of farmers, particularly small-scale ones are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected. So it was agreed with the need of local authorities and farmers on the need to open new agricultural roads to facilitate access Farms to new lands and facilitate the transfer of crops in addition to the need to rehabilitate reclaimed lands in the past to expand the area To replace the areas swallowed by the settlements, facing various levels of drops in Agricultural crops productivity, and to build the necessary retaining walls to protect the soil from erosion. Also, there is a need to rehabilitate water harvesting cisterns to save water instead of buying it for exorbitant sums. The assessment reported a sharp increase in the unemployment rate in those villages mainly among farmers and especially young people and women, The Corona pandemic has also raised the unemployment rate among rural youth, and thus they are demanding to work in agriculture as a source to secure their livelihood.The project is targeting a total of 3824 direct beneficiaries (956 men, 918 women, 995 boys, and 955 girls) individual beneficiaries (disaggregated by gender) beneficiaries will benefit from increased access to their land to ensure inclusion of women, and other groups as well as involve men, girls, and girls at schools, youth, and elderly. Where women are considered a strong participant in the rural agriculture sector The assessment revealed that women are more vulnerable to limited access to land and low income, as they bear agricultural burdens of up to 65%, so the provision of qualified agricultural roads is an important factor in facilitating their agricultural work 15% and providing the burdens of transportation on bumpy roads that may cause them more pain and physical problems, and the children of retaining walls work To provide a safe environment that protects her and her children from falling while moving between the agricultural terraces and provides her with more time to take care of her family. These are the main issues that farmers are currently facing farmers from obtaining the optimal use of their main source of income that contributes significantly to their families' food security. AAA offers to work on the most badly eroded 10 km of agricultural roads, rehabilitate 400 dunum, and 10 rehabilitation of cisterns for the most vulnerable farmers. This will enhance farmers' access to their agriculture lands, stability, better access to livelihoods source. AAA will implement this project in close coordination with the MoA and other actors in the area to avoid any duplication and overlapping. The intervention objectives is to facilitate the access of farmers to their lands, and facilitate the transportation of the inputs and outputs to and from the land without any extra cost or effort, better access to water and other inputs, and this more sustainable income. It will reinforce farmers' resilience on their lands.

2 - Activities

Prior to the project implementation, AAA will strictly coordinate the final definition and selection of localities and beneficiaries with line ministries offices, and with any other organization working in the same area and sector of activities. AAA will be targeting farmers in all the communities mentioned with main activities including (rehabilitation of agricultural roads, rehabilitation of agricultural lands, and cistern rehabilitation). Through agricultural roads, farmers' mainly women's access to their agricultural land will be facilitated, with less time and more cost-effective. Farmers will perform farming practices more effectively and easily and secure, this will increase productivity and quality of the produce and easy access to the marketing of high-quality agricultural products. Furthermore, trees and humans including women and children will be safer from wild animals and deers’ attacks. AAA in coordination with local CBOs, related ministries, and village councils, will formulate a steering committee in each community, which will create clear bene ciary selection criteria with clear marks that will help in the selection of the poorest farmers. The criteria should include technical standards, household status, and income, 15% extra marks will be given to each household headed by women, a number of family members, disability members in the family, chronic diseases in the family, having university students in the family, the benefited land space, the type of crop, where we will target crops with higher viable economically, and land location if its near settlements and bypass roads, military camps... etc. AAA. will discuss the applications with the participation of the selected committee to each location with the implementation of field visits to verify the information written in the applications specifically (the technical standards, the economic situation) AAA will make sure to enhance women role through participation in the local committees and will give priority to women-headed households and youth. Rehabilitation roads will facilitate farmers mainly women access to cultivated lands, in addition, to transfer the product or production materials to the cultivated land. The agricultural road will ease the access to tools and machines to the agriculture land, which decreases women's workload, noting that 65% of the agricultural work is on the shoulders of women (Oxfam’s olive value chain report). AAA will make sure to women representatives on the selection committee. Also, they will receive positive discrimination during the process of evaluating the applications for the benefit, as the woman who is the breadwinner for her family or the widow will be awarded 5 additional points, and the same is for the woman who applies to benefit from the activities who owns the land, and families who have 3 or more female children will receive two additional points. It will also take into account the cases of disabilities and university students.The activities will be in the Al Kadr, Beit Tamir, Wadi Fukin in Bethlehem. And Al Eizariya, Al Isawiya, AlJudeira, Beit Hanina al Balad, Hizma, Jaba, Mikhmas, Qaatanna at Northeast and northwest Jerusalem as the following: - Land rehabilitation 400 dunum as the following: (Al Khadr 70, Wadi Fukin 50, Beit Tamir 30,Al Eizariya 30, Al Isawiya 30, Al Judeira 30, Beit Hanina al Balad 30, Hizma 30, Jaba 30,Mikhmas 30, Qatanna 40).-Agri. Road 10 KM as the following: (Al Khadr 2, Wadi Fukin 1, Al Eizariya 1, Al Judeira 1, Beit Hanina al Balad 1, Hizma 1, Mikhmas 1.5, Qatanna 1.5)- Cisterns 10 as the following: (Al Eizariya 3, Al Isawiya 2, Beit Hanina al Balad 2, Qatanna 3). Rehabilitating or restoring essential productive assets. 1- Rehabilitation of 400 dunum, owned by over 640 individual beneficiaries in total The activity will provide 213 workers with over 3200 working days, ( 133 H.H beneficiaries). 2- Rehabilitation of 10 kafri cistern. that serve around 300 dunums of agricultural lands, owned by over 100 farmers beneficiaries in total, The activity will provide 20 workers with over 380 working days, ( 576 individual beneficiaries). 3- Rehabilitation of 10 km of agricultural roads that serve around 2000 dunums of agricultural lands owned by over 1680 individual beneficiaries in total. Additional Targets: The expected objectively verifiable indicators and target of the project are: 1. A total of 3824 number of individual beneficiaries (disaggregated by gender) beneficiaries will benefit from increased access to food. 2. Increase the outcome of confrontations with settlers.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

AAA will follow protection mainstreaming guides disseminated by the FSS Cluster: - All activities aimed at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individual in the good spirit of the relevant bodies of human rights law, international humanitarian law and local laws. - Key Protection principles AAA will ensure will include: Do no harm: by avoiding exposing people to further harm as a result of your actions, and ensure that: * The environment and way in which assistance is provided do not expose people and environment to further human rights abuses or violations; * Take all reasonable steps to ensure that the affected population are not subject to violent attack, or forced or induced into undertaking actions that may cause them harm or violate their rights; * Manage information in a sensitive manner so that the security of informants or others who may be identifiable is not jeopardized. * Assistance and protection measures do not undermine local capacities for self-protection. Support the efforts of the affected population and local community to find security and restore dignity. * None- discrimination: ensure equitable and impartial access to assistance, without discrimination on any grounds: * Challenge any attempts to exclude parts of the target communities. * Promote respect for human rights, and assist and support affected people to claim their rights and access remedies from relevant authorities; to obtain information on their entitlements and secure the documentation needed to demonstrate their entitlements, and to recover by providing psychosocial and community support. * AAA will Ensure consultation with the target population at all stages, and the participation of all in the design and targeting of interventions, in particular vulnerable and marginalized groups. * Environment-building - AAA project will contribute to creating an environment conducive to respect for human rights and the rule of law, including the reduction of exposure or vulnerability to protection risks.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AAA conducted the needs assessment in a very collaborative way, with the targeted municipalities, women club, cooperative, and community leaders. The needs assessment results then validated by an open workshop in the community which includes affected people, farmers, women, youth, and elderly and owners. Moreover, AAA agreed and set up the local committee which consists of the municipalities, women club, cooperative and community leaders. The committee will govern the implementation of the project throughout the project life. AAA is mandated to and work with rural communities and Farmers across the WB, AAA team will ensure the implementation of its feedback complaint mechanism utilizing the following actions: - Regular daily site visits by AAA field supervisors with on-site response to concerns from people in (East Jerusalem & Bethlehem). - Facilitation of information sessions between people and the councils & municipalities where all people's concerns are publicly heard and adjustments made accordingly to any activity. this implies both men and women are participating. - The communities will have access to a complaint box at the municipalities, also can reach up to the AAA office and team through phone calls and direct visits. - Updated AAA feedback/complaint database will be shared accordingly with Donor reporting. - Legal awareness sessions with people to be aware of security assumptions and legal status of the area. By including different stakeholders in the community and other actors from outside the community, including farmers and owners, AAA believes they have taken concrete steps in considering Do No Harm policy. AAA will keep an eye on any new issues in the area to avoid any negative impact as a result of the project activities.
