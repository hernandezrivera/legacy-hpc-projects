Project Summary

In the context of spontaneous returns of IDPs and Sudanese refugee like returnees returning from Ehtiopia and other countries in the region, this project aims to provide immediate humanitarian assistance upon arrival as well as medium to longer term assistance to support durable solutions for returnees and resilience for the hosting communities.
IOM in Sudan adopts an approach to durable solutions focused on a progressive approach to resolve displacement situations. This includes the identiﬁcation and mitigation/reduction of potential risks and negative coping mechanisms and the reinforcement of mobility opportunities to transition towards self-reliance and increased resilience. Across the four programmatic areas of intervention: protection, safety and security; adequate standard of living; sustainable livelihoods and employment; and inclusive governance, IOM will therefore aim to continue supporting communities in return areas by increasing the self-reliance and resilience of returnees and affected local communities who remained behind to support transition towards durable solutions through sustainable reintegration, social stability development. Reintegration and social stability support will be carried out through the identification of community needs and the impact the returnees’ movement can have on the community as a whole. The project will therefore apply a flexible and community-based approach to respond to humanitarian needs and to ensure that socio-economic needs and basic services of communities are met based on priorities identified directly by the community through a Community Needs Identification Workshop. Under HRP this project ﬁts under Objective 2 – “Contribute to building resilience to recurrent shocks and improving vulnerable people’s access to basic services” and ensures to the UNDAF and IOMs programmes.
This project is in line with IOM’s Progressive Resolution of Displacement Situations Framework and in support of the implementation of programming along the Humanitarian-Development Nexus by foster the resilience of displaced persons, migrants and affected communities to transition towards durable solutions. IOM Sudan’s Transition and Recovery Unit will deliver the project drawing on its extensive experience implementing locally-driven and contextualized longer-term community-led activities that empower affected populations ownership over addressing the conditions of their displacement.

1 - Needs

Most displaced populations return to their places of origin permanently, on a seasonal basis or are at risk of secondary displacement to another location due to cyclical floods/natural disasters or outbreak of conflict. IOM plans to create favorable conditions for returnee communities in Sudan by providing an inclusive, resilience-based approach that supports the progressive resolution to displacement (while ensuring safety nets are in place to avoid potentially harmful mobility strategies). This project will assess the speciﬁc needs of the returned populations in these localities and implement the most suitable interventions including the: (a) Rehabilitation WASH infrastructure, health and education services in the most vulnerable locations; and, (b) Organization of market orientated vocational trainings to improve food security, livelihood and employment opportunities for vulnerable community members and returnees. This will include a community driven approach to ensure ownership via establishment of committees, and including introducing elements like support of WASH-tariff system via community-led water management committees from the start to ensure the sustainability of the water services. This project will also beneﬁt from the results and lessons learnt of the Durable Solutions Proﬁling pilot project and various other durable solutions (multisectoral-integrated) projects implemented by IOM across Sudan since start of 2019, where IOM is working in close collaboration with local CBOs/CSOs and international and national partners including the Government of Sudan. Under the HRP this project ﬁts under Objective 2, whereas it also ensures links between several other IOM’s activities implemented in the context of UNDAF. Gender Equality and Women’s Empowerment: Special needs of women, men, boys and girls will be incorporated into the comprehensive needs assessment and response plan for return and reintegration. Sustainability and Resilience: this will include community and government engagement in the planning and implementation of this intervention and capacity building trainings to support local authorities and beneﬁciaries to have necessary skills to support self reliance.

2 - Activities / Outputs

Project Output 1: Community led workshops and initiatives (including community-committees, water, waste and resource management committees develop community action plans for initiatives to be launched in the areas of intervention).Project Output 2: Water and sanitation infrastructures are rehabilitated and hygiene awareness activities are promoted at sites of return in line with SPHERE Standards. Project Output 3: Health facilities are rehabilitated and clinics are supported to provide Minimum Basic Health Package to returnees and the host community. Project Output 4: Facilitating training of water technicians (female and male) and health workers to support the provision and maintenance of basic services. Project Output 5: Vulnerable Returnees and host community members are provided with market oriented vocational and essential livelihood training sessions based on community priorities. Project Output 6: Cash based support will be provided to the most vulnerable returnee households to foster self-reliance. Project Output 7: Education facilities, including teachers’ accommodations are rehabilitated or constructed and teachers are trained in coordination with the Ministry of Education.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Disasters and environmental degradation threaten to hamper progress towards the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and durable solutions, especially for those countries left furthest behind. Climate change is expected to trigger growing population movements within and across borders. IOM aims to build the resilience of communities to risk factors and expected changes in their natural environment, while contributing to sustainable development of livelihood opportunities; and support efforts to strengthen the interaction with responsibility holders (national and local authorities) and develop their capacities. IOM is working on establishing integrated water management systems and will also continue working on establishing sources of renewable energy (solar energy) and improving local practices to reduce the environmental footprint and waste of natural resources. Livelihood and agricultural training will continue to focus on improving the targeted beneﬁciaries' knowledge of agricultural practices so that they are able to better respond to climate shocks and replace the negative coping strategies currently used and that contribute to ongoing land degradation and desertiﬁcation (e.g. ﬁrewood collection). IOM will consult with UNEP/ISCG on the community environmental action plan tools and conduct area impact assessments before implementation of any supports on rehabilitation of basic service facilities such as water and sanitation facilities.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Women and girls tend to be identiﬁed more frequently as victims of traﬃcking for sexual exploitation and forced labour. Protection threats such as harassment and rape targeted to women and girls are still reported in Darfur. To address these issues, IOM will continue to provide direct assistance through psychosocial support, counseling and medical assistance to mobile populations and affected communities in vulnerable situations and make efforts to raise awareness among all community members of the risk and danger of irregular migration, human right and protection. The project will plan to deliver community-based health education, awareness raising activities for the most vulnerable segments of the population (women girls, children and elderly) will not be limited to promotion of health, hygiene and prevention of infection diseases, and nutrition but also address issues of irregular migration, and protection risks faced by women and girls in vulnerable situations. Such activities will encourage participation of local community, relevant stakeholders, people with disabilities and women’s representatives to foster an inclusive environment for all community members affected by violence, displacement and migration.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project activities will be designed in a way to support women, children and people with disabilities. Special attention will be paid to women, lactating mothers or pregnant women. These women have limited access to basic health and maternal services upon their return, therefore the project will assist these women in vulnerable situations through delivering improved basic health services (e.g. Minimum Basic Health Package (MBHP). Furthermore, the majority of the displaced returnee women have little to no assets/household goods and have no means to engage in income generating activities as they did before the displacement, therefore, there is a need for star-up kits and trainings specially designed to cater the speciﬁc needs of women. The project will aim to empower women through speciﬁcally designed interventions informed by a combination of targeted assessments, by enhancing their knowledge and skills on asset-based contribution and small businesses management. In addition, as described previously, women will be involved in all decision making processes when determining types of prioritized interventions. For the WASH activities, consultative participatory approaches will also be employed to allow for the communities to participate directly in the activities, in the decision-making process and in the identiﬁcation of priority needs based on gender and vulnerability. This inclusive participatory approach will assure the inclusion of ideas from vulnerable women and girls, and people with disabilities, who are often excluded from community decisions or under represented during decision making processes, especially with regard to selecting the location for the implementation of the activities. Throughout the implementation of the project and the activities, the community will be able to actively provide their feedback and voice their concerns so that corrective measures can be employed to support the communities and their views.
