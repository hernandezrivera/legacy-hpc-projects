Project Summary

The proposed actions have been designed as a part of an overall Integrated Program aiming to respond to WASH, Health and Protection needs for the Southern and Western of Libya, Sebha, Murzuq, Ghat, Ubari, Aljfara, Azzawya and Tripoli mantikas.
- The health component will be focusing mainly on the rehabilitation of primary and secondary public health facilities by upgrading their basic services through the supply of the needed equipment, commodities and/or refurbishment. The support provided will strengthen the capacity of the health care facility staff through the delivery of personnel capacity trainings and workshops for efficient and inclusive management, operation and maintenance of the upgraded facilities. The project will highlight the inclusion of the most vulnerable groups in the areas such as IDPs, Refugees, Migrants, women and children, by facilitating their accessibility to the facilities and include staff training related to GBV and protection. Furthermore, awareness campaigns will be conducted among the targeted local community populations on inclusive access to basic services and key protection information regarding communicable diseases like the COVID 19 as well as Non-Communicable diseases. The awareness campaigns that are to be conducted will not cover the health staff but will target 4000 community beneficiaries with health-related knowledge, however they are not covered by any indicator in the Project Module Platform
- The WASH component (separated HRP proposal submitted to the WASH Sector) will ensure safe and dignified access to water and sanitation through the rehabilitation of the WASH facilities and infrastructures in targeted urban communities, focusing as well on providing children and all people with COVID-19 assistance (regular disinfection, regular provision of PPE to medical staff, awareness raising campaigns).
- The protection components (separated HRP proposal submitted to the Protection Sector) integrated into above responses will enhance protection through safe identification and referral of people in need of specialized protection and multi-sectoral services, based on outreach to individuals, households and community levels focusing on vulnerable groups such as children, women, PWSN.

1 - Needs

During this last year, Libya has witnessed a humanitarian crisis with the combined impact of a continuous security unrest and a global new pandemic. In the absence of a centralized national authority and social security, public services have significantly deteriorated in Libya. These challenges have resulted in a serious deterioration of service provision, especially in the health sector. Furthermore, due to continuous conflict, including direct attacks on public infrastructure, many healthcare services have been abandoned or became non-functional. The combination of poor condition of the health facilities along with the lack of the necessary medical capacities, supplies and equipment, made the country unprepared for an efficient COVID-19 response. Acute shortages of health care facilities and health workers have been reported all over the country, especially in Western and Southern Libya, while the medical support is still inaccessible in some geographical neglected areas.There have been even reports of health staff who refused to work their duties due to a lack of adequate personal protection equipment (PPE). Others are unable to perform their duties because of non-functional vital health services, or absence of the necessary devices to provide the minimum health care for the patients. Not to mention, the continuous interruption of electricity mainly due to the damage inflicted upon several power plants and grid assets as well as the lack of maintenance, and the absence of enough safe water.With more than 880,000 people – according to an IOM study on Libya Crisis Response Plan on 2021 – having severe humanitarian needs as a result of exposure to physical and mental harm, the dire health situation worsened by the wide spread of COVID-19, has deepened the vulnerabilities of almost a million of IDPs, refugees and migrants. Often victims of discrimination, these vulnerable groups are now witnessing episodes of racism and refusal of access to the health care services. Indeed, a recent IOM assessment have shown that 83% of migrants and refugees have no or limited access to health care services, whereas 75% of the assessed migrants reported only having limited or no access to the health services in Libya while seeking health care.Local communities suffering from poverty are in a similar situation: limited access to many of the health facilities, combined with the restrictions on internal movements due to COVID-19, have hampered travel to nearby health facilities. According to HNO 2021 Health sector report: the average distance to the nearest health facility was 14 km. People often spent more money on travel costs than on health care itself. This practice led to a decrease in the number of visits to the health facilities in late 2020.The need is even more persistent considering the outbreaks of other non-communicable and communicable diseases. A total of 77% of assessed communities reported outbreaks of diseases such as diarrhea, lice and scabies and influenza-like illnesses according to 2021 HNO. However, what makes the situation in Libya even more urgent is the absence of a national or a country-level corrective strategy and planning to implement the necessary response in order to minimize the current risk of a bigger crisis threatening the entire Libyan community. Indeed, to prevent another humanitarian crisis, urgent and direct solutions need to be implemented as soon as possible, health systems must be enhanced to control the present wide infection of the COVID-19 mainly and other communicable diseases, isolation centers and public laboratories equipped with the required equipment and consumables. Moreover, health centers and health care facilities need to be urgently rehabilitated and equipped for a preventive care for communicable diseases, particularly with hygiene and test kits. In addition to that, medical staff in most of the HCFs need capacity building in order to maintain the continuity of essential health services

2 - Activities

Based on an integrated Health, WASH and Protection approach, and with its expertise gained through the rehabilitation of WASH public infrastructure in the urban areas in south Libya, WWGVC will focus on the rehabilitation of health facilities under SPHERE guidelines and international WASH standards.Focusing mainly on the areas with the highest number of affected people in Western and Southern Libya, the action will aim to upgrade the health facilities infrastructure and working conditions through the improvement of the sanitation and sewage systems, PWD accessibility, water and electricity supplies and hygiene protocols especially in relation with COVID19 prevention.Primary health care facilities, Emergency Units and national laboratories with non-functional vital services and a high flow of daily patients will be targeted on a primary level. The intervention will also include the provision of medical equipment and supplies to safely deliver services, such as PPE kits for the staff, general medical equipment, and laboratory materials. In order to strengthen the health system capacity and delivered services, the projects will include capacity building trainings for the medical staff based on a ToT approach to cover a larger number of beneficiaries. The trainings will strengthen health providers capacities and resilience to absorb and respond to the current needs with topics related to behavioral change when working with people at risks, focusing on their engagement with the community and including the vulnerable groups like IDPs and refugees, with an emphasis on protection and GBV to ensure equitable access to quality health care services.WWGVC also aims to work on a larger scale by reaching the Libyan community in need and creating awareness campaigns where Libyans can access sufficient knowledge related to basic prevention against communicable diseases such as COVID-19.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

The project will be accountable to the affected population at each phase of the cycle project thanks to GVC’s Community Protection Approach (CPA; https://cpainitiative.org/). Population engagement is a process of empowerment, following the Ladder of Citizen Participation. Different AGD groups will be identified based on a sampling approach and classified according to a scale of prioritization. Attention will be paid to ensure that underrepresented groups are considered. A selection committee will be composed of the heads of targeted facilities, community representatives and GVC and its implementing partner Migrace staff to design the project. They will also be included in the design of the M&E tools. The beneficiaries will be included in the implementation of the activities (ToT of Community mobilizers; Personal from targeted facilities; FGDs during the implementation for potential readjustments etc.). A complaint mechanism will be put in place: 1) Complaint boxes made available in each targeted structure/community; 2) On the GVC project management platform, an online complaints form, in English and Arabic will be accessible. This platform has been developed in in compliance with the GDPR and the principles of PIM; 3) During the monitoring visits of the M&E officer, s/he will may will complaints with beneficiaries (i.e. including PwD, people who are illiterate and marginalized groups). A mechanism for identification, assessment and referral of families and individuals ensures meaningful access to assistance and services. A final evaluation will measure the impact of the intervention. An exit strategy will assure the durability of the project and the re-appropriation by the beneficiaries and the stakeholders. At last, GVC Libya will pay attention to apply GVC Policy such as the Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy, PSEAH. A reporting mechanism for cases of violence and/or abuse will be set up. GVC also aims at using the Common Feedback Mechanism.
