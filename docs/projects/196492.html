Project Summary

White Hands (WH) proposes a 12-month action using a flexible integrated programming approach that includes NFI distribution, using pre-positioned in-kind and cash/voucher assistance modalities, construction of dignified shelters with integrated infrastructure improvements, and basic shelter repairs.
WH will contribute to the two objectives of the SNFI sector, which are:
SO1) Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support.
SO2) Reinforce an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure.
The project formulation has been based on needs identified by the SNFI cluster, supported with RNA conducted by WH in Nov 2022 and firsthand consultations with camps management and local authorities/stakeholders.
The proposed project aims to address emergency and more sustainable shelter and NFI needs of vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and affected host communities with acute needs living in Afrin, Atareb, Jebel Saman, Daret Azza, Azaz, Aghtrin, and Mare sub-districts (Aleppo) in addition to, Bennsh, Idleb, Ariha, Mhambal, Armanaz, Dana and Harim sub-districts (Idleb) in NWS.
WH’s shelter & NFI program recognizes the different needs and vulnerabilities of boys, girls, men, and women of different ages and strives to address the specific needs of each group across the range of shelter and NFIs interventions.
Considering their vulnerability, IDPs living in hard-to-reach locations, female-headed HHs, PWDs, and elderly persons at risk will form the priority groups of WH’s shelter & NFI program, particularly among the high numbers of new IDPs & returnees requiring core Shelter & NFIs assistance.
WH’s NFI response aims to assist 2,000 HHs/10,000 individuals with a one-off distribution of emergency NFIs kits, including cash/voucher for NFI, and 6,000 HHs/30,000 individuals with the provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs including heating fuel including cash for winter, to heat their shelter during the winter season 2023.
WH’s dignified shelter solutions aim to:
(1) provide essential infrastructure rehabilitation in prioritized IDP camps that may include site upgrades, graveling main and sub-roads, rain drainage system & channels, paving paths between tents, and lighting on main roads and pathways as needed.
(2) Provision of dignified shelter solution by piloting an innovative shelter approach that comprises steel metal frame and sandwich panels to accommodate 500 HHs/2,500 individuals with all accessible features for HHs with PWDs to ensure accessibility, privacy, and safety for all vulnerable groups.
These components will be guided by Action Plan for Dignified Shelter & Living Conditions in NW Syria and Dignified Shelter Technical Annex published by the SNFI cluster in March and April 2022, respectively as well as TWiG on Flood Risk Reduction 2021 and ensure that HLP documentation is well carried out in parallel with field coordination with the relevant local councils.
Equally, the basic shelter repairs component will enhance sustainable, recovery-oriented shelter solutions through support to IDPs returning and returnees to their original homes and contribute to the rehabilitation of buildings in newly accessible areas. This will include assisting 500 HHs/2,500 individuals by rehabilitation of damaged houses.
This component will be guided by the shelter rehabilitation guidance published by the SNFI cluster in 2018 and ensure that HLP documentation is well carried out in parallel with field coordination with the relevant local councils.
The program will seek feedback from both male & female beneficiaries and fully interact with both genders to ensure equitable access & equal opportunities.
The overall target number of beneficiaries is 45,000 individuals for 2023. 40,000 individuals provided with NFIs, 2,500 individuals supported through dignified shelter solutions, and 2,500 individuals assisted by basic shelter repairs.

3 - Needs

According to the SNFI HNO 2023, 5.5 M PiN for Shelter, 4.7 M PiN for NFI. In accordance with the MSNA 2022 findings, 34% of the overall population live in ‘sub-standard’ shelters, of which 19% reside in finished residential buildings classified as damaged and/ or displaying ‘adequacy issues.’ Lack of access to electricity/lighting, insulation from heat and cold, deterioration of shelter integrity (notable increase in damages to windows/doors), in addition to lack of privacy are among the top shelter adequacy issues identified.With funding shortages currently at a historic low, the NFI sectors’ limited response in 2022 is further expected to exacerbate needs as 2023 unfolds. Harsh winter conditions further affect the most vulnerable, and access to basic NFIs is needed to ensure that health, well-being, and protection concerns are met. Key NFI items such as winter clothing, thermal blankets, and heaters are essential and accessible yet unaffordable to most individuals.According to Action Plan for Dignified Shelter & Living Conditions in NW Syria published by the SNFI cluster in March 2022, in NW Syria, there are 2.2 M people in need of shelter assistance, 1.7 M people live in camps, and unplanned, self-settled IDP sites, 800,000 people live in tents with 90% of tents are more than one year old, 80% of people living in IDP sites are women and children, 53% of households report having a member with a disability, and 83% of IDPs live in ‘critically overcrowded’ conditions.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from the property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitated. Likelihood: H/Impact: H. MS: Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by SNFI cluster, don't proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed & Have an HLP section in the M&E assessment form. Risk2: lack of approval from property owner which may prevent from implementing the activity. Likelihood: H/Impact: H MS: No start of the activity in the camp without obtaining approval from the landowner or the stakeholders, Include HLP documentation into the MoUs or agreements signed. Risk3: Risk of conducting rehabilitation of building with poor structural integrity and/or not fully cleared from land-mines and ERWs. MS: Conduct a thorough shelter assessment & infrastructural technical assessment of the buildings that are aimed to be rehabilitated, Coordinate the interventions with local authorities, Ensure that ERWs clearance is conducted if needs be, Check with other actors working in the same area to understand what kind of issues they encountered. Risk4: Risk of exclusion from SNFI assistance due to lack of civil status document/ID. MS: Work closely with communities, community leaders & authorities to identify an alternative way of identification.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk1: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. MS: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, Train staff on code of conduct, Code of conduct in all staff contracts & Address any SEA complaints. Risk2: The risks of GBV can be compounded by overcrowding and lack of privacy. In multi-household dwellings, lack of doors and partitions for sleeping and changing clothes can increase exposure to sexual harassment and assault. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. MS: Advocate for appropriate and adequate assistance to be provided. We will include women, girls, and other at-risk groups to determine such things as if systems/criteria are in place to determine how shelters are being allocated; if they are built for safety and privacy; and/or if shelter materials and shelter-related NFIs are being distributed in areas that are safe, Consultations with women, girls, boy, and men should be organized & community safety audits. Risk3: Crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender-segregated sanitation facilities can lead to an increase in GBV and sexual exploitation as well as communicable diseases. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. MS: provision of gender-segregated sanitation and hygiene facilities sufficiently close to dwellings, provision of lighting around shelter and WASH facilities, and work closely with protection and other sectors.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: WH has full access to the targeted communities & the beneficiaries regularly throughout the life cycle of the project. Risk: Escalation of conflict exacerbates insecurity in the targeted area affecting the WH’s ability to operate and access it. Likelihood: M/Impact: H MS: WH will commit to ongoing monitoring to ensure theoperational context is secure enough to ensure access to the targeted communities, purchase of materials from the local market with guarantee the Do No Harm principles, communicate, coordinate, and consult intensively with LCs, stakeholders, and other NGOs operating in the area, re-direction of programming into other areas. Assumption2: The activities can be completed without delay. Risk:-Adverse weather conditions will affect the implementation of rehabilitation works, and at certain times all site works may be stopped. Probability: H/Impact: M MS: A fast track approach shall be followed to maximize the implementation of the rehabilitation works period, so there will be a flexible schedule. The rehabilitation works will be divided into 3 parts to be performed simultaneously by WH, hence ensuring fast delivery before winter arrives around November. Risk3: Spread of COVID-19 pandemic cases in NW Syria. Probability: M/Impact: M MS3: 1. Follow strict preventive measures, building on the SNFI guidelines and recommendations. Those will be reflected in each activity. 2. All project teams and staff will receive sensitization on COVID-19 precautions.
