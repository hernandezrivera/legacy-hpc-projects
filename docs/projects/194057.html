Project Summary

In North Darfur, GOAL works closely with the Ministries of Agriculture and Animal Resources in Khartoum and in North Darfur as well as FAO on identifying needs/gaps, coverage and program implementation. In addition, GOAL remains an active participating in the FSL Cluster at both national and state levels where FSL programming is implemented, including North Darfur. GOAL’s FSL programming is closely integrated with
other sectors, especially nutrition, health, and protection through targeting vulnerable households (including affected by malnutrition, people living with disabilities, and women headed households) in catchment areas of supported clinics. GOAL will also incorporate protection and women’s empowerment approaches. GOAL proposes to focus on the IDP populations in Kutum Town as well as Kassab and Fata Borno Camps near Kutum to reach vulnerable host communities in Kutum and Al Waha localities to ensure all relevant communities have their needs addressed. Activities are closely coordinated with other FSL actors in North Darfur to eliminate any duplication of effort. Through the FSL Cluster and Cash Working
Groups. GOAL incorporates and shares lessons learned and harmonized approaches to livelihoods activities in order to maintain high technical standards in its work. In South Kordofan, GOAL will work with Village Development Committee (VDC) in areas where they are already established and functional. If the VDC are not available, GOAL will work community leaders to form a VDC consisting of community leaders, including influential community members (such as teachers, health workers, religious leaders etc.) as selected by
community members. A close effort will be made to ensure that gender representation is observed in the composition of VDC members. GOAL will identify and support existing Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) in the targeted areas to provide training and ongoing monitoring and care for the distributed animals. GOAL will provide refresher training to CAHWs and facilitate their work, in particular by supporting their transportation to rural and IDPs areas, as the lack of vehicles or resources for transport has been identified as a major challenge to effective support for animal welfare services across South Kordofan. GOAL has identified that displaced households in North Darfur and South Kordofan are facing multiple shocks including the looming drought that will severely lead to limited livelihoods, which has come on top of economic erosion, failed harvests that are stretching the lean season, tribal violence in South Kordofan, and displacement. In response, GOAL plans to target 800 households with Multi-Purpose Cash (MPC) transfers in order to meet immediate food security needs and to prevent further deterioration of assets and use of more harmful coping strategies, and to put households in a better position to engage in productive livelihoods in the future.

1 - Needs

GOAL assessments in 2021 have found adoption of irreversible coping strategies for maintaining survival in the face of growing acute food insecurity among the community members. The average price of the local food basket increased by nearly 200 per cent compared to 2020, according to the World Food Programme (WFP). The inflation is pushing up prices of basic food, like sorghum, which is now 240 per cent higher than one year ago and more than 680 per cent higher than the five-year average. Spiraling inflation is expected to continue affecting staple food prices. In addition, prices of agricultural inputs increased dramatically. Both have not only affected vulnerable and impoverished communities, but also aid agencies (including WFP), which have had to reduce the number of people they can support with assistance. In March 2021, the price of 100 kg bag of sorghum was the equivalent of 20 USD, compared to 44 USD in March 2022 which reflects an increase of 141%. In the same period, a 100 kg bag of millet was 58 USD compared to 81 USD in March 2022. The price of a basic plough was 29 USD in 2021 while in 2022 the cost has increased to 37 USD. Hence inflation has affected vulnerable households’ production capacity and purchasing power for basic needs. GOAL’s food security and basic needs interventions are built on its experience implementing agricultural and livestock support programming and are consistently integrated with nutrition through targeting of vulnerable households. Agricultural programming in South Kordofan targets smallholders who have access to their land to farm, which will include both IDPs and returnees. GOAL will also continue support for existing beekeeping groups, under this award targeting women in the households to produce a variety of products with wax and honey to rebuild livelihoods. For multipurpose cash interventions, GOAL will prioritize the poorest and most vulnerable IDPs and other conflict-affected households, who lack access to agriculture, productive assets or other means to meet their basic needs, also focusing on those affected by malnutrition, disabilities, or vulnerable elderly households. Multipurpose Cash Assistance activities will be coordinated through the FSL Cluster and the Cash Working Group, and GOAL will work closely with the Ministry of Social Affairs (women and family department in North Darfur and South Kordofan who provide beneficiary lists for verification and support. GOAL remains an active participant in the FSL Cluster at both national and state levels closely integrated with nutrition, health, and protection through targeting vulnerable IDP households (including affected by malnutrition, people with disabilities, and women-headed households) in catchment areas of supported clinics. Activities are closely coordinated with other FSL actors in North Darfur to eliminate any duplication of effort. Through the FSL Cluster and Cash Working Groups, GOAL incorporates, and shares lessons learned and harmonized approaches to livelihoods activities in order to maintain high technical standards in its work.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

GOAL Sudan incorporates protection principles in every aspect of programming andpromoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian action while makingsure that response is participatory and that it is accountable to the people GOAL seeksto assist. For GOAL Sudan, protection mainstreaming is crucial both internally andexternally to ensure that extremely vulnerable groups including children, women,people with disabilities and the elderly can access services and are protected fromboth intentional and unintentional harm. GOAL has ensured that all staff and partnersare aware of protection issues through training, and protection mainstreaming toolshave been used in programming and internally to ensure the safety of the extremelyvulnerable groups. Throughout its intervention GOAL is promoting gender equality andpromoting beliefs and norms that foster respectful, non-violent gender norms to helpprevent and mitigate GBV. Safety, respect, confidentiality and non-discrimination inrelation to survivors and those at risk are vital considerations for GOAL.
