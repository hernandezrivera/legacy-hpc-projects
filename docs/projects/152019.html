Project Summary

From ROHI's previous project implementation and the field knowledge in Borno state, children of Mobbar, Damboa, Dikwa and Ngala LGAs have been identified to be among those that have been significantly affected by armed conflict in recent years and were often the direct targets of acts intended to cause maximum civilian casualties and terrorize communities, including by depicting children as “executioners” or forcing them to be suicide bombers and spies.
In addition to being recruited and used by Boko Haram children are often systematically treated as security threats rather than as victims, and are administratively detained for their alleged association. Since 2015 children were detained for alleged association with Boko in Maiduguri by the Nigerian army and only few have been released following thorough intelligence screening and investigation. Depriving children of liberty following their separation is contrary not only to the best interests of the child, but also to the best interests of society as a whole. This approach further complicates efforts to reintegrate children, given that it separates them from their families and can also lead to the creation of community grievances.
Despite the progress made by child protection actors across different LGAs of Borno State, some gaps and needs remained due to the high influx of people on daily basis from the remote villages after military clearance and operations. There is an urgent need to strengthen the system of Case management especially to UASC, and the community-based system of identification and acceptance of CAAFAG and children released and escaped from both military detention and JAS and provide them with sustainable socio-economic reintegration services linked to their individual needs and services availability, aimed at reducing their vulnerability to other grave violations of their rights but also to address violations that already occurred to them. ROHI Community Volunteers will work in collaboration with Child Protection actors in different LGAs of Borno State. ROHI will use CPIMS tools developed by UNICEF and currently being used by other partners. This will ensure standardized approach to data collection and information management for CAAFAG and vulnerable children including UASC, child at risk etc. Identification needs to be strengthened by ensuring that case workers apply the definitions CAAFAG within the local context and culture and account for varying levels of vulnerability. This understanding includes the formal and informal association of children with armed groups including the CJTF and local vigilante groups and also those rescued, released and escaped from armed groups.
Through this project, ROHI with the support of CPSWG and other stakeholders proposes to implement a broad-based programme using six main strategies. These include:
i) Continue using the LGA-based actors and networks to strengthen the monitoring, verification and reporting of incidents of grave violations against children;
ii) Continue building the capacities of community actors on MRM and CAAFAG programming;
iii) Putting in place community-based mechanisms to prevent the recruitment and use of children by armed groups;
iv) supporting initiatives for non-formal release of children from armed groups;
v) Providing short-term reinsertion support (packages) to CAAFAG and children released from military detention; and
vi) Providing economic reintegration and case Management services based on age and gender sensitivities of the CAAFAG and other highly vulnerable children including children in CJTF.

Needs

The insurgency in Borno State has led to the prolific recruitment and use of children by armed groups including Boko Haram and Civilian Joint Task Force (CJTF).It is estimated that more than 8000 boys and girls are associated with Boko Haram and the plan is to provide reintegration assistance to more than 2500. UNICEF’s recent assessment in Borno state noted that many of the children within the Boko Haram are in fact the biological offspring of Boko Haram members. This adds a new dimension to reintegration efforts, as these children may not be able to return to their nuclear families.ROHI’s previous project implementation and the field knowledge in Borno state, children of Konduga, Mafa, Gwoza, Dikwa and Ngala LGAs have been identified to be among those that have been significantly affected by armed conflict in recent years and were often the direct targets of acts intended to cause maximum civilian casualties and terrorize communities, including by depicting children as “executioners” or forcing them to be suicide bombers and spies. In addition to being recruited and used by Boko Haram children are often systematically treated as security threats rather than as victims, and are administratively detained for their alleged association. Since 2015, more than 480 children were detained for alleged association with Boko in Maiduguri by the Nigerian army and only about 150 have been released following thorough intelligence screening and investigation. Depriving children of liberty following their separation is contrary not only to the best interests of the child, but also to the best interests of society as a whole. This approach further complicates efforts to reintegrate children, given that it separates them from their families and can also lead to the creation of community grievances.Though it is the role of the relevant State Ministries to provide protection services to displaced children through identification and case management, health care, high-quality education and psychosocial support, significant capacity gaps have been highlighted and assistance has been requested. It is therefore imperative that the humanitarian community scale-up child protection work for this highly vulnerable group and other groups at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse and neglect, abduction both in terms of immediate direct support for CAAFAG and capacity building of community structures and the Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development (MWASD) (also referred to as “Ministry” hereafter) to be able to fulfill their mandate.
