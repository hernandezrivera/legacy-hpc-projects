Project Summary

This project seeks to address critical Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) needs in displacement-affected communities, focusing on returning families and individuals to help them achieve durable solutions and overcome the constraints of displacement. Through a comprehensive community-level resource assessment, NRC team, in close collaboration with the National Institute for Disaster Management (INGD) and local authorities, will identify gaps in pre- and post-conflict resources. This assessment will uncover vital HLP needs, creating a foundation for both immediate assistance and long-term programming while facilitating connections and referrals to other NRC and support programs.
The resource assessment findings will guide the finalization of the HLP framework and tools, especially in areas with significant loss and damage issues, sustainable housing requirements, land regularization needs, and property rights concerns. With a focus on aligning with INGD's regional risk reduction strategies, the project will prioritize underserved areas with limited intervention by other actors. This targeted approach maximizes the positive impact and potential for community resilience through sustainable HLP solutions.
A critical component of this intervention is the reactivation of collaborative dispute resolution (CDR) mechanisms via community courts in areas of return. These courts, distinct from traditional legal systems, will consist of impartial mediators, trained facilitators, and community members, allowing for culturally sensitive and inclusive conflict resolution. Men, women, and marginalized groups will participate actively in these courts, ensuring diverse representation and input. Trained facilitators will lead the process to ensure fairness and alignment with Mozambican law (Lei 4/92 de 6 de Maio), equipping local mediators and volunteers to manage community disputes.
Through this community-centered approach, the reactivated courts will empower individuals to manage their own conflicts, fostering a strong sense of ownership, social cohesion, and collaboration. The intervention will not only provide an accessible alternative to formal legal channels but also strengthen community capacity for self-sustained conflict resolution. By promoting inclusive participation and culturally aligned solutions, these courts often lead to longer-lasting resolutions that address underlying causes of disputes.
The project also establishes a comprehensive roadmap for NRC’s future HLP programming, contributing to the long-term stability and resilience of communities transitioning from displacement to reintegration

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project will focus on the districts of Pemba, Metuge, Mueda, Muidumbe, Mocimboa da Praia, Nangade, Meluco, and Quissanga. In these areas, priority activities will include providing both collective and individual HLP legal assistance to returning families and host communities, helping them secure property documentation and resolve land tenure issues. This effort will be complemented by HLP due diligence exercises, where the project team will engage with host communities to verify land ownership and prevent disputes, thus fostering trust and cooperation.To address conflicts, the project will establish community dispute resolution courts with trained facilitators, impartial mediators, and diverse community representatives to manage land-related disputes. These courts will ensure culturally sensitive resolutions in alignment with Mozambican law. Additionally, one-on-one legal counseling sessions will be offered to individuals seeking guidance on their HLP rights and available support services.The project will also carry out awareness-raising initiatives through community and door-to-door information sessions, as well as the distribution of Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials to educate communities on their HLP rights. Special attention will be given to training community leaders through capacity-building sessions, equipping them with the skills to advocate for land rights and support dispute resolution efforts. Advocacy engagements will be conducted to influence policy changes and improve HLP rights protections, particularly focusing on vulnerable groups in these targeted districts. Continuous monitoring and evaluation of these activities will ensure their effectiveness and adaptability to local needs, promoting sustainable HLP solutions and enhancing community resilience.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project has a strong focus on protection, recognizing the vulnerability of displacement-affected communities and the potential risks that may arise during the implementation of activities. Given the context of conflict, displacement, and loss of property, it is essential to proactively identify potential risks and implement mitigation measures to avoid causing harm.One of the primary risks the project faces is discrimination and exclusion, particularly affecting women, the elderly, and marginalized groups. These communities are often left out of decision-making processes or denied their land and property rights due to prevailing social norms or gendered inequalities. The project will ensure that these groups are actively included in all activities, particularly in legal assistance and community dispute resolution, to avoid any form of exclusion.Increased tensions and conflict is another significant risk. The provision of legal assistance and support for land and property claims, especially in post-conflict areas, can lead to disputes between returning families and host communities. This can be exacerbated if land ownership is unclear or contested. The project will work to address these tensions by creating spaces for dialogue and ensuring that conflict resolution is carried out in a fair, transparent, and culturally sensitive manner. This includes using community dispute resolution mechanisms led by trained facilitators who can mediate land-related conflicts equitably.Lack of community ownership and participation could undermine the project’s effectiveness. If the activities are imposed without involving community members, there may be resistance or lack of sustained impact. To avoid this, the project will adopt a highly participatory approach, engaging communities in all stages of the project, from assessment to design and implementation. This approach ensures that the interventions are rooted in local realities and that community members, including women and the elderly, have an active role in decision-making processes.Inadequate security and protection for vulnerable participants is another potential risk, particularly for women and children, who may be exposed to retaliation or harassment due to their involvement in land and property dispute resolution. The project will implement comprehesinve protection measures, such as providing secure spaces for discussions, ensuring confidentiality, and offering protection for individuals at risk of violence or discrimination.Finally, there is a risk that the project’s approach may not be sufficiently culturally and gender-sensitive, particularly when implementing legal or dispute resolution mechanisms that may conflict with local traditions or gender norms. The project will ensure that all activities are designed and implemented with deep respect for local customs and traditions. Special efforts will be made to ensure that women and the elderly are not sidelined in discussions, decisions, or conflict resolution processes.To address these risks, the project will emphasize a rights-based, gender-sensitive, and inclusive approach, where all stakeholders are encouraged to participate equally. Safeguards will be put in place to protect vulnerable individuals, and continuous monitoring will allow for the identification of risks and the implementation of corrective measures when necessary. Through this comprehensive risk analysis and mitigation strategy, the project aims to avoid harm and provide protection to the most vulnerable members of the community.The project is committed to ensuring that all affected individuals, particularly women, elderly people, and marginalized groups, have meaningful access to assistance and are empowered throughout the implementation process. 6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The proposed project recognizes that people with disabilities face unique challenges, particularly in displacement-affected communities. These individuals often experience multiple layers of vulnerability, especially in post-conflict settings where infrastructure and support services may be limited or inaccessible. The project is committed to ensuring that people with disabilities are not left behind and that they have equal access to all assistance and protection services provided by the project. The first step to achieving this is through a comprehensive process of identifying people with disabilities within the target communities, followed by a focused effort to remove barriers that prevent their participation in project activities.Identifying people with disabilities begins with raising awareness at the community level. The project will engage in sensitization campaigns designed to educate community members, both in displacement areas and host communities, about various types of disabilities. These campaigns will provide information on physical, intellectual, sensory, and mental health-related disabilities, helping people identify those in need of support. Furthermore, the project will incorporate disability-specific questions into its initial needs assessments and baseline surveys, ensuring that data is disaggregated by disability type and that all community members are accurately represented in the project’s planning.Another significant barrier is physical accessibility. The project will make every effort to ensure that all physical locations where activities occur—such as community centers, legal counseling sessions, and dispute resolution courts—are fully accessible to people with mobility impairments. In cases where people with disabilities are unable to travel to a central location, the project will deploy mobile legal support teams and outreach services to deliver assistance directly to individuals in their homes or local communities, reducing the need for travel and ensuring that no one is excluded due to mobility challenges.To ensure that all individuals, including those with disabilities, receive comprehensive support, the project will also establish strong referral systems. People with disabilities will be referred to specialized services such as healthcare, rehabilitation, and assistive devices, ensuring they have access to the full range of services necessary for their well-being. Case management services will be provided to track the progress of individuals with disabilities and ensure they receive the support they need throughout their involvement in the project.
