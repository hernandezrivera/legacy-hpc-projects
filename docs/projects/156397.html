Project Summary

1. Ensure lifesaving preventive nutrition services for girls and boys under five and Pregnant and lactating women.
2. Support the nutritional situation of the host communities and IDPs in northern Syria
3. Monitor, strengthen and report ongoing activities through advanced surveillance system in North Syria (Idleb & Aleppo)
These objectives are planned to reach through capacity building of health facility and community workers on appropriate IYCF practices to cover 20,000 PLWs , management of complicated SAM (500 children with SAM and 100 with complicated SAM) and nutrition surveillance, and early childhood development. Support facility and community based screening (20,000 PLWs and 350,000 children) for acute and chronic malnutrition and referral via training and anthropometric equipment. Improve the treatment of severely acutely malnourished children with complications at hospital (100) level via supporting with training, nutrition therapeutic supplies and equipment. Support facility and community based counseling and awareness raising on IYCF-E and optimal maternal nutrition. IYCF practices are planned to cover the whole area of intervention in Idleb, Hama and Aleppo (north and west), while other activities will be focused in Northern Aleppo (Azaz, Jarablus and Afrin) with limited presence in Aleppo (Atareb)

Needs

The nutrition sector estimated 4.7 million girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women are at risk of under-nutrition and in need of preventive and curative nutrition services in 2018. 92,000 girls and boys aged 6-59 months are acutely under-nourished, of which around 19,000 are in the severe category and are 11 times more likely to die than well-nourished children. 865,295 girls and boys under five years of age suffer from micro-nutrient deficiencies. 3.05 million girls and boys under 5 years of age require optimal feeding for adequate nutrition status while 1.6 million pregnant and lactating women require preventive nutrition services.QC through the nutrition surveillance system will continue to monitor return movements to plan and implement the response for nutrition services.Across north of Syria, more than 20 sub-districts are found to be facing critical problems and in need of immediate humanitarian assistance (these sub-districts are located in Aleppo, Al Hasakeh, Ar Raqqa, and Idleb).Therefore, the affected population living in sub-districts of categories 4 and 5 should be prioritized for acute and immediate humanitarian response. Although the level of global acute malnutrition among under five boys and girls in the majority of the assessed locations was found to be within acceptable levels. IYCF practices in assessed locations remain sub-optimal and are all below pre-conflict levels. In light of ongoing displacement, anticipated returns, problematic access to health services and food, high food prices, diminishing employment opportunities and low service coverage nutrition partners are forecasting more under-nutrition cases in conflict affected areas.

Protection Risk Analysis

The Whole of Syria (WoS) conducted a review to identify the most pressing nutrition related protection risks facing healthcare and nutrition providers and the affected population. A set of recommended mitigating measures was identified to guide health and nutrition agencies and operational partners during the process of program design of the 2019 Humanitarian Program Cycle (HPC). * Gender Based Violence GBV:Mitigating measures:- Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention.- Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals.- Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health and nutrition services. * Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA:Mitigating measures:- Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance.- Effective and comprehensive mechanisms should be established to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic.- Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff. * Duty of care:Mitigating measures:- Staff Care programs which provide psychosocial support services and resources to staff.- Measures ensuring regular and consistent delivery of salaries, bearing in mind the ethical and logistical imperative to go further than basic labor rights. * Provision of Healthcare and nutritional services for Vulnerable Groups and Equitable Access to all Services:Mitigating measures:- Implementation of standard essential services package across the sector interventions, including improved quality reporting and robust monitoring through standardized SOPs.- Addressing the specific needs of vulnerable groups through supporting dedicated nutrition subgroups groups (IYCF, CMAM, Capacity building, etc.).

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Through all project locations, environmental risks (floods, earthquakes, etc.) may have an effect on implementation of the activities in the case that a major disaster happen in one of the areas of implementation. The likelihood of this risk to happen is low. While the impact on project activities will be very high. QC through its staff, will have a preparedness plan and mitigation mechanisms in all the activities and this to be shared with all staff and schools’ principals. In this intervention, QC assume that the implementation of the activities will go smoothly in all targeted locations. In regard to this, some risks might be beyond QC control. 1) The reinforcement of health facilities does not provide sufficient protection for patients, exposing them to physical harm (some nutrition activities are provided within health facilities).* These can be mitigated by:- putting in place task shifting guidelines as part of care protocols- providing Training and adequate follow- up of competency- decentralizing services, clear messaging to communities around risks associated with health care facilities and underground care2) Increased malnutrition rate due to increasing the opportunity of diseases and medical conditions that needs professional intervention.* Mitigating measure:- Hire a qualified medical staff- Distribute nutritional supplies and rations. After Sushi treaty between Russia and Turkey, QC assumes that areas out of Syrian government control in north-west of Syria will be stable for the whole next year. The risk of increasing pressure on health services influx of IDPs is very high. Program activities will be negatively impacted when the number of IDPs some areas suddenly increases.
