Project Summary

The project aims at directly improving the food security and livelihoods of 18,396 individuals including IDPs and armed conflict effected persons, including persons with disabilities in Algeneina locality, West Darfur. The 18,396 adult individuals include 8,646 women and 9,750 men.
The project will provide food and livelihoods assistance through the most appropriate modalities including in-kind and or through cash-based transfers. The project will also provide emergency agriculture life-saving assistance to ensure continued food production throughout the upcoming rainy season which will be followed by the lean season. Thus, there will be timely emergency life-saving livelihood assistance in the form of crop seeds and tools to facilitate the affected population`s plans of planting during or soon after the rains in addition to restocking pastoralists with livestock and veterinary inputs and extension services for secured and diverse food production while safeguarding livelihoods sources.

1 - Needs

The fighting that erupted between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) in April 15, 2023 has continued into its second month.The conflict has escalated in Ag Geneina, West Darfur and resulted in the displacement of at least 85,000 people and left other civilians in the locality without access to health care, water and basic supplies. Thus, the state is expected to continue experiencing high humanitarian food assistance needs through the rest of 2023. Reports by OCHA, IOM, Save the Children and other INNGO Forum members organizations, in addition to remote interviews with communities FPDO had worked with, showed that all 86 gathering sites for displaced people throughout Ag Geneina have been burnt to the ground, forcing people into secondary displacement in neighborhoods in the northern parts of the town. Many are sheltering with relatives and friends, while others are taking refuge in public buildings or staying in the streets. Food production, supply chain disruptions, fuel shortages, inflation and currency depreciation have led to shortages and price hikes, making essential goods unaffordable for the majority of the project target populations. Also imported staples such as wheat flour, oil and tomato paste are scarce. WFP expects that the number of acutely food insecure will increase to 19.1 million people in the next 3-6 months, if the conflict persists and the people of West Darfur state have been mentioned among those from other states such as West Kordofan, Blue Nile, Red Sea and North Darfur. The level of food insecurity and lack of livelihoods opportunities among other needs in West Darfur increased by 26 percent following the clashes. The security situation in West Darfur is volatile, and the state has been affected by intercommunal and ethnic conflicts resulting in the increase of internally displaced persons and loss of property, livelihoods and lives. Pastoralists moving their herds to Central Darfur due to conflict caused damage to crops in some areas along their routes, which negatively impacted agricultural production. This year`s agricultural product during the rainy season in West Darfur is expected to be lower than the average figure in the traditional rainfed sector in the 2021/22 season which was 61 percent below the five-year average (88,000 tons compared to 223,000 tons) (CFSVA report). Also the poor harvest would further the shift in primary livelihood activities, from agricultural wage labor, which declined from 10 to 5 percent, to non-agricultural wage labor, which increased from 18 to 30 percent; the prevalence of food-based coping strategies would increased over the then estimated 12 percent and livelihood-based coping strategies would exceed 14 percent (from 53 to 67 percent) (CFSVA-2021/22). These factors underpin the worsening food security situation in West Darfur.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project aim to providing an urgent response for displacement affected population including relocated IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in Ag Geneina, west Darfur. To reduce mortality and morbidity among its targets, the activities of the project will include the provision of life-saving and dietary food items package (i.e. rice, dried okra (Weekah), sorghum, cooking oil, onion, peans, sugar, tea, coffee, powdered milk) to 5400 individuals from both displaced persons and host communities in the targeted parts of the locality. These households will also be provided with non-food items (household supplies) to ensure healthy cooking and intake of the provided food items and improved hygiene practices at the household level. Utilizing cash and market-based approaches, FPDO will provide other 3000 individuals vulnerable persons representing burdened households with multi-purpose cash assistance (with special focus on women and young girls), in addition to the provision of agricultural support (seeds/tools) to 6996 small farmers. The project will further include the distribution of livestock (04 goats) to 3000 individuals representing pastoralist households as support to vulnerable, women headed house hold and disabilities and other at risk groups in targeted locality.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will conduct climate vulnerability assessment that examined climate vulnerability with a focus on the implications of vulnerability to climate change on the environment, energy and food security situation of targeted communities in West Darfor. The assessment identifies the climate/environmental risks based on observed climatic trends and variability that have contributed to the vulnerability of the target areas. Understanding of the vegetation dynamics is expected to help for better planning of livelihood activities to a wide array of beneficiaries in the area where the majority of the population depends on natural vegetation and agriculture to secure their livelihood.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

As part of its commitment to protecting the rights of the targeted populations (men, women, boys, girls and people with special needs) FPDO will adopt non-discriminatory practices across all activities, encompassing gender and age considerations. FPDO will also ensure that protection concerns of at-risk groups will be taken into account during the implementation and that can be through focus group discussions with diverse gender and age demographics throughout the lifecycle of the Project. Any collected and analyzed data will be disaggregated by age and gender to ensure equal access to the project`s assistance. FPDO is an active member of the Protection Sector, as well as Gender Based Violence and Child Protection subsector working groups at country and state levels, the Protection Technical Advisory Group (TAG) under the Refugee Consultation Forum, the Counter Trafficking in Persons/Mixed Migration Working Group and the Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Network. Participation in these platforms will enhance collaboration, coordination and learning to incorporate best relevant practices within our operations. In particular, information shared by the Protection Sector, including the Gender-Based Violence, is analyzed and incorporated within project.
