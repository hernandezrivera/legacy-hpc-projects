Project Summary

The overall goal of the proposed project is to contribute to improvement in conditions in IDP sites through strengthening the predictability and effectiveness of service provision and protection for displaced populations, with the aim to reach durable solutions through strengthened community self-management and in addition to that the project will strengthen coordination and information sharing between all stakeholders. Through this intervention, DRC will ensure that all displaced persons are enabled to partake meaningfully in the governance structures and management of the sites. This project will also encourage camp residents to partake community led care and maintenance activities aimed at improving safety and hygiene in sites, reducing protection and health risks. DRC will work with the camp management committees to facilitate site maintenance through cash-for-work while utilising members of the IDP community by enabling them to earn temporary income. Through this project, DRC will establish or strengthen an inclusive community governance structures and thematic committees at each site level, the engagement of these structures in CCCM coordination mechanisms alongside active humanitarian partners in the respective sites and representatives of relevant government and local authorities, as well as through continued support for capacity building, mainly through the delivery of trainings adapted to each type of participation (thematic IDP committee members, site governance members, local authority representatives). The proposed project will enhance community communication and feedback mechanisms through construction of community information centers, employing community outreach mobile teams, establishing complain feedback mechanisms, installation of information boards, and conducting awareness campaigns.
This project aims also to support opportunities for displaced people to achieve durable solutions, the project will contribute through community-based initiatives as well as support communities in IDP sites towards self-reliance in locations where prospects of obtaining land rights is achievable. The project aims to reach over 110,000 drought and conflict displaced women, boys, men and girls.

Project Activities

The following activities make up the proposed project or in other words, will be undertaken, in line with the objectives of the Somalia CCCM Cluster Strategy: Continue and strengthen coordination role in CCCM Sub-National Cluster structure, in Bosaso, Galkacyo and Beletweyn, with strong quality and timely reporting, utilization of coordination tools, consistent meeting facilitation with consistent documentation and actioning of issues and follow-up, and a strong commitment to advocacy. participation in detailed site assessments exercises in 9 Districts in Somalia; conducting three intention surveys (in Mogadishu, Baidao and Galkacyo); establishment of 9 mobile site management teams (one in each of the districts targeted) for identification and engagement with community governance structures, improved monitoring, coordination and support for site management; establishment/refining and training on CCCM principles and community engagement for 120 site committees and Establish and strengthen inclusive site-level community governance structures; training on CCCM principles for 150 government officials/local authorities; improvement and maintenance of 120 sites through cash for work, formation of site maintenance committees and provision of 2000 tool kits; installation of 100 solar lights in areas with high protection risks; distribution of 1,500 solar torches; construct and furnish 10 community centers; establishment of 40 outreach teams (outreach workers from the IDP communities) for information sharing and sensitization of beneficiaries on services and activities at site level; HLP awareness raising interventions targeting 80,000 individuals; training of duty bearers on HLP (20 committees); development of contingency plans for potential new IDP influxes in each of the targeted districts; advocacy with local authorities for public land for displaced people; mass sensitization through radio programming, sign boards, text campaigns in all targeted districts; sensitize on the use of DRC complaints and feedback mechanism for beneficiaries in targeted communities.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on Gender.Exclusion by gender and age is rife in Somalia, generally, women, girls and boys remain vulnerable more to protection risks and harmful cultural practices, GBV, child recruitment and hazardous child labor in times of conflict or climatic shocks. Their ability to enjoy rights and access services is often limited and weakened in case of displacement. Moreover, stigma related to sexual violence against women and girl often deters survivors from accessing assistance or reporting incidents. To address this exclusion based on gender and age, DRC will ensure that Protection is mainstreamed throughout all phases of the project cycle. Implementation of activities, including site maintenance and construction works, will be done allowing participation of different groups of people. DRC will pay special attention to vulnerable groups through consulting them during the designing of the constructions. Focused group discussions will be convened with women and girls to address their specific needs. Applied vulnerability criteria for prioritization of assistance will include single women, single-headed HH, as well as elderly, especially if they live alone, among others. DRC will also reinforce activities that focus on women and their role within communities, as an enabler for protection outcomes in conflict affected areas by leveraging women’s roles and leadership in expanding the humanitarian space, ultimately promoting community resilience, and furthering protection of civilians and civilian assets. HLP rights for women will be prioritized whenever title deeds or land rent agreements are signed. Where cash for work will be implemented, activities will be designed to allow participation of men and women, girls and boys.More than two decades of persistent civil war and cyclic natural disasters have left millions of people displaced, either internally or as refugees - within the Horn of Africa. Protracted IDPs in Somalia are estimated at around 2.6 million. The figures are likely to be much higher due to significant population mobility, but also recurring secondary displacements, as well as significant rural-to-urban migration. There more than 100,000 returnees in Somalia mainly from Kenya, Yemen and surrounding regions. Both IDP and returnees live in situations of extreme economic vulnerabilities and insufficient social network. Provision of public services in Somalia is constrained by conflict and poor economic status of the country. In addition, due to large number of the displaced and limited resources, there is likelihood that displaced communities, who are extremely vulnerable, will be excluded from service provision. To address this risks in this intervention, DRC will ensure continue consultation with IDP communities throughout all phases of the project cycle. The project will be initiated with a familiarization meeting held with relevant authorities and communities in targeted locations, to create awareness of the scope of the project. Field staff with sound understanding of the community dynamics will provide detailed information on vulnerability-based selection criteria. DRC will advocate on behalf of the IDPS on promotion of land tenure security. DRC will advocate with clan elders, regional and federal governments to provide land with title deeds to enable IDPs access durable solutions. DRC will employ a complaints and feedback platform (hotline) which is free of charge, easily accessible to everyone, and well-known throughout the targeted communities. The system will be further optimized and expanded in view of its use to serve the present CCCM project, and reports will be issued and shared with the CCCM cluster and within the wider humanitarian. Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violenceTo mitigate on the impact of conflict and violence, DRC will seek to provide Psychosocial support through case management activities that will be linked to this project.

Please provide further explanation

Durable solutions is one of the key pillars of DRC Somalia programming, and continuous efforts are directed towards promoting durable solution and self-sufficiency of the displaced and vulnerable people across Somalia. However, DRC recognizes the complexity and long milestones required to reach this vision. This project will contribute towards supporting opportunities for displaced people to achieve durable solutions given people centered response, capacity building for both the government and IDP communities, linking community priorities with national government plans and as well as implementing community-based initiatives that can promote community recovery. IDPs have limited options for durable solutions to their displacement. According to JMCNA findings indicate that the majority of IDPs prefer local integration. This however has so far remained a challenge due to lack of land availability, repeated evictions and minimal livelihoods options. Returns to the area of origin are extremely limited, as most IDPs have lost livelihoods and are reliant on humanitarian assistance. DRC will make use of the CCCM coordination platforms to advocate Housing, Land and Property rights for the displaced. In the immediate short term, the envisioned relocation sites in Baidoa and Galkayo - both locations in which DRC intends to continue and enhance its CCCM interventions - are directly linked to early solutions in Somalia, therefore falling firmly within DRC’s commitment to further develop solutions programming in a continued effort for bridging the gap from relief to recovery as part of the focus on nexus programming.
