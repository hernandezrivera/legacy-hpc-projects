Project Summary

The proposed project will improve access to sustainable WASH services namely safe water, sanitation facilities and promote good hygiene practices for 7,846 female and 7,539 male beneficiaries in underserved and hard-to-reach villages and IDP camps in Belet Xawa, Dollow, Garbaharey and Luuq districts in Gedo. Among the target beneficiaries will be 2,308 persons with disabilities (1177 male, 1131 female).
The below-average rainfall forecast for the upcoming Deyr season (Oct to Dec) which may be compounded by a harsh Jilaal dry season in 2021 calls for a greater focus on WASH and a re-prioritization of life-saving approaches in Gedo. Access to improved water sources remains low in Gedo with a significant portion of the population relying on the rivers and other seasonal streams as their primary water sources. A joint multi-sectoral assessment led by Trócaire in Dollow riverine communities in December 2019 indicated that 90% of households consume untreated water from the Juba river. The same assessment identified poor latrine coverage as a challenge, with 80% of the population lacking access to latrines. This results in wider open defecation, which is suspected to be one of the factors contributing to the frequent occurrence of diarrheal diseases in the area. A detailed site assessment by REACH in February 2020 further reported widespread open defecation at 64 and 100 percent respectively in assessed sites in Luuq and Dollow. The most severely affected community members are women and girls, who are often forced to use nearby bushes, putting them at a greater risk of SGBV. The ongoing COVID-19 pandemic has further compounded WASH needs in communities, with Trocaire noting serious gaps in knowledge, skills and resources to prevent and control the pandemic especially in IDP and rural communities.
The project aims to contribute to the achievement of specific objectives of the WASH cluster: delivering life-saving WASH assistance to reduce acute needs, and restoring access to safe and equitable basic WASH services in target locations. This will be done through the construction of one (1) solar powered shallow well with piping to communities in Dollow, rehabilitation of one flood-damaged water system in Dollow and provision of Genset, construction of 110 permanent gender-sensitive latrines with handwashing stations, desludging of 85 latrines, hygiene promotion sessions with distribution of hygiene kits, and environmental cleanup campaigns. In addition, Trocaire will conduct water testing and treatment of water facilities in target locations, and conduct trainings and refresher trainings for representative Water Management Committees while equipping them with tools necessary for effective management of water points. Trocaire will collaborate with Somali Humanitarian Relief Action (SHRA) and Active in Development Aid (ADA), national NGOs with strong experience in implementing WASH projects in Gedo, in delivery of this project in the four target districts.
Trocaire shall work closely with local partners identified to promote protection of beneficiaries and ensure access to services especially for people living with disabilities. The Water Management Committees will particularly be sensitized on inclusivity in water access for people with disabilities and their families. Hygiene promotion outreach teams will be trained to identify and deliver services in homes with people living with disabilities. Gender inclusivity will be ensured through creation or support to adequately representative community structures, as well as working closely with women groups in target location. All data collected will be dis aggregated by gender, age and disability, to inform adaptive programming in meeting the different needs of the different social groups reached.

Project Activities

1. Procure, install and construct 1 solar-powered shallow wells with piping to communities - This will target one marginalized riverine village in Dollow in order to improve distance and access to water for men, women and children as well as people living with disabilities and their families. The activity is intended to reduce workload on women and girls involved in water collection as well as enhance sustainability to provide continuous supply of water close to them during and after the emergency period. Committee will be formed and trained on operation, maintenance and inclusivity in ensuring water access to all vulnerable groups. Discussion has already been made with the beneficiaries to ensure security of the solar system when installed2. Rehabilitate and install one flood-damaged water system in Dollow and fitted with genset to improve access to safe water for boys and girls as well as ensuring women and men in the IDPS and Host communities have access to water3. Conduct training of 1 Water Management Committee on management of water resources and inclusivity principles. This will increase their skills in identification of marginalised groups and ensure equal access to water especially for people living with disabilities. 4. Training of 50 (25M, 25F) hygiene promoters on WASH. They will be engaged to provide gender-sensitive sessions to Communities in target villages. They will focus on key hygiene and COVID-19 related messages for behavior change in line with inter-cluster hygiene promotion plan in the WASH cluster. They will also be trained to identify people with disabilities and provide them much- needed assistance at the points of contact, referring them to other points of service delivery.5. Water quality testing/Monitoring - to ensure that drinking water is safe and in accordance with the WHO standards.6. Construction of 110 emergency, gender-separated latrines in 4 districts fitted with handwashing facilities and spacious enough to accommodate people with physical disabilities. The design and location of the latrines will be discussed with communities and will be constructed depending on a detailed pre-implementation technical assessment that will be conducted at the start of the project implementation. . Ramps, wide doors as well as other design considerations will be made to provide access to disabled beneficiaries.7. Desludging of 85 latrines in Dollow and Luuq IDP camps.8. Distribution of 2000 emergency hygiene kits (20 litre Jerican, a bucket, bar soaps, and 200 Aqua tabs) for affected host households and IDP families through the use of cash vouchers in Luuq and Belet Xawa. An additional 1000 hygiene kits will be sourced from the Regional Supply Hub at Luuq to be distributed in Luuq. Trocaire will only provide transport for the stock obtained from the RSH.9. Conduct behaviour change hygiene promotion activities (door to door and group sessions) in 4 districts10. Reproduction and distribution of 1000 IEC materials on COVID 19 prevention will be done. These will in local language and will be utilized as part of the resources to be used by hygiene promoters during their routine hygiene promotion sessions.11. Conduct 4 Environmental clean-up campaigns in 4 districts.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In addition to being a recommendation of WASH Cluster, the use of cash as a form of market based programming whenever feasible, helps address system failures of WASH including disruption of both supply of and access to WASH related goods and services, stimulate local markets, increases the speed and flexibility of humanitarian response and reduces the costs of delivering humanitarian aid.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Trocaire has just recently worked with SHRA, a local organization to implement the distribution of hygiene kits using cash vouchers in Dollow and Belet Xawa. This activity was successful and provided lessons on the viability of the modality in the proposed locations targeted by this project. Trocaire has extensive experience in conducting market needs assessments and will conduct market assessments and monthly market price monitoring to guide the feasibility of this activity where necessary.

Mitigation Measures

• RISK 1: Likelihood – Low; Impact – High; Mitigation – Trocaire has strong institutional processes and policies on PSEA, which integrate the IASC Guidelines for Integrating GBV interventions in Humanitarian Action throughout all stages of our programming. All Trocaire staff are expected to uphold the highest standards of personal and professional conduct at all times to protect beneficiaries. In addition, Trocaire has capacitated the management and program staff of its local partners on PSEA. The project will put in place measures to prevent sexual violence by actively engaging women and girls, along with men and boys, in community-based groups and incorporating their views in the project implementation. WASH facilities will be sited appropriately from dwellings in line with the SPHERE standards. Additional design considerations have been made for latrines which will be lockable from inside and not only separate but separated for males and females especially in IDP camps. Finally, awareness will be created on the existing GBV hotline managed by Trocaire through which appropriate support can be accessed. • Risk 2: Likelihood – Medium; Impact – High; Mitigation – Protection is central to Trocaire’s work in Somalia and is integrated across project implementation to ensure safe and dignified programming. Trocaire has an active protection team that utilizes robust protection monitoring systems and that works closely with the Trocaire Health sector teams to provide targeted GBV services including Clinical management of Rape (CMR) and Psychosocial First Aid. A toll free GBV line has been established as part of the existing referral mechanisms to ensure women and girls receive assistance in a timely and safe manner. In addition, awareness creation on the services provided by Trocaire is ongoing through both mass media and field teams. As the regional GBV sub-cluster co-lead, Trocaire is well positioned to coordinate with other partners in protection and raise critical issues around needs and gaps in assistance hence better and more planned response. In addition, Trocaire avails resources that are required by survivors to increase their access to protection services, including cash grants and logistical support. • Risk 3: Likelihood – Medium; Impact – High; Mitigation – Trocaire will adopt a do no harm approach through which various actions will be taken to ensure inclusion of all last mile groups in the program interventions. This will include the collection of sex, age and disability disaggregated data, ensuring meaningful participation of beneficiaries in the project by consulting and adapting interventions to meet the different identified needs of PWDs within the target population. This will be done through Trocaire’s comprehensive Feedback and Response mechanism that allows a two- way interaction between Trocaire, its partners and project participants- both male and female. This will be through face- to- face conversations, suggestion boxes placed at strategic places, the monitoring of information that is collected through a Toll Free line, and community leadership structures. Information will also be collected during regular monitoring activities by both MEAL and program teams. • Risk 4: Likelihood – Medium; Impact – High; Mitigation - Trocaire has an active beneficiary feedback and response system (a dedicated toll free call-in line, suggestion boxes and email address), which provides a safe, accessible and effective channel for women, girls, men and boys to give feedback and raise complaints for a response or redress to be given. Whereas this mechanism has worked well particularly in Trocaire’s past/ongoing projects in Gedo, project teams will ensure beneficiaries are aware of its existence and are familiar with its use through regular communication and dissemination of IEC materials on Trocaire’s feedback and response mechanism during project activities. Complaints/feedback desks, will also be set up.

This initiative has been successful.- Does the project support a government initiative or policy that protects the rights of displacement affected communities? Trocaire, through its humanitarian programs, supports the FGS’s mandate to meet the basic rights of the Somali people including the right to water and sanitation among others. This project will specifically contribute to the aspirations of the national policy on internally displaced persons (IDPs), and refugee-returnees that seeks to protect IDPs, and refugee-returnees from further forced displacement, provide protection and assistance during displacement, and find a durable solution to their displacement.- Does the project leverage on existing community structures, systems and existing networks with a clear benefit for the acceleration of processes of local integration return or resettlement? Trocaire works closely with District Health Boards, IDP leadership and WASH committees to build their capacities and enhance resource sharing and ownership. This has often contributed to sustainability of interventions. Through this project, Trocaire will build on these existing structures by training 3 water committees comprising of 15 members. - Will the entitlement of beneficiaries to services/utilities provided be legally recognized?Trocaire is guided by the human right to water and sanitation which acknowledges that clean drinking water and sanitation are essential to the realisation of all human rights. In addition, the action aligns with principle 18 (2)(a) of the Guiding Principles and Human Rights–Based Protection which safeguards internally displaced persons’ secure and non-discriminatory access at all times to sufficient safe potable water for personal and domestic uses and to sanitation services, both of which are crucial for the well-being, health, and even survival of the displaced. Respect for the right to adequate water also constitutes a key precondition for achieving durable solutions.
