Project Summary

Chronic WASH needs continue to emerge in parts of Greater Upper Nile and Eastern Equatorial States, which are likely to be exacerbated by COVID 19 restrictions, unprecedented flood waters, food insecurity as well as logistical challenges. Provision of adequate WASH services is key to achieving sustainable solutions to communities in emergency. CMD, a national NGO working in the locations of Jonglei, Upper Nile and Eastern Equatoria envisages a targeted response aimed at reaching internally displaced persons, returnees and vulnerable host communities with water, sanitation and hygiene services, integrated with nutrition, health and protection programming. Spanning a period of 12 months, the project targets the locations of Ayod, Akobo, Duk, Nyirol, Uror, Fangak, Bor South and Twic East (Jonglei), Ulang, and Longechuk (Upper Nile) and Kapoeta South, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta East (Eastern Equatoria) with WASH temporary and sustainable services, to address the vital needs, public health and protection issues.
The response aims at reducing levels of chronic malnutrition especially amongst children, pregnant and lactating women, mitigating WASH related GBV by providing gender appropriate sanitation and hygiene facilities and tackling issues surrounding gender, safety and WASH. Persistence of food insecurity, signalled by high GAM rates, with <5s and PLW mainly affected will be mitigated by the provision of WASH in Nutrition services, through targeted WASH minimum packages at OTP/SC mainly for mother/caretaker – malnourished children.
Water related disease outbreaks have been traced to health facilities and community infrastructures/settings without adequate WASH facilities. A WASH in health outbreak control response is planned especially in disease prone counties.

1 - Needs

The counties of Ayod, Akobo, Bor South, Duk, Fangak, Nyirol, Twic East, Uror (Jonglei State); Ulang, Longechuk (Upper Nile State), Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South and Kapoeta East (Eastern Equatoria State) continue to face challenges in the thematic areas of WASH, nutrition, health, FSL and protection with an estimated 2M persons in these counties in need of humanitarian aid. According to the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), the highest level of needs and vulnerabilities are observed in Jonglei and Upper Nile states, due to multiple and recurrent shocks. Counties targeted are all under vulnerability index 4 and 5. Inadequate access to clean water, improved sanitation and acceptable hygiene practices within the counties of Ayod, Akobo, Bor South, Duk, Fangak, Nyirol, Twic East, Uror (Jonglei State); Ulang, Longechuk (Upper Nile State), Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South and Kapoeta East (Eastern Equatoria State) contributes greatly to the high levels of malnutrition, disease burdens, food insecurity as well as WASH related protection issues. Less than 15% of households in these communities have access to an improved water source under 30 minutes; with inadequate water available for domestic use. Deterioration of the economic situation reduced ability of communities to purchase items required such as soap, purification tablets, NFIs and hygiene items. Majority of persons resort to unsafe open water sources, that are usually contaminated with feacal material and are co-shared with livestock. Access to sanitation facilities is equally low, WASH severity maps indicate less than 30% of households having access to a latrine, yet majority of the latrines are shared. Open defecation is widely practiced, leading to cycles of water contamination, resulting in acute watery diarrhea, cholera, eye infections, and skin diseases. Hand washing is equally low, with socio-cultural practices unfavorable to achieving acceptable WASH standards. Women are exposed to a series of protection risks due to distance and time taken at water points, lack of hygiene items and livelihood opportunities, as well as community attitudes towards women. Agro – pastoral communities usually while looking for water for domestic and livelihood purposes tend to migrate to the areas with open water sources yet lacking adequate sanitation facilities. Torrential floods across parts of Greater Upper Nile have undermined efforts by communities to attain better living standards, due to the destruction of infrastructure and livelihoods and massive displacement of communities. COVID-19 related disruptions, including those rightfully implemented to curb coronavirus infection rates have exacerbated lack of access to services. There’s a direct correlation between low WASH coverage and high levels of malnutrition, as a result of occurrence of water related diseases amongst U5s, PLW and persons with special needs. About 150,000 children under five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021 based on same season historical data of food security and nutrition monitoring system, SMART nutrition surveys and admission trends for 2020. This is the highest caseload for acute malnutrition since the start of the crisis in December 2013. Of the counties in IPC AMN Phase 4, nearly 80% are in Greater Upper Nile. Further deterioration of the nutrition situation is projected during the lean season of April to August 2021. The major factors contributing to acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases (up to 36%), poor quality and diversity of food (Minimum Acceptable Diet: 7%, Minimum Dietary Diversity: 15%). Provision of WASH services is critical in improving living standards and well being of communities in the targeted counties in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Eastern Equatoria state.

2 - Project activities

The following activities are planned through this project in the locations of Ayod, Akobo, Bor South, Duk, Fangak, Nyirol, Twic East, Uror (Jonglei State); Ulang, Longechuk (Upper Nile State), Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South and Kapoeta East (Eastern Equatoria State) • WASH service provision in nutrition facilities (WASH NFI kits and hygiene promotion to SAM children) and access to safe water to facilities in IPC 4/5 locations and provision of sustained water to communities showing high GAM prevalence and poor access to water. • Access to safe water, sanitation in areas showing high risk of outbreak and hygiene promotion (domestic, water and food hygiene). • Mitigate Gender-Based Violence through the conduct of safety audits at site level (or use protection mainstreaming tip sheet) to identify GBV concerns and implement mitigation measure. • Building WASH resilience through the provision of permanent access to water and sanitation services for IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable host communities

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA information will be cascaded to beneficiaries through clear communication channels, starting with staff and stakeholders who will be trained on PSEA protocols. Posters, Banners will be availed spcifically on PSEA but also other IEC materials will have information on PSEA. Workshops, trainings, seminars will have sessions of PSEA, while CMD takes on specific campaigns on PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Feedback from the most vulnerable will be obtained through direct interviews in languages they understand most, and focus group discussions. Visual and picture messaging will be utilised too, to pass on and obtain information from the most vulnerable.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

In accordance with humanitarian protocols pertaining to feedback or complaint mechanisms, CMD will implement specific activities in regard to this. Feedback mechanisms will be enhanced through establishment of clear communication pathways, that will be clearly communicated to communities in local dialects that are most understood by beneficiaries. Mechanisms of obtaining information from beneficiaries will be enhanced, through interviews with key informants, beneficiary households, as well as community members. Suggestion boxes will be installed at key field locations, that will be regularly attended to by designated personnel. CMD will set up hotlines, that key stakeholders can reach incase of any feedback/complaint. Feedback/complaint reports will be documented and shared with stakeholders, aimed at informing future project decisions.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback on key project activities will be used to make adjustments on project deliverables, as well as enable proper future planning. Beneficiary feedback will be documented and shared with key stakeholders.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Clear communication and feedback pathways will be established to reduce risks related to lack of information or awareness; with trainings carried out with key stakeholders. Services will be brought closer to the persons targeted to avoid long distances that expose women and children to protection risks. Targeting will be based on vulnerability indices, with the most vulnerable targeted first, in agreement with key stakeholders. Local authorities will be involved in the project cycle to mitigate risks around governance, bureaucracy and access. Safety audits will be conducted, as well as use of protection tip sheets before, during and after each of the activities.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

This will be done through the establishment of clear pathways, with referrals enhanced through inter cluster collaboration and programming. All messaging will have major cross cutting areas clearly explained, and feedback/complaint pathways well communicated to stakeholders.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Adequate water and sanitation services play a role in enhanced livelihoods and food security in communities. Majority of the populations targeted are agro - pastoralist in nature, and require water and sanitation for both livestock and human use. Lack of water leads to displacement of communities, who settle in areas with limited sanitation and hygiene services. WASH services are both a pull and push factor and contribute to localized communal insecurity. Reduced incidences of AWDs boost food utilization amongst children and the elderly, thereby reducing food insecurity and malnutrition.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

WASH services provided will be equitable, inclusive and accessible to all gender groups. Establishment of WASH services will be designed to meet the needs of persons with special needs, including the elderly. Safety audits will be carried out to ensure inclusivity, safety in access. Referral mechanisms will be enhanced, with clear pathways provided to all stakeholders.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Provision of WASH services reduces the likelihood of waterborne diseases that are solely responsible for U5 and PLW malnutrition. The project will provide WASH+Nutrition programming, providing water and sanitation services at nutrition sites, providing WASH NFIs, hygiene and dignity kits to nutrition caregivers, utilizing nutrition volunteers to pass on WASH messaging.
