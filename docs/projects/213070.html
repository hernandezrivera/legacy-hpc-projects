Project Summary

The proposal aims to respond to the emergency and basic needs of women and girls in areas severely affected by the earthquake and flood, so that intervention within the project activities will be at three levels:
1- Enhancing women and girls’ access to physical and mental health services by providing direct health services in women’s and reproductive health care in both Lattakia and Tartous and through mobile medical teams.
2- Raising awareness of gender-based violence, its consequences and forms, especially among those directly affected by the earthquake and flood, and providing protection and mental health services to women and girls who benefit from it in Latakia and Tartous.
3- Providing support and economic empowerment for young women and girls by providing them with labor market skills and income-generating grants, especially for young women and girls who are breadwinners for their families, with a focus on new professions and skills related to technology and the new developing labor market in Latakia and Tartous.
Services will be provided through a CWC center in Baniyas covering Baniyas and its countryside through 2 IMT
A CWC center covering southern Tartous in the city of Safsafa covers the countryside affected by the flood by 2 IMT

0 - Project Summary (extended)

II. context and situation analysis)Maintaining access to life-saving reproductive health assistance, integrated reproductive health and gender-based violence services, and other essential health services remains a critical response priority. Deep-rooted, multidimensional crises and the effects of earthquakes are expected to significantly amplify stresses and have a disproportionate impact on the most vulnerable groups, especially women and girls. Insecurity and economic hardship have become a major driver of need, exacerbating protection concerns and gender-based violence, especially among groups at increased risk due to age, gender, health, or social status. Families headed by women, the elderly and people with disabilities continue to be disproportionately affected by the worsening drivers of the crisis in Syria.One of the catastrophic consequences of the earthquake was the direct damage to the health sector, which exacerbated the difficulty for women and girls to access reproductive health services within and outside temporary shelter centers. It also exacerbated the problems of gender-based violence for them and increased the economic pressure on them, which was already deteriorating from the Syrian crisis.In the areas proposed for implementation, women and girls suffer greatly from several challenges related to their access to reproductive and public health care services, the risks of violence that are deeply rooted in those areas, and the lack of social representation for them, thus increasing the pressure and burdens on them, especially those who are responsible for supporting their families.After examining these areas, it is expected that a large number of women will be waiting to give birthThe coming months. 3.7 million children in earthquake-affected areas across Syria face potentially catastrophic threats, including contact-borne infectious diseases, water-borne diseases, and lack of access to basic services.As a result of the psychological pressures exacerbated by the earthquake, the risks of women and girls being exposed to gender-based violence increase, and therefore it is expected that the need for protection services related to them, services for raising community awareness of issues of violence, and the general protection structure and services provided by all partners on the ground will increase. Therefore, women are responsible for more Social and economic pressures to meet basic needs resulting from the current situationAnd thereforeIn light of this situation, the Youth Council will work according to three aspects:The first: Providing protection and response services, especially for women and girls, networking with relevant authorities, and referral to temporary protection centers when needed and according to the desire of women and girls, such as the Family Protection Unit supported by the United Nations Population Fund.
