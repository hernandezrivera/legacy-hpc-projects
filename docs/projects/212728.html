Project Summary

During 2024, 2025 Shafak will respond to the increased child protection concerns of the crisis-affected Shafak will deliver a comprehensive protection package via Seven Child-Friendly Spaces and 15 child protection teams deployed in Idleb, Azaz and Afrin Jarablus districts in Aleppo.
Shafak will adapt the CFSs and the static facilities to be accessible and friendly for CWDs by establishing ramps and toilets for CWDs.
The total Target is 56100 (5000 M, 5000 W, 22550 B, and
22550G).
In 2024, 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023. The earthquakes uprooted hundreds of thousands of families, resulted in almost 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 people injured in Syria, and severely damaged infrastructures already weakened by the conflict. Many families lost their main breadwinner due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, increasing the vulnerability of millions of people previously unable to meet their basic needs. (HNO, Summary, 2024). Within the protection sector, numbers indicate an increase in the number of PiN in 2024 to 2,375,850 in Idleb, and 3,566,142 in Aleppo who are in dire need for protection services.
In alignment with the child protection cluster’s strategic objectives for 2024-5, Shafak aims to expand the response where working on adapt the CFSs and the static facilities to be accessible and friendly for CWDs by establishing ramps and toilets for CWDs.The total Target is 56100 (5000 M, 5000 W, 22550 B, and
22550G).
Providing specialized services for at-risk children including CwD and will be focusing on high-risk cases such as:
1 Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC).
2 GAFFAC
3 Worst forms of child labor.
4 Trafficking
Emergency funds and cash voucher assistance for children will cater to specific unmet needs. This response is based on the CP-CVA SOPs developed by a Task Force established by the Northwest Syria CP Sub-Cluster. A monitoring system using the ECF and CVA M&E toolkit will be established to regularly assess, and measure the living conditions of children and ensure that assistance does not create a pull factor including the passing of children between families to be eligible for assistance.
Furthermore, Shafak will provide PSS structured and unstructured PSS for children and caregivers for children and caregivers within the CFS and communities using different tested programs such as Child Resilience (CR) , IRC curriculums, and Safe Families Approached.
These sessions will depend on a child’s age, life experiences, family situation, gender, and even individual personality. Psychosocial improvement will reflect positively on other aspects, including a return to daily routines.
Furthermore,To reduce any possible risks of GBV if Wash facilities are not separated or there are no locks on the doors, female team staff will consult with women and girls on location of these latrines and ensure locks are on the door, with post-monitoring feedback forms and regular repairs of these facilities (if locks are broken). For the risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls, Shafak ensures a confidential complaint mechanism is in place, with staff signing on code of conduct, with proper training provided to schoolteachers and education personnel on PSEA to know how to report misconduct.
