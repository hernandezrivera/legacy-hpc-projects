Project Summary

In line with the CCCM cluster strategy for 2021, ACTED will implement its CCCM interventions through static response in Juba and Bor former PoC sites and mobile responses in locations with emergency needs and in hard-to-reach areas.
ACTED’s added-value in multisectoral emergency response lie primarily in building resilience through site planning, coordination and advocacy which ultimately empowers the Community. In particular, the response includes:
- Restructuring and capacity building of governance thru representative Community Management Committees.
- Communities identify prominent risks in a facilitative process to agree on site maintenance and planning works, as well as contingency plans which mitigate risk of corresponding hazard.
- ACTED provides a mapping of service provision and site profiles to be shared with humanitarian coordination forums for advocacy for future action.
- Protection Focal Points are trained in the Community to establish a referral pathway identified during service monitoring to be maintained after the intervention.
- Communities are supported thru a Community-Led Initiative to improve livelihoods.
- Local partners are also trained in coordination and service monitoring
A shock is flagged through clusters, working groups, ICCGs, and/or humanitarian coordination fora. After a shock, ACTED is able to deploy a team in 3-5 days to conduct a scoping assessment, which will last apprx. 3 days to complete. Tools such as site profiles and site monitoring matrix 3Ws are shared with partners. In line with pipeline availability, ACTED will immediately launch a multisectoral response as per the identified needs of the targeted communities. The intervention duration will usually last between 4-6 weeks. ACTED will coordinate with all the clusters, working groups and humanitarian coordination fora as well as reaching out to ECHO and RRM partners to avoid duplication. ACTED will also coordinate with RRC, local partners, mobile partners, and cluster state focal points. In addition to activities surrounding service monitoring, ACTED will train local partners on coordination mechanisms to avoid duplication of response. Apprx.,2-3 months after an intervention is complete, ACTED will return to the location to conduct a post intervention/distribution monitoring and will share the results with the relevant clusters.
In addition to the current modality responding to emergencies resulting in displacement, ACTED mobile team will pilot a response to areas of potential and actual returns to improve governance and resilience. This response will be coordinated with UNHCR’s intention survey, or target sites of shocks from previous years.
ACTED will start to move from static response to semi-static activity provision in former POCs by gradually transferring more responsibilities of camp management to IDP leadership for the purpose of sustainability.
ACTED will conduct border monitoring activities at 8 border crossings in Eastern Equatoria to ensure humanitarian actors have reliable and accurate data to inform programming.

1 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, there are currently 1.6 million people in need, out of which 310,000 are refugees and asylum seekers living in South Sudan, whose dignity and living standards are dependent on coordination structures in camp and camp-like settings,. Out of the 1.2 million people in need of CCCM service in 2021, an estimated 150,000 people were affected by unprecedented floods in 2020. The protracted nature of displacement continues to burden the displaced populations, returnees, and host communities. Meeting the multi-sectoral needs of the population largely exceed the ability of humanitarian actors to respond, especially given the challenging security environment in many locations. Of the approximately 1.6 million IDPs in South Sudan, around 20% are living in formal camp settings in 1 of the 6 current or former Protection of Civilian (PoC) sites which house over 200,000 civilians, around 29,000 of whom reside in the Juba (UNHOUSE) IDP camp and a further 1,925 IDPs reside in Bor IDP camp. The transition of Juba and Bor PoC sites to conventional IDP sites was an important development in 2020. The security and safety of displaced populations in former PoC sites is a top priority while more durable solutions are found. ACTED as the Camp Manager in Juba and Bor IDP camps created a contingency plan to ensure continued advocacy for service provision following the departure of UNMISS. Up to 80% of IDPs in South Sudan who are not living in formal camp-settings are often residing in faraway hard-to-reach locations with little access to humanitarian assistance. Furthermore, there have been an increase in spontaneous returns in some Areas of Habitual Residence and Areas of Origin, where returnees are often living in IDP like conditions. Influxes of IDPs and returnees put significant pressure on the host community and limited resources in those areas. In response to the needs of these populations, ACTED launched in 2018, with the support of BHA and the South Sudan Humanitarian Fund (SSHF), the ADAPT (Assisting Displacement Affected Populations Together) strategy. Through this series of mobile CCCM responses, ACTED found that both, newly displaced people and those in protracted displacements, often require support in organizing their informal settlements, in terms of physical space, social linkages and representative leadership structures, in addition to having needs for basic items such as shelter and NFI materials which were not being met. Therefore, ACTED will continue implementing CCCM interventions in 2021 in line with the following cluster objectives:Sectoral Objective 1: Ensure a protective environment in camps and camp like settings for the displaced population and host community. Sectoral Objective 2: Ensures predictable and coordinated delivery of service, provision of information aimed at improving the quality of integrated services for the displaced populations in camps and camp like settings.Sectoral Objective 3: Enhance self-governance through participation and engagement with displaced persons. Sectoral Objective 4: Strengthen community coping mechanisms geared towards lasting solutions

2 - Project activities

In line with the CCCM cluster strategy for 2021, ACTED will continue to implement its CCCM interventions through static response in Juba and Bor former PoC sites and mobile responses in locations with emergency needs and in hard-to-reach areas. Having been the camp manager for both Bor and Juba (former) PoCs since 2014, ACTED will continue providing vital camp management services in 2021, whilst in tandem working with partners to plan for a responsible scale-down of activities in a way that does not adversely affect the IDPs. The camp management services provided at former PoC sites will include dead body management, information management, site maintenance, flood mitigation, strengthening safety and security in communal spaces, strengthening community governance, strengthening communication and community engagement, coordination of service provision, and communication and messaging on COVID prevention measures. ACTED expects to transition out from its static response in Juba and Bor former PoCs tentatively in late 2021 following the strengthening of community structures to facilitate community coordination. After the transition, ACTED will continue to support the former PoC sites through conducting ad hoc mobile interventions. ACTED’s CCCM mobile teams will address the needs of displacement affected populations in camp like settings and informal settlements with a focus on quality and information-driven interventions that help people adapt to shocks and prepare to transition towards self-reliance. ACTED will utilize mobile responses in response to critical needs for the provision of life-saving support in emergency scenarios, specifically in hard-to-reach areas, where service provision and partners presence is limited. ACTED’s added-value in multisectoral emergency response lie primarily in building resilience through site planning, coordination and advocacy which ultimately empowers the Community. In particular, the response includes: - Restructuring and capacity building of governance thru representative Community Management Committees. - Communities identify prominent risks in a facilitative process to agree on site maintenance and planning works, as well as contingency plans which mitigate risk of corresponding hazard. - ACTED provides a mapping of service provision and site profiles to be shared with humanitarian coordination forums for advocacy for future action. - Protection Focal Points are trained in the Community to establish a referral pathway identified during service monitoring to be maintained after the intervention. - Communities are supported thru a Community-Led Initiative to improve livelihoods. - Local partners are also trained in coordination and service monitoring In addition to the current modality responding to emergencies resulting in displacement, ACTED mobile team will pilot a response to areas of potential and actual returns to improve governance and resilience. This response will be coordinated with UNHCR’s intention survey, or target sites of shocks from previous years. In both mobile and static responses, ACTED will where feasible conduct cash based interventions through cash for work modality. CFW can be used for a variety of activities including site maintenance, community center construction, dyke construction in line with the identified needs per location. ACTED will conduct border monitoring activities at 8 border crossings in Eastern Equatoria to ensure humanitarian actors have reliable and accurate data to inform programming. Activities will be conducted in 8 locations along the border with Uganda. Finally, as co-lead of the CCCM Cluster, ACTED will specifically continue engaging with humanitarian organizations and agencies, along with donors and UNMISS, to ensure a coordinated humanitarian response, filling gaps and avoiding duplications, while enhancing the Cluster strategy towards IDPs’ self-management.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The Camp Management Committees who are trained under the project activities receive specific trainings on PSEA and are expected to disseminate this information to the communities.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

As per its standard operating procedures for CRM, throughout the project, ACTED will ensure that multiple channels of communication (e.g.: help desk, staff registering complaints, hotline etc,) are available for ACTED to receive feedback from communities including the most vulnerable members such as individuals living with disabilities and individuals who are not literate. Furthermore, ACTED will have at least one women staff member in help desks to encourage women to register their feedback to ACTED. For the mobile responses ACTED will set up Community Based Complaints Mechanisms (CBCM) to support the community’s self-management of complaints.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During the implementation project activities, beneficiaries will be sensitized on the complaints and response mechanism in camps, and on how to submit concerns or complaints to ACTED. Moreover, help desks at project sites will be installed at central locations and equipped with visibility materials.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ACTED’s independent AMEU will continuously register the complaints and feedback regularly of the target population and insert them into ACTED South Sudan’s learning database. The findings will be discussed at least on a monthly basis with the project implementation and project development teams to ensure that the feedback is incorporated into the ongoing project as well as for the design of upcoming projects.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Within the PoCs, ACTED establishes and supports inclusive community governance mechanisms. These groups will be consulted throughout with a focus on ensuring all minority groups, as well as specific interest groups such as women and youth, are included in consultations and project design. ACTED’s mobile interventions all include a protection officer to ensure training of leadership, identification of referral mechanisms and identification of PSNs. However, in mobile interventions which are often remote, it has previously proved challenging to identify protection partners able to take referrals. Therefore, ACTED is increasingly collaborating with protection specialists to run joint missions where possible. The RSRTF project looks to address the risks related to dispute on land ownership and intercommunity conflict over access to resources. The project design will be very closely coordinated with UNMISS and dependent on the prevailing security situation, to ensure that the project will support HLP rights of affected populations and do no harm. Living in insecure settlements exposes displaced women and girls in particular to GBV. Site maintenance activities will therefore be designed with a GBV angle in mind. Furthermore, women and girls will be prioritized by vulnerability criteria for shelter/ NFI interventions (e.g. torches). ACTED’s interventions will target not only IDPs, but also the host community, returnees and local polity (known collectively as a displacement -affected community) with mobile CCCM activities. Where multi-sectoral assistance is targeted, vulnerable host communities will also be considered under vulnerability criteria. ACTED has previously experienced low utilization of CRM in mobile responses. This will be mitigated through clear and consistent communication with the community, as well as establishment of a community based complaints mechanism (CBCM).
