Project Summary

As outlined in the needs section, the overall nutrition situation deteriorated significantly in NWS during 2020. This deterioration has been driven by poverty, low socioeconomic status, unsafe living conditions and diseases, suboptimal maternal, infant and young child feeding (MIYCF) practices, and high rates of maternal malnutrition. The mass displacement that occurred at the end of 2019 and in early 2020 being a significant contributing factor. Due to the economic downturn and depreciation of the Syrian Pound as well as the impact of COVID-19 in NWS, a further deterioration of the nutrition situation is anticipated.
In response to the identified need, NGO X will implement the following package of activities which will benefit 39,200 individuals.
1. Provide facility and community-based one-to-one counselling on WHO recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding and maternal nutrition practices
2. Provision of specialized nutritious food (LNS) to children 6-59 months
3. Early identification and referral of Acute Malnutrition cases
The project will be implemented in both Idleb and North Aleppo (subdistricts defined below). NGO X will be implementing this project directly, and 100% of funding has already been secured from multiple different donor sources.

4 - Needs

OCHA’s July 2020 Situation Report shows that nearly 3 in every 10 children under five in NWS were suffering from stunting due to chronic malnutrition. According to the November 2020 Nutrition Cluster brief, the stunting rate has increased by 5% since January to 34% in October 2020. In June 2020, the Nutrition Cluster reported that the proxy prevalence of acute malnutrition across NWS was 2% among children 6 to 59 months and 8% in pregnant and lactating women (PLW). The main drivers of the deteriorating nutrition situation in NWS include poverty, low socioeconomic status, unsafe living conditions and diseases, suboptimal maternal, infant and young child feeding (MIYCF) practices, and high rates of maternal malnutrition. Due to the economic downturn and depreciation of the Syrian Pound as well as the impact of COVID-19 in NWS, a further deterioration of the nutrition situation is anticipated.

5 - Implementation of activities

Nutrition activities: 4. Provide facility and community-based one-to-one counselling on WHO recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding and maternal nutrition practices5. Provision of specialized nutritious food (LNS) to children 6-59 months6. Early identification and referral of Acute Malnutrition casesImplementation: NGOX will provide the LNS product, nutributter, to children 6-59 months in highly vulnerable IDP families living in informal settlements and IDP camps in Idleb and North Aleppo. Alongside these supplements women of reproductive age and caregivers of children 6-59 months will be trained in the family MUAC approach and provided with a MUAC tape to take home and information on the nutrition treatment and prevention services available in their local area if they need to access them. This aims to increase early self identification of acute malnutrition within the households and increase health seeking behaviours. Nutrition counselling will be provided to the same populations on the prevention and treatment of malnutrition and maternal and infant and young child nutrition. Coordination and referrals will be carried out between all health & nutrition actors in the area through the Nutrition Cluster and bilaterally. Coordination and referrals will also take place between the food security actors in the area to ensure the most vulnerable to malnutrition receive both food assistance and nutrition services

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

NGO X will ensure a Do No Harm approach, and mainstream PSEA in all interventions R: Risk of sexual exploitation/abuse for beneficiaries at or travelling to and from distribution points M: Distribution site considerations include proximity to active hostilities, presence of armed actors, and timing of distributions. Distribution points are located close to beneficiary villages so they do not have far to travel, to minimize the risk of violence (GBV and theft). NGOX ensures they are accessible and safe for women and children to travel to in daylight and all distributions are undertaken in daylight. If beneficiaries can’t or don’t want to attend distribution points they can authorize another household member to collect their assistance for them. In cases where a beneficiary is unable to attend the distribution point, home delivery can be organized. Risk of sexual exploitation or abuse by NGO X staff, partners or stakeholders,Mitigations: All staff, partners, and stakeholders receive comprehensive training upon starting their contracts on NGO X’s safeguarding policies and measures, behavior protocols and reporting mechanisms. NGO X has a dedicated safeguarding focal point at national level, trained in handling sensitive issues arising from actual or perceived breaches of GOAL’s policies, as well as focal points in all field sites. A confidential safeguarding reporting mechanism and a framework for investigation, response is in place,

Risk- Continued military offensives presents the risk of death or injury to NGO X staff damage to or destruction of NGO X premises) or assets; reduced access to programme areas and of border closure. Mitigation - Safety and Security Evacuation Plans and Standard Operating Procedures are in place, and an experienced security team A: That NGO X & partners have the capacity to safely and securely store 2 rounds of stockR: Border closure disrupts supply chain M: NGO X Keep stores stocks sufficient to meet requirements for two rounds of assistance and continues to explore the possibility of local procurement A: It is assumed that large scale vaccinations will not be possible in NWS in 2021 and therefore assumed that COVID19 will continue to be a risk for all of 2021. R: Spread of novel Coronavirus (2019-nCoV/SARS-CoV-2) infection in Northwest SyriaM NGO X has identified food security as an 'essential service' in light of COVID-19, which means NGO X will aim to continue, in as much as possible, food security delivery. As the nutrition delivery is highly integrated within the FS programme, NGO X foresees continuation of this activity. However, certain measures such as MUAC screening, outreach may need to be curtailed in light of high transmission rates. Where there are localized lockdowns, NGO X will aim to deliver assistance, perhaps at HH level with increased CV19 mitigation measures being utilized to protect staff and beneficiaries.
