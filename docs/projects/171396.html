Project Summary

TASS proposes to support primary and community health care services, including the prevention and control of epidemic prone diseases in vulnerable communities in Xudun (Lafawayne, Awrbogays, Kulaal, Daryaogesaweyn and Holhol), Taleex (Caroolay, Kalcad, Dhubuq-dhubuq, Bardadawanle) and Laascanood (Godqaboobe, Hadhwanaag, Faliryaale, Buurawadal, Gambadhe)to meet the following objectives:
1. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases through supporting early detection, investigation and response to disease (e.g. outbreak) through Rapid response teams (RRTs), HCW training, monitoring (EWAR) and supplies vaccination campaigns, Health awareness, social mobilization, education and advocacy activities in disease control, case management, emergency management, Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) and Provider and patient safety (e.g. PPE, triage, hand-washing).
2. Contribute to improvement in physical and mental wellbeing of the population affected by conflict and displacement including people with disabilities through: Integrated Mental health / psychosocial support services (MHPSS) HCW and community training: MHPSS (PFA, PSS, MhGAP-HIG) and Health awareness, community mobilization, education and advocacy activities on MHPSS, disabilities.
3. Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders through Clinical management of rape services (CMR)GBV / IPV services and referral and STD / HIV treatment and prevention
4. Improve equitable access to quality emergency and essential lifesaving health services for the crisis affected population for both IDPs and vulnerable host communities through Essential PHC (EPHS) clinical care, Child health care, Emergency and trauma care, Communicable disease treatment, NCD treatment and STD / HIV treatment
TASS will respond to the most urgent needs of the beneficiaries, including emergency clinical service provision, community health care and maternity (supporting ANC, PNC, normal delivery, Basic emergency obstetric and newborn care (BEmONC) services and newborn care, outbreak investigation and response for epidemic prone diseases and the provision of essential medical supplies. SGBV, protection services to support MHPSS services and CMR in the health facilities through case management, training staff on MHPSS and CMR and conducting Community dialogue on the prevention of GBV and Harmful social norms
Minority groups and the disabled, who are often omitted from humanitarian assistance, will be participate at all phases of the project cycle. The project will also strive to include all age and gender groups. To ensure staff have capacity to promote these while ensuring protection, all staff will be trained in PSEA, social mobilization and disability inclusion in programs. Strong monitoring will ensure realization of this with all data disaggregated by age and gender Gender will be mainstreamed throughout the project: data will be disaggregated by sex and age whenever possible and activities will be tailored to respond to the specific needs of women, girls, men and boys affected by crisis in three targeted Districts of Sool region. The needs of girls and women in conflict will be taken into account by tailoring specific training to the female health workers and by strengthening the referral mechanism to assist women and girls who have been subjected to sexual gender based violence. The access to integrated lifesaving emergency health services will be particularly ensured for pregnant and lactating women and U5 children.

Mitigation Measures

There will be a deliberate effort by TASS to ensure accountability to the target population through open, transparent and inclusive project implementation and management processes. The target beneficiaries will be highly involved in the planning, implementation and monitoring of the project activities. In addition, proper feedback mechanisms will be set in place to continuously improve on the quality of programming and ensure that TASS is responsive to the community needs. A Complaints Response and Feedback mechanism with a hotline is already available for use. The use of this system will be enhanced by making beneficiaries more aware of its existence in the proposed project locations. Furthermore practical measures will be taken in the activity design to minimize risk of exposure to violence as a result of distance to medical services. Most notably, mobile clinics to remote areas will be prioritized as a modality that reduces the need for women to travel to reach high quality maternal and child health, SRH and GBV/CMR services. The health workers running the mobile clinics will receive training on GBV and mental health topics in order to better address protection needs that are identified in the rural communities.TASS will ensure that activities being implemented will not lead to or perpetuate discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect or exploitation. All activities will be tailored to ensure that they promote and respect human rights and enhance protection of women, girls, men and boys. Specifically, this action will mainstream protection by ensuring that from the outset, it will deploy female and male local health workers. In addition, this action will also ensure that survivors of sexual and gender based violence when encountered by the mobile clinics and CHWs will be referred to TASS supported health facilities where they will be able to access medical and psycho social support. In addition, the community members will be informed of the nearest health facilities where SGBV are available.

Please provide further explanation

This project links directly to Strategic objective 5 of the Somalia Resilience and Recovery framework to improve basic service delivery. Primary aim of this project is to save-life among affected and vulnerable People including host community and IDPs affected by natural and man-made disasters. the capacity building of the local and national staff and the exit strategy or road map will be part of the project for sustainability. The response will focus on local capacity building for health service provision, close and strong collaboration with the local leaders, local authorities and the beneficiaries. Community engagement, consultation and dialogue will be central to all interventions through life of project to ensure local ownership and sustainable solutions. Through preventive health promotion and information dissemination as well as capacity building on safe practices in Health, nutrition and WASH, communities are expected to build resilience and invest in long term solutions
