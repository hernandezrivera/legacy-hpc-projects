Project Summary

The proposed integrated life-saving and essential emergency health project aligns with the South Sudan Health Cluster’s Objectives CO1, CO2, and CO3 for 2025, which are in turn aligned with the South Sudan HNRP Strategic Objectives SO1, SO2, and SO3. The project aims to address critical humanitarian needs in prioritized payams and villages within Mayom County, currently classified as HNRP 2024 level 4 in Unity State. By fostering effective coordination, complementarity, and integration of the development and humanitarian nexus, the project will collaborate with the State Ministry of Health (SMoH), Mayom County CHD, partners, and other local stakeholders. Central to this approach is the proactive involvement of local communities and authorities at every stage—design, implementation, monitoring, and ensuring joint accountability.
Yo’ Care will employ a range of service delivery modalities, including both static and mobile health services, to address accessibility challenges and improve healthcare coverage at all levels of the health system. These challenges may be exacerbated by emergency events such as conflict and flooding. The implementation of static and mobile services in Mayom County is designed to meet the urgent health needs of the target population, particularly vulnerable groups, including those with disabilities, mental health disorders, and those affected by SGBV, who face significant barriers in accessing healthcare.
In addition, health-related activities will be closely integrated with other relevant clusters, including nutrition, WASH, and protection, by establishing and maintaining reciprocal referral channels. This will ensure that affected individuals receive both clinical care and psychosocial support.
The project is expected to reach 16,022 individuals in Mayom County with essential, life-saving health services, including at least 6,950 residents, 2,055 internally displaced persons (IDPs), and over 3454 returnees, mainly from neighbouring Sudan. The total funding required to achieve these targets is US$ 500,000.

1. Needs

The needs identified and proposed solutions in this project by Yo’ Care South Sudan align with the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (HNRP) 2025 issued by the South Sudan Health Cluster on 22 November 2024 and the Health Cluster Strategy 2024–2027. Mayom County, located in Unity State, is classified as a priority humanitarian setting, with intersectoral prioritization at level 2 and a Health Cluster severity classification of level 4. Recent data from the South Sudan HPC Population Baseline Report (10 October 2024) estimates that 144,290 people—89% of the county’s population—are affected. This marks a significant increase compared to 42% (76,904 people) reported in the 2024 HNO. The affected population faces critical challenges, including poor health, malnutrition, hunger, and violence, with women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities being particularly vulnerable. In 2024, Mayom County has been heavily impacted by ongoing conflicts and flooding. Persistent internal and cross-border conflicts, combined with recurring floods, have severely disrupted access to and utilization of quality basic health services. This is particularly evident in Ruathnyibol, Kueryiek, Wangkei, Riak, Pub, Mankien, and Bieh Payams, where medical services have been critically lacking. Notably, Wangkei PHCC has been non-operational since March 2023, following the ICRC’s withdrawal. Conflicts and flooding have caused significant internal displacement in Mayom, with 34,957 internally displaced persons (IDPs) and 13,817 returnees, according to the Displacement Tracking Matrix and ISNA assessments by IOM and UNHCR. A pressing concern is the strain these IDPs and returnees place on host families, whose already limited resources are further diminished by raids, lootings, flooding, and environmental challenges. This situation is exacerbated by the fact that most households are headed by women, who face heightened exposure to violence and an increased likelihood of food insecurity due to ongoing inter-communal conflict and flooding. The vulnerability of IDPs and returnees is further compounded by inadequate and inequitable healthcare services. Issues include poorly distributed health facilities, understaffing (both in numbers and skills), a lack of essential equipment and supplies, insufficient funding to sustain routine services such as vaccinations, and the inability to deliver essential preventive and curative care. According to the 2021 Annual Health Sector Performance Review Report, health indicators in Mayom County remain alarmingly low, with ANC4+ coverage at 41.7%, stillbirth attendance at 11.9%, and outpatient department (OPD) utilization at 61.3%.

2. Project activities

As outlined in the strategic approach, Yo’ Care’s activities align with the Health Cluster's objectives (CO1, CO2, and CO3) and directly address the needs identified in the South Sudan HNRP 2025 Strategic Objectives (SO1, SO2, and SO3). These activities will be implemented in selected primary healthcare (PHC) facilities and villages, as determined in coordination with the Health Cluster, the State Ministry of Health (MOH), and the County Health Department. The selection process will be informed by assessments of needs, service gaps, and local readiness, alongside consultations with beneficiaries and community leaders in the county. Priority will be given to areas where existing services are insufficient to meet the demand or have been neglected by common funding mechanisms. This includes locations burdened by the effects of IDP influxes, disease outbreaks, flooding, and violence. Specifically, the targeted areas include Ruathnyibol, Kueryiek, Wangkei, Riak, Pub, and Bieh Payams in Mayom County, which face severe healthcare challenges due to their overwhelmed or absent services. Activity 1: Provision of Comprehensive, Integrated, and Quality Preventive and Curative Primary Healthcare Services at Static Facilities (PHCCs and PHCUs)Aligned with Health Cluster Objective 1 (CO1), this activity focuses on ensuring the availability of basic life-saving services at static facilities. Yo’ Care will prioritize the prompt management of common endemic infections, including malaria, pneumonia, and diarrheal diseases, to address urgent health needs effectively. Activity 2: Delivery of Life-Saving Essential Healthcare Services, Epidemic Control, and Management of Non-Communicable DiseasesThis activity, aligned with Health Cluster Objective 2 (CO2), involves providing essential healthcare services through static (hospitals, PHCCs, PHCUs) and mobile healthcare units. Yo’ Care will focus on outbreak prevention, detection, investigation, and integrated response. Collaboration with UNICEF and WHO will support regular antigen provision and cold chain maintenance, ensuring effective prevention of vaccine-preventable diseases. Surveillance systems, such as IDSR and EWARS, will be strengthened to maintain consistent and timely outbreak detection and response. Activity 3: Data-Driven Decision-Making and Stakeholder Engagement in Mayom CountyAligned with Health Cluster Objective 3 (CO3), Yo’ Care will actively participate in weekly or bi-weekly performance review meetings with health cluster teams, local health authorities, and donors. Identified gaps and challenges will be addressed collaboratively. Yo’ Care will submit regular 5Ws data to the Health Cluster and DHIS2 and provide monthly and quarterly reports to donors. Enhanced disease surveillance will be achieved through timely and comprehensive IDSR and EWARS reporting in targeted facilities. Activity 4: Ensuring Continued Provision of Integrated Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) ServicesThis activity, aligned with Cluster Objective CO1 and Strategic Plan Objective (SPO) 1.1, focuses on delivering integrated MHPSS services. Yo’ Care aims to address the mental health needs of affected populations through facility-based and community-level interventions. Activity 5: Provision of Integrated Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Care and Prevention ServicesYo’ Care will deliver knowledge and training to recognize, prevent, and manage cases of IPV/SGBV. Community-based GBV education, screening, and psychosocial support services will be sustained, ensuring continued prevention and referral systems for survivors of violence.
