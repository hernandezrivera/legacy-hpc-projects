Project Summary

The project aims to 1) improve the quality, accessibility and safe referral of existing response services for survivors, especially linkages between case management, medical, and legal services 2) mitigate the risk of both sexual and gender-based violence through multi-level and cross-sectoral capacity building (including on PSEA) and community-led (especially by women and girls) initiatives and 3) prevention through sustained, evidence-based programming that changes the social norms undergirding violence against women. Highly vulnerable, underserved populations will be targeted.
UNHCR and partners’ approach is community-based and survivor-centred, with refugees—especially women and girls—engaged as active agents and emerging leaders within their communities. We will increase women and girls’ participation in decision-making and leadership by building their confidence and capacity with skills building and training, engaging female refugees in our community-based protection work and engaging men and boys as allies in promoting gender equality. Key components include safe spaces, life and vocational skills, PSS, community volunteers, engaging men in accountable practices (EMAP), women-led initiatives, and women’s leadership training.
UNHCR and partners will continue to provide case management and psychosocial support services for survivors of sexual and gender-based violence, with special attention to strengthening the survivor-centred care practices within the multi-sectoral referral network and increasing accessibility for, and capacity to serve, target populations. Service delivery modalities have been adapted for risk mitigation, including for COVID-19 and natural disasters. Lessons learned from 2021 have led to more integrated GBV response services, including in health centres, legal service centres, and drop-in centres for transgender population and individuals engaged in sex work. The approach will make our services more resilient in the face of lockdowns, help to close the ‘GBV-legal’ referral gap, and improve access for key populations. Capacity building components include case worker minimum training package, and GBV sensitization for health, legal and law enforcement service providers.
GBV risks will be mitigated through a three-tiered strategy, working at community, UNHCR multi-functional, and inter-agency levels. Community-level work will include GBV risk mapping and generation of risk mitigation measures by refugee women and girls, with community GBV risk mitigation actions supported through a dedicated community fund. UNHCR multi-functional work will see the development of GBV risk mitigation action plans across functional units. The inter-agency work will be done in collaboration with UNICEF as part of the global Blueprint for Joint Action, with the aim of mainstreaming GBV risk mitigation across all eight sectors in the Rohingya humanitarian response and also an initiative for protecting children from sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by humanitarian workers.
UNHCR and partners will implement a variety of evidence based GBV prevention activities, both those that take a whole of society approach (SASA! Together) and those that target specific groups (Girl Shine, EMAP, Male Role Models). The multi-year SASA! programme is implemented in close coordination with UNFPA and its partners, to avoid operational overlaps. Promoting the centrality of protection and an age, gender and diversity approach, UNHCR and partners will invest in AAP with community-based complaints mechanisms, SEA risk mitigation through protection mainstreaming across sectors and targeting highly vulnerable populations. Target populations include child and adolescent survivors, male survivors, adolescent girls, gender diverse populations and individuals engaged in sex work in host and refugee communities, and persons with disabilities.

1 - Identified Needs

Rohingya refugees who fled Myanmar in 2017 were escaping violence that included widespread gender-based violence and sexual violence against both females and males, which inflicted lasting traumas on the population. These and more recent arrivals joined an existing Rohingya refugee community in Bangladesh, which share social norms that maintain and reinforce gender inequalities that enable and perpetuate violence against women and girls within their communities. Pre-COVID-19 research conducted in 2020 (University of New South Wales) found a clear pattern in the types of gender-based violence risks faced by women and girls that tracks with life stages. The risk of rape and sexual abuse was the top concern for girls 18 and under, followed by child marriage and human trafficking. Although risk of rape and sexual abuse remain high across all age groups, the risk of intimate partner violence (IPV) increases as a concern with age, including for LGBTI women and women with disabilities. The threat of trafficking is greatest for young women and girls. Assessments over consecutive years (MSNAs, RGAs, FGDs, GBVIMS) have revealed a grim protection picture. Refugees feel unsafe both within the community and inside their shelters, particularly women and girls. Women have reported violence in the home (26%) and violence in the community (25%) as their top two perceived risks. IPV accounts for over 84% of reported GBV incidents. Social norms that restrict females’ movement in public create barriers to services, with only 28% of surveyed households reporting that a female could go alone to a women friendly space. Camp design presents other risks, with 33% of surveyed households with a female respondent reporting access to safe and functional latrines as a priority. The COVID-19 pandemic has reduced the number of available response services, while simultaneously increasing the barriers to access what remains. The reduced number of humanitarian actors on the ground correlates with an increase of violent crime within the camps, heightening fear and a sense of insecurity. Refugees face risks of sexual exploitation and abuse at the hands of humanitarian workers, particularly as COVID-19 restrictions have simultaneously increased reliance on volunteers while limiting UN monitoring presence. Parents fear for the safety of their adolescent daughters. Facing closed schools and failing livelihoods, parents see marriage as a protective measure, even for girls under 18. Men in the camps are opportunistically practicing polygamy, while men in neighbouring countries are arranging smuggling of young brides, despite the journey’s grave risks. Human smuggling and trafficking in the region have become ‘feminized’, with more women and girls than ever forced into dangerous journeys.

2 - Activities or outputs

Community-led GBV risk mapping and mitigation. Community GBV risk mapping (‘safety audit’) with women and girls who attend WGSS and community centres. This includes identifying GBV risk mitigation measures. These community-led initiatives will be supported by a community GBV risk mitigation fund, allowing women and girls to take the lead in making their communities safer. Training will be conducted by UNHCR and partners targeting refugees, government authorities and UN, INGO and NNGO staff. Trainings will aim to increase awareness about GBV prevention (including PSEA), risk mitigation, and response; build GBV related technical capacity; and influence knowledge, attitudes, and beliefs regarding gender equality. Community-based GBV prevention through social change. The root causes of violence against women will continue to be addressed through the multi-year SASA! Together GBV prevention progamme. Male engagement in GBV prevention. Male community members will be targeted for engagement in GBV prevention activities through two different programmes—Male Role Models & EMAP. Targeting adolescent girls in GBV prevention. The IRC developed Girl Shine GBV prevention and girls’ empowerment programme will be scaled up in 2022 to include more adolescent girls and their male and female caregivers. Women and girls’ protection and empowerment.Through a total of 19 WGSS and 2 community centres (Hindupara and Omani sites), UNHCR and partners will provide structured skills building for women and girls, such as training in leadership, literacy, numeracy, and vocational skills. Communication with communities, community-led messaging and awareness raising. Continuing a network of GBV focused ‘community outreach mobilizers’ who take a primary role in sharing key messages with their fellow community members on GBV prevention and response, COVID safety, SRH, emergency preparedness, PSEA and related topics. They link survivors to services through identification and referral. GBV response service points. Operation of GBV service points that meet the needs of different profiles of survivor (ex. males, gender diverse population), with 19 WGSS, 11 male engagement centres, 5 counselling centres and integrated GBV services in 15 community, 13 health, and 10 legal centres, plus 5 drop-in centres for transgender population and sex workers. Services for male and female survivors will be available in all locations. Safe shelters. The community-based safe shelters will continue. The partner-run safe shelter in Cox’s Bazar city was expanded in late 2021 to better accommodate males, families, LGBTQ+ individuals and individuals with mental health needs. Inclusive, survivor-centred GBV case management & PSS. Maintaining and strengthening multi-sectoral GBV response services with attention to increasing inclusivity of case management (child, adolescent & male survivors and survivors with disabilities, from gender diverse populations, or engaged in sex work). Emergency preparedness and response including disaster risk reduction. Mainstreaming GBV interventions in emergency preparedness and response plans, through specialized capacity building for partners and refugees, stocks of dignity kits, targeted awareness raising sessions, and implementation of the rapid GBV risk assessment in collective shelters used during acute emergency displacement. Inter-agency GBV risk mitigation mainstreaming. In coordination with the GBV Sub-sector and in partnership with UNICEF, mainstreaming of GBV risk mitigation at the sector level across all sectors. Accountability to affected populations & protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. Reporting and feedback mechanisms in place include a 24-hour protection hotline, protection desks, info hubs, letter boxes, face-to-face and a direct line to UNHCR’s global level Inspector General’s Office. Community-based complaints mechanisms will be further developed in 2022. PSEA Network membership and PSEA training of UNHCR and partner staff.
