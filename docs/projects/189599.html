Project Summary

The proposed 2022 FSL cluster HRP highlights the gaps identified among vulnerable communities affected by crisis and in IPC 4 to 5, particularly limited access to improved high yielding vegetable and crop seeds, limited tools for farming, limited access to cash for essential food and HHs needs and inadequate fishing equipment. The project aim to provide immediate lifesaving intervention for crisis affected vulnerable communities by targeting 58,973 Fangak,15,683Tonj East and 42,609 Pibor which are 117,264(56,521m, 60743f) people (Host 50%, IDPs 30% and Returnees 20%) and 10% disable people which are in crisis due to insecurity, yearly flooding, drought and were the farming practices among the predominantly peasantry communities are very poor culminating in to problems of food insecurity, poverty and environmental degradation.
The action will provide conditional cash assistance for most vulnerable households during the 6 months lean season to enable HHs community asset creation however enabling continues access to diversified food and HHs needs. Targeted HHs provided with vegetable and crop kits for cultivation ensuring continuous access to food for a long period of time, fishing kit to complement household food needs during floods times since most flooding in this areas comes with fish mean while supporting continues fishing in areas close to rivers as fish is a rich nutritious protein source for consumption and surplus sold for income generation by the community. The project will ensure year round production of vegetable crops through recession irrigation practices during dry season and diversification of the livelihood activity options among the vulnerable communities, increasing dietary diversity for the farming community and the fishing groups. The project aimed at complimenting the current interventions in an effort to prevent an even more severe food shortage as well as strengthening the coping capacities of the vulnerable communities with special focus given to people with disabilities and chronically sick to make sure they are not left out including pregnant and lactating mothers. The project inputs will be procured and transported to the field to the field with timeliness of delivery of these inputs very critical. Capacity building support will be conducted for selected beneficiaries and continuous practical sessions through the central demonstration gardens learning sites within the communities including health/nutrition centres. The cash beneficiaries will be trained on creation of community dykes to prevent flooding within their gardens. The project seeks to create synergies with other thematic areas such as Wash, health and nutrition of AAH and other implementing partners on ground through integrated approach. The program will also integrate with nutrition project focusing on areas of food preparation, utilization and cooking demonstration and technically supporting rolling out of training by establishment of kitchen gardens for the targeted groups.
The project will benefit the most vulnerable households (IDPs, disable, Child-headed, chronically sick, PLW, Elderly and Female-headed households) which account for the 27.9 % households under IPC 4 and 5 suffering crisis and acute food insecurity and at high risk of malnutrition as food insecurity is likely to continue affecting these counties negatively.
The project will ensured continuous collaboration with key stakeholders including the government ministries at both county and state level to be part to support ongoing activities.
The project will target at least 60% women for support, as women are the most vulnerable group mostly involved in farming activities. The project considers Do No Harm principles by encouraging environmentally friendly activities including strategies to improve soil fertility, water tolerant crops such as rice and drought tolerant crops such as sorghum/millet will be promoted and awareness on protection against GBV provided.

1 - Needs

The overall humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains fragile since the beginning of 2021, the number of people in need had reached an all-time high of 8.9 million people, an increase from 7.24 million people recorded at the same time in 2021 and in need of humanitarian assistance HNO Feb 2022. According to December 22, 2020 WFP’s research initiative on “Climate Change and Food Security Analysis”, in addition to conflict, climate variability and extremes is one of the main drivers of global hunger. South Sudan is also prone to natural and manmade disasters, unprecedented droughts resulting in failure of crops, high level of deforestation, overstocking resulting in over grazing and increasing numbers of hurricanes floods resulting in soils erosions and heat waves, most striking effects of climate change affecting the poorest and the most vulnerable by threatening all the pillars of nutrition security.The IPC December 2021 analysis indicated; widespread flooding, inflation, high food prices due to disruption of markets, deepening food insecurity, insecurity, population displacements, livestock mortality, the economic crisis, limited access to basic services has exacerbated an already fragile food and nutrition insecurity situation and COVID19 effects exposing the highest estimated number of 6.35 million people to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3)or worst acute food insecurity leading to the level of malnutrition alarming high even in the presence of ongoing and planned humanitarian assistance in 2021.Hunger and malnutrition have escalated, with about 1.4 million mothers and children under age of 5 estimated to be acutely malnourished and in need of lifesaving services, women and girls face a double threat as food insecurity exacerbates protection risks.The April – July 2021-OCHA Situation report No 2, the number of people likely to be in crisis-level acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3) or worse is projected to increase from an already high of 5.8 million to 7.24 million people of which represents 60 per cent of the South Sudanese population and during this time 1.38 million are in Jonglei and 698,000 Warrap have some of the highest numbers of people in need in South Sudan. According to UNOCHA flood situation report, among the 07 counties of most concern and affected by flooding, the most extreme case being (Tonj East) Warrap and (Pibor & Fangak )in Jongolei State, prioritized based on the severity of food insecurity and high prevalence of malnutrition and protection needs. Record-breaking floods of 2019-2020 and 2020-2021 led to many areas turning into wetlands throughout the dry season, preventing return of populations and planting activitiesThe REACH humanitarian situation monitoring report (October 2021), indicated that Fangak, Tonj East and Pibor experience extreme food consumption gaps and people have largely exhausted their emergency coping strategies pushing population to revert to negative coping mechanisms such as poorer quality meals or selling of assets in exchange for food, reduced number of meals in a day and consumption of wild fruits and with prices of main staple cereals remaining high in the target areas.The response proposed targets Tonj East, Fangak and Pibor of great concern were AAH have huge static programmes with long presence in which integrated multi-sector programmes are been implemented to address malnutrition and other various forms of vulnerabilities however despite the ongoing responses in previous interventions, there still remains some enormous gaps needing immediate humanitarian attention(IPC January 2022).It is against this backdrop that AAH proposes to respond to the gaps identified in the above proposed locations as of limited access to improved high yielding vegetable and crop seeds, limited access to cash for essential food and HHs needs and inadequate fishing equipment to enhance resilience in dealing with the recurring shocks that are inherent part of the vulnerable community reality.

2 - Project activities

The project will conduct the following key activities to response to the vulnerable communities of Fangak, Pibor and Tonj East; Distribution of crops, vegetable seeds and fishing kits, AAH will distribute vegetable seeds kits to crisis affected and vulnerable households in the selected counties. Assorted crops and vegetables kits will be access through the FAO pipeline and transported from Juba to field locations and to the distribution sites by the road network created by the widow of opportunity provided by the dry season minimizing air transport except areas completely inaccessible during dry season. The distribution of assorted vegetable, crop seeds and fishing kits to the vulnerable community will then be planned to take place immediately after delivery of the kits to enhance early planting. AAH will issue all project beneficiaries with identification cards, to be used exclusively for the project (distribution and post distribution monitoring). Provision of market information to inform decision-making, AAH will collect market price information to support the forecasting of the market prices in the project locations and use the information to advice cash assistance beneficiaries on price changes. Conduct training to beneficiaries on improved agronomic practices, post-harvest handling, local seeds selection and marketing, AAH will provide technical training on best agronomic practices in crop; vegetable production and good fishing practice and pass on key messaging to the beneficiaries on improved agricultural practices, pest and diseases management, nutrition education, diet diversity etc. Beneficiaries will also be provided with simple marketing and value addition knowledge and skills to ensure beneficiaries’ prolong storage of vegetable crop/fish and excess produce sole for income. Establishment of central demonstration kitchen gardens within the communities and around health and nutrition sites to teach beneficiaries and mothers improved vegetable cultivation methods. Cooking demonstrations using vegetables produced from the demo plots will be conducted. Awareness will be provided on nutrition sensitive agriculture production especially in selection of nutritious dense vegetables crops and the nutrition centres will serve as learning centres for both beneficiaries and mothers enrolled at the centre. Distribution of conditional cash to the vulnerable communities, cash will be provided link to construction of community assets eg Construction of floods control dykes and access routes through cash, the project will promote the protection of beneficiaries’ fields from recurrent flooding through the construction of flood control Dykes. The project will also identify people with special needs including those who are physically challenged including PLW with children under 5 and willing to cultivate backyard gardens and those un able and supported with cash to ensure continuous access to food while participating in activities that can be performed by the beneficiaries. Awareness sessions on GBV and environmental conservation measures will be provided. The targeted beneficiaries will be provided with knowledge and skills on environmental management through use of environmentally friendly farming practices by encouraging local trees raising and management, use of cover crops and drought tolerance crops in drier areas including flood resistance crops such as rice in flooded areas. Post-distribution monitoring (PDM), post-fishing and post-harvest assessment: AAH Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (M & E) including the project team will carry out a rapid Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) within two weeks to one month after each distribution (with a flexibility window for hard-to-reach locations up to two months). The findings of the PDM will allow AAH to assess the quality and impact of the action, as well as to continuously improve the response approach taking into consideration the feedback from the beneficiaries’ lessons.

5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Usually during the year, there are specific periods where farmers experience severe hunger gap especially between planting and harvest time referred as lean season; therefore cash voucher/assistance helps the families to ensure the continuous food purchase at the household level as the families are cultivating during the 6-month lean season to enable households continuous access to nutritious food and immediate livelihood needs. Furthermore, cash provides several opportunities and options for family to consider priorities; address the most urgent needs and decide food preferences and other varieties. Additionally, during harvest period, when food commodities prices are lower; the families have more opportunity to restock food for the next lean season. Through AAH Beneficiary Feedback Mechanism System at the community level, it has revealed that the in-kind food handling process from prepositioning up to distribution may result to food spoilage or contamination compared to cash, where families could decide to buy fresh food and consider dietary diversification.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The project has been designed to involve all market actors including farmers, traders and other associated entrepreneurs by using integrated approaches that would benefit direct and indirect project participants. These are inclusive and broad based approaches that would outline key steps, including networking, consultations and collaboration at all levels. The Project will also focus on the following components to ensure market linkages at all levels: • Weekly analysis, market assessment and production of food and non-food commodities prices to update and keep farmers informed of existing market conditions, which would help them in making decision on when to bring or withhold their produce for trading • The project will also link farmers and traders through the execution of input trade fair where the different actors (farmers and local seeds producers) can negotiate and determine best price and quality of seeds that satisfy their individual needs. • Value addition through processing facilities and training of targeted farmers/groups to make their processed food more marketable and attractive to consumers and will be linked to other traders and shop owners for more profitability and sustainability • The local savings groups will be supported to save and utilize groups’ saving wisely and provided simple basic business management techniques to further enhance their knowledge and skill to establishing meaningful businesses for income generation; this would eventually contribute to the viability of the local market • Targeted women groups will be engaged and supported through Kitchen gardening all year long for the production of vegetables to increase household dietary diversity and surplus sold on the local market, where the local population will have access to the availability of nutritious food. Simple marketing knowledge and skills will be provided to enhance marketing of the surplus produced.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Action Against Hunger considers the feedback from beneficiaries as an instrument to improve the organization’s acceptance, and improvement of interventions in the community. Hence, all feedback from beneficiaries are analysed and used to improve the relevant intervention/project as emphasis on the organization being a learning entity. Feed back from beneficiaries will be utilized to obtain information on how the intervention have positively or negatively affected the community and the way forward to improve or change for better impact. Therefore, beneficiaries’ feedback will constantly inform AAH actions throughout the entire intervention, allowing adjusting and adapting actions according to the needs, priorities and aspirations of beneficiaries. The feedback enable the M&E to formulate recommendations, which will be key to adjust the intervention, inform future actions and provide lessons learnt.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

A diverse set of feedback mechanism modalities promotes the access of those with varying genders, ages, and disabilities. Community Volunteers and extension workers are also able to collect feedback at the community level when project beneficiaries are unable to access facilities. Feedback and Complaints Mechanism IEC materials include images and text in both English and the local dialect to ensure comprehension by a maximum number of individuals in target communities. These visuals are displayed on project facilities, structures, and equipment used by beneficiaries. Confidentiality and single-sex/age group Focus Group Discussions (including facilitators of the same sex/age group) promote a more comfortable environment for beneficiaries, especially women and girls, to share their concerns. The organization will solicit feedback from women, people with special needs and all project beneficiaries through different media including 1-on-1 sessions, focus group discussions, complaint box, and hotlines where appropriate to ensure that anyone regardless of disability, age or academic background has the access to provide feedback to the organization.AAH will ensure the needs of the most vulnerable groups, including persons with disabilities, individuals who are illiterate, women and girls are prioritized and addressed in all locations. Before activity, the most vulnerable will fast be-tracked and consulted on the distance of the activity and feed back is utilized to fix the activity location however in cases where the most vulnerable agrees to be represented by a family member, a chance is given and follow up is conducted to ensure the represented vulnerable feedback is received and he/she is satisfied. The AAH M&E and implementing staff team will too conduct household visits to obtain feed back taking precaution of COVID19 guidelines of social distancing, putting on mask, hand washing/use of hand sanitizer, no hand shake and also hotlines mobile numbers receiving and providing feed back in the local language and accessible by beneficiaries in all project location, display of illustrative pictures of key activities or feedback on inquiry in the local language at the community level. AAH will ensures facilitation of the provision of feedback to the most vulnerable on the services and information available to local communities in local languages through community recorded mobile megaphone, radios, forums such as church meetings amongst others. AAH will ensure feedback is provided to the most vulnerable taking in mind the DO NO HARM implementation plan.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Action Against Hunger trains all of its staff and volunteers on the organization’s PSEA Policy. During project inception, beneficiaries are brief on the services provided to be free and any practice involving PSEA by staff or any member of the community should be reported through the AAH confidential reporting channel. During AAH mobilization meetings, Action Against Hunger shares information about PSEA from the organization’s PSEA Policy. During project implementation, the organization regularly shares PSEA information with beneficiaries as reminder/refresher to emphasize the premium the organizations places Zero Tolerance for PSEA.AAH beneficiaries groups are prioritized and addressed including protection on PSEA. Information are also given on PSEA through community meetings and any community mobilization meeting for activity so that beneficiaries are aware on any case on PSEA to be reported immediately. The policy of AAH requires that every new staffs consent to the policy of PSEA by signing and old staffs are frequently reminded on the PSEA policy during staff meetings. AAH has a strong code of conduct and policy on sexual exploitation and abuse which all staff are required to review and sign before working. In to reiterate also, AAH has a robust CRM that ensures anonymity and confidentiality and that is widely advertised and will set up a complaints and feedback/ response desk to record any feedback from the beneficiaries, referring action points to the relevant departments/ persons.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

To prevent instigation of SEA and other forms of GBV by leading gender-targeted interventions, Action Against Hunger will aim to sensitize all gender and age groups on project activities before their implementation. All AAH project activities are designed to safely challenge harmful gender dynamics in a context-appropriate manner. Results of gender analyses will directly inform project design and implementation strategy. All staff implementing GBV referral pathway awareness, Men Group Discussion facilitation, Psychological First Aid, and any other sensitive discussion with target community members will be instructed on AAH mandatory policy for maintaining the identity of project participants strictly confidential. Data related to GBV and other sensitive topics will be anonymized in organizational databases to preserve the privacy of survivors. Moreover, survivors or reporters will not be solicited for further participation/discussion. Any GBV case concerning staff or beneficiaries which proceed to investigation will only involve the Country Program Ethics Committee, consisting of the Country Director, Head of Logistics and Head of Operations. All information will be confidential with access restricted to those personnel. AAH will train key stakeholders on the use of the feedback mechanism at each project location. Additionally, the organization will print complaint procedures in the local language and in simple English. The project team will regularly review the use of the feedback mechanism and information gathered from the review process will be used to make improvements. A diverse set of FCM modalities ensures that differing gender, age and disability groups have access.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The provision of services will be based onthe organization’s needs/vulnerability analysis. 12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Action Against Hunger mitigation measures involve the management andcontrol of flood water movement, such as redirecting flood run-off through theuse of floodwalls and flood gates (Dykes) by physical construction of the dykes structures using the cash, rather than trying to prevent floods altogether. Action Against Hunger will also manage the people, through measures such as awareness creation on where to settle to avoid suchincidences and strengthening water run way drainages, awareness creation on early warning information system analysis and communications to the community, this will work well through community engagement in physical labour works such as cash for work schemes and natural resource management plans through planting of water absorbing trees such as the ticks using the agroforestry initiative proposed in this project and if it comes to worst then supportive evacuation of the people to dry lands.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

This project will contribute at providing sustainable sources of foods, which will positively impact a reduction in the level of malnutrition in AAH intervention areas. AAH nutrition sensitive agriculture activities are specifically intended to reduce the GAM rate among the U5 children. This is complemented by AAH intervention in the nutrition programs through creation of kitchen gardens at the nutrition and health facilities and providing training for the mothers on establishment of the kitchen gardens and management being implemented by Action Against Hunger at static and outreach nutrition sites. The production of more food, promotion of resilient activities will go along way in supporting populations in IPC 3, 4 and 5. Enhancing the productivity of people in need of humanitarian aid will reduce the number of people in IPC 3, 4 and 5 as they become self-reliant and continue to fend for themselves. This will also include sensitization of the community on Kitchen gardening to improve variety of food production and cooking demonstrations to enable them to prepare nutritious foods at home using available materials.
