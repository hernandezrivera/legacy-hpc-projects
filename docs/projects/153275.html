Project Summary

Cisda is proposing an Emergency and Resilience light support interventions in three major hot spots food insecure States in Unity, Jonglei and Western Bahr- El Ghazal . The proposed project runs from January 2019 to December, 2019 .
The key objective of the project are to increase Vulnerable household access to food through support to increase food production and to reduce the level of vulnerability amongst malnourished children through access to nutrition vouchers and production of high nutritious vegetables. The project is line with HRP strategic objective 2 ( SO2) and is in support of FSL cluster objective 2 and FSL cluster objective 4
The project will target mainly IDPs and Vulnerable host population in these areas. A total of 50,000 people will be targeted. The program will be delivered through direct distributions of inputs, voucher assistance support to vulnerable households, training in improved vegetable production and vaccination of livestocks. These activities are mainly agricultural and livestock related and they are
Women , children, girls that are at high protection risks will be targeted throughout the implementation of the project . Additionally the project has major consideration for the accountability to affected population, thus a very strong AAP committee will be established.
CISDA has over three years strong experience in implementing similar Emergency food security and livelihood projects in the same geographical context. The project thus will leverage on the experiences gained during the past implementation inorder to adequately address the food insecurity problem.
The project will integrate or work closely with both the protection cluster and the nutrition cluster. With protection cluster, the project will support beneficiaries identified by the protection partner that are high protection risk. they will be mapped out and supported during implementation. With the nutrition project, the project will set up demonstration sites at OTP centres and train households with malnourished children. These households will also be supported with high nutritous vegetables which they will produce to address the nutritional needs of their malnourished children.

Needs

The conflict in South Sudan that started in 2013 which have led to over 2 million of people displaced in the Country also have deteoriating effect on the food security situation of the Country . A country with morethan half of its people are facing food insecurity which according to the recent September 6.1 million people (59% of the total population2) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity3 at the peak of the lean season (July – August), of whom 47,000 were in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5) and 1.7 million were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). In Nyirol County:Persistent insecurity: local (inter-communal cattle raids/child abductions) + national level in southern part of county -> lower cultivation + grazing land access;Dry spell in May / June; heavy rains in July causing flooding affecting crops;Pests and diseases affecting crops ;Large IDP presence – pressuring host community resources; inaccessibility due to rains + conflict – negative impact on market supply routes; Northern Jonglei: 1.5 years of conflict + displacement were the key hazards affecting food insecurity. Most vulnerable HH are expected are IDP HHs and most affected is Northern part of the county (Chuil population) which is also conflict affected, with no access to assistance. In general out of total population of 174,578 of Nyirol County 122,205 are in Phase 3 and 4 . Also FSNMS report indicates tat GAM Prevalence is pointing at Phase 4 (GAM: 19.9%,SAM: 4.3) in the County In Wau food security situation in the Western Bahr-El Ghazal State is deteriorating in September 2018 compared to the same time last year (Wau urban in phase 4, Greater Bagari in phase 4, Raga in phase 4 and Jur River in phase 3).The main driving factors includes Insecurity, looting of livelihood assets, poor access to food due torestricted movement to productive farmland,limited access to natural food sources, Seasonally poor feeder road conditions causing low market supply andirregular salary payment to the civil servants. However, with presence of Raga and Greater Bagari in phase 4 and few population amount to 5% IPC phase classification are catastrophe phase. In Mayendit,, the key shock is persistent and chroni insecurity which started since week 4 April , 2018. The conflict have resulted to most households lost cattle and food stock, Households who have exhausted or nearly exhausted social coping capacity, households lacking male labor for coping strategy utilization, especially fishing.Most households affected are women and children who are not only facing food insecurity risk but also other protection risks relating to them trying to access food
