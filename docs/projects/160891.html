Project Summary

This proposed Top Relief Organization (TRO) project will respond to the immediate needs of conflict affected IDPs and the surrounding host communities in Greater Upper Nile, Maiwut, and Longichuk Counties (Upper Nile), Mayandit and Panyijar Counties (Unity) with integrated WASH services. The proposed WASH project will improved water, sanitation and hygiene services in an integrated approach with nutrition and GBV, with the expected outcome of reduced morbidity, mortality and sufferings from protection threats and incidences of most vulnerable in the four (4) targeted counties. The project will also address perennial borehole water contamination by floods. This will be by elevating WASH infrastructure to be floods-resistant in the targeted community.
WASH services will be integrated with nutrition by providing services to Outpatient Therapeutic Program/Stabilization Center (OTP/SC) sites in Panyijar , Mayendit, Maiwut and Longechuk in in a span of 12months, with a target of 48,500 individuals. The boreholes near the OTP/SC sites and villages reporting high GAM rates will be rehabilitated to provide safe and adequate water to the patients in the nutrition sites. Water points will be availed to cattle and small stock during dry season. Rehabilitation will consider floods-resistant platforms in floods prone areas to reduce borehole water contamination and ease water collection even during floods. Gender segregated latrines with hand washing facilities will be constructed in the targeted nutrition points to ensure safe sanitation facility to the mothers and children visiting the nutrition site. In addition, Nutrition Volunteers from nutrition partners will be trained in hygiene promotion to conduct awareness session to the mothers/guardians of children-under-5 admitted for SAM in the OTP sites. Caregivers will receive hygiene kits when the children are discharged to for sustainability of hygiene practices learned in the OTP. Villages with high cases of malnutrition will receive minimum WASH package to mitigate rising cases. This will also encourage mothers of SAM affected children to visit OTP sites for treatment of their children. MAM affected children will be targeted for hygiene kit distribution and awareness in this project. Children (under 5) with MAM will also be considered for hygiene kits and awareness stop deterioration of cases. Hygiene kits will be requested from core-pipeline propositioned in Malakal and Bentiu for Panyijar and Mayandit.
This project proposes rehabilitating of existing boreholes in several nearby communities to provide adequate water supply. Water management committees, each consisted of with 9 members, will be trained in safe water chain. Pump mechanics will be trained for repair and maintenance of hand pumps. This will also help to enhance/build local capacities and resilience to organize themselves and maintain their water resources. As nearly 99% members of the population practice open defecation (ICWG assessment in, 2018), sanitation awareness will be conducted through public health mobilization meetings. Community hygiene promoters will be trained on hygiene promotion. They will conduct hygiene promotion in the communities as well as encourage people to build household latrines. Household sanitation program are planned where plastic slabs and digging tools will be provided through the core pipeline. The project also targets adolescent school girls to receive Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits along with orientation during distribution.

1 - Needs

Longichuk and Maiwut counties, in former Upper Nile state, are among the counties projected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) August 2019 to April 2020. Together with Panyijar and Mayendit, they are ranked among the highly populated counties. Multiple displacements coupled with perennial floods destroying livelihood and WASH infrastructure further complicates the dire situation. Displacement Track Matrix (DTM) places Panyijar highest with 29513 individuals displaced (June 2019 DTM), increasing the number of people in need of basic WASH services. Mayendit displaced population totals to 6710 individuals. Food insecurity is expected to persist in Longichuk and Maiwut increasing the levels of Malnutrition as dry season approaches. Gains of revitalized peace agreement are likely to be experienced if peace holds beyond dry season. The just concluded ICWG October 2019 Floods reported collapsed sanitation facilities including borehole platforms resulting into contamination of borehole water. These are driven and exacerbated by poor WASH indicators and also an influx of returnees and IDPs to Longechuk and Maiwut, who are putting pressure on already insufficient facilities, while increasing the proportion of the population who have not received hygiene and sanitation messaging during previous interventions and who therefore continue with poor hygiene practices. According to the local Relief and Rehabilitation Agency (RRA), currently there are 1372 returnees (IDPs) and 3537 IDPs living with the communities in the Longichuk. A REACH WASH assessment reports that 34% of households in Greater Upper Nile (Maiwut & Longechuk) reported having safe access to an improved source of drinking water as their main source (REACH, August / December 2018). However no much improvement over the period of 2019 with latest indicators of 57.1% of households in Longechuk and Maiwut collecting water that is below 15L/person/day (Sphere standard). This has been caused by regular breakdowns in boreholes due to pressure on the WASH infrastructure, which continues to increase with the continued influx of returnees to the targeted counties and low water levels during dry seasons. Sanitation is also poor in Mayendit, Panyijar, Longechuk and Maiwut, with only 23% of households reporting having access to a latrine (private, shared, or communal/institutional).. REACH also found that 38% of households claiming that the bush was the common defecation location (REACH, August 2018). Hygiene and sanitation indicators are also poor. Only 28% of households reported owning a jerry can or bucket with a lid, access to soap, and that every member of the household slept under a mosquito net, while 71% of households had no access to soap at all (REACH, August 2018). The report indicate that 49.6% of households did not treat drinking water before consumption and that knowledge of the critical times for hand washing was also low; 27.86% of respondents could recall after cleaning a child’s bottom, before breastfeeding a child was 22.14%, before cooking was 15.71%, after visiting the toilet was 14.29%, before eating was 11.43%, and after eating was 8.57% (KAP survey, July 2019). The impact of prevalent open defecation, poor hygiene practices and usage of open water sources is evident in the incidence of waterborne and feco-oral diseases in the targeted counties. TRO proposes a multi-Sectorial humanitarian assistance to the targeted Counties. The recent conflict in August-September 2019 in Maiwut has pushed the communities deeper into the bushes across border into Ethiopia as they feared living near the roadside where the chances of being attacked was high. Since most of the boreholes are along the road, women and girls must walk 3 to 4 hours to fetch safe water. Panyijar is no exception having the highest number of IDPs among the targeted counties under Top Relief Organization WASH response. This has increased their exposure to Gender based violence (GBV).

2 - Activities

TRO WASH integrated intervention starting from Nutrition response in which WASH services will be provided to Outpatient Therapeutic Program/ Stabilization Center (OTP/SC) sites in Mayendit, Panyijar, Maiwut and Longechuk in a span of 12months.The following activities are planned for villages with high GAM rates: • Boreholes near the OTP/SC sites and villages reporting high GAM rates will be rehabilitated to provide safe and adequate water to the patients in the nutrition sites. • Water Management Committee (WMC) will be trained to provide community driven access to safe water. • Assessments will be carried out to ascertain Gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, secure access, location and design of water points and showers. • Gender segregated latrines will be established with hand washing facilities in the targeted nutrition points to ensure safe sanitation facility to the mothers and children visiting the nutrition site including villages with High GAM rates. • Hygiene promoters and Nutrition Volunteers from nutrition partners will be trained in hygiene promotion to conduct awareness session to the mothers/ guardians of children- under-5 admitted for SAM in the OTP sites and to do Household hygiene promotion to villages with high GAM rates.• Hygiene kits will be provided to the children discharged to ensure that the hygiene practices learned in the OTP sites are followed even after their return.• Minimum WASH package will be provided to villages with high cases of malnutrition to mitigate rising cases. This will also encourage mothers of SAM affected children to visit OTP sites for treatment of their children. • MHM kits will be distributed to vulnerable women/ girls of menstrual age to ensure dignity and mitigate risks of GBV.• Distribution monitoring will be done to target beneficiaries of hygiene & MHM kits.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Major core principle of Top Relief Organization is protection mainstreaming as a foundation for all other protection work. Top Relief Organization has integrated proactive measures to minimize all SEA risks through continuous staff capacity development related to PSEA and Child Safeguarding. As a policy, all new Top Relief Organizations staff complete a mandatory induction which includes the Code of Conduct, PSEA and Child Safeguarding policies, and all employees are trained on PSEA at the begging of their assignment, and once in a year as a refreshment training. Top Relief Organization has Child Protection and Safeguarding focal point to address any allegations of SEA on adult population and harm on children, and beneficiaries and community members receive information about Top Relief Organization zero tolerance on SEA/child abuse, and information how and to whom to report any breach of PSEA/Child safeguarding policies. GBV is widespread in South Sudan, and women and girls, as main users of water sources face significant threats to be exposed to GBV incidents. To prevent further GBV cases, boreholes, water points and latrines will be designed to protect women and girls from harassment, attacks, physical and sexual violence by establishing them on safe distance, separated latrines for women and men with doors, lockers. To prevent GBV cases, Top Relief Organization will conduct awareness sessions on hygiene and GBV; distribute MHM dignity kits based on beneficiaries’ selection of items; and, refer GBV survivors – identified of self-referred to service providers specialized in GBV survivors’ assistance.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

TRO planned intervention is in line with WASH cluster Objective that will seek to provide sustainable WASH services addressing the vital needs, public health and protection issues through an AAP community driven approaches like water management committee mobilization and training for the targeted water points. This will impact on the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition by decreasing the GAM rate among under-5 children in severity of IPC Phase 3. The cross sectorial approach will ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical basic services to most vulnerable population while taking into account shocks and stresses caused by displacement like floods and conflicts.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

TRO has a security management system in place, where the Executive Director is overall with the line managers taking the responsibilities of staff below them. Working a long side the local authority in project location, the environment is sufficiently safe for project implementation. TRO has an updated security management plans for all bases. As part of the monitoring system, we have mechanisms in place such as the provision of security training ( HEAT)/ Security briefs, tracking staff movements and a sharing the security information to staff to equip them with the knowledge of what's happening in their contexts. This is through regular security reports. We ensure that all incidents are reported by staff with provision of analysis of the incidents to better understand the impact to the program and staff in order to prepare and prevent where necessary. Field level staff will be recruited from the local community who are familiar with the security of the place and that the community will accept. To ensure understanding, ownership and acceptance of the project, local staff have advantage of explaining the protect contents to the beneficiaries – what their entitlements, roles and responsibilities are, and the expectations of the implementing agency. There have been cases of local rising against other non- nationals or non-natives of the project location. Using locals is therefore, provides safe atmosphere for the project activities. Staff will also undergo security training and operational guidelines that will be followed strictly. The RRC which is the focal point for humanitarian in the counties will be contacted and informed about the plan to carry out an intervention for a specific duration. Safety audits will form part of the monitoring exercise, assessing do no harm standards, as well as reviewing complaints and feedback on safety from beneficiaries

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Beneficiaries selected for training of WASH actors will involve hygiene promoters, pump mechanics. Water management committee and local authorities concerned for the project will be chosen locally from the beneficiaries. Their capacity enhancement will involve both formal training and on job training. This project will provide refresher training to local pump mechanics distributed in the 4 counties of the project locations. Hygiene promoters and county resource committee will help in management of community resources. During the training TRO will emphasize on cost recovery to users for basic maintenance and chain of logistic for supplies needed. Much weight will be placed on daily care and routine maintenance of community resources. At the end of the project the water and sanitation facilities will be handed over to relevant line ministries.

Safe programming and mainstreaming gender at all stages will be ensured. Top Relief Organization (TRO) will implement and hold itself accountable to TRO Minimum Standards for Gender Equality and Women’s Rights in EmergenciesProtection risks analysis is incorporated in all Top Relief Organizations (TRO) programming to ensure safety of response team and the larger community benefiting from the services. Safety audits are carried out before activity implementation, during implementation and at end of the project. Feedbacks received through FGD and Community Complaints Response and Feedback Mechanism are acted upon during scale ups and shared as lessons learned, the project identifies specific areas of risk which include safety and dignity, humanitarian access to crisis affected populations, populations access to aid, beneficiary privacy, individuals with special needs/risks, social relations, fraud and diversion with protection implications and market impacts with protection implications. The project will include affected communities as participants in all phases of the programme cycle to ensure that affected populations identify their own protection risks, benefits and self protection mechanisms. TRO will consider whether intervention will create or exacerbate protection risks or benefits for individuals, households and communities, engage individuals with different and specifc needs and protection risks, establish two way feedback mechanisms and focal points to ensure regular communication.
