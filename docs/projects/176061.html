Project Summary

PURPOSE: MAG will clear high priority cluster munition strikes and/or minefields in order to provide safe access to land in areas with high rates of returns and expected returns, building resilience by releasing land back to the communities that can be safely used for agriculture, livelihoods activities such as making charcoal or collecting building materials for sale, settlement and the establishment of public services. In addition, MAG will provide explosive ordnance risk education to IDPs, returnees, and host community members, with a special emphasis on reaching children.
TARGET: The multi-task team will clear 398,000m2 of land during one year, and survey and clear 24 hazardous areas. The community liaison team will provide risk education to 19,200 women, girls, boys, and men. The beneficiary target assumes that the current requirement to deliver EORE on a house-by-housebasis, due to the COVID-19 outbreak, will be lifted in the near future.
SUMMARY OF ACTIVITIES: MAG will deploy one multi-task team to conduct clearance of cluster munitions and minefields in the Equatoria region, along main routes and in areas where returnees from Uganda are expected to settle. In addition, MAG will deploy an integrated community liaison team to provide explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) to vulnerable community members, targeting IDPs, returnees, and children. Depending on funding availability, the project can be scaled up with more teams, enabling a larger response.

1 - Needs

The history of multiple conflicts has left South Sudan scarred with the effects of these wars. Landmines, cluster bombs and other explosive ordnance continue to pose a threat more than a decade and a half since the CPA ended the civil conflict between the southern states of Sudan and the Khartoum government. More recent civil conflict has compounded this problem—displacing people from familiar land and putting IDPs and returnees in danger from unknown hazards. South Sudanese refugees in Uganda often did not receive explosive ordnance risk education; putting them in danger when they return to South Sudan, especially the highly contaminated Equatoria region. According to the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview, 19.33 million square meters of land is suspected of being contaminated with mines and explosive remnants of war and 0.7 million people are in need of mine action assistance. In 2020 there were 57 casualties from explosive ordnance accidents according to the IMSMA database, with boys making up the vast majority of those killed (14) and those injured (37). In the past five years, a plurality of accidents (38%) took place while playing or doing recreational activities, while 15% of accidents took place while traveling. In addition, 56% of victims/survivors in the past five years are boys and a further 12% are girls (compared to 22% of victim/survivors who are men and 9% who are women), demonstrating the risk face by children (all data is from the IMSMA database). However, explosive ordnance doesn’t discriminate and all members of the community are at risk. MAG will reach all members of a community with EORE, recognizing the specific risks they are faced with according to the gender, age, and disability status, MAG will place special emphasis on reaching children, IDPs, and returnees. The Equatoria region is expected to receive high numbers of returnees, and this region is also the most heavily contaminated region. As of December 2020, there were 183 total hazardous areas in Central Equatoria and 114 total hazardous areas in Eastern Equatoria. The two states combined contain 53% of the known contaminated area and 72% of number of known HAs. Within Central and Eastern Equatoria, Juba county, Magwi county, Terekeka county, and Torit county are the most heavily contaminated. These explosive hazards can block communities from safely accessing public services, such as schools and health clinics (for example, in Lobonok county, MAG previously cleared a minefield that was blocking both a primary school and a health centre), infrastructure such as boreholes (MAG recently cleared a BAC task in Amee that surrounded the local borehole), agricultural land (for example, in Panyagor, MAG raised a hazardous area after conducting survey and learning of a woman who detonated a submunition while preparing a new area in preparation for planting), and housing (such as in Amika boma where MAG removed a submunition that had been buried under a residents door step, only to become exposed after a period of heavy rain.

2 - Project activities

MAG’s activities will support strategic objective 1 by reducing morbidity and mortality, protection threats, and incidents for the most vulnerable populations in three counties with the highest severity level. The activities will reduce the vulnerability of women, men, girls, and boys at risk of mortality and morbidity, through risk mitigation measures such as survey and clearance and EORE. MAG will deploy one mixed-gender MTT team to conduct survey and clearance in three counties with the highest level of need: Magwi county, Juba county, and Torit county. When appropriate for the terrain, the MTT will be supported by a Bozena with mulcher attachment (part resourced by the project), which speeds the rate of clearance by efficiently cutting through vegetation to allow the use of metal detectors. MAG expects to clear 398,000 square meters of land. Whenever reports of explosive ordnance are received by the communities, MAG will immediate survey these areas, and if necessary, destroy the threat through an EOD spot task. MAG expects to survey and clear 24 hazardous areas. In addition, MAG’s mixed-gender community liaison team will provide EORE to 19,200 community members, IDPs, and returnees (this target assumes the ability to conduct community EORE sessions and is contingent on the COVID-19 situation). In addition to providing EORE to community members, MAG will train 12 community focal points, half of whom will be women, who are able to provide safety briefings to newcomers to the community (including IDPs and/or returnees) and can also gather information about explosive ordnance known in the community and relay this information to MAG. MAG’s response is scalable and the project capacity can be increased if necessary, depending on funding availability.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

MAG has robust safeguarding policies (which include PSEA) and procedures in place that govern all aspects of MAG’s activities. In order to ensure that beneficiaries are provided with the necessary information and able to make complaints. MAG’s community liaison teams will be able to communicate this information to beneficiaries during the course of their activities.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

MAG’s community liaison team (which is mixed gender) visits individual households to provide EORE and to find out about contamination the area. This also gives community members and opportunity to provide feedback or express concerns. Because visits are made to individual households, vulnerable individuals who are not able to access community and public services are able still able to be reached with EORE, and it also allows them an opportunity to interface with MAG community liaison staff and provide feedback if necessary.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

MAG deploys a community liaison team. Community Liaison teams meet with both community leaders and community members (through focus group discussions) to introduce MAG and the work we do. They explain the activities that will take place within the community, and conduct community mapping exercises to learn how the contamination impacts the community and what the communities priorities’ for clearance are. In addition to meetings with community liaison team, Technical Field Managers, often accompanied by their team leaders, will also meet with community leadership. Frequent meeting between MAG and community members and the different local authorities have allowed MAG to address concerns that the community may have.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from communities is transmitted to the community liaison manager and/or the technical operations manager as appropriate. Communities are able to request additional meetings. MAG will then work with the community to address any feedback or concerns including safeguarding and PSEA. For example, EORE sessions can be held at different times as requested by communities. Communities may request the training of community focal points.
