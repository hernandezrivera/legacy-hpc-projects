Project Summary

"The project aims to Improve the well-being and health and provide integrated healthcare supports to the Rohingyas in the camp and host communities in Ukhiya and Technaf Cox’s Bazar funded by FCDO formar DFID as part of their commitment to financial support till March 2021. The project includes operation of One Comprehensive Maternity Center, 5 Primary Healthcare Center (PHC), 5 Basic Clinics (Health Posts) and 26 mHealth teams (Outreach Health). Comprehensive Maternity Center is listed as Referral Point and ensure comprehensive maternal Healthcare services including Caesarean section for both Host and Rohingy. Two Primary Healthcare Center (PHC) in camp 7 and 26 is already providing services in affiliation with DGHS. In camp 26 which has about 40,000 population, the PHC has been handed over to Frienship by DGHS for operational support including all human and material support. Other three Primary Healthcare Center (PHC) is in planning for construction following the need asseessment of Health Sector. 5 Basic Clinics (Health Posts) are providing primary Health services in different camps. The project focuses on protection, equitable access to health services without any discrimination specially targeting the most vulnerable groups (women, children, pregnant/lactating women, people with disabilities, the elderly, GBV and conflict-affected survivors).
"

1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya influx started in August 2017, and it is one of the worst humanitarian crises of today. An estimated 860,697 Rohingyas are currently sheltered in various camps in Cox’s Bazar, as of August 31, 2020 (Joint Government of Bangladesh - UNHCR Population Factsheet), putting a lot of pressure on existing infrastructure, services and the environment. As in 2020, ensuring adherence to quality, respectful implementation of the minimum essential service package remains a top priority for 2021, however the arrival of a pandemic to the camp setting created additional challenges to service provision. To date, more than 350 refugees were diagnosed with COVID-19. In April, ANC visits dropped by 36.7%, and between January and June, institutional deliveries by 23%. The demand for MHPSS services increased further as a result of fear, isolation, and stigmatization. Outside of COVID-19, essential services require continued attention. Critical gaps continue to exist for reproductive health including family planning. Long-acting reversible contraceptives are not widely available, with IUD devices offered in only 43% of facilities. Referral pathways for obstetric and other emergencies and for specialty services continue to be a challenge in terms of cost, medically-equipped transport, and availability and capacity of human resource. Services for gender-based violence are not yet offered at all facilities; less than 50% of primary health care centers offer comprehensive GBV medical services. There is a need to enhance timely childhood immunization uptake and awareness to protect against vaccine preventable diseases; in 2020 only 25% of children completed measles 2 vaccination. Around 287362 services were provided reaching 264054 patients from January 2020 to November 2020 through this project. 204 delivery services were provided among which 138 were C-Section services. In the mentioned period 10% services among the total were SRH services. 60% of the beneficiaries were female and 40% were male. Despite the progress made so far, critical disparities exist in availability, access, quality and distribution of health services among sexes and age groups within as well as outside the camps. The needs and vulnerability vary likewise.
