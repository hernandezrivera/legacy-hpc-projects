Project Summary

UNRWA estimates that 420,000 Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to require humanitarian assistance in 2022. Many among them struggle to secure basic shelter and NFI need, and will be supported by UNRWA through this project, as part of the 2022 Humanitarian response plan. This intervention has three components:
1-Cash assistance for winterization: UNRWA will provide 420,000 PalRefs ( 123,529 families) with cash assistance for winterization ( one-off distribution of $ 265.37 for each family ) in all locations ( Damascus, Rural Damascus, Homs, Hama, Lattakia, Tartous, Aleppo and Deraa governorates/ urban and rural areas/. This is a direct disbursement of cash transfers through 02 financial service providers.
2-Cash assistance for minor shelter repair ( small scale): UNRWA will target 700 families (2380 beneficiaries) in three camps ( Ain Al Tal, Yarmouk and Deraa). The transfer value per family is $ 3,500 to be distributed through 02 service providers.
3-Inkind distribution of NFIs to 2500 families / 8500 beneficiaries (1250 returnee families and 1250 IDP families / 4250 returnee beneficiaries and 4,250 IDPs beneficiaries). At this stage, the locations are not identified and the eligibility of the beneficiaries to this intervention depends on their hardship. The unit cost of NFI kit is $ 61.52/beneficiary for returnees (blankets, mattresses, kitchen sets, jerry cans and Mats) and $66.92/beneficiary for IDPs (blankets, mattresses, kitchen sets, jerry cans Mats and Tarpaulin)

4 - Needs

According to UNRWA assessments, 420,000 Palestine refugees (96%) are in need of humanitarian assistance and 145,000 Palestine refugees (35%) are categorized as most vulnerable (female-headed households, families with members with disabilities, families headed by an older person, and unaccompanied minors/orphans). According to UNRWA crisis monitoring report on the Socio-economic Situation of Palestine Refugees in Syria (May 2021), 82 % of Palestine refugees live with less than USD 1.9 per day (at the rate of USD 1= SYP 3,250) after receiving UNRWA cash assistance. A total of 54 per cent of families reported having taken debts within the last 3 months. Around 40% of Palestine refugees remain in protracted displacement. As per HNO 2022, Adequate shelter conditions are vital to maintain physical health, mental well-being, and to promote and sustain learning and educational opportunity. Such conditions, when combined with overcrowding, significantly increase the possibility of exposure to protection risks and promote the spread of respiratory and epidemicprone diseases, including COVID-19. Around 70 per cent of the overall population in Syria reported a deterioration in their ability to meet basic needs. Consequently, basic items are further out of reach as families’ purchasing power is diminishing over time. The number of people in need of NFI support continues to increase in 2022 and is expected to continue to grow if a further worsening of the economic situation continues.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Abuse and exploitation: the Beneficiaries face the risk of abuse an exploitation when travelling to and from NFI distribution points or when withdrawing their cash assistance entitlement from the FSP outlets. - Mitigation measure: UNRWA ensures the proximity of cash distribution to the beneficiaries considering (1) a big network of FSPs outlets and (2) UNRWA gives the opportunity/flexibility to the beneficiaries to collect cash assistance from the nearest FSP outlet even though they are invited to attend another outlet. In addition, UNRWA assigns a staff to each FSP outlet to assist the beneficiaries withdrawing the relevant cash assistance amount and guide them through the disbursement process. -Monitoring: a complaint and feedback mechanism is in place to address the concerns of the beneficiaries on timely manner. Risk 2: Exclusion (request of documentation): Beneficiaries who do not have official IDs are not able to collect cash from FSPs outlets.Mitigation measure: UNRWA established a distribution center to exclusively distribute cash to the Beneficiaries who are not able to collect cash assistance from FSPs outlets due to lack of official IDs.Risk 3 Inflation: commodity prices are monitored by UNRWA area offices and if significant UNRWA will explore the opportunity to increase the transfer value.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: SEA in the context of NFI distributions and/or shelter repair (identification of beneficiaries and/or implementation) • Likelihood: medium • Impact: high • Mitigation Measures: 1. UNRWA staff prioritize vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and persons with disabilities in order to reduce their waiting time. 2. UNRWA provides training on protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion. 3. UNRWA is conducting protection audits on a biennial basis to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve its services and how to optimize its compliance to the main protection principles. 4. All UNRWA staff sign the code of conduct on commencement of their contracts. 5. Training to staff on the code of conduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA. 6. Reinforced messaging to community members on the importance of reporting SEA and ensure mechanisms are in place to ensure confidentiality and set up accessible and efficient complaint mechanisms. 7. UNRWA is enhancing focus on community engagement and participation, and makes sure feedback is provided.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS: 1. Humanitarian needs of of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. RISK 1 UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lack of funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Mitigation: 1. continue to monitor the situation and to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status of funding. 5. Continue to monitor its interventions through a results-based monitoring framework. RISK 2: UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. RISK 3: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. PPEs will be provided to frontline staff and sanitization materials will be made available at offices and distribution centres.
