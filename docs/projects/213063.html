Project Summary

In 2024, a staggering 16.7 million people in Syria, comprising 8.4 million females and 8.3 million males, require humanitarian assistance, witnessing a rise from the 15.3 million recorded in 2023. This dire situation is exacerbated by the aftermath of a series of earthquakes in February 2023, causing significant displacement, loss of lives, and severe damage to already fragile infrastructures. HNO for 2024 reveals a critical scenario where 5.5 million people, including over 2 million residing in last resort sites, are displaced. The earthquakes resulted in nearly 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 injuries in Syria, further straining a population already grappling with prolonged hostilities and economic crises. Families lost breadwinners, adding to the vulnerability of millions unable to meet basic needs.
Through the proposed project, SIF will directly contribute to strategic objectives of Education Sector for Syria through providing safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education (NFE) for out-of-school children (OOSC) and children at-risk of dropping out both within and outside public schools providing a path to enter formal education.
Proposed locations were identified according to the severity of needs, their level of accessibility, and SIF experience. Targeted locations are of severity scale 3 and 4. The comprehensive, multisector and multiyear approach was the determining element in the selection of the locations, with the aim of promoting sustainability of interventions, community self-reliance and resilience.
In line with the results of consultations with local community and authorities, and taking into account sector priorities, proposed intervention will contribute to enhance access to quality education and teaching for 60,865 children and adults in 2024, with an estimated 5% of target CWD. Response will seek to address education needs of children currently not in school and those at risk of dropping out through:
Self-Learning Programme for 6,414 OoSC
Remedial classes for 9,280 students at risk of dropping out
ECE classes for 382 preschool children
Rehabilitation of 4 damaged schools, targeting 3,060 PiN
Training for 665 teachers
Reach out to 10,642 OOSC through BTL campaigns
Supporting 550 young adults through FLN classes
Strengthening 17 PTA
Structured PSS for 6,922 children
The proposed project complements the activities of SIF and its partners in the target regions, particularly in the Shelter (damaged houses rehabilitation and shelter packages), WASH (water system and reservoirs rehabilitation), FSA (vocational training and bakery rehabilitation) and protection sectors (awareness and training of frontliners).
The inclusivity of Persons with Disabilities in the interventions, coupled with a focus on local capacities and community empowerment, exemplifies SIF's commitment to creating sustainable solutions. By encouraging contractors to recruit locally, SIF not only enhances the effectiveness of its intervention but also fosters a sense of ownership and collaboration within the communities served. Guided by safeguarding and protection principles, including the Do No Harm approach and adherence to SPHERE standards, SIF ensures an ethical and effective project cycle. SIF's commitment to humanitarian principles ensures accountability, transparency, and community empowerment.
SIF will implement this project in close coordination the MoE, Education sector, NGOs, SARC, local committees in order to bridge gaps in available services and ensure better access of People in Needs to services. SIF will closely follow Education Sector guidelines. In 2023, SIF targeted 25,763 PiN through Education interventions.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

SIF will seek project and implementation approvals from relevant authorities, collaborating with sector stakeholders, donors, SARC, and municipalities to select locations and beneficiaries. NFE activities will be conducted in public schools after hours, weekends/holidays, and community centers. Out-of-school children will be reached through the Back-to-Learning campaign in collaboration with SARC, while Early Childhood Education will benefit children aged 3-5 in community centers or schools. PTA support will be provided in targeted learning spaces, regular testing and monitoring of child progress will be conducted through field visits and data collection tools (Child Monitoring System and 4Ws). Rehabilitation of schools and WASH facilities adapted to CwD and gender-based specific needs will be carried out by contractors selected through a transparent tender process, ensure that a period of guarantee is included in the contracts for sustainability.Maintaining transparency and inclusivity, SIF engages continuously with affected populations through participatory approaches, ensuring structured dialogues with diverse groups to address protection concerns. Regular feedback mechanisms, community consultations, and grievance redressal processes are integral to SIF's accountability framework, incorporating community needs into project planning and implementation, fostering ownership and empowerment among beneficiaries.The risk of sexual exploitation is addressed by implementing comprehensive measures. These include integrating PSEA indicators into the monitoring framework, conducting rigorous vetting during recruitment with a focus on application and reference stages, ensuring the signing of a code of conduct that strictly prohibits SEA for all individuals associated with SIF, including staff, volunteers, and contractors. Additionally, contractors sign the SIF Code of Conduct at the time of the purchase order. SIF employees undergo PSEA training, and each targeted governorate has a designated PSEA focal point. Annual awareness sessions for community members cover the definition of SEA and reporting/whistleblowing channels. Community-based complaint mechanisms are accessible in all response locations, and SIF has developed a structured process, steps, and timeframe for the investigation of SEA allegations. SIF faces various risks, each with a likelihood, impact, and mitigation strategy. Measures include dialogue and follow-up to mitigate delays in local authority approvals, donor coordination and BoQ adjustments to manage inflation-driven price hikes, monitoring and preparedness to address security deterioration risks, financial controls and beneficiary verification to mitigate fraud and aid diversion risks, prevention measures and testing to address COVID-19 and cholera spread risks, and monitoring and planning to mitigate fuel shortage risks.
