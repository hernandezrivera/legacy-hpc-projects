Project Summary

The project aims to improve the education outcomes for male and female teachers, learning facilitators and other educational personnel by providing them with tailored training based on the Teachers in Crisis Contexts TICC Training , Literacy and Numeracy remedial training.
The TICC implementation module consists of three-month direct training for teachers with coaching session during this period. the project is planned for 12 months, and the training packages will be provided for 2300 Teachers, while the teachers will and report back improvement of the educational outcomes in their school thought the coaching and mentoring support.

4 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Syria, now in its eighth year, has forced 2.1 million, over one third of children, out-of-school and has put a further 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Over 5.8 million school-aged children (including more than 100,000 Palestine refugee children) and about 300,000 education personnel are in need of education assistance inside Syria, 61 percent of whom are in acute and immediate need. The provision of education services inside Syria is limited. Hostilities have resulted in over one in three schools being damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for shelter and other purposes. Schools are reported as the most common building type used for IDP sites in Syria, accounting for an estimated 32 per cent of collective centers. Due to a shortage of functional classrooms, classes are overcrowded and over one million children required to attend double-shift schools. An extreme shortage and underpayment of qualified teachers also limit the quality and provision of education. Children who are out of school face increased protection risks including child marriage and the worst forms of child labor. The vulnerability of school-age children with no access to learning opportunities has increased over the seven years of the crisis, putting children at higher risk of being exposed to exploitation, abuse and rights violations. Schools, accounting for an estimated 33 per cent of collective centers, were reported as the most common building type used for IDP sites in Syria. Increased internal displacement has put the current infrastructure under enormous pressure.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Gender dynamics can affect learners’ ability to access and participate in education. Learning barriers are often different for males and females, who face different risks and have different needs. Sometimes gender dynamics are obvious, but they can also be subtle or hidden. Education projects can be made responsive and fair by: • Addressing gender-based barriers so that all girls and boys, women and men can learn; • Respecting differences based on gender, and acknowledging gender, together with age, ethnicity, language, disability, and religion are all part of a learner’s identity; • Enabling education structures, systems and methodologies to be sensitive to all girls and boys, women and men; • Ensuring gender parity in education as part of a wider strategy to advance gender equality in society; The teacher training contains protection principles for all staff. Orange highly encourage people with disability to enroll in all levels of education as teachers, students or other the educational staff. Teaching staff will be trained on child protection ( as part of the TICC ) to become familiar with child protection mechanisms and procedures. All staff is required to sign a code of conduct adhering to PSEA principles. The number of children in the classroom enables the teacher to provide proper assistance for each child and deliver their specific needs. PRA matrix: Child safety incidents in school due to bullying and overburdened teachers (probability = 4/5) Resistance to gender mainstreaming from male leadership structures. (probability = 3/5) Attacks on schools (probability = 3/5) Security situation affecting implementation capacity negatively (e.g. denial of access) (probability = 3/5) Limited number of Master trainers in all Governorates who will receive training to provide cascade training (probability = 2/5) Education authorities delaying the approval of the training; as such other locations will not conduct the training in a timely manner (probability = 2/5)

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The project is designed in a way to minimize the risk for the teachers mainly female teachers during the capacity building process. In this respect, the training program will mitigate for risk factors accompanying the process like safety during courses and travel for long distances and its difficulties especially for female teachers. Activities will be implemented by active NGOs with experience in education sector and in cooperation with local actors like the education directorates. The type of educational assistance doesn’t cause any harm to the beneficiaries or their environment and relatives, as the selected curricula for teaching are either taken from UNRWA or from the official curriculum in the area of project implementation. The locations of implementing the project are chosen in coordination with the local councils in the targeted areas, to be the safest areas, to ensure that teachers are not exposed to any danger while they are in the areas of project implementation
