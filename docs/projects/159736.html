Project Summary

In reference to the oPt Strategic Humanitarian Objectives and in response to the protracted protection crisis continues in the oPt, which remains largely
attributable to Israel’s ongoing occupation, now in its 52nd year, the continuing internal
Palestinian divide and violations of international law, Humanitarian action will include
providing cash assistance for the most vulnerable communities and people in two main
modalities:
(1) Cash for Work (CFW): which is considered efficient and effective in provision of
immediate direct access to basic needs (especially food), and response to shocks and
thus an immediate relief to economic/poverty stress among population. Thus, the overall
response will contribute to enhancing economic access to basic needs especially food for
250 from the most vulnerable and protection threatened Households (HHs) of skilled and
unskilled persons through temporary employment opportunities in Community Based
Organizations (CBOs), Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) in addition to the private sector.
(2) Food vouchers: The intervention will also have the flexibility to provide food vouchers to 750 HHs for 6 months with average monthly value of 280 ILS. Humanitarian action is in line with the FSS cluster objectives especially Strategic Objective 2: “Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs”

1 - Needs

* A protracted protection crisis continues in the oPt, which remains largely attributable to Israel’s ongoing occupation, now in its 52nd year, the continuing internal Palestinian divide and violations of international law. After years of a relative absence of armed conflict since the 2014 hostilities, there has been a sharp deterioration in the humanitarian, human rights, security and political situation in the Gaza Strip in 2018. There is a sense of hopelessness and desperation among the population in Gaza, which is eroding coping mechanisms and resilience, while rising violence and tension are fuelling concerns of a renewed escalation of hostilities. * The economy is in ‘free fall’ according to the World Bank and poverty, unemployment and food insecurity are increasing, as are other core drivers of humanitarian need (OCHA 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview). In reference to AIDA’s “70+ INGOs call on AHLC donors to address the dire humanitarian situation in the oPt, The Gaza Strip is on the brink of collapse, its crippled economy resulting from Israel’s decade-long blockade, and further compounded by recent public employee salary cuts by the PA. The purchasing power is at record lows and the impact of additional income cuts in Gaza may result in a full humanitarian crisis. The fragile economic situation is indicated by high rates of poverty reached 53%, unemployment rates 54% (62% among females and 38% among males), which is compounding low resilience and high vulnerability to shock. * Food security in the Gaza Strip continues to be affected by the protracted nature of the crisis, repeated shocks, continued restrictions on freedom of movement, constrained productive capacities and a lack of economic opportunitiesAbout 80% of people in Gaza receive food assistance and other forms of social transfers which enable scarce cash resources to be spent on other essentials, preventing a further deterioration of food security and livelihood status, and reducing the impact of negative coping mechanisms. According to Socio-economic & Food Security Survey 2018, the level of food insecurity is particularly high in Gaza, where an estimated 68.5% of the households were severely or moderately food insecure in 2018 compared to 62% in 2013.

2 - Activities

(1) Temporary employment opportunity (Conditional - Not restricted): for one member of 250 HHs (Skilled or Unskilled - males and females, including at least 5% of persons with disability) for a period of 5 - 6 months at 100 Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) in addition to the private sector. (2) Food vouchers (Unconditional - Restricted): Cash assistance for 750 HHs for a period of 6 months. The average monthly cash will be around 280 ILS. The duration of assistance is determined based previous experience in dealing with food assistance as well as Islamic Relief’s integration approach in providing assistance to vulnerable HHs. Other Humanitarian assistance maybe provided after assessing the family profile. Value of voucher is calculated based on FSS cluster discussions and Socio-economic & Food Security Survey formulas (3) Inclusivity & protection mainstreaming that will include: Awareness and capacity building activities among the project stakeholders including beneficiaries and hosting institutions. Incorporating the necessary I&P requirements and actions into work policies, procedure, forms, as well as the work environment / conditions at the hosting institutions. Total BNFs are Now 1,000 HHs (5,700 individuals) in comparison with previous project (Was 400 HHs) as intervention is optimized into two main components (CfW + Food vouchers). Food vouchers modality to 750 HHs is added to the project intervention as a second main component with decrease in CfW BNFs to 250 HHs instead of 400. The two main components are detailed in activities and budget as well. Main Activities: • Community sensitization and beneficiary selection with gender consideration (The lists of potential beneficiaries will be nominated from the database of Islamic Relief (Poor families in Focus, study by Islamic Relief) in consultation with FSS partners. Priority will be given to: female-headed household, those who have no source of income, no person working in the family families with disabled family members and elderly and families living in rented houses. Gender perspective will be promoted where both genders will be given the opportunity to benefit from this project. The BNF selection mechanism will ensure no one is neglected, abused, discriminated or exploited. Older people, women, men, girls, boys, minority groups and individuals will be identified in the needs assessment. • Selection of hosting organizations/businesses (CfW modality): Islamic Relief has a network of over 300 local CBOs and private sector companies to host the graduates. In particular CBOs that care for female graduates and working women will be given priority. • Identification of beneficiaries (CfW and Food vouchers) • Employment (CfW modality): The beneficiaries will be given temporary work placements at local civil society organizations, women organizations, and private sector companies and agricultural CBOs / farms for periods from 5 to 6 months, with various levels of wages. Female employment process will ensure safe and secure conditions in addition to social acceptance through different contexts. The employment periods, and wages will be according to the following preliminary breakdown depending on rates approved through Food Security Sector and Cash Programming Working Group. • Selection of purchasing points (Food vouchers): through local competitive bidding.

Midterm and final evaluations will be conduct by M&E department to learn lessons and improve performance.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Islamic Relief encourages and facilitates communities and people affected by crisis to provide feedback on their level of satisfaction with the quality and effectiveness of the assistance received, paying particular attention to the gender, age and diversity of those giving feedback. Tools will include:• Face-to-face feedback: in orientation meetings, focus group discussion sessions, lesson learned sessions, regular follow up visits by project team and MEAL officer to project sites to listen to beneficiaries and organizations; • Landlines and mobile numbers: easy access from all places and available on website and all publications;• Feedback boxes: at IRPAL premises and at host organizations;• IRPAL and partners’ staff at field level.The frequency of using each communication tool will be specified MEAL and project team, The effective use of feedback will be on two levels:• Short-term: Corrective actions, enhance services, improve deliverables, strengthening relationship with BNFs, solve problems, avoid problems and corruption.• Long-term: Learning for future interventions, improve proposals and CNs, monitoring and evaluation, satisfaction of BNFs

8.6.b - If 'Other', please specify

IRPAL adopts and applies professional complaints and response mechanisms in line with recent Humanitarian standards and polices especially the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) and Accountable Now (AN) where all beneficiaries have the right to a Say and IRPAL has the duty to respond. According to the Complaint Policy, the Quality Assurance Unit is the Complaint focal point / department. IRPAL strives to provide any help needed to bring complaints in an accessible manner, in accordance with the stakeholder’s needs, age and abilities through:• Complaints by telephone/SMS: Landline and mobile• Complaints in person• In writing/through post• Online: through e-mail and website

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

• At the onset of the project, IRPAL team will do the necessary analysis of the geographic and social context and the relations between the beneficiaries’ and the hosting institutions. this will also include the analysis of dividers and sources of tension, and the connectors and capacities for social cohesion, and the analyse the impact of the project on dividers and connectors, finally the risk analysis will also be essential to analyze the possible risk and do the necessary risk management planning and response.• Based on the core humanitarian principle of “do no harm”, IRPAL will take all necessary measures to prevent conflicts within target groups and with the hosting institutions, including consultations and complaint/feedback mechanisms, making efforts to prevent corruption or any type of exclusion or violence during project implementation.• The active engagement of different community groups into the project planning and implementation will minimize the divisions and will strengthens connectors will enhance social cohesion.• Adherence to the local labour law and international labour standards will help ensure that decent work conditions and improve the sense of human dignity among the beneficiaries. It will also ensure an increasing women’s participation into the project and engagement into the labour market. • Ensuring that any terms of employment do not discourage women’s participation and ensure that men and women are paid equal wages for work of equal value • Tailor the employment type to provide appropriate jobs for women and/or target female headed households, and give the women workers (whenever possible and practical) the choice to select where they want to be assigned (closer to town, outside town or inside the towns) for them to be able to work comfortably without any harassment/embarrassment and within easy reach to get back home. • Ensure the women workers can collect their wages in person instead of the household head; • Create a safe and inclusive work environment by offering women solutions to overcome the challenges that hinder their economic participation, and thus contribute to achieving inclusive and sustainable economic growth; • Applying feedback and complaint mechanisms: Feedback and accountability mechanisms are a key part of conflict sensitivity and are essential to ensure positive relationships with local communities. Proper information and complaint mechanisms available to both literate and non-literate persons will can help reduce the potential for tension and conflict to be exacerbated by the project. Complaints may be made regarding unfair treatment, misconduct, rights to appropriate assistance, workplace and labour conditions, wage related issues etc.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The proposed humanitarian response will be provided without exposing targeted populations to further protection threats or risks, and, to the extent possible, contribute to improve their physical security and integrity. protection principles are considered throughout the humanitarian action. The four principles include: (1) Prioritise safety and dignity and avoid causing harm: All BNFs will have clear and regular access to project information through orientation sessions, BNFs of CfW will be employed in safe places according to their specialization and clear tasks. BNFs of food assistance will have smooth access to vendors at their locations with all needed consideration (2) Meaningful access: BNFs access to assistance and services - in proportion to needs and without barriers (e.g. discrimination). Particular attention will be provided to the most vulnerable groups like: Women, Elderly, PWDs who may face particular problems in accessing services, because of reduced mobility, marginalization, or other constraints. This applies to Orientation sessions, hosting parties and vendors markets(3) Accountability: Accessible Feedback and complaint mechanisms is in place and will be detailed in Orientation sessions and through the tools . (4) Participation and empowerment: Support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights through effective participation in focus groups, Orientation sessions at the beginning of the project, data collection and analysis and technical committees.
