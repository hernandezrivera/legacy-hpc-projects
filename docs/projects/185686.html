Project Summary

Save the Children International (SCI) will implement preventive nutrition services through 8 health posts in 8 camps. The nutrition program will target pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and under-five children with a special focus on two children who attended the health post for any occasion. SCI will follow the “IYCF-E Multisectoral framework for action” for designing an integrated service model with Health and Child protection sector. Management of non-breastfed (NBF) children will be one of the priorities with appropriate logistics support. SCI will maintain referral linkage with Integrated Nutrition Facilities (INF) for identified malnourished cases. SCI will continue leading the IYCF-E technical group and provide strategic guidance to the nutrition sector for evidence-based decision making and partner’s capacity building on IYCF-E and CMAMI.
The nutrition program comprises of nutrition assessment of all the PLWs and under two children, nutrition education and counselling on IYCF-E, cooking demonstration, and referral of identifying malnourished cases to INF or stabilization centre. Community awareness sessions will be organized with community influential (Imam/Majhi) for creating a community support system. Gender roles and negative practices that restrict optimal nutrition behaviour will be highlighted during these sessions. Transformative gender role targeting support for unpaid care tasks and mobility will be focused during household negotiated counselling and group awareness sessions. Knowledge to practice transformation requires repeated contact and counselling support. SCI will utilize health post visit as an additional contact opportunity and emphasized nutrition promotion and ensure referral. In targeting, the nutrition coverage gap of around 20% is considered and SCI will report service received and only new case referral for avoiding duplication error with existing INF coverage.
SCI will continue its technical assistance to implementing partners in managing NBF children and required lifesaving logistics will be distributed to specific cases following all the regulations and technical protocols. Capacity building and on-job training on CMAMI and NBF management will be organized with support from nutrition sector partners.
Inclusion of PWD is one of the priorities and health posts included nutrition corners will be made suitable for their access and comfort for service seeking. Community workers will ensure that PWDs are practising the optimal nutrition practices and berries can be minimized through regular household visits.
SCI is a member of the PSEA network. SCI will give importance to the protection from and responding to sexual exploitation and abuse. SCI has a strong reporting and monitoring mechanism to identify PSEA cases. SCI will conduct periodical training, awareness sessions for the staff, volunteers, and other stakeholders on PSEA policy.
SCI will lead IYCFE TWG as before and provide advisory support to the nutrition sector on generating evidence-based strategic guidance and implementation modalities on IYCF-E.

1 - Identified Needs

The levels of acute malnutrition among Rohingya refugees living in Cox’s Bazar remain high. Rohingya refugee children under the age of 5, pregnant and lactating women (PLW), and adolescent girls represent particularly vulnerable groups, with high levels of acute malnutrition. The reasons for this are multifold and include limited support for mothers and young children, lack of safe breastfeeding spaces, suboptimal hygiene and sanitation practices, poor WASH facilities (in particular low access to safe water), and low dietary diversity. There is thus a need not only to maintain but to further scale up and improve, the quality of nutrition services currently available in the camps.The latest Joint multisectoral need assessment indicated that almost all households have unmet multi-sectoral needs (LSG severity score > 2), for nutrition which is 12%. The main drivers of nutrition LSGs were found to be Households reporting children not having been screened or not having received treatment for malnutrition when needed. 18% of children aged 6-59 months were reported as not having been screened for malnutrition by community nutrition volunteers or nutrition facilities. 41% of PLW were reported as not having been screened for malnutrition by community nutrition volunteers or nutrition facility staff during the current pregnancy or while breastfeeding. 15% of households with PLW reported not having received messages related to infant and young child feeding practices and around 30% of PLW were reported as not having received iron and folic acid tablets during the current pregnancy or while breastfeeding.Moreover, SC conducted an MHPSS assessment in 2019 and found concerning signs of developmental delay and malnutrition in Rohingya children, implying that nutritional status, psychosocial distress, lack of cognitive stimulation (education), and psychological trauma are all contributing to developmental delays. Much of this is still valid in the camps. Continuing the efforts in nutrition messaging and social behavioral change communication (SBCC) in camps and host communities with partner organizations is also necessary. Meeting these gaps requires service modality beyond a single nutrition service point rather than reaching every possible contact facility available for multiple exposers to counseling and follow-up of compliance.
