Project Summary

PUI has been operational in Central and South Iraq (CSI) since 1997 and in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq (KRI) since November 2013, responding to the Syrian refugee crisis, IDP and returnee movements. In 2019, PUI intends to continue and enhance its activities within the country: we are currently intervening in Anbar, Baghdad, Ninewa, and Dohuk, and plan to extend our activities within these governorates, as they have been heavily affected by the conflict and/or by the populations’ movements. PUI is intervening in areas considered as very high and high severity hotspots in IOM DTM return index from September 2018. The majority of PUI’s activities are located in Anbar and Ninewa governorates, as they are ranked among the areas the most affected by the conflict. More precisely, the current and planned interventions are in Ninewa are in: East and West Mosul, Al Hamdaniya, Muhalabiya. The interventions in Anbar governorate are in Qaim, Heet, Ana and Haditha districts and on the Falluja-Ramadi strip.
First, PUI in 2019 intends to continue and extend its health and mental health activities: by maintaining the management of 2 in-camp PHCCs, as long as the local authorities are unable to take-over (Salamiyah 2 in Ninewa and Bajet Kandala 1 in Dohuk governorate). In Salamiyah 2, comprehensive primary health care services and referrals to secondary care are provided, as well as reproductive health services and Mental Health and Psychosocial support (MHPSS) services. In Bajet Kandala 1, primary health care services and referrals to appropriate facilities are provided by the camp population, and PUI is extending the delivery of care to MHPSS services and plans to increase this activity over 2019 due to the high needs observed in the camp.
PUI plans to keep on sensitizing the populations and increase the MHPSS services coverage in Iraq, especially in the governorates which were the most affected by the recent conflict (i.e. Ninewa and Anbar). There is an important stigma around mental health care in Iraq, and few professionals specialized in this field, while the needs of the populations are huge, considering that they experienced decades of conflict within the country. Thus, PUI will be supporting the MHPSS services at community level, primary health care level, specialized secondary health care level and at a public mental health, tertiary care level. PUI will engage in technical, material and financial support to government facilities, payment of incentives, equipment donations, MHPSS materials donations in order for them to deliver quality and essential mental health care services to the people in need.

Needs

In the IDPs camps where PUI is currently intervening in Ninewa and Dohuk governorates, the populations living there are most of the time unwilling to return, due to security challenges, lack of livelihoods and basic services. There is a persistent need for intervention in these camps, as no others actors have the capacity to take over. Salamiyah II PHCC is one of the biggest PHCCs in Iraq, and receives referred cases from other camps and surrounding villages. BK1 PHCC is receiving populations from BK2 camps and surrounding villages as well.In Anbar governorate, as most health facilities have been damaged (including secondary and tertiary health facilities), suffer a shortage of health personnel andthere is a limited capacity to respond to medical emergency cases. Health priorities have been identified as follows: providing primary health care services, including immunization, reproductive health services, communicable disease surveillance and management, treatment for non-communicable diseases, clinical assessment and management of mental health cases to the affected population and host communities. Although actors like PUI are working in health facilities to provide these services, there is still a dire need for MHPSS at community level. The population of Mosul has been living under ISg for three years in a conflict zone that has seen gun fire, brutal public executions, aerial bombardments, mines and suicide bombs on a daily basis. There is well documented evidence of targeting of civilians in Mosul with reports of mass killings, beheadings, abduction, sexual violence and the forced recruitment of adults and children alongside indiscriminate means including airstrikes and the use of heavy weapons in densely populated urban areas, arbitrary detentions, revenge and extrajudicial killings. Reports by MHPSS actors such as PUI, IMC, and ACF reveal huge needs in terms of direct support to populations who have often undergone dramatic journeys to reach safety and have witnessed multiple potentially traumatizing events.

Activities

PUI is currently intervening in areas considered as very high and high severity hotspots in IOM DTM return index from September 2018. Most of PUI’s activities are located in Anbar and Ninewa governorates, since these regions are among the most affected by the conflict. More precisely, the current and planned interventions are in East and West Mosul, Al Hamdaniya, Muhalabiya, Qaim, Heet, Ana and Haditha districts and on the Falluja-Ramadi strip.Health activities:- Provision of comprehensive primary healthcare at in-camp Primary Health Care Centers (PHCC) in Dohuk and Ninewa governorates (comprehensive primary health care services and referrals to specialist/secondary care, integrated Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and community based health education)- Support to existing Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care facilities in Ninewa and Anbar. PUI will coordinate all of its Reproductive Health interventions with UNFPA. - Community health screening by outreach medical team conducting home and settlement visits; - Appropriate referral and follow-up support via cash transfers for referral (covering transportation) for the most vulnerable individuals, including women, children, the elderly and people with disabilities; - The promotion of health and hygiene either through home-visits or community awareness sessions conducted by the Community Health Workers (CHW), with activities paying particular attention to children under 5, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) and people with disabilities and individuals with chronic diseases. In addition to promotion of key health messages, PUI will provide information on the health systems and the services available; MHPSS Activities:- Provision of case management provided by outreach MHPSS team, including MHPSS activities tailored to the needs of the individual;- Appropriate and supported referral to specialist MHPSS services and follow up of patients or individuals needing additional MHPSS clinical treatment or intervention.- Provision of information and education on MHPSS services in Ninewa (Mosul, Muhalabiya) and Anbar (Ramadi, Falluja, Qaim, Haditha, Ana, Heet districts). - Integration of comprehensive MHPSS services in health facilities in Ninewa, Anbar and Baghdad) and MHPSS training and capacity building for DOH staff - Supporting the delivery of MHPSS services in Mosul at community, primary health care and specialized secondary and tertiary health care levels.

Protection Risk Analysis

Critical living conditions for a significant number of displaced families in Ninewa and Anbar Governorates, as well as the incapacity of the public health system to meet primary health needs, have led to increased risk of transmission of communicable diseases and lower level of self-care, which primarily presents a risk for children under 5, pregnant/ lactating women, people with a disability and people with chronic diseases including mental health disorders. Poor hygiene practices and the lack of latrines in many communities, but especially amongst the displaced in rural areas, increase the risk of diarrheal diseases, including outbreaks of cholera. Continued population movements and the interruption of regular vaccination cycles are expected to also increase the risk of outbreaks of other transmittable diseases. Finally, people with chronic or underlying longer term diseases may develop a more serious condition without proper access to health care services.

Mitigation Measures

PUI will measure the vulnerability of all potential beneficiaries before the selection, taking into account the vulnerability of individuals or groups such as: number of children under 5, female headed HHs, child headed HHs, people with a disability, those with chronic disease and people with limited or no source of income. PUI commits to report and share protection concerns with the Protection cluster, incl. GBV and Child Protection sub-clusters. Furthermore, in all of its interventions, PUI ensures its teams are gender balanced, as this approach allows reaching equally girls and boys, women and men. Awareness sessions and focus groups are organized for females/males only, and it allows getting more feedback as the participants feel more comfortable to raise their concerns. This approach enables the teams to address gender specific issues. PUI will aim at including a sufficient number of women in its teams for more direct exchanges with women from the community, at involving women in all levels of surveys and complaints and response mechanisms and at collecting their feedbacks and recommendations to address any protection issue. Health and Hygiene Promoters will organize campaigns or sessions to address issues focusing on women’s health, like breast feeding, birth spacing between pregnancies, nutrition in pregnancy and lactation etc. PUI will also be working on training and capacity building to create a supportive environment within which Sexual and Gender-based Violence survivors can present as part of a longer term strategy to identify and address the issues faced by survivors in accessing care. This work will be conducted in close coordination with UNFPA and key partners in this sector.

How is your project accountable to affected population

PUI is committed to ensuring that its interventions are appropriate and designed in a way which positively contributes to the targeted communities. PUI has implemented the IASC commitments on AAP as follows:Leadership Governance: PUI is equipped with an institutional Program Quality Assurance policy that encompasses PUI’s commitments toward the supported communities. PUI Iraq has a dedicated MEAL department. Together with the field-based teams, the MEAL Coordinator will support the program teams in respecting the IASC’s Five Commitments and ensure the objectives of the project are reached in as highly qualiy manner as possible. All PUI frontline staff will conduct or arrange training by specialist technical agencies on basic MH principles, Psychologicall First Aid (PFA), and identification and referral mechanisms to enable PUI staff and DOH staff in PUI supported facilities to identify MHPSS issues and direct patients through the correct referral pathway. Transparency: PUI provides information to the communities about ongoing and future activities through Community Leaders and key stakeholders, including health professionals and DOH and MOH. PUI’s responsibilities to deliver project activities are outlined in memorandums of understanding, or in exchange of letters with government authorities. PUI coordinates closely with government authorities and community leaders throughout the delivery of project activities to ensure that activities remain aligned with national and international standards and are responding to the identified needs of the population. Feedback and complaints: PUI has established several communication channels (including IDP Call center, hotline, feedback boxes, frequent field visits to ensure a dialogue is held with the beneficiaries) to be accessible to the complaints and feedbacks of the beneficiaries. This ensures the transparency of the action, the participation and inclusion of the beneficiaries through dialogue, as well as systematic feedback and responses to their complaints. PUI produces lessons learned thanks to these mechanisms and is capable to adapt its interventions as close as possible to the people’s expressed needs.Participation: PUI considers effective Communication with Communities (CwC) as essential within its interventions to ensure accountability towards people benefiting from its health and mental health activities. Community consultation is incorporated into initial needs and vulnerability assessments and regular site assessments for mobile activities. A minimum of 2 satisfaction surveys are held for each activity to assess the perceptions of the beneficiaries about the services provided, as well as the participatory process implemented in the communities. This satisfaction surveys will also enable PUI to identify issues and enhance the quality of services during the project.Design, Monitoring and Evaluation: In addition to the presence of dedicated monitoring staff, PUI conducted frequent monitoring missions to assess the quality of implementation and ensure that PUI’s interventions remain tailored to the evolving needs of the populations throughout the project.PUI has the in-country experience and technical capacity to conduct Rapid Needs Assessments, to be able to react to emergencies and share information with partners.PUI activities in Iraq are based on a comprehensive integrated approach, focusing on Protection as cross-cutting theme.PUI will ensure supported facilities are safely accessible for all communities present in the areas of intervention with a special focus on the most vulnerable populations, to ensure that their needs are addressed. To understand broader humanitarian needs, PUI will work with the Protection Cluster and regional WGs to contribute to Rapid Protection Assessments.PUI will coordinate with the Protection Cluster to obtain information on macro-level protection issues, and to address the main risks for the populations in target locations.
