Project Summary


This project aims at providing lifesaving shelter and NFI items to the most vulnerable newly displaced IDPS, refugees, returnees and host communities in Kajo Keji, Yei, Lainya, of central equatoria state and Magwi and Lafon counties of Eastern equatoria state. These populations that consists of the IDPS and host communities have been experiencing a confluence of consistent clashes between warring parties in the country especially the most recent which was between September and October 2018 in yei and Lainya which had spill overs into kajokeji leading to displacements into the korijo idp settlements in liwolo payam in western kajokeji. the recent idps who fled the crises from mukaya,Lainya and some parts of yei and sokare in liwolo payam were forced to settle in korijo which is the newest idp settlement in kajokeji. This report was verified through an Irna conducted on the 18/10/2018 by Titi foundation teams and partners operating in kajokeji. Given that this areas where titi foundation operates as a static partner in, are also close to the border of Uganda, Kenya and DR.Congo we have experienced an inflow of mainly returnees and some refugees into areas like kajokeji through the moyo and mijale points, magwi and Lafon through lobone from Uganda and yei where the returnees are hailing from Uganda and DR.Congo escaping the harsh conditions in the various refugee camps in search for better living conditions in south sudan. These locations have not just been experiencing the effects of war alone but also in the 2018 september –october IPC released results we have seen that areas like kajokeji have been classified in phase 4 and the other locations between phase 3-4 which depicts emergency situation and depicting the high need for an intervention in various sectors including shelter/nfi.These areas have also been unique in that politically they are controlled by both IG and IO parties especially locations like kajokeji, Lainya and partially yei in central equatoria which has also to a greater extent escalated the suffering of the populations in this areas.
Its in this regard and background therefore that Titi foundation has identified through information gathered from sources like iom DTM,REACH SURVEYS and the recently just concluded IRNA as of 18/10/2018 that the population in some locations like Korijo in kajokeji is hosting a total population of 40294 newly displaced idps and hence the huge Shelter and NFI needs for vulnerable populations in these locations (according to the October/18/2018 IRNA report). With Titi foundation as the only shelter/nfi partner that has demonstrated the ability and competence to deliver s/nfi assistance to the most vulnerable populations in this hard to reach areas like kajokeji where we just recently concluded some assistance to nearly 766 households. Titi also has an advanatage over other partners in that we have existing agreements with URA(Uganda revenue authority) to use Uganda routes to deliver services to border areas like kajokeji, morobo, and magwi through its territories during our project implementation process and hence curbing the problem related to access.
Titi foundation in its planned intervention will distribute inkind s/nfi items like blankets, plastic sheet mosquito nets, kitchen sets, sleeping mats to beneficiaries which will improve both their living conditions and access to emergency shelter which will protect the displaced persons from harsh weather conditions and protection risks, support the communities in an integrated and dignied manner by using locally available materials to support livelihoods through skills training. The shelter and NFI items that will be distributed to beneficiaries will also contribute to improvement of their health, enhance protection and better hygiene through a multi-sectoral survival kit distribution. The purpose of the project is also to engage the community in a way that stimulates active participation hence increasing their ability to respond to new shocks and build on existing skills. The project will also ensure quality monitoring services which will foster fast life saving response to the right beneficiaries, enhance the ability to use S/NFIs for supporting results based health, protection, livelihood, and hygiene needs, and really to improve understanding of trends in order to reduce overlap and reach the truly vulnerable populations who can’t help themselves.
Titi foundation which already has office spaces and implementing ongoing projects like FSL, GBV and protection activities in all this locations will mainstream its activities like the ongoing fuel efficient stoves and briquettes program which is aimed at mitigating gender based violence will be mainstreamed to our planned s/nfi interventions to ensure that the beneficiaries will also be trained along the (FES) project to reduce on the possibility of gender based violence that they could face. Titi will also blend ongoing projects like child protection especially to our s/nfi beneficiary category such as child headed households and people living with disabilities to acquire psychosocial support (pss) by involving our specialized staffs from the various sectors to assist our s/nfi beneficiaries acquire our extra services.
Titi Foundation will target 25000 (5500 household) most affected vulnerable people with distribution of s/nfi kits, upgrading or repairing of substandard shelters and will explore the targeted beneficiaries on relevant and environmentally friendly emergency shelter materials to reduce costs of construction and improve shelter conditions based on content.

Needs

Since the signing of Khartoum Peace Accord (12/09/2018) between SPLA – IG and SPLA – IO, armed clashes in Southern parts of Central Equatoria State between (September and October 2018) (Kajo keji, Lainya & Yei) and magwi and lafon in Eastern Equatoria continues to displace people into new locations within the states. Previous humanitarian systems established and support given to these people in the last two to three years were highly disrupted in this areas raising same priority needs responded to in the past. We have to acknowledge that frequent armed clashes in this areas poses high risk to civilians and humanitarians operating in the area alike given the complexities of the operating environment at hand, yet the high needs of emergency lifesaving requirements like food, NFI/ES for hundreds and thousands of IDPs, host communities, refugees and now for the returnees from Uganda cannot be just neglected. The overall picture is that almost 1.9m IDPs in South Sudan are in need of emergency shelter and NFIs support which information was obtained from the recently concluded ipc analysis (September-october) indicating areas like kajokeji at phase 4 and the rest between phase 3-4. This number is expected to rise due to the hectic transitional activities of migration, cattle raiding and land disputes causing immeasurable property loss to the survivors. This includes spontaneous settlements of around 250,000 people due to the conflict. People are forced to flee their homes with nothing. Looting and burning of houses have been witnessed which poses severe challenges in dignified living among the crisis affected people. Most displaced are women, children, elderly men and also people with disabilities who remain vulnerable with exposure to protection risks for-instance Gender Based Violence (GBV) . Lack of emergency shelter and non-food items in overcrowding settlements also poses resource conflicts, impact on health, security, and poor protection concerns for the population in the spontaneous settlements and IDP camps. Overcrowding also causes stress and mental health concern for the affected population and increase protection risk for women and girls. The host community members are also in need of assistance as they use their own limited resources to provide for the displaced. Although this project focuses on NFI/ES needs of the four counties with an estimated population of over 110,083 (HNO, 2018) we have to put in mind that this intervention is not holistic in itself to fully meet the needs of all the new arrivals and displaced civilians in these areas given the complexities of access to all vulnerable people living in isolated areas. This is why this project focuses on the needs of the most vulnerable groups within the IDP , refugees, host and returning communities. Joint IRNA assessments conducted on 18/10/2018) and also based on Titi Foundations knowledge of the areas as a static partner in previous and ongoing projects have identified emergency shelter and other life sustaining non food items as a priority need for the most vulnerable populations who are recently displaced and settled in various IDP camps and spontaneous settlers among host communities. The project aims to provide emergency response to vulnerable people with focus on the newly displaced and those who are being affected by a confluence of crises like returnees and migrants who find themselves fleeing into new settlements with the need for basic s/nfi materials like plastic sheet, kitchen sets, mosquito nets and blankets. Titi Foundation will target most affected vulnerable households through acquiring NFI/ES from core pipeline, and distribution of non food items and shelter materials. Due to diverse needs of various groups, community mobilization and participation with hosts communities and IDPs’ full involvement will be included in the process.

Activities

Titi foundation which is a focal s/nfi static partner and is also the gender focal point and a co-coordinator in the s/nfi cluster operating in these locations will intervene through distribution of in-kind items with close coordination with the s/nfi cluster and mobile partners like PAH and Medair for purposes of complementarity during the project implementation. • Conduct Initial Needs Assessment (IRNA) to identify beneficiaries . this exercise will help in identifying the needs of the affected populations in the various sectors such as (s/nfi, WASH,FSL and protection). • conduct registration and verification exercise. This will be done in collaboration between titi teams and area authorities and representatives of the various groups to identify most critical needs and also to be able to disaggregate beneficiaries in terms of vulnerability, age, gender of which the list will be shared still with cluster for an early prepositioning of the NFI/ES. • Distribution of NFI kits which will be acquired from core pipeline to 25,000 (5500 households) most vulnerable people (the vulnerable households include women headed HHs, Child Headed HHs, the Disabled Women and Men, the Blind and Deaf as well as Other Households that have experienced repeated displacements. Titi foundation will also mainstream its ongoing projects like FSL, WASH, Child protection and GBV in these locations with our s/nfi beneficiaries who will have access to our services such as the fuel efficient stoves and briquettes (FES) under our GBV program which is aimed at mitigating GBV exposure or risk.• Internal Project Monitoring and Evaluation – participatory approaches used at beneficiaries levels. • Post Distribution Monitoring and Reporting (PDM). This activity will be undertaken after the distribution and This will help to determine the efficiency and effectiveness of the intervention to see if the project has met its objective.

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Titi Foundation has several methods of passing information to communities and beneficiaries that are targeted by our interventions before project implementation. These approaches include: • information passed to target groups like local authorities and various groups representatives by conducting focus group discussions during initial rapid assessments • information dissemination through community leaders and sensitization meetings • information through beneficiary identification, registration and verification exercises • information through beneficiary training and workshops relevant to the beneficiaries of the project and through visibility materials like banners and flyers which informs on the scope of the project.

Yes

Titi Foundation messages the Es NFI are context specific and tailored to the design of the project. Sometimes there are generalized messages which seek to inform all highly vulnerable groups of IDP , host community, refugees and returnees. Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is based on protection risk analysis which takes into account the different and wider protection needs of the community into consideration. Titi Foundation uses, Protection Cluster Flash Assessment Reports, Joint IRNA protection reports and also we carry out our own access, Safety Audit Reports based on tools prepared by the cluster by conducting interviews, and focused groups discussions with women, authorities, men, boys and girls. All these take into account internal, and external risks that may cause protection risks during the project management.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Kajokeji right after the December, 2013 internal fighting, became a very difficult place to access for most humanitarian actors. In 2016, Titi Foundation on its own made Local Access arrangements with local authorities in SPLA-IG and SPLA-IO controlled areas by involving key figures from Kajokeji, Diocese of Kajokeji, and Uganda District Local Authorities bordering Kajokeji and Uganda revenue authority(URA) to come to a common understanding on the use of uganda routes to take in humanitarian assistance to locations like kajokeji which until now cant be accessed from south sudan. This was based on a catastrophe which was looming over Kajokeji for the civilians trapped there. This method worked for us and until date this has attracted more NGOs to work in the area and till present titi has access into this locations as a result of such arrangements.
