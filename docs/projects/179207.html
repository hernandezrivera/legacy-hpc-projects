Project Summary

ATAA aim to continue providing emergency Child Protection services, integrated with Education and FSL, within IPDs densely populated locations in Idleb and Aleppo, after starting delivering those services in 2020. In addition, ATAA will add new Protection services to the most vulnerable adults, including the elderly and those with disabilities, to ensure inclusive intervention and to leave no one behind. ATAA also aim to mitigate the risk of UXOs in targeted locations.
Protection activities will deliver emergency services to most vulnerable girls, boys, women, and men in Jarablus, Maaret Tamsrin, Teftnaz, and Dana Sub-districts, which are rated 3/5,4/5 and 5/5 or the Protection severity scale, through 4 static community centers within 4 relatively densely populated communities, in addition to 8 mobile units. ATAA will ensure the services are accessible by PwDs and will consider all possible precautious measures (social distancing and using the PPE) and Cluster guidelines
related to adapting Protection services delivery modalities in light of COVID-19, to ensure the safety of staff and beneficiaries and avoid causing harm. Protection services will be delivered to nearly 15700 children (7850 girls) , and to 2400 of their parents/caregivers (1200 women). There will also be coordination with local service providers from other sectoral services (FSL, WASH, Education, and Health-Nutrition actors) to facilitate access of persons with specific needs to those services through referral. The outcome of the proposed Protection intervention will be; Contribute to saving the lives of conflict-affected and most vulnerable GBWM, including PwD, through delivering Protection, Child Protection, and Humanitarian Mine Action services. This Outcome has been informed by the ASO 2, as outlined in the 2021 1st SA paper.
Protection services include Protection and CP case management, awareness-raising on Covid-19 and Protection/CP issues, parenting programming, supporting community-based child protection committees, case management/child protection trainings, Protection monitoring, and Risk Education.
They will provide a secure and supportive environment, building on existing capacities within the local communities and expanding the population of the ATAA response team. ATAA Protection Teams will implement all Project activities directly with support from ATAA Turkey Office

4 - Needs

Syria is the second least peaceful country globally according to the Global Peace Index although a reduction in active hostilities can be observed following the Russian Federation- Government of Turkey Idleb ceasefire agreement of March 2020 with active fighting having largely subsided compared to the peak of the crisis, and as a result largescale displacement also slowing. A decade of conflict has trapped civilians in a pervasive protection crisis where people’s rights to safety, security, and well-being are regularly violated. The overall protection situation defined by the protracted crisis and characterized by complex and interconnected protection concerns remains dire (HNO 2021). ATAA MEAL team visited the locations of planned targeted camps/communities in Idleb and Aleppo and conducted KIIs to collect data and analyze the needs. Regarding key findings, all respondents said that lack or loss of personal documents and Housing/land and property issues is among the most important risks that both male and female are facing, in addition to both restrictions in movement and psychological distress were reported by interviewed KIs. Interestingly, 100% of respondents in Atma, Kafr – Kafrehmul, Maaret Elnaasn, and Kelly, reported the issue of movement restriction for women. However, this issue was reported in all other locations with less percentages. Risk of explosive hazards was mainly reported in Maaret Elnaasn, Tal Elamara, Upper Um Rotha, Jarablus, Sreisat, Thahr Elmaghayer, and Marma Elhajar.Begging, children dropping out of school to work, early marriage, and relying on humanitarian assistance were the prominent coping mechanisms the KIs reported.-The rest in the comments section-

5 - Implementation of activities

Generally, there will be fixed-centers within relatively densely populated locations. Mobile units will be attached to those fixed centers to reach out to smaller populations and random IDP camps. The fixed centers will be rehabilitated mainly to ensure they are accessible by PwD including children. This includes adding ramps to the entrances and adapting some toilets to be accessible by PwD. Moreover, ATAA will prioritize those who understand and can use the sign language while hiring the project staff to ensure better reaching out to children and adults with disabilities. ATAA will also hire gender-mixed teams to ensure reaching out girls, boys, men and women. ATAA will coordinate with local authorities to ensure access and community acceptance of the proposed activities. Besides, ATAA plan to deliver the proposed services as integrated services with Education/FSL services to make them more appealing to targeted communities. The proposed activities will be delivered through adapted modalities to combat the spread of COVID-19 and avoid causing more harm. Awareness-raising and PSS for children will be delivered online; this includes Risk Education for children. While trainings and awareness-raising sessions (including Risk Education for adults) for parents/caregivers will be delivered physically with reducing the number of participants to 8-10 individuals per session. PPE will be provided and physical distancing will be considered. Case management will be delivered in a mixed manner with considering COVID-19 precautious measures during physical follow-up sessions.-The rest in the comments section-

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk: A member of the ATAA team uses their position of authority as a method of exploiting vulnerable populations. Their actions cause significant long-term damage to this individual or group. All trust between ATAA and the affected community is damaged. ATAA face severe reputational damage and loss of funding. Mitigation: All staff who join ATAA Project are going to a thorough and transparent recruitment process. References are obtained for all staff during the recruitment process. The Project Team will sign ATAA PSEA and CSG Policies ATAA have developed PSEA, Code of Conduct, and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms in place to ensure every effort is made to protect all Education/WASH and Protection beneficiaries. ATAA’s PSEA Mechanism is designed around four core components: Awareness Raising (for staff regarding power dynamics, risk and vulnerabilities, etc.), Prevention (awareness regarding the existing controls which are in place and how PSEA is prevented), Reporting (how participants can report and what are the specific steps which need to be taken regarding SEA cases) and Response (how and what ATAA will do when cases of SEA are reported). A similar mechanism is available for CSG where the staff are familiar with how to approach and deal with CSG cases of child abuse, exploitation, or violence. All community centers/schools and mobile units’ staff will sign ATAA CSG and PSEA Policies to ensure a safe environment for all Project beneficiaries
