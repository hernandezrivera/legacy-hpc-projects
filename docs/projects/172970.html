Project Summary

The UNHCR refugee response aligns with the key priorities and solutions raised by targeted refugee populations through UNHCR’s annual Participatory Assessment exercise. It aims to ensure that the protection and humanitarian needs of refugees and asylum-seekers are addressed, while promoting solutions through integration, resettlement and voluntary repatriation opportunities where possible. UNHCR will advocate for the adherence of international protection standards by expanding civil, social and economic rights to refugees, including through increased self-reliance opportunities for solutions.
UNHCR will advocate to improve refugees’ access to freedom of movement, labour markets, land and assets. Timely delivery of protection assistance, including access to registration, documentation and reception services will bolster refugees’ access to their basic rights, including access to lifesaving assistance. Capacity building and sensitization initiatives with local authorities, host communities and service providers will be used to address discrimination and social cohesion issues.
In camps, individual and household-level assistance will be provided to address ongoing needs in protection, shelter, NFIs, energy, health and WASH. UNHCR will work with COR, and with relevant line ministries where possible, to ensure the sustainability of interventions. This also includes greater participation by refugees in camp management, shelter and latrine construction, protection and security functions within their communities, community mobilization and service delivery.
The response will also seek to integrate cash-based interventions (CBIs) across all locations where feasible to support stabilization of existing assistance programs and complement self-reliance initiatives. This is especially important for refugees in urban areas, where they often form part of the “urban poor” and struggle to meet their basic needs which leaves them at heightened risk of harassment, exploitation and abuse, with women and children being particularly at-risk.
To enhance sustainability of service provision particularly in the WASH, Health and Energy sector UNHCR will invest in sustainably energy sources particularly solar energy and energy efficient stoves to reduce the environmental footprint. This will reduce the dependency on an unreliable supply of fuel and address protection risks coming from a lack of lighting and the need to collect firewood outside the village.

1 - Needs

Already in a precarious humanitarian situation Sudan was not only hit by the global COVID-19 pandemic but also by record floods during the rainy season affecting over 900,000 people in Sudan including refugees. This has been compounded by a sudden influx of Ethiopian refugees starting in November 2020 with 50,000 arrivals in one month. These emergencies took place in an environment of an economic downturn was deepened by lockdown restrictions due to COVID-19 in early 2020 which resulted in inflation rates of over 200% in September 2020 and a rapid depreciation of the local currency. The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification report in 2020 foresees the highest figure of food insecurity levels ever recorded in the history of IPC in Sudan compared to previous years. The World Bank estimates that poverty levels will remain extremely high on the back of severe food insecurity and limited access to basic services across the country. Refugees are particularly affected by these conditions facing additional protection risks and often being heavily reliant on humanitarian assistance. UNHCR plans to provide assistance to South Sudanese refugees, recently arrived Ethiopian refugees and other refugee populations living in Sudan. On national level 30% of refugees are estimated to live in camps that are in need of basic services in WASH, NFI/shelter, Protection, Health and Nutrition, Education and Food security and Livelihoods. This includes South Sudanese refugees in the Darfur states, Kordofan states and White Nile States, recently arrived Ethiopian refugees in Kassala and Gedaref State, existing caseload of Eritrean refugees in Eastern Sudan as well as Chadian and CAR refugees in Central and South Darfur. Where Eritrean and South Sudanese refugees are hosted in established sites the ongoing influx of Ethiopian refugees requires the establishment of new refugee sites in Eastern Sudan. All refugee camps are reliant on humanitarian assistance through parallel systems. The 70% of refugees that live outside of establish camps are living in rural areas and camp like settlement as well as in urban areas especially in the “open areas” around Khartoum. Areas with high needs include WASH, Protection, Education, Health/Nutrition and ES/NFI especially shelter upgrades. In addition to the above-mentioned refugee populations Khartoum hosts a large number of Syrians and Yeminis who are considers “guests” in Sudan under the Islamic notion of asylum.

2 - Activities/Outputs

For SSRs in Sudan, the focus areas will remain to be the provision of basic needs and essential services; mainly services related to education, water, health, ES/NFI, livelihoods, and nutrition. UNHCR will work with its partners to implement livelihoods activities that have the least financial cost but greatest social benefits benefiting both refugee and host communities. Additionally, UNHCR will enhance mainstreaming of services into national systems and inclusion of refugees into national plans; mainly education, health and WASH. UNHCR will continue provision of essential services for new arrivals including means, NFIs and transportation and ensure capacity building for NGO & government partners in order to better serve POCs. Registration activities mainly completion BIMS enrolment for the remaining SSR and rollout of V4 will be supported. Further, in 2020, the operation will endeavor to expand the CBI programme in the sectors of shelter, NFIs and education. Protection of women and children will continue be a priority area for SSR and UNHCR will strengthen CBP services for UASC & other vulnerable groups. UNHCR will support the residual caseload of Chadian refugees. In Darfur the VOLREP of Chadian refugees will be continued in 2021 leaving a residual caseload if some of the returnees would not opt for the VOLREP. The suspension of the VOLREP exercise due to the political unrest in Sudan as well as COVID-19 was the main reason why the repatriation of the Chadian refugees was not completed in 2020. UNHCR will continue to support the provision of basic services and protection assistance for the Chadian refugees as well as CAR refugees in Um Shalaya camp while UNHCR continue to advocate with gov. & other stakeholders for the local integration of the remaining Chadian refugees as well as integration of services to the national system. Planned assistance including health, education, water & protection will continue the smaller scale given the meagre resources allocated for this population. In addition, the ongoing biometric registration of CAR refugees will continue in 2021. NAs will receive protection assistance and NFIs while CAR refugees residing in urban and other out of camp settlements will continue to benefit from self-reliance and livelihood assistance. While emergency shelter solutions are provided in camp setting, shelter upgrades will be carried out in off-camp settings. CBI, especially targeting vulnerable HHs & PWSNswill be provided. UNHCR will continue working with partners to respond to the basic needs of refugees alongside host communities in Um Dukhan, Um Dafoug, Dimsu (Maramousa and Karakada), and El Radom. Area of priority will include WASH, Education, Health/Nutrition& ES/NFI. In 2021, the East operation will be prioritized key activities including but not limited to anti-traficking protection & assistance services as well as assistance to newly arrived Ethiopian refugees and the existing Eritrean caseload. Safe houses will be sustained to ensure individual case-managements. Advocacy will continue with State level authorities to mainstream basic social services, but at the same time pursue camp closure and consolidation (for the Eritrean caseload). The operation will also address needs of new arrivals by providing transportation, wet feeding, and appropriate protection (RSD) and security. Reception facilities will be adequately maintained to accommodate new arrivals. Capacity building of local authorities and line ministries to integrate basic services in addition to partners’ staff capacity development will continue. Interventions are expected to lead to reduced protracted refugees in the East as well as stabilization of new arrivals by end of 2021. UNHCR will endeavor to mainstream the services provided in camps into national systems. This will strengthen peaceful coexistence between refugees and host communities.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

During the Participatory Assessment undertaken in Kordofan states (South and West Kordofan) in November 2018, South Sudan refugees (SSR) women, indicated sexual harassment and assault during firewood collection far from the settlement. Findings from the energy and environment baseline assessment undertaken in May 2018 in the same states also revealed that refugees and the hosting community depend entirely on firewood for cooking, they travel an average of 3-5 kilometers to collect firewood. The heavy reliance on firewood has negative impact on the forests. It also impacts refugee safety and dignity and create conflict between the refugees and host communities. The same findings were reported during the Energy baseline assessment conducted in East Darfur, April 2017 and the joint baseline assessment conducted in October 2017 for the South Sudanese refugees in Khartoum Open Areas. In 2020 UNHCR distributed fuel-efficient stoves to refugees in Sudan and to 20-25% of the host communities. The Energy environment comprehensive response plan for 2021 targets some 80,000 households of South Sudanese Refugees representing 76% of the South Sudanese Refugees population who will have access to sustainable energy, including the South Sudanese Refugees in South-West Kordofan and Khartoum open areas who will be targeted for the provision of fuel efficient stoves and mix energy sources such as gas stoves, charcoal briquettes and ethanol for White Nile. The provision of solar energy to ensure sustainable light in the street and homes of refugees will improve protection of women and children. The solar solution will replace diesel fuel systems that powers clinics, drinking water systems, and schools. Ensure reforestation of degraded forest surrounding the South Sudanese Refugees settlements and camps. The priority response for energy and environment in 2021 is to ensure that refugees have access to sustainable energy by providing them with alternative cooking gas stoves, charcoal briquettes, and ethanol, the construction and distribution of fuel efficient stoves, the provision of chargeable solar lamps, and the installation of solar energy centers in refugee camps to charge home lamps, street lamps, and mobile devices. In addition, the operation will seek to solarize some of the water supply systems in refugee sites.

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

UNHCR will ensure that protection concerns are mainstreamed throughout the implementation of all proposed activities. A protection lens will be applied to Health & Nutrition, Education, WASH and shelter/NFI programming and the UNHCR age, gender and diversity approach will be applied. Through is role in co-leading the Refugee Consultation Forum (the national coordination forum for refugees in Sudan) with COR, UNHCR will seek to strengthen protection mainstreaming with all partners who are responding to refugee needs in Sudan. UNHCR carries out an annual participatory assessment whose results are directly incorporated in all proposed activities in 2021. Additionally, UNHCR will ensure that protection mainstreaming is accentuated through all partnership agreements and projects.

UNHCR will apply a age, gender & diversity approach in all its interventions to ensure that the voices and priorities of women, girls, boys, men, including the most marginalized and at-risk people among affected communities are included in the planning, implementation and evaluation of all response activities.
