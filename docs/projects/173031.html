Project Summary

Manicaland Province like other parts of the country has had consecutive drought seasons over the past two seasons due to climate change has negatively affected the supply of safe water and sanitation services in both urban and rural settings, thereby increasing the risk of WASH related diseases among vulnerable communities. The province has a total of 5,754 boreholes according to the RWIMS report and 273 waterpoints have dried up, 305 now low yielding and 311 collapsed waterpoints further constraining the water supplies for the populations. The travel distance for women and children increases as more water sources dry up. This predisposes them to other dangers and risks of sexual exploitation and abuse. The reduction in water availability is leading communities to compromise on safe sanitation and hygiene thereby increasing the risk of WASH related diseases. Urban growth has not been matching with WASH services delivery compromising the quality of life and increasing exposure to water related diseases risks. There has been a decline in the investment into WASH services by local authorities and government due to economic challenges. The outbreak of the global COVID-19 pandemic has further compounded the cotail of economic challenges thus increasing the risk of exposure to waterborne disease outbreaks like cholera, typhoid and others.
The proposed intervention ensures inclusive access to quality, clean and potable water for women, men, girls and boys in Manicaland through climate smart adapation methods such as solar powered water supply, water harvesting increased adoption of WASH behaviours.

1 - Activities

Mercy Corps would like to leverage on the work that it is currently doing in the province. The proposed intervention seeks to address some of the water and sanitation challenges through increased access to water for the affected populations of the 3 target districts of Manicaland Province, through rehabilitation of water supply points and drilling new boreholes. The project aims to reach out to 32,297 people with clean, safe and potable water, sanitation and health and hygiene promotion messages. The populations are further dis-aggregated by district Buhera 19,627, Chipinge Rural 8,146 and Chipinge Urban 4,524 people. The key activities would include: 1. Water Supply and rehabilitation of water points (260 b/hole rehabs, rehab of 3 piped water schemes, drilling 6 new b/holes, family well upgrading and water quality trainings). Proposed project acitivities include support for the health system to ensure availability of basic water and sanitation services in key prioritized health care facilities in the target districts. This would include dixystribution of vouchers for waterguard access for the urban vulnerable populations. 2. Sanitation; Support construction of emergency sanitation facilities for for affected IDPs, at education and health institutions including rehabilitation of related infrastructure. 3. Hygiene Promotion, supporting school and community health clubs, promotion of menstrual hygiene management. Provide access to critical handwashing facilities including soap and alcohol-based sanitizers, WASH- related hygiene kits. Support mass media campaigns on COVID-19 prevention using various channels of communication taking into account literacy levels and access of different groups. The project woukd also ensure field and front-line staff engaged in WASH humanitarian responses have adequate access to PPE and practice key risk reduction practices.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Mercy Crops upholds principles of Do-No-Harm and crisis management for operational success. This means applying strategies for Do-No-Harm through mechanisms for accountability, transparency and staff and beneficiary protection. Mercy Corps programs use these approaches in its design, through measures such as soliciting responsible government departments’ input into design, selection, targeting and validation of beneficiaries for all interventions, creation and institution of a Community Accountability and Response Mechanism (CARM), careful planning of operations to empower beneficiaries and taking advantage of opportunities to strengthen social bonds. Community dialogues, constant feedback and information provision will be implemented. Communication and information with the affected populations will be shared in a two-way communication channel that ensures the targeted communities are aware of their rights in the project and the obligations of the Partner while working in their communities. Feedback and complaint mechanisms will be established and utilized throughout the project through suggestion boxes, complaints and feedback logo, help desks and a toll free number all feedback received will be recorded and adequately addressed in a manner that also safeguards the communities and ensures that their concerns are attended to.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Mercy Corps uses an evidence-based approach to help develop the plans by conducting an initial assessment of environmental and social impact issues. This incorporates identifying elements that impact implementation and potential negative effects on environment and social structures that could emanate from program interventions. These factors if identified need to be addressed as they will have implications on community participation and the success of the program interventions. The provision of safe drinking water to residents will also improve health conditions of the affected communities and reduces the burden of fetching water, which disproportionately falls on female members of households. However, where risks are noted including potential risks from natural hazards these will be embedded into the water safety and security planning component. Mercy Corps will undertake the followings among other measures, monitor the quality of water on a quarterly basis in the targeted areas. This will ensure that the project will follow guidelines from Ministry of Health when constructing any sanitation facilities to avoid contaminating underground water; ensuring adequate drainage including where handwashing stations are installed and issues coming through the PSEA, Child Protection and GBV platforms will also be used to inform the social safeguards required within the programme implementation.
