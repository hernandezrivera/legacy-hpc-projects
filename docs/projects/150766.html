Project Summary

Contribute to protecting the rights of vulnerable women who suffer from residency rights issues in East Jerusalem through documentation of violations of residency rights; provision of legal and psychosocial services; and advocacy.
The Objective responds to the Protection Cluster Objective 2: Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence, in line with HRP SO1
The project contributes to protection mainstreaming through:
1. prioritizing the safety and dignity of targeted people by ensuring their anonymity due to sensitive legal and psychosocial issues;
2. enabling equal access to services by doing outreach and targeting the most vulnerable women in communities such as Silwan,Jabal Mukkabir, Old City and Surbaher;
3. providing psychosocial and legal support, the project empowers and assist women to claim their rights specifically the residency rights and family reunification

Needs

“Sometimes I feel guilty because I have put my family in this situation by marrying someone with a West Bank ID card.”Thikriat Sh; 42 year old woman from East Jerusalem. Following the 1967 annexation of East Jerusalem, the Palestinians living in East Jerusalem were given the civil status of “permanent resident” under the Law of Entry into Israel. These residents, under the State’s “centre of life” policy, are forced to repeatedly prove their physical presence in Jerusalem and ties to it to the Ministry of the Interior (MOI) and National Insurance Institute (NII), which conduct investigations and inquiries explicitly designed to gather evidence of residency status, often used to revoke the right. Under Israeli legislation, permanent residence is not granted automatically through the spouse holding a Jerusalem identity card: “they must submit an application for family reunification of the spouse who holds a different identity document. Moreover, permanent residence is not transferred to the children of the holder as a right and there can be difficulties in registering children born to a couple holding different identity documents. “ In October 2000, Israel froze the processing of family reunification applications and in May, 2002, adopted Resolution No. 1813, which formally halted the processing of all family reunification applications. The revocation of residency rights also affects rights to family life, employment, education, security, movement, protection from displacement/transfer domestic violence and abuse, health and social benefits (including child registration rights); all of which are guaranteed under Israeli and international law, and all of which are in constant jeopardy from Israel. A woman holder of West Bank ID and married to a man who holds Jerusalem ID describes how it is difficult for her to live with her husband in Jerusalem: “There are all sorts of complications to my life now. For example, without a valid permit I cannot get health insurance and must pay full price for any medicine. I can no longer accompany my children on school trips like the other mothers. We have all of this trouble now even though my husband and I have no security record.” These policies have a great negative impact on Palestinians residence in East Jerusalem, especially on women. In a study prepared by the Women’s studies Centre on the situation of Palestinian women in Jerusalem, it was found that “The effect of the political violence, displacement, and refugee life has raised women’s vulnerability and increased their chances of falling victim to physical, psychological, sexual, and social violence. In addition, the harsh political and economic conditions often prevent women from calling out for help”. Women’s lives are not only threatened by patriarchy and negative cultural practices enshrined in law but their lives are in double jeopardy due to Israeli occupation practices. As per JLAC’s beneficiaries records in Jerusalem for the year 2017, more than 64% of the beneficiaries are women. The records show that 85% of women sought legal service; 12% sought legal advice; and 3% needed follow up on their cases or filed court cases. This project comes to address the needs of women with residency rights issues in East Jerusalem for social support; legal services; and awareness raising on legal and social rights.

Activities

UN Women together with its partners (WCLAC and JLAC) will address the above needs though: 1. Provide social support to women and men in East Jerusalem to increase their awareness on their legal and social rights. Two women support groups and one men support group will be established. Each group will consist of 18 people from East Jerusalem who are impacted by the residency right policies in East Jerusalem especially family unifications. A comprehensive needs assessment will be done during the first session to identify issues women would like to focus on during the sessions. Besides, general topics will also be presented such as personal status rights, women’s human rights and legal system. Women in the groups who would like to seek further support, will have the chance to receive individual consultation, where advice can be provided immediately or by referring them to receive long term social and legal counselling at WCLAC office or other specialized organizations. 2. Provide legal services to women in East Jerusalem Throughout the project women in East Jerusalem will receive the following legal services:a) Legal Services (help in filling forms and submit them to the relevant authorities; and legal intervention and representation before courts when needed) as follow:a. Provide 100 legal services (preparing affidavit; filling and preparing forms with official Israeli institutions such as the Ministry of Interior; national insurance; and Jerusalem municipality) to women;b. Provide 48 legal consultations for women; andc. Legal representation of cases of 20 women including cases referred to JLAC by WCLAC. The legal representation will be a full representation of women cases with relevant Israeli authorities and / or courts. b) Awareness raising sessions: women in the social support groups will also be provided with legal awareness raising sessions on residency rights. The awareness raising sessions will include sessions on family unification; child registration; and social and economic rights including women, children and elderly benefits and other welfare benefits. 3. Advocate for women’s rights in East Jerusalem The cases of ten Palestinian women who have residency problems due to holding Jerusalem ID and marrying to a Palestinian with West Bank ID or vice versa and could not obtain residency access due to Israeli restrictions will be documented. The documentation of these cases ensures that Palestinian women’s rights violations on residency rights in East Jerusalem are effectively monitored then collected through a process of documentation. This process will then ensure there is a historical record of women’s experiences, and provide tools to lobby and advocate nationally, regionally, and internationally, to inform and educate both Palestinian women and the wider public. Further, some of these cases that require legal support will be referred to JLAC for follow up. The documented cases of women will be published in a report that will be used for advocacy purposed including advocacy tours. Besides, field visits will be organized targeting international advocacy targets based locally to raise their awareness on the issue and listen to the voices of women subject to these violations. In addition, one advocacy visit targeting UN bodies and civil society organizations in Europe will be organized to advocate for residency rights of women in East Jerusalem. In addition, a documentary film on the impact of residency rights policies in East Jerusalem on women will be produced.

Indicators

UN Women is committed to be an active member in the Legal task force and to coordinate the activities and report on indicators under the LTF and the PC. Building trust and confidence among the beneficiaries in need of psychosocial support, as well as ensuring the privacy and confidentiality of their issues, limits the number of targeted beneficiaries: one PSS group can only consist of 20 persons maximum. The project will focus on providing quality, long term support and to ensure the participants’ safety and dignity, aligned with the do no harm policy. Each of the three groups will convene for 12 sessions. Regarding the legal support, it is important to note that majority of Palestinians in Jerusalem do not trust the Israeli institutions in Jerusalem and fear of e.g. revoking residency rights hinders women from seeking legal support. Therefore, to ensure equal and impartial access to services, the service providing project partners will do outreach in areas such as Silwan, Jabal Mukkabir, Old City and Surbaher to be able to target the most vulnerable groups of women in need of legal services. Due to the sensitivity of the legal issues and in order to ensure the full anonymity of project beneficiaries, aligned with the do no harm policy, the project will target a limited number of beneficiaries and focus on providing high quality, year long legal support for them. In addition, the project will provide full legal representation to 20 cases that are identified as the most severe. The provision of legal services component of the project is costly, however it is vital in order to ensure that the women, who would not be able to afford the services themselves, will fully benefit and be able to claim their rights. The outcome of the project: Palestinian women in East Jerusalem are empowered and able to access and claim their social and legal rights. The outcome indicators are:a) Number of women who receive information on their rights and how to access legal services;b) Number of women who receive social and legal support/ services; andc) Number of filed briefings raising protection concerns to international advocacy targets locally and internationally. The outputs of the projects are:1.1 The legal and social awareness of 40 women and 20 men in East Jerusalem is increased;2.1 Women access to legal services has increased;3.1 A report documenting the cases of 10 Palestinian women who could not obtain residency access due to Israeli restrictions on family unification is published;3.2 Five field trips and advocacy tours are organized; and3.3 A documentary film is produced. The project will target directly 238 women and 20 men in East Jerusalem as follows:• 40 women and 20 men will be provided with social support and awareness raising sessions;• The cases of 10 women who have residency rights issues in East Jerusalem will be documented;• 120 women will be provided with legal services;• 48 women will get legal consultations; and• 20 women will get full legal representation. The project activities will be monitored through regular field visits, quarterly narrative and financial reports, and documentation of activities using forms which will be developed to collect relevant data for monitoring and evaluation purposes. In addition, an internal evaluation session will be organized at the end of the project to document the process, challenges, and lessons learnt.
