Project Summary

According to education cluster need analysis note 2021, an estimated three-million school-aged children and youth (six-18 years) are out‐of‐school. Enrolment rates are lowest for the most vulnerable children such as girls, rural and internally displaced children (IDP). Middle Shabelle and Mudug are part of the regions with the lowest attendance rates. Other locations with high out of school children includes areas that host Internally Displaced Persons including Galkayo and Bosasso and hard to reach rural areas such as Callula, Qandala among others. Children with disability are cut off from mainstream education system. The project will target rural/hard to reach areas and IDP locations to address this gap. Girls are disproportionally being negatively affected, due to increased chores and responsibilities, as well as GBV risks including FGM, early marriage, sexual and physical violence. In Somalia’s patriarchal society where gender inequality shapes gender roles and responsibilities, girls’ education is barely prioritized by caregivers. Girls consistently face greater challenges to accessing education. While the gender parity for enrollment at primary school level is 0.91 (1 is equal enrolment) it drops to 0.83 at the secondary level. In times of crisis such as the current economic crisis due to COVID 19, girls are the first victims of family’s economic stress, experiencing school dropout and early marriage. Other barriers that affect girls’ access and retention in school include; lack of sanitary kits, unfriendly school latrine for use during menstrual periods, harassment from boys among others. The project intends to work at school, family and community level to mitigate barriers faced by girls to continue their education. At the school level, the project will work with teachers to promote gender responsive and inclusive teaching practices and with CECs to promote a conducive learning environment for all children. In particular the provision of education materials and food rations will directly address economic barriers to girls’ education. The rehabilitation of WASH facilities at school will be done with a gender and disability lens to ensure that girls, including those with disabilities, are safe and feel comfortable to go to school, including when they are menstruated. The provision of sanitary kits will contribute to address to prevent adolescent girls’ absenteeism during their periods. The strong focus on protection will ensure that girls are safe when going to school. COVID-19 school closure had affected 1.1M children enrolled in the schools across the country from March to August 2020. This impacted not only their learning but also their wellbeing as schools also contribute to psychosocial wellbeing and protection of learners. When families cannot afford to pay tuition or other expenses associated with education, children drop out of school. These repercussions can lead to child labor, child marriage and other negative outcomes, RI will use Cash assistance to address the economic barriers to education. Barriers to access for children with disabilities are considerable and includes negative attitudes towards children with disabilities, unfavorable learning environments compounded by teachers lacking training on inclusive education, bullying by peers among others. An estimated 216,004 school aged children in need of humanitarian assistance are living with some form of disability though there is insufficient data on children with disability. The project will train teachers on inclusive education and create positive learning environment for children with disability and work with school management to ensure school facilities are disability friendly. Schools will be supported to serve as a critical entry point for critical lifesaving services, including access to safe drinking water, food, AWD/cholera prevention, protection mechanisms and psychosocial support which is critical for learners to recover from crisis.

Project Activities

RI will implement the following activities: - One back to school enrollment campaigns targeting out of school 1,800 girls and 1,500 boys as well as 330 children living with disabilities for school enrollment across all locations of implementation ; - Refurbishment/construction of 14 learning spaces to make them safe, disability and child friendly; - WASH facilities available to emergency-affected school children and youth (target 23 ( 13 girls and 10 for boys); - Distribution of dry food rations to 7100 (3500 girls and 3600 boys; marginalized children and cash assistance to very vulnerable households such as elderly grandparents responsible for school age children targeting 210(120 girls and 90 boys) learners; -Training on 140 (90 Male and 50 female) teachers on social-emotional learning, inclusive education, protection and Psychosocial Support /Psychological First Aid to 15,152 children (8,334 boys and 6,818 girls);- incentives to 140 (70 male and 70 female ) teachers; - Provision of water and soap as well as hygiene education including COVID prevention and early detection awareness raising targeting of 140 teachers and 15,152 learners - Training of 210 (120 male and 90 Female) CECs on school management, resource mobilisation, child protection and safety of all learners including how to put in place appropriate COVID protocols in school. - Identification and training of 70 female child protection focal points (one per school). - Positive parenting skills and Psychosocial support training to 2,800 (1,100 male and 1700 female) caregivers/parents, -Six radio and community sensitization campaigns on importance of education and supporting girls and children with disability to enroll and be retained in schools, - Establishment/fostering of referral linkages with CP, MHPSS and health facilities (for COVID) service providers for each of the 70 schools. – Provide school equipment, learning and age and gender responsive recreational materials to 15,152 children (8,334 boys and 6,818 girls); - 15 Education Cluster partners and MoE staff (8 male and 7 Female) trained in emergency preparedness and response planning and coordination.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

This is a very small proportion of the budget that will be utilised to support survivors of abuse and their families to access specialised services on time. This will mainly cover transportation costs on needs basis. The other element of cash in the project will be targeting very poor households, in specific situation of vulnerability, to ensure children can go and/or remain in school. Such households will include grand-parents who are primarily responsible for their school aged grand-children.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The amounts factored in are based on current market rates for transportation using various modes depending with target locations. For the cash transfer to households in specific situation of vulnerability, the amount will be calculated based on market costs of school materials and uniforms. For the cash transfer to HH in specific situation of vulnerability, the amount was calculated based on market costs of school materials and uniforms, food basket will be based on market prices and family size. Furthermore RI will work closely with Cash Working Group to ensure alignment to the set standards and modalities.

Mitigation Measures

-Mitigation measures to address risks associated with unsafe distances for learners accessing schools: the project will work with the CECs to identify safety and protection concerns affecting learners in schools, whether these concerns are within the school compound or on the way to and back from school. CECs will be supported to identify mitigation measures for these risks including working with stakeholders outside school such as community leaders. Girls and boys as well as their teachers will be reached with safety and protection messages and supported to identify their responsibility in keeping themselves safe such as safety planning for girls where they move in groups, during day light and in company of an adult CEC or parent.. The project will also create awareness in the community and through radio outreach on various issues including safety and protection of learners on their way to school as well as within the schools. Parents/caregivers, including of children living with disabilities will be sensitized to ensure safety of their children on their way to and from school. -Mitigation measures to address exclusion of marginalized people from decision making and support: the project will specifically target displacement affected communities, including IDP settlements. Based on an awareness that children from displaced families are less likely to be enrolled in school, ‘back to school’ campaigns will be rolled out in IDPs settlements, including door-to-door visits to households in IDP camps, following COVID prevention protocols, to enhance the enrollment of IDP children in schools. One of the key considerations while selecting target schools with MoE will be that these schools are serving children living in IDP settlements. RI will encourage CECs in school to ensure they are representative of the communities they are serving; the project will thus promote the inclusion of female CEC members in decision making positions within the CECs. Schools will be encouraged to create CECs reflecting the diversity in the community, including IDPs, persons with disability and minority clans and to encourage for their views to be integrated in decision making. RI will conduct Post Distribution Monitoring to verify that all those beneficiaries targeted for specific assistance did receive support. During criteria development and beneficiaries’ selection, RI will ensure to include potential factors of exclusion and vulnerability such as displacement, disability, gender, age, marital status, clan etc.. Data collected by RI at the school and individual level will be disaggregated by displacement, gender, disability among other vulnerability criteria wherever possible. RI acknowledges that there are differential risks for girls and boys and across the age cohorts. The project is designed to ensure girls are consulted for meeting their speciﬁc needs and addressing the particular challenges they face. -Mitigation measures for barriers faced by beneficiaries to access complaints and feedback mechanisms; RI will hold consultations with different members of community (boys, girls, women, men, IDP, minority clans, host community, PLWDs and elderly) to gather their preferred mode of providing feedback, identify issues of access, safety and community confidence in the feedback mechanism. These inputs will be factored in the design and operationalization of the feedback mechanisms. RI will ensure there are diverse age, gender, and diversity friendly ways of providing feedback and that, once the mechanisms is set-up, it is highly socialized among beneficiaries using diverse user friendly and appropriate communication modes. Communities will be made aware of the expected support and code of conduct for personnel involved in the project and encouraged to report any concerns and unacceptable behaviors.

Please provide further explanation

Increased access to education for displaced community will lead to empowerment including having access to information and education that will eventually lead to improved standards of living. Women with additional years of schooling have increased livelihood chances, improved quality of care for their children and families and increased maternal and child health. Increased access for more children in schools in future will lead to increased country GDP and stem conflicts related to scarcity of resources. It will also reduce crime rates among the youth such as conscription to armed groups which will contribute to peaceful co-existence amongst community. Education is one of the key tools in break the poverty and exclusion viscous cycle across generations leading to more equality among various communities and families from poor social economic status. This project will in this sense contribute to durable solutions in the targeted districts and communities.
