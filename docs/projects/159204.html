Project Summary

In the Bay region, the number of acutely food insecure People (Rural, IDP and Urban Combined) who are Most Likely (October-December 2019) includes 218,000 People who are in IPC Phase 2, 196,000 in IPC Phase 3 while 71,000 in Emergency IPC Phase 4 (FSNAU September 2019). In the absence of assistance during the October- December period, food security is expected to deteriorate in central Somalia and some parts of the South including the Bay region. Emergency (IPC Phase 4) is most likely in the Bay-Bakool Low Potential Agropastoral livelihood zone, given the severity of Gu food deficits.
In response to this, READO in line with the FSC & HRP 2020 objectives aims to improve households’ immediate access to food through conditional and unconditional cash transfers in Baidoa, Hudur and Dinsoor districts in the Bay region. Also, READO will protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources, through the provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs provide livelihood inputs/training and technical support in rural and peri-urban settings.

Project Activities

Objective 1: Improve households’ immediate access to food through the provision of conditionaland unconditional assistance. Activities:- READO will prioritize unconditional cash transfers especially for 9000 vulnerable people (1,500 HHs: 900 male, 600 female) in the IDP population for four months. READO will prioritize cash in areas where markets are viable; are physically and socially accessible to all segments of the population. In regards to the markets in Hudur and Dinsoor, local grain (red and white sorghum) prices exhibited mild to moderate (less than 25%) to significant (36-96%) increases compared to June and July 2019 and a year ago as a result of the poor 2019 Gu harvest in adjacent crop-producing areas. Also, prices of imported food items (rice, sugar, vegetable oil, wheat flour) changed at mild rates (by less than +/-10%) for most of the food imports in allHudur and Dinsoor markets compared to the last two months (June and July 2019)(FSNAU Market Update September 2019). In line with recent Somalia Cash Working Group and FSNAU guidance, transfers will be 100 % of full Food MEB. Throughout the year, READO will coordinate with the cash working group to determine the value of cash transfers. Cash transfers will be done through mobile money as it will not only ensure the safety, dignity, and integrity of recipients but limit fraud and diversion of funds. Activities:- Provision of conditional cash transfers (cash for work), which will target key infrastructure of community assets rehabilitation during the lean seasons to 9000 vulnerable people (1,500 HHs: 900 male, 600 female) for four months. The transfer will be conditional for completing water catchment rehabilitation work in Bay regions. For the minority of participants, especially those who are unable to partake in labor-intensive work, specifically female heads of households with young children, the disabled and the elderly, the cash transfer will be unconditional cash transfers. READO opted for cash transfers instead of in-kind because cash transfers support the local economy, enabling households to meet other priority needs, provide recipients with more choice, supporting their dignity, supporting livelihoods, strengthening local markets and promoting financial inclusion. Objective 2: Protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources, through the provisionof seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs and technical support in rural and peri-urban settings.Activities: Provision of livelihood specific inputs READO will distribute the seeds package to the 1,000 HHs (600 male, 400 female). In line with FSC guidance, each household will receive 40-kg which includes: Sorghum (25kg) Cowpea (15kg) and hand farm tools (Knife, Hoes, and Axe). Before the distribution of the livelihood inputs, the farmers will receive GAP training to improve their yield. Activity 3.2 Livelihood support through Vocational Skills training: READO will train 500 youth (300Female 200male) IDPs in vocational skills (masonry, traditional weaving, tie & dye, business entrepreneurship, tailoring, and carpentry, etc.) so that they can boost their income levels through increasing their employability. This will be followed by beneficiaries receiving start-up grants for IGA.READO will also conduct a Post Distribution Monitoring after the distribution of the cash transfers and livelihood inputs. The PDM will also be disaggregated by persons with disability analysis of barriers faced by persons with disabilities and any instances of exploitation or other protection risks created through the delivery of assistance.

Mitigation Measures

As far as protection risks are concerned, READO through security mapping will ensure that the location, distance and access routes to the distribution sites of livelihood inputs and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. Also, READO will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age, and diversity to effectively inform programming. For the minority of participants, especially those who are unable to partake in labor-intensive work, specifically female heads of household with young children, the disabled and the elderly, the cash transfer will be unconditional cash transfers Furthermore, READO will involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to food security needs. READO will consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women, and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for food security target locations, design, and services. In regards to protection risks, READO will use mobile money transfer to avoid forced taxation/extortion to beneficiaries. READO will also establish a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for accountability. READO will use information boards to display CRM phone numbers during training sessions activities/beneficiary selection. Beneficiaries will be able to call or text this number to register their complaints. READO will ensure this information, which could potentially be sensitive, remains internally. All complaints will be documented and reviewed regularly. Mitigating protection risksInsecurity: READO will ensure that the distribution of livelihood inputs takes place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. In regards to conditional and unconditional cash transfer, READO will distribute through mobile money transfer to the beneficiaries to reduce the risk of theft/intimidation/ forceful coercion to give the money to unintended people. READO will ensure, that the location and access routes to livelihood inputs and farmers' training sites are safe. This will be done by identifying central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Also, READO will ensure that beneficiary lists are confidential and private. READO will not share information on the identity of beneficiaries unless informed consent has been given (e.g. names, addresses, or traits and characteristics about the case that can lead to identification). READO will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by identifying and prioritizing the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups. For instance, READO will use mobile money transfer which will ensure female beneficiaries and those who face exclusions benefit. Besides, READO will identify the power dynamics in the area which includes who has access to food services and who can influence decisions on the provision of food services. READO will, in turn, use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups. This information will be considered when determining locations for food security interventions. In regards to avoiding tensions between the host and displaced communities, READO will employ the DO NO HARM approach. For instance, READO will provide the local/host community with access to camp food security services in situations where they are experiencing food insecurity. Also, READO will consult both displaced and host communities about food security needs. READO will also ensure child protection by ensuring that children are not subjected to any child labor during CFW and agricultural activities.
