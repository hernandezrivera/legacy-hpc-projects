Project Summary

Establishment of safe house for the survivors of GBV and child protection and provision of women and girls’ safe space In Sool and Sanaag project
are consist for three main components which includes GBV prevention and responses where by the women and girls (including those with disabilities) have access to safe, timely confidential quality coordinated GBV specialized services through integrated referrals, and case management.
CP components in which that the most vulnerable children & at-risk girls and boys have access to quality CP prevention and response services including persons with disabilities and receive quality& timely response services and benefit from risk reduction and prevention measures.
The proposed project has three main objectives incl. Improve quality services for SGBV survivors and strengthen protection for children through established safe house and women and girls’ safe spaces, and scale up community-based protection through mobile outreach. The proposed project will increase GBV and Child protection direct service delivery through emergency referral support system, early identification and referral of GBV victims, CMR and improve quality specialized assistance and support, improve awareness and provision of free medical PSS.
To strengthen response services for GBV survivors the project will provide timely access to life saving protection service, strengthening existing safe house and stablish new safe house and women & girl safe space. Moreover, the project will deploy frontline that will be provided to conduct referral to SGBV services, identification of the child abuse cases, PSS, provide safe, dignified and meaningful access with protection and response service through provision of first line support, early identification, case management service, advocate immediate service, facilitate referrals and emergence assistance, provide safety and security, legal advocacy and representation, referral to medical services, and improve quality specialized assistance and support.
WAAPO Shelter is the only safe house exist in Somaliland. WAAPO is currently manages the 3 fully functional SGBV shelters in three regions. The project will extend the existing safe house in a new location called ‘Lascanod” In Sool region. The project will strengthen protection to children at risk and child survivors incl. UASC, through providing CM services that meets their unique needs, which include family tracing and reunification, alternative care and CBPM to support the case management process.
The project will undertake MHPSS and positive parenting trainings among the care givers and families of children facing protections to increase their capacity to protect their children from all violation, risks of abuse and provide support. In addition, it will also deliver session at the community level led by CBCPMs and WAAPO outreach workers on prevention massage on recruitment/CASSFAG, SGBV harmful practice, family separation, and child rights.
The project will Increase the capacity of CBCPM to prevent and mitigate CP risks through multi-layered, CP PSS interventions and CM in emergency, CP care to reduce vulnerabilities and risks by building a protective environment at family and community levels. Thus, the project will initiate new CBCPMs committee, strengthening existing CBCPMs and scale up community outreach program. The child safe guarding prioritization to maintain safe programming, the project will provide technical training to WAAPO staff about child safeguarding manual. The project will develop specific risk vulnerability analysis for the targeted groups that may impact on safety e.g. disability, children without parent’s care, and children who have already experience of abuse.
WAAPO will scale up current hotline in the project target location to provide information related to the disability rights. Finally, WAAPO’s safe house will also furnish wheel chairs and other mobility devices.

Project Activities

• Establish a safe house in Laas,anod district in Sool region to provide survivors of gender-based violence and girls freeing from being abused and mutilate and child abuse cases temporary accommodation and food.• Establish women's and girls’ safe space in the Ceerigavo district in the Sanaag region. • Establish three compassionate survivor-centered care and Inter-agency counseling desk in three main locations including Ceel afwayne, Ceynaba, and Buuhoodle district in Sool and Sanaag regions to provide routine PSS, MHPSS, counseling and case management. • Provide sanitary material and hygiene kits for SGBV survivors and other vulnerable women and girls and set-up hotline in the WAAPO Safehouse. • Support children in the safe house and safe spaces with recreation material support, psychosocial support, parenting messages, positive parenting, and other kind support leading to improved relationships with the children under their care. • Establish Five mobile units with protection, SGBV & PSS to carry out participatory community education, dialogue, and community mobilization to sensitize communities about SGBV, child protection. • Carry out mobile outreach activities led by the specialized mobile units with protection, SGBV & PSS to conduct remote PSS, MHPSS, referrals, case management service, and service mobilization. • Arrange 38 Youth-led sessions mobilized by the mobile outreach team that encourages youth to raise GBV, gender equality, educate to reject attitude that discriminates against girls, and to communicate openly about all matters relating to GBV and FGM/C. • Arrange peer to peer awareness-raising session organized by WAAPO mobile outreach teams CBCPMs youth and community volunteers to prevent child protection risks, identify vulnerable children needed CMs and care. • Deliver awareness-raising session on prevention massage and an awareness session on recruitment/CASSFAG, SGBV harmful practice, family separation, and child rights. • Conduct capacity building workshop for WAAPO staff incl. caseworkers, social workers, outreach teams and volunteers on case management, MHPSS, safe referral, home monitoring visits and offering peer support and PSEA, code on conduct to ensure that intervention is safe for children. • Training community structure committees on community led-massage on key child protection risks, social norms, harmful practice, MRE, and related information on CP service and mitigation strategy. • Conduct training for caregivers and families (MHPSS, positive parenting, and other kinds of support) of children facing protection risks in order to enhance their capacity to care for and protect their children. • Provision of inclusive community reintegration services for children associated with armed forces and groups in the community affected by the clan conflict in the Sool region, or those at-risk categories.• Conduct capacity building workshops on CBCP to increase their knowledge, skills, and competence and to prevent and mitigate CP risks through multi-layered, community-based psychosocial support interventions and to reduce vulnerabilities and risks to children. • Arrange Peer to peer awareness-raising led by the CBCPMs, youth, and community volunteers’ networks to prevent child protection risks, identify vulnerable children in need of support such as case management and emergency child protection care (i.e. alternative care or basic PSS activities)Conduct training among the CP actors and non-child protection actors on child protection services and case management service to increased knowledge and skills in providing age- and gender-sensitive child protection services

Mitigation Measures

o This risk will be mitigated by enforcing a policy of zero tolerance for sexual misconduct. All WAAPO program activities will obliged to create an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. Whenever an incident is reported indicating sexual exploitation and sexual abuse, or any serious breach of the code of conducts, WAAPO will to report the incident to UNOCHA without delay. In addition, WAAPO will print posters that will outline all the key contact information of WAAPO employees so that anyone accessing our services can engage with our staff directly and have a space to report any SEA related incidents. Finally, to ensure proper reporting, WAAPO will put compliance desk in the protection desk and will sensitize communities to report through the established hotline and child help-line. o All the WAAPO employees signed a code of conduct that outlined their responsibility to report any SEA related incident. The staff also participated in workshops and training on how to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA. Furthermore, WAAPO has collaborated with security officials in the region, and existing community-based protection groups to establish mechanisms to prevent PSEA. WAAPO remains accountable for any misconduct and expects it’s staff members to always report any SEA incidents. o This risk will be mitigated by creating campaign awareness about our project through various social media outlets. WAAPO will collaborate with local radio channels about our protection related ongoing projects and the services that we currently provide including the Safe House for victims of gender based violence. WAAPO will ensure differential needs, concerns, and priorities for the target individual through the application of the AGDM approach; and will maintain beneficiary selection tailored fair beneficiary disaggregate data through participatory approach, vulnerability assessment, outreach missions, mediate power of dynamic, accountability mechanism system. In meantime, WAAPO work very closely with local community actors, security official in the region and existing community-based protection groups, as well as other actors include acted ARC, ACTED, DRC, UNICEF. And government line minister incl. MESAF and NDRA will regularly supervise and monitor project activities. WAAPO staff will also sensitize about the self defence and communities will be also sensitize conflict resolution, family mediation, awareness creation about the mitigation of the risk. WAAPO’s offices are all located in Somaliland, a breakaway region in Somalia that has relative peace and no ongoing violence whatsoever. Our strategic location definitely provides a safe space for all of our beneficiaries.
