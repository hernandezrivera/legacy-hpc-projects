Project Summary

In this project, Violet aims to provide lifesaving health and medical interventions in Idleb and Aleppo governorates through the following health facilities: 1 CEmONC facility named as Amal maternity hospital in Azzaz; 1 PHC in Azzaz; 2 CCTCs in Idleb and Ariha; in addition to an ambulance network into which 15 ambulances are linked in seven different locations across both governorates to ensure access to quality lifesaving and live sustaining health services.
Through the above mentioned health facilities and services, the community will have access to primary and secondary health services comprise:
1) comprehensive sexual and reproductive health
2) pediatric care service out and inpatient.
3) communicable and non-communicable disease
4) BEmONC service at the PHC
5) referral network operating on 7 different locations in Idleb and Aleppo Govs
6) COVID 19 Isolation and follow up of mild and moderate COVID-19 cases
Through the hospital at the heart of the project, Violet will work to reduce maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality through provision of ANC, safe delivery, early initiation of breastfeeding, and PNC. To uphold health standards, Violet will make sure that all the midwives are trained on IYCF concepts and best practices with special focus on early initiation of breastfeeding and skin-to-skin contact, including within the first few hours after delivery.
To contribute to the stationary aspects of the project, a mobile network of community health and community outreach workers will be conducting outreach activities in the communities around the health facilities to deliver key messages to community members with special focus on pregnant and lactating women, communicable disease and non-communicable disease and air borne diseases.
The community work is complemented by an ambulance network led through seven emergency centres. The currently 15 ambulances are referring patients from their accommodations to health facilities as well as between health facilities. The referral networks supported by Violet is working and will continue to work in coordination and under the umbrella of the local referral networks for both governorate (Idleb and Aleppo referral networks).
Throughout the life of the project, Violet will insure proper coordination with the Health Cluster and WHO and the SRH TWG and other working groups to be sustained mainly in determining the needs and locations of the proposed activities, which will contribute effectively to the success of the project and properly implementing it. Also coordination with other hospital operating in the area supported by other NNGOs to insure proper referral for relevant cases will minimize risks related to patient lives.

4 - Needs

As per HNO draft for 2022, 12.23 million people are in need of health services in 2022, including 4.4 million displaced persons, 1.33 million children under 5 years – including an estimated 503,000 live births expected and 3.38 million women of reproductive age (15-49 years). With more than 164,000 confirmed cases and close to 5,800 deaths, COVID-19 continues to disrupt the already-fragile health services and systems due to low levels of COVID-19 vaccination, lack of adherence to preventive public health measures, and emerging variants which, together, strain attempts to stabilize and restart services affected by the pandemic, including routine childhoodimmunization services which are reporting reduced coverage rates. In terms of health facilities functionality as per last report from HeRAMs covering the third quarter of 2021 out of 610 health facilities only 454 one is function as per the following breakdown- 78 (17.2%) are Hospitals, 191 (42.1%) are fixed PHCs, 79 (17.4%) are mobile clinics, 46 (10.1%) specialized care centers, while 60 (13.2%) others health facilities. 55 health partners managing and supporting were reported.

5 - Implementation of activities

VO will provide integrated primary and secondary health services with nutrition and protection. This will be implemented in all levels of supported health facilities; 1 CemONC facilities, 1 PHC, 2 CCTCs and through the referral network. All supported health facilities will provide free of charge preventive and curative services. In PHC, VO will ensure providing primary health services aligned with WHO essential Primary Health Care Package: child health, reproductive health, communicable disease, NCD, MHPSS and nutrition. In secondary health care services: specialized reproductive health care CEmONC services; the services will be provided ER, OPD, IPD, department and the laboratory. Neonatal, reproductive health care incl care during pregnancy and childbirth and conducting normal and C-section deliveries will be provided at the RH facility. Supported facilities will receive payment of salaries for staff (medical and non-medical), monthly operational costs, professional training and monitoring, provision of pharmaceuticals, and other medical and non-medical commodities (including equipment, medicines, and consumables). MEAL activities include a) daily visits to the facilities and observation notes, b) patient satisfaction surveys, c) AAP, d) monitor number of visitors/patients, e) conducting verification checks and f) exit interviews with patients. The team will prepare the IPTT, observation reports and means of verification. Plus uphold the AAP mechanism and learning.

Service mapping to inform about alternative services.2) PWD or at-risk groups might be excluded from services or facilities.Likelihood: Low; Impact: HighInformation sharing, services and facilities are suitable for at-risk groups, conduct AAP sessions to inform about requesting special assistance for PWD/elderly/pregnant BNFs, consultation with stakeholders to identify excluded beneficiaries, training staff on inclusion.3) Risk of GBV to/from/in overcrowded health facilities Likelihood: Medium; Impact: HighSelection of suitable locations and facilities, referral of special needs cases, avoid long queues and allow clear space for waiting, provide child care services, suitable service times, guarded facilities.4) At-risk group do not receive needed servicesLikelihood: Medium; Impact: MediumCoordination with sectors to avoid duplication, collaborate with protection and GBV teams to ensure access, consult BNFs in sex-segregated FGDs, use Washington Group Questions as tool to include disability in selection.5) Risk of COVID-19Upholding WHO recommended measures, provision of relevant information to BNFs, increased GBV response to mitigate domestic violence as result of economic pressure on the family.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Violet targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements. Potential risks:1) Request of favors, e.g. SEA, for selecting BNFs to the services Mitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return in any case, emphasise on complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as alternative, ensure appropriate staffing during any distribution or service provision. 2) SEA misconduct from staff to selected beneficiaries or staff Mitigation: ensure regular staff training on PSEA and how to engage with beneficiaries, communication of PSEA policy and complaints mechanisms to BNFs, ensure diverse and gender balanced project and MEAL teams, clear visibility of staff, avoid overcrowded facilities and allocate clear waiting areas, identify at-risk households and provide special services e.g. transportation if needed.Violet will conduct sex-segregated FGDs and KIIs to ensure participation of women and girls during all phases of the project, especially in design of the activities and monitoring.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Staff infected with COVID-19 do not exceed 2% at all times. - Community acceptance for this project continues. - Current conflict doesn't affect program activities. - Border crossing from Turkey is maintained. - Drugs and medical supplies continue to be received through WHO medical supply-line.Risks: Unstable security situation: VO regularly monitors the local context, and employs security focal points. VO staff complete mandatory security trainings. Lack of the health services quality: VO has a health technical team who will be responsible on following up the quality of the health services provided in the health facilities as well as link the staff with the updated international guidelines and protocols. Moreover, a training plan for the staff will be set in coordination with health facilities teams and the staff needs. Complaints mechanism is already set in the health facilities and the patients can raise any issue regarding the quality of the health services and will registered and directed to the relevant department to deal with. the Cross-border resolution is not renewed: VO will advocate to the importance of the cross border operations to the people in NWS and will shed the lights on the humanitarians need that still exist in the area.
