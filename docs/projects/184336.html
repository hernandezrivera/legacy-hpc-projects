Project Summary

Somalia's people have consistently had some of the worst health outcomes with the highest infant and child mortality rates, with 76.6 and 121.5 deaths per 1,000 live births, respectively. The maternal mortality rate (692 deaths per 100,000 live births) and fertility rate (average of 6.9 children per woman) are both among the highest in the world, and 79 percent of all births are delivered without the help of a trained professional at home (Humanitarian Needs Overview Somalia 2021). Beyond the disease's consequences, the COVID-19 pandemic continues to pose serious health risks, affecting service delivery, children's education, the economy, and other areas (UNICEF humanitarian situation report August 2021). Like major cities in Somalia, Kismayo has a rapidly growing population due to the immigration of population in search of aid, straining available Health services, especially in the IDP. According to the results by FSNAU and its partners in 2021 post-GU, the overall median Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate in Somalia among IDPs was at 11.2 percent, with a particularly highest prevalence of SAM rates was recorder among IDPs, including Kismayo IDPs at 3.3 per cent respectively. In these areas, the drivers of acute malnutrition include particularly high morbidity, low immunization, low vitamin-A supplementation, reduced access to milk, and food insecurity. The key reported barriers to access health services were the cost of services and medicines, the distance to treatment centres, and access to qualified staff.
In Hobyo district in Mudug region has poor coverage of functional health services and high morbidity rates. The nutrition situation is critical, vaccination coverage low, and WASH status very poor. SAF UK will therefore focus on reducing loss of lives, sustaining lives and centrality of protection, activities that support prevention and treatment of disease and resilience building. This will be accomplished through the use of four health centers, one PHU, and one mobile clinic, including a strong community component that ensures accountability to the people who are affected. At all stages of the project cycle, marginalized groups and the disabled, frequently overlooked and excluded, will be involved. The project will also make an effort to include people of all ages and genders. All staff will be trained in PSEA, social mobilization, and disability inclusion in programs to ensure that they can promote these while also ensuring their safety. Strong monitoring will ensure the realization of this with all data disaggregated by age, gender and disability. The project will integrate with Nutrition, WASH, protection and food security and livelihood activities. The project will also seek to improve health-seeking behaviour through community mobilization. In all sites, COVID 19 prevention will remain a key focus. Projects will continue to adhere to containment measures during implementation. Men, adolescents and the youth who have traditionally been neglected when it comes to programing will fully participate to ensure sustainability and mutual inclusivity. Peer support groups will also be included to promote peer learning. Access and coverage in these areas will be increased by the use of mobile teams and community health workers (CHW). Coordination will be enhanced by participating in health and other cluster forum. The project will adhere to government policies, use government-approved national and international guidelines, and work closely with MoH at the national and state levels.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

For IDP the main risk is displacement. For this, the SAF-UK has maintained a database for all beneficiaries with telephone contacts and that of relatives. For those in rural Mudug, the risk is spontaneous clan clashes. While it is difficult to deal with this, we have ensured that services are provided equitably for the different clans in their areas. Interclan conflicts, therefore, have minimal effect on our programs. in all locations, the organization will seek to provide safety nets by implementing livelihood programs. Victims of GBV will also be provided with an organizational GBV focal point to link survivors to appropriate assistance. Programs will also ensure IDP has access to essential services equitable and dignified with equality observed in job allocation in projects serving the IDP population. Staff recruitment is not based on tribe but professional competence; this has produced a team from a hybrid of cultures who have learned to work together well. Some positions are also reserved to the target communities and community groups that are marginalized like IDPs, PLWD and women, so that equity is achieved. All gender will be awarded the opportunity to benefit inequitably. Women, and youth who are mostly left out in recruitment will be considered in all projects, and positions will be awarded equitably. The needs assessment will include all community groups so that all conclusive, fair and agreeable evaluation is reached. Both men and women leaders with an additional youth and PLWD representative will be part of the community health board as per organizational policy. This will ensure decisions made are all-inclusive. In addition ,PLWD will be actively and meaningfully involved in the needs assessment, design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation as part of the project. Attitude, and institutional barriers to accessibility will be removed, allowing PLWD to access and use services more easily. SAF UK policies have sought to put men at the helm of needs affecting their wives, daughters and female relatives. The organization's APP framework seeks to ensure men participate through affirmative action for men ensuring that men are not excluded from interventions targeting women beneficiaries. Children who are often left out will not only be seen as beneficiaries, but their views on service delivery ad need will occasionally be sought by a professional child specialist so that the whole community participates in program design and implementation. Community diagnosis, community dialogues, evaluations and assessment will include all community groups to ensure all are reached. Discussions, assessments and conversations will consist of women, children, PLWD and youth with their own forum so as to ensure their views are considered and used in programming. The programs far and hard to reach areas in rural settings will be served by mobile teams so as to reduce risks and hazards faced by these groups enroute to accessing services.
