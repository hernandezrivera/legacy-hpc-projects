Project Summary

The project contributes to supporting social and economic realities in Al-Bab subdistrict in the NW of Aleppo and in Salqin in NW Idlib for a period of 12 months. It directly targets 2,125 BNs (50% M&50% F, 1010 IDPs & 1010 HCs (161 returnees), %6 PWDs) from both subdistricts with different types of modalities.
The project has two key tracks, with many links and integration between them in a way that maximizes the impact of the intervention.
FIRST TRACK: The social track that aims to empower the local communities and enhance social cohesion in both targeted subdistricts through:
- Building the capacity of two local development committees LDCs (one in each subdistrict) in community development and conflict-sensitive community initiatives.
- Providing guidance and consolation services to LDCs in developing 3 community initiatives in each subdistrict (2 to support social cohesion and one to support the community) and then reviewing the initiatives proposals according to the finings of “participatory market assessment” (PMA) and the social cohesion assessment. After that, provide micro-grants to the approved initiatives as per the initiatives plans.
- An extensive assessment of social “connectors” and “dividers” to identify the factors affecting social cohesion in the targeted subdistricts from social, economic, legal, and NGOs intervention aspects.
The findings of the assessment will be organized in a comprehensive report and disseminated to internal and external stakeholders, and hold a conference to present the main findings to the service providers in the targeted subdistricts, e.g. INGOs and NGOs, to benefit from the findings.
SECOND: The economic track that aims to improve the access of targeted communities to livelihoods and basic services through:
- Conduct a Participatory Market Assessment PMA to identify community priorities by engaging the L communities and LAs in assessing key value chains in their communities, market analysis, community assets, mapping services. LDCs, who have been trained in participatory evaluation, are involved in managing this evaluation.
- Based on PMA, and the high coordination with local authorities and service providers in the two targeted subdistricts, providing 300 beneficiaries from most vulnerable groups (50% F&50%M, 150 IDPs & 150 HCs (20 returnees), 7% PWDs) access to short-term employment in a manner that helps them meet their essential needs through work in the rehabilitation of socio-economic structures and societal initiatives. This, in turn, contributes to improving the access of targeted communities to basic services. A referral mechanism for labor will be established with prioritization of rehabilitation of socio-economic infrastructure.
Based on PMA, 100 BNs are supported to establish or expand their small enterprises and 100 BNs to start-up their vocations. This support will be through the provision of business development skills (BDS) and training vocational training courses, remotely. Guidance and consultation services will also be provided to BNs during the development of their business plans and during implementation. 200 micro-grants (cash, vouchers, and/or in-kind) will be awarded to the 200 beneficiaries based on the evaluation and approval of their business plans.
As BNs participated in project designing phase already, so again, while implementation: the partners will ensure that project is introduced to the L community and LAs including goals, activities, duration, location, and criteria of selecting BNs. To ensure well introduction process, it will include: consultation sessions with LAs, seminars with the L community, sessions with BNs, flyers, social media posts, road posters, and banners. Several project activities, for example: community assessment and community initiatives, are designed to be BNs an essential part of the outputs and implementation in addition to support the M,W and PwDs to ensure non-discriminatory and dignified access.

4 - Needs

Economic Reality:- The most important sources of income in the targeting areas are humanitarian organizations, agriculture, trade, and industry.- 32% of HHs have either headed by women or has no breadwinner.- The local communities in the targeted locations stated that the most important factors that accelerate the economic recovery process is (ordered by importance); 1) access to funding opportunities for entrepreneurs, 2) establish/rehabilitate economic infrastructure, and 3) provide vocational and skills training.- The most important training needs that an entrepreneurs need to establish or expand their projects (ordered by importance): 1) feasibility study, 2) financial and accounting skills, 3) Developing business plan, 4) marketing, and 5) ICDL.- The most important vocational training that the local community needs (ordered by importance): 1) housewares maintenance, 2) computers and mobile phones maintenance, 3) Software and website development and programming, 4) vehicles maintenance, and 5) hairstyling/hairdressing. Social Reality:- 33.4% of the local community from both locations stated that the participation of women and youth in public life is limited or poor. - 42% of local community from both locations stated that the relationship between IDPs and HCs is strained.- The most important factors that affect the social cohesion between IDPs and host communities (ordered by importance) are: 1) unjust opportunities to access livelihoods, 2) corruption, 3) crime, 4) exploitation, and 5) unjust access to health and education services.- 48.8% of the local communities stated that they prefer to attend trainings remotely within the current threat of COVID-19 pandemic.
