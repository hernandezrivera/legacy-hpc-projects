Project Summary

In 2024, UNRWA will continue to deliver basic, quality and inclusive education to 51,000 Palestine refugees through 104 schools managed by the Agency across Syria, of which 40 are running a double-shift in buildings on loan from the Ministry of Education (MoE) to compensate for UNRWA schools that have been destroyed or severely damaged by the conflict.
UNRWA, through its Education in Emergencies (EiE) programme helps to ensure that Palestine refugee children can continue to access education, even in times of emergencies, and facilitate linkages between the school and the community through PTA platforms. The UNRWA approach to EiE involves "doing things differently" (delivering education in alternative ways), "doing more of other things" (more PSS to children) and "doing things that had not been the focus before" (safety and security training for students, staff and parents).
In 2024, UNRWA will provide back-to-school kits (including school bags) at the beginning of the next school year (2024/25) to all 51,000 students; stationery will be procured and provided to students at the beginning of the 2024/2025 academic year to alleviate some of the economic burden faced by parents.
UNRWA will also provide (1) catch-up classes for 17,500 students including including for Grade 9 students to help them prepare for their official end-of-school exams as well as (2) summer recreational programmes for 12,000 UNRWA students where PSS, together with educational activities, will provide them with the opportunity to address learning gaps while sharing experiences and forging friendship with their peers.　UNRWA will also provide transportation (contracted buses) for students from the destructed Ein el Tal camp and Senaya to take them to schools in nearby areas.
The Agency will continue to provide PSS to address anxiety, stress and trauma caused by years of conflict and deteriorating socioeconomic conditions. Psychosocial counsellors will be deployed in all Agency schools to provide individual and group counselling and support to students and teachers. This is to address the emotional toll associated with: (i) the conflict; (ii) socio-economic crises; (iii) natural disasters, including the 2023 earthquakes; and (iv) the effects of bullying, low self-esteem and other concerns which, if not addressed, may lead to school drop-outs and other societal issues.
A number of training sessions will be conducted for teachers and school principals on topics such as GBV, child protection and addressing violence against children (AVAC). Similar training sessions will be delivered to parents through the PTAs, which will continue to play a pivotal role in developing a holistic approach to education for Palestine refugees.
UNRWA will also aim to rehabilitate one school and conduct minor repairs of 74 UNRWA school facilities that require urgent maintenance.
As an international relief and human development agency and in order to further effective service delivery, UNRWA requires high standards of accountability, transparency and refugee participation, in line with its protection mainstreaming principles. In this regard, the Agency has committed to establish and use feedback and complaint mechanisms as valuable means to: (a) further working partnerships with Palestine refugees, (b) incorporate refugee feedback into programme delivery; and (c) secure early detection and community response to issues of importance to the population served.
Lastly, this action contributes to the achievement of all three strategic objectives under HRP 2024-2025 as all three of Education Sector's objectives (objective 1 - availability & access, objective 2 - quality, and objective 3 - resilient systems).
M&E data on student enrollment, school attendance, students reached with PSS counseling will be recorded in Agency’s online Education Management Information System and in the Agency's Result Based Monitoring System. Data will also be reported externally to the Syria HRP’s 4Ws system.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection risk: Violence against children in and around UNRWA schools. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigation: 1. Deliver training to address violence against children (AVAC) and decrease violence in and around UNRWA installations, including peer-to-peer violence. 2. Conduct self-care sessions with the UNRWA education staff and provide counselling and psychosocial support to the students through UNRWA school counsellors 3. UNRWA addresses cases of violence against children from staff members using available investigative and sanctions regimes. 4. UNRWA provides training on positive behaviour management techniques to teachers. 5. UNRWA creates child-friendly learning environments. 6. Enhanced participation of students’ parliaments. Risk: Exposure to Explosive Ordnance while accessing services. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: 1. Facilitator and focal point training of staff including psychosocial counsellors and teachers and widespread awareness-raising activities with children to increase their knowledge and reduce their exposure to these risks. 2. 14,150 school children in higher-risk areas are prioritized for EORE sessions. 3. Advocacy for rubble removal around UNRWA installations.Risk: Students/families resorting to negative coping strategies due to the declining economic situation (dropout, child labour, early marriage, drug abuse). Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigation: 1. Enhance psychosocial support activities in schools. 2. School counsellors in conjunction with UNRWA social workers provide personalized assistance to students who are identified as having specific needs. PSEA Strategy: UNRWA’s peculiar modality of work through direct implementation and the consequent large footprint of approximately 4,000 mostly frontline staff in Syria heightens the risk of SEA. UNRWA Syria conducted a PSEA risk assessment in December 2023. SEA risks: 1. Decline in reported cases. 2. limited awareness of official reporting mechanisms by beneficiaries and frontline staff. 3.Limited community outreach. 4. Lapses in confidentiality in handling allegations. 5. Cultural challenges and stigma.Mitigation measures: 1. Country-level SOP on managing SEA allegations developed. 2. PSEA awareness posters (including child-friendly and “reporting misconduct” posters) displayed in all approximately 120 UNRWA facilities (schools, Health clinics, distribution centres, community centres, administrative offices etc) and quarterly checks are done to ensure availability. 3. Train staff on PSEA policy (almost 300 trained in-person in 2023). 4. Ramp up community outreach. 5. All personnel contracts include affirmation on PSEA. 6. UNRWA is part of the UN ClearCheck recruitment screening database. 7. Frontline staff are identifiable with UNRWA vests and IDs. 8. Complaints and feedback mechanisms established and set up at all UNRWA installations.
