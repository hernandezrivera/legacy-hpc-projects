Project Summary

The intervention will focus on three main objectives which will include; Improving access for conflict-affected children and adolescents to inclusive quality basic education and vocational skills opportunities within a safe learning environment, Supporting the provision of quality and conflict-sensitive educational services to enhance learning, Enhance/maintain the capacity of government ministries, departments and agencies (MDAs) and communities to plan and deliver a timely, appropriate and evidence-based education-in emergencies response.
These three objectives will help address the issues of out-of-school children among IDPs, returnees and host communities in the BAY (Borno, Adamawa, Yobe) states. The intervention will also aim to improve the quality of education through teacher training and the distribution of books. These objectives will contribute to reinforcing inclusion of all vulnerable groups of children, and improving safety in and around learning spaces. Much of the work will be done in collaboration with government structures within the Ministry of Education to ensure the education-in-emergency response is part of the national and state plan.

1 - Needs

According to the HRP 2022 and in relation to the education sector encouraging investment in innovative informal or formal education modalities (remote, blended, and self-learning) for both in-and out-of-school children, formal schools that accommodate displaced students, through staged returns or bridging classes, will be supported to provide conducive learning environments. Classrooms will be rehabilitated or constructed and equipped in coordination with the Ministry of Education and State Universal Basic Education Boards, and according to government standards. The intervention will be implemented in close working collaborations with the ministry of education on an intervention strategy. The response and intervention strategy will focus on increasing access to informal and vocational skills opportunities required to address the multifaceted learning needs of children who have had years of disrupted learning; catch-up classes accelerated learning programs, vocational skills training, mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS), and pathways back to inclusive age-appropriate levels of formal education. The intervention will prioritize the most deprived population groups, in particular, those in areas with high severity of inter-sectoral needs, and where learning facilities and educational thematic areas are disproportionately underserved. Similarly, considering the majority of the out-of-school children are internally displaced, often more than once. Children living in communities or catchments where there are few or no available education services will be prioritized as well.

2 - Activities

The intervention will support the activities of the existing education system and school structure to strengthen capacity. It will encourage government-led initiatives to mainstream minimum standards for safe schools within educational programming. Efforts will also be made to operationalize the safe schools' declaration and create a sense of safety for learners, which is expected to encourage them to stay in school and improve their learning outcomes. The intervention will sensitize school-based management committees on school safety, and empower school administration structures to analyze and mitigate safety risks. It will collaborate with relevant sectors to maintain a resilient education system, particularly in collaboration with the child protection sub-sector, teachers will be equipped with safeguarding skills to recognize and respond to distress in children and refer those in need to appropriate service providers. The intervention will also engage in a number of initiatives including protection activities in schools with a specific focus on the prevention of abuse and exploitation of girls to reduce the rate of dropout. A sense of safety and well-being is key to achieving learning outcomes and fostering a conducive learning environment. Furthermore, the intervention will also aim to increase MHPSS activities to address the stress and psychosocial pressure from the crisis for both students and teachers alike. Recent studies and assessments have shown that teachers also need support to address both their own and their students’ psychosocial and emotional needs.
