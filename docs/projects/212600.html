Project Summary

In 2024, 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. At least 15.4 million people across Syria need food assistance. High inflation, currency depreciation, and increases in commodity prices continued to drive humanitarian needs in Syria. This resulted in an increase in poverty and reliance on humanitarian assistance (HNO 2024). The project aims to support the conflict affected communities and households in North-West Syria through a combination of the following interventions: 1- Agricultural inputs, fuel and climate-smart agriculture, fodder preparation and marketing trainings will be provided to small scale wheat, vegetable and forage farmers. 2- SMEs support for women who will receive food processing training and hybrid support including food processing kits or cash in hand. 3- Cash for work activities where workers will be enrolled to engage in seasonal agriculture activities such as vegetable weeding and harvesting. 4- Distribution of bread bundles in the communities of most vulnerable groups. These interventions will be announced in familiar places that are easy to reach all communities. The registration will be in-person and online to be more accessible to women and PwDs. Promoting equitable access to skills-building opportunities, including offering childcare to trainees, will help avoid the adoption of harmful coping mechanisms. All venues including distribution and trainings, and cash distribution points and other services will be accessible and safe, particularly against the risk or threat of gender-based violence and other protection-related risks. Moreover, transportation services for PwDs to and from the training center will be provided. For specific cases, door-to-door distribution will be applied. Furthermore, training will be provided for project staff on how to interact with and support individuals with various types of disabilities. The project participants under CFW activity will receive a package of training prior to enrolment. The training set includes (Occupational Safety, Safety, and Security Precautions, PSEAH, Gender, complaint and feedback mechanism, code of conduct, and enrolment agreement terms and conditions).

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project aims to support the conflict affected communities and households in North-West Syria through a combination of the following interventions: 1- 3025 farmers will be supported in Aleppo and Idleb governorates with agricultural inputs, fuel and climate-smart agriculture, fodder preparation and marketing trainings will be provided to small scale wheat, vegetable and forage farmers. Vegetable, wheat and forage crops farmers will receive agricultural inputs and fuel support through hybrid modalities. The average of the provided agricultural inputs and fuel support is 450 USD per farmer.2- 1117 SMEs support for women who will receive food processing training and hybrid support including food processing kits or cash in hand. The project participants will receive a set of support packages for establishing or expanding small enterprises creating/sustaining their livelihoods and enhancing self-reliance. The participants will receive technical food processing training, business development skills training, one-off grant of 315 USD with hybrid modality in addition to mentoring and coaching support sessions.3- Cash for work activities where the project' participants will be supported by short-term income-generating activities. The cash for work workers will participate in seasonal agriculture activities such as vegetable weeding and harvesting, forage crops, winter crops purification and pest control, and nurseries for a period ranging from 20 to 40 days. The average daily wage of the participants will be 9 USD.4- 1500 HHs living in IDPs camps will receive daily free bread for 3.5 months as follows:- Households with 4 or less members will receive 1 Bread Bundle BB, HHs with 5-8 members will receive 2 BBs, HHs with 9 or more members will receive 3 BBs according to the Bread and Bakeries Technical Working Group Guidance. In addition, it’s worth mentioning that each bread bundle weighs 1 KG. The registration will be in-person and online to be more accessible to women and PwDs. Promoting equitable access to skills-building opportunities, including offering childcare to trainees, will help avoid the adoption of harmful coping mechanisms. All venues including distribution and trainings, and cash distribution points and other services will be accessible and safe, particularly against the risk or threat of gender-based violence and other protection-related risks. Moreover, transportation services for PwDs to and from the training center will be provided. For specific cases, door-to-door distribution will be applied.
