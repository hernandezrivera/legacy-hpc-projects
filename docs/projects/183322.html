Project Summary

The ERP acknowledges that estimates of extreme poverty also known as “food poverty” amongst the Lebanese stand at 36% (1.38 million). Food security has become a major threat to the Lebanese and migrant community in general and specifically to the female-headed households, lactating, and pregnant women, and households with people with disabilities. As it is also expected to remove what is left from current subsidies “Lebanese and migrant households are unable to rely solely on social safety nets to cushion the impact of these shocks”. Despite existing systems and collaborating efforts of local and international communities, needs are exponentially increasing.
The proposed project under the LHF 2021 1st standard allocation, responds to vulnerable Lebanese families that have found themselves unable to meet their food security needs and critically food insecure as a result of the deteriorating situation of the country. After the passing of yet another year since the beginning of the Lebanese crisis, Lebanon is still facing the quadruple challenge of economic, political, social and health crises. Despite the collaborative efforts of local and international communities, needs are exponentially increasing thus, there still remains Lebanese families that are in need of emergency assistance at least during the coming months to cover their food and health needs. By conducting several assessments through our work in South and Nabatieh areas it was identified that there are approximately 500 HH in need of food assistance/
DPNA will work on providing 500 families in the areas of South and Nabatieh (after validating the criteria through an endorsed survey in compliance with WFP and Ministry of social affairs – MOSA- standards) that fall under at least one condition of the targeted vulnerability criteria of this allocation with in-kind food assistance for a period of 6 months. Also, under this project DPNA will distribute for a period of 2 months in-kind food assistance for COVID-19 positive cases referred by DPNA’s call center (endorsed by UNICEF and MOH) and collaborating municipalities. The second activity will be achieved through the establishment of a contingency stock of food kits. Through these two components we would contribute to the strategic food objective of the allocation which is the provision of immediate life-saving food assistance to meet growing and critical food needs of vulnerable Lebanese and migrant workers, not covered under the LCRP.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Distribution of Food parcels to 500 families (LHF Allocation - FSS); outreach, assessment and validation of most critically food insecure families as per criteria and survey endorsed by WFP and MoSA in coordination with Municipalities; procurement of 4,000 food parcels in line with sector composition endorsed by WFP via a competitive tender process. Provision of parcels through a rolling monthly distribution to most vulnerable families in South and Nabatieh areas via door-to-door and distribution centers (municipalities). Distributions will go over a period of 8 months under the LHF fund. COVID-19 Food Distribution to 150 families (LHF allocation): 300 food kits, will be distributed to 150 COVID-19 positive individuals over the period of 2 months. COVID-19 cases will be identified in this distribution through referrals of established call center run by DPNA endorsed by UNICEF.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

DPNA adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards sexual/child exploitation, abuse, or violence, enforced via policies in the organization and in the program and all individuals involved with it.. DPNA will take in consideration GBV guidelines when implementing the activities: https://gbvguidelines.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Interagency-GBV-risk-mitigation-and-Covid-tipsheet.pdf Moreover, DPNA shall ensure that all its employees and personnel comply with the provisions of “Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Sexual Abuse”. As well as ensuring that involved staff and volunteers have had training on the PSEA. Adherence of article 19 of the General Terms and Conditions in the PCA and SSFA documents. PSEA will also be mainstreamed in the distributions. DPNA will address issues of inequality, paying special attention to gender and disability. DPNA will do this by assessing needs and establishing the risks and vulnerabilities of the various gender and age groups.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

The proposed project was developed based on the needs of beneficiaries (the most vulnerable and critically food insecure families), hence being included in the design phase to meet their most urgent needs in the implementation phase. DPNA coordinates with UN agencies (mainly UNICEF and WFP), meeting the IASC Commitments to Affected Populations. Moreover, DPNA will put in place a practical and well-documented mechanism to receive and respond to beneficiaries’ complaints and inquires. This will be achieved by allocating a designated hotline number of the project, providing the number to all beneficiaries and training project team on how to respond and effectively clarify any inquiries. Any complaint during the field visits of the team is noted for further investigation. Every complaint will be resolved, documented and reported. Moreover, DPNA applies the Do No Harm approach through all its projects implementation ensuring transparency and communication with beneficiaries.
