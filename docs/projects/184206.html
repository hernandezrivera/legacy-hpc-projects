Project Summary

FSNAU and FEWS NET Post-Gu Assessment in September 2021 projected that nearly 3.5 million Somalis would be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) through at least December 2021, in the absence of humanitarian assistance. The 2022 humanitarian needs overview (HNO) shows the continued political instability, population’s displacements, ongoing armed conflict, and longstanding environmental stress have rendered many of the country’s estimated 15.7 million people acutely food insecure, 2022 presents the potential for drought to be one of the primary drivers of food insecurity.
Jubaland Development Organization (JDO) hereby propose support livelihood through response to 5000 beneficiaries; supporting up to 3000 individuals with lifesaving food assistance anticipating 500 individuals in urban setting, 500 in rural areas and 2000 from IDPs centers in the project locations. JDO will aim to provide access to sufficient, safe, nutritious and easy to use food, based on local preferences, availability and market functionality and ensuring equal access for men, women, boys and girls who fit collectively or individually within set criteria. JDO anticipate supporting between 600 to 1400 beneficiaries per location with cash and voucher assistance (CVA) either directly (in hand) or as electronic cash (e.g. mobile phones) as well as commodity voucher or value voucher worth food or NFI as a paper or electronic voucher contributing to environmentally sustainable and safe programming including cash for work in urban, rural and IDP centers.
JDO will also support 2000 individuals with agriculture, livestock and other livelihood supports, anticipating 1000 non-IDPs (500 urban, 500 rural) and 1000 IDPs in the target location with aim of dealing with the underlying causes of food insecurity and mitigating risk of new crises, famine and further displacement. Targeting relevant communities with relevant opportunities which can promote healthy and diversified food choices that leads to improved diets such as farm inputs (seed package) or tractors hours and support where possible to reactivate and protect affected agricultural food and livestock which include hay/fodder and/or veterinary services (animal vaccination and treatment). JOD will work with partner on promoting climate smart agriculture production practices to reduce the risk of production loss due to the adverse effects of climate, innovative methods that enable people to access food using limited land and water resources and link to small and medium enterprises and market linkages during engagement, focusing on the inclusion of local market actors and small businesses. The is anticipated to reach beneficiaries in Banadir, Doolow and Kismaayo
The project anticipate reaching up to 500 HH supporting people living with disabilities. Additionally, JDO will endeavor to ensure gender balance in staff, and beneficiaries ration anticipating up to 50% representation of women and girls in all activities.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Beneficiaries feedback as key informants as well as market assessments show the need for individuals to uniquely identify their products based on access and functionality. In kind distribution usually are of a one product fits all nature, which does not explore individual choice or compatibility of product to beneficiaries context. Most beneficiaries currently possess mobile phones which are linked to mobile money transfer which is also acceptable to most retailers or their contractors. With cash vouchers, the project will also address the risks of exclusion and discrimination, including those based on societal discrimination, power structures, vulnerability considering no beneficiary will go through physical strain to access service since cash can be disbursed remotely. JDO logistics, finance, IT and information management staff will be involved through the project cycle inline with policies which will ensure accountability to affected population mechanism and transparent to external monitoring and evaluation processes . CTP additionally provide flexibility, choice, dignity, it is also empowering, cost effective and protect beneficiaries from negative coping strategies (unlike when aid is given in kind, families are often forced to seek cash in ways that may be detrimental).

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Most vulnerable beneficiaries in or from IDP camps or even non-IDP communities use EVC (mobile money) on daily basis for most of their needs. The project will involve small and medium enterprises and market linkages engagement, focusing on the inclusion of local actors and small businesses. And when possible supporting the reactivation and protection of affected agricultural; seed, food and livestock markets creating an enabling environment for the recovery and strengthening of local market systems. The cash transfer aims to protect balance in the markets limiting alteration and/or disruption in the availability of goods, services, labour and income which is likely to happen with introduction of in-kind items in the community.
