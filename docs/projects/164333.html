Project Summary

Syria is now in its ninth year of conflict and while the Education system is slowly recovering in some areas, 2.1 million children are still out of school and 5.9 million are in need of educational support. In
North East Syria (NES) most of the schools which were not heavily damaged or destroyed have re-opened and are lightly supported by the self-administration but large gaps still remain and the recent crisis from October 2019 has further stretched the capacity of the formal system. With the recent events, 810 schools have closed, disrupting the education of over 86,000 children.
The organization reached around 7110 beneficiaries in 2019 with a budget of 1,600,000 USD.
The Proposed action is covering around 8900 beneficiaries with a cost of 180$ per beneficiary, the estimated percentage of target beneficiaries that may have disabilities is around 3-4 %.
The proposed Action will offer Non-Formal Education (NFE) opportunities in three subdistrics in NES namely Raqqa,Thawra and Kisreh with a 66 % severity 5 and 33% severity 6 and a target of 1104 children and adolescents between 8 to 17 years who are out of school or in school in a very low grade for their age. Children will receive literacy, numeracy and Social-Emotional Learning sessions. The NFE will help them build the skills required to transfer to formal education or to catch up for missed years.
The project will include tailored NFE activities in the different previously mentioned locations:
1) NFE Centres will be open where possible to target OOSC from 8 to 14 years old to support with an
Accelerated Learning Programme for 9 months. The aim will be for children to reintegrate formal
Schools.
2) Homework support groups with remedial programme for former OSSC attending school in a lower
Grade for their age. This can be done in the NFE Centre or within the schools in the afternoon while
there is no teaching. The support will be designed for 3 months but could be extended according to the needs to ensure the children can catch up with their age peers.
3) NFE in collective centres or informal settlements in locations with a possible reintegration to formal schools. This will be done for 6 to 9 months according to the level of the children and their previous education. The project will ensure strong involvement of the host community to ease school reintegration.
4) Female and male adolescent groups (14 to 17 years old) with only male or female facilitators providing a catch up programme and reinforced protection and life skills sessions. This will allow this vulnerable age group exposed to risks of early marriage, military recruitment and hard unskilled labour, to be supported and empowered to protect themselves and build their personal project. According to the level and personal interests, the project will support for transition back to school (late primary or secondary) and vocational training/school.
According to their needs, the families of children receiving NFE will be supported by parenting sessions and the access to specialized services; if a child is joining the NFE activities, the household will be assessed to receive food, shelter, NFI assistance or vocational training opportunities to ensure the support is comprehensive and barriers to education access are reduced.
The project will also support formal education by holistic assistance for 10 schools in Raqqa and Derzor covering around 7000 children and 250 formal teachers. These schools will be made physically safe with essential repairs to WASH facilities and infrastructure, ensuring SPHERE minimum standards are met. Teachers working in these schools along with other education personnel that may include supporting mechanisms such as parents-teachers associations, mothers-students associations, and school management associations will be trained on the Teachers in Conflict Context training and regularly supported through a mentorship programme in addition of receiving teaching and learning materials.

4 - Needs

Children in Syria face numerous challenges, including limited access to education. The 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) notes that 7 million children are in need of education support, 86% of these children have acute and immediate need of humanitarian education assistance (severity of 4-5-6) . In North East Syria (NES) most of the schools which were not heavily damaged or destroyed have re-opened and are lightly supported by the self-administration but large gaps still remain and the recent crisis from October 2019 has further stretched the capacity of the formal system. The situation for returnees and host communities was already fragile with a significant number of children who have never received any form of education and school buildings which do not allow a safe learning environment. As a result of the renewal in conflict in October 2019, 810 schools have closed with over 86,000 children having their education disrupted and over 5,000 teachers being affected. As of end of November 2019, there are up to 77,622 people who remain displaced. Hassakeh governorate has been especially and largely impacted by these displacements and many schools had to close to be used as emergency collective shelters. In Raqqa and Deir ez-Zor (DeZ), a large number of IDP arrivals has also been observed, most of whom have been hosted by host communities.The project will work on Increased safe and equitable humanitarian access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children by providing children and youth with NFE program in addition of the full rehabilitation of schools and the provision of learning and teaching materials.The project will also work on enhancing the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth by providing professional development for teachers and school personnel, provide teachers and facilitators with teaching materials and kits in addition of working on the children psycho-social support.

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposed Action will improve access to formal education for current and former out-of-school children as well as quality and safe education in the formal schools in Raqqah, Tabqa and Deir ez-Zor. This will be achieved through 4 various NFE interventions mentioned in the project summary that include SEL for 1104 children and youth and through support to 10 formal schools.To address the gap in school infrastructure and the safety risks posed by damaged and nonclimate-proofed buildings, as well as limited access to sanitation facilities and clean water and theunaccessibility of the school to persons with disabilities, the project will undertake full rehabilitation of 10 schools in Raqqa and Deir zor.In consultation with the school management committees, the team will undertake repairs of school WASH infrastructure to ensure hygiene, sufficient number of latrines (according to SPHERE Standards) and access to safe water and this will be monitored by a satisfactory survey at the end of the project. The project will carry out training on the Teacher in Conflict Context training for 250 teachers and other education personnel in the 10 schools rehabilitated to better understand the importance of their wellbeing and their role in promoting children’s safety. This Action will also improve access to formal education for current and former OOSC through variousNFE interventions including self learning program and competencies based retention support program in addition to the social and emotional learning curriculum. Activities will target children from 8 to 17 years old currently or formerly out of school with different modalities and will include literacy, numeracy PSS and SEL sessions.For NFE activities, a baseline will be conducted when the activities begin and then an endline using learning outcomes assessment (EGRA and EGMA). Training pre and post tests will be used for the different teacher trainings in both the NFE centres and formal schools.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Following the assessment done in Raqqa for formal schools, it appears that there are 50 functioning schools in Raqqa city welcoming 45,209 students and 1,481 teachers. From the assessmentdata, it is clear that about 10 schools are in urgent need of rehabilitation, posing extremely high risk tothe safety of about 8,800 children. Equally striking was the lack of windows and doors which poses a high risk to the children' safety (due to the lack of protection from the windows on the upper floors) andhealth, particularly during the winter months when temperatures can drop below zero degrees Celsiusand the classrooms cannot be effectively heated. The situation of the WASH facilities is also alarming with 15 schools without any functioning latrines. The students go to the bathroom in nearbyhouses, exposing them to the potential risk of physical or sexual abuse, outside of the controlled schoolenvironment. In Deir ez-Zor, whereas the host communities declared a relatively high attendance for primary schoolage children (95% of communities estimated that more than half of children were in school at least 4days a week in the West line, 61% in the East line), this number is much lower for IDPs with only 22%in the West line. The identified barriers to education in the West line are that children have to work(82%), there is a lack of trained teachers (62%)and poor conditions of the facilities (41%).All schools rehabilitation contractors will receive an induction on the child safeguarding policy.In the NFE classes the project will ensure that all staff , facilitators and contractors are abiding and signing on the child safeguarding policy and will ensure all classes are free from any kind of risk including physical, sexual and psycho-social risks. the project has Community Response Mechanism in place. Participants in all our activities candirectly provide feedback or complaints about programmes, staff and systems via WhatsApp,email, telephone and complaints boxes.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The assumptions will be as following:_ The ability of the project to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the program accordingly. - The security situation will be stable enough to operate in the proposed target area. Some of the risks :_Possible escalation in the ongoing conflict between the SDF and GoT/SNA, extending beyond the current areas of operation. This scenario could see renewed conflict along the length of the border or the 32km GoT ‘Security Zone’, which would potentially restrict staff and supply access routes as well as key trade routes with widespread repercussions on NGO access and operations. - Possible non-renewal of the cross border resolution and possible change in the education authorities - A delay in receiving approval, only receiving partial approval for some project activities or only receiving approval in some areas from the Self-Administration and/or local civilian councils or authorities. This would have knock-on effects on project implementation. Mitigation Measures : - Ensuring a constant state of preparedness for the risks identified above, specifically this entails: ensuring the regular updating of duty of care policies, contingency measures, and scenario plans.-Strengthening the resilience of operations to sudden changes and shocks and ensuring adaptability is built into all activities.
