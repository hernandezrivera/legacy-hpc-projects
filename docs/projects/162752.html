Project Summary

Summery
Based on several recent assessments, the main needs of the most vulnerable communities, including IDPs, returnees and host communities, remain the physical and financial access to adequate and sufficient notorious food through their own production or markets. Farmers faced challenges related to high costs of and lack of agricultural inputs on the market, and difficulties in accessing credit. Post-harvest losses around 14 % is also a major constraint on farmers’ profitability. Resulting in approximately 57 percent of farmers selling their crops immediately after the harvest without waiting for the most profitable time to sell. FAO conducted a rapid survey, in the first week of July 2019, for the assessment of the main crop summer season in the five Darfur states, South Kordofan, West Kordofan, Blue Nile, Kassala, and Red Sea states. The survey revealed some concerns and challenges facing the general livestock conditions in the surveyed states. Massive cattle losses happened due to water shortages during the last winter. Lack of fuel for pumping water for livestock consumption was therefore the main reason for the reported death in cattle. Suspected cases of BQ and HS were also reported in Darfur and Kordofan states.
The FAO project for Rest of Sudan will support 1,075,000 vulnerable farmers, pastoralists and agro-pastoralists households in 31 localities in five States of Sudan; Blue Nile, South Kordofan, West Kordofan, Kassala and Red Sea States. These localities has been classified as IPC phase3 and above. The neediest of them will be selected and supported with a suitable packages. The target include; 387,000 women, 36500 men, 1615250 girls and 161250 boys. They will receive improved varieties of seeds, tools, animal feed, mineral licks, dairy goats/ sheep, animal vaccines, capacity building and income generating activities while protecting their environment through the application of good practices. This is to mitigate the impacts of the current shocks on the livestock and agriculture sectors and increase resilience for the targeted vulnerable people. The project have the following activities:
1- Provide agricultural inputs; vegetable and legume seeds and tools (weeding hoes and rakes) for 279500 individuals with emphasis on female-headed HHs; within the targeted HH 500 person will receive cash+ agricultural as a pilot intervention.
2- Provide vaccination services to 2, 000, 000 animals belonging to 322,500 individuals and provide veterinary services to protect the livestock asset;
3- Provide milking goats and sheep to 10,000 vulnerable households with emphasis on female headed HHs to improve their protein intake and income;
4- Provide supplementary feeding and training to 10,000 vulnerable households giving priority to female headed HHs;
5- Provide training and tool kits to 354,750 individuals including at least 30,000 female head households as sources of income, with protection objective;
6- Provide training and awareness sessions in environment protection and conservation to 118,250 individuals, of whom 10,000 female headed HHs receive support with vegetables and legumes seeds and hand tools and will be further trained in manufacturing and use of fuel efficient stoves, with protection objective;
7- Train 125 staff and sector partners to monitor the project activities at output and outcome levels including collection and analysis of disaggregated data on GBV risk associated with the livelihood activities.

1 - Needs

Despite the satisfactory outcome of the 2018-cropping season, market availability is low, with traders reported to hoard their agricultural produce, regarded as a more reliable form of savings compared to the fast weakening local currency. In addition, foreign currency shortages are seriously constraining availabilities of imported food commodities, mainly wheat (CFSAM 2018). More than half of the households are spending at least 75% of their expenditure on food and are unable to create or invest in livelihood assets. Similarly, 58% of the households cannot afford the local food basket due to limited purchasing power resulting from inflation and high food prices (CFSA 2019). The household’s food stocks are expected to deplete during the lean season from June –September 2020. Affected families, particularly female-headed households, families with larger number of children, and people with disabilities, have difficulties in accessing food due to high market prices of food commodities and weak purchasing power because of limited means of livelihood and due to recent economic and financial policies. Food utilization is affected by poor food diversity. Malnourishment and food insecurity rates are found to be higher among female-headed households and children under five. Farmers faced challenges related to high costs of and lack of agricultural inputs on the market, and difficulties in accessing credit. Post-harvest losses around 14 % is also a major constraint on farmers’ profitability. Resulting in approximately 57 percent of farmers selling their crops immediately after the harvest without waiting for the most profitable time to sell. According to FAO rapid survey, livestock in, South Kordofan, West Kordofan, Blue Nile, Kassala, and Red Sea states have some concerns and challenges facing the general conditions in the surveyed states. Massive cattle losses happened due to water shortages during the last winter. Lack of fuel for pumping water for livestock consumption was therefore the main reason for the reported death in cattle. Suspected cases of BQ and HS were also reported. Women in Sudan play a significant role in agriculture, with 80 percent of farming households having at least 1 woman involved in harvesting on average, 73 percent in post-harvest, and 64 percent in storing. Lowest women involvement in agriculture was reported in Blue Nile (31 percent) and Red Sea States (9 percent). However, only 54 percent of the households reported women’s involvement in marketing. In general, smallholder farmers exhibited high vulnerability to food insecurity compared to big farmers; while households engaged in non-agricultural wage labor, selling firewood, charcoal; were the most food insecure. Households engaged in sustainable and high return livelihood activities (business, salaried work) are most food secure. The government efforts in peacebuilding and the new polices that support agriculture might increase the rerun to the home villages and increase the demand for agriculture and livestock support.to support larger number of farmers and agro pastoralists. The project will target 387,000 women, 365,000 men, 161,250 girls and 161,250 boys. The target include 430,000 IDPs, 107,500 returnees and 537,500 from vulnerable hosting communities.

2 - Activities / Outputs

FAO will provide drought-resistant Quality seeds of the main crops which are usually cultivated in the targeted areas based on the priorities of the beneficiaries. It will promote diversification of crop production including cultivation of nutritious crops such as vegetables, legumes, fruits as well as cultivation of the traditional staple food crops. This intervention will enable households to produce their own food and obtain income from marketing the surplus production. FAO will implement cash+ interventions which have proven to be very successful in achieving immediate food security, supporting food production and restoring livelihoods in similar contexts in Africa like Burkina Faso, Mali, Mauritania, Somalia, Ethiopia and Kenya.Through this intervention, veterinary services such as livestock vaccination for the endemic and TADS will be provided mostly on cost recovery basis; only for the most vulnerable households under emergency to be provided free. Training of CAHWs will be provided. The project will provide access to animal feed concentrate on cost recovery. Develop capacity of some categories of the population and supported with assets for income generation will target poor households, special attention will be given to women headed households and people with disabilities.The proposed FAO for rest of Sudan project would contribute across all three HRP priorities, articulated under three outcomes. 5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project by its nature has some negative implications on the environment particularly soil due to agricultural and livestock activities. Soil erosion related to deforestation and inappropriate agricultural practices is a key challenge to sustainable natural resources use in the targeted areas. The project will promote practices that favour soil protection and regeneration and don’t harm the local ecosystems to favour their progressive recovery. It will promote the cultivation of multipurpose legume crops that enrich and protect the soils, and strongly contribute to improving the nutrition of humans and their livestock. It will ensure that wastes from livestock vaccination and treatment are disposed in a proper way. Awareness rising in better natural resource management will be part of training. The project will engage in a number of mitigation activities that include tree plantation, training of households in effective and durable caretaking of the planted seedlings, and on agro‐forestry practices and manufacturing of fuel‐efficient stoves by trained women.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will consider the protection of people depending on their needs and requirements, the project will provide suitable interventions that improve women’s capacity to produce all or part of good-quality food requirements and generate cash income for their families, contributing to empowerment and social protection of women, especially women headed households. The project will give equal opportunities to all categories of the population to access the provided livelihood support, addressing the rights of all population categories to decent work to restore their food security and livelihood situation. The targeted communities have been involved in dialogue through meetings, interviews and group discussions throughout all steps of this project design and preparation and will be directly involved in the implementation processing including beneficiaries selection, registration and monitoring of the implementation at the field and beneficiaries level. As explained in the above paragraphs, FAO follows the necessary guidance for promoting the four pillars of decent work for the beneficiaries in the targeted rural areas.

As per the 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), FAO’s livelihoods interventions are aimed at responding to the needs and addressing the vulnerabilities of the disaster affected farming, pastoral and fishing communities in Sudan.
