Project Summary

Pakistan currently ranks at 151 out of 153 countries on the gender inequality index of the World Economic Forum’s Global Gap Report of 2020. The current system is based on strong patriarchal and social values where one gender is more privileged and has rights over the other and exiting social and structural barriers reinforce gender inequalities. This is further exacerbated with the onset of an emergency crisis as WHO reports levels of sexual Violence faced by women and other forms of Gender Based Violence (GBV) tend to grow more acute due to factors such as broken social and protective networks and lack of essential services.
As the pandemic of COVID-19 continues to spread in Pakistan, it is necessary to recognize the gender dimensions of the impact from the outbreak and for humanitarian organizations and government to respond to the immediate and intermediate needs of people particularly vulnerable groups of women and adolescent girls/children. Pakistan ranks as the sixth most risky country in the world for women, with rampant cases of sexual and domestic violence. The situation calls for a gender-responsive emergency measures to mitigate the harmful impacts of COVID-19 on protection of women, adolescent girls/ children and people living with disabilities (PLWDs). Evidence shows that the pandemic has exacerbated cases of violence against women, particularly domestic violence and sexual abuse.
Like with other provinces in particularly with the Province of Sindh which is the worst hit by COVID implementation of GBV services remains a big challenge. Despite several women-friendly laws and policies, and protection initiatives and services, challenges in implementation continue to
mar the prospects for women and children in Sindh. Majority of vulnerable women continue to lack access to free or affordable essential services in health, police, justice and social support. Essential services for survivors and victims of GBV remain scarce in the province of
Sindh. The administrative departments Women Development Department (WDD), Human Rights Department and the Social remained closed as part of the lockdown and precautionary measures of the Government of Sindh. The crisis centres and complaint cells have been rendered non-functional posing serious threats to women’s security and protection needs from GBV.
CARE International in Pakistan has a strong presence in the Districts of Sindh and has been actively responding to the emergency and development needs of the people in collaboration with the government mainly Social Welfare, Women Development Department and Provincial Commission on the Status of Women. CIP works on preventing GBV approaches while at the same time focusing and maintaining a survivor-centered approach to violence against women seeks to empower the survivors by prioritizing their, needs and priorities. CIP is recognized for its work and implemented a project preventing and protecting women from harmful practices in two districts of Sindh, Umerkot and Mirpurkhas. CIP has worked in collaboration with NCSW on Creating Safer Workplaces for Citizens of Pakistan focusing on ending GBV and harassment in the world of Work. CIP would like to continue this work through the HRP 2021 call in three districts of Sindh on preventing, responding and protecting GBV survivors in collaboration with the local authorities.
The project aims to strengthen health facilities and build capacity of health service providers to identify survivors of GBV and provide them with the necessary support by following the national and international guidelines. At the same time setting up referral for a coordinated response among key stakeholders to prevent, respond to cases of GBV and providing protection to complainants.
The project will engage communities to provide awareness on the GBV services, in addressing GBV and on the laws passed by the government of Pakistan.

1- Project Activities

1) Upgrading Health facilities to include GBV services• Assessment of health facilities DHQ/THQ/BHUs/RHUs in District Thatta to identify the gaps/priorities in providing GBV services. Developing a needs based strategy to upgrade existing health facilities, provide GBV and Clinical Management of Rape related services to women, girls/children & PWDs . • Conduct in person or remote training sessions with health service providers (HSPs) in identifying and addressing GBV (sexual violence, domestic violence etc. etc.), guidelines in supporting survivors of GBV (Essential Services Package).SOPs can be developed or existing ones of Government, WHO/UNFPA will be rolled out with HSPs for handling cases/disclosures of GBV especially in COVID-19 patients and setting up gender friendly quarantine centers for men, women & children • Develop awareness materials on GBV in the IEC toolkit for health centers to be mounted in conspicuous places. These will include, posters, brochures & videos 2) Developing Referral Mechanisms • Map existing referral services available at the district level to assess their condition and kind of services available, capacity of the staff and what are the challenges/requirements of the existing referral/s.If the district has already established referral systems CARE International Pakistan (CIP) will strengthen those services through sensitization trainings of staff on prevention, response & protection from GBV and supporting GBV survivors. • Where referrals are unavailable CIP will set up referral in the social welfare department (SWD) & include representatives from police, district bar association, doctor/psychiatrist/clinical psychologist, humanitarian representatives to oversee, track progress and support GBV survivors. The referral will work in close coordination with the health facilities, • in situations where the spread of COVID-19 is high and requires remote case management, in coordination with local organizations, doctors & counsellors, police and lawyers, CIP will develop an online or strengthen an existing referral pathway. Which will be easily accessed by women, girls/children through a helpline number, to safely report their cases for legal aid/psycho social support. • A TORs will be drafted and approved by the SWD & subsequent to this a plan of action will be developed. • All members/staff in the referral established will be trained on national and international standards as well as using the WHO/UNFPA, Government and CARE related guidelines in training on GBV, case management, and the laws passed by the government of Pakistan. The members will be linked with international resources experts will be invited online for developing the capacities and mentoring of members. Approximately 500 to 700 beneficiaries will access the service for counselling support related to COVID-19 and GBV 3) Community Awareness on GBV • Developing Community GBV Response Groups in 2 villages with 25 members to engage with communities in responding and facilitating GBV survivors, linking them with GBV services. The group will be trained on basic understanding on addressing GBV and counselling survivors. Awareness materials will be developed for the group such as handouts and videos to monitor any violations related to GBV in coordination with the community leaders/influential, referrals and health systems within their village. The group will meet twice every month to discuss progress and ensure in creating an enabling and safe environment for survivors to report their cases or access GBV services.• The community groups will mobilize & facilitate conducting awareness sessions on available GBV services with men, women, adolescent girls and boys such as referrals, psycho social support, helplines to report cases of GBV, through in person or door to door sessions strictly observing COVID SOPs. Additionally community will identify & discuss challenging harmful gender norms that reinforce gender inequalities & GBV

2 - Project Indicators

Project Indicators • The number of health facilities or centers upgraded and providing GBV services to survivors/women, girls/children and PWDs• The number of training/sessions conducted with GBV service providers• The number of women, girls/children and PWDs seeking assistance in the health system• The number of referrals established and running effectively• The number and type of GBV cases registered through health centers/referrals• The number of community response groups established and reaching out to communities
