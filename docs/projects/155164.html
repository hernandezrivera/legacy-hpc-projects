Project Summary

This project addresses the identified need to increase and improve response to at-risk men and boys and male survivors of sexual violence and engaging them in different PSS activities. In addition, this project reduces the gap in access to quality PSS services and includes activities to engage women, girls and children in the women in child friendly spaces. In the WFS skill building activities to support women and girls with income-generating activities will be focused on. The opportunity to have access to IGA through the women and child friendly spaces will enable women and adolescent girls to get opportunity to earn without facing any cultural and social barriers and enabling them to make contributions and decisions in the household level at the same time, it is hard for PwSNs to have access to different type of services available in the camp and to get information about the specialised services available in the camp for them. Hence CAID continues to support and improve existing inclusive referral systems which are linked with BDC, CDC as part of the SM programming, which reaches individuals and addresses their specific
Protection and vulnerability aspects worsen during monsoon season women are at higher risk of the impact of cyclones, access becomes difficult, WFS and CFS are likely to be affected and not operational, reducing the possibility to receive support and to engage women in empowerment and capacity building activities such as skill development. Thus, some of the GBV service points, community centres might be affected by flood and landslides and need to be identified and fortified to keep them accessible and operational, which will be done under the SM activities under IOM funded project. CAID protection will link up with the SM team in camp 14 and 15 to ensure these aspects are not overlooked. During cyclone preparedness plan, contingency planning community infrastructures are listed that need to be fortified, protection aspects are included in this process and protection specialist present during the Cyclone Preparedness Working Group.
Furthermore, also there is a need to have provisions of host-community level protection-related structural support and inclusive programs targeted for host community population based on Community risk assessments.
Key protection activities comprise of the establishment of male and adolescent boy's centres to engage men with awareness sessions on human rights and GBV, PSS and behavioural changing activities, support the operation and running costs of WCGS and the establishment of new WCGS, improvement of existing referral services for persons with specific needs (women, men, boys, girls, PwDs, elderly people and people with vulnerabilities). Continuation of a comprehensive complaints and feedback system to raise community concerns.
In addition, also various community engagement activities with gender inclusive protection messaging, awareness on human rights and GBV will be conducted and awareness raising sessions for community leaders (e.g. Majhis, imams and informal women leaders)
In total 86,165 people will be reached by this approach.

Needs

There are 49359 poeple in camp-15 and 31,306 poeple in camp-14. According to the 4W report as of 13th November, 2018, PSS support facility and awareness to male and adolescent boys on reducing GBV is inadequate in camp-14 and 15 as there is a need to increase and improve response to at-risk men and boys and male survivors of sexual violence and engaging them in PSS activities. There is also a gap in access to quality PSS services and engaging activities for women, girls and children in the women and child friendly spaces along with skill building activities to support women and girls with income-generating activities. A 2015 gender analysis study, which included a focused group discussion and key informant interviews among the 3,000 Rohingya refugees who were then living in Cox’s Bazar’s official refugee camps, revealed that 94 per cent of women respondents reported that they did not make decisions about their current marriage, and that 45 per cent were married as children. Ninety-five per cent of them said that the main role of women is cooking; 53 per cent believed that women should not be allowed to leave the house (42 per cent of surveyed women reported spending an average of 21 to 24 hours a day inside their house). These factors have had tremendous effects on women’s and girls’ mobility, leadership, decision-making and access to control. So, it is important to have establishments like WCFS for women, girls & children. Access to IGI is also scarce, so the opportunity to have access to IGI through the WCFS will enable women and girls to increase their capacity and resource. at the same time, it is hard for PwSNs to have access to different type of services available in the camp and to get information about the specialised services available. An inclusive referral system, linked with BDC, CDC as part of the SM programming reaches to individuals to address their specific needs with an intersectional approach is a big gap in both camps. In addition, 52 community kitchens and WCFS of CAID each have 1 trained dedicated volunteer on protection, able to detect cases and refer it to the existing accountability systems.It is well known that during monsoon season women are at higher risk of the impact of cyclones, it is expected this will lead to an increase of GBV risks for refugees, because normal checks and balances systems do not exist such as in the host communities where DRM committees are active for decades. CAID acknowledges this gap and constrain and hence mainstreams protection into all programming; during training sessions, ToT of field staff and block and camp level awareness raising campaigns on evacuation, fire drills, and it is embedded it into risk assessment action plan (RRAP) and cyclone preparedness plan at camp level. All frontline staffs are sensitized on protection aspects, can detect cases and bring those forward to the relevant existing accountability systems, linked with protection referrals.In addition, GBV service points, community centres might be affected by flood and landslides and need to be identified and fortified to keep them accessible and functional which would be done by CA protection by linking up with SM team in both camps. During CPP, contingency planning community infrastructures are listed that need to be fortified, protection aspects are included in this process.Furthermore, there is a need to have provisions of host-community level protection-related structural support and inclusive programs targeted for host community population based on CRA. A recent Rapid gender analysis led by OXFAM shows that on average, women perform 70 hours of care work a week and men do 11 hours, with firewood and water collection being the most difficult tasks in host communities. It is important that they be aware about human rights and protection and GBV. So, mass awareness and community engaging activities based on local cultural context are much required for the people of local host community.

Activities or outputs

1. Establish 10 (5 in each camp) male and adolescent boy's centres to engage men with awareness sessions on human rights and GBV, PSS and behavioural changing activities. 2. Maintain existing 8 WCGS in camp-15 and establishing 8 new WCGS in camp-14 with PSS services, awareness session for women and girls on human rights and GBV, art therapy and skill building activities e.g. tailoring, handicrafts and kitchen garden to address protection, psychosocial and livelihood needs 3. Improve existing referral services for persons with specific needs (women, men, boys, girls, PwDs, elderly people and people with vulnerabilities) for improved access to dignified services in collaboration with ISCG GBV sub-sector, UNHCR protection sector, expert organizations to support PwDs and elderly people. A comprehensive complaints and feedback system currently exists in the camp (Complaints, box, help desk, voice recorders and reports directly made to staff) and this is utilized the community to raise concerns. Focus group discussions will be conducted with the women and girls in the Women Friendly spaces and Community kitchens to identify community concerns and voice recorders will also be availed in these spaces at regular intervals to collect feedback from women and girls. Protection is linked with the SM activities that are operational and will be fortified. Feedback received will be analysed and common trends identified monthly. This forms the base to improve programming, specific sensitization addressing the needs of women. 4. Multisectoral protection needs assessment in working areas of host community, and design gender based and awareness programming based on it. (existing staff community – volunteer respond – focal point, awareness raising sessions with women and will link up with protection case workers)The NA includes aspects of DRR: it depicts the risks during disasters, the vulnerabilities and coping capacities of most vulnerable groups during disasters. CAID includes addresses protection aspects into RRAP and Cyclone preparedness working group and refer these to protection actors present at camp level. During cyclones children, disabled and elderly are identified and prioritized in terms of relocation, family reunification and care and first aid provided at community shelters. Protection is mainstreamed and part of the assessment, action plans and measures making the existing and newly established infrastructure accessible for women, girls, such as gender segregation during disasters, awareness raising programming, training volunteers, develop and roll out of fire and safety training for women – CAID has 52 existing community kitchens, where fire drills and awareness of safety aspects are included. Cyclone preparedness committee members are trained on specific protection risks during emergencies. 3. Community engagement activities with gender inclusive protection messaging, awareness on human rights and GBV e.g. street drama, songs, pre-recorded radio shows, observing days and GBV campaigns, pictorial IEC materials in camp-15, 14 and adjacent host communities. During the community engagement activities CAID incorporate standard PSEA messages to ensure that communities are aware that aid is free, what their rights are and to understand their vulnerability towards exploitation, especially in duress. Information on existing feedback mechanism and referral pathways will be shared with communities, so that they are aware of where to receive support. 6. provide awareness sessions to community leaders (e.g. Majhis, imams and informal women leaders)The sessions for men will be led by male members of the protection team, to combat the issues of cultural barriers to ensure maximum participation. The ones for females will be led by female staff of CAID. Protection and GBV issues will be featured, in courtyard session, making it as participatory as possible.

Key project/performance indicators

1. number of men and boys with improved awareness on human rights and GBV (3000 men and 3000 adolescent boys for awareness and engaging activities of mae and boy's centers 2. 500 community leaders and informal women leaders (250 men and 250 women) 2. number of women, girls and children with improved access to PSS and awareness on human rights and GBV (2700 women, 2700 girls and 5400 children to be provided with PSS) 3. number of people provided with referral services (1800 women, men, girls, boys, PSNs) 4. number of needs assessment conducted - 15. number of people reached through community engagment activities - 15000 (7000 people targetted in each camp, and 1000 in host communities)
