End Date

Project Summary

Project Objective:
Is to enhance the income of vulnerable and poor female and male farmers in the most affected areas of the Gaza Strip.
Project Summary :
The project is mainly assisting vulnerable and poor female and male farmers by providing them with one or more of the following services:
* Rehabilitation of open lands
* Rehabilitation of existing water wells run by electricity or fuel
* Installation of solar systems for agricultural water wells
* Rehabilitation of existing water ponds
* Rehabilitation of greenhouses
The project activities will be implemented all over the Gaza strip. NDC will partner with two local registered cluster NGOs who have extensive agricultural experience to implement the project. In addition, the project activities will be completely coordinated with the Ministry of Agriculture and the Food Security Sector.
The estimated number of direct beneﬁciaries is 1075 farmers (At least 175 female farmers and 900 males' headed household) while the total number of the farmers and their families is 5912. The estimated total budget for the project is $ 2,320,400.
The project's objectives match the HRP 2021 oPt, Strategic Objective country #( 3): The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
Furthermore, the project is speciﬁcally in line with the Food Security Sector (FSS) objective
#(1) The livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced.

1 - Needs

Two million Gazans under Israeli blockade, electricity crisis and the unstable political and security situation continue to prevent access to livelihood opportunities and limit the free movement of people, goods, and services. The situation became more difficult with the spread of COVID-19 pandemic in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip and the deterioration of the state of emergency and the frequent lockdown. Furthermore, the PA decision to stop accepting clearness revenues that Israel collects had also increased the deterioration. As a result of these factors around 72% of households are severely or moderately food insecure (HNO2021), moreover, the rate of unemployment increased and exceeded 49% in second quarter of 2020 (PCBS 2020). The World Bank forecasts that by the end of 2020 the poverty rate in Gaza will increase from 53% to 64%, or equivalent to 37,300 new poor families. According to OCHA COVID-19 Emergency Situation Report number 19 “Agriculture and livelihoods continue to be severely affected in the Gaza Strip: while traders had inputs in stock, these remained partially inaccessible due to movement restrictions. Farmers and breeders are buying on credit, and traders suffer from insufficient financial liquidity, placing them at risk of losing their ability to import agricultural inputs.” Farmers in border areas Access Restricted Areas (ARA) are still suffering from limited access to their lands to cultivate. According to the Ministry of Agriculture there are still 20% of arable lands still not planted and farmers suffering from diﬃculties of practicing agriculture leaving in (ARA). NDC is partnering with two NGOs operating within the food security sector. These NGOs have extensive experience in implementing agricultural projects including rehabilitation of damaged lands, water wells, greenhouses rehabilitation, water ponds, and installing solar systems for water wells, which are all vital components towards building resilience against food insecurity in the Gaza context. During October 2020, ten focus groups (two in each governorate: one for female famers and the other for male farmers) were conducted by the two local NGOs in the five governorates of Gaza Strip taking into consideration all the required health precautions related to Covid-19. The objectives of these focus groups were to discuss the main agricultural demands and stand on the top priorities to help developing the agricultural sector and improving the farmer's lives. Two focus groups were conducted in the north Gaza and attended by 19 persons (11 males and 8 females). Two focus group were conducted in Gaza area attended by 22 persons (13 males and 9 females). Additional two focus group were conducted in the middle area of the Gaza Strip and attended by 17 persons (10 males and 7 females). Two focus groups were conducted in Khanyounis and attended by 15 persons (8 males and 7 females). Two focus groups were conducted in Rafah and attended by 25 persons (13 males and 12 females). In total, the 10 focus groups were attended by 98 persons (55 males and 43 ). Besides, the ages of participants in the focus group's were between 22 to over 65 years old and with different marital status. During the needs assessment, farmers, both men; and women indicated that open land rehabilitation, greenhouses rehabilitation, water wells rehabilitation, and rehabilitation of existing water ponds are the top priority. All the farmers indicated that utilizing solar systems to irrigate their lands is the highest priority due to the severe lack of electricity and the high costs of fuel. Solar systems will provide them with water at very low cost. Upon analyzing the results of these workshops, it became evident that, in addition to the previously mentioned needs, 92% of the participants in those focus groups confirmed the increase in their debts due to agricultural work, and 85% of them emphasized the high costs of agricultural inputs, as well as maintenance and restoration work for agricultural wells, water ponds and greenhouses. While 90% of them emphasized the high prices of irrigation water, the lack of suitable water for some agricultural crops, and the low sales prices of agricultural products were equally emphasized. Due to the nature of these activities, there was no signiﬁcant difference in needs between male and female farmers and also the same also applies to the different age groups. Furthermore, NDC discussed the needs with the Ministry of Agriculture and FAO. Both parties confirmed the results of the focus groups. In particular, FAO indicated that there is a real need to convert water wells that operate on electricity or fuel to solar energy. The need is big and all efforts in this field is welcomed. The project will mainly target farmers, who are smallholder farmers with minimum assets, and little or no access to livelihood enhancing methods. These farmers will be selected through a consultative process undertaken by the partner NGOs. The process will include gap analysis to identify indicators of food insecure or at risk of food-insecure HHs that are excluded from the socio-economic development process on grounds such as locality, poverty, and gender. The selection is further based on a community gap analysis, involving village councils, cooperatives, HHs who lost their source of income, small farmers, livestock breeders, and women-headed HHs. Besides, farmers who are suffering from severe electricity cuts and facing considerable diﬃculties in running their water wells and pumping systems and thus are not able to irrigate their ﬁelds will be also considered.

2 - Activities

This project is designed in a participatory manner taking into consideration the interest of the project's stakeholders. Beneﬁciaries' dignity, independence, contribution to food security are achievable through the project's activities. Selection criteria based on the identiﬁed needs, gender equity and sensitivity without any bias; in addition the project will be based on the Do No Harm framework with a gender transformative approach to empower female farmers in the Gaza Strip. All targeted households will be selected according to protection-related, and socio-economic vulnerability criteria. Furthermore, 17 protection awareness workshops will be conducted all over the Gaza Strip. The protection section includes some details about these workshops.The project is highly coordinated with MoA and the FSS during the design process. COVID-19 health precautions has been taken into consideration in the design phase. It will be also taken into consideration during the implementation and the monitoring and evaluation phases. Furthermore, gender, protection, and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) are taken into consideration in the needs assessment, design, implementation, and monitoring evaluation phases (more details are available in the relevant sections within this proposal). The project has five main activities: * Rehabilitation of open lands (1,250 dunums distributed equally all over Gaza Strip)* Rehabilitation of existing water wells run by electricity or fuel (10 water wells 7 in Gaza and 3 in Khanyounis)* Installation of solar systems for agricultural water wells (15 water wells will be converted to solar system; some of these wells will be selected from the previous activity if there is a real and clear need)* Rehabilitation of existing water ponds (30 in North Gaza, 30 in Khanyounis and 30 in Rafah)* Rehabilitation of greenhouses (150 green house distributed equally in north Gaza, Gaza city, and middle area of the Gaza Strip: 50 greenhouses in each one of the three governorate). The project targets three main categories. The ﬁrst is the affected farmers in border areas Access Restricted Areas (ARA) who are still suffering from limited access to their lands to cultivate. The second is male and female farmers whose agricultural assets such as open ﬁelds, agricultural water wells, water bonds are in need and/or damaged in the last few years due to the current political situation. The third category is farmers who are suffering from the sever electricity cuts and facing considerable diﬃculties in running their water wells and pumping systems & thus are not able to effectively irrigate their ﬁelds. It is expected that 1075 (At least 175 female farmers and 900 males' headed household) will directly beneﬁt from the project's activities. Their immediate families’ members with 5912 persons will beneﬁt also. At the start of the project, local advertisement will be published in each of the ﬁve governorates to ask farmers to submit applications to beneﬁt from the project based on predetermined general criteria such as being small farmers; own less than 5 dunums; and located in the target areas. More speciﬁc criteria will be developed for water wells rehabilitation and solar system installation such as licensed water wells, irrigating at least 20 dunums, and located in a safe areas. The advertisement will be published in mosques, local NGOs premises, local committees locations, etc. Five selection committees from NDC and its NGOs partners, local committees’ members, local ﬁgures will be formed to select the best applications that meet the criteria. NDC will encourage women to apply to benefit from the project activities. NDC will announce that women beneficiaries will get additional marks when evaluating the needs and selecting the beneficiaries. Also NDC and their partners will coordinate with local committees and MoA to ensure that the selected 175 female farmers having all supporting documents proving ownership of the targeted agricultural facilities. and legal opinion will be consulted to ensure minimum risks on the implementation is likely to happen. Final lists will be developed and passed to the MoA to get their feedback on the selected beneﬁciaries. Based on the comments of the MoA, a ﬁnal list of beneﬁciaries will be developed. NDC will be responsible for the overall management of the project activities, procurement, ﬁnancial disbursement, monitoring and evaluation, and reporting. Two local NGOs partners will be responsible for implementing the project activities on the ground, form the ﬁve local committees, ﬁnalize the beneﬁciary list in coordination with MoA, and implement the project activities on the ground. In implementing the project activities, NDC will coordinate with the MoA, FAO, FSS, & INGOs. Coordination with NGOs, local committees, municipalities, and other governmental bodies will be the role of the two partners. To ensure the completion of all project activities within 12 months, the activities will be implemented by forming various work groups distributed in each area and according to the required intervention. Moreover, the project activities will be implemented in an overlapping manner.

3 - Indicators

Outcome: The capacity of agricultural assets of 1075 farmers vulnerable in the Gaza Strip (At least 175 female farmers and 900 males' headed household farmers) to restore livelihood and the agricultural assets became more effective. 1. # of dunums rehabilitated in agricultural production (Target: 1,250) for 625 Beneﬁciaries including 500 males and 125 females 2. # of water wells rehabilitated (Target: Ten [10]) for 60 farmers at least 10 female farmers and 50 male farmers. 3. # of water wells installed solar systems (Target: Fifteen [15] with 375 KW output) for 150 farmers at least 20 female farmers and 130 male farmers 4. # of water ponds rehabilitated (Target: 90 ponds, 9000m3) for 90 farmers at least 10 female farmers and 80 male farmers. 5. # of greenhouses rehabilitated (target: 150 greenhouse) for 150 farmers at least 10 female farmers and 140 male farmers. To ensure good implementation, monitoring, and evaluation, NDC's M&E Monitoring and Evaluation Oﬃcer will ensure that the protection standards are fully respected and provides guidance, tools, and methodologies including on gender analysis and the assessment of gender outcomes. Due to the spread of Covid19 in the Gaza Strip, NDC and its partners will do their best to ensure that Ministry of Health directions are fully followed. For example, in the protection awareness workshops, NDC will only allow limited number of participants to ensure social distancing. Moreover, each participant should wear a mask. NDC and its partners will also provide detergents, masks, etc. Ministry of Health directions will be strictly followed by NDC and its two partners throughout implementing the project’s activities. Additionally, a compliant response mechanism will be put in place to ensure that feedback and complaints from beneﬁciaries are reported conﬁdentially. NDC management will ensure that these complaints are immediately investigated and addressed in the most appropriate manner. In addition, NDC will implement its well- developed system of Community Score Cards (the system was developed with the support of the World Bank) to ensure the quality of service provided to beneﬁciaries. NDC will implement 15 sessions during the project implementation (three sessions in every governorate). The ﬁrst session will be implemented at the start of the project, the second session will be conducted in the middle of the implementation period, while the third session will be conducted towards the end of the project. The results will show how the beneﬁciaries assess the quality of the service provided to them. If the quality of the service is not as expected, the Project Manager will immediately take corrective actions.Furthermore, a beneﬁciary satisfaction questionnaire will be distributed to selected representative sample of beneﬁciaries to assess their satisfaction with the project activities. The ﬁndings of the survey along with the results of the community scorecard sessions will be used as learning tools to improve project implementation and the realization of objectives and indicators. Furthermore, 5 local committees will be formed and will participate in the implementation of the project activities, monitoring and evaluation.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

NDC will ensure the dignity and safety of the targeted farmers by conducting the following four steps: 1. Before implementing the project activities: NDC and its two partners NGOs will conduct 20 awareness raising workshop (four in each governates) about the project objectives, outputs, and activities. This to ensure that all the farmers are fully aware of the project and if any of them thinks or believes he will be negatively affected he/she will have the chance to inform NDC and its partners NGOs of his/her concerns. Consequently, NDC and its partners NGOs will immediately take into account this farmer concerns and will solve all issues that might negatively affect him/her. 2. Beneﬁciaries selection: Beneﬁciaries will be selected on standardized social and technical vulnerability selection criteria consistent with humanitarian principles and practices and through gender-appropriate activities 3. Throughout the implementation of the project: NDC will conduct 3 community scorecard sessions in each governorate: one session at the start of the project, a second in the middle of implementation, and the third towards the end of the project. In total 15 scorecard sessions will be implemented. These community scorecard sessions will ensure that no harm is done to any member of the targeted community and will also work on improving the quality of services provided by the project. 4. Complaints mechanism: NDC has an active complaints system that will be also available to the local communities to use if any harm has been done. NDC will take corrective actions immediately. Furthermore, to avoid any sexual exploitation and/or abuse, NDC will make sure that all necessary measures are well taken through the project design, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation of the project such as staff training, enforce already PSEA NDC's established procedures, conduct awareness workshops for beneficiaries, etc. In addition, at the start of the implementation of the activities, one-day workshop for the selected beneficiaries, especially women, about protection will be implemented in the 5 governorates of the Gaza Strip. The workshop will be repeated 3 times in each governorate and 5 times in Gaza (in total 17 workshops will be implemented all over the Gaza Strip). The aim of the workshop is to educate the beneficiaries about protection guidelines and concepts, and how to deal with any abuse or harm that they might face. Furthermore, the workshops will inform the selected beneficiaries of the grievance/complaints system that is available at NDC and how to use it if they were subject to any abuse or harm.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project activities will be published and communicated in the targeted areas. Adds will be placed in governmental departments, public entities, NGOs facilities, mosques, supermarkets, etc. Furthermore, ﬁve local committees will be formed in the target communities. One of their roles is to communicate the project activities to the local communities and ensure that target beneﬁciaries have equal and impartial access to these activities. In addition, NDC and its two partners NGOs will conduct 20 workshops (four in each governates) in the presence of local community representatives, including women association, key persons, and farmers of both sexes to discuss the project objectives and activities with the targeted communities. Also, an active complaints mechanisms will be operating so that any beneﬁciary who has any problem can use this complaints system, Additional priority in the selection will be given to the female-headed household as well as people with disabilities to empower them and strengthen their ability to face their challenges. All the project activities will be coordinated with the Ministry of Agriculture and the FSS and hence ensure equal and impartial access to the project activities.
