Project Summary

Unreliable access to a temporary and/or inadequate WASH services and facilities remains an important feature of the humanitarian landscape in Somalia. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021, about 4.6 million people are in need of humanitarian WASH support in Somalia. A cholera outbreak fueled by limited access to safe drinking water, poor sanitation and displacement has already been reported in some of the flood affected districts in 2021, underscoring the need for urgent repair and rehabilitation to damaged WASH infrastructure and the provision of clean water. More cases of AWD are being reported in children under five years old, who are particularly vulnerable to disease, especially if their immune systems are compromised by malnutrition.
HAPEN in the proposed intervention will work towards improving access to water, improved hygiene practices and sanitation facilities to the affected people from Gedo and Lower Juba. This will be achieved by repairing water wells, which are the main water sources in the targeted locations, construction of gender sensitive VIP latrines and handing-washing facilities as well as conducting hygiene promotion campaigns. The project will aim at increasing timely and equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene services to 11,004 individuals from 1,834 HHs (1,200 IDPs HHs & 634 HC HHs), including 4,292 women (39%), 3,631 men (33%), 1,541 girls (14%) and 1,540 boys(14%) IDPs and host communities. Among the 11,004, 15% of them (1,651) will be person living with disabilities.
The proposed project activities fall in line with the WASH cluster identiﬁed objectives. The key activities will be the rehabilitation of 15 water wells, construction of 350 gender segregated VIP latrines with 10% of them made accessible to PWD, installation of 350 hand washing facilities, conducting hygiene promotion campaign and distribution of 1,834 hygiene kits.
The project which is in line with the 6th goal of the Sustainable Development Goals is anticipated to help achieving dignified and safe access to drinkable water needed as part of the life basic needs. Also, it will remarkably result in reduction of the water-borne diseases and COVID-19 spread through improving the sanitation services for the targeted communities and providing them with adequate quality water safe for drinking, cooking, and maintaining personal hygiene. In the same contest, this project will contribute to the reduction of the diarrhea–related morbidity and mortality rates among the targeted population through the provision of clean water that can help minimizing the exposure to water- borne diseases and managing the cases of dehydration resulting from the diarrhea itself. Furthermore, the proposed intervention will potentially result in a substantial reduction of the GBV incidents particularly among the girls and young women within the targeted communities as they will have improved access to more nearby sources of drinkable water and also will have the VIP latrines set in closer locations to their residences.
This project will mainstream gender and ensure that the needs of women, men, girls and boys are taken into account from the initial consultations on the needs to the design, construction/rehabilitation and use of water and sanitation facilities as well as provision of hygiene services. At the critical decision making level, the needs of women, girls, boys and persons living with disability will be taken into serious consideration. Enhanced community mobilization will be undertaken to involve all the community in the project area.
Accountability to the affected population will be ensured by providing the target beneficiaries with a platform of airing their views & perception with regards to the project. This will be achieved through rolling out a comprehensive feedback, complaints & response mechanism which will be easily accessible to all beneficiaries.

1. - Projects activities

• Construction of 350 ventilated improved pit (VIP) latrines to serve 7,200 IDPs and 3,804 individuals from the HC. The latrines will be gender segregated with 10% of them made accessible to PWD while they will be strategically located in areas that promotes dignity and safety of the vulnerable groups such us women, girls and children.• Installation of 350 handwashing facilities. These will be erected in close proximity of the latrines to promote good hygiene practices.• Rehabilitation of 15 water wells approximated to serve the targeted 11,004 individuals both IDPs and HC. The project will seek to build the capacity of the locals on how they can repair and maintain these wells for longer-term benefits. • Hygiene awareness campaigns which will be proceeded by training of trainers. The TOT will be expected to carry out monthly awareness sessions within the IDP camps, villages and in the nearby schools to promote good hygiene practices amongst the community. The campaigns will also focus on awareness raising on containing the spread of COVID-19 pandemic. • Distribution of 1,834 hygiene kits to the targeted 1,834 families from both the IDPs and HC.• Establishment of an effective Feedback, Complaint and Response mechanism.• Conducting monitoring and evaluation of the activities to assess the appropriateness, effectiveness and efficiency of the intervention.
