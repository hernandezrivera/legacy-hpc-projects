Project Summary

After more than a decade of conflict and economic decline, the humanitarian crisis in Northern Syria persists, affecting all sectors and population groups. In 2024, an estimated 16.7 million people across Syria require humanitarian assistance, up from 15.3 million in 2023, with 5.5 million displaced individuals. Escalating prices, unaffordable services, and limited job opportunities have pushed over 90% of households to resort to negative coping strategies. This crisis disproportionately affects the most vulnerable, including those with disabilities, exacerbating social cohesion strains. Emotional distress is prevalent, with 56% of households reporting signs of distress in men and 52% in women. Extensive damage to property and infrastructure, along with deterred investments, hampers sustainable economic recovery. In Northern Syria, 19-20% of households report signs of distress in girls and boys.
The proposed project focuses on improving the protection of the population affected by the crisis and the protection environment of both HCs and IDPs in 8 communities in Idleb (Maaret Tamsrin, Dana, Qourqeena), This will be achieved through the provision of quality and integrated protection services, including awareness-raising, psychosocial assistance, cash assistance, and targeted support to vulnerable groups.
GAN aim to target of 25000 individuals, six mobile teams will be established to conduct awareness-raising sessions on protection risks. Also, these teams, comprised of CP officers and outreach workers, will identify vulnerable cases, record them throughout the project, and provide monthly cash grants for three months 700 families (3500 individuals), valued at $145 or equivalent TL, will empower beneficiaries to meet their needs autonomously and with dignity.
Where most vulnerable cases will be identified and registered by the mobile team during the provision of awareness sessions in the communities throughout the project period, after that, the field team will verify the applications through another field visit to the beneficiaries at their place of residence
Money transfer agents will be selected through a competitive process to distribute cash grants, utilizing e-cards instead of paper to streamline administrative processes.
Additionally, CP case management services and PSS for children and adolescents will be provided within the equipped CFS in the schools previously supported by GAN (6 CFS)
Parenting programs will also be offered, for caregivers.
The project will strengthen the capacity of teachers in schools to provide quality protection services, by providing them with training on Child Safeguarding, Psychological First Aid, and Safe identification and referral.
Community and beneficiary participation will be ensured through various channels for feedback and complaints, including dedicated phone numbers, complaint boxes, email, and social media platforms. A comprehensive monitoring plan will track all project activities, with regular data collection on baseline, end-line, and beneficiary satisfaction to ensure effectiveness and accountability.
Protection Risk Analysis:
R: Exploitation or abuse risks among staff/service providers
M: Regular, clear communication about available services, emphasizing that humanitarian aid is free and encouraging reporting through a complaints mechanism. Provide training on the Code of Conduct and (PSEA).
R: Family tension leading to violence or disruption
M: Implement selection criteria based on vulnerabilities, prioritize holistic family-based approaches, and ensure comparable services for all family members. Conduct periodic staff refresher sessions, establish standard operating procedures, and prepare for emergencies.
R: Low participation of girls due to community resistance
M: Engage communities from project inception, conduct gender analysis, involve religious and community leaders, and target adolescent girls with socially acceptable activities like life skills training.

PSEA strategy:GAN is a member of the PSEA network, attending a monthly meeting and following the guidelines, so to mainstream the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse in the project, GAN has developed a policy stating standards of conduct, including acts of SEA, and a work plan to implement the policy in place, staff members will sign the policy and received systematized and refreshed training on PSEA. GAN Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies are in place, practiced and monitored. Polices cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. The policies address risks at the organizational level and the program level. GAN will provide awareness sessions about the organization’s policies and procedures. GSN ’s staff and targeted communities will have access to safe and effective reporting systems including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report anonymously. Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures Assumption: Access to Syria remains possible and safe through the neighboring countries the good security situation in all targeted areas, and the Closure of the borders between Turkey and Syria 1-Risk of money transferMitigation: GAN has a prior fundamental condition for money transfer agents: the payment is conducted after distribution 2-Risk of shelling by Government of Syrian (GoS) forces and its allies in all or some targeted locations and/or infighting between different armed groups continue causing loss of access to the targeted locations Mitigation: GAN will assess several options like the temporary suspension of activities until the areas become more secure again,3-Risk heavy shelling by the Government of Syrian forces and its allies for a long time (months) or the target locations become under its control Mitigation: Shifting program locations to more accessible areas in coordination with the Protection cluster and others.
