Project Summary

With this project NRC will ensure that most vulnerable displacement affected populations living in camps, camp-like, out-of-camp and urban settings will have access to safe drinking water and sanitation facilities and will be supported to achieve positive behavioural changes through effective hygiene promotion campaigns.
NRC WASH programmes will offer a progressive and phased response approach with a focus towards longer-term goals and durable solutions. Where appropriate, upgrading/expansion of existing water infrastructures will be promoted, along with the development of sustainability plans. Market-based solutions for sanitation and hygiene items will be the preferred option. WASH programme will target primarily displaced population in neglected, hard-to-reach locations in Khartoum, White Nile, South Kordofan and Gadaref.
The WASH objectives will be achieved through an integrated approach with Shelter, LFS, ICLA, Education and CM programmes.
Emergency Response:
Newly displaced populations will be targeted with WASH response to enhance their access to safe drinking water and sanitation through household level interventions. For the water component, the programme will focus on the Water Safety Plan from the distribution points up to the Point of Use, including proper water collection, handling, treatment and storage (adequate water containers and household level water treatment systems complemented by relevant awareness sessions). The programme will be complemented by family-shared emergency latrines and vector control interventions to minimize high-risk practices and reduce faecal and vector-borne diseases transmission, contributing to improve the health and dignity of the affected communities.
Longer-term goals and Durable Solutions:
The populations living in protracted displacement or returned to areas of origin will be primarily targeted with upgrading/expansion of small-scale water infrastructures, benefitting both the displaced and the hosting communities with longer-term impact. The interventions will aim at increasing the water supply and improving the water quality and in parallel will reduce potential social conflicts generated by the sharing of already scarce natural resources. The sanitation response will primarily promote the CLTS approach in alignment with the Sudanese WASH sector strategy. Sustainability plans, engaging local water authorities and target communities on the facilities’ Operation & Maintenance through the creation Water and Sanitation Users Committees will strengthen the response. To complement the communal/infrastructural components, market-based activities will be promoted to meet effectively people’s WASH needs and contribute towards market rehabilitation and community resilience.
Integrated (multi-sectoral) Response:
The WASH response will be highly integrated to the other Core Competency programmes to ensure longer-term goals and greater impact. In particular, synergies will be ensured with ICLA on HLP issues and community engagement around durable solutions; Shelter to strengthen the overall scope of creating home and building communities; Education on supporting WASH in School programming and contribute to create safe learning environments; LFS to support agricultural and market-based livelihood programmes; CM on Communication with Communities for greater engagement of the target communities on both household and communal level response.

1 - Needs

According to the HRP released in July 2020, in Sudan 9,8m of people – 24 per cent of the total population - are in need of humanitarian assistance. Of them, 7,6m people require essential water, sanitation and hygiene services. Across the country, only 74 percent of the population has access to drinking water, 39 percent to limited sanitation services, and 23 percent to basic hygiene services. The most affected population groups are IDPs, returnees, non-displaced host communities and refugees. In particular, 1.7m IDPs, 0,9m refugees and 291,000 returnees are in dire need of WASH support, almost 30 percent of the total people in need. Amongst the population groups, in Sudan people with specific needs including unaccompanied and separated children, survivors of gender-based violence (GBV), women-headed households, elderly persons, persons with disabilities and people with specific health concerns represent a significant portion. Overall, 60% of the People in Need in the WASH sector are boys and girls below 17 years. Access to WASH in school and primary health centres is limited. Approximately 30% of schools do not have any functional latrine and fewer than 50% of schoolchildren have access to soap and water for handwashing. This is further compounded by the lack of sanitation facilities for physically disabled children and gender segregated. This negatively impacts children’s attendance and enrolment, especially for girls. In this critical context, the COVID19 pandemic has significantly increased WASH needs at school and consequently the humanitarian assistance required to ensure that children can study in safe learning environments (handwashing stations, latrines and long-term sustainable solutions for the provision of safe water). As part of the peace process initiated by the Transitional Government, the peace talks ongoing in Juba should contribute the internally displaced people to achieve durable solutions; in particular, it is expected that many thousand of IDP families in South Kordofan and Darfur will leave their temporary settlements and return to places of origin, or to ‘normalise’ their current informal settlements. Both processes will require substantial humanitarian assistance through integrated programming, including WASH long-term solutions.
