Project Summary

JNER is a common appeal by 13 WaSH NGOs, constituting the majority of non-UN active sector partners. Modalities are adapted from crisis-sensitive components of the HawkaMaa-EU development project consortium. Coordination at the sector level was led/facilitated by the coordination team, which encouraged collective action, and which is also invited to support the implementation of the JNER.
The ERP acknowledges that public services are “overstretched and underdeveloped due to years of under- investment” and adopts a “system approach” to maximize reach and cost effectiveness while minimizing inter/intra-community (including host-refugee) tensions and adverse effects of parallel services on public utilities. JNER reinforces this approach by tackling the two main components of water service provision, production and distribution, across three categories of localities/communities; those served by Water Establishments (WE), others by municipalities, and those unserved by public systems. It assumes that UNICEF, as sector lead and owing to its capacity will provide the necessary consumables (notably fuel and chlorine) and maintenance required for continued operation of priority pumping stations.
Recognizing that water production does not guarantee its effective and equitable delivery due to blockages, bursts, pressure imbalances, and faulty operation fittings and regimes within networks and exclusion of households and communities from coverage altogether, JNER will primarily tackle distribution problems to ensure the minimum standard of safe water (35 l/c/d) is received by the most vulnerable communities and key outbreak-prone institutions. This entails addressing urgent maintenance requirements of WE and municipal networks. Unserved communities/households will be served through temporary water trucking or private pumping systems. Against referrals from the Health and Food Security sectors, JNER will implement a rolling Hygiene Kit distribution activity. Also, it’s relevant to mention that in line with the LHF strategy, ACF, GVC and DPNA will provide 2824 WASH kits to mitigate hygiene risks for a duration ranging between 6 to 8 months in Akkar, South, and Bekaa benefiting approximately 13,500 individuals.
Participation will be subject to partners’ proven access, capacity, and readiness to implement timely interventions. This will be enhanced by inviting them to participate in joint national tenders soon to be launched by WW-GVC under HawkaMaa-EU and establishing framework contracts with infrastructure contractors and suppliers. Similarly, LebRelief will lead a joint national tender for hygiene items, as well as use Hawkamaa-EU (which has dedicated funding) to provide (with ACTED and LEWAP) capacity building for local NGOs.
National coverage will be ensured through a geo-split akin to that employed within the refugee (IS) response, adopting a wider unit of WE branch/section jurisdiction to ensure compatible coordination scales in line with the vulnerability assessment adopted by the Water Sector Stabilization Thematic Group, except for Hygiene Kit distribution which is divided by District/Caza. JNER will coordinate nationally to (re)allocate funding dynamically as the understanding of vulnerability evolves with incoming analysis.
JNER will refer access challenges due to public production systems to UNICEF and offer to accompany assessments and interventions. Off-grid renewable energy for pumping stations will be exceptionally considered where feasible to help break WEs’ perpetual ‘energy crisis mode’. JNER will prioritize service recovery/improvement in collaboration with public entities by providing the necessary resources while maintaining direct oversight, reinforcing rather than undermining community trust in the public sector. This is critical to support the wider drive to reinforce public institutions, but JNER will stop short of providing them with direct support, conscious that the “ERP is strictly of humanitarian nature”.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

1.1.1 Installation/maintenance of renewable energy for pumping stations; assessing pumping stations relying exclusively on EDL without on-site backup generators nor access to alternative energy sources and determining the feasibility of installing RE systems. Install or maintain RE systems where they. 1.1.2 Transmission and distribution network urgent maintenance responding to referred or directly identified distribution system failures severely impacting services, providing maintenance to repair bursts, blockages, and faulty components, as well as connecting unserved communities/households (permanently if subscribed, temporarily if not) to networks where more cost effective than providing emergency/temporary supply. This may be done by providing resources (supplies, rented equipment, etc.) to WEs/municipalities or hiring contractors to implement directly, with direct oversight from partners in all cases. 1.1.3 Water point installation, water trucking, and private borehole activation; providing temporary supply to those not served by public systems through water trucking and/or temporary activation (repair, etc.) of private boreholes to supply households directly or central communal water points. 2.1.1 Provision of Hygiene Kits and Protective Personal Equipment; procurement of hygiene and COVID protective materials in line with sector guidelines for kits and provision through a rolling distribution exercise to most vulnerable through referral from health and food sectors.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Partners adopt a zero-tolerance approach towards sexual/child exploitation, abuse, or violence, enforced via policies in the organizations and in the program and all individuals involved with it. Staff will be trained on protection (Safe identification and referrals, safe distribution, etc.), PSEA, and proper handling of complaints. Partners will ensure relevant gender/age groups are involved in FGDs, assessments, etc. These will include gender focused questions on WASH knowledge, roles, and needs. Community consultations will also be held for women only groups, helping adapt implementation and M&E as needed. Partners will also focus on the participation of head of HHs of both genders. Additionally, partners will implement Hygiene Kit distribution focused on Menstrual Hygiene Management and SRHR awareness. PSEA will also be mainstreamed in the distributions/services provided, e.g., ensuring distributions/water trucking in daylight and public water points in central locations.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

artners assigned to serve a geographic unit will declare to the WE local office, municipalities and their unions, and other CSOs and community representatives the scope, scale, and priorities of their intervention. For the wider community, reporting urgent needs through the WE hotlines or the aforementioned representatives will be encouraged through appropriate media broadcasts, allowing for multiple referral pathways. Interventions will be published through partner, WE, and municipal media channels just ahead of commencement and within one week of completion. Partners will also conduct dedicated Third-Party Monitoring activities of each other’s interventions within the bounds of logistical feasibility. The JNER’s focus on tackling the distribution aspect of water services naturally leads to partners actively seeking the least served, most peripheral/marginalized localities, communities, and households. It forces partners to address the challenges faced by those excluded from public.
