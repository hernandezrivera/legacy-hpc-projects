Project Summary

Through the 2023 Humanitarian Response Plan, World Vision International (WVI) South Sudan will provide emergency lifesaving health interventions which will be integrated with nutrition, WASH, protection and FSL activities. The approaches shall revolve around mobile clinics in hard-to-reach areas primary healthcare and secondary healthcare at the static sites and emergency health intervention which will include sexual gender-based violence (SGBV), clinical management of rape (CMR), mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). Owing to the high mortality rate of under 5 children (90.7/1000), maternal mortality rate (789 deaths/100,000 live births) in South Sudan, World Vision will provide also maternal and child health interventions, mainly skilled birth attendance, prevention of post -partum hemorrhage, ante natal care, post-natal care, basic and comprehensive emergency neonatal and obstetrical care (EMNOC), Sexual and Reproductive Health/ family planning service and Expanded Programme Immunization (EPI) and quality management of Severe Acute Malnutrition with medical complication and community health care through the Boma Health Initiative. The Boma Health Workers (BHWs) shall be trained to provide health promotion, treatment of CU5 for the three common causes of morbity and mortality (malaria, pneumonia, diarrhoea) and identification and referral of severe cases to the nearest health facility for treatment. Additionally, World Vision will ensure that there is continuum of care for under 5 with SAM and MAM so that they have access to OTP and TSFP facilities run by nutrition partners, including World Vision who is a provider of Community Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) and Maternal Infant Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practice. World Vision will ensure that people with disabilities and mental disorders have priority access to emergency health services and are included as vulnerable groups owing to their high-risk during emergencies. The response plan will be prioritized based on epidemiological status and disease outbreaks, cholera hot spot locations, measles outbreaks, malaria seasonality. The emergency lifesaving response will be done in line with the recommended health cluster emergency clinical services package for primary healthcare units, primary healthcare centers, county hospital, mobile medical team, and Boma Health Initiative. These interventions will be in line with the South Sudan Ministry of Health’s policies and guidelines, named as the basic package of health and nutrition services, Boma Health Initiative, sexual and reproductive health, immunization guidelines and health policy of the Republic of South Sudan. This project plans to reach a total population of 100,000 (51,000F, 49,000 M), 21,000 (10,290M, 10710F) children aged below 5years and 1000 (490M, 510F) persons with disabilities with a budget of approximately USD 2,895,000 over a period of one year.

1 - Needs

South Sudan’s health system relies heavily on international humanitarian assistance and is still unable to cope and respond to humanitarian emergencies and provide essential life-saving health services. Limited health facility functionality, capacity, and poor coverage including recent closure of health facilities due to reduced funding, have made it difficult to meet health needs worsened by floods, food insecurity and frequent conflicts. Women, children, the elderly and persons with disabilities are particularly vulnerable and most frequently face limited access to healthcare. Government’s contributions to health services have been increased from 1.9 percent in 2020/2021 to 7.9 percent in 2021/2022 national budget allocations. However, actual expenditure is not in line with budget allocation. According to ISNA 2022, the main barriers to access and utilisation of care services were lack of medicines, staff and equipment transportation cost, insecurity, floods, long distance to health facility and high cost of healthcare (mentioned by host communities, IDPs, and returnees). Only 49% per cent of the population can access health facilities in one hour’s time. While 66 per cent of the population has difficulty in accessing health services, women and girls specifically faced additional barriers. Inadequate health workers, limited supplies, long waiting hours, water, maternal health services and lack of follow up care were reported. The drivers of health outcomes and needs cut across various sectors including food, nutrition, shelter, protection and water sanitation and hygiene. Mental health, PSS and interventions on sexual gender-based violence (SGBV) are integrated in health facilities both for health and protection. The destruction of WASH facilities coupled with overcrowding due to presence of IDPs, inadequate hygiene and sanitation facilities and limited access to clean drinking water contribute to poor health conditions with communities at risk of water-borne diseases such diarrheal diseases, cholera and Hepatitis E., combined with the presence of flood waters vector-borne diseases such as malaria, lymphatic filariasis, schistosomiasis is likely to increase. Elevated levels of malnutrition driven by natural disasters and aggravated by conflicts will undoubtedly result in increased cases of severe acute malnutrition Essential life-saving health care services including maternal, child, adolescent health provision through static health facilities, medical mobile teams and community health volunteers will be key priorities. Renewed focus on sub-national health delivery systems (particularly at community and county levels) and quality improvements. Disease outbreaks need to be timely detected, investigated and responded to by supporting and strengthening disease outbreak surveillance and response systems. Maintaining the core pipeline for essential medicines and supplies to respond to emergency health needs will be vital. Strengthening routine immunization and vaccination campaigns will be necessary in preventing morbidity and mortality due to vaccine preventable diseases as well as improving the COVID-19 vaccination coverage. Humanitarian Health response needs to be COVID-19, EVD and NTDs response and prevention inclusive. Technical support and coordination to provide quality humanitarian health services will be needed at all levels. In conclusion the prevention and response to sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment (PRSEAH) in health service delivery, as well as the safeguarding of health care, the supply of mental health and psychosocial support, the attacks on healthcare, and the incorporation of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) all remain among the most pressing concerns.

2 - Project activities

In 2021, World Vision supported primary, secondary and community health care services provision in Western Equatroia, Upper Nile and Warrap States. A total of 100 project and over 1700 incentive staffs were deployed to support health services across the supported counties. A total of 112 government health facilities and 4 mobile outreach teams were supported. During the period, a total of 551,609, CU5 were provided with outpatient curative consultations, 28,279 children were vaccinated and 42,183 pregnant women received ANC1 services. A total of 2,640 pregnant women were given LLINs to prevent malaria and 6,198 received clean and safe delivery services. Of the total deliveries, up to 15,576 were skilled births. In 2022, World Vision will conduct activities in line with the health cluster strategies outline below: Under Objective1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations. World Vision will implement activities that include: improving IPC/WASH at the health facilities, Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases through static and outreach programs, support the Boma Health Initiatives, support targeted number of static integrated facilities (Hospitals, PHCC, and PHCU), capacity building for health care workers, IEC production and dissemination, Deployment of Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM), Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control measures, procurement of outbreak response commodities, procurement of essential medical kits, procurement and transportation of essential RH commodities, training on rationale use of essential healthcare, capacity building on cold chain management and handling of vaccines, establish mobile response units, scale up rapid response mechanisms, management of SAM with medical complications at the stabilization centers, provision of CMR services, provision of BeMONC / CeMONC services, provision of rehabilitation services to improve access for the disabled, capacity building of skilled birth attendants and provision of essential medicines and skilled human resources for providing Reproductive health services (Midwives, Clinical Officers) Under Objective 2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases World Vision will undertake epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, case management, investigation and response, Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and procurement of outbreak response commodities and emergency antigens for immunization and cold chain commodities. Additionally, community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives shall be done. A special emergency mobile team shall be deployed whenever there is an outbreak. Under Objective3. Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms World Vision will support timely detection and response to disease outbreaks, establish and support surveillance systems (IDSR) and Early Warning Alert System (EWARS), support health services that are adapted to the current COVID-19 context and ensure COVID-19 prevention while continuing to provide essential lifesaving health care, promote community consultation, feedback and partnership, provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and accountability to the affected population, conducting community consultative meetings within the community regularly and implementing feedback mechanism and ensure compliance and promote humanitarian-peace-development nexus bringing humanitarian actors in strengthening synergies.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

In line with the Core Humanitarian Standards, WVI will maintain its commitment to engage with affected communities at all phases of the project cycle, through focus group discussions with women, men, boys and girls on issues concerning their education, WASH, and food security concerns. Establishing/strengthening local structures such as Project Management Committee is one example of how WVI engages the community in a sustainable and accountable manner to determine and support appropriate need-based responses. WVI's Quality Assurance framework ensures that each project implemented is carried out effectively and continually reviewed in line with community needs and in line with other humanitarian frameworks.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

World Vision will ensure that all staff are involved in the implementation of this project will involve and inform the beneficiaries and other key stakeholders at all stages of the project management cycle starting from the initial assessment, design, implementation and close out stages. The project participants will also be sensitized on their rights to launch complaints, express grievances, and give other forms of feedback about the implementation process, quantity and quality of services they are getting from the project. Communities are asked which complaints and feedback mechanisms they prefer, with efforts to ensure that unique needs of vulnerable populations are met (for example, considering child- and gender-friendly methods of collecting feedback, accounting for literacy or individuals’ safety, protection, and access to mechanisms, etc.)

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Complaints regarding any protection-related issue are handled by a Protection Officer to ensure confidentiality and to be able to determine an appropriate response to the feedback received, particularly in the event that such responses require CP or GBV intervention from the case management team. Any complaint involving sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) will be immediately escalated to the human resources team in Juba. All WVI staff are trained on SEA and all have signed WVI ’s code of conduct. Cases of SEA will be investigated by a team led by WVI South Sudan’s human resources staff in collaboration with the Safeguarding Manager in a safe and confidential manner.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Project participant feedback is compiled and shared with the project teams on a weekly basis. The feedback is then verified and responded to by the relevant teams. To accurately respond to feedback and complaints, an investigation is conducted on each feedback item submitted. Thus, after the team verifies the concerns raised, corrective measures are taken and incorporated into project implementation.
