Project Summary

The situation is further worsened by continued violence, climatic shocks, coupled with the effect of floods resulting in worsening of community WASH infrastructure, nutrition, health and protection/SGBV concerns amongst women and girls. According to Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System(FSNM August 2022 report revealed between July 2022 and June 2023, an estimated 1.4 million children under five are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition based on analysis and estimations from the results of the SMART nutrition surveys
Africa Development Aid anticipates that, if supported, it will ensure 34540 vulnerable population experiencing multi-sectoral severity levels 3 , 4 and 5 IPC targeted by this response will have reduced mobility and mortality through integrated WASH, Nutrition, Health and Shelter/NFI essential services to meet their needs. It is expected to cost $750,000 in one year. The six counties (Ayod, Fangak, Pigi/Canal and Nyirol (of Jonglei State), Nasir (of Upper Nile State) and Mayendit (of Unity State) have remained consistently with low level of WASH coverage, leaving vulnerable communities without resilience to withstand the chronic and acute crisis that has engulfed the county. Access to safe drinking-water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services is a fundamental element of healthy communities and has an important positive impact on nutrition. This proposed intervention will provide an overview of the evidence of nutrition and health gains that can be achieved with integrated nutrition, health and SGBV a description of key WASH practices, and practical knowledge.
Poor access to water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), whilst not the root cause of violence, can exacerbate the vulnerability of women and girls to violence. Men and boys, people of other gender or sexual identities or other marginalized groups can also sometimes be at risk. Vulnerability to violence can have a significant impact on people’s access to adequate water and sanitation and hygiene.
The proposed intervention will be to reduce morbidity and mortality among children through integration with nutrition, protection/GBV and health by providing access to safe water, rehabilitation of water points and water treatment at nutrition facilities as well as communities and promote hygiene promotion /campaigns.
Nutrition facilities will be provided with WASH/NFI kits/minimum WASH packages to care takers of children admitted and screened with SAM/MAM at functional OTP and training on the use of the items/kits.
The continued conflicts has resulted in devastation of WASH infrastructure largely contributing not only to disease outbreak, but also to increased malnutrition, public health risks and increased gender-based violence among women and girls and other vulnerable groups.
WASH,-Nutrition, health and GBV integrated programming will aim at areas of high level of malnutrition, by targeting of SAM children and their families. These areas will further converge with other Health-related priorities, acknowledging that poor WASH services are a key driver to poor health
These proposed counties already experienced high food insecurity and malnutrition (GAM rates have also heightened WASH needs. Women and girls are the primary direct users of WASH services and GBV risks have significant implications for the success of WASH interventions. ADA will ensure GBV risk mitigation is a priority area of focus.
ADA WASH will ensure delivery of specific activities such as gender segregated latrines that meet the needs of women, children and people with disabilities. Consultations by conducting safety audit will be conducted at site level to identify Gender based violence concerns and implement measures. ADA will focus on emergency hygiene promotion targeting community level. Emphasize will be made on disease prevention such COVID 19 and WASH related illnesses and hand washing with soap.

1 - Needs

The recent fighting between Agwelek, Kitgwang and SPLA-IO forces in Tonga, New Fangak and Canal/Pigi has exacerbated the already dire humanitarian situation in Fangak County due to displacement of the entire population of New Fangak and Tonga who are currently seeking safety in Old Fangak. Inter-agency rapid needs assessment was conducted on August 22, 2022 in Old Fangak where an influx of IDPs has been witnessed. Local authority (RRC) and humanitarian partners on the ground estimated the arrival of 9,251 individuals in Old Fangak with commercial boats from New Fangak Payam. Majority of IDPs are currently settled at the Women League center in Old Fangak. Phom/ New Fangak has an estimated total population of over 26,000 individuals according to estimates from RRC Fangak, including population that was displaced from Tonga of Panyikang county in the previous conflict between Agwelek and Kitgwang Forces. Out of this population, 3,873 individuals are believed to have remained in locations of Wuntur, Lele, Tongdiok, Pakan, Kuerkan, Wichmoun, Kuerjiel respectively. Therefore, this population is in urgent need of humanitarian assistance.Poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continued to have a detrimental impact on the health of children and nutrition of children under five , as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases in Ayod, Fangak, Mayendit ,Pigi/ Canal, Nasir, and Nyirol and which are among priority 1 and 2, counties reporting high GAM rates, also having high WASH needs. Mothers and care takers of malnourished children lack basic hygiene knowledge and access to safe water at domicile jeopardizing the benefits gained in nutrition programs (WASH Cluster Severity Needs assessment report 2021) The report stated that 36% of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. The remaining 64 per cent of people across the county rely on unimproved or surface water sources (41 per cent), either take more than 30 minutes to reach an improved water source (19 per cent), or are able to reach an improved source in less than 30 minutes but face protection concerns while accessing the water (4 per cent). The highest proportion of households relying on surface water found in Upper Nile, with 66 per cent, followed by Jonglei with 43. The quantity of water available per day is also below standards in many locations, with 17 per cent of people unable to collect enough water for drinking, and 39 per cent of people reporting insufficient access to water for hand washing. WASH conditions in Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity driven by some factors such as conflicts and lack of access due to flooding and roads which severely limited WASH service provision and access to WASH facilities for a large proportion of the population. Lack of access to WASH can affect a child’s nutritional status in many ways. Existing evidence supports at least three direct pathways: via diarrhea diseases, intestinal parasite infections and environmental enteropathy. WASH may also impact nutritional status indirectly by necessitating walking long distances in search of water and sanitation facilities and diverting a mother’s time away from child care. Diarrhea caused by poor sanitation, lack of hygiene, and unsafe drinking water, is one of the leading causes of child death in the country, and the leading cause of child death in South Sudan. Worse yet, diarrheal disease inhibits nutrient absorption leading to starvation even if sufficient nutrition is consumed. Under nutrition also reduces a child’s resistance to subsequent infections. The health burden of under nutrition due to lack of WASH services may be massively underestimated. Therefore, safe drinking water, proper sanitation, and hygiene can prevent malnutrition and stunting in children by preventing the development of environmental enteropathy and diarrheal disease.
