Project Summary

The objective of the project is to strengthen the protection of most vulnerable girls, boys, women and men affected by conflict and climatic shocks by reducing risks and vulnerabilities and increasing community capacities. Key to this action will be a focus on improving capacity for immediate response to violations and remedial action as well as strengthening the protective environment in Somalia.
On immediate response, the action will aim to address acute protection needs of girls, boys women and men, stemming from violence, including GBV, child rights violations, as well as coercion, and abuse. Using a survivor centered multi-sectoral approach DRC, vulnerable women and girls will receive GBV services through Women and girls safe spaces, psychosocial support ( PSS) and case management.
To enable recovery and restore dignity of individual and community victims of violence, neglect, abuse and exploitation and to improve positive coping strategies and prevent further violations, DRC will establish child friendly spaces through which psychosocial support will be provided. In addition, material assistance and livelihood skills training for GBV will also be provided.
On environment building, DRC will reinforce formal and community institutions for better legal and community-based protection of vulnerable groups from violence, coercion and abuse. Key to achieve this objective, activities will include awareness-raising and outreach among communities on protection issues, capacity building and training for duty bearers, service providers and humanitarian workers on GBV, CP and HLP. In addition, DRC will map out civil identification documentation required in Somalia to obtain/access government services such as national identity cards, passports, driver's licenses, title deeds as well as birth and death certificates. The mapping will also establish the cost of obtaining the documents and issuing agencies at Federal and Regional State level. The information will be disseminated to strengthen governance structures and to support service delivery to affected populations.
On advocacy, DRC will strengthen coordination of protection services and enhance advocacy on protection with relevant stakeholders. Key activities will include strengthening of referral pathways, mapping of service providers and Protection Monitoring of rights violations. DRC will further identify and provide protection-oriented support to people with disabilities in the target locations.
The proposed action will reach 108,400 persons in Somalia (22,50 girls, 18,250 boys, 39,550 women and 28,350 men).

Mitigation Measures

Mitigating the risk of Exclusion and marginalization of marginalized community’s and minority groups Minority groups in Somalia are rarely targeted by key interventions or considered in policy design. Minorities as a whole are marginalized and invisible. Women and children are among minority groups are even more sidelined and excluded and do not benefit from structures that affords others some degree of protection. Recurrent insecurity caused by conflict creates an environment where minority groups are vulnerable and abnormally displaced from their homes. In addition, at least 15% of the population are people with disabilities who face heightened risks and exclusion from services. To mitigate on this risks, DRC will intentionally target and empower, protect and promote the rights of minority communities, with a focus on women and children who experience multiple-discrimination on account of their gender and minority status. DRC will follow a selection criterion for beneficiaries that include members of minorities as vulnerable groups that require special attention. Selection of beneficiaries will be through participatory methods in collaboration with trained community representatives. Through protection monitoring, DRC will identify trends of exclusion and marginalization. Data collected will be used for targeting and prioritization, ensuring that no one is left out. DRC will consult people with disabilities and/or their representatives during the design and Identify and address barriers to participation for people with disabilities. Mitigating the risk of Exclusion and marginalization of women, girls and boys Women, girls and boys remain vulnerable to protection risks and harmful cultural practices, GBV, child recruitment and hazardous child labor. Their ability to access justice and remedies and to enjoy rights is often limited and further weakened in case of displacement. Furthermore, stigma related to abuses often deters survivors from accessing assistance or reporting incidents. To address this, DRC will include specific needs of women, girls and boys when designing and implementing the project. DRC will seek ways to increase the women and girl friendly spaces providing opportunities for recognizing their role within communities, as an enabler for protection outcomes. The design of the spaces will in safe locations. Focus will also be put on awareness raising for HLP rights of women. Any response will be implemented using the survivor-centred approach and in line with principles of safety, confidentiality, respect and non-discrimination. Protection of personal data will be key. Daily field presence of well-trained and gender-balanced DRC protection staff, high quality of services will support building a strong relation of trust. Using the rights-based approach DRC will ensure that all activities build on protection standards but also leverage national frameworks. DRC will ensure proper coordination, especially on referrals to maximize quality use of already limited resources Mitigation the risk of external stakeholder interference in beneficiary selectionThere are significant number of displaced communities and people in locations that are not easily accessible to humanitarian assistance, this could be due to insecurity and people living in far to reach areas. In addition, there are instances where external stakeholders demand targeting of particular groups or prevent others from receiving assistance. To mitigate on these challenges, DRC will use beneficiary selection criteria that considers vulnerability as an entry point to assistance. DRC will engage in consultation processes adequately gathering relevant information including power dynamics from the communities and other stakeholders and taking these views into account when selecting beneficiaries and implementing the action.DRC will work with existing or revitalized community committees among the target groups that will be part of the beneficiary selection.

Please provide further explanation

Durable Solutions: Recognizing that the project is set in a protracted and complex emergency context, the project will partially contribute to durable solutions. The project will aim at addressing all 3 dimensions of safety, key to the achievement of durable solutions – physical, material and legal. Material safety will be addressed through integration with DRC response in other sectors in which protection will be mainstreamed. The program is designed taking into account the needs and perspectives of the communities that ensuring that the intervention will contribute to physical, material and legal safety of the targeted communities. DRC will conduct conflict analysis before and during project implementation and identify among other things, root cause of conflicts and other factors that may lead to conflicts. The information from conflict analyses studies will inform program design and re-design. Social cohesion and peaceful coexistence will be supported analyzing how targeting can minimize inter-community conflicts. The action is directly linkage to linked to government processes and will aim at building long term protective environment through building capacity of government officials on human rights which will at the end improve governance. In addition, community based structures will be strengthened through training and awareness creating leading to a more empowered community that can prevent and respond to violations on their own. Through protection monitoring, DRC will monitor key protection concerns across Somalia to identify trends and patterns of protection risks. Based on this, DRC will further engage in evidence based joint advocacy with other protection actors, as well as the international community to address these protection concerns and ensure that rights of displaced communities are addressed. Duty bearers will also be part of the quarterly protection monitoring joint analysis workshops. In collaboration with the HLP sub-cluster DRC will monitor eviction risks and ensure securing housing, land and property rights is part of both protection and shelter activities. DRC will also work closely with key stakeholders to map out civil identification documents issued or required in Somalia to obtain government services including but not limited to national identity cards, passports, driver's licenses, title deeds, birth and death certificates and any other document required to access government services. The information will be disseminated to strengthen governance structures and to support service delivery to affected populations.
