Project Summary

The TelKaif district is one of nine districts in Ninewa governorate. North of Mosul, it is home to a diverse population consist out of Christians, Yazidis, Kurds and Arabs. The occupation of ISIL and the war to retake the area through the military coalition between 2014 and 2017 lead to massive displacements, infrastructure destruction and thus livelihood opportunities are limited.
This project aims to support the recovery efforts of the international community to restore basic infrastructure as a basis for future livelihoods of the district inhabitants by the usage of the CfW tool. Water, as one of the basic needs for people to live a healthy life, have income generation and prevent future conflicts will be restored and support returnee movement from IDP Camps back to the district. The current lack of water is becoming a cause of tension in the area exacerbating the precarious cohabitation of the different ethnic and religious groups, already undermined by ISIL occupation of the area. Beside the social tension, the local populations rely on non-controlled sources or water trucking for its supply, with increased cost, and the consequent reduction of the quantity of water available for personal and home hygiene and cleanliness. This situation poses a risk to the human health, especially of babies, children and elderly people, livelihood and returnee movements.
NCA aims to ensure a sustainable access to safe water infrastructure, as well as enabling people to stabilize their income on household level and ultimately enhance livelihood opportunities in the intervention area.
Recently, the Tel Keif water pump station has been rehabilitated and improved with the installation of new booster pumps, chlorination dosing units and general maintenance of the structure within the framework of a project funded by UNDP. In order to fully support its positive effects, NCA will upgrade the water distribution system linked to the booster pumps, as the general condition lack of adequate quality due to the age, maintenance and the deliberated sabotages perpetrated by ISIL during the occupation.
The networks with direct potential access to 200,000 people need deep maintenance, including the change of old pipes and gears, reconstruction of water tanks and connections with the distribution lines in the villages.
In phase one, the project aims to ensure the water supply of 34 villages in Tel Keif area through sustainable and permanent water network with Cash-for-Work (CfW) activities. Around 1,300 beneficiaries from the local community will benefit from the infrastructure rehabilitation by daily labour and thus, enhancing ownership.
After completion of the 34 villages, is it envisaged to connect more villages with the water supply system. This is dependent on the active collaboration with the local municipalities and the Directorate of Water (DoW) Mosul.
Through the "work intensive direct implementation” mechanism, this project will become an income generating source for the most vulnerable families turning out to be a pull factor for the local economy, favouring the restoration of the economic framework of the area.
The necessity to recover the pre-conflict condition of life implies an active support to the local economy through the restoration of the existing income-generating activities.
In this phase, small / family enterprises will be granted with an economic aid to restart the activities and, in the short medium period, become a booster factor generating a virtuous circle with positive consequences for the community.
The main aim is to give the local economy an impulse that reactivates the local commerce and possible employment even of the unskilled labourers otherwise devoid of any means of livelihood.

Needs

A rapid assessment was carried out by NCA and GIZ (donor for this project) in 34 villages of TelKaif, Ninewa namely Jarahiya, Kavroke, Ain Baqar, Al-Qawseyat, Alqosh, Badriya, Bapeer, Baweza, Biyaban, Bozan, Chakana, isherfa, Chrkhan, Daka, Dashqotan, Der Ster, Doghata, Kalata Farhan, Karanjoke, Khatara, Khoshaba, Masqalat, Neferiya, Nusariyah, Sada, Shalalat, Shekhka, Sindanic Olya, Sindanic Sofla, Sirechka, Tel Sin, Telkif, Telsqif, Wana. According to the findings of the assessment, a large majority of inhabitants of these villages were relying of unsafe water sources with limited capacity of storage and transport. Also a high number among the population were assessed to have access to insufficient quantity of water for drinking, cooking, domestic and hygiene purposes. (less than 15 liters of water per person per day - SPHERE). It was also noted that some of the residents also buy water from the vendors. Furthermore, treating water at HH level especially that is collected from open sources and surface water with risks of biological contamination is not treated at HH level. On the parallel, the residents do not have sufficient livelihood to support their families and improve access to basic needs such as clean water which is adversely affecting their HH economic condition. Activities

The overall objective of the project is to ensure the access to adequate quantity of safe drinking water supplied through new constructed or rehabilitated permanent water distribution infrastructure, and to restore the living conditions supporting the local economy. NCA will employ no less than 1,300 manual laborers, selected among the unemployed inhabitants of the targeted villages, who will work under the supervision of the NCA technical team to perform the “manual” works (such as digging trenches, load and unload trucks etc.) related with the rehabilitation or construction of the water supply infrastructures in the selected villages. They will be employed with “service contract” or “incentive contract” for no more and no less than 40 working days per person. The typology of workers requested for the implementation of this activity is culturally typical not suitable for women and people with disabilities. To include these categories of particularly disadvantaged beneficiaries, NCA will employ women and disables as supervisors and focal points and where possible, to include them directly in the manual work at a total of 15% of the overall beneficiaries of the project. The proposed activity aims to reach two main goals: to offer a source of income to the inhabitants of areas, whose economic activities are not yet fully recovered, and to increase the sense of ownership of the infrastructure involving the beneficiaries directly in the implementation of the activities. NCA will limit the recourse to specialised companies to those works that require the use of machineries (i.e. cranes, welding machines etc.) and trained and skilled staff (i.e. machineries operators, electricians, plumbers etc.) to ensure that direct beneficiaries indicators are met. To restore the living condition, NCA consider priority to support the small local enterprises to recover the local market to the pre-conflict level. A minimum of 200 entrepreneurs will receive a grand (about 1,500 /2,000 USD) to restart or improve their activities. The beneficiaries will be selected among the shopkeepers, small / family farmers in the area whose activities need an initial impulse to activate a virtuous circle with positive repercussions on the economy of the area. Based on a GIZ pilot survey for water in Telkaif, NCA will implement these activities through a mixture of different selection processes. With the help of micro-economical assessments in the implementation area, NCA will have an in- depth knowledge about the market dynamics in and around Telkaif. Based on the outcome of that assessment, different selection processes for potential beneficiaries are conceivable. Either through a recommendation system from direct beneficiaries about small investment ideas – NCA will implement on case-to case decision Livelihood Activation Grants (LAG) and closely monitor the investments, with technical and compliance support. Furthermore, NCA will follow-up the beneficiaries in order to ensure as much sustainability as possible. Another strategy would be small calls for proposals for LAGs in a particular area (e.g. a cluster of 2-3 villages) a) around one specific topic directly related to outcome 1 – water and water infrastructure or b) a non-thematic call in clustered villages. NCA will make sure, that every decision will be thoroughly documented, monitored and technically supported. Ten Livelihood Activation Grant Mentors will be employed by NCA. After receiving a mentorship training, the NCA mentors will consult the LAG recipients on an individual basis throughout the project period. Protection Risk Analysis

The beneficiaries may be exposed to risk of GBV and sexual abuse due to limited access to water at present. Women and children traveling long distances to fetch water may be exposed to such risks. Furthermore, lack of clean drinking water in terms of quality and quantity can also be a major factor for conflicts between the community to have control of resources and use it for benefit. Specific challenges could be posed for female headed households and disabled members of the communities that are more vulnerable and have difficulties access unless specific arrangements are made. Since the project is based on hard labor for the cash for work component, inclusion of women in the CFW activity might be a cultural issue as well.

Mitigation Measures

The project is designed to protect people basic right to safe water and improved health. The activities will ensure beneficiaries dignity and will include consensus and involvement of the targeted beneficiaries at all stages of the project. Similarly, project will equally benefit girls, boys, women and men in terms of access to safe drinking water, however, in terms of livelihood opportunities the project will provide equal opportunities to men and women while respecting the local culture and social contruct keeping in view the extend of labor involved. The intervention will also reduce fetching of water from far-flung areas by women, adolescent girls and young children which are the major contribution in GBV and protection issues. This will result in curtailing of GBV and protection issues in the areas of return. The project will focus on improving livelihoods through cash for work for the rehabilitation of water facilities and systems and improve access to clean drinking water with and reduced contaminated water usage in the respective areas. Ultimately, the project will significantly affect the community well being and also contribute towards addressing the protection issues in the long run. The project will also address issues of vulnerable groups such as women, children, elderly, female headed households and disabled through involving community groups and elders so suitable arrangement could be made to ensure equitable access to all project beneficiaries. The project will also develop strategies to ensure inclusion of female headed HHS in the CFW component so they could be benefited while respecting local culture.

How is your project accountable to affected population

In order to ensure accountability to the target group, the project will be implemented with close coordination and participation of the target group/benefiaries. NCA is a HAP certified organisations and prioritizes principles of accountability and transparency. This delegates ownerships in the community and they feel responsible for the outcomes of the project which will help NCA in the successful completion of the project. NCA’s experience shows that participation of the community leads toward sustainability and effectiveness. For this very purpose, the project will be implemented with full participation of the affected population and the beneficiaries. Community mobilization will be an integral part of the project for participation of the local communities (affected population and the beneficiaries) which is essential for success and sustainability of the project. Project will involve community structures of the respective villages for their role and involvement of the project planning, implementation and monitoring. All information will be shared with the affected population and the beneficiaries at large. The project will involve community and recruit workers from the community for the cash for work component that directly contributes to improving access to safe drinking water. Using Common Humanitarian Standards and HAP as benchmark, NCA also has a Complaint Response Mechanism in place that is strictly followed as an integral part of the program. As part of the SOPs on CRM, the project beneficiaries (men, women, boys, girls, PWD, elderly persons) will be oriented on the complaint mechanism and on their right to share their feedback regarding the quality of the project and their satisfaction. Each complaint received by the NCA team will be recorded and an acknowledgement will be given to the complainant with a unique complain number. Once the complaint is registered, it will be classified based on high-low importance level and will be assigned response time accordingly between 1 week and 24 hours. Different channels will be identified for based on the sensitivity of the complaint so proper action could be taken. If the complainant is not satisfied with the outcome, a appeal process will also be introduced that will go directly to the management of NCA to respond to. Further Pana flexes and Banners will be displayed at the main sites in the field having information about the contacts of complaint focal person from NCAs Management. NCA will also ensure that beneficiaries must know about the project activities as well as the selection criteria. Complaint Boxes (locked) will be installed in each targeted areas and hygiene sessions sites and the community to lodge their complaint (if any). The complaints log and action taken will be shared with the donor team accordingly. All complaints will be recorded with action taken and feedback provided to the complainant. The complaints could be lodged either through email, in writing, telephone or in person. NCA will ensure the information sharing with all relevant stakeholders including government, other CSOs/NGOs working in that areas and the beneficiaries to avoid any confusion, conflict and duplication/overlapping and ensure transparency through meetings, data, plan and reports sharing.
