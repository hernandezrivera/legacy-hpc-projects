Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen protection and resilience of Syrian IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities through advocacy, coordination, provision of community-based protection services and legal services. UNHCR Syria will continue to lead the Protection Sector to ensure coordinated protection interventions programming for IDPs and affected people as well as executing leadership roles in refugee return programmes in collaboration with the Government. UNHCR’s protection interventions will include provision of integrated protection services through a network of 120 centers in all 14 Governorates. The network of Community Centres, paired with some 120 mobile units and around 3,600 outreach volunteers of multifunctional thematic capacities, are fundamental to the implementation of UNHCR’s Protection and Solutions Strategy for Syria through outreach to affected populations, assessment of protection risks and vulnerabilities, consultation, identification and mobilization of community resources, strengthening of community self-help networks, and offering protection services and support to displaced persons, returnees and host communities in response to the priority needs expressed by the communities, while applying a highly participatory age, gender and diversity approach. The protection services extended at the UNHCR Community Centres include, but are not limited to, psycho-social support, GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response and child protection case management, legal awareness and legal aid, non-formal education programmes, services for persons with disabilities and older persons, provision of general and medical in-kind assistance, social and recreational activities, curriculum based awareness activities on various subjects targeting women, men, boys and girls, referrals and information sharing, social protection through livelihoods support to most vulnerable population.
As part of its commitment to Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), UNHCR will ensure meaningful participation of communities based on age, gender and diversity approach, structured focus group discussions and enhancement of the community-based feedback and complaint mechanisms. UNHCR will continue its capacity building efforts to ensure services are delivered according to the standards and that accountability to affected population and do no harm principles are respected.
UNHCR will continue co-leading the AAP Technical Working Group and the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) In-Country Network and coordinate activities to strengthen AAP and SEA prevention, risk mitigation and response.
Monitoring and evaluation of the planned interventions is based on the reports and observations by the partners and on regular direct observation and ongoing assessment and consultations with the community by UNHCR and partners and the comparison of achievements and related financial expenditures with objectives. Evaluation is conducted through different channels, including post distribution monitoring to determine the effectiveness of/measure the satisfaction of people of concern with the service and the distribution process in addition to multiple satisfaction surveys and tracking tools related to different protection interventions. Monitoring and evaluation activities are carried out at various levels by partners and agencies implementing subprojects, UNHCR Country Office, Sub and Field Offices. Situation reports are submitted by all UNHCR Field Offices to their respective Supervising Office and Country Offices submit a corresponding report to Headquarters.

3 - Needs

The plight of Syrians has further deteriorated over the course of 2022. The crisis continues to impact the protection and well-being of vulnerable people across the country. As per the 2022 HNO, GBV remains a main concern, women and girls are subjected to increased denial of economic resources and education, movement restrictions, exploitation, intimate partner and family violence, technology facilitated violence, physical, psychological, emotional, sexual, and social violence. Children including those with disabilities are at heightened risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect. Deepening poverty and lack of access to services also have a negative impact on children’s well-being.HLP and civil documentation continues to be key protection concerns, people live in communities where they lack/loss of civil documentation which limits their access to basic services and humanitarian assistance. Illegal or undocumented HLP transactions; disputes; access to land for livelihoods; land contamination with landmines are key challenges. Based on 2021/2022 UNHCR participatory assessment, lack/loss of civil documents and HLP were also identified as key risks, legal awareness and assistance to obtain civil documents as a need. CP and GBV including safety, sexual, physical and emotional abuse, child labour and neglect were also highlighted as concerns and the need to address them through prevention and response. PSS and support to elderly and PWD were also identified as key needs.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented through integrated protection services with a focus on community-based approaches including 1)targeted support to persons with specific protection needs, persons with disabilities and elderly; 2) legal counselling and legal representation before courts and administrative bodies to the most vulnerable Syrian on civil and HLP documentation, documenting civil events and enhance the protection of women and children; 3) legal awareness raising across the country 4) advocacy and support to government on civil documentation, HLP and other legal matters, 5) provision of response services in respect to GBV and child protection, which will include individual case management and referrals to other services; 6)psychosocial support; 7)community-based committees; 8)PSEA awareness raising and response 9)support community centres, community led initiatives and enhance community engagement and resilience. UNHCR will strengthen its outreach to all people in need. Curriculum based prevention awareness sessions will be provided to reduce harmful behaviors and promote gender equality. UNHCR will continue to implement its global GBV policy and mainstreaming in 2023 ensuring GBV is mainstreamed in all sectors and survivors are considered when planning for legal or livelihood activities, engaging men and boys on GBV prevention and response, supporting the women committees to enhance GBV prevention and response and conduction GBV safety audit will in Community centres.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Increased community tension due to the assistance, and/or unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to perceived discrimination. Mitigating measures:Provision of standardized assistance packages with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Harmonized and transparent selection criteria used by all partners and conduct awareness raising and information sharing within the communities on those criteria. Regular monitoring, consultation, client satisfaction surveys, PDM and adjustments to the program to prevent and manage any tensions which may arise. Risk 2. Lack of involvement and technical capacity of national stakeholders in areas where the response to needs require their involvement e.g.access to civil documentation and HLP documents, specific child protection interventions. Mitigating measures: Proactive coordination with relevant authorities. Technical and capacity support to strengthen national institutions in service delivery. Risk 3: Lack of/low quality of services and referrals expose survivors to further harm, discrimination, stigmatization. Mitigation measures: Ensuring all services are provided in an environment that ensures confidentiality safety and adhering to the survivors centered approach. Engaging men and boys on CP and GBV prevention and response activities. Conducting training and supervision of case managers; service mapping and awareness raising to the community on expected services. Enhance referral pathways with other services providers.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Inter-Country Network (ICN) in Syria. UNHCR will be co-funding the PSEA Coordinators' position in 2023 to strengthen PSEA coordination and implementation of the ICN work plan. UNHCR will work closely with other stakeholders, including the GBV and CP Sub-working Groups and PSEA network, to ensure adequate and multifaceted prevention and response mechanisms are in place regarding the “do no harm” principle. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR. In addition to delivering PSEA awareness-raising activities in all UNHCR community centres, PSEA mandatory training and annual refresher sessions are compulsory to UNHCR own and partners’ staff. Complaint and feedback mechanisms, including hotline numbers, email addresses, and complaint boxes, are functional -at the agency and partner’s levels- to ensure and support a confidential and effective reporting system. Since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment has become mandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partner capacity improvement plans have been developed for the partners scored medium and high risk, and UNHCR will be supporting and monitoring the improvement of partners in addressing SEA. Partners who fail to meet the standard of the PSEA checklist will not be able to continue its partnership with UNHCR in the following year. UNHCR will continues to assess, monitor and enhance the capacity of partners to prevent and respond to SEA allegations and apply mandatory SEA screening when recruiting staff members.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Fluid context, shifting frontlines and unstable political situation in North Syria. Mitigating measures: Proactively coordinate with UNDSS and sister agencies in terms of security and access issues. Advocate at senior levels of all stakeholders to identify political solutions to ensure safe and continued access. Risk 2: Limited ability to conduct monitoring of delivery of assistance results in potential loss of resources. Mitigating measures: Advocacy with authorities for access to beneficiary data. Strengthen risk based monitoring tools and establishing remote/third party monitoring arrangements where UNHCR does not have direct access. Support partners to establish harmonized monitoring systems and provide training to partner staff on monitoring. Strengthen communication with communities and feedback mechanisms. Risk 3: Delayed/lack of approvals. Mitigation: Regular follow up with relevant authorities, advocacy for uninterrupted access. Risk 4: Resistance and non-acceptance from the community,especially men on CP and GBV messaging and activities. Mitigation: All community members engagement on all phases of the project cycle; adapting awareness and information messages to the specific context. Risk 5: Deterioration of the security situation. Mitigation: monitoring and info sharing with relevant stakeholders and temporary closure of facilities if risks raise; security/surveillance at facilities, premises. Risk: Reduced fund: Mitigation: Expand mobile teams role.
