Project Summary

South Sudan, generally is still a tough place to live especially for the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community. The intercommunal violence spread almost across the entire country, common in Jonglei, other areas in Upper Nile and Bar-el-ghazel regions. It is mostly the women and children who are often at most risk plus the youth who are mobilized to fight inappropriate wars.
The impact of COVID-19 and failing economy continue to bite, affecting mostly the locals who lost their casual jobs, businesses. The greatest impact is on land tenure linked with returns. Though there is no data collected to prove this, but as a local NGO, many vulnerable South Sudanese are being evicted from time to time; landlords increase their rents at will or evict their tenants for their returning families. It seems many are returning due to inability to sustain their living standards in the neighbouring countries of Uganda and Kenya for those living in the suburbs. This is also the case with refugees in the camps – unavailability of cultivation land, decreasing food rations, long COVID-19 lock down are some of the reasons fostering returns apart from the struggling and holding peace.
In Jonglei and Unity States, subnational violence that results in child abductions, cattle raids, loss of lives and displacements, destruction of property including shelters razed down, continue to severely affect the two states. Currently Bentiu in Unity is the only County rated by IPC in the HNO 2022 at the catastrophic level in South Sudan. The rest of the counties in Unity and Jonglei are just at level 4 which is still extreme for human conditions.
Floods have also caused displacements, increased animal diseases which is wiping out the livelihoods and exacerbating shelter and non-food items needs among the internally displaced persons.
These two states have been made inaccessible by the floods and the chronic insecurity in the area making it difficult for humanitarian aid to reach. This makes delivery of aid even more expensive
Central and Eastern Equatoria have recently been impacted much by conflicts especially with the cattle keepers from Jonglei who seek pasture for their cattle and often drive cattle to the farms of the host community. These conflicts continue to cause further displacements at a small but well spread scale. The destruction of crops and other livelihoods including the cattle that are finding no pasture due to severe impact of climate change is having its toll on South Sudanese. The hot temperatures have scorched most green pastures including crops putting the country at the brink of famine.
Central and Eastern Equatoria have high number of anticipated returns as reported by REACH monitoring team. This will mean huge needs for shelter and non-food items. However in order to build the local economy through the local markets, HDC will respond through cash and voucher assistance since the markets are highly functional in these areas.
Cash and Voucher Assistance is highly recommended by the cluster in the year 2022, HDC has piloted and implemented cash programming the past years and is well positioned to implement the project with experience.
Though all areas in Central and Eastern Equatoria have functional markets, parts of the areas in Jonglei; Twic East, Pibor and Duk plus Koch in Unity have limited functionality in the markets if not functional. In-kind response will be prioritized in these areas.
HDC is capitalizing on its static presence to promote integrated response by responding in targeting locations through both shelter and non-food items, and Protection. This will embed the strategic objective 2 of ensuring centrality of protection in all emergency responses. This will ensure dignity and protection of the most vulnerable groups among the internally displaced, host community and returnees
The project will target 35,000 vulnerable individuals; 14,000 women, 7,000 men, 14,000 children, 5,250 older persons and PWDs.

1 - Needs

Women in South Sudan are the ones majorly bearing the impact of consequences of conflict and failed economy in the country. They are left widowed or negligence from their husbands, to take care of the huge families that often includes orphaned children of their sisters or brothers.These families often find it difficult to get shelter or non-food items during displacements e.g. floods or when returning. If not supported in time, it may lead to negative coping mechanism that includes risks of sexual and gender based violence for women and children. Men continue to neglect their families and partners due to lack of proper jobs. The traditional norms discourage men from being stigmatized as weak when they do meaner chores to earn a living, yet there is decreasing number of white collar jobs in the country.Many use the negative coping mechanism of begging to continue to take care of their families which is degrading their dignity and impacting on their psychological wellbeing in the society.This makes it difficult for men and take on their responsibilities of providing shelter and other needs in their families. Children currently in South Sudan are involved in child labour. Many have dropped out of school due to lack of school fees, left on women who are unable to afford them. There is also lack of parental control for the children as their mothers are often engaged in laboring for a day’s meal. Those that lack what to eat are seen, loitering rubbish heaps, looking for left over foods to eat. The girl-child find themselves marrying early or being forced into. Some of their mothers often engage them in survival sex to support their families.It is really absurd and dangerous for children who are the future of the country in South Sudan. The South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview also iterates to different needs of women, men and children under general protection, GBV, children protection even in HLP as many are evicted due to lack of rent especially in Juba. The HNO as quoted indicates “The top three shelter/NFI-related needs are access to emergency shelter, NFIs and housing, and land and property (HLP) rights to ensure security of tenure. Findings from the qualitative component of the FSNMS+ 2021 indicate that among host communities, IDPs and returnees access to shelters was impaired due to destruction of shelters by conflict and flooding.” Older persons and persons with disabilities are increasing disadvantaged; the schools have no brails, no one teaches sign language at school. To some older persons are seen as vulnerable rather than asset, a library to learn from the rich culture.Many institutional designs do not cater for the disabled persons including at schools that are also dogged with low income.Females with disabilities often fall victim to gender based violence and hence there is need to heighten protection response to the persons with disabilities especially the females.

2 - Project activities

HDC shelter and NFI activities will take three categories in harmony with SNFI Cluster Objectives. These activities will be implemented through the standard cluster methodologies for response cycle that includes alert, needs analysis, response, and evaluation. The team will use the standard cluster tools for data collection and reporting; needs analysis report template on kobo collect, distribution report, rapid monitoring and post distribution monitoring tools.All communications will be done through the state focal points who analyse the alerts raised to approve for a response. Below are the major activities; 1. PROVISION OF ASSISTANCE TO NEW DISPLACEMENTS (CONFLICT AND FLOOD AFFECTED) AND PROTRACTED DISPLACEMENTSThis will target about 4,650 individuals with in kind emergency shelter and or standard lifesaving non-food items. About 1,500 are anticipated to provided with in kind loose shelter and non-food itemsA higher number of 19,650 will be provided with in kind flood kits.This activity will mainly target locations of Jonglei and Unity States where floods and conflict are anticipated to displace individuals.For protracted displacements in Juba and Bor IDP camps, cash and voucher modality with employed to provide assistance that includes essential shelter upgrades and non-food items replacements to 7,100 individuals.This activity is in line with the cluster’s specific objective 2.2 to Provide shelter and non-food items for displaced people in situations of emergency and transition in an ability-challenged accessible, gender-responsive manner. 2. PROMOTION OF COLLECTIVE ACTION TO ENSURE CENTRALITY OF PROTECTIONHDC will put up a complaints and feedback mechanism desk that is child and gender sensitive. A feedback box, that is secure and durable will be put up in accessible area in locations of protracted displacements and areas of return. During emergency distributions, a desk will be put up and community made aware of the purpose to receive complaints. The individual manning the desk will have to be a professional able to handle children and adults alike – a protection staff.Rapid monitoring will be carried out after each distribution in order to promote accountable practices of project programming. Views received during this exercise and also from the CFM desk will be analyzed and utilize to improve on the project implementation during reviewsThis activity is in line with cluster specific objective 2.2. to Promote collective action on Centrality of Protection and ensure the population groups targeted to receive assistance are consulted throughout the entire cycle of the response, and their needs are taken into account in decision-making by humanitarians. 3. ENHANCE RESILIENCE CAPACITY OF RETURNEES AND HOST COMMUNITYThis activity will mainly target 850 returnees and host community Central and Eastern Equatoria where there are anticipated high number of returns in Kajo Keji, Juba, Magwi, Ikotos and ToritThis will involve cash and voucher assistance to provide shelter renovations and rehabilitation to the 600 most vulnerable. Transitional shelters will be provided to the 250 most vulnerable in kind out of 850.There will be awareness raising to the population of 1,250 on disaster risk reduction mechanism in order to prevent another conflict disruption recovery process of the returnees and host community.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The response will be in Central and Eastern Equatoria plus parts of Jonglei and Unity where markets are functional.Protection and SNFI Cluster have clearly made a communication that with past experience, cash assistance has proven to provide shorter term impact of improving the lives of the most vulnerable.SNFI Cluster is facing high costs of transporting in kind shelter and non-food items through air to access hard to reach locations like Koch, Pibor, etc. Using cash as a modality will greatly reduce on the cost.South Sudan humanitarian community are encouraging cash assistance due to its advantages associated with recovery and resilience and South Sudan is now at the recovery phase.HDC has also been implementing cash assistance for over 4 years Juba, and parts of Upper Nile region with UNHCR and DCA, and has all the financial requirements to carry out the implementation including experience.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

A good percentage, 30% or more of the project will be through CVA modality. Most of the markets are opening, South Sudan is now at the recovery to development stage. Cash assistance is associated with the advantage of building the local economy, hence why it is appropriate to step up cash assistance.
