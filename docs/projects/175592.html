Project Summary

Reduction of mortality by strengthening tracing and linkage of 11,200 HIV patients who have defaulted back to HIV treatment and care in seven districts in the provinces of Cabo Delgado and Nampula through community-based tracing and adherence support groups with quality mental health and psychosocial support.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

UNAIDS is initiating activities in Cabo Delgado and an expansion plan to Nampula to provide support to community activists, organizations of people living with HIV, and IDPs living with HIV that have defaulted HIV treatment. The target population for this project are people being accommodated in emergency shelters. As part of the intervention, community advocates will provide outreach services based on a MOH essential package of HIV services during the Covid-19 pandemic. UNAIDS, working with community-based organization and activists aims to identify and link approximately 11,200 IDPs living with HIV who have defaulted HIV treatment.UNAIDS is proposing to train community activists and mentor mothers among these target IDPs in Pemba, Metuge, Montepuez districts in Cabo Delgado Provincie and Meconta, Nampula city, Nacala Porto, Erati districts in Nampula to trace IDPs that have defaulted HIV treatment. This would include the placement of a humanitarian technical advisor in each province who will be responsible for the following: Integration of HIV in humanitarian response through the cluster system, focusing on health, protection, and food and nutrition.Generation of information on the status ART distribution points, access to HIV treatment and care by IDPs and PLHIV, supplies of HIV medication and related commodities, Number of PLHIV LTFU, and status of Health care workers Facilitation of training activities on ART adherence and retention in emergency settings.Monitoring visits to project sites in coordination with the Provincial Department of Health and the Provincial AIDS Council.Enhance protection by increasing access to measures to prevent sexual and gender-based violence, for key and priority population including male and female adolescents and young women.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

UNAIDS will contract, train, and supervise 2 Humanitarian Technical Advisors to support Pemba, Metuge, Montepuez dstricts in Cabo Delgado and Meconta, Nampula city, Nacala Porto, Erati districts in Nampula. In partnership with the Provincial Department of Health and the UN Joint team on AIDS, strengthen integration of HIV in the provincial Humanitarian cluster system and ensure engagement of networks of people living with HIV. The project will provide technical assistance to PLHIV support groups to develop costed emergency contingency plans which include innovative models to scale up HIV services. Working with local Community Based Organizations, each Technical Advisor will contract a total of 50 community activists and ART adherence workers who will trace HIV patients who have defaulted treatment within the communities and also sensitise and refer them to HIV and COVID-19 services.Based on nationally approved curriculum, the project will train 100 community activists on HIV service provision on how to conduct active case finding and the provision of HIV services including mental health and psychosocial support, community adherence support, HIV services, and referrals to protection and community nutrition programmes. Working with the Department of health, the project will link HIV services delivered by mobile health brigades with community activities spearheaded by the contracted Community activists.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Through the joint programme on AIDS in Mozambique, UNAIDS has identified that Adolescent girls and young women are at highest risk of harm or neglect due to pervailing gender norms, or from the harmful beliefs and practices which disproportionately impact young children, girls, and the disabled. This often includes child marriage, child labor, and discontinuing child schooling or re-enrolment. Due to increased conflict and displacement, alcohol abuse and crime rates (such as physical and sexual assaults) typically increase, creating disfunction in households and communities. Consequently, more than 50% of the project beneficiaries will be women. The project will also utilize the Linha Verde hotline reports on cases related to sexual and gender based violence in Cabo Delgado and Nampula to train community activists and mentor mothers. Additionally, UNAIDS conducted a national rapid assessment among People living with HIV to identify among other things, women’s specific needs and coping mechanisams during COVID-19. The findings from this assement will also inform the gender approaches that will be adopted by the project in tracing and and re-linking female HIV patients who have defaulted treatment. UNAIDS will also work with community activists, mentor mothers, and religious leaders, to identify adolescent girls and young women at the highest risk of contracting HIV and put in place measures to mitigate these risks in a sensitive and appropriate way.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

The results of UNAIDS’ rapid assessment of PLHIV will be used as the basis for discussions with the provincial government, local leaders, and with the provincial Humanitarian Team to ensure that project interventions, approaches and messaging are appropriately tailored to the target groups. Regular planning meetings will be used to report back on the results of project interventions, and health facilities complaints boxes, mobile health brigades and communities, will also serve to anonymously report any concerns related to project implementation (for example, concerns related to exploitation, favoritism in the beneficiary selection, or fraud), including any concerns relating to the performance or behavior of UNAIDS’ project staff.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Through collaboration with civil society organisations and the humanitarian social protection cluster, UNAIDS will identify the disabled individuals in the communities. During the initiation of the project, the community activists will identify families that have people living with disabilities with HIV, and ensure that they become beneficiaries of the project thereby receiving the requisite support. In turn, through the mobile brigades and community adherence support groups, the project will ensure that People living with HIV and disabilities, to the extent possible, are supported to access to HIV treatment and adhere to treatment. Issues concerning people living with disabilities will be continuously raised with the local leadership to help provide additional support to their families.
