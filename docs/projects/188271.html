Project Summary

This project focuses on the continuity of the ongoing project during the COVID 19 pandemic, addressing the needs of both internally displaced persons and host communities in the Idlib and western and northern countryside of Aleppo. SRD provides integrated protection activities in the health setting through prevention and risk mitigation activities like awareness-raising sessions, and response activities like case management approaches and consolidated referral channels. Psychological and Social Assistance Services (PSS) are provided in a safe place that is currently functioning and builds resilience of survivors and vulnerable men, women, boys and girls with disabilities. All activities are carried out in consideration of WHO recommended precautionary measures, health clusters, protection and COVID 19 Task Force guidelines. SRD works with community-based support services in the Idlib and Aleppo provinces through women and girls safe spaces (WGSS), community centers, or child-friendly spaces. SRD provides integrated services for GBV, psychosocial support, and early childhood development within health facilities. SRD provides center-based and outreach services in relevant areas, including community-based protection, CP, and GBV's structured awareness sessions through field activities. By integration and mainstreaming approaches, accessibility is improved and barriers to vulnerable groups are broken. This successful model will be replicated and continued within this project, as the Health Center has proven to be a secure and sensitive entry point for accessing GBV services. SRH services will include a focus on adolescents and family planning utilizing young mothers club through raising awareness of relevant services available which include psychosocial support, clinical management of rape cases and providing safe spaces for women and girls. Qualified and trained psychosocial workers will be present in the safe spaces and PSS rooms to manage the activities and provide private sessions for GBV survivors, referred cases, or other individuals seeking focused specialized PSS services. Work includes integrated case management, communication skills, community protection practices, behavioral change communication, maternal and child health, hygiene promotion, nutrition, support for survivors of gender-based violence. Includes guidance on referral routes. Protection awareness messages are delivered to beneficiaries through daily home visits, prioritizing messages on civil documents, HLP, child labor, child marriage and gender-based violence. Individuals in need of protection or other forms of support are referred to facilities and service providers within the referral channel to minimize the risk of breach. Considering what is best for your child through case management services, an integrated emergency outreach team, structured and unstructured PSS, parenting skills, early childhood development, referrals, and child care. Provision of specialized and community-based child protection services considering what shall be in the best interests of the child through case management services, integrated Emergency Response Outreach Team, structured and unstructured PSS, parenting skills, early childhood Development, referrals, and addressing children and caregivers’ vulnerabilities that are related to disability, physical/psychological injuries, severe neglect, lack of appropriate care.

5 - Implementation of activities

Centers continue offering the following services: Case management and referral, vocational training and prevention empowerment activities, GBV awareness sessions and protection monitoring. In order to remove physical barriers for women and girls with disabilities, in coordination with the local council, an additional caseworker is recruited that is specialized in disabilities. Plus, elderly and disabled people will be identified and helped with IPA cash module. Case management has in-kind/cash funds to cover the needs of vulnerable beneficiaries who cannot be referred to other services. Un/Structured PSS activities, CPM, parenting skills, and early childhood development are provided to children and their parents, identifying children with vulnerabilities to be referred to CP case managers. All the mobile staff will be trained on emergency plan and therefore ready to expand coverage in order to conduct rapid protection assessment and protection monitoring to identify risks faced by WMBGs, providing emergency service package for newly displaced households. persons with disabilities and older persons, persons with protection concerns; particularly woman- and child-headed households or recent IDPs are served through integrated community-based approach. 9 - Protection Risk Analysis

There are numerous risks, but we can mention the main risks below:Risk 1: Exposure to COVID-19 by staff while working at office or facility.Impact: High Mitigation Measures: Ensure the application of IPC protocols; implementation of triage system; social distancing within the health facilities and in waiting areas; ensure the availability of PPE. Risk 2: Lack of qualified staff and/or suppliers in areas of implementation.Impact: ModerateMitigation Measures: SRD has worked in NWS since 2011 and is very well positioned to implement the project. The project area is currently considered a relatively stable area, and SRD has access to big markets and various suppliers in the area of implementation. Risk 3: Restrictions of movements and forced quarantine measures are reportedly impeding survivors’ access to services and significantly impacting survivor’s individual safety plan during COVID-19 Impact: HighMitigation Measures: Continuing to work with individual cases especially critical cases; follow up with cases physically or remotely as needed during this stage of the response and develop safety plans with the survivor in addition to the preparation of personal safety bag for the survivor Risk 4: children, ado and PWDs face challenges in accessing the centers to attend sessions.Impact: MediumMitigation Measures: A designated vehicle will be provided to facilitate access for elderly, adolescents, and persons with disabilities to the centers.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The likelihood of Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups and its impact is rated high protection risk analysis. Moreover, Exploitation by service provider or by other actor in the community. And low knowledge on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report. SRD has policies, procedures, and other measures in place that relate to sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment. Sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment are never acceptable, under any circumstances and SRD has a zero-tolerance policy. SRD recognizes that achieving a significant reduction in SEAH is a long-term mission. Zero tolerance is not the same as zero incidents. Reports of incidents may increase as organizations improve safeguards and reporting channels. Increasing reports may indicate a growing awareness of SEAH and changing attitudes, with survivors feeling more comfortable to report and organisations more likely to take action. The reporting of incidents and responses is an indicator that the risk of SEAH is being managed. Such measures taken are train staff/service providers regularly with context appropriate information on code of conduct and PSEA, Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation reporting ways. Establish good relation with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles and stick to them. SRD is an active member in UN SEA Network.
