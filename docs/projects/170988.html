Project Summary

The project addresses main problem “limited access to GBV multisectoral services for most vulnerable women & girls GBV survivors and GBV survivors with cancer (SWC) amidst COVID-19.” It responds to time critical needs identified in needs assessment conducted on 24- 29/9/2020: MHPSS services like group & individual PSS, specialized mental health & couple therapy; legal counselling & court representation; SRH; case management; economic enablement training & support for affected businesses; cash assistance; dignity/hygiene kits; awareness raising & information materials; emergency helpline; assistive mobility & hearing aids for WWDs/GWDs; diagnosis, medication & rehabilitation for GBV SWC; capacity building for front line staff of GBV actors; communication, community engagement & advocacy to raise women's voices on their humanitarian needs/gaps.
Its overall objective is "Improved protection & respect of rights of most vulnerable women>18-59 and >59 years & girls 14-17 years GBV survivors and increased their access to safe, gender responsive & inclusive multi-sectoral GBV services based on GBV Minimum Standards,” and outcomes are: 1. Most vulnerable women/ girls GBV survivors and GBV SWC manifested well-being, legal protection, good health, met basic needs & knowledge; 2. GBV actors have capacity to implement GBV prevention & response amidst COVID-19; 3. Duty bearers sensitized & increasingly act to protect most vulnerable women & girls GBV survivors and GBV SWC.
The project targets directly 8521 beneficiaries (4772 women, 495 men, 3109 girls, 145 boys, out of them 852 PwDs) identified based on vulnerability criteria: with disabilities; chronically ill, IDPs, refugees, pregnant/lactating women, FHHs, GBV survivors & below poverty line according to their location, status, protection risks & needs severity. Benificiaries are selected from North (Jabalya- Izbat Abed Rabbo); Gaza (Gaza City- Zaitoon); Deir Balah (Zahra); KhanYounis (Um Kilab) & ARAs in coordination with GBV WG.
The project links to HNO 2021, Gender Alert, HRP Strategic Objective # 1, PC Objective # 2, eligible actions & standard/ custom indicators.
Partners introduce an effective partnership that consolidate their experience in geographical & thematic areas and coordinate with local CBOs to address critical needs of women & girls GBV survivors & GBV SWC. Activities are: A) GBV multisectoral services like group PSS to 720 women, 240 girls & 192 SWC; individual PSS & awareness raising to 360 women,120 girls & 96 SWC; awareness raising to 447 men & 129 boys; specialized mental health to 27 women, 9 girls & 21 SWC; couple therapy to 120 women & 48 SWC; REMOTE individual legal counselling to 120 women, 30 girls & 60 SWC; court representation to 51 women, 12 girls & 12 SWC; health care to 180 women, 72 girls & 60 SWC; dignity kits to 180 women, 30 girls & 96 SWC; economic enablement training & support to 21 affected businesses; cash assistance to 100 women & 50 SWC; case management to 30 women & 12 SWC; B) capacity building to 120 staff of GBV actors & partners on self-care; remote counselling & case management, Minimum Standards for GBV & PSEA policy; 5Ws and GBVIMS to effectively/ safely collect, store, analyze and share data to ensure needs met without gaps/ duplication and advance GBV programming & advocacy and monthly supervision for partners’ staff; C) communication, accountability and advocacy like: 1 radio ad, 6 press releases, 9 community meetings, electronic pamphlet, 1 fact sheet; 3 position papers; feedback/ complaint mechanism; lessons learned; PSEA & digital advocacy initiative.
Partners ensure strategic relevance; gender/age difference; mainstreaming protection in COVID-19 context via prioritizing safety/dignity and avoid doing harm; respect informed consent, privacy & confidentiality; meaningful access to services for PwDs; participation & empowerment; facilities are safe for benificiaries & staff and adhere to COVID-19 precautionary safety measures.

1 - Needs

Partners conducted needs assessment on 24-29/9/2020 using remote modalities (online platforms & social media) and concluded participants’ differentiated needs in alignment with the HNO 2021 and recent Gender Alert to be addressed by this project: 1) phone calls for 20 women18-59 and>59 years (chronically ill like diabetic, hypertensive); FHHs (widows, divorced, suspended); IDPs, pregnant/ lactating women, refugees, living below poverty line, women small farmers, out of them 10 are WWDs); 10 adolescent girls<14- 17 years; and calls of women 18-59 and>59 years and girls<14- 17 years via partners’ helplines asking PSS and legal support. Those women and girls explained that they were subjected to psychological, physical, sexual, social & economic violence; lack of awareness on their rights and GBV services; denied rights and increased risk to COVID-19 infection. They expressed their main problem as limited access to specialized GBV multisectoral services amidst COVID- 19 pandemic. They requested partners to meet their critical needs: 1) MHPSS services like group and individual PSS, specialized mental health and couple therapy; 2) individual legal counselling and court representation; 3) SRH & family planning; 4) emergency hotline to provide legal, psychological counselling & referral services towards LEAVING NO ONE BEHIND; 5) case management including cash assistance and shelter services to address sophisticated women cases with multiple problems; 6) economic enablement training & support for small business owners whose businesses have been impacted and financially struggling amidst COVID-19; 7) unconditional cash assistance to purchase basic needs; 8) dignity/hygiene kits including cleaning and disinfection products; 9) awareness raising and information pamphlets on women's rights, reproductive health, GBV services and protection mechanisms and COVID-19 protection measures; and 10) advocacy campaign to raise women's humanitarian critical needs/gaps. WWDs/ GWDs have additional needs including assistive aids and adapted infrastructure to facilities.2) phone calls for 10 men> 18 years and 10 adolescent boys 14-17 years from families of targeted women and girls who expressed need for awareness raising on women’s rights and GBV services. 3) WhatsApp groups for 100 women 18-59 and>59 years and girls<14- 17 years GBV survivors with cancer (SWC) who reflected additional needs like diagnosis, medication, rehabilitation and primary health care kits4) Zoom meetings targeting frontline staff (women and men 24-55 years) of GBV specialized actors like case managers, psychologists, lawyers & health workers and women and men humanitarian staff (non-GBV specialists, 24-55 years). Frontline GBV staff explained that they were affected by working with GBV survivors which can be traumatic and result in vicarious or secondary trauma. They need training on: self-care and safety to manage stress, support better job performance and overall well-being; remote service delivery, contingency planning and IASC Minimum Standards for GBV; mainstreaming protection of benificiaries in the context of COVID-19, while partners’ staff need monthly supervision and psychological support; training on PSEA policy and feedback, response and complaint mechanisms.Humanitarian staff (non-GBV specialists) need training in initial detection and referral. Men and boys requested awareness raising on women's rights, GBV and protection mechanisms. 5) GBV needs in HNO 2021 indicates that COVID-19 related restrictions have exacerbated prevalence of GBV, including domestic violence, sexual abuse and forced marriage since March 2020. COVID-related measures have resulted in lack of available, acceptable and accessible services to GBV survivors, lack of quality GBV care and functioning referral system. Main response gaps include fragmented and insufficient GBV systems, suspension of face-to-face interactions, paralysis of judicial system and the reduced access to primary health care centers and shelters. WWDs survivors of GBV, have been particularly affected by these gaps. As the crisis continues, there is a need to scale up emergency GBV services, build capacity in remote delivery, and support frontline teams with stress management and psychological support.6) OCHA (2020) reflected in its monthly reporting system 65,123 registered GBV cases during 2017-2020 while only 52.2% treated GBV cases, 10.6% were referred to another facility and 8.2% of cases did not receive any services due to lack of resources and funding. 7) Gender Alert report (August 2020): identified vulnerable groups facing critical problems relating to access to essential services in Gaza as women GBV victims/ survivors, WWDs, pregnant/lactating women, FHHs, living below poverty line & refugees. 8) PCBS (2020) indicated that women experienced psychological, economic, social, physical and sexual violence at 63.5%, 55%, 46.7%, 26.4%, 10.6% respectively.9) UN Women (2020) found that 92% of women’s micro/ small businesses are negatively affected by pandemic. So, crisis may erode households’ coping mechanisms & subject families to further vulnerabilities.10) WAC recent researches: impact of COVID-19 on GBV, economic violence and women owned micro-businesses recommended cash-based assistance for home-based micro/small business owners affected by lockdowns to strengthen their resilience.

2 - Activities

Partners implement activities while mainstreaming protection in COVID-19 context via prioritizing safety/ dignity & avoid doing harm; respecting informed consent, privacy & confidentiality; ensuring meaningful access to services mainly for PwDs; promoting accountability, participation & empowerment; PSEA policy. Facilities are safe for in-person & group PSS/ awareness sessions via cleaning/sterilization, staff training in hygiene measures, masks, measuring temperatures, physical distancing & isolating suspected cases in a specific room. Activities are: - Conduct structured group PSS sessions for 72 groups including 54 women groups targeting 864 women (87 WWDs)>18-59 and >59 years) and 18 girls groups targeting 288 girls (27 GWDs) <14-17 years GBV survivors including with disabilities, chronically ill, IDPs, FHHs & GBV survivors with cancer- SWC. Every group has 16 participants and receives 6 group PSS sessions using COVID-19 safety measures based on WAC's PSS to GBV survivor's manual.- Carry out REMOTE monthly supervision & psychosocial support sessions targeting partners' 15 GBV project staff. - Conduct awareness raising sessions on women’s rights & GBV services for 447 women (45 WWDs) & 129 girls (12 GWDs) GBV survivors including with disabilities, chronically ill, IDPs, FHHs & SWC. - Conduct awareness raising sessions on women’s rights & GBV services for 447 men>18-59 and >59 years (45 PwDs) and 129 boys 14-17 years (12 PwDs) from families of targeted women & girls. - Provide REMOTE individual PSS counselling to 456 women (45 WWDs) & 129 girls (12 GWDs) GBV survivors including with disabilities, chronically ill, IDPs, FHHs & SWC according to WAC's manual.- Provide in-person specialized mental health treatment to 110 traumatized women and girls (95 women (10 WWDs) & 15 girls (1 GWD)) of high-risk cases of suicide and CMR, which requires applying COVID-19 minimum safety measures. - Provide REMOTE couple therapy sessions to 168 couples of targeted women survivors of GBV including with disabilities, chronically ill, IDPs, FHHs & SWC. - Provide REMOTE individual legal counselling to 180 women (18 WWDs)>18-59 and >59 years, 30 early married girls (3 GWD) <18 years on inheritance, marriage, divorce, alimony & child custody. - Provide legal representation in courts to 63 women (3 WWDs) and 15 early married girls (3 GWDs). - Provide emergency SRH services to 180 women (18 WWDs) and 72 girls (6 GWDs) GBV survivors. - Provide screening, diagnostic, treatment, home visits and rehabilitation to 60 GBV SWC (54 women and 6 girls) at RCS4GS. It is a mix of REMOTE counselling and in-person services, while applying COVID-19 minimum safety measures. - Provide dignity/hygiene kits (including women basic needs & cleaning and disinfection products) at homes to 276 women and 30 girls GBV survivors and GBV SWC. - Provide economic enablement training & support at 400$/business for 21 small business owners whose businesses have been impacted and financially struggling amidst COVID-19. - Provide unconditional cash assistance at 100$/women to 150 women GBV survivors including with disabilities, chronically ill, IDPs, FHHs & SWC. - Provide REMOTE case management services to 45 women GBV survivors and GBV SWC including: dignity/ hygiene kits, individual PSS counselling, specialized mental health treatment, couple therapy, individual legal counselling, court representation, social services, assistive mobile tools and referral to Hayat shelter services. - Conduct REMOTE 8- hour training on self- care for 120 (90 women and 30 men) front line staff from GBV actors and project partners in 12 groups. - Conduct REMOTE 15- hour training for 120 front line staff from GBV actors on remote service delivery, contingency planning & GBV Minimum Standards. - Conduct 9- hour training for 60 humanitarian staff (non-GBV specialists) in initial detection and referral. - Carry out REMOTE 6-hour training on PSEA measures indicated in UN Secretary-General’s SEA Bulletin ST/SGB/2003/13 for 180 (90 women & 30 men of front line staff from GBV actors and 60 staff of law enforcement authorities). - Use 5Ws and GBVIMS by partners for gathering, maintaining and analyzing data related to GBV, drafting reports, providing strategic directions to GBV programmes & advocacy. - Undertake periodical monitoring and coordination with GBV humanitarian key actors. Systematize & document lessons learned & good practices from community engagement mechanisms used by partners, like feedback and complaints mechanisms, community meetings and produce a joint briefing note identifying key recommendations for upscaling/replicating community-based engagement GBV programs. - Participate in different meetings in GBV using remote modalities with institutions and within PC & GBV sub-working group. - Broadcast 1 radio ad in local radio station & 6 press releases to explain the project to affected communities. - Organize 9 REMOTE community meetings at beginning of project involving 45 girls, 45 boys, 45 women & 45 men to explain project’s objective, outcomes and support of GBV survivors & GBV SWC. - Publish & disseminate electronic pamphlet directly to 5000 women & 3000 girls using WAC Facebook page on COVID-19 prevention and control, rights of WWDs, SRH, GBV risks and services. - Elaboration & dissemination of 1 fact sheet and 3 position papers. - Implement feedback, complaints & response mechanisms (FCRM) amid COVID-19 related restrictions on movement and access to communities using helplines, a special email (RM.complaints@wac.org), or SMS messages at 059934319, or phone calls at 2877312/ 2877313. - Prevent and respond to sexual exploitation & abuse at partners and CBOs’ staff level vis-a-vis beneficiaries. - Conduct pre & post evaluation for project activities and end of project evaluation. - Organize a digital advocacy campaign to raise humanitarian critical needs/gaps for most vulnerable women & girls GBV survivors and GBV SWC.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

- The location of partners’ facilities and the routes to them are safe and distant from the risk or threat of GBV.- Partners have infrastructure adaptations including fitting ramps and railings to facilities so that all PwDs can access and use the facilities in safety and with dignity.- Partners will provide WWDs/GWDs with assistive mobile devices and women/girls with hearing disabilities assistive hearing devices.- Partners will provide sign language interpretation to benificiaries with hearing disabilities.- Partners will respect informed consent, confidentiality and privacy in all forms of consultation, counseling and personal information sharing. - Partners’ GBV specialists will maintain the highest levels of professionalism in receiving and counselling GBV survivors.- Partners ensure that their facilities and office arrangements are safe for the beneficiaries and staff, and they adhere to minimum precautionary safety measures, including physical distancing and hygiene promotion, as defined by MoH.- Partners train GBV specialists on applying PSEA policy and complaint mechanism.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

- Partners provide GBV services at an adequate time when all targeted women and girls can realistically access them.- Partners facilitate access to GBV services for persons with reduced mobility (e.g. persons with physical disabilities, the elderly, bed-ridden individuals) via making access paths smooth and fit ramps for wheelchair access.- Partners facilitate access to GBV services for persons with non-mobility related disabilities (e.g. the blind, hard of hearing, intellectually disabled) via providing information about services in both verbal and written forms and developing pictorial representations and explanations.- Partners raise awareness of direct beneficiaries about their rights and GBV services.- Partners monitor access and discrimination through disaggregated indicators (# of individuals accessing services) by age, gender, and location or specific group (e.g. persons with disabilities).- Partners use their helplines to facilitate access of women and girls in remote communities (e.g. ARAs) to GBV services towards implementing UN 2030 agenda of “LEAVING NO ONE BEHIND” and to “endeavor to reach the furthest behind first”.- Partners adapt activities in line with hygiene and safety measures.- Partners involve women CBOs and local communities in these localities to keep up implementation of project activities during all times including times of crisis.- Partners’ access approach is consistent with the humanitarian principles (impartiality, neutrality, independence and humanity) in accordance with IHL. Partners apply: 1) Humanity in addressing the human suffering of most vulnerable women and girls; 2) Neutrality so that humanitarian aid must not favor any political side; 3) Impartiality in providing services to women and girls solely on the basis of need and without discrimination; and 4) Independence in advancing the autonomy of humanitarian project objectives from political, economic or other objectives.
