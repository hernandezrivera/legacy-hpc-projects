Project Summary

World Vision will continue supporting A’zaz camps and informal settlements:
1. Levelling and graveling in camps and informal settlements in A’zaz (10,000 individuals)
2. Repair and extend drainage systems in A'zaz camps and informal settlements (10,000 individuals).
3. Distribute operational information on IDP movements to CCCM cluster in a timely basis
4. Set up Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) in the project’s geographical target areas to capture HLP issues before they escalate.
5. Perform regular monitoring with a gender balanced monitoring team, which will specifically target vulnerable groups, including female-headed households, to ensure the needs and concerns of the most vulnerable are taken into consideration
6. Prepare and submit accurate and timely reports
*Please note that comprehensive WASH activities in camps and informal settlements provided by WV in A’zaz has been reported under the WASH cluster and all winterization activities has been reported under SNFI project.

Needs

Hostilities and deprivation of basic rights continue to fuel large-scale displacement in Syria. During the past year, IDP sites in northwest Syria have had to quickly adapt to new arrivals and developments on the ground, in turn witnessing a significant increase in population numbers. Between September 2017, and August 2018, the CCCM cluster has tracked almost 1.1 million population movements across northern Syria – a significant increase from the 804,300 reported in the 2018 HNO. During this time period, an estimated 18% of IDPs resorted to, or transited through, IDP sites. Across Syria IDP site population increased by an estimated 16 per cent, with such increases witnessed almost uniformly across all parts of the country. To cope with sudden displacements, the CCCM cluster has actively promoted the establishment of new Reception Centers for sudden displacements in Idleb and northern Aleppo governorates. These centers have hosted 42,034 IDPs since September 2017.The majority of these IDPs live in crowded temporary shelters lacking basic needs and who have insufficient clothing. This leaves those most vulnerable (women, children and newborn babies) at high risk of disease and malnutrition, especially as Maternal, Newborn, Child Health and Nutrition (MNCHN) services have collapsed. Projects are therefore crucial to survival, especially in winter months.A Space of Hope (SoH) study conducted in November 2018, provides insight into the main protection concerns and issues in North Aleppo for child, adolescent and women. In the assessed villages, 73% of respondents believe that there is a community committee who provide the basic services (such as water, sanitation, waste removal…etc) and ensure these services are available and functioning sufficiently. However, 23% of respondents reported no presence of this committee in their villages, leaving a large gap to respond to basic needs. Received data from the study also showed that the percentage of IDPs to Hosts was high in areas such as Azaz and Souran, due the high waves of displacement in the last 8 months, adding extra strain to basic services. The increase in population coupled with the unstable situation has also led to increased safety and security concerns, particularly for women, such as sexual exploitation. In the target area, and more generally across Northern Syria, women can be understood to have a subordinated role in society with poorer access to services and resources; and are more commonly subject to physical and gender-based violence and sexual violence (HNO, 2018). Women’s decision-making power is low at all levels, making the exercising basic rights (e.g. access to life saving WASH, health and social services; registering for humanitarian assistance; or claiming their property to secure shelter) extremely difficult. Boys are also under threat of GBV and sexual exploitation and older boys and young men are at risk of being recruited by armed groups and criminal gangs. The children and youth of Syria have also become collateral damage in a situation beyond their control. A report by the Global Child Protection Working Group, the global level forum for coordination on child protection in humanitarian settings, indicated that 98 percent of Syrian children surveyed reported deterioration in their psychosocial well-being and humanitarian agencies are warning of a potential “lost generation”. Further to this, a War Child report writes that the provision of support for the psychological and social well-being and recovery of children – ‘psychosocial support’ – is being overlooked, despite confirmed evidence that psychosocial support during emergencies is critical. In February 2018, World Vision spoke to 1,254 Syrian girls and boys; their stories included war-induced violence, displacement, and missing, separated and deceased family members.

Implementation of activities

Providing streamlined life-saving, humanitarian multi-sectoral assistance and strengthening the basic infrastructure support in IDP sites.Providing operational information on IDP movements in a timely manner in order to adequately respond to needs. The project will be implemented directly through WV staff, who have many years experience working in this area, building robust communication channels with IDPs, camps management and local councils. Activities will be only in Azaz with direct implementation, targeting 25,000 beneficiaries. Activities will include. Repair and extend drainage systems 10,000 individualsGravelling and levelling projects for camp sites 10,000 individualsEmergency WASH Support (water trucking, latrines, hygiene kits) 25,000 individuals (overlap)All staff have been hired form target communities in Syria consisting of engineers, assistants and hygiene promoters. WV has found this builds better acceptance from the community and increases WVs understanding of essential needs of those communities.All infrastructure activities will be implemented through local contractors with daily site monitoring by field engineers.

Protection Risk Analysis

There is a risk that intentions surveys, assessments will raise unrealistic expectations among IDPs living in the RCs and camps and the support that can be realistically provided for assisted movements, in addition to services, this can be mitigated by ensure that the intention surveys are as light as possible and done at registration or shortly after and ensuring that healthy communication with the people of concern to avoid raising unrealistic expectation. As much as possible inform IDPs of the intention survey purpose, noting that there is no guarantee of support on movements or return, and that IDP need to make voluntary and informed decisions on their future. Consent does not need to be in writing, but will cover all these elements. WVI will try to employ women enumerators to achieve gender balanced teams. Protection risk: There is a risk that IDPs will interpret intentions surveys and data gathering/information packages (service mapping, security, etc) on various districts as an indication that they are able to return or move to an area without properly review and analysis of the information. According to the CCCM protection risk analysis, the proposed activities might increase risk of inter-community tension between Collective Centre residents and host communities due in part to unequal level of assistance given to Collective Centre residents in comparison to host communities, this risk can be mitigated by ensuring that the Collective Centre are all needs based, and that CC residents are amongst the most vulnerable, and where possible, WVI and its partner will provide a wider services including IDPs and HC.3. If a collective centre has dislodged a key community infrastructure or service, WVI and its partner will arrange a replacement service or a structured phase out at the earliest possible time. Moreover, if tension within the camps raised in terms of services. WVI will monitor the situation, trends and involved community committees in the decision making process and consult beneficiaries on priorities to mitigate this risk IDP movement will be monitored through CCCM and REACH reports and other WVI direct channels however, when IDPs fled to areas where CCCM members are not present and thus have no access to service, WVI will contribute to the collective IDP tracking and concentrations during sudden displacements. With the creation of community committee, the management structures could include actors who are not truly representative of the IDP communities. WVI will ensure that management structures are inclusive to different populations ensure that any management structure is representative of the entire community with strong selection criteria Safe and confidential complaints and response mechanisms would also be in place to respond to any reported incidents of intimidation, violence and sexual exploitation (that have been committed by community members and/or staff). Gender balance teams will be considered to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the camp/ RC areas, this will reduce the potential for exploitation by staff, as well as providing a greater opportunity for protection issues to be picked up by a broader range of staff with different expertise.

Do no harm is ensured through intense monitoring and evaluation but also thorough coordination with stakeholders. Firstly, this project involved the beneficiaries by establishing the IDP camp management committees. These committees will be able to channel a feedback mechanism to WV staff and ensure that activities are constantly evaluated and adapted to the needs of the IDPs. Pre-implementation and post-implementation monitoring will be conducted to assess the impact of the project and WV will conduct systematic post-distribution monitoring to evaluate the satisfaction of beneficiaries and act upon the level of satisfaction if necessary. At an inter-agency level, WV, will continue to coordinate with the CCCM cluster and will ensure continued coordination with other NGOs operating in the same areas. Coordination with local authorities will also ensure do no harm and ensure that WV understands the local context.
