Project Summary

The conflict in Syria has created and exacerbated large-scale humanitarian needs around water, sanitation, and hygiene. 10.7 million people are still in need of access to safe water (53% of the population), with 28% considered in acute need and exposed to life threatening health risks. Up to 42% of the population relies on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their daily needs. Water quality is poor, increasing the risk of contracting water borne diseases. The combination of lack of rehabilitation and poor maintenance of destroyed or damaged water and sanitation systems affects equal and safe access to basic WASH services for returnees and host communities, leading to an increased dependency on water trucking. Access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies remain a challenge for newly or protracted displaced populations. Women are disproportionately affected by a lack of access to WASH services and forced to adopt inadequate coping strategies. IDPs leaving in formal or informal settlements remain highly dependent on continuous humanitarian assistance including for WASH services, whose disruptions would have direct negative impact on their physical and mental well-being and protection consequences, specifically for women and girls. Poor hygiene conditions lead to disease outbreaks, especially in areas of high concentration of IDPs and camps. Moreover, gender-specific sanitation needs are considerably higher among displaced people compared to the rest of the population, with over-crowding, cleanliness and protection concerns.
Considering the forecasted contextual scenario combined with the severity of needs, AAH aims at achieving an impact in the short-term, while reducing the vulnerability of targeted communities in the medium to long-term increasing the access to sustained and equitable WASH basic services, thus promoting the right to dignity and safety. AAH will implement life-saving and life-sustaining activities through repair and maintenance of water and sanitation systems coupled with emergency WASH services and distribution of NFIs - including water trucking, distribution of jerry cans and gender-specific hygiene kits, installation of water tanks, provision of de-sludging/latrine cleaning services, SWM and rehabilitation/maintenance of sanitation facilities, including in schools and PHCs. AAH will prioritize the maintenance and/or rehabilitation of existing water supply systems (water stations, boreholes, networks), including water quality testing. Given the lack or irregular distribution of electricity in many parts of Syria, gen-sets and/or Solar Photo Voltaic systems might be provided as main or complimentary source of power. Rehabilitation works of sewage systems, including waste-water plants and sewage networks, will also be conducted to prevent public health risks and the contamination of water and soil due to the discharge of raw sewage. Communities with no access to water and populations affected by new or protracted displacements will be supported with emergency WASH services such as, water trucking, the distribution of jerry cans, and the installation of communal water tanks. AAH will also protect the health and hygienic environment through the rehabilitation and/or the maintenance of WASH facilities (including in schools and PHCs) and the provision of services such as de-sludging and solid waste management. Emergency hygiene kits will be distributed in collaboration with SARC, coupled, whenever possible, with hygiene promotion awareness sessions. AAH can cover needs across all Syria, with priority to 8 Governorates (Dar’a, Rural Damascus, Hama, Homs, Aleppo, Raqqa, Deir ez Zor, Hassakeh), in benefit of 820,000 vulnerable conflict-affected host communities, returnees and IDPs, i.e. 213,760 men, 243,840 women, 168,800 boys and 175,600 girls. AAH response is in line with the 1st and 2nd 2020 WASH strategic pillars.

4 - Needs

The operating environment in Syria continues to be complex and demanding. Humanitarian needs are expected to remain high as consequence of the evolving scenarios. Assessments conducted by AAH confirm how the combination of lack of rehabilitation and poor maintenance of destroyed or damaged water and sanitation systems significantly affects equal and safe access to basic WASH services for returnees and host communities as well as for newly or protracted displaced populations, particularly those living in IDP sites. The reduced discharge of wells, the decreased productivity of water stations and the damages to the distribution systems have created an increased dependency on water trucking in many communities. Women are disproportionately affected by a lack of access to WASH services and no means to manage the simple biological necessities of defecation and menstruation, thus are forced to adopt inadequate coping strategies. IDPs leaving in formal or informal settlements remain highly dependent on continuous humanitarian assistance including for WASH services, whose disruptions would have direct negative impact on their well-being and protection consequences, specifically for women and girls. At least 80% of sewage is discharged untreated while at least half of the sewage systems are either not or only partially functional; wastewater accumulates in the streets while breeding insects and rodents, exposing the communities to public health risks; approximately 26% of garbage is inappropriately disposed. Poor hygiene conditions lead to disease outbreaks, especially in areas of high concentration of IDPs and camps. Moreover, gender-specific sanitation needs are considerably higher among displaced people living in informal settlements and camps compared to the rest of the population, with over-crowding, cleanliness and protection concerns. Groups with specific WASH needs include children under U2, women of reproductive age and people with disabilities.

5 - Implementation of activities

AAH aims at achieving an impact in the short-term by providing life-saving emergency WASH interventions, while reducing the vulnerability of targeted communities in the medium to long-term increasing the access to sustained and equitable WASH basic services, thus promoting the right to dignity and safety. AAH has capacity and resources to cover the needs across all Syria, with priority to eight Governorates (Dar’a, Rural Damascus, Hama, Homs, Aleppo, Raqqa, Deir ez Zor and Hassakeh), in benefit of vulnerable and conflict-affected host communities, returnees and IDPs. AAH will prioritize life sustaining WASH interventions through maintenance and/or rehabilitation of existing water supply systems (water stations, boreholes and networks), including extension of existing infrastructure. Through the rehabilitations, beneficiaries will have increased and equal access to safe water to meet their domestic and drinking requirements. Water quality testing will be carried by AAH WASH team in collaboration with the Directorate of Water. Rehabilitation works of sewage systems, including waste-water plants and sewage networks, will be conducted to prevent public health risks and the contamination of water and soil due to the discharge of raw sewage. Communities with no access to water and populations affected by new or protracted displacements, will be supported with emergency WASH services such as, water trucking, distribution of jerry cans, and installation of communal water tanks. AAH will also protect the health and hygienic environment through the rehabilitation and/or the maintenance of WASH facilities (including in schools and PHCs) and the provision of services such as de-sludging and solid waste management. Emergency hygiene kits will be distributed in collaboration with SARC, responsible for the distribution in the camps as well as at community level; whenever possible, the distribution will be coupled with the implementation of hygiene promotion awareness sessions.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: inequality in distribution; physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; timing and process of distribution put at risk of GBV. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: promote establishment of management plan; promote engagement with communities in distribution planning and feedback on package for improvement; training of staff and enforcement of code of PSEA among partner staff. Protection risk: water emergency supply, quality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards. Water distribution not safe or accessible, especially for women, girls, children, elderly and people with disabilities; inequality in distribution Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: ensure provision of safe water as per AAH quality standards and establish regular water quality monitoring including water source, water trucks and end-user samples; collect water from sources certified by water authorities or local councils. Consult separately girls, boys, women, men, older people and those with disabilities; make sure that distance of water collection points remains at minimum. Protection risk: toilets represent GBV or health safety risk; access to toilet is not available to all 24/7. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: ensure agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation. Ensure women and adolescent girls are consulted during design, implementation and monitoring phase of projects. Accountability mechanisms are put in place and feedback from users is followed up. Protection risk: potential exposure to explosive hazard during assessment or rehabilitation of water and/or wastewater infrastructure. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: work with local authorities and conduct assessments and/or rehabilitation works only in areas cleared; monitor and check with mine action actors on hazard contamination/infrastructure clearance; conduct safety training with stuff and contractors.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: security situation allows safe response; access to target areas and beneficiaries is granted; partners are willing to collaborate on timely manner; availability of suppliers and items in the market, prices remain stable; no major displacements occur. Risk: security situation is unstable and/or worsens, AAH has no access; there are additional administrative procedures imposed on D-INGOs, and restrictions on access to beneﬁciaries; lengthy processes to obtain approvals. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: AAH coordinates with local authorities, Ministries, and stakeholders to monitor the situation, to maintain access to target areas and beneﬁciaries, to develop relationships and avoid or mitigate restrictions. Security rules and plans are in place and regularly reviewed. Re-planning of activities to recover eventual delays. Advocacy and coordination with the humanitarian community. Risk: significant devaluation of EURO/ USD against SYP; International bank transfers are not ﬂuid and timely. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: signiﬁcant changes in the value of the Syrian currency will be monitored and, if materialized, communicated to donors and options discussed. Risk: aid diversion, financial fraud, and corruption. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: strict policies and procedures in place for procurement, financial management and risk management; internal controls set up. Monthly monitoring of financial spending. Risk: markets and services are not functional for planned deliveries; procurement of equipment/materials are delayed. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: diversify procurement according to AAH guidelines. Launch framework agreements or tenders for medium time frames. Risk: any kind of abuse (sexual, power, etc.). Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: compulsory trainings on organizational ethical policies. AAH mission has a policy for PSEA and a reference person against harassment at country level.
