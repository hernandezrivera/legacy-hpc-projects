Project Summary

"The main objective of the project is to improve protection, safety, and security and enhance access to justice and peaceful coexistence between refugees and host communities in additional 6 camps in Cox’s Bazar, with particular attention to vulnerable groups such as youth, women, elderly, and people with disabilities. The project will provide a forum through which refugees and host communities will have improved access to protection services and conflict resolution mechanisms, as well as access to legal assistance and victim support services. The expected results will reinforce the capacities of actors that are currently working on community safety and security, justice and protection in both refugee and host communities, focusing on addressing crime, promoting women’s empowerment, and preventing and responding to SGBV more inclusively and sustainably under national legal and local administrative structures and refugee protection standards. The project will focus on establishing community safety forums to enable partnership and dialogue to develop local solutions to problems identified jointly by refugee and host communities and strengthen different forms of social accountability. The project will be jointly implemented by UNDP, UNHCR, and IOM in close collaboration with the RRRC and Camp in Charge officials, APBn, District Police, and local government institutions in Cox’s Bazar. Specifically, interventions focus on capacity building, access to justice, and raising awareness where different gender and age groups will be included. The implementing agencies will ensure that men, women, youth, and people with disabilities are appropriately represented in community consultations, implementation, and project field staff. The project will also demonstrate the capacity to integrate gender considerations into the design, beneficiary targeting, and implementation. Further the project will work closely with UNHCR and IOM Protection sector and staff in the camps, SGBV staff and Camp Management. As the camp is comparatively more difficult for the vulnerable population specifically the elderly, women, children and persons with specific needs, the project aims to create a supportive environment for all the beneficiaries where the vulnerable people will feel more comfortable and find a favorable environment to be in peace.
In terms of participation and empowerment project combines top-down processes and institutions, including supporting police and justice actors, good governance, and strengthening accountability and social justice instruments, with a bottom-up focus in which inclusive and participatory processes support the important roles of individuals and communities as actors in defining and implementing their essential freedoms. For disaster preparedness, agencies will work with a range of partners, including other Sectors, community-based protection networks in the camps, and relevant authorities as appropriate to the situation to identify the threats and support the people in need. Agencies will verify the needs of EVIs, coordinate services, and support other partners to mitigate harm during disaster response. The agencies will work closely with protection staff and partners to support outreach sessions for Rohingya and host community members, camp authorities and partners to raise awareness on available protection services. The three agencies UNDP, UNHCR, and IOM will build the capacities of Law Enforcing Agency members to sensitize them on GBV, Protection, Counter-Trafficking, Overcoming Cultural Barriers in communicating with Refugee Community, and Ensure Dignity and Respect of the Camp dwellers."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Nearly all Rohingya refugees living in the camps in Cox’s Bazar depend on humanitarian services for their survival. They are confronted with limited opportunities for livelihoods and complex and intersecting financial pressures despite efforts by the Government of Bangladesh and humanitarian agencies to provide for their basic needs. They also face a range of safety issues theft, lack of lighting, drug use and drug trafficking, human smuggling and trafficking in persons, disputes over aid distribution and gender-based violence.IOM will collect information on prioritized needs by the community through FGD, KII and community consultation meetings, including bilateral meetings with the Camp in Charge and APBN commander in IOM protection-led camps covered with this project.Through the pilot project on Community Safety in 2020 and 2021 three agencies conducted Community safety baseline FGDs in 2 pilot camps, which additionally emphasized the need for 24/7 presence of the police, different perceptions of safety and security, risks and mitigation measures between refugee men, women, boys, girls, camp leaders and camp administration. (e.g. women and girls face additional layers of violence as a result of the social culture of practices and lack of adequate response and crime report mechanisms. As the camp is comparatively more difficult for the vulnerable population specifically the elderly, women, children, and persons with specific needs, the project aims to create a supportive environment for all the beneficiaries where the vulnerable people will feel more comfortable and find a favorable environment to be in peace. Based on the assessment done by the Centre for Peace and Justice and The Asia Foundation in February 2021, refugees stated they need better access to civil justice for cases involving interpersonal disputes, divorce, domestic violence, and polygamy, among other issues, and in dealing with challenges around birth and marriage registration. The lack of access to civil justice is experienced by refugees as connected to a web of interrelated problems around social issues, safety and security, and broader human rights issues. One-third of respondents said they had been able to obtain legal support and found that support satisfactory. This is a sign of the effectiveness of efforts already undertaken by the NGOs and other stakeholders to strengthen refugees’ access to justice. But improvements and expansion of services are needed, as two-thirds of respondents said they could not access information, justice-related services, and expert help in the camps when needed. Many who did receive support and services found them inadequate to resolve problems. The lack of an adequate camp dispute resolution system and the absence of a national policy framework outlining the rights and responsibilities of refugees and displaced persons in Bangladesh are the two key areas impeding progress toward access to justice for Rohingya."

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

"IOM will ensure mainstreaming of protection throughout the implementation of the activities. “Do no harm” is the guiding principle for implementing activities. The privacy and confidentiality of the beneficiaries will be considered while designing and implementing the project. Beneficiaries will receive awareness-raising sessions to help them protect themselves from any unwanted incidents. IOM will promote a holistic lens, incorporating age, gender, and diversity needs of the affected population into programming through capacity building, training, and activity monitoring. IOM will ensure Protection mainstreaming in the service provision by providing training for APBn members to uphold the principles of safety and dignity, meaningful access, communication and participation and empowerment of community members of all age and gender. IOM will provide needs-based assistance while delivering culturally appropriate services and assistance, to avoid any unintended negative consequences. "

3.2 - Gender

"IOM activities will target all gender in the targeted camps. IOM will institutionalize and integrate gender into the entire spectrum of activities -projects, programs, and staff policies. For gender issues to be fully integrated into the Organization’s vision, all staff need to understand and implement the Strategy in their work. This mainstreaming process is not exclusively related to women and their specific needs and concerns. It also includes initiatives for men when needed, provided they promote overall gender equality. IOM will target 50% men and 50% women in the program activities. Gender-sensitive training will be provided to the beneficiaries. Since women are left behind in many societies, IOM will especially focus to empower women throughout the program."

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

IOM plans to include youth and adult community members as the direct beneficiary of community safety and the activities will indirectly benefit all the age groups of the targetted camps.

3.4 - Diversity

IOM is committed to integrating age and diversity in all aspects of the community in the project. The activities will try to benefit all the beneficiaries in need. IOM will especially consider the minority people in the community to mainstream them into society. The activities will be tailored to their specific needs based on the outcome of the needs assessment.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability remains a vital and integral component of this proposed project. The refugees in all the targeted locations will be consulted for their needs and priorities. All the beneficiaries will receive orientation on the Common Feedback Platform where they can provide any feedback on all the services and required need. Beneficiaries will be consulted during the baseline survey to understand their needs and thoughts. As people are direct participants of the project, they will be consulted throughout the project implementation process where they will be able to provide feedback. Also, IOM has a strong PSEA policy which includes periodical training of staff on PSEA and a smooth reporting platform to ensure accountability to the beneficiaries. They will also contribute to finding solutions for their improved security and cohesion with the host community.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

For disaster preparedness, agencies will work with a range of partners, including other Sectors, community-based protection networks in the camps, and relevant authorities as appropriate to the situation to identify the threats and support the people in need. Agencies will verify the needs of EVIs, coordinate services, and support other partners to mitigate harm during disaster response. The capacity building modules and community engagement will include environmental awareness activities including homestead gardening, vegetation, stop hill cutting, keeping the environment clean etc.
