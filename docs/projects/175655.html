Project Summary

CMC aims at provision of equitable access to safe and adequate water, dignified Sanitation, and safe hygiene practices to mitigate WASH related malnutrition and GBV through integrated WASH intervention mainstreaming protection, enhancing resilience, communities coping mechanisms and sustainability, targeting population with extreme needs under WASH vulnerability (PIN) 4 and 5 mainly disaster (Flood, Conflicts, Famine) affected population within the IPC 3, 4 and 5 to achieve cluster strategic objectives 1, 2 & 3 (SO 1:1, 1:2, 2:1, 2:2, 3:1) , and cluster objectives (CO1 and CO3) tackling 70% WASH-nut and 30% WASH-GBV in the areas of Pibor, Pochalla and Raja targeting crises affected population, IPC3 to IPC5 population, mainly IDPs, returnees and most vulnerable host communities in Likuangole, Gumuruk, Verteth, Manyibol town, Pochalla in Jonglei and Raja County in Western Bahr Al Ghazal to reduce morbidity and mortality resulting from WASH-related GBV and malnutrition within the affected population incorporating COVID 19 new normal and WASH cluster standard operation procedures. CMC focuses on lifesaving WASH activities mainstreaming protection, accountability to affected population (AAP) and strengthening the beneficiaries copping mechanism and building resilience through training of different groups of people in the community including; pump mechanics, Water management committee, women groups, community leaders, the AAP focal persons, teachers, pupils and nutrition workers on WASH practices. The respond will take into account close coordination with the WASH cluster EP&R partners, the FSL, Health, Nutrition and Protection cluster partners in order to deliver integrated, complementary and well-coordinated responses to mean the multi-sectoral needs of the population
The project targets 16,750 (Pibor=11,000, Raja=4100, Pochalla=1650 direct beneficiaries and 3,850 indirect beneficiaries facing IPC3&5and GAM ranging at 20.8%, the IDPs, most vulnerable host communities affected with flood, COVID 19, inclusive natural disasters, and malnutrition patients with limited access to WASH services and food insecurity, through supported coordination and complementation of WASH needs in the targeted areas, achieving Sphere standards, the core humanitarian standards and the WASH cluster objectives CO1 and CO3, and contributing to eradication of the IPC. Instigating WASH sustainable solution, enhancing cost-recovery mechanism through market integration and maintaining high level of quality infrastructures with adherence to conflict sensitivity analysis taking into account disability inclusion approaches and beneficiaries centered approaches throughout designing and undertaking all related activities in an aim to close gender gaps in WASH.
CMC aims at structured, effective preparedness and response programme through improving access to safe water, enhancing community awareness on safe hygiene and sanitation practices within population with limited access to WASH services and food insecurity, supporting institutional WASH Services through coordination with nutrition, protection and FSL partners to integrate WASH in their interventions by enhancing the knowledge of mothers and children with equitable WASH practices to mitigate WASH related GBV and malnutrition within the population. Enhancing sustainability and resilience by building capacities of the communities to cope with current shocks through sustaining livelihoods linkages with development action to promote ownership, participations, and accountability to affected people through beneficiaries centered approaches.
CMC will ensure that services delivered are gender-sensitive, considering people with special needs and disables by establishing strong feedback and complain mechanisms at all stages of the implementation, training of the AAP focal point dominated by the beneficiaries, and the CMC M&E team to ensure feedbacks and complains are addressed.

1 - Needs

Attributing to the protracted conflict in South Sudan, the intercommunal fights and natural disasters has caused erosion of physical and social infrastructure, death and displacement of hundreds of thousands of people in Jonglei State and Western Bahr Al Gazal. According to IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix, 89.7% of IDPs and 90.6% of returnees in host-community settings live in settlements without standard WASH facilities, compared to 30.8 per cent of IDPs living in camps and camp-like settings. Pibor areas in Jonglei and Raja in Western Bahr Al Gazal State are among the most affected with recurrent attacks often causing more displacements, death and destruction of infrastructure. 42.4% of IDPs and 48% of returnees live in settlements where the main water source distanced at least 40mins footing, while 32.7% of IDPs and 35.8 % of returnees live in settlements reporting insufficient. The national financial crises have worsened the situation. The prevalence of open defecation is over 40% in IDP and returnee settlements across the 10 states, with over 80% of IDPs and returnees in settlements. Mass displacement across the country affects the majority of the population, the WASH situation assessments alarms 32.7% of IDPs and 35.8% of returnees live in settlements reporting water for human drinking. 20.3% of IDPs, or 286,634 individuals, and 27.9% of returnees, or 383,957 individuals, live in settlements where people feel unsafe when they go to collect water. Renewed inter-tribal fighting, cattle raiding, and political frictions as a results of the boundaries conflicts in the mentioned counties early June 2019 displaced an estimated at least 11,723 people in Pibor and population returns in Pochalla according to IOM-DTM report. Despite the humanitarian community efforts to deliver life-saving services to the affected population, these have been limited as a results of COVID 19 pandemic restriction on movements and commodities in the markets as an erosion for catastrophic vulnerabilities.The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) ‘Key IPC Findings indicates: December 2020 to March 2021, an estimated 5.82 million people (48.3% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 11,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Pibor County. At the period, an estimated 1.79 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Between December 2020 and March 2021, the food security situation is expected to seasonally improve with an estimated 190,000 people (29.4% of the population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 153,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 37,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Similarly during this period, Raga and Wau counties are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, whereas Jur River County is classified in Stressed (IPC Phase 2) acute food insecurity. A key driver of the improvement in the food security situation is the availability of harvests. Although market prices will likely remain high across the state, and some COVID-related restrictions will remain in place, trade routes from Sudan and from other countries via Juba will become more accessible thus increasing availability of food commodities in the markets. Additionally, households will be able to sell and consume farm products, and the availability of casual labor opportunities will improve. The food security situation is expected to deteriorate in the lean season period of April to July 2021

2 - Project activities

Activity 1: Conducting needs assessments in areas of crisis prior to any response to determine the exact needs of the community and establish relevant recommendations. CMC will participate in IRNA assessment and conduct its own assessment and share reports with the WASH cluster and other partners. Activity 2: Providing timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations through repair and rehabilitations of water points, provision of water purifiers (PUR and aqua tabs), building capacities of local pump mechanics and water management committee to ensure sustainability and resilience, water quality analysis at water points, Households and public facilities (Nutrition centers, Schools etc.). Consideration will be made concerning the location of the water point through involving women in deciding for the area where the water point can be constructed to ensure secure access to women and girls. Activity 3: Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. Hygiene promotion activities like HH visits by trained Community Hygiene Promoters targeting key messages like hand washing at critical times, safe water chain, food hygiene and safe excreta disposal. CMC will also target schools and nutrition/health centers focusing on mothers who are taking care of children with malnutrition. Menstrual Hygiene Management will also be an area of focus considering the fact that MHM is still misunderstood in most communities. Activity 4: Enable vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and Hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services. At institution level like nutrition centers and schools, latrines will be constructed to cater for boys and girls, men and women separately. For the girls and latrines will be equipped with water disposal bins for disposing used sanitary pads and adequate space for changing and washing. Additional Households latrines will also be constructed as samples using local available materials for community to learn from. Activity 5: Mainstreaming cross-cutting issues across all activities with particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to affected population and committing to improve effectiveness of cooperation with communities. CMC will complement other sectoral responses through integration and mainstreaming methodologies.

3 - Project indicators

M&E plan will be developed for the project to determine the type of data that need to be collected, frequency of data collection, tools used for data collections, responsible person and track the progress against key results and output indicators and the information captured in different databases. CMC’ M&E team will develop standard cluster approved data collection approaches participatory in joint needs assessments the cluster’s M&E working groups. Periodic data analysis and interpretation through weekly reporting to the donor and the 5Ws reporting for the cluster will be establish at diverse levels of project implementation and shared with relevant stakeholders to inform about the project progress. A mix of quantitative, qualitative, participatory and observatory data collection methods will be engaged for collecting data in relation to the key indicators and the findings to be incorporated into the CMC M&E reports. Data collection and analysis will be done separately for women, men, children and the elderly. CMC will utilize sex and age disaggregated data throughout the action. Baseline survey will be conducted to establish pre-project benchmarks for key project level indicators and will be used to measure the project progress towards the end of the project. Quantitative data collection methods will be used for this purpose. Post intervention monitoring (PIM) and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted in all target areas at the end of the interventions and/or after 6 to 8 weeks of each distribution to assess the short-term impact of the interventions and perception of people on the quality of items distributed, convenience of distribution location, safety of people traveling to and going back from distribution locations, etc. and inform subsequent distributions. Households Interviews, Focus Group Discussions (FGD), Key informant interviews (KII) and observations will be the major methodologies during PIM/PDM. All survey data collection will be supported through mobile-based systems. Some of indicators used will be: number of water points rehabilitated at health/nutrition sites and schools; number of institutions with functioning, safe and dignified sanitation facilities; number of people (disaggregated by sex and age) received WASH related NFI, including. dignity kits; number of people trained and sensitized thanks to hygiene promotion or protection combined with other cross-sectoral topics despite of quantitative indicators, CMC will collect qualitative data about assistance, satisfaction, changes resulting from the project. CMC conducts periodic Lessons Learnt reviews which fuels the process of constant improvement. All reports will be shared between relevant stakeholders, other partners and clusters. . In order to protect the health of the community and staff and to curtail community transmission of COVID-19, these surveys will be conducted depending on the COVID-19 situation in South Sudan

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CMC’s project will take into the account the needs for unconditional commitment and increased awareness about the intolerable acts of PSEA and consequences unlimited to the project staff, vendors, suppliers the stakeholders with background checks conducted for all to avoid employment of sexual offenders. The project staff will constitute of the protection specialist, which will carry out the role of ensuring that both staff and beneficiaries are aware and practicing PSEA throughout the implementation. Ensuring increases in knowledge and information sharing through workshops, publications (IEC materials), continuous focus group discussion (FGDs), counseling and reporting center taking into account the need for confidentiality in flow of information that gives beneficiaries sense of assertiveness and immunity to report and object any PSEA related act throughout the implementation. The project will engage protection partners and community leaders to actively address any arising issues of housing land and property during project implementation and work closely with the local authorities for in-depth investigations and accountability for the PSEA allegations/cases and the project staff will work closely with other protection partners for effective referral pathways and protection mainstreaming. The project will committed to ensuring that frontline staff engage in timely and proper Community Communication and Engagement (CCE) while being guided by the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) guidelines all through the project implementation. Project staff will carryout entry and exit meetings prior to the end of the project implementation to have discussions with not only community leaders but also directly with all targeted beneficiaries who are women, men girls and boys to capture their interests and recommendations and asses the vulnerability. In addition, awareness sessions on AAP rights and how to use the established Complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFMs) will be conducted including; an open-door policy, complaints, response and feedback mechanism (CRFM) boxes, hotline and CRFM desks set up at each targeted locations where beneficiaries will be given opportunity to air their feedback to the project team through using both leaders and open-door policy. The hotline will be linked directly to the management. This will facilitate Communication, transparency and Community full engagement.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Protection will be mainstreamed in the project to ensure equal gender distribution of power and participations in decisions making, CMC will ensure protection measures are in place and conduct beneficiaries awareness on protection threats. During distribution of WASH NFIs, the team will ensure that the vulnerable and people living with disabilities are served fast so that they don’t cue for long and also where new water points will be constructed, consideration will be made through consultation with women on the distance and the location to ensure that women are safe in the process of collecting water. Protection will be at the central point in this project, putting communities at the canter of the response. CMC will ensure that services delivered are gender-sensitive and will consider the needs of older people, children (boys and girls) and people living with disabilities. CMC will ensure that the “Do no harm” principle is adhered. CMC includes protection officers in its Emergency WASH team to incorporate protection & GBV mainstreaming from the planning phase and throughout all stages of the intervention cycle. Protection staff will support the WASH team in identifying protection needs, and advise on the course for further action. The CMC protection team will ensure beneficiaries centered approach in all interventions through the formation, training of Community based protection networks (CBPNs) on prevention of GBV related cases in WASH, and beneficiaries will be encouraged to report GBV cases to the GBV partners. CMC protection staff together with the CBPNs will continue to respond to incidents of gender-based violence during implementation and recommend effective links to case management partners and maintain confidentiality. CMC will do mapping of the GBV/Protection partners in the project locations for easy development of referral pathways. CMC protection staff will work closely with well-established protection partners.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

CMC’s project will be focusing eradication of the IPC3-5 within the affected population in the locations where IPC is declared of an impact throughout 2021. CMC has taken measure to compact the IPC derivatives using the cluster standard operation procedures and strategies equipped to build resilience and the existing coping mechanisms in the locations. According to the IPC 2020 report; The high levels of acute food insecurity in the country are driven by flooding, insecurity, population displacements, declined crop production, diseases and pests, the economic crisis, the effects of COVID-19, limited access to basic services, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods. The continuing currency depreciation and associated impacts on commodity prices and incomes coupled with the effects of COVID-19 continue to erode the purchasing power of vulnerable households who rely on markets to purchase food and other basic needs. Seasonal scarcity of food, the flooding in 2020 (with limited recovery from the previous floods), and the much lower than required humanitarian food assistance in the face of increased needs, will likely result in an increase of acute food insecurity during the projection periods, especially during the lean season. Therefore CMC believes to have contributed to through integrated and complementary approaches to avail necessary humanitarian services to alleviate human suffering by 2021.
