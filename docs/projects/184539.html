Project Summary

Significant disparities in educational provision in Somalia are clearly evident in Gedo, between rural and urban areas, girls and boys, dominant and marginalized clans and groups. Primary GER for pastoralists/ nomads, remains a shocking 3.1%, with the IDPs rate at only 16.6 % (ESA, 2016. Food insecurity has forced households to send children to schools with feeding programs (CBNS, August 2021). Consequently, the Trocaire Education and Nutrition programs have been overwhelmed despite having in place contingency plans to respond to influx. This presents a challenge in provision of quality education given the high pupil to teacher ratio. Children from displaced communities also face a greater challenge of accessing educational opportunities. Dominant and deeply rooted socio-cultural issues militate against girls attending school. The capacity for educational provision across Gedo is extremely weak, with a dearth of trained teachers and teacher training opportunities, institutional administration expertise, teaching resources and professional, experienced leadership. The lack of adequate female teachers impacts adversely on girls’ safety and potential role models.
The project aims to increase access to education and ensure retention in schools for 8,310 boys and 7,516 girls (including 1,582 children with disabilities) from IDP and vulnerable communities in Gedo Region. This will be implemented in Belet Hawo, Luuq, Dollow and Garbaharey districts with the aim of addressing the lack of access to physical, safe, and inclusive learning facilities for primary school children, the lack of quality learning materials, the lack of trained teachers, and economic barriers. Trócaire, working with a local education partner, will provide Teacher incentives to 120 teachers; provide inclusive teaching and learning materials to 26 schools; renovate and construct accessible latrines for 18 schools; and construct/rehabilitate eighteen (18) learning spaces based on universal design to accommodate the needs of learners with disabilities. Trócaire will facilitate monthly hygiene promotion sessions (across 78 schools); hold monthly sessions to promote protection; identify and train 26 Child protection focal points; and establish/revive, Child Clubs. Trócaire will conduct teachers training on pedagogy for 120 new teachers, as well as refresher trainings for 142 teachers; erect fences in 5 schools; manage trainings for school heads, as well as Community Education Committees (CECs), across all schools of intervention; and train key stakeholders on accountability. Protection will be mainstreamed by creating a safe learning environment (that encourages positive dialogue on inclusivity) in schools; with tailored service provision (especially for girls), and collective monitoring of protection outcomes in schools. Child protection activities will be incorporated to further tailor services to the needs of children. The project is based on the gaps identified in Trocaire’s internal monitoring within its integrated Humanitarian programme, findings of the Education cluster, and the Joint National Multi-Cluster Assessment; and aligned to the 2021 Education Cluster objectives.
Children with disabilities suffer from stigma and discrimination, and a result, many are hidden (made invisible) thereby excluding them from community affairs while heightening their overall protection needs. They face multiple and intersecting identities that limit their enjoyment of rights and freedoms on an equal basis with others. The project will focus on provision of quality services and with centrality of protection at the core for vulnerable children particularly girls and CWDs, by focusing on the intersecting vulnerabilities that hinder their full and effective participation in learning both in and out of class. Reasonable accommodation and support will be provided to address the context specific and unique barriers to learning that they face.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance/services or indiscriminate attacks from armed actors.HighConcrete mitigation measures: While protection referral services remain novel in Gedo Region, Trócaire’s protection team has prioritized protection services, including the strengthening of existing referral mechanisms, to ensure women and girls receive assistance in a timely and safe manner. One key activity planned for year 2022 is to link schools to this referral mechanism, and train CEC focal persons (one per school) as a referral point for school- going children. To mitigate against this risk, Trócaire shall strengthen the presence of girls’ clubs in schools, and support their in-house forums on a monthly basis, as a platform to discuss common challenges and share the same to the school management (the headteachers/deputy headteachers) and the wider protection stakeholders for improved service delivery/ risk mitigation. In addition, girls will be exclusively targeted Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. MediumConcrete mitigation measures: Schools will be fenced and staff trained on child protection. The school supplies will be distributed in schools; and girls encouraged to move in groups and report any fear/suspicion to the established Trócaire (or any other available) Feedback & Response Mechanism. Trócaire’s interventions are restricted to accessible schools in both rural and urban areas in as far as security is concerned, and involvement of CECs, DEOs and communities is prioritized in identifying schools to be targeted. This ensures safety of children while accessing schools, and reduced protection concerns to and from their homes. The children will be sensitized on the CFRM i.e., how they can report and or give feedback.
