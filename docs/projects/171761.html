Project Summary

An estimated 3.5 million people in rural livelihood zones, urban areas, and IDP settlements need food assistance. The recent Food Security and drought plights in Hirshabelle, Banadir and South West State of Somalia call for timely life-saving Food security interventions to enable the displaced, returnees and vulnerable host communities' men, women boys, and girls in the project targeted locations that are yet to recover from impacts of below-average 2020 gu cereal harvest, economic impacts of COVID-19, damage from bush cricket infestations and locust invasions. Furthermore, a long-term decline in rainfall performance and preliminary climatological research suggest an elevated likelihood of below-average rainfall in the March to May 2021 season.
Our project will address the Food Security need through conditional and non-conditional safety nets. The communities will also earn income in form of cash for work to sustain their families’ food requirements to mitigate the precarious food insecurity conditions and further restore their livelihoods and strengthen their resilience. We have suitably designed the food security support programs to ensure that the communities will be able to cope with the current and future shocks in the food security supply chain.
The project targets 20,100 persons (9,447 females 6,030 male, 1,407 boys, and 3,216 girls) with increased access to food and improved livelihood standards; and strengthened the resilience of the most vulnerable and displaced and host communities in Banadir and Hirshabelle regions respectively, through appropriate livelihood support initiatives during the project time.
The project objectives are to improve targeted vulnerable households’ immediate access to food security and livelihoods though distribution of conditional and unconditional cash vouchers. The project will target the most vulnerable affected people in three selected areas by providing Food security and livelihood assistances to become resilience against shocks.
The project targets persons who include IDPs, host communities, pastoralists and support urgent life-saving access to adequate food and nutrition and support livelihood recovery for 20,100 vulnerable communities in Somalia through conditional and unconditional transfers as well as in-kind provision of assistance.
Knowing of the fact that livestock is among main livelihood assets for pastoralists in target locations, SYPD protect and promote target beneficiaries' livestock assets through training of select community animal health workers on livestock treatment and vaccination of livestock against common diseases.
The project will aim to reduce and prevent acute malnutrition to the PLWDs and children under 5 who live in the targeted areas, by providing support mechanisms as well reduce mortality rates from crisis driven diseases and outbreaks.
The project will provide safe, dignified and meaningful access to life-saving assistance, provided through food voucher or conditional e.g. CFW program to the target beneficiaries including persons with disability with due account to age, gender, disability, and social status.
The project will target will include people with disability and chronic ill persons those who need special consideration and will ensure that project target beneficiaries inclusiveness is fully adopted minority and marginalized groups will be part of project initial stages to avoid exclusion errors and ensure that their voices are heard.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacementSomalia’s societal structure is highly complex, comprising numerous social groups, clans, sub-clans and ethnic minority groups that are not members of any specific clans. Divisions and constantly evolving relations between these groups are among the prevailing characteristics of society, and represent a key driver of vulnerability within the ongoing conflict.Internally displaced persons from minority groups, or those with weak social connections in host communities, face more obstacles in accessing assistance and protection. Minority clans and marginalized groups face heightened risks of eviction, arbitrary arrest, sexual violence, and discriminatory or denied access to humanitarian assistance and services. In this regard, internally displaced persons in Somalia face the greatest risk of exclusion due to absence basic services, lack of protection mechanisms, legal frameworks such as housing and property rights and discrimination. Minority clans, women and girls are particularly excluded due to cultural norms and community structures mainly dominated by men. SYPD will incorporate inclusion considerations into project design by prioritizing internally displaced persons, returnees and host communities in food security interventions including establishing and strengthening community committees that are balance in terms of age, gender and clan diversity. During the project startup, SYPD will organize consultative meetings with all stakeholders including minority groups to avoid social exclusion errors.To effectively target the most vulnerable households and Community infrastructure assets through CFW modality, SYPD will carry out thorough needs assessment to identify the differentiated needs of displaced people and other vulnerable groups. The beneficiary registration will ensure that needs analysis brings to the fore all dimensions of exclusion and village relief committee will participate the process of beneficiary selection and registration. SYPD will establish inclusive community communication mechanism by forming beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanisms to ensure that all vulnerable groups have a voice in the project implementation. Feedback will be processed through trained staff and resolved through established community mechanisms. The project implementation will mainstream capacity building on all dimensions of exclusion with proper hand over mechanism to ensure that the mainstreaming is maintained throughout the project implementation and handover. SYPD will carry out community consultations in order to identify obstacles which may hamper the full participation of IDPs, including from marginalized communities in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the projects. SYPD will include both IDPs, host communities and refugee returnees to prevent any aggravation of tensions between groups of IDPs or between host communities.SYPD with the consultation of local authorities and elders will constitute joint village committee and will train on necessary knowledge to oversight and manage the rehabilitated community infrastructures, the committee will maintain the asset and solve the conflicts among the community as well as mobilizing resource sharing. Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and ageSYPD will carry out needs analysis during the beneficiary selection and registration to ensure that all vulnerable people have access to assistance without discrimination. Beneficiary registration will involve existing community / newly formed community elders with respect of community structures and beneficiary representatives. SYPD will conduct constant community consultations meetings to identify barriers to the participation of women, girls, marginalized and minority groups and youth to ensure that risks regarding exclusion based on gender and age are identified and mitigated in a timely manner.
