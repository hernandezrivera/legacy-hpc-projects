Project Summary

The aim of INTERSOS is to reduce vulnerability, to build resilience reaching a reasonable level of preparation and to reinforce the coping capacity of communities in order to decrease suffering and to prevent future loss of life. This will be achieved through a combination of safe water supply, improved sanitation services and raising awareness around the importance of hygiene practices to prevent diseases and to keep children and families healthy and well.
This project proposal intends to provide timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene services, focused on prevention of cholera outbreak, to conflict-affected communities (returnees and IDPs/host community), settled in Torit and Magwi town and their surrounding suburbs.
INTERSOS gained experience of the targeted areas through 3 years of WASH projects implemented with different donors in Torit and Magwi counties. Currently, a WASH project funded by UNICEF, is under implementation and is targeting 32 villages with integrated CLTS approach and Behavior Change Communication (BCC) campaign.
The needs are still vast and through this proposal INTERSOS hopes to integrate and extend intervention to other areas filling gaps of assistance, counting in an experienced and WASH/CTLS trained staff.
Where the vulnerability of local communities is extremely high, and where environmental health risks are clearly life-threatening to local communities, it becomes necessary to integrate the component of Disaster Risk Reduction included in the HRP2020 shelter proposal targeting communities living in flood-prone areas.
Significant attention will be made in cholera endemic and risk-prone areas, to ensure adequate water supply and proper disinfection, water quality monitoring, hygiene promotion, sanitation, and safe excreta disposal at household and community levels and in-market facilities.
Provision of WASH services is a key element to the prevention of cholera outbreaks and it has been recognized as often being a more cost-effective way of working, particularly in situations where there is high possibility of occurring events. Work on the prevention of cholera focused on sustainable provision of safe water and sanitation and hygiene promotion, which also help reduce the transmission of other diarrheal diseases.

1 - Needs

South Sudan remains a fragile state, with 7.4 million in need of humanitarian assistance. Coping abilities have been further eroded by prolonged food insecurity, economic collapse, malnutrition, and disease outbreaks diseases (cholera, malaria, typhoid, HEV, etc.). The widespread lack of safe drinking water, underdeveloped sanitation facilities, and poor hygiene practices have left a large proportion of the population at risk of preventable WASH-related diseases. Cholera continues to represent a major public health concern in South Sudan and has persistently continued seasonal outbreaks between April to June and sometimes in January to February every year.The South Sudan National Cholera control plan (2019 – 2023) – classified Torit as IP2/Type 2 and Magwi as IP3/Type 1 based on the high infections and cholera-related death rate registered in the past 5 years. According to the WHO ranking, the classification IP2 corresponding to high and IP3 to medium. During the last 6 months, Torit and Magwi registered an increased number of population due to returnees (6,380 individuals in Torit and 2,200 individuals in Magwi as registered at RRC office), who preferred to resettle close to town instead of making return to their original rural areas, still insecure and for the absence of basic facilities. The poor sanitation set up of towns and the inadequate water facilities to provide safe water to this increased number of citizens has however turned-in Torit and Magwi to a prone cholera and/ or Acute water diarrhea (AWD) hotspot. Although Humanitarian Response is in place, the needs remain huge: in Eastern Equatorial only 20% of the population has access to basic sanitation in urban areas. The percentage is even lower in rural locations: the population do not have access to improved sanitation, approximately 6% of the population using improved facilities at household level, since 61% of the rural population continues to practice open defecation. Overall, WASH cluster estimates 27 646 people are in need in Torit, wheareas the number goes up to 93 906 for Magwi. Half of the population do not have access to improved water sources, with about 35 % of the existing boreholes estimated to be non-functional. Only 28 % of the population has access to a borehole within 30 minutes’ walk.Hand washing practices ad latrine use is also generally poor in South Sudan and the majority of population practices open defecation. Furthermore, those households and institutions with latrines are in many cases not hygienically maintained, resulting in serious health hazards for families by encouraging breeding of disease vectors, particularly where the latrines are located within household compounds. Transportation and storage of water in containers at household level is easily contaminated. Households store water on the floor, where it is accessible by young children and animals, rather than storing on raised platforms, out of reach of contaminating agents.Given the absence of a formal and functioning solid waste disposal system in the Eastern Equitoria state, the already insufficient infrastructure and poor waste disposal mechanisms cannot cope with the population increase due to returnees. The heightened population density and poor solid waste management becomes a serious issue during the rainy season each year, when ad-hoc waste dumping grounds become traps for stagnant water and breeding of disease vectors. All of the above significantly increase the risk of outbreaks of communicable diseases, which pose a severe threat to vulnerable populations, particularly for children under five years, pregnant and lactating mothers. In response to this, INTERSOS proposes to intensify hygiene promotion, including through community clean up campaigns; in addition to supporting vector control and proper management and disposal of waste.

2 - Activities

Targeting 25,000 individuals, settled in Torit and Magwi town and their surrounding suburbs (tot 20 hubs/villages), the project intends to provide them access to safe drinking/chlorinated water and functional handwashing facilities according to WASH Sphere Standards. The proposed activities will be integrated with the WASH project funded by UNICEF, under implementation and targeting 32 villages with integrated CLTS approach and Behavior Change Communication (BCC) campaign. The project activities will be implemented referring to Community-Driven Development (CDD) approaches to empower people in poverty by putting resources and responsibility for decision making in their hands according to the policy “to do what they can, with what they have at their disposal”.The main components of the proposals are:Construction of 10 elevated WASH collective water points in order to reduce the risk of water contamination in flood-prone areas./Rehabilitation of 10 existing boreholes (hand pump repairing and drainage improving) including preliminary water test, water points management for cattle and small stock requirements during the dry season, with the promotion of appropriate hygiene practices to prevent animal open defecation down to the household level./ Construction of 6 hand-washing points in the local markets of Torit and Magwi towns including water points management for vendors. Construction of latrines for 5,000 HH (25,000 individuals) in 20 hubs/villages through the CLTS approach in order to ensure safe excreta disposal.Improvement of the drainage system in the local markets of Torit and Magwi. To reduce potential health risks to the population, it will be necessary to unblock existing drainage to help drain the site, clear drainage channels and to repair small breaches.Behavior Change Communication (BCC) campaigns/ Host three awareness days/campaigns i.e., Global Hand Washing Day on 15th October; World Water Day on 22nd March, and World Toilet Day on 19th November.Community Clean-up Campaigns through facilitating and mobilizing communities to carry out periodic clean-up of their compound, including public places such as markets and schools. /Lobby the local authorities (Counties) to designated dumping sites out away from town. Establish Solid Waste Management Committees (SWMCs) in the 20 target villages supported with cleaning tools and training with active participation of both men and women. The awareness sessions to be carried out by committee members will focus on safe methods to keep villages clean and free of waste materials. With support of traditional leadership, these committees will also support clearing of standing water pools. Vector Control Campaigns will be supported both prior and post-rainy season when breeding of mosquito vectors is at peak. INTERSOS will promote use of locally available vector control mechanisms such as clearing the surroundings of stagnant water and empty containers. Raising awareness through specific Cholera Awareness campaigns. Tailored IEC materials including key messages to raise awareness about reducing local potential cholera transmission.Refreshers training for 312 health/hygiene promoters to conduct hygiene promotion at household and community level, early detect and manage cholera cases in the 32 villages targeted by INTERSOS with UNICEF projects. Conduct cholera prevention awareness to 30 hygiene officers of the Counties of Torit and Magwi and conduct community meeting to establish cholera focal persons teams in each village. Conduct community meeting to strengthen and build the capacity of 40 local community leaders as Cholera Focal Persons. The current capacity of INTERSOS in Torit State level, integrated with the requested resources in HRP2020, can be expanded to respond to new emergencies occurring along the 2020, such as mobilization of a Rapid Response Team and distributions of emergency Wash kits from Cluster Pipeline.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

In line with the specific INTERSOS mandate the IDPs monitoring it is a crosscutting activity among the different ongoing programs. 6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

All the governmental and CS stakeholders have been deep involved in the need assessment and strategy identification steps. INTERSOS strives to incorporate participation of stakeholders in all interventions that it conducts. Throughout the assessment, verification and distribution processes, it will involve stakeholders, meaningfully respecting their decision-making abilities regarding needs. Arrangements with local authorities and leaders will facilitate the involvement of stakeholders at all stages of intervention (assessment, planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation). INTERSOS team will always involve local authorities and community leaders to share design and objectives of the interventions

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The intervention is aligned with WASH Cluster Strategy and within the framework of HNO 2020. In the specific, the intervention aims at improving access to safe drinking/chlorinated water and functional hand washing facilities according to WASH Sphere Standards by preventing cholera outbreaks in Torit and MAGWI.Furthermore, it aims at improving the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities/affected non- displaced, who are unable to meet their WASH needs, through an ad-hoc assistance provided in the framework of the intervention.Overall, it aims at supporting the most vulnerable categories with durable solutions, rebuilding lives through Wash solutions.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

According with the evolution of the context and with the deep knowledge gained from INTERSOS in the specific areas the context result to be relatively stable. However the project foresees mitigation measures in order to cope with any kind of fluidity and change of the social, political and accessibility situation.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, INTERSOS work will focus on strengthening community structures and building local capacity through establishment and strengthening of village committees.The direct involvement of the governmental stakeholder in all the phases of the implementation will strengthen the ownership of the local actors and will guarantee a good level of involvement int he hand over phase. However the involvement of the local community thorough the creation of community committees will guarantee the self management of the structures at the and of the project.Moreover, specific awareness activities will be held in order to make the community members aware of the risk and the mitigation measures related to the use and maintenance of WASH facilities to avoid further constrains related as well as the awareness will guarantee the spreading of the good practices among the whole communities.
