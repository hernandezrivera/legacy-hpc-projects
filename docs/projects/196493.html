Project Summary

SRD project will strengthen covering the health needs of both IDP and residents in NWS by ensuring the continuity of its ongoing projects and aiming to enhance the sustainability and quality of medical services through the provision of technical support via capacity building and rehabilitation of the Infrastructure of two maternity and childhood hospitals and operating three medical incinerators in Aleppo, and Idleb.
Rehabilitation of the infrastructure of three hospitals (Jandairis Hospital-B0203076, Akhtarin Hospital-‎B0204070, and B0200124 ! Al Fardous hospital). Through the last three months, The three hospitals ‎provided basic and advanced life-saving healthcare services to 45000 patients (3000 men, 21000 ‎women, 12000 girls, and 9000 boys). 11% (5000 out of 45000) have disability cases. Through the ‎reinforcement of the three hospitals, allowing for the expansion of key departments, including surgery, ‎radiology, admissions, laboratory, and outpatient, the resilience of both staff and beneficiaries will be ‎supported and enhanced via protection mechanisms to ensure the safety and security of those served, ‎ensure the availability of medical employment(livelihood) opportunities at the hospital, and ultimately ‎serve as a local investment in health infrastructure in Jandairis and Aghtarin cities.‎
To compensate for the loss of dedicated, qualified maternal health care providers, in particular those with Midwifery competencies, there is a great need for a Midwifery capacity-building strategy that
will contribute to the rapid availability of health-based human resources; the project aims to support the availability of sustainable Maternal and Newborn Health programming at various levels, including building regulatory capacity through the scope of practice expansion and delineation for Midwifery. Contribute to building Midwifery capacity at the pre-service and in-service levels of clinical service delivery.
SRD will operate three incinerators in rural Aleppo (Mare'e, Darat Izza) and Idlib to ensure proper management of COVID-19 medical waste. These incinerators will be fully dedicated to medical waste disposal in most health facilities in the NWS.
SRD will utilize a detailed M&E plan to follow the implementation closely and ensure the targeting of the correct beneficiaries through verification of the beneficiary lists. Monitoring services delivery and activities implementation through random visits to project and activity sites using appropriate tools customized for the project activities. Including exit interviews, satisfaction surveys, complaint feedback mechanisms, collecting sex and age disaggregated data, and report 4Ws.
SRD utilizes the accountability to affected population tools which also consider the ‎PWDs’ inputs and feedback to include their needs within designed interventions, these ‎tools include FGDs, Surveys, and KIIs. Moreover, the rehabilitation of the hospitals will ‎include modifications to ensure access for PWDs to these facilities, including ‎entrances, ramps, waiting areas, WCs, and equipment for PWDs.‎

3 - Needs

In 2022, 14.6 million people will need humanitarian assistance in Syria. As the needs continue to increase, significant funding gaps persist. The fragile health system continues to face simultaneous emergencies and chronic challenges that affect the availability and quality ‎of health services, notably in northwest Syria, given its weakened health infrastructure and shortage of resources (human and financial). Women and children have borne the brunt of these conditions, given the disproportionate impact on them throughout the crisis. Displacements from areas still facing hostilities to relatively more stable regions will likely continue placing a further strain on already fragile essential services. Likewise, returns will require the rehabilitation of services and infrastructure and the resumption of economic activity. Lack of early recovery activities reduces the production capacity of local businesses; this also hinders the potential to expand local production of essential items and services related to priorities of other sectors, such as Shelter-NFI, WASH, Health, and Education. Analysis of the complex situation in NWS shows a huge need to expand access to the essential health services package by repairing damaged critical health infrastructure and strengthening linkages with cross-sector efforts with nutrition, water, sanitation and hygiene, early recovery, and emergency preparedness and response capacities.

4 - Implementation of activities

SRD activities contribute to HRP 22-23 and early recovery sector objectives by supporting the Maintenance & improve equitable access to essential services through rehabilitating hospitals and removing Waste. Establishment/strengthening of sustainable waste management systems (solid Waste, recycling, health, and hazardous Waste). Capacity building for service providers. Rehabilitation of Infrastructure of three Hospitals to provide basic and advanced life-saving health care services to patients and allow for the expansion of key departments, including Surgery, Radiology, Admissions, Laboratory, and Outpatient. SRD will operate and manage three incinerators, including training medical staff in health facilities on managing medical Waste. Logistical preparation for the operation and incineration of the Waste in medical incinerators. Midwifery school activities will ensure the graduation of about seventy-five midwives to meet the medical needs of women of childbearing age by offering a teaching course for 12 months. SRD's Monitoring & Reporting (M&R) Strategy will ensure that SRD can achieve project objectives using IASC Response Monitoring Guidelines. The framework of M&R relies on working closely with staff on the ground and with other local actors. SRD maintains an accountability structure that aims to fulfill the objective of the Syria HRP to 'support saving lives, alleviate suffering and increase access [...] for vulnerable people and those with specific needs.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: The displaced population, especially IDP women and girls, might face challenges in accessing SRH/GBV services due to multiple barriers to where/why to access them. Mitigation 1: Mobilization activities to share information with the community focusing on IDP women and girls about SRH/GBV services at PHCs, MCs, and hospitals. Protection Risk 2: Referrals to/from other services might be disrupted due to security. Mitigation 2: Regular coordination and updating service portfolio of available health services providers in the area of operation. Protection Risk 3: Partners' staff working in highly insecure environments might not be able to access counseling services. Mitigation 3: Self-care training is available for SRD staff in Gaziantep but not inside Syria. SRD organizes social self-care days for their staff. Protection Risk 4: Adolescents with disabilities might face challenges accessing the clinics/hospital to attend girls' club sessions. Mitigation 4: Designated vehicles will be provided to facilitate access of adolescents with disabilities to the clinics and hospitals. Duty of Care should be taken into consideration to provide for its staff's physical and mental safety, security, health, and well-being. In turn, staff members have a "Duty of Responsibility" to take care of themselves, follow organizational policies, and utilize available services.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SRD has a zero-tolerance policy toward sexual exploitation, abuse, and child abuse. SRD adheres to and implements the PSEA core principles of assistance beneficiaries that form the basis for protecting women, children, or others from sexual exploitation and abuse when providing services. SRD has a duty of care to aid beneficiaries and a responsibility to treat them with dignity and respect. Once a case has been confirmed, it undergoes a rigorous investigation to determine if policies have been violated and ensure that appropriate mitigation measures and disciplinary action is taken in immediate dismissal as per the policies, including Code of Conduct, SEAH policies, HR Disciplinary Measures, Whistle-blower Policies, Accountability through receiving and reporting complaints. SRD considers any complaint as a high priority and takes the following steps: - An independent investigation committee is formed under the direction of the Country Director or HQ if related to concerns at the senior level of the country office. - The employee(s) in question is immediately suspended until the investigation concludes and the findings and resolution(s) are disclosed to all parties involved. - The committee manages the investigation with the utmost confidentiality and propriety throughout and following the entire process - In complex cases and comprehensive investigations, HR consults with SRD’s legal counsel (external), who are called upon on a case-by-case basis, including cases of PSEA.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The safety and security of the staff, programs, and beneficiaries are of utmost importance to SRD, which has a duty of care policy with project operations. -The COVID-19 pandemic affects staff and projects. Likelihood: moderate, Impact: high, Mitigation: Staff is trained on IPC COVID. All project staff will be provided with PPE. Active triage system and implementation of IPC COVID measures for all beneficiaries, staff, contractors, workers, and laborers. -AIR STRIKE - IED/VBIED: Staff directly or indirectly impacted by the airstrikes and IED/VBIED attacks. Likelihood: Likely Impact: Severe. Mitigations: Avoid potential targets. Using routes used by people all the time. On-going threat monitoring/SRA. Hiring locals who are familiar with the situation. Medical Evacuation arrangements. Facilities' locations selected away from potential targeted areas. An excellent relationship with the local community. Staff first aid and security training. Compliance with the security manual. -Kidnapping of local staff, Likelihood: Likely, Impact: Severe, Mitigation: Follow code of conduct. Hire local contractors who know the context. Build acceptance with the host community and meet the community's needs with outstanding quality. - CASH ROBBERY: Likely, Moderate, Mitigation: Using the PTT to pay for the vendors and staff where possible. -Overlap with other services, Mitigation: coordinate response with HC and operating partners. Share information with the community about services.
