Project Summary

The project will aim at increasing timely and equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene services to 12000 (women, men, girls and boys) IDPs and host communities in Lower Shabelle region (Marka, Barawe and Qoryoley). WASH needs were identified by Juba Foundation nutrition project staff while providing nutrition services to IDPs and Host community through focus group discussion with community members especially women and community leaders. Limited access to safe clean water has conditioned the IDPs and vulnerable/ crises affected host communities in the Project target locations in Lower Shabelle. The aim of the project is to ensure that the affected populations are ensured with safe, equitable, and sustainable access to sufficient quantity of water, affected populations have access to safe, sanitary, and hygienic living environment through provision of sanitation services that are secure, sanitary, user­ friendly and gender appropriate. Currently the community use river water that is exposed to pollution due to careless dumping of garbage into and along the banks of the river. There are common cases of IDP and Vulnerable host communities covering over 500m in search of safe water as well as those queuing for around 15-25 minutes at water points to acquire water far beyond WASH cluster sphere standards of not more than 3 minutes. The target vulnerable host communities and IDPs are living precariously in absolute poverty having lost their livelihood assets during the past devastating famine and are yet to recover thus cannot afford to buy water from private boreholes and water vendors in the area hence increased cases of AWD/Cholera outbreak and other water borne diseases during the onset of seasonal rains (Gu and Deyr). The situation that has conditioned over 10 IDPs families to share a single latrine far much beyond the WASH cluster shere standards of 6 families per latine. The Project will ensure construction of 5 new water purification points in the five Project target locations consisting of (4m3 water tanks poly-glu, 4m3 chlorine and 4 m3 safe clean water) piped and purified to provide 7.5litres of water/person/day in the project target locations. The Project will in addition ensure construction of 100 VIP latrines in the Project target locations fixed with hand washing facilities, construction of 5 Ecosan latrines targeting 5 IDP and host community families as well as hygiene promotion campaign and awareness raising to mitigate against common cases of AWD/cholera outbreak. Project will improve the capacity of the community by training 40 hygiene promoters and 50 social mobilizers The above needs and gaps were identified during an assessment by JF WASH team and regular meetings with the local communities in Lower Shabelle

Needs

Limited access to safe and clean water has greatly impacted to the lives of displaced and vulnerable host women, men, boys, and girls in Marka, Barawe and Qoryoley in Lower Shabelle Region, Somalia. The situation is exacerbated by careless dumping of garbage into and along the banks while the same river water is drawn and directly used by the IDPs and vulnerable host communities that do not have purchasing power to acquire water from private boreholes and water vendors. The situation has impacted to high cases of AWD/cholera outbreak and other water borne diseases. The three target locations has been receiving a good number of IDPs and returnees from conflict prone, a situation that has piled pressure on limited sanitation facilities that has conditioned a good number of communities and IDPs to practice open defecation worsened by poor hygiene practices. JF is currently implementing Food Security and wash project funded by Save the Children- Somalia as in Gedo region- Bardheere, Luuq and Elwak districts. JF has very good working relationship with local communities and administration in Lower Shabelle regions that has been an integral part of the successful health, Nutrition and education and protection programs in these regions.
