Project Summary

The project purpose is to provide emergency food and livelihood assistance to 57,491 people who are in severe food insecurity situation of emergency phase (IPC 4) and crisis phase (IPC 3) in Terekeka and Kajokeji counties. The project will employ a social safety net approach to support targeted critical food insecure and extremely vulnerable persons with unconditional cash transfers to enable them meet their immediate food needs for 2 months. The unconditional cash transfer will target the most vulnerable/food insecure households and this category will include Female-headed households, Pregnant and lactating women, Households with children five years of age and below, Elderly and widows, Child headed households, Households with household head with a disability/who is chronically ill. Registered beneficiaries will receive $ 50 dollars per month for 2 months. To build the resilience of the affected rural population against future
shocks, AAHI will complement cash distribution with the following;
1)- Seed and tools support – emergency vital agricultural inputs including tools and quick maturing vegetable seeds and crop seeds will be provided for cultivation to improve household food security. One of the major limitations of agriculture production is limited access to quality seeds. Most farmers rely on preserving seeds from their harvest for use in the coming season. However due to shocks mentioned above, there was limited/no production in 2020 and whatever was growing in the gardens or stored in the granaries was burnt down or looted during the conflict. Improved seeds will be procured and distributed to each household.
2)- Provision of extension services – Farmers will be supported with training on modern vegetable and crop growing best practices. AAH-I proposes to identify Community Based Agriculture Extension Volunteers who will be trained as trainers, and with the support of AAH-I Agriculture Officers, will be responsible for cascading trainings to farmers and provide basic agriculture advice.
3)- AAHI will implement a nutrition sensitive project by incorporating nutrition IYCF messages and good hygiene practices throughout project implementation, during meetings and trainings.
4)-AAHI will establish collaborative arrangements with other actors in Terekeka and Kajokeji to build synergies especially with WFP implemented projects on GFD and BSFP .The interventions will revive and restore households livelihoods through cultivation of quick maturing vegetables, crops and training to foster recovery and longer term resilience.

1 - Needs

According to the 23rd December 2020 IPC report, during the period April-July 2021 lean season, 7.2 million people (60%) will be facing severe acute food insecurity. 31,000 people in catastrophe, 2.5 million in emergency and 4.7 million in Crisis. The proposed counties-Kajokeji and terekeka fall under IPC 3- crisis and IPC 4-emergency. Terekeka has 60% of the population (151,257 people) in IPC 3 and IPC 4. Terekeka payams (Gemeza and Mangalla north) along the Nile were affected by severe flooding in 2020, and every year the county experiences severe food insecurity, cattle raids, and internal conflicts. The Islamic Development and Relief Agency in September 2020, conducted an assessment and the findings were; The highland areas were fully submerged under the flood waters. Houses, cattle camps farmland with crops, fish ponds and grazing land were all submerged in water. Other Payams ( Rego, Tindilo, Tunguna mahi, Tombek and Tali suffered from inter tribal conflict towards the end of 2020. As a result of the conflict, properties were destroyed including houses and food stocks were burnt down. Thousands of people were displaced and fled to Terekeka town while others fled to Murle land and Lakes states. With the intervention of Juba Government, reconciliations involving all communities took place and people started returning to their homes, but only to find destroyed homes with limited or no food stocks. Due to insecurity and violence which resulted in loss of livelihood assets and also displaced people from fertile lands, coupled with added negative impact of extreme weather phenomenon of drought, flooding, incidences of Fall Armyworm, and lack of adequate seeds and tools, thousands of people in Terekeka have been rendered food insecure to the level of crisis and emergency. Kajokeji has 60% of the population (136,190 people) in IPC 3 and IPC 4. The year to year acute food insecurity has been caused by the armed conflict which erupted since 2016 in most counties of Equatoria, including Kajo-keji County. The conflict disrupted the livelihoods of the people and there were massive displacement of people and thousands fled to refugee camps in Uganda. Others went to IDP camps that were established within Kajokeji. With the signing of the September 2018 peace agreement, many people started returning to their places of origin on voluntary basis. However, the returnees are facing hard times as their belonging left behind were looted or destroyed and have nothing to start new life. According to the assessment conducted by the Islamic Development and Relief Agency in June 2020, the key findings were; There was limited access to food within the county due to the fact there were no agricultural activities taking place in the past years 2016 to 2019 due to the conflict. Some of the farmers in the different payams received seeds from one of the organizations in 2020, but the seeds failed to germinate so they lost the entire season. Farmers who planted cassava had their gardens destroyed by cassava mosaic virus disease. The farmers have no access to seeds and at all their livestock (Cattle, shoats, pigs, poultry) were stolen.In reference to the contextual analysis above, the mentioned shocks have disrupted the livelihoods of the communities in Terekeka and kajokeji and eroded their coping mechanisms, such that if they are not supported, there will move from IPC 4 to IPC 5 (catastrophe). In view of the above, the project interventions will prevent negative coping and help protect/restore livelihoods assets, build resilience, linking emergency response interventions to longer term development programming for households and communities by addressing household/community shocks.

2 - Project activities

AAH-I proposes the following key activities; 1. Project inception activities- these will include entry meetings with county, Payam and Boma officials, community mobilization, food security and market assessment, stakeholder consultations and project administration set up. 2. Community mobilization, sensitization- community and stakeholders in the project area will be sensitized on the project objectives, modalities of implementation, selection criteria and cash transfer value. Project management committees will be established to play a key role in community mobilization and beneficiary selection. 3. Establish complaints and feedback mechanism (CFM) - AAH-I will put in place a rigorous community based Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) in the project areas to enhance reporting and redress of beneficiaries complaints through the help desks and will be reinforced by providing a telephone number where beneficiaries will report any grievances and receive instant feedback.4. Beneficiary identification/selection – will be conducted by the committees supported by project staff using the agreed upon selection criteria. After the registration, beneficiary lists will be validated in community meetings to address any inclusion or exclusion error. A bio metric registration will then be undertaken that will capture all beneficiary bio data, photos and figure prints. Using a Biometric registration system will ensure that the rightful beneficiaries receive the humanitarian assistance and provide precise accountability to the donor. 5. Procurement and distribution of crop/vegetable seeds- quick maturing seeds will be procured. These include sorghum, maize, cowpeas, green grams, okra, eggplant and onions. 6. Contract a Financial Service Provider- The FSP will deliver and pay cash to the beneficiaries at agreed distribution points. 7. Conduct cash distribution to eligible beneficiaries once a month for 3 months after finger print authentication. 8. Training of project committees- project management committees will be trained on their roles and responsibilities. 9. Conduct ToT training of Community Based Extension Volunteers (CBEV) on agriculture extension approaches, practical skills for crop/vegetable growing and financial literacy. The CBEV and project staff will cascade the trainings to the beneficiaries. 10. Conduct rollout training - Beneficiary groups will be trained on good practices of crop/vegetable and field crop production, nutrition and hygiene. 11. Conduct monthly post distribution monitoring (PDM) - (3-5 days after distribution, a PDM will be randomly conducted to gather beneficiary views on their degree of satisfaction and ascertain the correct use of the cash, as wells as effectiveness of the cash modality. 12. Project conclusion activities- these will include project exit meetings, audit and final reports.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Based on the assessment conducted by Islamic Relief and Development Agency, in Kajokeji alot of goods are available at the Boarder with Uganda and many traders have started delivering their merchandise for sale within Kajokeji.In Terekeka, markets are functioning at the Payams and beneficiaries can buy the commodities they require.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

1) The cash is unrestricted and the beneficiaries will have no restriction on how it is used. Apart from food needs, the beneficiaries have many vital basic needs due to the destruction of their sources of livelihood during the conflict. they lost their houses and property. So the cash will give the beneficiaries the freedom to buy some food and other household basic needs since they have returned to their home with no assets. 2) Given that the road network was completely damaged during the conflict, it is logistically feasible to transport cash as compared to transporting tonnes of food on trucks.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

1) Beneficiaries risk exposure to violence during registration/distribution- Selection criteria will be discussed and agreed upon with all stakeholders and community. community will be involved in validating the selection criteria. Project management committees will be established comprising 50% female and 59% male beneficiaries who will participate in the registration using the selection criteria. Complaints and feedback mechanism will be established to receive any complaints and provide feedback on project interventions. These measures will ensure fairness in the registration process to avoid any conflict. 2. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance- All project staff, management committees, leaders and the project beneficiaries will be sensitized on Zero tolerance to sexual exploitation and abuse and keenly monitored throughout the project implementation. Complaints and feedback mechanism will play a key role to identify any signs of SEA and quickly implement mitigation measures. A phone number will be displayed in all project areas so that anybody can call and report any case. 3. Interference of external stakeholders in impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries- Stakeholders workshop will be conducted at the onset of the project to sensitize them on the project objectives, their roles, selection criteria and project activities. The county, Payam and Bomas leaders are among the stakeholders who will participate in the stakeholders workshop. This measure will ensure that there is consent on the process and results of the project.3) Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons do not participate in decision making processes- the project will facilitate the establishment of project management committees whose composition is made of representatives of youth, women, men, persons with disabilities and elderly. Through such committees the marginalised groups will participate in planning and decision making processes.
