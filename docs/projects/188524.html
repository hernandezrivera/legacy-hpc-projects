Project Summary

In 2021, Peace Hands is planning to Provide Hygiene kits to the most vulnerable HC and IDPs in Northern Syria as emergency assistance.
The project has been designed based on the assessment that has been done by the end of the year 2021 and January 2022 by Peace Hands. in cooperation with its partners and the needs of the people in Northern Syria for basic humanitarian assistance especially the WASH NFIs through direct distribution of Hygiene kits as follows:
- Distribution of 9,899 Hygiene kits for 9,899 crisis-affected groups (3,599 Kits are secured). Through the proposed project, Peace Hands aims to contribute to covering the basic WASH needs of the displaced through hygiene kits distribution.
The Hygiene kits will be designed in accordance with the Cluster recommended package. while designing the Hygiene kits, Peace Hands will take into account the beneficiaries' preference and their feedback through the latest PDMs conducted regarding the Hygiene kits distributed by Peace Hands.

4 - Needs

The majority of displaced (80%) are women and children, who are facing increased protection risks, combined without the ability to cover basic needs. Multiple displacements increase their vulnerability. It also forced them to leave behind belongings. The displaced are heading to northern Aleppo and northern Idleb, the heightened concentration of people puts an additional burden on already overstretched, but also damaged infrastructure. Most urgent is providing shelter to IDPs. However, there is a shortage of accommodation, which is forcing them to stay out of camps.The overcrowding and lack of WASH facilities have led to privacy and protection concerns for women and are increasingly forced early marriage among displaced girls as a coping mechanism. Not only rehabilitation of WASH facilities is needed, but also distribution of hygiene kits. Due to market access, multipurpose cash vouchers are a preferred modality in certain areas. Child protection concerns are heightening, also due to drop out from schools. The winter conditions are increasing the vulnerability further with the need for NFIs, food, shelter, health, and WASH. Basic services, such as health and education, are highly limited due to damaged and directly targeted infrastructure such as hospitals. Besides all lack of basic items and services, IDPs are in high need of psychosocial support and psychological ¦rst aid to accommodate them in their current situation.
