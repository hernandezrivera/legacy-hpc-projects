Project Summary

Within the broader Syria response, Mercy Corps is a leader in humanitarian coordination, chairing the Syria INGO Regional Forum (SIRF), and the NES NGO Forum, as well as providing unique, granular analyses of the context for the different coordination mechanisms through the Humanitarian Access Team (HAT) and engages in coordination at all levels to provide services, which keep the humanitarian community informed in order to adapt and design appropriate responses. This project supports NES Forum, SIRF , and HAT bodies.
Syria International NGO Regional Forum (SIRF): In light of the increasingly regional nature of the Syrian Crisis, and the humanitarian and political effects on neighboring countries, The SIRF is an INGO-led regional forum based in Amman, Jordan, set up in April 2013 to give voice to INGOs conducting humanitarian responses in the Syria region and to facilitate their collective engagement. The SIRF Representative is the internal port of call for all 58 SIRF Members and its 12 Observers, and is also the main external interlocutor for all regional humanitarian and development inter-agency coordination mechanisms led by the Regional Refugee Coordinator, the Regional Humanitarian Coordinator and the Resident and Humanitarian Coordinator for Syria. SIRF exists to serve the needs of its members and advocate on behalf of populations affected by the Syrian Crisis, articulated around four core functions: coordination, representation, advocacy and information management.
To achieve this end SIRF targets 45 WoS High level meetings and 3 policies and guidelines released by WoS coordination groups led/co-led by Mercy Corps.
NES NGO Forum: The NES NGO Forum is the lead coordination body for NGOs working from Iraq and within NES, into accessible parts of Aleppo, DEZ, Ar-Raqqa and Al-Hasakeh Governorates in Syria, as endorsed by the WoS Strategic Steering Group. Due to the unique coordination arrangements in NES and limited UN cross-border operation, the NES NGO Forum plays an expanded role, and leads an NGO-hosted sector coordination system, with core functions similar to UN OCHA field offices in other contexts. The NES Forum’s objective is to contribute to a principled, appropriate, accountable, and effective NGO response through inter-agency coordination, information management, representation and advocacy. The forum has made significant strides in ensuring a cohesive coordination structure and that NES response actors are well-represented. . The NES NGO Forum continues to lead needs identification, strategic planning and response monitoring around the humanitarian response in NES. The NES Forum also enables humanitarian space for NGOs through leading on negotiations with local authorities around NGO access and protection concerns, and direct engagement with the UN. Additionally, the Forum seeks to enhance communication and information-sharing across hubs through management of inter-sector data flow through 4Ws and response matrices.

3 - Needs

The humanitarian response to the crisis in Syria remains one of the most complex in the world. Increasing humanitarian needs, acute access restriction and segregation in aid modalities result in fragmented response. According to HNO 2023 an estimated 15.3 million people continue to require some form of humanitarian assistance in 20232, an increase of 0.7 million from 2022.The large scale of needs requires dynamic and flexible coordination support and systems to facilitate effective humanitarian response across Syria by numerous Syrian NGOs, international NGOs, the Red Cross/ Crescent Movement, and United Nations agencies. The different response modalities provided by the diverse humanitarian actors working on the Syria response, stress the heightened need for ensuring effective and efficient coordination structures.Of an estimated total population of three million people across NES, 2.8 million people have been identified in need within the HNO 2023.Households across NES are facing a continuous cycle of crises, as the spread of COVID-19 in the region compounds the vulnerabilities that were already exacerbated by a decade-long conflict and the October 2019 Turkish incursion, the effects of the Lebanese banking crisis and the consequent devaluation of the Syrian Pound and the constant worsening of the socio economic situation over the last 12 months. The NES Forum has continued strengthening its operations as the main coordination body for the NES NGO response.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Mercy Corps staff are trained on key protection principles and tools. Mercy Corps support to the coordination system in Syria aims to allow partners and participants to make safe, informed and protection oriented decisions. Mercy Corps endeavors to ensure that: 1. Information and coordination provided supports relevant, protection sensitive and community sensitive programming. There are significant protection risks associated with poor humanitarian planning and response, from exclusion of particular groups, exacerbating tensions between communities and with authorities. The SIRF, NES INGO Forum and the HAT support informed decision making and coordination with relevant groups, based in liaison with actors, staff with experience in the Syria context and analysis. 2. Information provided by coordination mechanisms and the HAT is accurate and allows for informed analysis. Mercy Corps coordination support aims to mitigate against protection risks associated with programming in unsafe locations for beneficiaries and staff. Our support is adjusted to the context, in providing daily, weekly and monthly updates where relevant to our partner organizations. Dedicated on-ground field staff support the accuracy and relevance of analysis and guidance provided. 3. Advocacy by NGO’s is relevant and informed. Coordination forums and the HAT support development of appropriate analysis for informed advocacy in the Syria context.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Participant safeguarding is central to Mercy Corps’ values, and we uphold a zero- tolerance tolerance approach towards any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. In order to prevent safeguarding violations of any kind, all staff, casual labor and external contractors receive orientation and training, and are required to sign the Code of Conduct. The safeguarding training is comprehensive and is delivered to all Mercy Corps and partner staff. Where Mercy Corps and partner staff have higher levels of contact with participants, they will receive tailored capacity strengthening on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and will participate in quarterly events, which address PSEA concerns in target communities, and facilitate the roll-out of PSEA tools and preventative actions. This process is led by our WoS Safeguarding Manager and is supported by a network of focal points within Mercy Corps. Partner staff will have the opportunity to risk assess their own areas in terms of safeguarding and create proactive action plans. Community participants will be informed on what safeguarding is and how they can report any concerns through sensitisation work across programmes. In addition, external contractors and staff such as suppliers or Third Party Monitors (TPMs) are regularly trained by department focal points who have completed tailored Training of Trainer (ToT) safeguarding training.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mercy Corps closely coordinates our contingency planning alongside the Humanitarian Access Team (HAT) which is hosted by Mercy Corps. Along with INSO, OCHA and clusteranalysis, Mercy Corps operational planning strives to minimise risk to beneficiaries, partners and staff. 2023 is a challenging operational context in northern Syria. The the humanitarian situation in north Syria deteriorated in 2022 due to the economic crisis and COVID-19 amongst other factors. According to the HAT’s Aug 2022 Report the rapid depreciation of the Syrian pound since Jan 2022 has strained the beleaguered economy of Syria and greatly diminished the already tenuous purchasing power of many Syrians. This report analyzes the continued erosion of the government’s economic and financial fundamentals following a decade of conflict, mounting economic pressures, and limited options to mitigate the decline. The depreciation is compounded by import difficulties, with sanctions preventing foreign trade with Damascus, and low foreign currency reserves limiting procurement funding. Additionally the government has sought to reduce its budget deficit through a gradual subsidy removal policy that ranges from excluding large segments of the population from receiving essential items at a subsidized price. Mercy Corps remains in close coordination with our donors and partners for appropriate contingency planning to enable and adaptable programming.
