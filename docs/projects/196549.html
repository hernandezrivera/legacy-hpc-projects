Project Summary

Over 1.8 million IDPs living in 1,421 IDP sites and camps in North-West Syria need assistance in 2023, increasing from 1.7 million in 2022 (CCCM NWS-ISIMM Plus, August 2022). Most IDP camps and sites are in Idleb (63%) Governorates, in which 80% of whom are women and children.
As a result of these challenges, in 2023, 15.3 million people will require humanitarian assistance, including 2.1 million IDPs living in last-resort IDP sites, with needs that keep increasing across all sectors. (HNO 2023)
This project aims to improve the food security status of the most vulnerable IDPs households living in camps in the Ma'aret Tamsrin sub-district within Idleb governorate through life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance by providing subsidized bread.
600 tons of flour will be secured by the donor (secured funds =264,000 USD) and sent to the bakery to be baked to distribute 690,000 bread bundles to 3870 HH (19728 individuals) in 21 camps in Maaret Tamsrin with 7860 bread bundles on daily basis 22 days monthly up to 4 months, from January 2023 to the end of April 2023.
The distribution criteria will be:
HH with 1-3 individuals will receive one bread bundle
HH with 4-6 individuals will receive two bread bundles
HH with 7-9 individuals will receive three bread bundles
HH with 10 individuals and more will receive four bread bundles
Each ton of flour can produce 1150 bread bundles, the weight of each bread bundle is 1000 gram
The targeted beneficiaries will be IDPs in 21 camps within four communities in Ma'aret Tamsrin subdistrict:
1-Kaftin, 1 camp (176 HHs, 880 individuals)
2-Maaret Elekhwan, 6 camps (1613 HHs, 8061 individuals)
3-Kafr -Kafrehmul, 9 camps (1302 HHs, 6861 individuals)
4-Haranbush, 5 camps (779 HHs, 3926)
the cost of the bread bundle will be 5 Turkish Lira.
The baking cost will include baking and transporting the bread bundles to distribution centers in the camps.
The procurement process will be in line with INSAN procurement policy by tendering process publicly. Technical specifications of the flour will be appropriate to the Syrian bread and according to the flour specification of BBWG as follows:
Moisture content: 14% maximum
Protein: 11% minimum of dry matter
Wet Gluten: 28 % minimum
Gluten Index: 85% minimum
Zeleny Index: 30 ml minimum
Second Delayed Sedimentation: Zeleny Index +5 ml minimum
HFN (max): 230 minimum including 60-second preparation)
Energy (min): 108
Ash (max): 0.65 % of dry matter
Chopin Alveograph: W 215 minimum; P 80 minimum; L 80 minimum.
Fatty Acid: Maximum 120 mg KOH per 100g dry matter.
The flour will be stored in an INSAN warehouse that is equipped according to WFP Warehouse Management A Handbook for Storekeepers of Food Aid (2001) technical standards (sterilization, pallets, windows equipped with sieves and food stocks, and preservation, storage, stacking, cleaning, pest control).
Also, storage operations will be carried out according to a cycle of papers, documents, and (GRN & GDN) signed by the receiver and the deliverer.
Production of the bread will be contracted to bakeries and the cost will cover the transportation of the
flour from the warehouse to the bakeries and the transportation of bread from the bakeries to the
distribution points. The baking cost (including transportation) per ton of flour is 140 USD.
The project will complement other free bread projects that ended on 30/11/2022 and provide 675000 free bread bundles to the same targeted beneficiaries in the same camps for four months.

3 - Needs

Over 1.8 million IDPs living in 1,421 IDP sites and camps in North-West Syria need assistance in 2023, increasing from 1.7 million in 2022 (CCCM NWS-ISIMM Plus, August 2022). Most IDP camps and sites are in Idleb (63%) Governorates, in which 80% of whom are women and children (HNO,2023). According to WFP remote food security monitoring, (especially female-headed and children) reported facing food shortages and engaging in some sort of food coping mechanism to maximize the use of little available resources. for instance, households adopting IPC crisis or beyond food coping like mechanisms reached 50 percent, although 15 percent of households had to withdraw children from school, increasing risks for child labor and related protection concerns, 52 percent of households made tough decision of restrict food consumption for adults.In addition, the end of the ongoing project "Improve resilience and livelihood through supporting Wheat Value Chain at Al Rouj Valley, and food security to IDPs residing in camps at Ma’aret Tmasreen"( on November 30, 2022) which was funded by OCHA and implemented by INSAN and covered these camps for 4 months with free bread will have a big impact on a majority of the families that live in that camps and will make a great burden on them, taking in consideration that the price of bread bundle weight 500 gram is 5 T.L in Idleb.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The risks of GBV Likelihood: Medium MM:1- Ensure that complaint mechanism is accessible and in place for all people by WhatsApp and email which is directly connected to M&E manger in Turkey office, complaints box in the distribution center, and face to face complaints by M&E officers in the field2-Capacity building for all the staff on GBV.3-choosing safe and secure distribution places and separating distribution queues one for males and the other for females.4-GBV complaints will be dealt with urgency and confidential manner with referral cases to the focal point assigned by the GBV sub-cluster.5-Distribution will be done at the daytime to avoid any possibility to GBV cases, and the distribution point will be located inside every camp to facilitate access for beneficiaries.6- INSAN team will hand over bread to the tents of Disabled, sick, and elderly people.7-The management team, monitoring and evaluation team, and accountability officer are comprised of men, women, and minorities, with no child labor.8- IC will respect the society customs (where men will not be allowed to enter tents for women) therefore Female staff will be involved in the project to hear the voice of the most vulnerable people such as disabled, widowed and girls.Risk of child labor:1-It will not be allowed for any child under 15 to receive bread bundles instead of his/he parents. 2-All the contractors and suppliers will sign the code of conduct for the prevention of any kind of child labor.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk of PSEA: MM:1- Effective systems will be in place for preventing and responding to acts of SEA by integrating PSEA in policies and making sure that all personnel read and signed the code of conduct, and that all contracts with suppliers/contractors include clauses on PSEA.2-INSAN will ensure that the complaint mechanism for reporting SEA is accessible (WhatsApp, email, etc.) to all beneficiaries. 3-PSEA focal points will be assigned and will be responsible for receiving complaints and taking the appropriate action to protect persons from retaliation where allegations of SEA will be reported in a timely and professional manner and all complaints will be treated with high confidentiality.4-INSAN will hire accountability female officers and female distributors to alleviate the sensitivity for families screened and to support societal customs (where men are not allowed to enter tents for women). This will make access to information easier for women and girls,5-Also, PSEA banners, and posters which include PSEA network phone numbers and emails will be hung on project sites.6-PSEA banners which include scenarios of PSEA cases will raise awareness of beneficiaries against any sexual harassment or abuse they may face during the implementation of the project.7-INSAN is a member of the PSEA network and reports regularly.8-All the staff will attend training on PSEA and mitigation measures to prevent any case and how to report in case there are any PSEA allegations.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

-Risk: Increasing fuel priceMitigation: sign a contract with the bakery based on stable prices throughout the project duration.-Risk: Beneficiaries selling breadMitigation: Applying distribution criteria with 250 cal/ person-Risk: low quality of bread.Mitigation: -Conducting daily visits to the bakery by MEAL team to verify the quality of the bread, -Daily Reports will be prepared about bread quality by distributors, camps managers, and project officers in addition to reports shared by the MEAL team ,- All the beneficiaries will have access to the complaints mechanism which includes ( Whatsapp numbers, call numbers, Emails, complaints boxes, and field MEAL team to report any complaints about bread quality where immediate actions will be taken to handle the problem.-Risk: overlapping with other organizations.Mitigation: Coordination with the FSL cluster and operating organizations. Assumption: Security situation is stable-Risk: Security issues such as shelling or local fighting Mitigation: Move to a different location plan B after consulting with donor, and cluster. However, the area is known to be safe.-Risk: outbreak of Cholera and COVID-19 Pandemic Likelihood: Medium. Impact: HighMitigate:INSAN will take the necessary measures, procedures, and precautions to protect workers and beneficiaries and will mitigate the pandemic effect if happened based on WHO, NWS health and nutrition cluster & CDC recommendations, and guidelines.
