Project Summary

The proposed project will contribute to improving the food security, nutrition and WASH services to people affected by emergency and protracted displacement in South Kordofan, South Darfur state (focus on East Jebel Marra), plus livelihoods programming for host communities/refugees in White Nile, Kassala and Gedaref. The approach is to implement activities that have the potential to achieving impact on resilience and food security for chronically food insecure households; improving their nutrition outcomes; creating market driven livelihood opportunities; and improving WASH services for these communities.

Needs

South Darfur/Food security: Conflict-affected households continue to struggle to restore their livelihoods and meet their immediate food security needs. According to a May 2018 WFP Food Security Monitoring report, 72.2 percent of households were classified food insecure. Displaced populations in South Darfur were most severely affected by food insecurity, with the proportion of food insecure households at the state-level found to be 87 percent. Food prices have increased significantly, with a 3 kg bag of sorghum in Nyala increasing from 31 SDG in January to 50 SDG in June 2018. Nutrition: In addition to the ongoing food insecurity, malnutrition rates in Sudan are amongst the highest in the region. 16.8 percent of children under five years experience wasting and 38.2 percent experience stunting. The situation is exacerbated by the lack of trained nutrition staff and supplies at the existing health centers.WASH: Preliminary assessment findings indicate that the current water infrastructure across the target locations in SKS is inadequate and far below the minimum Sphere standards of 15 liters/per person/per day (l/p/d). A full assessment of the existing water infrastructure will be completed as part of the proposal process to establish which water infrastructure will be rehabilitated. Insufficient access to water infrastructure forces the population to access unprotected water sources, risking exposure to waterborne diseases. In addition, the target communities have limited access to sanitation services and there are high rates of open defecation. South Kordofan/ Food security: Conflict-affected households continue to struggle to meet their urgent food security needs. In 2018, approximately 15% of the population in South Kordofan experienced IPC 3 (Crisis) andIPC 4 (Emergency) food insecurity. Mercy Corps completed community consultations in Abukershola in March 2018 and found that an estimated 90% of households surveyed in the target locations are unable to meet their food consumption needs during the lean season and are forced to rely on negative coping mechanisms. WASH: Much of the existing water infrastructure in the target localities has been destroyed by conflict and the few remaining water sources have been overstretched by the arrival of high numbers of IDPs and returnees. The current water infrastructure is inadequate to meet the needs of the population. A rapid needs assessment of WASH infrastructure completed on 17 October 2018 revealed that the targeted population lacks critical WASH services, including access to safe drinking water and access to sanitation and hand washing facilities. White Nile/Gederaf/Kassala: Refugee response needs detailed in previous submission

Activities

Activities will include water infrastructure development (using dual powered water pumps with solar energy); hygiene promotion and sanitation focused (CLTS) activities; integrated IYCF nutrition education; targeted agricultural input distributions; market-driven livelihoods focused income generating activities, including vocational trainings; local governance structure strengthening; and the potential use of cash as a modality for all potential activities. Mercy Corps has completed a market assessment in South Kordofan and is planning to carry out a market assessment in South Darfur which will inform future programming. Under its FSL programming, Mercy Corps plans to undertake one-off distributions of seeds and tools using restricted value vouchers. While in WASH, Mercy Corps also proposes to carry out once-off distributions of hygiene items using restricted value vouchers. The voucher amount will be determined by local price monitoring. Mercy Corps proposes also to implement a Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) programme that meets the food, NFI and WASH needs of the target population. Mercy Corps will coordinate with the Cash Working Group and WFP to determine the minimum expenditure basket which will help to set the value amount. The duration of this assistance will be determined by the needs of the beneficiaries and the project type. Any MPCA activities will be complemented by the development of sustainable livelihood to support the transition of households out of the MPCA.

Impact on environment/natural resources

Mercy Corps will adopt an ecosystem level approach to project implementation that assesses key ecosystems, identifies current and future vulnerabilities, and works to assure activities do no harm to these systems, and ideally enhances their capacity to support human activity over the long-term. At the start of the project, Mercy Corps will conduct research to analyze pertinent environmental and climate trends, challenges, and identify programmatic opportunities, risks, risk reduction and capacity-strengthening. Given Sudan is one of the countries in the world most vulnerable to climate variability and change, this process will ensure social and environmental do-no-harm, and support innovative, integrated climate and environment risk management best practices to foster long-term resilience-building of communities, livelihoods and ecosystems. In addition, Mercy Corps will ensure community engagement through the use of local governance structures, including the Community Action Planning approach, to include stronger community cohesion and decision-making about risk reduction, climate mitigation (carbon sequestration) and strengthening of critical environmental services through efforts towards reforestation, watershed management, among others. The process will be guided to ensure the highest order of mitigation, environmental restoration and protection, and improving the climate and environmental resilience of the ecosystems, communities and livelihoods within them, including criteria of equity, environmental sustainability and do-no-harm.
