Project Summary

The project will work with host Rohingya and community youths through livelihood development, skills building, entrepreneurship development. Key focus of the project is to reduce stress of the targeted population through creating economic opportunities.
Theory of Change:
If, vulnerable local communities and Rohingya, including women, are provided with tangible and sustainable economic options, and a better understanding of peacebuilding, leadership, mediation and social cohesion concepts
Then narratives exploiting inequality and injustice between groups in Cox’s Bazar will be less attractive, as local people and Rohingya community have both the skills and incentives to support peace;
Resulting in improved economic stability, women’s empowerment, and local capacity to address and defuse conflicts as they arise
This will in turn contribute to increased stability, social cohesion and economic well-being for the most vulnerable in the region, including women, helping Bangladesh to meet its international commitments in support of the Rohingya Joint Response Plan and the Sustainable Development Goals.
Activity 1.1: HOST COMMUNITY SKILLS DEVELOPMENT
Women will be encouraged to participate and benefit from all types of training listed below, though it should be noted that Cox’s Bazar is considered within Bangladesh to be a religiously and socially conservative region.
a) Hospitality and Eco-Tourism Industry: Cox’s Bazar hosts the world’s longest sand beach, at 120km, and is a popular domestic tourist destination. However, many of the city’s beach hotels rely on higher-skilled workers from other parts of Bangladesh. UNDP support to train both men and women from host communities in skills useful to the hospitality industry (including the region’s burgeoning interest in eco-tourism) could help local people take advantage of the region’s tourist appeal and contribute to an improved sense of local economic stability.
b) Blue Economy and Fishing Industry: The primary industries in Ukhia and Teknaf Upazilas are fishing (and the subsequent dry fish industry) both at sea and in the Naf river, as well as shrimp cultivation. UNDP is considering support to train local host community youth to develop skills related, not necessarily to fishing itself, but to the preparation and distribution of fish products to domestic market. Training on fishing can also be related to sustainable means of harvesting and producing seafood (fish and seaweeds), and can encourage young women in particular to become involved in more of the entrepreneurial and processing side of this important industry.
Activity 1.2: ROHINGYA SKILLS DEVELOPMENT
The proposed training for refugees will initially aim to build skills that will be applicable primarily within the camps.
a) Market and Trade-Based Training: UNDP proposes teaching refugees basic financial and numeric literacy and household budgeting, to help the Rohingya to be more reliable and predictable trading partners and neighbours. Possible trades will be selected based on value chain assessments that could deliberately link graduates to host community suppliers, building interconnectivity and trust through trade.
b) Life Skills Training: The purpose of life skills training is to empower refugees to be able to make informed decisions. Life skills training could include household budgeting or negotiation for women making daily purchases, to help them bargain more effectively for a better price for their families. UNDP will select possible modules based on assessments in the camps and consultations with refugees, to ensure that life skills training responds to pressing and legitimate local needs, ensuring that activities are inclusive, practical, and personally relevant.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

UNDP has consistently noted that host communities feel neglected and burdened by the crisis response in Cox’s Bazar after the influx in 2017. Although both Cox’s Bazar has coped with such influxes before, Rohingya refugees now outnumber the host community in many areas, notably by a factor of two-to-one in the Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazilas. Increased costs of living, increased reports of crime, and reduced access to scarce land and natural resources have fed a common perception that the Rohingya are an unwelcome burden for which the host community has to disproportionately bear the costs. The physical impact on the region has also been dramatic and alarming – an estimated three thousand acres of forest have been cleared to build new settlements and to provide firewood. The affected Upazilas lost most forest cover by end of 2018. Daily wages in Cox’s Bazar are estimated to have dropped significantly where Rohingya compete with host communities for opportunities, as Rohingya have been charging an average 50-200 taka less per day for labour (this undercuts pre-existing local daily wages by 20-60%). This is a serious concern, given an estimated 37-50% of households in the host community in Teknaf and Ukhiya rely on unskilled labour for their income. Simultaneously, the image of food convoys and supplies bypassing host communities for the camps has led to a perception that the international community does not care for anyone who is not Rohingya.

2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project aimed to cover the principle 3 through its work. The project will work with the host community youths and partly with Rohingya community through UN Women to ensure service delivery and capacity building in both communities.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The project will aim to reduce gender based violence as part of the bigger picture. As women participants will be specifically engaged, there will be significant contribution in women's economic empowerment and leadership development.

3.2 - Gender

The projects aims to ensure participation of women with a target of 60% in its training component. The trades for training will be selected in consultation with the women participation. Camp level work will only cover women and girls. At the host community, the work on community engagement will prioritize participation of women and girl. Through its cultural components it will highlight women's empowerment and leadership quaility.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The project will not specifically work with children, but it will include elderly people.

3.4 - Diversity

Indigenous community, persons with disability, religious and sexual minorities will get specific attention from the project.
