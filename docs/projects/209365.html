Project Summary

Sudanese civilians have endured more than eight months of violent conflict since clashes erupted between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) in April 2023. The conflict initially started in Khartoum but quickly spread to Darfur and the Kordofans, followed by an extension of fighting into central and eastern regions of Sudan in December 2023. This ongoing conflict has affected every state in the country.
Gender-based violence (GBV) remains a life-threatening concern across Sudan in 2024. The situation has been worsened by various factors, including armed conflict, tribal clashes, forced displacement, and recurring natural disasters such as floods. The fighting that began on April 15, 2023 between the SAF and RSF has resulted in hundreds of casualties and, according to figures from the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), 7.4 million people have been displaced, seeking refuge both inside and outside Sudan. Consequently, 6.7 million people require GBV prevention and response services, with the GBV Sub Sector aiming to provide prevention, mitigation, and response measures to 1.8 million individuals, prioritizing women and girls in conflict- and disaster-affected areas, and areas hosting refugees. A significant number of households in these areas have resorted to negative coping mechanisms. The severity of protection needs is categorized as critical (4-5) in 143 localities across the 18 states of Sudan.
UNFPA aims to fully address the GBV sub-sector target for individuals in severity ranking 5, achieve 50% of the target for severity ranking 4, and approximately 30% of the target for severity ranking 3 .
This project has three main objectives. First, the project will provide life-saving GBV services of high quality for the next 11 months. These services include comprehensive support for survivors, case management, psychosocial support, and reinforcement of referral pathways. The project will particularly focus on women and girls with disabilities and adolescent girls and is aligned with the Humanitarian Response Plan's Strategic Objective 1. The services provided will encompass case management, psychosocial support, referrals, assessments, community awareness-raising, distribution of dignity kits, and capacity building for frontline GBV actors.
Second, the project will integrate GBV risk mitigation measures into all aspects of the humanitarian response. This objective, aligned with the Humanitarian Response Plan's Strategic Objective 3, involves training non-GBV humanitarian actors on GBV-related issues. Third, the project will strengthen the national and sub-national coordination mechanisms for GBV, while aiming to enhance data collection and information management related to GBV.
Overall, the project seeks to deliver essential GBV prevention and response services, promote GBV risk mitigation, and enhance coordination and data management to address the urgent needs of vulnerable women and girls affected by the conflict and other crises in Sudan.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

UNFPA's primary focus is on promoting the active engagement and meaningful participation of targeted communities, regardless of gender, age, or vulnerability, throughout the entire project cycle. This approach aims to ensure that survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) and those at risk do not face additional harm. Furthermore, the project will prioritize the mainstreaming of meaningful access, safety, and dignity when implementing various activities.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The project will continue to strengthen the sharing of information regarding the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in the targeted states. This will be achieved by raising awareness among the target communities about their rights and establishing effective feedback and complaints mechanisms, including specific channels for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse incidents. Capacity building initiatives, which began in 2023, will be expanded to include community leaders, volunteers, aid workers, and local actors. These efforts aim to address the knowledge gap on preventing and responding to sexual exploitation and abuse among service providers and communities.The PSEA capacity building training will focus on enhancing the knowledge and skills of UNFPA staff, implementing partners, NGOs, and volunteers. It will provide participants with the necessary tools to effectively prevent, identify, and address cases of sexual exploitation and abuse, including the use of standard operating procedures (SOPs).The AAP training will focus on improving participants' understanding of AAP principles and practices. This training will ensure that humanitarian interventions are responsive, inclusive, and transparent.In collaboration with community members, including camp committees and vulnerable groups such as women, youth, and persons with disabilities (PWDs), UNFPA will develop or adopt information, education, and communication (IEC) materials on PSEA and disability inclusion. These materials will be translated into local languages for accessibility. They will provide guidance on how to report cases and access assistance. Community sensitization programs will then be conducted using these materials to raise awareness about the rights of beneficiaries and the expected standards of conduct for humanitarian workers. UNFPA with partners will also establish a complaint and feedback mechanism in WGSS and health centers supported by UNFPA. Additionally UNFPA will place focal points to ensure that there are multiple complaints and feedback pathways in place, either through frequent field visits by focal points during the post-distribution monitoring phase or over the phone. Furthermore, SEA risk assessments will be conducted in the target states to identify emerging risks and inform the project's response and mitigation strategies

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Accountability is a fundamental aspect of the UNFPA response, and it is strengthened through ongoing consultations with the affected population to ensure their satisfaction with the services provided and the modalities of implementation. UNFPA actively participates in the national Technical and Coordination Team for Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and leads the Survivor Support Task Team. Working closely with team members, UNFPA ensures that GBV referral mechanisms are readily accessible to survivors of PSEA. UNFPA will provide implementing partners and targeted communities with information about PSEA, including how to report incidents and access essential services such as healthcare, legal aid, and psychosocial support. UNFPA actively involves its partners and beneficiaries in the decision-making process, particularly in the planning, implementation, and evaluation of activities. This is achieved through focus group discussions with women and girls held in women's centers. The project aims to enhance the self-protection capacities of beneficiaries and seeks to improve their daily lives by strengthening both formal and informal protection mechanisms. This approach is considered the most effective way to address gender inequality, which is the underlying cause of GBV.
