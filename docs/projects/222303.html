Project Summary

The project aims to provide safe, inclusive, and quality primary education to vulnerable children, out-of-school youth, and children aged 3 to 18 in South Sudan by the year 2025. It will be implemented in Pibor County of the Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA), Jonglei State, Aweil East County in Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Gogrial West County, and Twic county in Warrap State The education project is expected to benefit a total of 16621 individuals, including 8771 learners, 8771 boys, 7691 girls; 119 teachers, comprising 74 males and 45 females; and 35 members of Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) and School Management Committees (SMCs), with 23 males and 12 females.
The project aims to provide children and youth with access to safe, protective, and inclusive educational services tailored to their age, learning level, and specific needs. This objective will be accomplished by facilitating the enrollment of 5489 learners (2807boys, 2682 girls) in pre-primary, primary, and secondary schools or learning centers; setting up and refurbishing 3 temporary learning facilities for children affected by various emergencies; building 3 gender-segregated WASH facilities in educational settings; organizing four back-to-learning campaigns to help enroll out-of-school children in four counties; tracking and mapping teachers to aid in reintegrating them into the education system to ensure educational continuity in four counties; establishing child help desks in 101 schools; supplying and distributing sanitation and hygiene materials to 101 schools; and providing dignity kits for 2630 girls in educational institutions.
Second, the project aims to ensure that children and youth receive quality teaching and learning services that promote retention and progress throughout the education cycle. To achieve this objective, we will train 119 teachers (74 males and 45 female) in emergency education (EiE) pedagogical skills and child-centered methodologies. This training will enhance the teaching and learning process for children affected by emergencies. Additionally, we will provide 8885 emergency-affected children (4340 boys and 4545 girls) with quality recreational educational and learning materials. We will also distribute of ECD kits to 2087(1044 boys,2087 girls) learners in schools and train the same group of 119 teachers (74 males and 45female) in Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) to aid in the mental health recovery of learners.
Finally, the project aims to enhance coordination and crisis management capabilities to ensure effective, accountable, and timely education during emergencies. This will involve training 45 individuals (23 males and 12 females), including School Management Committees (SMCs), Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs), and education authorities, on school management, governance, psychosocial support, and Disaster Risk Reduction.

1. Needs

According to HNRP 2024, 2 million children and adolescents between 6 and 17 years of age (53 per cent are girls), along with 1 million children between 3 and 5 years of age encounter significant obstacles in accessing education including early marriage and GBV, especially for girls. The IPC classification and inter-sectoral classification classified Awei East and Pibor county as geographical areas of high priority with high level of severity, targeted for humanitarian intervention for Education in Emergencies services; through a multi-sectoral approach to address the holistic needs of vulnerable children (South Sudan IPC classification 2024). Educational attainment is critically low due to a shortage of schools, qualified teachers, and educational materials. With one of the world’s lowest literacy rates—27% for adults and just 15% for women—approximately 2.8 million children are out of school, representing 59% of all children aged 3-17 years, of whom 53% are girls (World Bank (2022 Report). The education sector is further hindered by inadequate infrastructure in conflict-affected areas, where 30% of schools are damaged, destroyed, occupied, or closed. The shortage of trained professionals exacerbates the issue, with some urban schools having a pupil-to-teacher ratio as high as 100, and only 21% of teachers being properly trained(UNICEF). Various factors contribute to the sector's poor performance, including the direct and indirect costs of services, safety concerns around education facilities, low population density, language barriers, food insecurity, and limited access to accurate information and education. Additionally, there is a severe lack of technical and vocational education and training opportunities for young people, with demand vastly exceeding supply. In August 2024, South Sudan experienced heavy rainfall, which severely affected over 154,960 people, particularly in Aweil East and Aweil South. According to the Initial Rapid Needs Assessment Report from August 2024, 13 schools in Aweil East were impacted by the flooding. The assessment noted that schools had to close, leading to interruptions in educational activities at Kunyuk, Warakoon, and Wath-manual primary schools, which operate with only three classrooms. Many temporary schools also saw students drop out due to displacement and relocation to other villages. Approximately 1,800 pupils—750 boys and 1,050 girls—were affected by the floods in areas such as Riang Mal, Kunyuk, Mabok Tong, and Wath Malual, among others. Additionally, the floods impacted access to educational materials, with reports indicating that the water destroyed many important learning resources in the affected regions.
