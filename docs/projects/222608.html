1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Between October 2024 – March 2025 more than 1.3 million people in Cabo Delgado, Niassa, and Nampula provinces are estimated to be suffering “crisis” or “emergency” levels of hunger, of which over 874,000 are in Cabo Delgado alone with 235,545 people in IPC 4, as per the latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis. WFP’s assistance to conflict- and displacement-affected communities in northern Mozambique in the context of a deteriorating humanitarian crisis has a three- pronged approach: 1) Provision of life-saving food assistance through General Food Distributions (GFD) and Food Assistance for Assets (FFA) or Cash for Work (CFW) using different transfer modalities that include in-kind and cash-based modalities, 2)Provision of life-sustaining livelihood support for mid- and long- term food security.@. WFP is aiming to reach 661,457 people with humanitarian food assistance of which 21,500 will be under a Food Assistance for Assets scheme under Objective 1 of the FSC. In terms of livelihood support, WFP is aiming to reach 153,750 people under Objective 2. Where security and market conditions allow, assistance will be provided in the form of food vouchers. In-kind assistance will take place in locations where conditions are not conducive for market-based modalities.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

WFP is committed to ensuring the centrality of protection throughout the humanitarian assistance. WFP takes calculated measures to ensure a conflict sensitive approach so that actions do not negatively affect humanitarian access in the immediate nor long term, considering the rapidly changing dynamics in the evolving conflict. WFP works with humanitarian actors through local and national level coordination groups such as the protection cluster and community engagement/ AAP working group to share and apply feedback from the affected population. Findings from surveys and other community-based assessments are analyzed and reflected in operational planning, in parallel with reports from the CFM as relevant and possible in the dynamic context. WFP is committed to ensuring zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse, ensured through codes of conduct, online and face-to-face training, and several reporting mechanisms (see above). As the lead agency for Linha Verde 1458, WFP works with the PSEA network to ensure awareness of the service and to determine constructive approaches to handle SEA cases in a timely manner internally and with the Government as relevant. WFP is committed to action on SEA reports within 24 hours, prioritizing the safety and well-being of the survivor, working in collaboration with WFP’s ethics office and OIGI.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

WFP involves beneficiaries in all stages of the project lifecycle. In the design phase of this project, beneficiaries have been consulted on the choice of modality, considering security, distance from markets and food preferences. WFP has established community committees via a community election process. The beneficiaries engage in consultations via the community committee platform and via focus group discussions ensuring continuous feedback on the assistance provided. Minority and vulnerable groups are targeted for focus group discussions to ensure that they have a voice. Well-established community feedback mechanisms exist, consisting of help desks staffed by CP staff and complaints and feedback boxes at distribution sites. Linha Verde 1458, the tollfree inter-agency hotline, managed by WFP is accessible to the affected population, providing clarifications on beneficiary rights, capturing needs and associated gaps in the assistance as well as linking the affected population to vital services in the cases of SEA, SGBV and child protection. The field office benefits from a designated protection and AAP team that ensures that communications are up to date and that issues raised by the affected population cases followed up on accordingly in order to close the feedback loop in a timely manner. Finally, WFP will continue operating an inter- agency hotline, 'Linha Verde 1458', on the behalf of the HCT.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Recognizing the challenges faced by people living with disabilities (PWD) to access assistance, WFP is taking targeted action to address this through several methods. For instance, by ensuring inclusion and participation of PWD in community committees, by engaging the disability working group to directly refer PWD and their families to food assistance, and by working with the Trinity College of Dublin to develop communications supports with the dual goal of tackling both communication barriers caused by disability and language generally due to high illiteracy and mixed language needs of the displaced population. Moving forward, in order to identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to them accessing assistance, WFP will include Washington Group questions in its Vulnerability Based Targeting exercises in Cabo Delgado. These responses will be used to guide operations. Further, in 2024, we developed a checklist for safe a dignified distributions which includes actions to remove barriers to access, for example ensuring people with disabilities can easily enter and exit the food distribution point and have priority access to toilets.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

All program activities will be screened for environmental and social risks during design, at a stage when the implementation details (i.e. location, targeted beneficiaries, inputs, transfer modalities, outputs, and timeline) are being defined, but before the design is finalized. From 2020, The WFP Environmental and Social Safeguards are the set of principles, standards, and tools to avoid or manage the risk of causing environmental or social harm to people, communities, or the natural environment. The WFP Environmental and Social Standards will be used to serve as a benchmark for the risk screening and risk management. All the asset creation activities planned as part of the FFA will be analyzed using WFP’s Environmental and Social Risks assessments tools. Risk screening not only helps identify environmental and social risks: each activity will be classified by the level of the risk: low, medium, or high. Any program activity that entails some risks (medium or high) will come with risk management measures that aim to avoid or reduce the risks or mitigate or offset the expected adverse impacts.
