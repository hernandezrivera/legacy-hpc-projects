Project Summary

Somali region is prone to recurrent natural and man made calamities. Three consecutive failed rainy seasons have brought on severe drought in the region drying up water wells, killing livestock and crops and pushing hundreds of thousands of children and their families to the brink. “Children and their families are struggling to survive due to loss of livelihoods and livestock and it is projected that more than 6.8 million people will be in need of urgent humanitarian assistance by mid-March 2022. We are also witnessing major displacement out of affected areas.” There is a significant lack of clean water and the food security is deteriorating rapidly leading to an increase in malnutrition cases. The drought is compromising fragile livelihoods heavily reliant on livestock and causing a worsening food security and nutrition while eroding coping strategies for the most vulnerable: The number of livestock dying from lack of food and water is staggering and increasing by the day. The high number of livestock deaths is an important indicator of this alarming situation. Some 3.1 million people are facing water shortage in 83 woredas of Somali regional state, where 864, 043 animals have died from July last year to February. (UNICEF, NDRMC) The drought disproportionally affected women and children, exacerbating existing protection threats and vulnerabilities and creating new ones. Informed gender-integrated responses are needed to strengthen access of the most vulnerable people to humanitarian services, according to the specific needs of age, gender, and disabilities.
In response to this, the proposed intervention will address key child protection issues and put in place a resilient child protection system relevant in the crisis contexts with protective, promotive, and responsive services that protect children from violence, exploitation, abuse, and harmful practices among vulnerable and target group to protection risks, human rights violations and gaps in available service, are identified and addressed through protection monitoring and analysis and rapid protection assessment; access to essential services is enhanced through strengthened referral pathways (including transport referral cases) and advocacy by social worders) & MHPSS. Moreover, the response activities and support are based on the components of the Individuals receive one to one information, counseling, and technical/legal assistance and are supported through referrals to access to basic services (with a focus on health services) as well as Case management for girls and boys who have experienced violence (i.e., exploitation, abuse, harmful practices such as child marriage and/or female genital mutilation). Risk mitigation, prevention, and response interventions to address gender based violence (GBV) is accorded due attention. In addition, the proposed intervention addresses the needs to put in place a safe, accessible, and child-sensitive reporting mechanisms for PSEA. Through prioritizing the safety & dignity of people of concern rigorous effort made to avoid causing harm in so doing, prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended adverse effects of intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. As far as the project beneficiaries are concerned, there are 9,558 (3,900 girls, 2,750 boys, 1,865 women and 1,043 men) in worst hit area of Shebele, Afder, Dawa, Liben and Nogob Zones.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

To ensure community participation and collect feedback, regular community meetings and focus group discussions will be held. OWS DF will also use these forums to share information on OWS DF, the project activities, selection criteria, procedures for communities to raise complaints or give feedback as well as address other community concerns. This information will also be shared with communities through other platforms such as community representatives, posters, household visits. To ensure representation of vulnerable groups, FGDs conducted with communities will also purposely target women, persons living with disabilities, the elderly, the youth and other marginalized groups.In addition, OWS DF will use a complaint handling mechanism which is part of CFM where any members of the community can anonymously drop comments/complaints they may have regarding the services provided and behaviors of OWS DF staff for example. The aforementioned committee will be formed, strengthened to address such concerns. This committee will be responsible for regularly reviewing all complaints and based on their decision, take appropriate action to address any genuine complaint or grievance presented.Based on the feedback identified OWS DF has various response mechanisms to the beneficiary based on their decision levels.Field level: the need of the community or individual will be responded by field staff, volunteer and CFM Example. Home visit timing, discussion session, urgent consultation needed and similar issues.Project level: Project level staff can respond and solve community or individual needs.Office level: Regularly adjusting project monitoring activities based on community feedback and learning is key to delivering high-quality services accepted by the community. example, following monitoring finding decisions can be made by OWS DF management.Donor: If the need of community requires the involvement of the donor, in consultation with the donor modification can be made.OWS DF will protect and keep the confidentiality of reports, feed backs and any sensitive issues regarding the project both from the beneficiary and the employee. In response to any irregularity and sensitive issues regarding the project or protection issue, OWS DF will conduct careful identification, analysis and drafting results that can help to discuss with the concerned parties to solve the problem. After discussion and reach on common agreement, planning for the way out will be done together. Because the solution cannot be drawn in one side (OWS DF), but from different actors (stakeholders). To reduce existing gender disparity, harmful traditional practice, gender-based violence and protect the environment from degradation, awareness raising session with the communities will be prioritized. Women, persons with disabilities, older person, child-headed households will be enabled to participate in planning, implementation and evaluation. These marginalized sectors of the community will be encouraged to actively get involved in FGD and village project related communication.
