Project Summary

AVSI's 2023/2024 intervention utilizes an integrated approach to address the WASH needs of the population in Eastern Equatoria (Kapoeta East) and Lakes State (Cueibet).
The overall objective of the interventions will be to increase water and hygiene services access of the selected areas. The integrated approach will lead to increase also the food security of the selected communities, having as main targets food insecure communities classified in IPC 5, 4 and 3 with special attention to single mothers, returnees and vulnerable households. WASH
service delivery will integrate emergency to reliance interventions, aiming to strengthen the
productive bases of agricultural and livestock communities to enhance overall production, as well as school population. At the same time, targeting the most vulnerable by integrating them into active roles within more developed groups and production systems.
The project is designed to tackle the severe challenge of limited water access in the selected locations in EES and Lakes State. Grounded in global standards such as the Sphere Standards, the intervention’s approach intertwines direct infrastructural development with capacity-building initiatives.
AVSI aims at establishing and improving water sources. This includes drilling new boreholes in the remote payams where there is the higher lack of water access.
AVSI will also focus on motorizing pre-existing boreholes (solarization), ensuring optimized water access for these communities. Moreover, AVSI will build latrines, specifically targeting schools, to enhance WASH services for the students. Training and awareness sessions will be conducted in these schools and communities to emphasize the significance of proper hygiene practices for well-being and disease prevention. By enhancing water facilities and fostering community awareness, AVSI envisions a two-fold impact: immediate alleviation and long-term behaviour transformation.
A key aspect of AVSI approach is the incorporation of community-driven initiatives, exemplified by the establishment of water management committees. This strategy not only fosters a sense of ownership but also ensures that interventions are tailored on the distinct needs of each community.
The project will ensure existent of operational linkages and joint programming with other relevant sectors such as nutrition, FSL, health, protection and food security through effectives
coordination mechanism that will stimulate integrated approach and dignified access to
multi-sectoral basic service which will further reduce food insecurity and increase
resilience. Accountability to affected population (AAP) will be mainstreamed by ensuring
that programming of FSL services approach is community/people centred in such a way
that those in needs are fully engaged throughout the program implementation to ensure
that the intervention is responsive enough to the arrays of the changing needs.

1. Needs

In 2023, the well-being of an estimated 6.1 million people in South Sudan will be compromised due to inadequate access to safe water and improved sanitation. According to the latest Humanitarian Needs Overview , despite four consecutive long rainy seasons replenishing major waterways, 42% of the population lacks access to improved water sources. Alarmingly, 45% need to walk for over 30 minutes to access their primary water source, posing significant risks to women, girls, and persons with disabilities due to increased exposure to GBV. In certain areas, over 90% of the population relies on rivers or puddles as their main water source. Such reliance on untreated surface water, combined with prevalent open defecation, poses grave public health risks, especially during floods.According to AVSI’s 2022 Multisector survey conducted in Cueibet county, 53% has to walk more than 30 minutes to reach the closer water point and 58% has to spend more than 30 minutes in queue. Additionally, it has been revealed that 15% uses either surface water or rivers as source of drinking water and 53% has to change water source depending on the season. The situation is similar in Rumbek North, where 65% has to walk for more than 30 minutes, 64% has to spend more than 30 minutes in queue and 58% has to change water source each season. Data also shows that a staggering 29% uses surface water or rivers as main drinking water source.In Greater Kapoeta, 88% households do not have toilet, they use bush for defecation with no particular rules, the greater percentage is in Kapoeta North (AVSI Multisector Survey, Greater Kapoeta, 2021). In Kapoeta East, only 3% of the HHS are using toilets, meaning that almost the whole population commonly eased themselves in open defecation, which leads to more spreading of diseased. Furthermore, HHs in Kauto payam consume 16 Jerrycans of water in a day. 47% of the HHs reported that they are aware of conflicts over water and land. It is not surprising considering that there is shortage of water points in Kauto payam (AVSI Contextual Analysis in Kauto, 2023).In Ikwoto County, 31% uses water from rivers for drinking and 43% of the HHS reported water as a cause of dispute in the community (AVSI Multisector Survey, 2021).Poor WASH infrastructure has multi-sectoral impacts. The combination of inadequate WASH services and food insecurity severely affects the health of vulnerable populations. Nationally, the GAM prevalence for children aged 0-59 months stands at 16%, with a 5.9% SAM prevalence. The presence of water-borne diseases, such as diarrhoea in children under five, further underscores the dire WASH conditions. Furthemore, it has many implication on GBV and protection issues, since women are the ones in charge of taking the water from the boreholes (93% in Ikwoto County). For example, only in Ikwoto County, 31% of the HHs walks more than 30 minutes to get water. In Greater Kapoeta, Cueibet and Rumbek North, the situation is even worst. (AVSI Multisector Survey Ikwoto, 2021)Addressing the urgent WASH issues in Cueibet, Rumbek North and Greater Kapoeta, the project is poised to substantially enhance water access, sanitation, and hygiene education in these severely impacted regions. Elevating water accessibility is pivotal for improving the community's living conditions, and concurrently, amplifying their understanding of optimal WASH and hygiene practices is crucial to curtail waterborne diseases.

2. Project activities

CO1: Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition:- 21,600 of SAM children/caretakers provided with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion (21,600)- 36 latrines constructed/rehabilitated in nutrition facilities as long as selected schools and identified strategic places within the communities in the counties of Cueibet and Rumbek North in Lakes State, Ikwoto, Kapoeta East, North and South in Eastern Equatoria - 36 boreholes constructed/ rehabilitated in nutrition facilities as long as selected schools and identified strategic places within the communities in the counties of Cueibet and Rumbek North in Lakes State, Ikwoto, Kapoeta East, North and South in Eastern Equatoria. The boreholes rehabilitated will be motorized/solarized, so that the water access will increase compared to a hand- pump borehole and this will guarantee environmental and context sustainability. - Establishment of water management committees in the targeted payams of Rumbek North and Cueibet counties. This activity includes also the training of the committees, especially to guarantee ownership of the community and sustainability of the intervention.- Awareness sessions about WASH and hygiene practices for vulnerable people of Rumbek North and Cueibet counties in Lakes State, of Ikwoto and Greater Kapoeta in Eastern Equatoria.CO2: Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties.- 23.040 people having access to an improved water source- 21,600 people with access to sanitation facilities (including children and caretakers). The distribution will be occurring in strategic distribution points, including schools, institutions and few door-to-door in order to guarantee the sustainability and more equal access to the facilities, in line with the do-no-harm approach. The distribution shall be accompanied by capacity building and training (especially influential people and PTA in schools and hygiene clubs in nutrition sites)- 21,600 people reached with hygiene promotion and soap. The same as above will occur during and after distribution, including capacity building of targeted people. CO3: WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation.- 18.435 women and girls with improved access to water and/or sanitation after a safety audit- 18.435 women and girls with access to quality and re-usable dignity kits. The distribution will be accompanied by trainings and capacity building on how to use the pads both in schools and door-to-door. CO4: Community members exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures and infrastructure.- 36 water points rehabilitated/constructed with a flood-resilient design- 30 latrine stances rehabilitated/constructed with a flood-resilient design- 34 disability inclusive sanitation facilities designed
