Project Summary

This program will provide access to basic nutrition services for rural and marginalized communities including IDP's in four district of Gedo;Dolow, Luuq, Belethawa and Gabaharey. The program will primarily target children under 5 years, pregnant and lactating women suffering from acute malnutrition . The program will also put efforts in ensuring a robust community health and nutrition awareness and sensitization is done with special on emphasis IYCF practices to mothers and caretakers including fathers. Mother to mother support groups will also be use to create IYCF awareness alongside mother MUAC training.Mother MUAC training is done to boost early identification and timely referral of acutely malnourished children,pregnant and lactating mothers.The IMAM framework will be used in the implementation using the Somali national guideline to ensure a full package of services is integrated within health services. The program will be implemented in both static and outreach sites that have been selected through the cluster rationalization plan .Mobile outreach teams will work closely with a network of community nutrition workers who do active case-finding and referral along side other activities like awareness raising and community mobilization.

Needs

The FSNAU post Gu assessment results released in September 2018 indicate an improvement in levels of acute malnutrition at national level from critical to serious compared to similar season in 2017. The current median Global Acute Malnutrition rate is 14.0% compared to 2017 Gu median, 17.4%. Gedo like other regions recorded an improvement in malnutrition levels in 2018 post Gu assessment compared to a similar season in 2017 among the pastoral community but a slight deterioration among the IDPs and riverine community. In Dolow IDP population, the Gu 2018 assessment recorded critical GAM rate of (18.3 and serious severe acute malnutrition (SAM) at (2.8%) prevalence. This was an increased from 13.9 reported in Deyr 2017 and 17.6 in Gu 2017. In the North Riverine, the GAM levels deteriorated from 14.5% and 15.7% in the deyr 2017 and Gu 2017 seasons respectively to 18.3% in the post Gu 2018 assessment. Morbidity (11.5 %) and low level of crude death rate (CDR) <0.5/10 000/day and sustained Serious levels of under 5 death rates (UDR) of 0.77/10 000 day were seen among IDPs in Dolow during Gu 2018. The increase of GAM and SAM prevalence noted is mainly linked to deterioration in food security and loss of livelihood, as there is an increase of proportion of households in phase 3 or higher with the constant fluctuation in seasonality resulting in chronic cycles of malnutrition and food insecurity. Acute watery diarrhoea, malaria, upper respiratory tract infections are the key diseases affecting children in the population which could be partly due to weakened immune occasioned by malnutrition, but also as a consequence of inadequate water supply and poor household hygiene and environmental sanitation practices. Other factors that contribute to some of the causes of acute malnutrition in Gedo are internal displacement, poor hygiene practices, poor child feeding practices, low levels of literacy and knowledge on health and nutrition issues and poverty.

Activities

Some key recommendations form assessments have been to ensure complementarity of nutrition and health support interventions with sustained efforts to reduce morbidity by educating households on proper care and hygiene practices and improving health seeking behaviour. The high levels of acute malnutrition, high morbidity and high mortality situation among IDPs call for integration of humanitarian interventions in order to achieve positive impact. Trocaire intends to:Improve early detection and referral through engaging in community mobilization and active case finding and referral at community by community nutrition workers (CNWs). Additionally, use CNWs as TOT to training mothers on how to take MUAC readings and create awareness on existing referral pathways for sick and malnourished children Provide treatment for SAM and MAM through SC’s, OTP and TSFP integrated with health services. To achieve this Trocaire will provide technical input (capacity building, logistical support, program oversight, leadership and direction to teams), medical and non-medical supplies to carry out the services. Additionally, Trocaire will seek support for nutritional commodities which include therapeutic feeds (milks F100, F75, and plumpy nut) and supplementary (plumpy sup) feeds for the management of acute malnutrition. Provide capacity building of national staff and community resource persons on nutrition through formal training, on the job training and mentorship and supportive supervision in order to ensure quality of services.Conduct Health and nutrition education, sensitization and awareness with strong emphasis and focus on IYCF will be carried out in both facilities and in the communities.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

This project will be utilized to cover gaps resulting from ongoing humanitarian situation and will be linked to other longer-term projects that Trocaire is implementing such as the DFID CHANGE program and Irish Aid humanitarian project which are 5 year and 3 year funded projects respectively. The key focus of the two projects include among others:Building the capacity of the government to take over the management of health service delivery as the duty bearers by supporting in establishing the structures at the district and regional level. This is part of a longer term development programme of health that focuses on health sector strengthening through the six key pillars of health currently supported by DFID through the CHANGE (Community Health and Nutrition through Local Governance and Empowerment) program. Building the capacity of community governance structures (District Health Boards) in their oversight role of holding the government accountable to the health needs of the population as well as in resource mobilization Strengthening community based nutrition surveillance system as an early warning system and as part of DRR through existing community structures and community resource persons and thereby empower the community to take timely action at the onset of any emergency.Empowering community member through knowledge skills and resources by taking IYCF education up to household level, training of caregivers both male and female on MUAC taking and referral pathways, training of women groups on entrepreneurial skills, provision of cash grants to start small income generating activities and promotion of use of farms in riverine areas and where water is available for vegetable gardening as a source of complimenting household diet which lacks this important component of a healthy diet and for income.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

1. Lack of access to services due to language barrier – Some of the IDPs from other regions such as Bay and Bakool speak a different dialect ( Mai Mai) from the locally used language in Gedo. This creates a barrier to service access when the health and nutrition workers have do not understand the language. Mitigation:- Trocaire will deliberately source for staff who can speak the Mai Mai dialect and where not available hire a translator - Translation of information in local language or use of pictorials where need be 2. Tension between host community and IDPS over service provision particullay when the host community is excluded Mitigation: - Ensure access to services for both the host and IDP camps and ensure a certain percentage of vulnerable host community is targeted when distribution are involved.3. Consultations with displaced communities from marginalized communities risk being hampered by gatekeepers or local leaders.Mitigation:- Request the systematic participation of representatives of marginalized IDP communities in consultation meetings with local leaders.- Establish direct contact directly with leaders of IDPs from marginalized communities, outside camps if necessary.- Include beneficiaries from IDPs and marginalized community in feedback processes such as client satisfaction surveys, exit interviews and community feedback meetings. Where need be have FGDs exclusively for the IDP population

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

1. Lack of female staff and therefore exposing women and children at more risk of gender based violence Mitigation: - Ensure availability of female staff in nutrition service delivery points and where there are challenges in getting female staff, recruit female workers to accompany the mothers- Ensure that during recruitment process staff are screened well as all staff are trained on child protection and program participants safeguarding - Creating awareness on available GBV services i.e availale services, where and when and contact persons - Training staff on identification of GBV cases, psychological fast aid and how to effectively refer survivors to where they can get help while maintaining confidentiality and respecting the survivor’s wish.2. Exclusion of some categories of people due to gender related issues such as safety of locations, distance and timing of services among others Mitigation - Ensure data disaggregation by sex, age and diversity for gender analysis - Include gender analysis in assessment, planning and monitoring to identify key barrier/risks accessing and coming up with mitigation measures. Ensure participation of different groups of different ages in the population in all stages particulalry the programme design, assessmentsand monitoring tage- Provision of comfortable waiting areas

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

1. Attacks during distribution2. Exposure to IEDs Mitigation measures - Safety of locations for distribution – Involve beneficiaries in designing the project including location, timings and days - Ensure that activities and staff conduct (including dressing in case of non-local staff) are culturally acceptable and do not expose targeted communities to insecurity - Train staff on security and on basic first aid and referral pathways in case of a security incident - Maintain neutrality, transparency and non-partisan approach to our work therefore minimizing risks of interference from the armed groups - Working closely with local community structures to foster ownership of the project and community acceptance
