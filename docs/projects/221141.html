Project Summary

This project will provide Shelter and NFIs to support 17,174 vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities seeking refuge in Aweil North and Nasir due to internal subnational conflicts, flooding, and conflict in Sudan. The returnees need ES/NFI and resilience support, and are still either settling within the host communities in these locations, while finding it hard to sustain their families, or sheltering in public places like schools, churches and other government institutions. Narrative Hub, a women-led organization, is quite conversant with ES/NFI cluster strategy and methodology having been trained by the cluster and engaged in previous joint needs analysis and response with other partners such as IOM in Nasir and in Aweil with CCOSS. NH has also implemented and Emergency GBV project in Nasir County, constructing two (2) women and girls’ friendly spaces (one in Jikmir and another in Nasir Town), supported by IOM/RRF and now, by Diakonie, while also currently implementing a UNDP supported transitional justice project in Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal State and an IOM supported GBV in emergency project in Aweil East, Aweil Centre and Aweil North, running a women and girls friendly space in Majokyinthiu in Madhol Payam, and Maper to serve Aweil Centre/North, and according to local assessment reports and observation, ES/NFI support is needed. NH will provide lifesaving shelter and NFIs in-kind support focusing on the following cluster objectives: CO1: - Ensure provision of timely and adequate access to safe and dignified emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to newly displaced people or populations with new vulnerabilities; and CO2: - Improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and the host communities who are unable to meet their SNFI needs. The response will consist of approximately 70% in-kind assistance and 30% conditional cash and align our activities with resilience and recovery initiatives, promoting local and sustainable solutions. Narrative Hub will put much consideration to people with special needs, concerns and prioritize supports to women and girls mostly affected by the crisis, such as persons with disability, pregnant and lactating women, elderly, child headed families and other vulnerabilities with the aim of incorporating protection principles during the project implementation. As a short-term intervention, the impact on the population and on the contextual dynamics is sensitive and therefore delicate balances are at play, and for this reason the team will ensure that all accountability processes to the affected population are carefully followed. The intervention will be clearly designed and protection concerns taken into consideration so that distributions respect community dynamics, community engagement and participation in a dignified and meaningful way. Contextual feedback mechanisms will be established in line with the distribution method and the location dynamics. Beneficiaries will be identified using approved South Sudan S/NFIs cluster criteria of selection. In addition, Narrative Hub will endeavor to give priority to child-headed households, women, persons with disability, the elderly and other vulnerable people in the community who will benefit from Emergency Shelter and NFI distribution. Narrative Hub will aspire to conduct joint missions including joint assessments and responses when needed in order to better respond to the complex needs of the beneficiaries, pull resources together and minimize overlap. The project activities will include:
• Needs analysis of Shelter and NFIs needs
• Beneficiary selection based on the most critical needs
• Distribution of emergency shelter/NFI to population in need
• Rapid monitoring and Post distribution monitoring survey
• Training to local partners on ES/NFI cluster response methodology, CVA programming
• Awareness creation on disaster risk reduction and climate change; setting up committees and plans

1. Needs

Nasir has been the center of two armed fights between government and Nuer youths this year. Armed fighting between government and Nuer youth white army in Nasir first broke out in February, deteriorating the situation for women and girls. The conflict displaced about 29,500, some 15,000 of them women and girls, and more than half into Pandanyang (9,000), Torpuot (5,000), and Koat (8,000) alone. A 20th February 2024 tentative agreement reached at a meeting between the political leadership the community resolved that the government keep the army out of town for its own safety and for that of the population, allowing only soldiers’ wives out of the barracks. The resolution was regardless of the outcome of the community demand that the government relocates the barracks out of Nasir. But fighting again erupted in August 2024, affecting 40,000 households (Inter-Agency Rapid Needs Assessment to the Conflict Displaced and Flood Affected Population in Greater Nasir County, 2024). Since the breakout of several conflicts in Nasir that resulted to displacement, women and girls’ vulnerability increased, according to the Rapid assessment which was conducted on 2nd may 2024, the findings shows high number of displacements, that has resulted to shortage of food, shelter, lack of basic non-food items. The ongoing conflict in Sudan has had a significant impact on trade between Sudan and South Sudan. According to market monitoring conducted by humanitarian partners, the cost of the food basket has increased significantly since the start of the Sudan crisis. Life-saving assistance is needed for returnees, including shelter and non-food items, food, water, sanitation facilities and protection services. According to Humanitarian Situation Overview in Aweil East and Northern Bahr el Ghazal conducted by REACH South Sudan in October 2024, since April 2023, South Sudan has received more than 800,000 people displaced by fighting in Sudan. Although roughly 80% have crossed the border through Upper Nile State, intensified fighting in the neighboring Kordofan region of Sudan in 2024 has resulted in the emergence of another major entry area in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State. The Majokyinthiu border crossing in Aweil East county, and Kiir Adem in Aweil North are now the most trafficked points of entry from Sudan, and Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal State one of the most common settlement areas for new arrivals. Aid organizations have recorded more than 50,000 people crossing the border into Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal State (Aweil North). Findings suggest that gaps in monitoring along the Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal State border have left many South Sudanese “returnees” without humanitarian assistance since their initial displacement. The war in Sudan has upended the local economy of NBeG, driving up prices, restricting income opportunities, and reducing access to food at markets even though the markets are functional. Food, shelter, nonfood items, and healthcare were commonly identified as priority unmet needs that sectoral cash assistance can support. Large-scale arrivals from Sudan, steep price increases, and atypically severe flooding are exacerbating an already critical humanitarian situation. According to the REACH South Sudan report of October 2024, by August 2024, flooding had also affected the accessibility of routes into South Sudan. The routes through Majokyinthiu and Kiir Adem are seasonal and become impassable during the rainy season. The recession of these floods in November – December 2024, could precipitate an increase in new arrivals early in 2025. Available data indicate that, in parts of Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal State, rates of acute malnutrition have increased significantly over the past 12 months, while severe acute food insecurity is widespread. This population, particularly is in urgent need of ES/NFI services. Most of the families came without any household items. Many are sharing spaces without any privacy to women and girls.

2. Project activities

This project will focus on the following cluster objectives: CO1: - Ensure provision of timely and adequate access to safe and dignified emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to newly displaced people or populations with new vulnerabilities; and CO2: - Improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and the host communities who are unable to meet their SNFI needs. The following activities will be implemented: • Conduct a needs analysis of Shelter and NFIs needs. Narrative Hub will engage enumerators and train them on how to conduct needs analysis, beneficiary identification and verification in all the two counties of Aweil North and Nasir. Assessment will be done from house to house using a cluster developed assessment tool (questionnaire) for collecting data. The enumerators will use this tool to interview prospective beneficiaries to identify the extent of NFIs needs among the affected population. • Conduct a beneficiary selection based on the most critical needs. Enumerators will visit identified households that were identified during assessment to be vulnerable and register them as beneficiaries and issue of tokens for the distribution. The assessment conducted together will identification and registration of beneficiaries in advance in any of the locations will form part of a verification exercise in the said location. The criteria for selecting beneficiaries will first focus on new displacements, which comes with multiple risks to the affected population. • Conduct distribution of in-kind, cash and conditional voucher assistance emergency shelter/NFI to beneficiaries in need. A team of volunteers will be recruited to help in distribution of NFIs guided by Narrative Hub field team. The volunteers will be identified in an interview in collaboration will local authorities in each county. Where markets are functional like the whole of Aweil and Nasir as well, Narrative Hub will provide sectoral unconditional cash and voucher assistance to beneficiaries depending on assessed needs and agreed response. This will be geared towards addressing all the two objectives above of timely and adequately supporting both newly displaced beneficiaries access ES/NFI but also improving living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs and returnees. • Construct transitional shelters, maintain and upgrade shelter to beneficiaries with people with special needs and critical condition. Narrative Hub will construct transitional shelters, maintain and upgrade shelter, to newly displaced IDPs from within the host population and also for returnees coming back from neighboring countries particularly from Sudan. Preference will be given to beneficiaries with special needs and those in critical conditions • Rapid monitoring and Post distribution monitoring assessment using cluster recommended tools. Immediately after the distribution of items, Narrative Hub field team will conduct post distribution monitoring targeting a smaller percentage of the total beneficiaries per payam using a PDM tool to collect feedback for future projects and accountability to beneficiaries. • Conduct training sessions for partners on CVA programming. • Conduct training sessions for partners on cluster response methodology. Narrative Hub will support to local partners particularly national NGOs and local CBOs in each county with training on how to provide cash and voucher assistance to beneficiaries in counties where local markets are functional, the process and methodology of response to ES/NFI • Conduct awareness sessions on disaster risk reduction • Develop Disaster Management Committees and Climate Change Adaptation Plans. Due to the impact of climate change in South Sudan and the need to build resilience to climate change, Narrative Hub will conduct awareness on disaster risk reduction and support communities develop disaster management committees and climate adaptation plans in each of the two counties.
