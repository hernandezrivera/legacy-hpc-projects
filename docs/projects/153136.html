Project Summary

This project will be based in Juba and will benefit an estimated 25,000 people from both the internally displaced persons and the host communities comprising 10,000 women, 5,000 men, 5,000 girls and 5,000 boys. This project is expected to cost 350,000 USD. After the August 2018 displacement of IDPs from POC 3 in Juba, UNOCHA reported that over 7,000 IDPs displaced to Jonduru in Juba were adult women, adolescent girls, children and youth. And an estimated 18,000 inhabits the host communities.
An assessment conducted by STEWARDWOMEN in August 2018 highlighted the urgent need for livelihood support and GBV prevention and response services for IDP women and girls against incidences of sexual violence, primarily by armed actors. As such, a holistic GBV prevention/response is urgently needed, specifically for women, adolescent girls and female youths.
The project will provide life-saving GBV prevention and response and risk mitigation activities through the extension of existing protection interventions in Juba to ensure a holistic approach to addressing community GBV concerns for women, girls, boys and men through: (1) establishment of a static Women & Girl Friendly Space; (2) provision of integrated GBV prevention and response services; (3) provision of GBV case management services including focused PSS for survivors of GBV; (4) conducting legal awareness, assistance and counselling and provision of legal aid clinic assistance in Juba; (5) dissemination and support to the already existing referral pathway for Juba urban; (6) conducting protection awareness and GBV prevention through neighbourhood assemblies; (7) provision of IGA and livelihood support for persons of concern; (8) conducting prevention, mitigation and response to protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience and; (9) provision of in-kind support to women, girls and youth to strengthen their resilience during displacement.
We will ensure that all our activities take a “Do No Harm and conflict sensitive approach”. IDPs and local host communities will be targeted, as well as all other survivors and all those at risk of GBV.
STEWARDWOMEN is a women organisation and the only partner implementing a victim support group (UNDP) and FSL (NPA) interventions in Jondoru, Juba- Central Equatoria State (CES).
STEWARDWOMEN has a protection and economic empowerment team in place in Juba, as well as other protection interventions, and can meet the urgent and specific lifesaving and GBV prevention and response and risk mitigation needs of this project.
In September 2018, STEWARDWOMEN held a meeting with IsraAid on the possibility of life-saving GBV prevention and response and risk mitigation intervention in Jonduru. This was followed by a community consultative meeting held with Jonduru community on the 7th September 2018.

Needs

In August 2018, STEWARDWOMEN conducted a needs assessment in Jondoru and Juba Way Station with the main objective to identify community needs, priorities and capacities of women survivors, girls and boys (Aged 14-49), local structures and duty bearers to inform and provide evidence base for the design of a project.The assessment found out that the commonly and widely experienced violence included: physical violence such as fighting, raping, killing, emotional violence, rape, domestic violence, social violence, economic violence, and sexual violence, with rape as the most experienced form of violence in these communities.The communities also highlighted that the main causes of violence are closely related to the current economic crisis, lack of information/awareness on educational and formative issues that are life changing, lack of training, unemployment and livelihood skills that has disempowered youth forcing them to live a violent life. Lack of water points in Jondoru is number one cause of violence among women. It is important to note that many survivors of rape have been raped in the evening when returning home from fetching water. A community member lamented that “violence has broken homes and made South Sudanese look like fools”. It is time for change.According to the community leader, a large number of ethnic tribes have migrated from different parts of the country to Jondoru due to insecurity and search for safety. It was further discovered that the majority of people living in Juba Way Station are from Jondoru. Surprisingly, however close Jondoru is to Juba Town, there are no GBV referral pathways and services including a police post, hospital, or social workers since there are no organizations working in the area. The only existing public structure is a market that was built by UNDP in 2006. There are also no existing school that has contributed to high rate of early pregnancy and school dropout especially for students who have to walk couple of miles to Juba Way Station in search of schools. Some of the drop outs have been as a result rape due to lack of protection. As a result, the community leaders has requested STEWARDWOMEN to lobby for more funds to build schools, hospital and a police post to mitigate the identified challenges.Furthermore, the community members urged that they have limited information on key issues such as HIV/AIDS, SGBV, referral pathways and services since there are no NGOs working in Jondoru to provide such services and information. Through the assessment, we also found out that there is increased number of idle youths. These youths requested to be supported with skills trainings on candle and soap making, mushroom growing and sports activities for male youths. The female youth requested to be trained on making reusable sanitary pads in order to build their economic resilience and contribute to reduction of number of early pregnancy and sex cohesion that are fuelled by poverty. The female youth leader of Jondoru stated that “a number of girls in her community are fond of engaging themselves in sexual relations with older men in order to get things like sanitary pads to use during menstruation” and hence “they end up pregnant”. However, she noted that through skills empowerment and trainings, such behaviours will be reduced in the community.Jondoru with a population of over 7000 (according to the community leader) has only one reliable source of water point built by South Sudan Brewery Limited (SSBL). The assessment team also noticed that the community understand their challenges and know possible solutions to their problems. The community members further revealed that they have not received any training on human rights. Therefore, the community requested for training opportunities that targets both women and men and suggested topics such as SGBV, psychosocial support, agriculture, and skills trainings in knitting, tailoring and plaiting.

Activities

Through the extension of existing protection interventions in Juba STEWARDWOMEN will ensure a holistic approach to addressing community GBV concerns for women, girls, boys and men by: 1) Establish1 static, temporary Women & Girl Friendly Space (WGFS); STEWARDWOMEN will construct 1 temporary women and girl friendly space at Jonduru to be used by women and girls for various activities including group counselling, centre for training on sustainable IGA and meetings for savings aimed at promoting safe positive environments for women and girls.2) Provide integrated GBV prevention and response services using the WGFS Guidelines for South Sudan; this will include among others increased awareness on rights and the available services.3) Provide GBV case management services including focused psychosocial support for survivors of GBV; STEWARDWOMEN will conduct two trainings; one on GBV case management services for 2 social workers and 6 project staff and two PSS training for identified GBV survivors.4) Conduct legal awareness, assistance and counselling through our already existing legal aid clinic in Juba with the aim of addressing the legal needs of survivors. STEWARDWOMEN has a legal aid basket fund that will support interested survivors to seek for redress in courts of law within the jurisdiction of Juba.5) Disseminate and support the already existing referral pathway for Juba urban; Jonduru community has no existing support services except for one borehole serving a population of 7,000 people, one market built by UNDP in 2006 and a chicken house used as a church. Hence, we shall disseminate the already existing referral pathway during sensitisation meetings and also develop big banners with the referral pathways displaced at the bore hole, market and church.6) Conduct protection awareness raising and GBV prevention messaging through neighbourhood assemblies; a neighbourhood assembly is a gathering of 2-10 households within a radius of 50-100 meters to discuss pertinent issues affecting them. The discussion will also derive action points for implementation. This method of awareness raising leaves no one behind because it caters for the disabled, the elderly and those who are unable to leave their homes. It also involves all in decision making.7) Provide IGA and livelihood support for persons of concern; STEWARDWOMEN will train the women, girls and youth on identification of sustainable business enterprises such as women coffee groups and selling in the market by women and girls aimed increasing family income and improved nutrition; phone charging for male youth aimed at keeping them busy from idleness and associated effects and making income for the family. 8) Conduct prevention, mitigation and response to protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience. There will be community sensitisation awareness raising issues on pertinent protection challenges and possible solutions to those challenges will be arrived at by community members. 9) Provide in-kind support to women, girls and youth to strengthen their resilience during displacement. STEWARDWOMEN will provide in-kind support to these groups in a pass-out ceremony/event witnessed by community leaders who will also monitor the progress of the IGAs.10) Distribution of dignity kits containing pads, soap and Vaseline to adolescent girls in Jondoru aimed at supporting and improving the health of the girls. During distribution days, adolescent girls will also be discussing issues related to their reproductive health, GBV advocacy, protection from violence etc.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

STEWARDWOMEN is committed to ensuring accountability to affected populations through a Communicating with Communities (CWC) strategy and installing a rigorous beneficiaries and other stakeholders’ feedback mechanism which will be part of the project collected through Focus Group Discussions and also linking with other actors operating in Juba urban. Women, men, girls, and boys within target communities will be directly involved at all stages of the program cycle in order to encourage ownership and participation in project activities. So we shall continue to have constant consultation with the community throughout the project cycle.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

GVB protection issues tend to affect age groups and populations differently and so their reactions differ. With this in mind, we will promote tailored age-groups specific messages for dissemination. We shall ensure such messages are always tested before they are finally disseminated to the population. This explains why during the project assessment we focused our learning on women and men of the ages of 14-49.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

STEWARDWOMEN will conduct an in-house project inception meeting to orient the project team on implementation modalities including the code of conduct in the field. Deliberate efforts will also be focused towards orientation on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) issues. Project staff will be provided with pocket copies of the same SEA.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Our approach to consultations is not only participatory but inclusive. We approach and work directly with all project beneficiaries and stakeholders so that we increase representation and future project ownership. Our approach therefore does not start and end with the consultation with community leaders. Rather, it covered a representative number and group of the population under service.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Complaint and feedback mechanisms will be set up to monitor programme implementation and ensure the voices of the beneficiaries are heard. Accountability groups will be formed based on community-defined vulnerability criteria and consulted each month to provide feedback and influence our programming on an ongoing basis. Also, documentation of activities is a critical component of our communication strategy in the project. Stories of change will be documented in form of videos and newsletters. A video compilation will be produced showcasing how the project has impacted the lives of beneficiaries. The videos will be supported by a monthly newsletter showcasing the progress of the project. All video compilations and newsletters will be translated into Juba Arabic, so that all the targeted beneficiaries can comprehend the information being disseminated widely with them and the stakeholders.STEWARDWOMEN will also orient project staff on the humanitarian accountability standards including global initiatives such as Core Humanitarian Standards of Accountability, People in Aid, and ALNAP. All activities of this project will be linked with other INGOs, and UNDP, OCHA, UNICEF, IOM, and UNHCR accountability systems to ensure collaboration and key information is shared to support the overall implementation and protection of beneficiaries.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

STEWARDWOMEN will conduct a joint stakeholder project inception workshop with project beneficiaries aimed at among others informing stakeholders about their roles in the project implementation. We expect this project to accrue gains in learning, building and increasing stakeholder relationships, institutional memory and effective partnerships for STEWARDWOMEN. We also expect that we will emerge at the end of this project in a much stronger position to increase our protection and humanitarian responsibilities and impact as key national actor in South Sudan. This can be made possible is we engage community in the monitoring and evaluation of this project.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project is based on a protection risk analysis done during the needs assessment conducted by STEWARDWOMEN in August 2018 and hence the specific needs of the different population groups has been taken into consideration.While this proposal is considerate of the protection needs of various groups, deliberate efforts shall be made to undertake regular protection analysis and needs identification for remedial actions in the cause of the project implementation.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will put in place measures to address barriers to access that different population groups could face in relation to the project. The following barriers were identified during a focus group discussion done in Jondoru on the 7th September 2018. Barriers such as lack of access to community information will be addressed through sensitization and awareness raising platforms; language barrier addressed through translating the key messages into Juba Arabic; accessibility to people with special needs such as the elderly, disabled, young children and other persons of concern who cannot attend public events will be done through neighborhood assembly awareness raising that aims at “leaving no one behind”.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

STEWARDWOMEN is a women organization established to address the problems of sexual and gender based violence, harmful customs on women and girls, child labor and trafficking, community insecurity and conflicts, poor governance and illiteracy in South Sudan. With that in mind, STEWARDWOMEN will make use of the referral pathway already developed for Juba Urban; disseminate it to the community to seek the various services in case of inequalities or protection threats. We Shall hire two social workers and train them together with other project staff on STEWARDWOMEN so as to provide first aid and psychosocial support to the most vulnerable. STEWARDWOMEN also has a legal aid clinic in Juba with competent legal aid attorneys ready to take legal action when needed.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project activities will address inequalities or protection threats by; (1) livelihood support to women, girls and youth aimed at providing additional income to the family, (2) community outreach information dissemination through the neighborhood assembly to people with disabilities and elderly persons, (3) conduct awareness raising, community sensitization, radio talk show and trainings on on inequalities or protection threats.
