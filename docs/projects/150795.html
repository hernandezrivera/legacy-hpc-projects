Project Summary

This project focuses on conflict-affected vulnerable households and small scale farmers in the Access Restricted Area (ARA). It is designed to protect the livelihoods of ARA residents and build their resilience to cope with protracted protection threats through protecting their livelihoods and generating temporary income to cover their basic needs. PUI focus is on open land rehabilitation in the ARA, and on the infrastructural challenges such as shortages of electricity and water in agriculture using innovative solutions and as much as possible link intervention to development actors through coordination with key development actors and joint advocacy initiatives.
The Action will enable farmers, including vulnerable women, to re-launch their agricultural activities through different activities such as solar energy for irrigation, agricultural inputs, cash assistance, etc. In addition, the project will target acutely vulnerable households who are food insecure through the provision of Cash for Work opportunities to meet their basic needs.
Finally, the project includes a dedicated component on protection, coordination and advocacy based on PUI’s role and responsibilities as co-chair of the ARA working group (as mandated by the FSS under the umbrella of the ICCG).
The proposed intervention is part of a multi-year program. The same activities will be implemented in the second year but with new beneficiaries and possibly new geographical areas.
The planned action was closely coordinated with the Food Security Sector and the Ministry of Agriculture, as well as with other relevant local actors.
The project will contribute to the following overarching country strategic objectives:
SO1: The rights of Palestinians living under occupation, including those living under the blockade and other restrictions, are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with IHL and IHRL.
SO3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
CO1: Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced
CO2: Households suffering from limited of access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs.
CO3: FSS activities, including advocacy and awareness, are planned and implemented in a coordinated manner, and partnership with key national ministries is strengthened.

Needs

The humanitarian situation has rapidly deteriorated in 2018 in the Gaza Strip (GS), with around 80% of the population receiving international assistance from the UN and INGOs, and 40% of households were estimated to be moderately to severely food insecure. The Strip witnessed a substantial increase of poverty rates (from 38% in 2011 to 53 % in 2017), with unemployment rate reaching 53% in 2018 according OCHA recent reports. PUI needs assessment with farmers in the ARA in late 2017 showed that: - The access to agricultural inputs represented a significant barrier to small farmers in resuming their livelihood;- The majority of households/farmers faced difficulties accessing irrigation water due to power cut, as the assessed water wells did not have any alternative power source;- 69% of the farmers stated that their agricultural assets had been affected by extreme weather conditions during the last 12 months;- Farmers confirmed that their average net income decreased by 47.8% due to the increased expenditure on larger quantities of irrigation water and the increase in the prices of agricultural inputs;- Farmers in the ARA were under protection threats, which included: destruction of agricultural assets, spraying the crops with chemicals (herbicides), shelling, injuries, and loss of livestock. More recently, PUI conducted Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) in August 2018 with acutely vulnerable households, including women, in the ARA. Key findings were as follow: - Participants expressed high food insecurity levels in terms of decrease in quantity, quality, and diversity of food; - A significant increase of children dropping out of school to work to support their families; - Increase in child labor (i.e. increased number of children collecting scrap from garbage containers/bins and selling it to eke out living); - Inability of households to pay the University fees (skipping or postponing the university education especially for females); - Low purchasing power and lack of liquidity caused by the PA salary cuts and delayed MoSD’s assistance; - Families are unable to afford medical care expenses for their sick members. Finally, PUI conducted FGDs in September 2018 with farmers affected by the Great March of Return (GMR). Key findings were: • 217 farmers were found highly affected by the demonstrations, with damages in their crops and agricultural assets; • Farmers reported significant losses of the summer cropping season and as a result accumulated high amount of debts; • Farmers reported difficulties to meet basic needs for themselves and their families, which in some instances had led to imprisonment due to financial issues; • Farmers reported not having a safe access to their lands as the GMR activities were still running; as a result of this, they were concerned about missing out the next agricultural season (in winter). The main priority response expressed by the different affected households, including farmers, stressed on a rapid cash injection in order to protect their livelihoods and meet their basic needs. PUI coordinated its planned interventions with FSS and MoA. Coordination with key actors in the area of intervention took place with ICRC, Action against Hunger, ACAD, PARC and UAWC.

Activities

PUI focuses its intervention in the ARA, with special focus on the farmers affected by the GMR in the southern parts of the ARA. Selection criteria will include: households who have members with disabilities and/or members with chronic diseases, IDP families, ARA farmers, farmers affected by the heat waves and other extreme weather condition and/or GRM demonstrations. In addition, PUI will target rural women and unemployed men through Cash for Work (CfW) activities. PUI will continue building the capacity of the Arab Center for Agriculture Development (ACAD), through a partnership in this project (PUI has already collaborated with ACAD during 2018 on a FSS project). ACAD will have a direct role in implementing the different planned activities and participate in the monitoring and evaluation process. The project will work to identify and reduce any risks related to women and men active participation in the project. Further, PUI will consult with women on the best approach for them to ensure they retain control over resources and assets intended for their benefit. Activity 1: Rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs, for farmers, herders and breeders1. Provision of agricultural inputs to 200 vulnerable farmers affected by protection threats in the ARA. Assistance provided will cover the needs to 2 dunums of land. 2. Provision of restricted cash assistance to 90 farmers affected by the GMR in the southern governorates. Cash assistance will be used to buy agricultural inputs, rent land in accessible and areas and repay debts. 3. Set up of two solar panel systems on two agricultural wells which will serve 100 farmers in average (23 kw/well). Based on PUI current experience, the average of farmers who benefit from 23Kw is 70 beneficiaries. In addition, the irrigation timing among beneficiary farmers is planned in advance.Activity 2: Cash-based support 1. Provision of CfW activities to 100 vulnerable households/farmers, including: a. 50 women (5 skilled, 45 unskilled) will receive CfW opportunities in sewing to produce school uniforms which will be distributed to 2000 pupils of 6 to 15 years (girls and boys). PUI already has sewing machines in place with four local partners CBOs.b. 50 men (5 skilled, 45 unskilled) will receive CfW opportunities to work on farmers’ land including land leveling, plowing, removal of weeds, pest control, harvesting etc. 2. Provision of UCT to 100 affected farmers victims of violations of IHL and IHRL. Activity 3: Enhancing coordination and advocacy initiatives with other sectors (mainly protection)PUI, as co-chair of the ARA working group, will contribute and facilitate other members to achieve the following objectives (as per the ToRs):- Maintain detailed information and documentation on IHL/IHRL violations (injuries, property damages, access restrictions…)- Develop in depth understanding of impact arising from incidents and a comprehensive overview of needs in the ARA, particularly focusing on FSS and protection.- With the aim to fill gaps in response, build on and expand existing referral initiatives established in collaboration with other Clusters/Sectors/NGOs. Network with other related Clusters/Sectors and Working Groups to ensure the inclusion of the ARA matter as a cross-cutting issue in their discussions, feeding ICCG, OCHA and HCT.- Provide a forum for joint human rights/protection analysis and discussion of trends in the ARA. Conduct joint advocacy with relevant clusters/sectors (PUI being the advocacy focal point for the FSS in Gaza).Finally, the ARA tends to be neglected by the local municipalities and farmers and residents complain about the effective assistance of the MOA. As a result, advocacy initiatives for better coordination between all stakeholders is needed to raise the voices of farmers’ rights to access their farmlands and natural resources in a safe way.

Indicators

PUI and partners’ M&E teams will develop a monitoring and evaluation plan to manage the process of assessing and reporting progress against project targets. This plan will describe the whole M&E system, including indicators, tools, breakdown of responsibilities and communication channels within the organisations and between them. Introductory meetings with affected population and community representatives will be arranged to provide accessible and timely information on procedures, types of project activities, wages, beneficiary selection process and criteria and any other relevant information. Monthly monitoring visits will be conducted to follow up the progress of project activities and to ensure that the activities are being implemented in accordance with the work plan developed at the beginning of the activity. Written report summarizing the main findings will be issued after each monitoring visit. A complaints and feedback mechanism will be set up, in collaboration with the local partner and CBOs, to make sure that the opinions of affected population are heard and taken into consideration. Complaints regarding the beneficiary selection or suggestions related to the design or the implementation of project activities will be timely responded to. Focus group discussions and/or semi structured surveys will be carried out to assess the impact of project activities against baseline survey results.
