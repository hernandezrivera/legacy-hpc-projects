Project Summary

The project focuses on ensuring and maintaining access to essential life-saving health services and vital public health interventions for refugees and their closest host communities. Direct health service delivery through operationalization of 39 clinics to cover around 844,000 refugees (South Sudanese, Eritrean and Ethiopian). The plan will be mainly focusing to address the pressing needs for high rates of communicable diseases, lack of basic services delivery and a collapse of basic infrastructure due to impact of the long-standing camps for several years. The project activities have been discussed with health sector to align the sector priorities. The plan will sub-grant funds to NGOs and state MOH in areas with critical needs for essential health service provision
The implementation plan strategies are; provision of free-of charge health services and medicines. Aiming to ensure access to essential emergency health care, for targeted population. Mainly composed of refugees and host communities. The expansion and management of emergency health information system and early warning, alert response systems (EWARS), will be established/strengthened to facilitate the prompt identification and timely response to emergencies, that are critical for preventing the avoidable mortality and morbidity amongst the refugees and affected communities, as well as health and well-being of targeted humanitarian case-load in these areas.
WHO will procure and distribute the necessary medicines for the supported clinics serving the refugees in 8 states along with small medical equipment as necessary. The WHO medical kits are adapted to protracted nature of the Sudan refugees crisis, and includes, medicines for men and women, pediatric medicines, Maternal and reproductive health, Personal Protection items, PEP kits and laboratory reagents for basic confirmation of most common diseases
The plan would also complement the ongoing projects funded through the humanitarian mechanisms. The training and functioning of states’ and locality Rapid Response Teams (RRT) in charge of assessment and initial response to acute events such as outbreaks alert investigation and initial response will be implemented by WHO with the broader scale up of the response interventions as the most cost-effective way for the protection of the lives. Affected communities will be involved in planning and implementation of the project by recruiting paramedics and training of CHWs from the same communities for sustainability and self-resilience and minor rehabilitation using semi-permanent materials will be considered for the key health facilities

1 - Needs

According to UNHCR bulletin in 2019, more than 800,000 refugees (South Sudanese, Eritrean and Ethiopian) verified and settled into Sudan. Results of recent assessments and monitoring missions carried by different agencies including UNHCR and WHO, indicated that, there have been nearly 620,000 Ethiopian and Eritrean, and 224,000 South Sudanese who arrived during 2013-2019 in the country. Most of South Sudanese are settled in White Nile camps (34%), East Darfur 11%, South Kordofan 6% and other states, while Eritrean and Ethiopian refugees are settled mainly in Kassala and Gedarif states. The situation of the refugees shows continuing influx of arrivals with very poor living condition, accommodated at congested camps in White Nile, East Darfur, East Sudan and South Kordofan. The burden posed on the existing public health facilities in 8 states, serving the local community and the refugees which have been tremendous; the health status of the refugees in these settings is poor due to unmet of basic services including nutrition and food security in addition to access to secondary health services especially for people with non communicable diseases, The poor living conditions, including access to safe water and proper sanitation, overcrowding, heightening the susceptibility to communicable diseases, The assessments showed also the gaps on the access to quality and lifesaving emergency health services are significant in eight states, the health services need to be augmented by support to existing health facilities to cover the needs of an increasing number of people coming from South Sudan. The plan is to address the needs of refugees in eight states, increased access to primary health care in term of health protection against disease outbreaks through access to medicines and medical supplies, trained staff on case management, infection prevention and timely investigation and confirmation of alerts.Urgent support is needed in term of strengthening the early warning system, coordination with specific projects on cholera preparedness and strict reinforcement of universal infection prevention and control measures in all supported clinics serving South Sudanese refugees Support for diseases confirmation and public health interventions (surveillance, case management, referral of emergency cases, health education, vector control, vaccination, and inter sectorial coordination) became challenging with the steady increase of refugees caseload and decreased humanitarian funding for Sudan. Women and children are the most vulnerable due to long distances of travel exposing them to gender exploitation or affecting the health care seeking behavior.The nutrition inputs will be mainly focused on lifesaving in-patient services for SAM through establishing the first ever stabilization center in all camps hosting refugees. The activities will be implemented by WHO with the support from SMOH focusing on in patient management of the severe acute malnutrition. The capacity building of health and nutrition staff of the stabilization center on lifesaving In-Patient SAM services and will provide opportunity to the staff from other health facilities. WASH activities aim to contribute to the reduction of the avoidable mortality and morbidities related to water, vector and hospital acquired infections among 844,000 Refugees. The WASH activities will address essential determinants of health among the vulnerable groups (IDPs, returnees and refugees) targeted also by the WHO health and nutrition intervention. Conduct water sources monitoring and quality testing for identification of the sources of infection during alerts of outbreaks of water borne diseases to guide and support the response. Utilizing the existing equipment, which will complement the proposed procurement WHO will support the integrated vector management with focus on the mosquitoes and flies control, this will be guided by the entomological surveillance results

2 - Activities / Outputs

WHO has established field offices at the 8 states targeted in this proposal, all WHO offices are structured with epidemiologists and public health officers for monitoring of the health situation and coordination of the overall health services deliver. Coordination mechanisms in these states are already established with involvement of all partners on the ground and MOH. more than 10% of the activities are designed to serve host communities vulnerable groups such as vector control, SAM management and curative consultation.• Procurement and distribution of emergency medicines ( for most common diseases), medical supplies including trauma and cholera kits, and laboratory reagents (PHC level) and Rapid Diagnostic kits for malaria and trauma surgical kits enough to cover essential needs of 844,000 refugees and their closest host communities in 8 states • Rehabilitation of clinics with semi permanent (corrugated iron sheets and cement base) for Maternal and child health space, fences, latrines, incinerators, shaded area for nutrition, health promotion and vaccination • Operational support for the running of 39 emergency clinics in 8 states to enable these clinics to provide free of charge emergency integrated PHC services. • Operational costs for response immunization coverage against measles (children up 15 years of age), polio and other antigens; • Training of medical staff categories on case management, surveillance and infection prevention control.• Training of CHWs to deliver integrated health promotion messages at clinics including psychosocial counselling and support on mental health • Conduct alert investigation missions, including collection of samples and their transport for confirmation, identification of source, initiation of response through outreach and active case finding• Strengthening of EWARS for weekly collection of epidemiological data and enhanced detection of communicable diseases outbreaks• Monitoring and supervision of the health situation• Implementation of public health screening at entry points including nutritional status, vaccination status, HIV/AIDS tests• Training of community volunteers and supervisors on water sampling, protection of water sources, HH level water safe management, and integrated vector control.• Conduct water sampling for quality testing; only operational costs for new caseload are included. The state laboratory personnel and portable water testing kits have been provided through previous funding• Conduct regular vector control, with a focus before and during rainy season when the campaigns will be conducted at 2 weeks interval (WHO guidelines) when health awareness and education messages • Support the collection, centralization and analysis of SAM inpatient care to identify the trends and challenges (admission, recovery, death, defaulter, re admission rates) dis aggregated by sex and age. Disseminate to all stakeholders• Strengthen the linkages with communities and their awareness through active social mobilization conducted by trained community mobilizers/CHW in coordination with UNICEF to promote early treatment and referral of SAM patients• Increase the demand for, and access of boys and girls suffering from SAM with medical complications to life saving in patient care with a focus on the quality of service delivery through Rehabilitation/small repairs of 15 TFUs

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The climate changes; drought and floods affect most part of Eastern Sudan, with consequent food insecurity and high acute malnutrition levels among the host communities and refugees. Climate change influenced the pattern of the outbreaks of vector and water borne diseases that indeed increased the severity and re-emerging and expansion to new geographical areas of Sudan of Dengue Fever, Chikungunya, Rift Valley and cholera. Malaria is currently flaring all over Sudan. Measures on medical safe disposal is factored in the proposal for implementation of Universal Precaution, aggressive pathogens (microbes, Viruses, protozoa, fungus, etc) and medicines, sharp medical tools, disinfectants, lab reagents etc would contaminate/affect staff, other patients and environment outside the clinics creating severe risks for population living in the areas. The training program of WHO includes modules (initial training and re-fresher) on Universal Precautions that includes Infection Prevention, Injection safety, Solid and liquid medical waste safe disposal for all levels of care. All WHO medical kits include barrier protection equipment, and disinfectants that are biodegradable. In addition, the WHO/MOH monitoring and supervision check list includes indicators for Universal Precaution, and the repairs of incinerators taking into account the standards for environmental protection through correct medical waste disposal. The disposal of any expired medicines will be done according with WHO/National Guidelines by incineration in the presence of Health Authorities.

7 - Accountability to Affected People and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse
7.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback and use it to improve your activities? Please select as many as relevant.

Face to face with individuals (in other venues/locations) through aid workers

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The proposal based on results of recent WHO and RCF assessments, as well as WHO supervisory visits. It includes interviews with community members (male and female) and group discussions, to facilitate their participation and to the design of the project, community leaders meeting has been used to inform the people and receive the community concern regarding their needs and availability of the services, during the implementation; banners and signboards will be fabricated to inform the communities about the elements of the project’s services.Supervision missions in the implementation areas will include random interviews with patients (community members ) as well as community group discussions to assess their perception of provided services and immediate needs, and identify the best solutions. Corrective measures will take into account their feed-back.The trainings participants would be selected from the same communities and will include a balance between women and men to ensure alignment of the implementation with the local culture. For the training of the health staff, the aim is to have at least 30% female, and for midwives 100% female. During the health awareness campaigns, female health promoters will ensure the access to information/education of female community members; especially in camps where about 60- 70% (depending on camp) of inhabitants are female. All training sessions and awareness campaigns conducted during the Rapid Response Teams mission will include key message on environmental protection from biological contamination as part of safe waste disposal and improved community level sanitation. Infection prevention measures aiming to ensure the protection of staff, patient and communities will be strictly implemented by the supported clinics/activities. Health and nutrition services will be established and provided inside the refugee camps to avoid the risk of PSEA and also as do no harm
