Project Summary

The project aims at ensuring that unaccompanied and separated children and other
children with protection concerns are identified and receive holistic support through
case management, psychosocial support and reintegration. The project target areas are
Bolori II in Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC) in Borno state and the Local
Government Areas of Gujba, Geidam, Potiskum, Fune, Bade and Damaturu in Yobe
State.
The project includes an explicit focus on building capacity within communities to
deliver child protection services, including prevention and response to abuse and
violence, and calls for investment in capacity building on local partners to
strengthen case management, referral and socioeconomic reintegration. At the same time the project will focus on socioeconomic reintegration services based on the needs expressed by children during COOPI orientation and assessment process. The proposed action is designed for getting comprehensive coverage for social economic reintegration with provision of appropriate services.
Gender equality measures have been mainstreamed since the needs assessment phase and
integrated into the design of the project to uplift the status of girls and boys and
ensure that attention to girls’ and boys’ protection issues will be equitably
balanced.

Needs

North East Nigeria still faces immense humanitarian and protection challenges. The Protection Sector’s operational response will target 2.6 million (1.4 million girls and 1.2 million boys) of the most vulnerable people in the most affected areas of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe with severe protection needs, including Internally Displaced Persons, returnees, returning refugees, members of host communities with heightened protection needs. The HRP 2019 guidelines estimated that at least 39,500 children in need of care and protection including unaccompanied and separated children are in need of integrated case management services. Additionally, 1,405 child-headed households will also require support alternative care and access.The protraction of the conflict heightens children’s risk to secondary separation as host families find themselves unable to continue to care for these children. More than 4,000 children have been abducted, killed, recruited or have been victims of violent attacks in the ongoing crisis. Those children are lacking the fundamental protection of a caregiver, whether parents, extended family members or care professionals and are exposed to heightened risk of sexual violence, physical abuse and exploitation. Moreover, they have been subjected to grave violations with resulting psychosocial distress, including from abduction, exposure to sexual and gender-based violence, separation from or loss of family members, destruction of property, looting of food and livestock and forced displacement.Furthermore, the project’s community strengthening initiatives will directly engage community leaders and stakeholders to create a conducive environment for reintegration. This will ensure that going forward the target communities are better able to receive returning populations, both children and adults. The impact of the project will ensure that these communities are better able to receive children previously associated with armed groups, and that their leaders have the tools and capacity facilitate their acceptance in the future as well. This will allow the project to continue impacting these communities beyond the scope of the project and benefit the recipients of future reintegration efforts. The project will be carried out in an inclusive and gender-positive manner by prioritizing full and equal participation for girls and young women in the project activities. No discrimination will be based on gender. Specific attention will be put on gender-balance to ensure that girls and boys work together for greater girls’ empowerment and reintegration. The team will identify gender dynamics of the conflict and the context of the communities to adapt female participation strategies to ensure that girls are fully represented and substantively engaged. COOPI will also enforce gender-balance in its hiring of project staff, including key leadership positions, and the project’s monitoring and evaluation will include gender-specific analysis through the collection of sex-disaggregated data. The intervention is proposed to ensure essential protection support and services to children at risk and survivors that include:• case management services: direct support to unaccompanied and separated children and children with other protection concerns. Referral and liaison with specialized services and care in coordination with the CP partners and according to the established CPSWG referral pathways and follow-up;• support to foster parents (and vulnerable caregivers), training on parental skills and follow-up, includingpsychosocial support and integrated assistance (Food/Nutrition/NFI);• provision of psychosocial support (individual counselling and group counselling);• empowering local counterparts’ capacity to identify and respond to children protection needs (State Ministry of Women Affairs – SMWA and State Ministry of Youth, Sport, Social & Community Development – SMYSSCD);

Activities

• Vulnerable children, including UASC and other children with protection concerns, are identified, assisted and supported through the inter-agency case management system.Case management will include: _Case identification: identify through either direct disclosure or through community members; _Family Assessment: determining a child and family’s needs, current and potentialstrengths and weaknesses; _Case Planning: develop a specific, comprehensive, individualized and/or family-based treatment and service plan; _Implementation of the Plan: provide direct services to meet the client's needs or refer to services (access to medical care,psychiatric services, psychosocial support);_Monitoring the Case: conduct ongoing evaluations of the child's progress and ongoing needs; Child Advocacy: promote the best interest to ensure fair treatment and services. • Children detected with distress reactions are reached by psychosocial counselling and improved their psychosocial well-being. The project directs towards the community as a whole, and involving the beneficiaries in decision-making. Coordination with existing processes of community mobilization, as well as local authorities and external actors involved in the humanitarian response; the communities will be involved in understanding the impact of the situation on their children and in determining the approaches that will benefit them. Local knowledge, beliefs and practices will support children’s recovery from stressful events if they are appropriate and in the best interest of the child.• Vulnerable caregivers are supported through regular follow-up, psychosocial assistance and integrated assistance (Food/Nutrition/NFI). Awareness-raising about psychosocial, Promoting the rights and strengthening the capacities of women; Family responsibility activities for fathers and other care givers;• Local counterparts including local NGO are empowered through provision of dedicated coaching and mentoring to staff within the target LGAs.• Community Protection Committees, community groups and leadership trained to identify, address and refer child protection concerns and risks. Training for key community care givers;• Provision of social economical reintegration services to children • Support to children for self-employment or wage-employment • Rapid assessment on skills and capabilities suited to the local economy (e.g. agriculture, mechanics, embroidery)• Life-skills and financial savings and business skills trainings• Provision of supplies for housing reintegration sustenance package Gender inclusiveness will be addressed within the planned training/awareness activities: key messages will include the principles of equal opportunities (encouraging participation to the activities for girls and young women), prevention of gender-based violence and all forms of discrimination, mutual respect and solidarity, social cohesion, social inclusion.The placement in foster families will be performed considering the specific risks that girls might incur. All data will be disaggregated so that the impacts on girls and boys, women and men can be tracked. Besides being age and gender sensitive, in order to maximize the impact of care, the package of services provided by the project staff will be as much as possible standardized to rule out any unequal treatment on the basis of religious belief, income, physical attributes, geographical location etc. Clear activity plans will be developed for staff to follow during the delivery of all services; adequate training and supervision will be led by the program manager and psychosocial supervisors to guarantee the best quality of service for all children, without discrimination. Non-compliance will be sanctioned.
