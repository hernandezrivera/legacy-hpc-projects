Project Summary

This project will provide protection assistance in Batbou (is a community in Atareb sub-district/ Jabel Saman district) and Suran (is a community in A’zaz sub-district/ A’zaz district) both in Aleppo governorate, through GBV services in women and girl safe spaces (WGSS) and community based protection centre. The several displacements from different to the north of Syria caused a huge influx of IDPs to western and northern country side of Aleppo as well as Idleb governorate.
There is a huge need for protection services and especially GBV services in many areas of northern Syria and till now there is a huge gap in the coverage of the WGSS due to many reasons related to funds and lack of capacities and most of the established centres are suffering from the cutting in the fund.
Therefore SOH (Space of Hope) chose to help in reactivate an already established centres to ensure and secure fund and to not waste the human and financial resources to help those affected families where women and girls will be able to feel physically and emotionally safe. Safe in this context refers to the absence of trauma, excessive stress, and violence and express themselves without fear of judgement or harm, where they can build their social networks, receive support from their peers, and learn something new.
The main approach for SOH in this project is to provide life-saving methodologies for women and adolescent girls whom facing different challenges in the Syrian context.
The proposed activities are:
- Outreach activities including (awareness session, life skills and PSS awareness): Outreach Team will target households, community establishments, schools, and any reachable places that are considered suitable for such activities. The activities will also include lectures about GBV issues and their consequences, psychological effects of the crises on adults and self-care skills, and other topics that will be found interesting to the beneficiaries. Culture and social sensitivities will be highly considered when planning for these activities:
- rpovision of specialized services for most vulnerable women and adolescent girls ( Individual and group counselling / Focused PSS, Psychological First Aid (PFA), Focused PSS for GBV survivors (only), Case management for GBV survivors, and Referrals of survivors).
- Vocational training: The proposed activities include: 1- administrative skills, 2- sewing courses, 3- computer skills, 4- hairdressing skills, 5- handcraft, The targets are mainly Girls 13-18 years old and women till the age of 45.
- The centres will offer recreational activities, including tea and coffee meetings, weekly group and individual PSS sessions, art groups (e.g. drawing), fun activities to build community support and trustful relationships. Women and girls will have the opportunity to meet in the space to listen to stories and have any discussions.
- Capacity development for the centre’s staff on basics of GBV, PSS, and case management.
- Advocacy campaigns for women rights (leaflets and videos)
- There will be 2 case managers at the centers, where they will receive cases from other activities, They will do case study, basic counselling and referrals to other services e.g. Psychologist when needed

Needs

In the 1st of Nov 2018, Space of Hope (SoH) M&E team conducted a protection needs assessment in western and northern countryside of Aleppo governorate in three Sub-Districts, called Atareb, A'zaz and Akhtarin. The assessment was launched in order to determine the level of special needs of women affected by the Syrian crisis.The assessment took KIs interviews approach, conducted to obtain special women needs with local councils.Findings indicate there are approximately 2,928 highly vulnerable people in the area of assessment. It is believed that up most of vulnerable individuals are single women, male and females with chronic illnesses, and people with low mobility.KI in Akhtarin city indicated there are problems related to education, when 50% of women and girls in the city are illiterates, while in Batbu and Kafar Aleepo the percentage was 25% of the women.All KIs reported that the majority of school age girls are dropping out from schools.Key informants reported that very low percentage of women, both host and IDPs, have the possibility to work and the opportunities to access proper work is very limited.The main work activities that women are able to access are sewing, teaching handcrafts and farming.The average income for women who are able to work is estimated between 15,000 to 25,000 SYP.No dedicated women centers in the assessed locations, where the need for establishing this kind of center is highly demanded.KIs suggested some activities to be conducted if there is a possibility of open women centers, identified as below:• Handcrafts training: such as embroidery and knitting• Literacy activities• Cultural activities• Comprehensive courses for dropped out girlsKIs believe there is no any challenge or constrain in order to open a center in the city and no access restrictions are expected if the community centers will be launched, and people in the community are interested to establish such of this center.No mobile campaigns or outreach activities were conducted in the assessed locations to aware the community about women empowerment, and the KIs believe there is a lack of interesting among the humanitarian actors.KIs are strongly expect that these centers will achieve very good results to the community, when women can improve their knowledge about handcrafts and other activities, education importance and their rights. In addition, this will give the women good opportunity to meet and exchange their experience among themselves.According to the KIs and analysis of the assessment results, the needs for creation of safe spaces for women and girls (community centers etc.) to socialize and interact as well as psycho-social support services is highly recommended.Many of the same needs were addressed in the HNO report of 2019 and the monitoring reports of the protection cluster shows that the need for the WGSS centers is wild and the need for this protection activities for women and adolescent girls is highly needed.

Implementation of activities

SOH will prepare a training plan, an activity plan and a monitoring plan and will share these with the GBV staff at the start of the project.SOH will have overall responsibility for the project, including technical advice, monitoring of outputs and reporting.The main activities of the project are:• Training of GBV staff on GBV basic concepts, gander equality , PSS training, case management and SOPs• Design and print of context and audience specific GBV materials;• Provision of tailor-made audience-specific targeting GBV ;• Provision of safety briefings to humanitarian staff.• Provision of specialized services for most vulnerable women and adolescent girls.• Coordinated awareness raising campaigns on awareness raising sessions on protection risk mitigation with leaflets.The project teams will provide direct face-to-face empowerment and GBV prevention activities in a number of different ways:• House to house awareness raising sessions on protection risk mitigation targeting Women and Girls .• Relaxation sessions, discussions, meetings and empowerment and GBV prevention activities in Women and Girls Safe Space and outreach activities.• Provide video (A cartoon about the negative effects of existing violence, social diversity and about the importance of girls education) indirectly through posters campaigns and media campaigns.SoH MEAL team will focus on monitoring the activities on daily basis, field reports will be provided to ensure smooth and quality of the project’s implementation. Monitoring tools will include: interviews, questionnaires, opinion boxes and FGDs with the beneficiaries, community and other stakeholders as needed. Obtained results will be presented and discussed with the concerned authorities in this area, which includes beneficiaries and partners in a transparent manner. SoH has developed several tools and approaches to ensure close monitoring of its programs including: • Weekly Activity Reports, Monthly Plan • Feedback and complaints mechanisms (complaint boxes, hotline number, Whats App and Email address)• Routine site monitoring visits and observation monitoring • Training Assessments • Mobile data collection through KoBo toolbox.Indicators tracking will be monitored by the M&E Manager in monthly basis through excel spreadsheet. This tool is shared on a monthly basis with the executive manager for review and feedback.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centred services 2. Promotion of SOPs roll out for all staff of organizations involved in the referral 3. Traditional legal systems put women more at risk, message to be mainstreamed 4. Self care strategies as part of programmes and organization HR policy 5. Include supervisory structures in response services 6. GBV case management service providers ensure confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place.6. Ensure all assessment teams and emergency response team have trained protection team members 7. Dignity kits distribution as safe entry point in the community to reach vulnerable women and girls 8. Outreach team in place to be ready fr emergency deployment, keeping core GBV centre staff in place.9. Community engagement from onset of project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders 10. Ensure the support of male family members by engaging them in awareness-raising activities 11. Adaptation of GBV messaging to ensure sensitivity to the context and culture 12. Thorough gender analysis prior to start of programming 13. Equip all WGSS with child-care services 14. Provide transportation support for persons with disability 15. Include persons with disability in all conducted assessments at the level of WGSS and/or community to understand their needs and gaps in their access to services 16. Ensure all WGSS staff are properly trained on how to identify persons with disability in need of treatment for a timely referral to relevant services.17. Consult women and men, boys and girls in the design of the programme. 18. Ensure beneficiaries know services where to disclose if violence occurs 19. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report 20. Establish good relation with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles and stick to them. 21. Targeting adolescent girls with socially accepted activities like life skills as safe entry point 22. Engage with local authorities and clear communication on project objectives 23. Service mapping including only those that can provide girls' centred services 24. Promotion of Adolescent girl strategy, SOPs and awareness toolkit roll out, for all staff of organizations involved in girls' programming. 25. Action plans for risk mitigation interventions 26. Strong buy in from senior humanitarian community and WOS 27. Inclusion of GBV survivors in distribution criteria28. Use of SOPs and other guidelines and resources from GBV AoR in line with global standards
