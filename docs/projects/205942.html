Project Summary

This project will provide Integrated, lifesaving, emergency health interventions for populations affected by conflict, flood, disease outbreak and displacement, targeting a total of 168,057 people (82,348 Males and 85,709 Females). Out of these, 35,292 will be under the age of 5 years (17,293 Males and 17,999 Females) and 132,765 above the age of 5 years (65,055 Males and 67,710 Females). 6,722 will be Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW). 2,689 of those targeted will be people living with disability. 21,087 of those targeted will be IDPs (under-5 – 5,063 and above 5 – 16,024), and 36,581 of those targeted will be Returnees (under-5 – 9,056 and above 5 – 27,525). The project will focus on women, girls, boys, and men among the Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) , Returnees and host populations with limited or no access to health services. The project will ensure improved access to integrated emergency lifesaving healthcare services focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality for conflict-affected, flood-affected, and vulnerable IDPs, host community and returnee households in Bor South, Wau, Twic East, Pariang, Pibor, and Duk Counties. In addition, due to its geographical presence and health programming in Wau and Pibor Counties under the Health Pooled Fund funding, MOFA, and ECHO funding, CARE will have the capacity to provide emergency health interventions in the 2 counties (Wau, and Pibor) depending on the need and availability of funding. To address the identified health needs within the 5 counties, CARE will deliver a package of emergency and essential health services in line with the three 2024 Health Cluster Objectives as follows:
(1) Vulnerable people who experience multi-sectoral severity levels of 4 and 5 have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to meet their basic needs.
(2) Vulnerable people are exposed to fewer protection threats and incidents, and those who are exposed have access to tailored, timely, appropriate services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action.
(3) Vulnerable people withstand and recover from shocks, have their resilience to shocks and stresses built, and seek solutions to displacement with respect to their rights.

1. Needs

The health system in South Sudan remains weakened by multiple shocks experienced in the past years and continue to deteriorate further with the influx of refugees and returnees from Sudan crisis (OCHA). This project will provide Integrated, lifesaving, emergency health interventions for populations affected by conflict, flood, disease outbreak and displacement, targeting a total of 168,057 people (82,348 Males and 85,709 Females). Out of these, 35,292 will be under the age of 5 years (17,293 Males and 17,999 Females) and 132,765 above the age of 5 years (65,055 Males and 67,710 Females). 6,722 will be Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW). 2689 of those targeted will be people living with disability. 21,087 of those targeted will be IDPs (under-5 – 5,063 and above 5 – 16,024), and 36,581 of those targeted will be Returnees (under-5 – 9,056 and above 5 – 27,525). Flooding has continued to detrimentally affect many locations where CARE South Sudan works (Pariang, Bor, Pibor, Twic East and Duk) leading to destruction of health facilities and properties which heavily affected the capacity of the health systems and its ability to function well. According to OCHA and Prof. Leben N. Moro, 907,000 people are exposed to flooding while another 386,000 are affected by flooding, of which 167,000 come from states where CARE works in. The majority of those affected and exposed are in Jonglei (49,000) and Unity (118,000) where CARE implements its health programmes (OCHA, October 2022, HNO, 2023). The current flooding has destroyed health facilities and WASH facilities (Boreholes, Water Points and Latrines) leading to widespread contamination of water sources and loss of water storage containers as well as long lasting insecticide treated nets (LLIN). As a result, Malaria and Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) related morbidity and mortality trends in the last Quarter of 2022 were above those of 2020 within the affected counties, particularly in Twic East and Pariang. In addition, and more worryingly, flooding has hugely impaired access to health services in Twic East, Duk and parts of Pariang. Roads have been flooded or destroyed, making transportation difficult and expensive. Canoes and speed boats, which are the only means of transport available, are expensive and not easily available, further negatively impacting access to health facilities for critical maternal and child health services. Poor access to health services (Only 28.6% of the population living within 5 km of a health facility, Macharia et al, 2017) leading to only 2.6% of achievement of recommended 9 vaccination doses (L. Belaid, 2022, ISNA, 2023) coupled with population displacement due to flooding provide a conducive environment for disease outbreaks.Moreover, outbreaks of cholera, measle, meningitis, and Hepatitis E have been declared in the CARE areas of work by the Ministry of Health and WHO in 2023 due to low immunization coverage of 27.5% in states like Jonglei State (Jok. P. M. Jil, 2022) due to frequent population displacement by flood and other factors. In response, vaccination efforts for measles, Meningitis, and Cholera are ongoing in various counties. Low immunization coverage within the Counties where CARE operates continues to be an issue of concern, with Penta 3 coverage at 58.4% and Measles at 56.6% coverage at the State levels. Malaria continues to be the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounting for 64% of all morbidities and 69% of all mortalities in 2023 (WHO; MOH, ISNA). The ongoing crisis in Sudan is driving up the humanitarian needs, with the few available resources being diverted to cater for returnees and over 300,000 refugees’ response activities that have crossed to South Sudan , and there are many more to come (OCHA, 2023). Access to maternal health services remains a challenge, with Maternal mortality in the country is one of the highest in the world, with 1,223 per 100,000 live births (HSSP, World Bank, WHO, 2023).

2. Project activities

CARE will provide Emergency Life-saving Health services in a total of 40 Static Health Facilities (under the World Bank and Health Pooled Fund) funding and 10 Mobile Clinics across 3 States of Jonglei, Unity and Western Bar el Ghazal in line with the South Sudan’s Ministry of Health Standards and Guidelines as well as the WHO and SPHERE Standards and guidelines. The response activities implemented by CARE will be based on the epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, disease burden and seasonality. CARE will work with other clusters and partners to ensure integrated response across the field locations. These Clusters include Nutrition, Protection and FSL. CARE will also put in place a robust system of soliciting, receiving, processing and responding to the feedback and complaints from the communities that we serve. Analysis and results of the feedback and complaints provided will be shared monthly and this will contribute to learning and provide information for evidence based decision making in order to improve programme quality and performance. In the 5 Counties where CARE proposes to work, it is also the partner that is implementing development related health funding from HPF and UNICEF. CARE will therefore ensure that the activities carried out under the HRP will be linked to the Development /longer term funding. To address the identified health needs within the identified counties, CARE will:1. Improve access to lifesaving essential health services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, provision of GBV and MPHSS services to the vulnerable population including those living with disability. 2. Reduce mortality and morbidity by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases3. Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. CARE will train staff on emergency preparedness and response for diseases of outbreak potential. This will ensure early detection and response. In addition, CARE will strengthen its existing surveillance systems through the IDSR and EWARS. CARE will provide health services that are adapted to the COVID-19 context in South Sudan. This will be in addition to ensuring COVID-19 prevention measures are taken into account while continuing to deliver health services. Specifically, in response to the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) CARE will carry out the following activities. - Ensuring epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response across all its health projects. - Providing immunization against vaccine preventable diseases and procurement of outbreak response commodities for the health programmes. - Provision of BeMONC/ CeMONC services including procurement of essential RH commodities and building the capacity of skilled birth attendants- Provision of life-saving essential health care services including management of non-communicable diseases through static (Hospitals, PHCC, and PHCU) and mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers. - Integrating MHPSS and SGBV services in health facilities and provision of supplies for MHPSS services. - IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities - Views of the communities are taken into consideration in the provision of health services - Provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services –- Community health service provision linking with facility- based services using initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives - Provision of rehabilitation services and assertive devices for the for disabled.
