Project Summary

In South Sudan, the IPC findings indicate that an acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation is deteriorating due to the economic crisis, climatic shocks - primarily widespread flooding - conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile country. In 2024, an estimated 702,000 people (60.6 percent of the state’s population) are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity in Unity State and Ruweng Administrative Area, of which 177,000 people are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 525,000 people are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. . December 2024-March 2025 cites onset of food security deterioration with its 64.7% population (unity) at IPC 3 which 204,000 people at Emergency IPC 4.It’s expected to worsens in April -July 2025 with 71.6 % of state population at Crisis IPC 3, which 255,000 people are likely to be in Emergency IPC 4 with 5 of its counties including Koch, Leer and Mayendit at IPC 4 acute food insecurity.
The ongoing conflict in Sudan and reported disease outbreaks such as cholera puts the country at risk of imported cases. The most recent WHO/UNICEF estimates of measles and Penta 3 coverage is 72 per cent and 73 per cent respectively. Malaria remains endemic and the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, often exacerbated by frequent flooding events. Findings from the 2024 ISNA show that only 57 per cent of the population can access the nearest health facility in less than an hour, while 44 per cent cited long distance as a barrier to accessing health care. Women and girls in remote and crisis-affected areas facing additional challenges due to, limited trained personnel, recurrent supply chain breaks affecting family planning commodities and essential drugs for clinical delivery assistance and treatment of sexually transmitted infections. While the Health Sector Transformation Programme (HSTP) is currently supporting 50 per cent of functional health facilities, 18 percent are non-functional. The July 2024 GBV health analysis revealed only 28 per cent of health facilities provide GBV care, with 43 per cent offering services. Lower-level facilities struggle with staffing and infrastructure, limiting early intervention to referrals. This project is aiming at enhancing access and strengthening the quality provision of lifesaving primary health care services in Leer, Mayendit and Koch counties through support of 2 PHCCs (Gany PHCC in Koch and Adok PHCC in Leer), 3PHCUs (Ngony PHCU in Koch, Madol PHCU in Mayendit and Padeah PHCU in Leer) and 3 mobile clinics response (Jaak in Koch, Rubkong in Leer, and Rubnor in Mayendit). UNIDOR will encourage provision of CME or capacity building for HCWs to improve their skills and knowledge in order to improve on low EPI and DSA coverage across the three project targeted counties. UNIDOR will enhance awareness sessions on endemic prone diseases especially at the congested IDPs sites and community levels. This project proposal will target all gender and age groups with a total beneficiary of 60,000 individuals, 20,000 in Koch, 20,000 in Leer and 20,000 in Mayendit counties respectively. The target beneficiaries include 23,520 men, 24,480 women, 5,880 boys and 6,120 girls. The SMART surveys conducted by WR shows Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates in Koch @21%, in Mayendit by REACH show GAM rate @22.8%), and in Leer by Medair shows GAM rate @20.1%, all are above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%. The project will also support 250 people with disabilities including 80 men, 100 women, 30 boys and 40 girls through case identification, psycho social support and basic mental health services, first aid counseling, treatment and referral of the clients to nearby health county or state hospitals.

2. Project activities

UNIDOR seek to enhance access in provision of life-saving primary healthcare by scaling up response in 3 Mobile clinics, Support 3 PHCUs and 2 PHCCs. In line with CO1, the 2 PHCCs will provide equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations. More than 40,000 people will benefit from OPD curative consultations with 15,680 A5 male, 16,320 A5 Female, 3,920 U5 male, 4,080 U5 female. The project will target 300 pregnant mothers for safe deliveries at health facility by SBA. Over 80 HCWs will be trained on IMNCI, Infection prevention and control, CMR and Quality of Care (QOC). To improve the routine immunization coverage especially on Penta3, the project will target over 2,300 males under 1 year and 2,500 females under 1 year. Over 1,500 children under 15 will be reached with reactive measles vaccination campaign across the three counties as to reduce the reoccurrence of measles outbreak in three project targeted counties. About 300 people will benefit from MHPSS services. 120 SGBV survivors will be receiving CMR services. The project will provide rehabilitation service to over 250 beneficiaries. The project will target 3,400 (M,1,666 and F, 1,734) above 6 years and adults to be reached with health education and promotion awareness sessions on general hygiene and sanitation, timely health seeking behavior, 6 months exclusive breast feeding and importance or benefit of routine EPI vaccination. Through the EWARS and IDSR system, the project will report 15% of disease outbreak alert for investigation within 48 hours of notification. Improve resilience among vulnerable populations including disabled groups and mentally ill persons through psycho-social support services. The project will integrate response for health/Wash/Nutrition/ food security and protection including Management of SAM with medical complications. There shall be flow of information sharing on service gaps and achievements for planning and accountability to affected population. Quarterly joint assessments and monitoring of services through M&E and RRM team will be of priority. The project will enhance Advocacy for development emergency funding to support unforeseen emergencies through other development donors like MDTF, etc.
