Project Summary

Provide safe space for quality learning, build capacity of inspectors, teachers, and PTA, mobilize community supports learning.
This is a one-year project to be implemented in Awerial and Pibor counties and designed to provide quality education to children and adolescents, particularly girls and young women, affected by conflict and flooding in Jonglei and Lakes states. The focus will be on IDPs and host communities who are in dire need of humanitarian assistance, enhancing access and quality of learning. The intervention will promote the physical and psychosocial wellbeing and development of learners in 14 schools of Awerial (Mingkaman) and Pibor counties by 2022.
Lakes State, and in particular Awerial (Mingkaman), has experienced a huge number of IDPs from Bor county of Jonglei who migrated as a result of flooding, displacing up to 50,376 (M=4,020, W=7,113, G=21,649, B=17,594) people currently camping in Awerial. Of this number, 40% are estimated to be children of school going age (6-10 years & 11 – 17 years) who have limited access to learning facilities, as most schools remain closed due to COVID-19. IDPs are currently using school structures of Mingkaman primary school, Tomrok primary school, Matt Academy, Roordit primary school, Padar primary school, Pannhial primary school & Kon Anok primary school as camps, which has led to the damage of infrastructure and school items. This crisis has affected men, women, boys and girls differently. Men were concerned with the animals while the women were concerned of family belongings and children in search for better places to settle. It is the role of the women to make shelter for both children and animals alike, girls were very much assisting their mothers with family works hence girls have difficulty accessing school because of domestic responsibilities. This is child labour which the project will tackle it through the awareness raising on the rights of a girl child and the back to campaigns. At current concentration points, a few available Pit latrines in all the schools where IDPs are residing are filled up. Open defection is the being practice along the road and at the schools surrounding and new market compound which is a potential risk for diseases outbreak. This has resulted in high demand for the rehabilitation of occupied schools in preparation for the school reopening due in April 2021. In addition, the IDPs will increase the number of school children in need of education services in Mingkaman/Awerial, as these institutions will be shared among IDPs and the host community, overburdening the few facilities currently available (IRNA Report Jonglei Flood 2020).
In the last two years, Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) and Jonglei State have been experiencing devastating intercommunal violence, followed by cattle raids, women and child abductions and killings, and flooding that has submerged half of the town and surrounding Payams and Villages. This has resulted in massive displacement of people from their homes as well as loss of livelihoods; and also destroyed and submerged school facilities, denying 19,168 children (9,965G, 9,201B) access to quality education and protection services, according to the IRNA report (Final draft) Sept. 21st – 22nd 2020.
Women and children are estimated to make up over 61% of this population affected by flooding and conflict (IRNA report 2020). Despite the hope instilled by the signing of the Revitalised Peace Agreement, the cumulative effects of years of conflict, violence, protracted and repeated displacement, layered on top of pre-existing development challenges, continue to impact people throughout the country. This has translated into sustained poverty, periodic famine, persistent education and protection concerns, and a lack of life skills and access to basic services, with women and girls being disproportionately affected hence girls have difficulty accessing school because of domestic responsibilities. 1 - Needs

On the 21st March 2020, the South Sudan government closed all schools to prevent the spread of COVID-19, pushing close to 2 million children out of school, 43% of which are girls. According to Save the Children, UNICEF and UNESCO released a press statement (https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/news/east-africa/covid-19-south-sudan-weighs-costs-of-keeping-schools-shut--1911350), the closure of schools due to COVID-19 has adverse effects on girls more than boys. In this period, school girls are involved in tea making business, lured to night clubs and couple with severe needs for personal materials like sanitary pads, washing soap, perfume/lotion among has resulted into unnecessary pregnancies, early/forced marriages. Boys equally have become sellers in the shops, washing cars, tone boys, riding motorbike as business, betting/gambling and loitering in the streets. They are seen taking drugs and getting in crimes. This is happening at a time when a number of schools that were either attacked or occupied by armed actors were trying to re-open, further delaying children’s access to learning. The sending back of children to homes further exposes them to child labour risks like (shop sellers, tea making, car washing, hawkers, riding motorbike for economic purpose, shoe polishing, canoe business etc.), abuse (physical and sexual) where especially schools girls are impregnated, married, raped and psychological distress due to violence committed against children, economic difficulties their parents that has affected their welfare at home, indefinite closure of schools (South Sudan COVID 19 Preparedness and Response Plan 2020). Yet according to UNESCO Report 2018, about 2.4 children were out of school prior to the COVID-19 outbreak as a result of the protracted conflict of 2013 and 2016. COVID-19 has led to a 30% increase in the number of girls and boys engaging in child labour (girls facing increased domestic responsibilities and boys seeking waged opportunities) and facing physical, sexual or psychological violence. In some cases, the loss of household income due to economic disruptions caused by covid-19 has led to a 45% increase in child, early and forced marriage among girls and increased their exposure to sexual and gender based violence. This has led to a 65% increase in unintended and early pregnancy.Despite signing of the Revitalised Peace Agreement, the cumulative effects of years of conflict, violence, protracted and repeated displacement, layered on top of pre-existing development challenges, continue to impact South Sudanese throughout the country and abroad. In addition to psychological distress, men and women lost key assets like land, shops, vehicles and animals, access to education has been interrupted for girls and boys, social networks deteriorated, housing and shelter damaged. Coping strategies at all levels have weakened, particularly as recurrent conflicts in Pibor and Lakes State have increased the number of female- and child-headed households as well as unaccompanied and separated children. Localized violence and conflict continue in parts of the country, especially Jonglei state, putting the national level peace agreement and prospects for returns and recovery at risk. As a result of conflict, COVID-19 and flood, this has led to massive displacement which in turn puts pressure on host community schools and increased number of children dropping out from school according to recent statistic of candidates for primary leaving examination in Pibor and Awerial counties. Lack of pit latrines, open defecation and poor sanitation services has caused an increase in environmental risks for the safety of schools for children.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

This will be done through awareness meeting and training of formed committees including teachers, inspectors, PTA etc, where PSEA will form one of the sessions of the training content and meeting agenda items and or any relevant any other business.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Plan international is committed to addressing the potential protection concerns, including sexual and gender based violence, and these are captured in the education project design. In this project, protection concerns for instance, PSS activities, reviewing safeguarding policies and procedures in schools, ensuring teachers understand and sign code of conduct, ensuring parents and caregivers and children understand the policies and how to report. Ensuring teachers know how and where to refer child protection cases will be done to address protection, deprivation and coercion threats in Awerial and Pibor counties.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

There will be use of a hot line number provided to the community to communicate any complaint. PI project team will receive and provide feedback to beneficiaries during field visits when deemed appropriate. 1. The feedback will be two ways. Listening from the beneficiaries and responding to raised concerns if any. But also updating the beneficiaries of gaps and way forward or changes in the project design. 2. Feedback will be collected through conduct of focus group discussion. 3. Help desks and suggestion boxes will be established in the schools. PI M&E will collect the complaints on monthly basis for analysis and action planning.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Plan International will ensure project staff, local authorities and beneficiaries report suspected cases through the hotline or available complaint mechanism for effective management. As voice of the voiceless, Plan International will advocacy and engagement with local authorities to ensure reduction in rate of abuses especially at schools. PI staff must adhere to the Code of conduct while in the field.
