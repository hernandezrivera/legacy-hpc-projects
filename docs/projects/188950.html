Project Summary

Humanitarian needs remained high across Jonglei State in the period between April and August 2021 particularly Ayod, Bor South, Canal-Pigi, and Fangak Counties, which were severely affected by flooding that resulted in widespread displacement and food insecurity (REACH Dec 2021). The cumulative impact of recurrent flooding contributed to the destruction and damage to water facilities, leaving vulnerable people in urgent need of water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services, the HNO added. The notable drivers of humanitarian needs are mainly flooding and conflict in the proposed location which has left the bulk of the population displaced without basic needs. WASH conditions are expected to worsen in 56 counties including the four proposed counties due to flood damage to existing WASH infrastructure during the wet season and the resulting increase in open-defecation practices, use of surface water for drinking, risk of disease outbreaks, and reduced humanitarian access for WASH actors due to insecurity and flooding (OCHA Feb 2022). According to the WASH cluster severity map, the four proposed counties (Aweil East, Twic East, Bor South, and Duk) are ranked level 4 or IPC 4. The project proposes to implement activities integrating WASH, FSL, Health, Protection, and Nutrition programming at households, institutions, and community levels while mainstreaming GBV and COVID-19 risk mitigation, prioritizing the immediate provision of integrated lifesaving WASH and nutrition services to the most vulnerable and food-insecure populations in WSC, 4 and 5. The project will support 15,300 people in (Bor South: 3082, Duk: 4128, Twic East: 4773
And Aweil: 3316) where boys, girls, men, and women are 4145, 4048, 3179, and 3928 respectively where 47.87% are males and 52.13% are females. The project seeks 500,000 United States Dollars to implement the integrated services in 2022.

1 - Needs

8.9 million people are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022, compared to 8.3 million in 2021 (HNO 2022). Humanitarian needs remained high across Jonglei State in the period between April and August 2021 particularly Ayod, Bor South, Canal-Pigi, and Fangak Counties, which were severely affected by flooding that resulted in widespread displacement and food insecurity (REACH Dec 2021). The cumulative impact of recurrent flooding contributed to the destruction and damage to water facilities, leaving vulnerable people in urgent need of water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services, the HNO added. Individuals displaced due to the flooding continue to gather in informal displacement sites or seek shelter in churches and schools in higher ground areas; many of those displaced by the flooding had already been uprooted by sub-national violence or by previous floods in 2020 (USAID November 2021). According to UNICEF Jan 2022, displacement and limited access have also resulted in more children out of school, which, combined with displacement and loss of household assets and livelihoods, is exposing them to multiple threats, including abuse and exploitation. According to the OCHA infographic released in February 2022, in Bor South County, Jonglei, sub-national violence displaced over 7,000 people, with at least 32 civilian casualties meanwhile in Aweil East an armed attack displaced 2,600 households, approximately 15,600 people. WASH conditions are expected to worsen in 56 counties due to flood damage to existing WASH infrastructure during the wet season and the resulting increase in open-defecation practices, use of surface water for drinking, risk of disease outbreaks, and reduced humanitarian access for WASH actors due to insecurity and flooding (OCHA Feb 2022). According to the WASH cluster severity map, the four proposed counties (Aweil East, Twic East, Bor South, and Duk) are ranked level 4 or IPC 4. GBI is a static partner currently implementing emergency food production to 5825 HHs with a total of 35,950 people in Duk county. With previous experience implementing emergency projects in FSL and WASH, with the ready capacity to scale up in locations of high needs. GBI through SSHF implemented Integrated WASH and Nutrition project targeting 1,600 SAM children and over 17,000 individuals in communities with safe water and sanitation in 2019/2020 and Emergency WASH flood response in Twic East County supported through Rapid Response Fund (RRF). In 2019/2020, GBI reached 12,623 households with emergency food production in Aweil East through the provision of crop kits, tools, fishing, and vegetable kits while providing capacity building to selected farmers in groups and kitchen gardening in nutrition centers. In line with the call for proposals and 2022 HRP WASH Cluster objectives, GBI plans to scale up in greater Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, where we have an operational presence. Activities integrating WASH, FSL, Health, Protection, and Nutrition programming at households, institutions, and community level while mainstreaming GBV and COVID-19 risk mitigation, prioritizing the immediate provision of integrated lifesaving WASH and nutrition services to the most vulnerable and food-insecure populations in WSC, 4 and 5.

2 - Project activities

1. SAM children and their caretakers as well as Pregnant and Lactating women are targeted at nutrition facilities with WASH services to reduce disease transmission at the household level. 2. Nutrition facilities are also targeted with a basic water and sanitation package to reduce the risk of HAIs transmission due to poor WASH conditions. 3. Provision of WASH NFIs, hygiene promotion, emergency sanitation, and water supply to immediately break down disease transmission cycles. 4. Construction of quality sustainable infrastructures such as flood-resilient facilities, robust supply systems, and self-sustained maintenance mechanisms through enhanced community engagement and ownership. Activities recommended under this approach include repair/rehabilitation of water points, extension/upgrading of existing water supply systems, and upgrading water points with flood-resilient approaches for those located in flooded areas. Sanitation may be recommended in locations facing a high risk of outbreaks and congested sites.5. In all WASH programming, mitigating Gender-Based Violence will be mainstreamed; women and other at-risk groups are involved in the siting, design, construction, and maintenance of WASH facilities and in hygiene promotion activities.

5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

As a cross cutting activity, market-based programing should be integrated in WASH programming to promote a multiplier effect in local markets and the intervention area. The project will recognize and seek to identify ways in which the local market can contribute to meeting the needs of the community. Decreased level of access to resources, logistical constraints due to rainy season road blockage, limited access and the uncertainty in the availability and prices of key WASH items in the local markets has impacted the ability for humanitarian agencies to respond to humanitarian needs in an efficient, effective and timely way (Reliefweb Jan 2022).

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

GBI will use monthly published market information from JMMI-TWG. In an effort to inform cash-based interventions and better understand market dynamics, the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) was created by the South Sudan Cash Working Group (CWG) in August 2019, led by REACH and supported by the CWG members. The JMMI aims to track the Multi-Sectoral Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (MSSMEB) across all assessed locations on a monthly basis.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback from the beneficiaries is used to guide programming and incorporate the inputs into activities. The feedback helps in addressing shortfalls in future interventions.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will conduct entry meetings with the local authority, RRC, beneficiaries as part of community mobilization and with different groups, for example, PWDs, women groups, youth representatives, chiefs, local authorities, and beneficiaries’ representatives at the project inception. The aim of the meetings is to orient and mobilize different stakeholders and disseminate the project’s information (project duration, target beneficiaries and selection criteria, distribution site selections, donors, and the role of the community in the selection of beneficiaries and volunteers). The project will prioritize the needs of vulnerable people such as men, women, boys, girls, PWDs, PLW, elderly, etc. and this information is communicated through community mobilization and inception meetings in presence of local authority. The project will consult people with disability and collect disability disaggregated data which allows them to identify specific needs/vulnerabilities, risks, and barriers that prevent people with disability from accessing WASH services on an equal basis with other people.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

GBI has a policy on PSEA which is served to all project staff for their understanding. Every time a new project is being launched, the staff is oriented on PSEA, as well as vendors and beneficiaries. GBI works with protection partners to handle protection issues including SEA cases. In case there are SEA cases, GBI through referral pathways available refers the cases to the protection partner to investigate and help the survivors. GBV focal point or officers are consulted to identify safe, confidential, and appropriate systems of care (i.e. referral pathways) for survivors, WASH staff have the basic skills to provide survivors with information on where they can obtain support, GBV messages (including where to report risk and how to access care) are incorporated into hygiene promotion and other WASH-related community outreach activities using multiple formats to ensure accessibility.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During community meetings, beneficiaries share their say or beliefs with the agency. During distribution suggestion boxes are provided or Rapid Post Distribution Monitoring (RPDM), FGDs, or exit interviews are conducted to collect views from the beneficiaries on their perception, satisfaction, or dissatisfaction about the project. Feedback from beneficiaries is reviewed from time to time for adjusting or guiding the intervention. If the feedback is within the project scope, the agency will respond positively.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Male and female, women and men or boys and girls are differently affected by humanitarian crises, situations, or disasters requiring an intervention. Community mobilization brings together all people of different sex, age group, and status to decide for themselves as far as humanitarian assistance is concerned. The project will prioritize the needs of women and girls by involving them in key decision-making processes and by being part of AAP so as to mitigate the risk of being left out on grounds of cultural practices that don’t allow women and girls to participate in community work. Forced taxation of Humanitarian Assistance is mitigated by seeking a letter of clearance from the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) in Juba for humanitarian items moving from Juba to field locations. The risk is also transferred to a third party by giving transportation work to logistics companies that take responsibility for the safe delivery of items. While delivering within the locality security reports or situations that might arise to forced taxation are analyzed before authorizing delivery. Movement of staff is planned to take place between 9:00 am – 4 pm when security is guaranteed – no travels allowed. Normally, the local authority and local leaders try to corrupt the process of identification of beneficiaries by bringing their close relatives instead of vulnerable people in need of assistance or even having themselves registered. To mitigate targeting of wrong beneficiaries GBI conducts community mobilization before identification of beneficiaries to inform them about the selection criteria. We also work with Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) Committees established at the Boma level to help in the identification of beneficiaries. GBI also works in close coordination with relevant government ministries who are aware of the humanitarian work.
