Project Summary

The overall objectives of the proposed project is to contribute towards the provision of critical protection assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys affected by floods, conflcit and disasters in Awerial County-Lakes State. The project will support the delivery of an integrated package of risk mitigation and prevention of GBV activities, at the community level, and specialized services to survivors in the areas with severe needs. The interventions will to provide immediate life-saving support to women and girls at risk, mitigate risks of GBV, and shift social norms that strengthen GBV by promoting gender equality and behavioral change and working with community-based structures.
To ensure that quality GBV case management services are available to survivors in 8 payams of Awerial County (Abuyong, Alel I, Alel II, Bun-agok, Dor, Magok, Nile, Puluk) and among six Bomas of (Gut-thom, Wunthou, Lim kethie/Ahou, Mingkaman, Duony-gok, Kalthok).The project will strengthen capacity of the existing community frontline workers and Government structures on critical sectors (PSS, health, and security) to provide quality services to survivors of GBV, and will increase access to support services through the establishment of Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) to increase their access to quality case management, psychosocial and medical services and livelihoods opportunities.This project targets women, girls, Men and boys in IDPs settings, returnees and host communities as primary beneficiaries.
This project will also increase access to community based protection services such as WGFS to over 8,271 women, adolescent girls, SGBV survivors, and other vulnerable groups of women. The project will provide improved livelihood options for survivors of SGBV to move out of poverty. More importantly, the project will target a total of 8,271 beneficiries; girls (30%), women (22%), boys (29%) men with ( 20% ) the beneficiry categories include the people with disability, Host communities ,IDPs and returnees.
SMAs comparative advantage is its long presence in Mingkaman - Awerial county in Lakes State ,SMA curretly a lead agent for GBV protection and has been implementing various projects in Mingkaman (Field office based) Kalthok and Mariik since 2018 it has a good working relationship with stakeholders across these three Counties of greater Yirol (Awerail, Yirol East and Yirol West). The organisation Piloted a Project titled The Women Alternative Peace Front (WAPF) in Awerial County – Mingkaman, Kalthok, Yolakot and Dakatwan as part of women peace and security projects supported by UN Women and UNFPA . “From Input to Impact: Monitoring and Evaluation of GBV Programming in Restrictive Environments and therefore this project is going to contribute to the reduction of GBV cases noted during the piloting phase.

1 - Needs

Awerial is one the county in Lakes State, comprises of 8 payams (Abuyong, Alel I, Alel II, Bun-agok, Dor, Magok, Nile, Puluk. It is a home to over 150,000 people including over 70,000 IDPs from Jonglei State who came to Awerial (Mingkaman and Kalthok) since 2014. The reasons for displacement include flooding and lack of food. The IDPs preferred Mingkaman to other areas because its highland, safe, closer to Jonglei, cheap in terms of transport, presence of relatives . In 2020 Awerial also experienced worst combination of flash flood from heavy rains and river Nile banks overflow which severely affected and displaced settlements along river Nile and lowland areas where women and children were severely affected. In August 2020, the Eastern Lakes Inter-Cluster Working Groups from 21 difference agencies include Samaritan Mission Aid (SMA) and the RRC conducted rapid flood assessment. Respondents informed partners that the flood has been one of the worst ever witnessed in their live time in Awerial County in particular. Consequently, more than 50 villages inhabited by nearly 60,000 residents in six Bomas of Awerial County (Gut-thom, Wunthou, Lim kethie/Ahou, Mingkaman, Duony-gok, and Kalthok) were affected, 50% of the population along the river Nile and 100% of some Bomas such as Wunthou, Gutthom and Limkethii/Ahou have been displaced. The floods affected assets, shelters, crops, fishing nets and some social facilities have been destroyed, 3 death (2 women and 1 child) have been reported in Kalthok and Yolakot villages where one woman is reported drowned when she tried to cross flood stream to Yol Akot village while in Kalthok, a tukul collapsed on a mother and child at night killing them instantly. Most household properties were lost due to flooding and the most vulnerable and people with special needs, women and children from the affected communities has been exposes to protection risk. In September 2020 IOM recorded, a total of 32,660 individuals displaced from Twic East (27,251 individuals from Nyuak, Pakeer, Lith, Ajuong, Kongor payams) and Duk (5,409 individuals from Payuel, Ageer, Duk Padiet Payams). On the 8th of February 2021, Protection cluster in Mingkaman conducted a field monitoring visit to Mariik IDPs, to understand the general protection situation, and the current needs of the most vulnerable persons. The team had met with an estimated of 300 HHs. The assessment report indicates; protection concerns, as displacement becomes increasingly protracted, the general lack of safety in communities continues to deteriorate. As parts of Responses Plan,the team recommended establishment of community center for economic empowerment initiatives, distribution of dignity kits for girls and women at high risk of protection. In response, NPA was recommended to response to emergency food security and livelihoods, CUAMM to establish an outreach nutrition center in Mariik . RUWASSA is to support WASH , CIDO and PAH to support both IDPs and Hosts with WASH items. SMA currently implementing protection integrated project in three (3) locations including Mariik, Mingkaman and Kalthok with components of women peace and security (WPS) supported by UN women (2) distribution of dignity kits, but the protection concerns remains high and unattended to. SMA is concern about protection needs in Awerial C SMA plan to scale-up the GBV interventions by increasing geographical coverage, from current three intervention locations ( Mingkaman, Marrik and Kalthok) to 8 prioritized locations in order to ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of 8,000 targeted people among vulnerable 2,400 girls, 2,320 boys, 1760 women and 1,600 men the project also will provide livelihood and economic support through Cash and Voucher Assistance to 4160, vulnerable women and girls and GBV survivors as part of a multi-sectoral GBV response.

2 - Project activities

To achieve the project objectives, SMA will undertake the following activities: 1) Provision of survivor centered psychosocial support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery, 2) Provision of appropriate GBV case management services including coordinated care and support, 3) Strengthen and establish GBV Referral Pathway in order to enhance access to essential GBV services and protection mechanisms and SMA will work with other partners to regularly review and update the GBV referral pathway with a functional contact person both primary and secondary which will be shared among beneficiaries and partners for incidents reporting, case follow-up and referral. 4) Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowerment. 5)Provision of legal services that protect the rights of GBV survivors and promote their access to justice; 6) Provision of livelihood and economic support including through Cash and Voucher Assistance to vulnerable women and girls and GBV survivors as part of a multi-sectoral GBV response; 7) Implement community engagement and awareness raising activities on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures and on how survivors can access support and services in line with the “do no harm” principle (disaggregated by age) as part of addressing differential needs and GBV risk mitigation measure;8) Engage men and boys for accountable practices to transform harmful social norms that perpetuate gender inequality, and to promote the health and safety of women and girls, 9) SMA will continue to work together with partners and line department and ministries to conduct services mapping and carry out protection assessments on quarterly bases and; 10) Train staff, GBV partners and community volunteers on GBV case management, psychosocial support (PSS) and psychological first aid (PFA) to provide individualized focused and non-focused psychosocial support for survivors of GBV and other most vulnerable women.11) Facilitate social dialogue and community engagement to promote peace and challenge/address harmful social and cultural norms, practices and Behaviors such as child marriage, dowry systems, cattle raiding, abduction, age-sects, youth defense groups, etc.12) Organize focus groups discussion with different age groups aggregating it to gender sensitive. 5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Socially, women and girls in Awerial County IDPs have limited freedom of movement. Displacement due to conflcts and disasters and life in Marrik and Mingkaman camps can exacerbate this isolation. Estranged from family ties and social networks and confronted with concerns around camp safety, women are often unable to leave their homes. A study of UN Women (2019)’Oasis model showed that involvement in full-time cash for work, along with access to public spaces and opportunities to engage with peers (building social capital) led to a reported decrease in domestic violence for 70 percent of the beneficiaries and an increase in household decision-making power for 78 percent of them. Additionally, 98 percent of women beneficiaries also reported increased self-esteem and fewer feelings of isolation and depression. At the end of cash-for-work rotations, on average 16 percent of women were interested in starting a microbusiness and 80 percent were interested in continuing to work and seeking another job. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The Samaritan Mission Aid is committed to ensuring that the rights and entitlements of the communities that we serve are protected and met. All programs and humanitarian staff conduct is guided by the Human Rights Framework and Humanitarian Principles including the right to dignified, safe and respectful access to humanitarian assistance. One of the gravest violations of the human rights standards to protect, respect and fulfil is the sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) of beneficiaries by humanitarian actors. SMA holds a Zero Tolerance Policy towards SEA which applies to all SMA employees (regardless of contract type) and all personnel associated with the work of SMA. Therefore to provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse SMA adopted the following criteria’s 1) Awareness Raising of Beneficiaries: The organization will ensures that beneficiaries are aware of the organization’s zero-tolerance policy on SEA and how to report allegations; 2) Reporting: The organization will set up clear procedures for personnel and beneficiaries to report SEA allegations that comply with core standards for reporting (i.e. safety, confidentiality, transparency, accessibility). Assistance and Referrals: The organization will ensure that there is functioning system to ensure survivors of SEA allegedly perpetrated by their personnel receive immediate professional assistance, by providing them with direct services or referring them to relevant service providers
