Project Summary

The objective of the project is to strengthen the coordination of the humanitarian response in Cameroon to save lives, protect the most vulnerable and improve the resilience of populations. Since the worsening of the crisis in the North-West and South-West regions, Cameroon faces three complex humanitarian crises affecting eight of Cameroon's ten regions. The impact of these three crises on the living conditions of the affected populations is reinforced by pre-existing structural vulnerabilities that hamper their recovery, particularly in the Far North. The security situation in the Far North further deteriorated in 2021, due to the widespread insecurity in the Lake Chad region, with an increase in attacks by NSAGs, including suicide attacks and attacks using improvised explosive devices (IEDs). In terms of the wider Lake Chad Basin region, 2021 has recorded the highest monthly numbers of security incidents attributed to NSAGs since 2018. Between 1 January and 30 October 2021, the total of security incidents in the Lake Chad Basin attributed to NSAGs increased compared to the same period in 2020. Additionally, In the Far North, particularly in the Lake Chad Basin area, the economic context is marked by poverty, the lack of natural and financial resources and market opportunities, and a drastic reduction in agriculture, livestock production and tourist activities due to prevalent insecurity. Agricultural activity has come to a standstill in areas prone to repeated incursions by non-State armed groups (NSAGs). Meanwhile, the agro-pastoral economy is negatively impacted by the increasing depopulation of herders who transit livestock out of risk areas and the disruption of seasonal transhumance, as main border transit points with Nigeria are closed.
The complexity and the scope of these crises therefore require more effective coordination, adapted to the different intervention areas and based on humanitarian principles. OCHA Cameroon's coordination project will thus make it possible to achieve a humanitarian response that is better funded, more strategic and that centralizes human rights and the protection of people in the Far North. It will also enable a better complementarity between emergency actions and development programs, in order to fight together the humanitarian needs and their underlying causes. To this end, OCHA will provide the essential services of disseminating key information products, coordinating the Humanitarian Programming Cycle and facilitating joint multisectoral needs assessments. Its presence in Yaoundé, Maroua and Kousseri will enable it to provide decisive support to coordination mechanisms and promote coherence between operational issues in the field and strategic decision-making bodies. This key position will also enable it to advance the overriding considerations of the Centrality of Protection and Cross-cutting Protection, Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and nexus humanitarian development and peace. From its sub-offices in the Far North, OCHA will also support the scaling up of the response in these regions, including through the management of CMCoord and humanitarian access responsibilities, as well as capacity building of local responders.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Cameroon is the most affected country by the conflict in the Lake Chad Basin, after Nigeria. Violence in the Far North has led to a steady rise of displacement since 2014. As of October 2021, violence has uprooted 580,000 people (341,000 IDPs; 114,000 Nigerian refugees; and 124,000 returnees), an increase of 20,000 people since September 2020. 54 percent of the 114,000 Nigerian refugees are women or girls, 60 percent are children, 4.6 percent are over the age of 60 years, 24 percent are people with special needs. The percentage of children is even higher for the IDPs and returnees: 64 percent are children. The coordination needs are related to: • The definition of an overall coherence between the intersectoral coordination system in force at the national level and in the Far North; and • The adaptation of access and protection strategies. However, the need for civil-military coordination remains. There also remains a need to improve efforts related to the collection and analysis of data on the displacement, needs and vulnerabilities of affected people, in order to propose the most appropriate interventions possible. Finally, it is still essential to strengthen the links between the humanitarian and development spheres through harmonized programming and common coordination mechanisms. The growing trend towards the return of IDPs to the Far North has served as a reminder that strategic collaboration between the authorities, development partners and humanitarians will be essential to enable IDPs to make progress towards durable solutions.
