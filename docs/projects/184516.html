Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Somalia remains fragile due to a multiplicity of vulnerable drivers such as conflicts, disease outbreaks, drought, locust infestation, recurrent flooding, population displacements, and acute food insecurity. As attributed to lack of adequate nutrient intake and disease, undernutrition in children below five years, pregnant and lactating mothers is worrying. The recent post-Gu results of FSNAU revealed that the overall median Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in Somalia remained Serious (11.1% weight-for-height z-score). In addition, the 2020 Micronutrient Survey, indicated that children less than six months are only 20% were exclusively breastfed thus demanding immediate lifesaving curative and preventive intervention.
DEH aims to provide timely and equitable lifesaving management of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable under 5 years’ boys and girls, Pregnant and lactating women, and increased access to the maternal, infant, and young child nutrition knowledge to nurture behavior change in Galgadud and Mudug region. Targeting 21643 beneficiaries from ceel Buur,dhusamarreeb, Cadaado,Gaalcayo,Hobyo,Xarardheere districts with integrated Management of acute malnutrition and optimal maternal infant and young child nutrition intervention. Mothers and caregivers will be sensitized on early identification of malnutrition through Mother led MUAC approach and referral pathways for comprehensive management at the static and mobile nutrition facilities. Acute malnutrition will be assessed using MUAC and weight for height z-score. Depending on the triage outcome they will be enrolled in the relevant program as per Somalia IMAM guidelines. The under-five boys and girls, diagnosed with SAM without medical complications will be admitted to the OTP and given weekly rations of RUTF and routine medications.
Thereafter, all children discharged as cured will be referred to TSFP for moderate acute malnutrition treatment. SAM with complications will be referred for inpatient management to stabilization centers. TSFP supplies will be provided by the World Food Program (WFP) and RUTF will be provided by UNICEF subsequently. DEH aims to strengthen MIYCN knowledge and nurture optimal feeding practices for the first 1000 days and beyond. The desired impact will be fostered through community engagement, promotion of breastfeeding, information dissemination, and cooking demonstration. Individual and group counseling will be conducted at the baby tents by the trained MIYCN counselors.
In collaboration with the community leaders, mother support groups will be formulated and lead mothers selected. The lead mothers will be responsible for engaging support groups members in optimal feeding discussions and providing basic health and nutrition education in an interactive participatory manner.
DEH will encourage MTMSGs in the use of ecologically-appropriate technologies for sustainable vegetable production to improve on nutrient security and consumption of micronutrient-rich foods Through a participatory community approach, members of the MTMSG and Men advocates will be supported to establish a keyhole garden to cultivate micronutrient-rich vegetables either in their kitchen garden or small farms. Cooking demonstrations will also be conducted to enhance essential nutrient utilization and diversify food intake. Vitamin A and iron-folic supplements will also be given to children and pregnant and lactating women following IMAM guidelines.
DEH upholds gender equality and inclusion principles thus aligning all the interventions to gender principles. Staff will be trained on protection mainstreaming and vulnerable populations will benefit from safe, dignified, and equal opportunity during service delivery. Accountability will be ensured by establishing a feedback mechanism platform and staff training on core humanitarian standards and accountability.

1. - Projects activities

Management of acute malnutrition Children aged 6-59 months identified with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) without medical complications, will be provided with systematic therapeutic treatment in OTP. Children aged 6-59 months and PLWs will be referred with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) will be targeted for supplementary feeding.Screening and referral Through CNVs, the screening will be done weekly, and mass screening quarterly. Children 6-59 months with MUAC of 11.5 to 12.4 cm and/or weight for height z-scores =< -2 and > -3, and pregnant and lactating women with a MUAC less than 21.0 cm are categorized as MAM and referred to TSFP. Children over 6 months of age with MUAC of <11.5cm an/or bilateral pitting edema who have the appetite and are free from medical complications are categorized as severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and referred to directly to OTP. Referral mechanisms will be strengthened from one nutrition site to another depending either on the improvement or deterioration of children and pregnant and lactating women undergoing treatment.Community mobilization DEH will scale up the nutrition activities and strengthen community outreach services, community mobilization under CNVs and Capacity building Health Workers will be trained and be supervised to adhere to the MoH IMAM guidelines, further sharpening their skills and knowledge in the management of MAM cases. Caregivers and mothers will also be trained on Mother led MUAC screening to support in early identification of malnutrition.Defaulter tracing Follow-up forms will be formulated to support trace absentees, defaulters, and non-respondents cases at the household level and ensure mothers/caregivers return to the program and complete the treatment.Vitamin A supplementation Children aged 6-59 months seeking treatment at the nutrition sites will be supplemented with vitamin A following IMAM guidelines.Inter-linkages Children admitted to the OTP will be referred to the TSFP once they are cured of severe acute malnutrition. In cases where they develop medical complications while in the OTP they will be referred to inpatient care at partner’s stabilization centers. Once the medical complication is treated they will continue their nutritional rehabilitation as outpatients. DEH will map out partners that provide essential services that are interrelated with malnutrition and establish linkage e,g GBV, NFI, WASH, Immunization, etcMobile outreach DEH will roll out mobile OTP/TSFP sites in hard-to-reach areas and provide comprehensive treatment of acute malnutrition. Covid 19 sensitization People seeking services at DEH static and mobile sites will be sensitized on covid 19 prevention and containment measures. PPEs and handwashing stations will also be provided to beneficiaries and staff.Counseling Individual and group counseling will be conducted on women with specialized MIYCN identified needs and women with optimal feeding practices challengesEstablish Mother-to-mother support groups.The mother support group will be formulated with women of childbearing age, lactating women, pregnant women, grandmothers/caregivers. The members will also be referrals from TSFP and OTP programming and the community to provide nutrition support.Promotion of breastfeeding Community engagement in promotion and advocacy of breastfeeding through information dissemination and campaigns.Baby tent Baby Tent will provide beneficiaries tailor-made support to optimal infant feeding needs. Mothers and caregivers experiencing exclusive breastfeeding and complementary feeding and early child development challenges.Cooking DemonstrationCooking demonstrations will be conducted to improve knowledge on age-appropriate diet, food diversification, hygiene nutrient retention, and bioavailability. Innovative kitchen gardensDEH will nurture sustainability in micronutrient utilization through environmentally appropriate vegetable production technologies.
