Project Summary

The early recovery and livelihood activities are much needed for the communities in order to respond to the acute need for the communities and enhance their resilience especially in the areas of high severity scale. The areas which witnessed a high level of returnee movements are suffering from lack of basic services like water, electricity, schools and income generation activities given into account the high unemployment rates for the population.
ADRA Syria has selected the most vulnerable communities, in line with the ERL sector and OCHA guideline and will respond to the most urgent needs within the populations in early recovery in Rural Homs (Rural Al-Qusair) and Rural Aleppo ( Der Hafer, Tal-Ed-daman and Bnan) . These areas have been assessed and considered high severity where resources and services were limited. In addition, ADRA Syria is proposing livelihood intervention through small business grants targeting the most vulnerable communities who lost their assets during the Syrian Crisis and capacity building along with Cash for work for the youth in Rural Aleppo.
The project will be targeting a total of 45,000 beneficiaries including (12,600 Men, 12,1260 women, 9,900 boys and 9,900 girls) , the project will be also targeting 500 youth including ( 250 boys, 250 girls) through life skills training sessions and cash for work in order to empower youth and support the families in generating income.
The project will begin from coordination meetings with related stakeholders like local ministries ( MoSAL, MoLA, MoE), to receive necessary approvals and conduct needed technical and vulnerability assessments in the targeted locations. Coordination with SARC, ERL Sector, Protection sector, education sector and GBV subsector throughout the project implementation in order to coordinate the response and avoid any duplication of provided services to the targeted communities. ADRA Syria will ensure proper monitoring of the project activities through developed MEAL plan, monitoring tools and evaluation tools.
The project activities will be conducted through qualified ADRA female and male staff in close coordination with key stakeholders. In addition, for the livelihood activities ADRA will partner with SARC in order to deliver the needed assistant to the most vulnerable groups among the community including youth girls and boys, women and men, people with disabilities. This will enhance the mitigation and response to address the needs of boys, girls, people with disability and vulnerable communities.

4 - Needs

In 2021, the ERL sector estimates 11.6 million PiN 51% of them are women, 49% are men 45% children, and 26% persons living with disabilities. At the end of 2020, unemployment was estimated at 50% of the working-age population, and over 60% of youth. The major damage of the socio-economic infrastructure, estimated at 74% has led to deterioration of life among IDPs, host communities and returnees. ADRA conducted interviews with KIIs in Homs (Rural Qusair) which showed that there is an acute need for solar street lights as lack of it restricts people’s movements at night. WASH infrastructure needs a transformer installation in al-Ghassanieh. Public water is reaching the villages once each three days, while in Al-Rableh the elevated high-water tank needs rehabilitation. There is one school in need for rehabilitation and both areas are depending on agriculture, where most of people assets were lost during the crisis. The need for small business grants to support the families in restoring their livelihoods and rehabilitation of shops was identified. In Aleppo, KIIs in Bnan and Qashota highlighted the challenge of income generation activities and the need for small business grants. Tal-ElDaman area, is facing a major challenge with the water network. ADRA rehabilitated a vocational training center in Tal-ElDaman in 2020, and after consultations with the youth in the center, it was suggested to develop life-skills training and cash for work activity focusing on equipping damaged schools in the area with needed furniture. The need for solar street light installation was identified to encourage the returnee movement to their home of origin.

The data collected will disaggregated by gender, age and disability where possible to highlight the vulnerability among the assessed population.Data collection will be as used as per the context especially with the COVID-19 situation. ADRA will ensure the safety of the staff and project’s key stakeholders, therefore, ADRA will adapt the best data collection methods and where needed, ADRA will use remote monitoring for data collection.ADRA Syria has a qualified trained female and male staff, who will be responsible on the implementation of the project and will ensure to develop the capacity of the project’s staff on regular basis.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Risk: Social tension between the beneficiaries and community members: Project activities may expose the community to social tension. Mitigation: ADRA will start by developing a clear and transparent selection criterion in consultation with the communities, ERL Sector, relevant line ministries to start the rehabilitation of shops and the distribution of the small business grants.2. Risk: Excluding People with disabilities from the intervention. Mitigation: ADRA will ensure the BoQs include the needs of people with disability for access of the building and WASH facilities. ADRA will ensure that issues related to protection, safeguarding, Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV), people with disabilities, women’s and girl’s dignity are addressed throughout the project lifecycle. The project will be guided by IASC GBV gender guidelines and ADRA protection principles. ADRA ensures regular performing of gender analysis of the project activities to foster dignity, participation and empowerment, and will implement an integrated approach to mainstream gender and protection approach in the project life cycle. ADRA will also conduct staff training in gender and protection mainstreaming.ADRA has already established numerous protection measures for the livelihoods and basic services components of the project, including:- Staff use of PPE to prevent the spread of COVID-19- Training centers will be prepared with needed social distancing and the center will be equipped with the PPE materials. - Complain mechanism, where all beneficiaries will be introduced to ADRA complain mechanism.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A number of potential project risks were identified based on ADRA Syria experience in similar projects: - Delayed approvals from different stakeholders due to the variation of project’s activities : SARC, MoR, MoLAE, MoSAL. This may result to a delay in the implementation to access the project’s areas in a timely manner. - - COVID-19 continue to spread and impact on project’s staff and beneficiaries. - Devaluation of the Syrian Exchange rate- Procurement of supplied could be subject to corruption or conflict of interest. ADRA will closely monitor the health situation and the COVID-19 cases in the targeted locations in close coordination with the health sector and WHO and will ensure distribution of PPE to staff and beneficiaries in the training sessions. ADRA has a data protection policy that will be implemented to ensure the protection of sensitive beneficiary information.Staff will undertake all necessary training including PSEA, and ADRA Syria ensure that all staff are aware of the safeguarding reporting guideline within the organization. ADRA will ensure that all staff are aware and adhere to the ADRA Syria Procurement policy which includes the segregation of duties, level of authorities, and conflict of interest declaration to prevent fraud, corruption and conflict of interest.
