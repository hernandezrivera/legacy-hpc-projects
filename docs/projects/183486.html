Project Summary

The main purpose of this action is to improve and rehabilitate of WASH facilities and infrastructure at house hold level for PWDs and elderly people in the east of Gaza city- Al Shejaeia area.
This action will last for 8 months, and it will be according SPHERE and IASC’s minimum shelter standards to provide safe and dignified WASH facilities to the target HHs.
Considering a list of 250 HHs of the most vulnerable families, PWDs families and elderly people provided to YEC through MoSD and verified by the WASH CLUSTER , YEC in close coordination with the MoSD and WASH CLUSTER will provide WASH Facilities adaptation and rehabilitation to 130 most vulnerable HHs, PWDs HHs and elderly people, also they will benefit from the hygiene kits and awareness sessions.
The final BNFs will be selected considering a combination of protection, economic and shelter technical vulnerability criteria to be implemented by YEC project team.
Factors reflecting protection vulnerability include age, disability, families with high numbers of dependents, women-headed households and families suffering from un safe conditions including protection related problems while economic factors will include main income, source of income, depts, and the status of household assets. Factors reflecting WASH facilities vulnerability include gaps in core wash items based on the Shelter Cluster agreed Minimum Standards for housing in Gaza.
The adaptation and rehabilitation activities will be developed in consultation with the targeted families including women to ensure that their specific needs, including protection and privacy needs, are addressed. Targeted beneficiaries will sign MoUs prior to the initiation of rehabilitation works in their home, including a delivery of service note following the completion of activities.
The rehabilitation and adaption works will be premised on technical assessments to be carried out by civil engineers during HH visits. The adaptations may include expanding areas for toilets, installing railings and ramps, expanding doors to be suitable for wheelchair access, installing entrance ramps to housing units or WASH facilities, tiling and other specific relevant works, etc.
Also this action will support the most vulnerable families, PWDs families and elderly people at high risk to adopt adequate WASH practices, with the provision of hygiene kits and improve for applying the related WASH COVID-19 infection, prevention, and control by distribution of hygiene kits including essential personal hygiene items and household cleaning materials.
YEC will following up the bidding and awarding processes and ensuring the commitment of contracted companies to adhere to required procedures and quality standards.

1 - Needs

WASH vulnerabilities in the Gaza have been driven by the longstanding Israeli restrictions and closure, the internal Palestinian divide, the COVID-19 outbreak, and the May 2021 escalation along with the fiscal and socioeconomic consequences of these factors. In Gaza strip these previous factors led to the erosion of WASH coping mechanism by vulnerable households, alongside the adoption of negative practices, such as reducing water consumption and consuming water from unsafe sources. Many families in the Gaza strip rely on water collection in bottles and buckets from public fling points. This exacerbates their exposure to waterborne diseases and the risk of a waterborne epidemics affecting the wider population. Additionally, there are significant shortages of adequate sanitation facilities at the household level. Based on HNO 2022 and WASH cluster Vulnerability Map conducted in Aug 2020 coordination effort there is more than 10,000 households (4,000 in the West Bank and 6,000 in Gaza) are suffering limited access to adequate household sanitation facilities. They are adopting several coping mechanisms, including sharing latrines with other families and using shelter spaces, other than bathrooms, for handwashing or bathing. Such practices generate privacy and protection concerns, particularly for women and girls. There are longstanding challenges and critical shortages in the health system with chronically low stocks of essential drugs, contamination of drinking water and poor electricity coverage. This resulted in the overcrowding of existing facilities and the undermining of hygiene practices, raise especial concern within the context of the ongoing pandemic. In March 2021 (before May 2021 escalation) YEC conducted a HH vulnerability need assessment for 160 beneficiaries nominated from MoSD, the data shows that 152 out of 160 HH has at least one problem in access their house WASH facilities w which 136 has at least a critical problem in accessing to water supply and safe storage condition, 139 has at least a critical problem in accessing to functioning shower/toilet facilities and 60 has at least a critical problem in accessing to functioning kitchen facilities. In September 2021 YEC carried out rabid field assessment (RNA) for 30 HH nominated from Palestine Avenir For Childhood Foundation (PACF) in Al Shejaiya area in east Gaza. The assessment highlighted the need to improve access to adapted and safe water and sanitation services at HH level and the assessment found that the majority of population have unsafe and indignity access to WASH facilities, also poor hygiene practices especially in terms of water handling which increases the risk of COVID-19 infection. WASH needs were prioritized to ensure safe and adapted access to WASH facilities and infrastructure at house hold level for the most vulnerable HHs, PWDs and elderly people and support them adopt adequate WASH practices with the provision of hygiene kits and improve for applying the related WASH COVID-19 infection, prevention, and control by distribution of hygiene kits including essential personal hygiene items and household cleaning materials.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Protection principles are considered in the project as part of the humanitarian principles governing YEC’s humanitarian interventions. YEC premised its project design on the do no harm approach’ ensuring that project activities or any resulting consequences do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to the essential WASH needs which would have put them at risk, had it not been available, and by conducting ongoing monitoring and collecting beneficiaries’ feedback to be aware of any potential harm and work on prevention measures. This action’s response is grounded on the fact that the lives and dignity of all women and men beneficiaries including people with disabilities, youth, children and elderly, both direct and indirect, must be protected. Key humanitarian standards, safeguarding and protection principles will be given careful consideration especially the do-no-harm principle, particularly important in Gaza’s context, where social and political instability and conflict are evident in many areas with conservative gender norms. Local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement and ongoing consultation with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the project, thus enhancing community engagement and reducing any potential protection risks or exclusion at community level

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As part of the main principles of protection mainstreaming in all of YEC’s humanitarian work, special attention is paid to achieve equal and impartial access to aid. This will be done throughout the different stages of the project; the selection of targeted communities is premised on concrete and updated needs assessments confirming the vulnerability and the need of the intervention. Similarly, selecting the beneficiaries follows a clear process with selection criteria and presents equal opportunity for all eligible to benefit. Gender and age are also taken into consideration meaning that women, men, youth, elderly people, people with disabilities and children will be able to benefit from the intervention as fit for their needs without any discrimination based on their gender, age or status. Having a feedback mechanism available and accessible for all the targeted community will facilitate knowing people’s concerns and comments that could highlight obstacles of access whether physical or related to the processes of implementing the intervention and would inform response to ensure equality and impartiality.
