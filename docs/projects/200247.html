Project Summary

Lebanon's complex crisis (protracted Syrian refugee crisis, political instability, severe financial and fiscal crisis, Covid 19 pandemic, and Beirut port explosions) heavily impacted the health sector.
First, Lebanon’s unprecedented devaluation of the currency forced the Lebanese government to remove subsidies on most medications leaving people unable to access or afford vital and life-saving medication. Moreover, the global shortage and the central bank restrictions lead to further disturbance in the availability of free and low-cost medication at primary healthcare centers which are also facing an increase in demand. Thus WHO hopes to support with ensuring acute and chronic medication to support the most vulnerable.
Second, catastrophic medication remains subsidized minimally by the government, however with severe shortages of life-saving medicine /cancer medications and very few available. In this domain WHO hopes to support further the MOPH to be able to serve a larger number of patients.
Third, with protracted delays in government payments of its dues to hospitals and the severe shortage of medications and supplies in the Lebanese market, access to health care is threatened including lifesaving care. Hence, the role of WHO is to provide hospitalization in cases of an outbreak and life-saving for the most vulnerable populations living in Lebanon.
This project comes as a response to the humanitarian need with the aim to promote universal health coverage, equity, and access to health for all vulnerable in Lebanon (Lebanese, Migrants, and Palestinian), in line with the WHO commitment of saving lives and reducing suffering during times of crisis across the response pillars (coordination, communication, case management and continuation of essential health services).

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Under this intervention, WHO will be focusing on ensuring access to life-saving and life-sustaining health services for those most vulnerable and in need in the context of worsening socio-economic conditions – primarily focused on expanded coverage for in-patient hospital care for vulnerable Lebanese and migrantpatients, as well as supply of additional medicines for acute, chronic, mental, and catastrophichealth conditions to complement existing interventions and address increased caseload among Lebanese and migrant patients. The main activities are: - Increased access to life-saving in-hospital care by covering patient costs based on pre-defined social vulnerability and medical criteria via Third Party Administrators (TPA). - Continuous access to acute and chronic medications through the national PHC network, with direct bidding from WHO and storage at the MOPH warehouse (chronic medication distributed, and monitored via YMCA)- Development of NCD awareness-raising material and rationale for medication usage - Access to catastrophic medications and supplies, including oncology and hemodialysis, with storage and dispensing via the MOPH warehouse to hospitals. - Prevention of and response to sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment (PRSEAH) training - Provide enabling, cross-cutting support to coordinated, safe, timely, and quality health responseswith an emphasis on vulnerable communities.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Access to acute/chronic medications and inpatient admission is inclusive and no discrimination of age, gender or nationality. Pre-defined social vulnerability and medical eligibility criteria clearly communicated with access to help desk 24/7 promoted and non-eligible vulnerable population referred by TPA relevant available support. Staff working directly with WHO will receive training on the code of conduct and PSEA. WHO will commit to gender equity and equality in access to care.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

1-Patient satisfaction surveysInterviews are conducted on a periodic basis within the national PHC network, the MOPH warehouse, and with the patients receiving in-hospital financial support. Communication and communication materials are provided to vulnerable populations and relevant stakeholders to increase awareness about eligibility criteria and how to access available support. Beneficiaries consulted throughout the project via people participation groups. 2-Project indicators are closely monitoredReception and distribution of medications to PHC centers as well as dispensing of medications from the MOPH warehouse directly to patients closely monitored using the new Logistic Management System (LMS).HC centers have access to an electronic feedback form integrated within MOPH medical software (Phenics) on the shortage of medications, allowing monitoring of timely distribution and availability of medications in these centers. Periodic field monitoring visits will be conducted.
