Project Summary

This project aims to address the problems of Water, Sanitation and Hygiene for affected population in the prioritizes areas of interventions to provide the people with chlorines, filtering clothes, aqua tabs, construction of more latrines and increase WASH awareness campaign to reduce the main WASH threat drivers and educate them on importance of use of WASH items and taking into considerations the WASH key messages.
According to 2023 HNO, the situation is particularly concerning in Kapoeta North and Kapoeta South where more than 90 per cent of the population reportedly use rivers or puddles as their main water source.140 Untreated surface water and the widespread practice of open defecation create a substantial public health risk, especially during floods when latrine pits seep into the waterways. Limited access to water is also compounded by only 16 per cent of households reporting to having at least one jerrycan and/ or bucket to safely store water.
This project will contribute to WASH cluster objectives and strategizes.
The Climate shocks, including abnormal levels of flooding and drought, exacerbate the vulnerabilities of the South Sudanese people. From July to October 2022, more than 1 million people got affected by floods. Torrential rain and flooding affected 36 counties across South Sudan and the southern part of the Abyei Administrative Area. People in Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei, upper Nile and Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap and Unity were the worst affected. In addition, water submerged boreholes, schools and latrines, contaminating water sources and causing outbreaks of waterborne diseases and other illnesses. In flood affected areas, there has been an increase in cases of malaria, diarrhea, pneumonia, anemia and skin infection diseases. In areas with combined shocks from conflict and floods, such as, Eastern Equatoria, people’s already dire living conditions further deteriorated as insecurity and violence affected the delivery of urgent humanitarian assistance. According to UNICEF https://www.unicef.org/southsudan/what-we-do/climate-change-and-flooding ,
Over 9.4 million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2024, The projected 9.4 million people in need for 2024 include 1.9 million IDPs, 1.4 million returnees, 5.8 million host community/non-displaced people and 337,000 refugees. Among them, there are 2.2 million women and 4.9 million children, including 2.4 million girls and 2.5 million boys. Nearly 15 per cent of the total people in need are PWD. According to the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview 2023 (June 2023). As access to water sources is predominately communal in South Sudan, limited access to WASH services impacts all population groups. In particular, however, IDPs, returnees from internal displacement and refugee returnees are highly vulnerable and report limited access to WASH services. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, recurring communal violence and flooding in many parts of the country. These shocks have led to the continued displacement of people and restricted their access to already limited functional WASH infrastructure. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites and creates barriers for WASH partners when implementing activities.
The challenge of inadequate WASH services is pronounced in fragile and conflict-affected states, where instability and violence may hamper public and private efforts to build sustainable WASH systems. Maximizing the impact of WASH programme, especially in fragile and conflict-affected settings, will be critical towards achieving this SDG target as well as the other health-related targets. Therefore, South Sudan is one of the countries experiencing conflict, instability and flooding in some part of the country that make it difficult for WASH services and infrastructures to improve for sustainable WASH systems.

As per HNO in 2023, the standard of living and well-being of an estimated 6.1 million people will be affected as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. The situation is trigger by consistence flooding and subsequence rainfalls in the mentioned areas of Kapoeta North and Kapoeta South, WASH facilities are washed off by either flood or torrential rainfall, most road are not functions, this cause hard transportations of WASH supplies to the affected people and will lead to rampant re-settlement of the communities and contribute to cause the dense over-populated towns that shall posted threat on hygiene and sanitation, pertaining this, this project shall provide WASH items, the like of soaps, aqua tabs, slabs, filtering clothes, chlorines, digging of latrines, increase awareness campaigns amongst others. South Sudan has continued to experience worse humanitarian disasters since its independency, brought about by the following factors: • Climatic shocks - Floods and droughts, led to displacements, destructions of health, WASH and educational facilities, destruction of homestead, wash off of WASH facilities, wash off of roads, loss of crops and live stocks and increased prone diseases of human and livestock. • Sub-national and inter - communal conflicts, coupled with widespread insecurity, affected livelihoods and loss of productive assets as well as reduction in ownership of livestock and other properties. • Macroeconomic crisis, causing continuous depreciation of the South Sudan Pound and corresponding skyrocketing of prices and thereby eroding the purchasing power of vulnerable households who rely on markets to purchase food and other basic needs. 6.1 million, 62% male, 41% female,15% children, 15% people with disability, will be affected as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation people are anticipated to be in high needs of WASH assistance, this is according to HNO and WASH cluster Persons in Need (PiN) 2023, these number is projected to be negatively impacted in 2023 due to inadequate or no access to clean drinking water, distant concern from the people, insecurity issues, lack of awareness in some areas and less practices of using water treated drugs.

2. Project activities

Provision of or improving access to clean drinking water, improved sanitation and hygiene facilities as well as NFIs WASH items: Following activities will be undertaken to address the identified needs, while remaining alert to any emerging needs: 1. Monthly community meetings at the areas of interventions. 2. Training of beneficiaries on good sanitation and hygiene practices. 3. Local rehabilitation of water boreholes. 4. Renovation of Sanitation and Hygiene infrastructures. 5. Provision of WASH facilities to include Soap, buckets, jerricans and other hand washing facilities 6. Support community resilience efforts through provision of latrine digging tools, slabs fore households to construct them with flood resilient design. 7. Monitoring and supportive supervision to the project sites by the technical project staff in areas of project implementations. 8. Placement of posters carrying key messages at the water points to keeps in alert for the WASH threats. 9. Establishing complaint feedback mechanism. 10. Providing beneficiaries with aqua tabs and other water treating drugs to enhance safe drinking water.
