Project Summary

This project will provide emergency nutrition interventions in Abiemnhom, Mayom, Pariang, and Twic East for populations affected by drivers of the protracted humanitarian crisis including political and communal violence, climate-induced shocks including floods, disease outbreak and economic hardships. The project is targeting IDPs, returnees, and Host communities affected by conflict, flood, disease outbreak and displacement in areas where CARE implements nutrition interventions. The project is targeting 21,522 children with SAM (male – 10,546 & female – 10,976), 35,908 children with MAM (Male – 17595 & Female – 18, 313), 26,968 PLW with MAM, 19,178 children with BSFP, 15,981 PLW with BSFP, 20,532 PLW & 41,064 children (0-23 months) with MIYCN, 87,261 children (6-59 months) with VASD, and 71,862 children (12-24 months) with deworming in Abiemnhom, Mayom, Pariang, and Twic East. During the emergency nutrition intervention, 2,441 and 3,857 children with SAM will be Returnees and IDPs respectively; 6,370 & 4,579 children with MAM will be Returnees and IDPs respectively; 4867 & 3138 PLW with MAM will be Returnees and IDPs respectively.
CARE will work with various stakeholders like SMOH, CHD, National Non-Governmental Organisations (NGOs), and other partners to ensure the collaboration and participation of the affected populations in project design and implementation. CARE will integrate the nutrition interventions with other clusters such as WASH, gender & protection, health, et al to ensure holistic comprehensiveness of the nutrition services.
CARE will recruit CHD staff in the nutrition facilities to be paid through harmonized incentive scale to ensure that transition of the implementation from partners to government as per the MOH guidance. The incentivized staff will be capacity-built and mentored by CARE to ensure the quality of the services.
CARE is working with community to identify locally available nutritious food stuffs which can be used to reduce the prevalence of wasting through scaling up their production and proper preparation. The use of locally available food stuffs will encourage the level of acceptance from the community.
CARE International is well known organization for its high-quality Nutrition Programmes in South Sudan. In 2023, CARE reached over 265,201 children under the age of 5 and Pregnant and lactating women/Girls with Nutrition Services, as well as 314,368 with health services across the 4 counties within the state. CARE is also a member of the Health and Nutrition Clusters, Strategic Advisory Groups (SAG) for both Clusters as well as CMAM TWG. This project will provide Integrated, lifesaving, emergency nutrition interventions for children under the age of 5, pregnant and lactating women (PLW), elderly men and women. Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and Returnees will be of special interest to this project because of their high levels of vulnerability. To do this, CARE will implement all the components of CMAM as follows:
1) Community mobilization.
2) Outpatient Therapeutic Programme for management of SAM without medical complications (OTP).
3) Outpatient Therapeutic Programme for management of MAM (SFP)
4) In-patient care for management SAM with medical complications (SC/ITP)

1. Needs

Using the FSNMS round 29, HNO, and the SMART Survey results, it is estimated that a total of 2,522,320 people will be suffer from of acute malnutrition in 2024 in South Sudan with national Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels remain above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%, of which 46 out of 79 counties, have GAM rates above 15%. 4 out of 46 counties with GAM rate above 15% are where CARE works. These include a total of 1,651,851 children under the age of 5 years (842,444 girls and 809,407) of which 484,502 will have SAM (247,096 girls and 237,406 boys), 1,167,394 (595,371 girls and 572,023 boys) will have MAM, and 870,469 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) will suffer from acute malnutrition. Unfortunately, more children and pregnant and lactating women will suffer from acute malnutrition in 2024 compared to 2023 (an increase of 346,774). In the counties where CARE works, the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates have been above the WHO threshold of 15% with Abiemnhom at 31.9%, Pariang at 18.1% (GAM-WHZ), Twic East at 23.6%, and Mayom at 29.8%. The key drivers for the high malnutrition levels include climatic shocks of flooding, conflict & insecurity (sub-national and communal violence), low production due to constant population displacements, and high burden of diseases, and disease outbreaks as well as the economic crisis. The 4 counties of Mayom, Abiemnhom, Pariang, and Twic East with a combined population of 1,039,272 (Nutrition Cluster PIN targets for 2024), have one of the highest malnutrition burdens in the Country with (361,638 in the host community, 336,116 returnees and 341,518 IDPs). The 4 counties have a SAM burden of 25,750, MAM Burden of 49,033 and PLW burden of 36,177. The burden for Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition within the 4 counties is 61,596 and for Vitamin A Supplementation is 87,261 and for Deworming is 71,862 . For children between 6-23 months and PLWs, the BSFP burden is 55,935. The SAM, MAM and PLW burden among the Internally displaced persons in the 4 Counties is as follows: Pariang (SAM 982; MAM 1,842 and PLW 1,350), Mayom (SAM 2,379; MAM 4,701 and PLW 3,073), Abiemnom (SAM 756; MAM 953 AND PLW 865) and Twic East ( SAM 481; MAM 1,123 and PLW 1,165). The ongoing flooding that has affected much of the country and the 4 counties where CARE implements its nutrition programmes has had a devastating impact on the Nutrition Programming, destroyed crops, cut off communities from accessing Health and Nutrition facilities, markets and other vital commodities and led to deaths of livestock which are the lifeline of the population in the affected counties. This is expected to lead to severe food insecurity and the attendant of malnutrition among children under the age of 5 and pregnant and lactating women in 2024. Moreover, flooding continues to impair access to health and nutrition services in Twic East, and parts of Pariang. Roads have been flooded or destroyed and most of the affected facilities can only be accessed by either speed boats or canoes, therefore making transportation difficult and expensive. The flooding and the associated poor access to Health and Nutrition services will further impact negatively on the Status of malnutrition across the 4 Counties where CARE works. The Economic crisis, currency devaluation and reduced market access and functionality are also expected to play a detrimental role in the deterioration of the food security situation in 2024 . Incidents of insecurity related to inter-communal violence and cattle raiding within the 4 counties are likely to be experienced during this period, further contributing to the projected severe acute food insecurity and the associated malnutrition.

2. Project activities

To respond to the above needs and in line with the 2024 Nutrition sector strategy and objectives, CARE will support the provision of Emergency Nutrition interventions in a total of 36 Static Nutrition Sites and 7 Mobile Facilities (Pariang 15 Static; Mayom 16 static, Twic Easy 4 mobile Sites, and Abiemnom 6 Static Sites). CARE will employ the mobile strategy when the needs arise to ensure access to populations displaced by either floods or conflict, so the number of mobile Nutrition facilities may increase based on the needs and wasting trends through the year of implementation. Specifically, in accordance with the 2024 Nutrition Cluster Objectives, CARE will carry out the following activities. 1. Sectoral Objective 1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific services for children, adolescents, and women in Twic East, Pariang, Mayom, and Abiemnom by the end of 2024.i. Support Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme (BSFP) for girls and boys between 6-23 months.ii. Provide counselling services for primary caregivers of children 0-23 months.iii. Support Vitamin A and deworming campaigns in the 5 counties. 2. Sectoral Objective 2: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under-five-year of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition Twic East, Mayom, Abiemnom, and Pariang by the end of 2024.i. Screen children 6-59 months for Acute Malnutrition. ii. Admit and treat children between 6-59 months diagnosed with SAM.iii. Admit and treat children between 6-59 months diagnosed with MAM. iv. Screen Pregnant and Lactating women for Acute Malnutrition. v. Admit and treat PLW with MAM.vi. Conduct programmatic visits to nutrition sites (nutrition manager/coordinator from Country Office).vii. Provide on job training to health and nutrition staff. viii. Provide CNVs with training and tools to enable them report on a regular basis. ix. Support Kitchen gardens at the Nutrition Sites in the 4 counties. x. Support Kitchen gardens at the household level in the 4 counties. xi. Support Family MUAC data reporting in the 5 counties. xii. Support the establishment of a feedback mechanism in the 4 counties. 3. Sectoral Objective 3: To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information in Twic East, Mayom, Pariang, Abiemnom and Koch by the end of 2024. i. Conduct Nutrition Surveys in at least 3 of the 5 counties. ii. Conduct MIYCN barrier analysis in at least 3 of the 5 counties 4. Sectoral Objective 4: To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and integrated programming targeting vulnerable groups in Twic East, Mayom, Abiemnom, Pariang and Koch by the end of 2024. i. Ensure that all OTP/TSFP facilities are performing ECD services. ii. Ensure that cooking demonstrations are conducted at each of the OTP/TSFP locations.iii. Train Health Workers on Gender/GBV including safe referrals for survivors. iv. Provision of WASH kits to caretakers of children diagnosed with SAM/MAM.v. Provide malaria diagnosis using RDT and treatment using ACT for children admitted with SAM/MAM. vi. Provide LLIN for malaria prevention for children admitted with SAM/MAM. vii. Provide Aquatabs to Households within the 5 counties. viii. Provide sensitization sessions to communities on hygiene and sanitation. ix. Conduct at least 1 GBV safety audit at the nutrition sites within the 5 counties.x. Provide Referral services to GBV survivors.
