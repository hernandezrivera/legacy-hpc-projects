Project Summary

The project aims to ensure non-formal education for Rohingya refugees children, adolescent and youth in a safe and equitable access environment. Education will be provided to the children of 3-14 years aged at LCs based on Myanmar Curriculum. The children already enrolled and will continue their education in 2023. Education provided for Rohingya Adolescent and Youths of aged 15-24, focusing on Community Engagement, Women Empowerment and Learning Dynamics. This project envisages consequences that will provide ideal access, ensure quality education to children, adolescent and youths, increase community engagement, strengthening teacher capacity and inclusive learning opportunities and facilities.
Pre-primary Education (KG) for the children of aged 3-5 years focusing on Early Childhood Care and Development (ECCD). Through interactive & joyful activities, playing, audio-visual session children prepare themselves for primary school.
Primary and Secondary education provides to the children of aged 6 to 10 in the primary level either Grade- I or Grade- II, based on competency assessment, done in September 2022 and Secondary Education will be provided to the children of 11+ aged. Community-based approach will be promoted and Radio, Tablet and IT based learning will be used as the better alternatives for the hard to reach children. The education facilities will be inclusive and sensitive as the children with disabilities and girls have easy access.
Access to information, education, and learning for adolescents and youth aged 15-14, through conducting numeracy, literacy, and life skills education sessions to equip adolescents and youths with competencies to function effectively in day to day lives. Established community library, equipped with an Ideas Box and reinforce 4 outreach teams with Ideas Cubes, provides learning and cultural resources to meet and engage in activities together in a safe space. The teams will conduct centre and outreach sessions to improve access to information and learning for extremely vulnerable or isolated people, particularly for adolescent girls, adult women, persons with disabilities and gender divers population. Indoor and outdoor games, cultural activities included with the process. Community Education Watch Committee, Parents Centre Management Committee will be equipped to support the education activities planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Daily routine, lesson plan, use of workbooks, learning materials and learning outcomes will be assessed as per the Education Sector guideline and procedure.
Recreational and cultural activities will be organized regularly and celebration of relevant national and international day like- Child Rights week/day, Girls Child Day, World Environment Day, will be held colorfully with the participation of children, parents, leaders, representatives from Government authorities and other actors.
CB follows Protection Mainstreaming Framework, Education and CP standard (CPMS- 23) and tip-sheets developed by the Protection Sector- Safety and Dignity, Do No Harm, Meaningful Access, Accountability, Participation and Empowerment. CB’s national policy for PSEA, is adapted and complaints will be collected through the several entry points and are directly processed by the dedicated PSEA focal point. Effective complain and feedback mechanism is in place to ensure AAP through setting Opinion & Complaint box, dedicated Hotline and e-mail account. These are processed, and regularly analysed by the Safeguarding committee and M&E team.
Better responding to improve the environment and activities related to DRR including monsoon preparedness CB has been working with sector and the PERU team. Capacity building of volunteers is a regular activities of CB aligned with the Protection Sector.
CB closely works with Government, Sector and regularly submits 5Ws and Sitrep and it participates in Coordination meeting at Cox’s Bazar and camp level and work with Youth WG.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Currently 945,953 Rohingya people have taken refuge in Bangladesh, of which 52% of them are children (GoB-UNHCR Population Factsheet, as on 30 September 2022). The statutory rights to education are preserved in many declarations and treaties that the host countries are obliged to follow. The UN's Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) 4: Quality Education and 5: Gender Equality, also emphasize Rohingya children’s rights to education.According to the JRP 2023 document 100% children of 3-14 age group is targeted as PIN and mentioned that, there is a demand of basic literacy, numeracy, life skills, pre-vocational skills for the youth and adolescent, particularly to those who never been to the education opportunities. Women and girls need to give emphases as boy’s enrolment shown 2.5 times higher than girls of 15-24 age group ((JMSN 2021). JSMNA 2021 indicating that the girls aged 15-18 may have been most likely to have dropped out of their education as a result of learning facility closures. Girls, marriage and/or pregnancy was the second most commonly reported barrier towards the most commonly reported reason for not sending them back. Unicef Monitoring report says, out of 10,778 children enrolled in MCP, only 1770 were girls. Out of 227 teachers, only 28 were female and out of 18 master trainers, only 1 was female. After LC re opened, almost 5,000+ girls did not return in LCs. Adolescents and young people between the ages of 15 and 24 are most at risk due to their stage of development and the reality is that- only 3% of 15-24 age group are engaged in any type of educational programing. Gender disparities also exist in the provision of learning opportunities in the camps, especially those who are enjoying limited access to schooling and recreational activities. Because of deep-seated social roles, they are also assigned less responsibilities among families (Source: UNHCR). The access to and delivery of education has been improved and expanded in camps considerably for children, but remain difficult to meet the needs of adolescents and youth. This shortcoming is the result of two main factors: restrictions placed on formal education, and a lack of equivalent education, like in Myanmar Curriculum. Children and youth in emergency situations are exposed to numerous risks, such as the safety of the space and access to resources. It is simply not enough to delivering education directly to children and youth in emergency conditions; rather, it is imperative to implement an approach such as multicultural education which is invented here and able to offer specialized contents and modes of delivery. Finally, Myanmar Curriculum has been scaling-up and continuing education through it. This transition is expected to improve the acceptance and quality of education in camps. LC closures and the challenges of remote learning process during the pandemic period reduced education access and achievement. Back to education and continuation is a crucial factor now a day.In 2023, the Education Sector will work to ensure that Rohingya refugee children and youth from ages 3 to 24 have access to equitable quality education opportunities through the continuation of Myanmar curriculum and progressive learning in a safe and protective environment. The Ideas box and the Ideas Cube constitute a key innovation for this project. Social emotional learning can mitigate risks and enhance efficacy of educational programs, demonstrating significant positive effects on academic outcomes, school retention and attendance rates, lower stress and anxiety, reduce negative student behavior like bullying, violence, SEA and juvenile crime, and improve health outcomes such as reduced drug use. Realizing the situation and necessity CB intends to continue it education program for Rohingya children, adolescent and youth in 2023.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Caritas Bangladesh’s protection program is built on an community power structures and cultural norms, and aim to strengthen existing community capacities whilst ensuring access for and inclusion of the most vulnerable and marginalized groups like women, girls, boys, persons with disabilities, elderly persons. Learning facilities are gender sensetive, in safe location, free from dark footpaths & risky slopes. In collaboration with protection team CB comply with protection principles through consultation with community people, Identify the threats, vulnerabilities and assesses the community’s capacities and mechanisms to cope with those protection risks. Ensure meaningful participation of all the diverse groups, in the committees, meetings, consultations. Regular monitoring and evaluation conducted whether Protection mainstreaming is taking place and how it influnce the affected populations in terms of safety, dignity, meaningful access, accountability, participation and empowerment.

3.2 - Gender

CB follows comprehensive Gender Policy in operation of humanitarian responses and gender is mainstreamed here. In the proposed project, it will continue services to ensure female participation in all committees and decision making process, trained equally both female and male staff, teachers, gender segregated WASH facilities including MHM and increase female teachers. Deploy female teachers and volunteers for girls only sessions, specially for adolescent girls and young adult women and adapting servicess culturally responsive and following gender principals. All project staff will be trained and equipped on gender sensitive programming. The program will also emphasize to enhance girls friendly and gender-equal environment in the learning facilities. Proper monitoring and early warning mechanism will establish and aligned with CFRM and MEAL structure. CB will also organize community consultation to remove the cultural barriers and involve men to improve the gender discriminations.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

CB follows a comprehensive Child and Vulnerable Adult Safeguarding Policy to this humanitarian context. CB has Safeguarding Committee and one dedicated Safeguarding Focal in all offices, including Cox’s Bazar office. Staff, volunteers are trained and Safeguarding, Code of Conduct, PSEA orientation organize each year for all. Staff and volunteers must sign a safeguarding, Code of Conduct and self-declaration form during joining and reference checked before offering job. Beneficiaries are oriented on safeguarding policy through providing orientation before any service delivery, distribution. Agenda on safeguarding keeps in all meeting, training and workshops. Bengali version of Safeguarding policy and one pager distributed among the staff and host volunteers. All visitors, advisors and vendors read and sign the safeguarding form before visiting the camps. All above discussion and obligation are applicable for the staff, teachers and volunteers under the proposed project.

3.4 - Diversity

Children, adolescents and youth both male and female including persons with disabilities and other diverse groups, receive effective remedial learning and comprehensive services to help recover learning losses and improve overall welfare. Students with disabilities are systematically targeted and their inclusivity is consistently mainstreamed in the program. There are reported difficulties in identifying students with disabilities due to lack of adapted tools but will be resolved through WGSQ. Individual Education Plan will be taken for children with disabilities for promoting inclusive education in learning centres. The project ensured inclusion of children, adolescent and youths from diverse group of population in the education services, will make sure that the facilities are accessible to all and include diverse group’s representative in the committes. CB will continue it’s collaboration with focused specialized actors working at camps.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Systemic MEAL mechanism ensures AAP principles, CFRM and stakeholder consultation, and periodic meetings with stakeholders are designed in the project towards improving the quality of education, learning and adoption. CB aims at capacitating staff and volunteers to facilitate cultural programming with a perspective of sustainability and potential replication and to promote empowerment of community. CB conducts regular community consultations in camps to identify priority issues and to obtain feedback in order to adapt protection programming. CB prioritises two-way communication through a variety of channels including mail, phone, box and in person mechanisms. A fundamental part of CB’s work on AAP includes its commitment to the Code of Conduct, Safeguarding and a Zero-Tolerance Policy for Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Harassment. Training and awareness is cascaded to staff and community members on identifying and safe reporting of SEA.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Emergency preparedness and response activities are in place in the proposed project. CB will continue to providing services so that each learning facilities has DRR contingency plan and continue following plan accordingly with community people. Capacity building of teachers, Community Volunteers, LCMC members, community stakeholders, and their active engagement in PERU, DRR programme management is the crucial part of the proposed project to prevent and metigate the crisis. Their knowledge and skills development in the decision-making process and implementation ultimately help the community to be sustainable and resilient. Learning facility-based EPR plan will be developed in 2023.
