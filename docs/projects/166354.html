Project Summary

The project constitutes an addendum to HNGA20-ERY-161715-1. The addendum has been developed to account for the emerging needs under COVID-19 in north-east Nigeria, in terms of early recovery and livelihood interventions. As of 8 May, a total of 3,526 cases and 107 fatalities have been registered in Nigeria, according to the latest data from the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC), with 150 cases in the BAY (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe) states. The numbers are expected to increase in the coming weeks.
IOM will support the restoration and maintenance of essential lifesaving health services through rehabilitation/equipping of primary health facilities. In addition, IOM will equip public facilities with COVID-19 prevention materials and supplies. IOM aims to support vocational training capacity to manufacture cost effective beds (from local material) and protective equipment (PPE) for nurses, doctors and first line responders.
In addition, IOM will provide immediate livelihood support to strengthen social safety nets for poor households and vulnerable groups affected by the pandemic. This will include the provision of telecommuting livelihoods kits for vocational training center beneficiaries whose skills and technical training programs are disrupted due to a closure of training centers. Lastly, IOM aims to strengthen local conflict early warning and conflict resolution structures with COVID-19 messaging to reduce the spread of the disease, combat stigma, detect community tensions, and resolve potential local disputes.

1 - Needs

As of 8 May, a total of 3,526 cases and 107 fatalities have been registered in Nigeria, according to the latest data from the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC). Borno State is now the fourth worst affected state with 125 cases and 14 deaths. Cases have been also been registered in Adamawa (15 cases/0 deaths) and Yobe (13 cases/1 death) states. The number of COVID-19 cases is expected to increase in the coming days and weeks, largely due to increased testing capacity and worsening community spread. IDPs, refugees and host communities are vulnerable, especially those living in conflict-affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe, which account for the highest number of displaced populations. The impact of an outbreak of COVID-19 on livelihoods in the North-East will be devastating to people’s livelihoods and employment. Initial estimates by the World Food Programme Nigeria indicated that the COVID-19 outbreak in the BAY states will impact the economic livelihoods of 7 million people resulting in an increase in the number of food insecure individuals by 3.4 million. Given the largely urban and peri-urban sources of livelihood in Borno, it is expected to be hit the hardest with 62 percent of its population, or 3.6 million people, adversely impacted by a potential outbreak. Depending on the trajectory of the disease and the ability of humanitarian actors to continue delivery and support to preventive and resilience measures, these numbers may rise even more (UNDP Nigeria: THE COVID-19 PANDEMIC IN NIGERIA, April 3, 2020).

2 - Activities

COVID-19 response SO1: Contain the spread of the COVID-19 epidemic and decrease morbidity and mortality. Activity 1: Support the restoration and maintenance of essential lifesaving health services such as rehabilitation/equipping of primary health facilities, extend water points in order to maintain supply chain and address the impact of the disease on most vulnerable people in affected host communities. Activity 2: Identify and equip public facilities and WASH and other COVD-19 prevention materials and supplies e.g. schools, VTCs, public markets, clinics, PHCs. Activity 3: Support the manufacturing / procurement of hospital equipment - build vocational training capacity to manufacture cost effective beds (from local material) and protective equipment (PPE) for nurses, doctors and first line responders to COVID -19. SO7: Decrease the deterioration of human assets and rights, social cohesion, food security and livelihoods Activity 1: Provide immediate livelihood support (e.g. cash transfers initiatives and agricultural inputs and tools) to support social safety nets for poor households and vulnerable groups affected by COVID-19 pandemic. Activity 2: Provide telecommuting livelihoods kits for vocational training center beneficiaries whose skills and technical training programs are likely to be disrupted due to closure of training centers, e.g. sewing machines, carpentry equipment, etc., to enable trainees to pursue localized livelihoods activities. Activity 3: Conduct awareness-raising of COVID-19 preventions measures and stigmatization of aid-workers/xenophobia. Activity 4: Strengthen local conflict early warning and conflict resolution structures with COVID-19 messaging in order to reduce the spread of the disease, combat stigma, detect community tensions and jointly resolve potential local disputes.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM will ensure accountability to the affected population and most vulnerable community members throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and get feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, and persons with disabilities, among others. This will ensure, for example, that the physical abilities of the elderly and people with disabilities are factored into any forward planning in terms of accessibility to services provided. IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, most notably through the complaints and feedback mechanism team and tools.. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaints and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the IOM Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. After the project has been completed, IOM will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure further accountability to affected populations and promote learning.
