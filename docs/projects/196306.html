Project Summary

SRD project will address the urgent health needs of both IDP and residents in NWS, through the ensuring continuity of its ongoing projects. Aiming to enhance the quality of medical services through the provision of technical support via capacity building, equipment, medications, supplies and operational costs (i.e. salaries, fuel, etc.) within 14 primary health care facilities, 5 maternity and childhood hospitals, 1 trauma hospitals, 6 RH MCs, 8 MMUs, 1 physical rehab center, 1 TB Center, 26 Ambulances and the provision of immunization services. Another component of this project is health system strengthening especially in light of COVID-19 through the existing referral system including the COVID-19 referral system and point of entry COVID-19 triage system. In addition to the implementation of IPC program within health facilities and for COVID-19 and triage protocols, noncommunicable disease (NCD) management and pharmacy management. The project will ensure and improve health access for the elderly and persons with disabilities (PWDs) through modified accommodations and mechanisms.
SRD maintains a strong working relationship with local health authorities, humanitarian actors in the area in addition to close collaboration with OCHA, WHO, Health Cluster, TWGs. SRD proposed project will complement the health services provided by operating partners within the referral system, NCD, IPC and mental health integration activities.

3 - Needs

As health needs continue to rise in Syria with over 12.23 million people in need for health services in 2022 according (HNO 2022) including 1.33 million children under 5 years, 3.38 million women of reproductive age (15 – 49 years), 300,000 older persons (age 60 and above), 4.4 million displaced persons –including refugees, IDPs and returnees, 3.5 million persons living with disabilities, and those with chronic disease. The health sector severity scale indicates that areas of catastrophic need are continue to be located in just 5 governorates in northwest and northeast Syria. As of August 2021, the Camp Coordination Camp Management (CCCM) Cluster has tracked 1.7 million displaced persons, with women and children representing the majority of these IDPs (48.5 and 23.65%, respectively) with many of these displaced to temporary settlements and camps in the sub-districts of Dana, Afrin, Salqin, Al Bab, and Idlib. the need assessment results conducted by SRD in Oct 2021 are inline with health sector projected needs reported in HNO 2022 including essential inclusive health services – such as sexual and reproductive health and safe delivery, child health; care for communicable and non-communicable diseases; mental health and psychosocial support; emergency services – including trauma and referrals; and physical rehabilitation.

4 - Implementation of activities

SRD activities contribute to HRP 22-23 and health sector objectives and logframe by following:Provide essential primary and secondary health services, including trauma care.Provide comprehensive RH services and integrated nutrition services through RRTs. Provide routine immunization through static and mobile teams. Provide case management for COVID-19 and implementation of triage system in all HFs.Provide HFs with essential medical supplies, including PPE and report on violence against health care. Moreover, Strengthen the capacity of health systems through Implementation of NCD integration into PHCs, IPC program, mhGAP integration and other health topics for health care workers.Increase access to quality health services by establishing functional health facilities and mobile medical units and supporting referral through provision of COVID-19 referral and implementation of mitigation measures at Point of Entries, Support 26 ambulance cars and 40 non-Emergency patient transportation within referral system.SRD was a part of the technical team (TT) that revisited the EHSP and currently SRD shifted its PHC services delivery in accordance to TT recommendation to the platform model to regroup all primary care delivery channels according to their logistic requirements as communities, PHC clinics and hospitals to enhance the quality of health service delivery as well through implementation of WHO/UNFPA quality protocols and standard packages at HFs.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: The displaced population, especially IDP women and girls might facechallenges in accessing SRH/GBV services due to multiple barriers on where/why toaccess.Mitigation 1: Mobilization activities to share information with the communityfocusing on IDP women and girls about SRH/GBV services at PHCs, MCs and hospital.Protection Risk 2: Referrals to/from other services might be disrupted due to the securitysituation. Mitigation 2: Regular coordination and updating service portfolio of availablehealth services provider in the area of operation.Protection Risk 3: Partners’ staff workingin highly insecure environments might not be able to access counselling services.Mitigation 3: Self-care training is available for SRD staff in Gaziantep but currently notinside Syria. SRD organizes social self-care days for their staff.Protection Risk 4:Adolescents with disabilities might face challenges in accessing the clinics/hospital toattend girls’ club sessions.Mitigation 4: Designated vehicles will be provided to facilitateaccess of adolescents with disabilities to the clinics and hospitals. Duty of Care should betaken into consideration to provide for the physical and mental safety, security, health, andwell-being of its staff. In turn, staff members have a “Duty of Responsibility” to take careof themselves, to follow organizational policies, and to utilize the services available.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SRD has a zero-tolerance policy toward sexual exploitation, abuse, and child abuse. SRD adhere to and implement the PSEA core principles of assistance beneficiaries that form the basis for the protection of women, children or others from sexual exploitation and abuse when providing services. SRD has a duty of care to aid beneficiaries and a responsibility to treat them with dignity and respect.Once a case has been confirmed, it undergoes a rigorous investigation to determine if policies have been violated and ensure that appropriate mitigation measures and disciplinary action is taken in immediate dismissal as per the policies including Code of Conduct, SEAH policies, HR Disciplinary Measures, Whistle-blower Policies, Accountability through receiving and reporting complaints.SRD considers any complaint as a high priority and takes the following steps: - An independent investigation committee is formed under the direction of the Country Director or HQ if related to concerns at the senior level of the country office. - The employee(s) in question are immediately suspended until the investigation concludes and the findings and resolution(s) are disclosed to all parties involved. - The committee manages the investigation with the utmost confidentiality and propriety throughout and following the entire process - In complex cases and comprehensive investigations, HR consults with SRD’s legal counsel (external) who are called upon on a case-by-case basis, including cases of PSEA.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The safety and security of the staff, programs and beneficiaries are of utmost importance of SRD which has a duty of care policy with projects operations.-COVID-19 pandemic affects staff and projects. Likelihood: moderate, Impact: high, Mitigation: Staff are trained on IPC COVID. all project staff will be provided with PPE. Active triage system and implementation of IPC COVID measures for all beneficiaries, staff, contractors, workers, and labourers.-AIR STRIKE - IED/VBIED: Staff directly or indirectly impacted by the airstrikes and IED/VBIED attacks. Likelihood: Likely Impact: Severe. Mitigations: Avoid potential targets. Using routes used by people all the time. On-going threat monitoring/SRA. Hiring locals who are familiar with the situation. Medical Evacuation arrangements. Facilities' locations selected away from potential targeted areas. A very good relationship with the local community. Staff first aid and security training. Compliance with the security manual.-Kidnapping of local staff, Likelihood: Likely, Impact: Severe, Mitigation: Follow code of conduct. Hire local contractors who know the context. Build acceptance with the host community and meet the community needs with very good quality.- CASH ROBBERY: Likely, Moderate, Mitigation: Using the PTT to pay for the vendors and staff where possible.-Overlap with other services, Mitigation: coordinate response with HC and operating partners. Share information with the community about services.
