4 - Needs

On February 2021, Blumont conducted a survey in the camp and collective center in NES survey respondents cited improvements to water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH)/shower facilities as a priority for site maintenance. For collective center respondents, WASH/shower facilities garnered all three top responses to the question “what are the most urgent needs for site maintenance and repairs to improve daily life.” Electricity or lighting improvements around WASH/shower facilities or other areas of the camp was cited by 87.26% of the respondents. Door or lock repairs at WASH/shower facilities was second at 81.84%. In third position were difficulties with the water tap, water tank, or water network (38.21%). For camp respondents, electricity or lighting improvements around WASH/shower facilities or other areas was the third most reported needed improvement (31.10%) and drainage problems or grey/black water control problems at WASH/shower facilities was fourth (28.08%). The two most cited urgent needs for those in camps were improvements to small roads and paths for walking (56.37%) and repairs or replacement of damaged tents (51.84%). The former was the only urgent need cited by at least 30% from all four camps demonstrating the broad need for such improvements throughout the camp setting. The latter was reported by 94.87% of Tuwayhinah Camp respondents which underscores the urgency felt by those at that location. Improved protection against flooding was also cited by 15.33% of camp respondents.

5 - Implementation of activities

1) Beneficiary Registration; Blumont will be serving as the primary focal point for registration of all new arrivals in the camps and updating the camp population database. 2) Information Management. Blumont will to collect, optimize, and share data for coordination and decision-making within NES helping to inform a rapid, effective, and principled humanitarian service delivery in planned camps, informal sites, and CCs among all humanitarian actors and donors.3) Camp Governance Structure and Community Leadership Support. 4) Conduct Community Mobilization. Blumont will conduct a range of community-based activities to encourage participation of camp residents, particularly youth, women, and children to help residents adjust to their new lives and foster a sense of social responsibility and cohesion. 5) Maintain and rehabilitate site infrastructure: Blumont conducts general maintenance and operations of site infrastructure to improve the functionality and cleanliness of site facilities. 6) Heating and Cooking NFIs. Blumont will provide fuel and heaters during the winter months for warmth.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Persons in camp settings may have specific needs due not only to the drastic change of circumstances caused by the crisis, but also stemming from vulnerabilities that existed prior to the crisis. In our commitment to protect all persons with special needs (PWSNs) in our CCCM response, Blumont undertakes to identify such persons as early as possible in accordance with a threshold approved by the UNHCR protection team for all vulnerabilities associated with camp residents. Blumont’s registration and protection volunteers work together to ensure all vulnerable persons with special needs are registered and appropriately referred. Upon the new arrival of any family to the camp, Blumont properly screens each family as part of the registration process. Cases of specific vulnerability and those at risk are referred to UNHCR and protection actors within the Protection WG. Blumont works closely with the beneficiaries, relevant WGs, CA and LAs, and highlights any protection needs or concerns. Blumont follows up with the protection partners to ensure activities are implemented and solve any challenges that may be preventing beneficiaries from accessing protection services. When identifying any case of gender or protection needs, it is referred to the proper service provider partners, including but not limited to UNCHR, NRC, and IRC. Blumont as camp management organization coordinates with all stakeholders and ensures concerns are raised and needs addressed when possible. Likewise, if the registration team identifies any family member with a chronic medical condition, Blumont’s protection team will inform a protection actor at the site level for further case management and rapid health response, after which Blumont will follow up.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk Identification and Analysis, Pre-Treatment, and mitigation; 1) Infectious Disease - Covid-19: COVID-19 threatens all staff and program beneficiaries, Blumont will continue to monitor developments in relation to COVID-19 through advice being published by WHO and the U.S. Government. Blumont staff wear gloves and masks, use hand sanitizer frequently and practice physical distancing by maintaining 2 meter distance between themselves and camp residents. 2) Civil unrest; Program staff or objectives impacted by groups of people protesting in the areas of program activity to the actions of Govts/State/Non-State actors. Constantly, Blumont will monitor national news, media and social media, and maintain low profile and avoid areas of demonstration or protest. 3) Physical Security & Crime : Program staff or objectives may be impacted through vehicle hi-jack. Blumont Security Team to monitor, advise and direct. Staff will adherence to Blumont Global Safety & Security Policy and Security SOPs.
