Project Summary

This Action aims to implement life-saving activities for vulnerable women and girls at risk in the context of armed conflict and COVID-19 in Cameroon. The interventions under this Action aim to reach 180,000 internally displaced persons and members of host communities with integrated protection services (medical, psycho-social, legal and protection). Furthermore, the Action will ensure the implementation of infection prevention and control measures (IPC) in 18 health facilities (7 in the NW, 5 in the SW, 3 in the Littoral and 3 in the West) in order to reduce the risk of transmission and spread of COVID-19 in hospitals and in local communities. The Actions interventions will cover 4 regions of Cameroon and will target 9 more affected Divisions:Mezam, Ngoketunjai and Bui (North West), Fako, Kupe Muanenguba and Lebialem (South West), Wouri (Littoral), Mifi and Menoua (West).Through this project, 80 service providers from 18 health facilities, including their maternity wards, in the targeted Health Districts will benefit from capacity building in IPC as well as provision of materials and equipment for infection’ protection and control - in particular personal protective equipment (PPE),emergency reproductive health kits for the management of rape and the safe management of deliveries and complications. In addition, this Action will help to facilitate access to quality remote and timely medical assistance with Specific attention on lifesaving services to GBV survivors with disabilities. For women and girls with disabilities, the intersection of gender and disability increases their vulnerability to violence. In addition, social norms often designate women and girls as caregivers of people with disabilities, which can reinforce their isolation and further limit their access to social, economic and material support, increasing their vulnerability to violence and exploitation.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Gender-Base Violence (GBV) constitutes a violation of the fundamental human rights and is one of the main symptoms of the unequal power relationship between men and women. GBV seriously affects all aspects of a woman’s life, especially their health, their participation in economic life, and their social integrity. Women victims of violence are more likely to have physical, mental and reproductive health problems. Indeed, the sociological and legal context of GBV is characterized by a spirit of tolerance towards this phenomenon in Cameroon and especially in the North West, South West, West and Littoral regions where there is a large number of displaced populations as a result of the Anglophone crises. In order to look for sustainable solutions to this situation, UNFPA has been carrying out GBV prevention and response activities in the North West, South West, Littoral and West regions in Cameroon. UNFPA’s efforts are aimed to improve the quality of basic services for survivors. Although UNFPA's interventions have had a positive impact in terms of behavior change and the support from community members to survivors and other vulnerable women and girls, there are still gaps to cover. In 2021 I UNFPA plans to continue activities in the sub divisions listed above. The main activities will focus on sensitization and awareness raising for communities, emphasizing the participation of men and boys, strengthen referral and legal assistance mechanisms for internally displaced persons and host communities with legal protection needs (detention/documentation, GBV survivors etc.), provide basic vocational training and economic/Empowerment support to women and girls, Provide cash transfer assistance, build the capacity of operational organizations on the holistic response to GBV. UNFPA and partners will continue providing individualized case management and psychosocial support to survivors of GBV and other vulnerable people. Finally, UNFPA, in collaboration with the Ministry of Women Empowerment and the Family (MINPROFF), will also continue to reinforce close collaboration between actors and service providers involved in responding to the needs of women and girls. Ensure that safe and ethical systems for the transfer of data including confidential personal records of GBV survivors are put in place by organizations and authorities. Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) is a form of GBV that constitutes an abuse of power by aid workers against the affected population. SEA can happen in any setting against anyone but the risks of SEA increase in humanitarian settings. The women’s committees help enhance women’s commitment to GBV prevention and response activities, and give women an opportunity to express their opinions and stand up for their rights. The CBOs will then conduct GBV prevention activities in collaboration with other volunteers in order to change the behavior characterized by cultural practices that favor male dominated decision making roles. This strategy will also foster ownership and sustainability and the continuation of GBV prevention activities since CBOs will be deeply embedded in the community.
