3 - Needs

During Sep and Oct 2022, IHR conducted a rapid need assessment to determine the best practices and most suitable methodologies to support the families in the protected areas in Ghandoura in rural Aleppo, where the KIIs mentioned that: women and children are the most vulnerable groups or community, especially when it is related to the family’s needs and the lack of work opportunities and the absence of many breadwinners, and 70% of women Headed-households suffer from lack of access to the basic food needs and non-food assistance especially older woman or women with special needs.35% of reviewed families have people with disabilities in a range of 1 person per family. Most of them need protection, support, and social inclusion. Also, the KIIs stated that the most common problems that the women and girls faced were the lack of security, traditions and customs, early marriage, deprivation of the rights of education and inheritance, and the parent's and husbands' control of their wives and girls. - About 51% of women and girls faced circumstances that prevented them from reaching the protection services, such as transportation, family prevention, and traditions and customs in the region. - More than half of IDP women and girls suffer from the lack of private sleeping arrangements, bathrooms, etc. People without civil documents face challenges in getting assistance.

4 - Implementation of activities

This project will support s 23087 beneficiaries affected by the Syrian crisis 4885 men,11600 women, 7110 boys, and 6431 girls, 200 men PWD, 250 women PWD, 200 boys PWD, 250 girls PWD)in Ghandoura - sub-districts by promoting their accessibility to the protection assistance significantly. IHR has high qualified protection team in Turkey and Syria and will build the capacities of the staff to ensure they provide specialized services, structured and recreational psychosocial support (PSS), life skills sessions, awareness sessions, Psychological First Aid (PFA) and dissemination of information about services to HC and IDPs. The specialized services include case management, individual and group counseling, and referrals. The WGSS centers will provide static support with more specialized services than the mobile teams. Case management will include direct support using the appropriate case management tools (Informed Consent Form, Safety plan form, Suicide prevention form). Monitoring of activities will be aligned with agreed work plans, the log frame, and the indicators performance tracking table (IPTT), and every activity related to this project will be documented and coded; all the staff will be trained in collecting data, protection-sensitive/issues, sharing information, and writing. The project team will ensure data protection, confidentiality at all stages, information sharing protocols, and acquiring informed consent from beneficiaries.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

IHR will recognize the risks faced by beneficiaries, and the difficulties they face in reporting harm they experience by the organization and will not tolerate our staff, partners, or associates engaging in or supporting any sexual exploitation and abuse or child abuse. The principle of ‘best interests will guide our actions in response to concerns and breaches of this policy. IHR understands that, for thePSEA/Child Safeguarding policy to be well understood and effectively implemented, it is essential that there are high levels of awareness regarding the policy and that staffand others are clear, confident, and competent in putting the policy into practice. Disseminate and promote the policy widely. Copies of the policy will be made availableand accessible to all stakeholders, both in hard copy and electronically, toensure that the policy commitments, principles, and practices are publicized and that everyone is clear about IHR's position concerning PSEA and safeguarding children.IHR mobile teams will encourage the beneficiaries to claim their rights and follow up on the issues with the project management in Turkey. Furthermore, the affected population will have access to the intervention services equally as appropriate to their needs and without discrimination. People with disabilities, women, and elders have been accessed during the needs assessment process and are involved in the discussions in the community of intervention.
