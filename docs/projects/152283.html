Needs

Areas near the Contact Line in Government Controlled Area (GCA) are characterized by very high vulnerability. The principal driver of vulnerability of the population is the continuation of fighting concentrated in densely populated areas and remote villages. Access to basic public services, health sector, lack of access to medical supplies are of particular concern. According to the recently released statistical data, in the first quarter of 2018, the unemployment rate among the economically active population in Ukraine was 9.7%, with 15% rate in Donetsk region. In addition, during this period, 13% of population in GCA have experienced a reduction of salaries and income. These findings were confirmed through Focus Group Discussions where respondents emphasized the loss of permanent jobs or pensions/other social benefits as well as the sharp reduction in income or the problem of salary arrears. According to the Humanitarian Implementation Plan for Ukraine and Eastern Neighbourhood, 2018, the situation remains volatile and the humanitarian crisis is not expected to be over in the near future. The vulnerability of the population affected by the crisis is assessed to be very high. Among the estimated 150,000-200,000 people living in 5 km zone along the contact line 10% constitute the registered IDPs, and the majority of population are elderly people as work-age young residents have fled the area in search of employment opportunities. According to the REACH Humanitarian Needs Trend Analysis 2018, households across all assessed areas are missing essential NFIs in their homes. However, in rural areas households lacking essential NFIs was most significant. Within the 5km area of the contact line the food consumption score has decreased from 21% reported in Winter Feb 2018, to 16% reported in July. Overall, households closer to the contact line are more likely to be either moderately or severely food insecure. In rural areas within 5km of the contact line, this was 13% and 15% in small urban areas within 5km.This points to a large population of isolated elderly females. The rest of the marginalized groups, residing on the territory, including single mothers, children, and IDPs are becoming increasingly vulnerable as 96% of military exchanges take place within 5km of either side of the contact line. Their coping capacity is stretched as the conflict disrupted health, education and market networks, and the on-going military exchanges do not allow for permanent solutions. People in Need’s (PIN) activities in eastern Ukraine are designed and implemented based on needs assessments, vulnerability assessments and gender analyses in order to provide comprehensive assistance and relief to the most vulnerable populations in the region of its activities. By applying protection mainstreaming standards through all humanitarian activities, PIN strives to provide equal access to all populations, with special emphasis on women, children, elderly, and disabled persons. Furthermore, women are fully included in all stages of project design and implementation through consultations with local government representatives, social services, local communities, and NGOs. Based on the assessment field visits and PIN’s generated experience, cash-based interventions and multi-purpose cash programs in particular continue to be among the most appropriate and timely ways to not only assist people in meeting of their basic needs but also to stimulate local economic growth through humanitarian assistance. With regards to vulnerability criteria and beneficiary selection, generally, PIN will follow recommendations of the Cash Working Group. PIN will continue to prioritize individuals in vulnerable categories which have limited access to income opportunities.

Activities & outcomes

Objective 1 Activity 1:PIN will provide multi-purpose cash (MPC) transfers to the targeted population of the front-line locations of Yasinovatskiy, Bahmutskiy, and Toretskiy districts of Donetsk region characterized by very high vulnerability. These geographic areas were selected within 5-20 km from the contact line following the important criteria of adherence to “do no harm” principle and necessity to provide essential services in ‘safe’ locations, avoiding unintended pull factors or returns to unsafe locations. Furthermore, the selection process followed the HRP 2019-2020: Strategic Priorities to ensure the intervention targets both critical areas and vulnerability groups of population. The population of selected locations has access to local markets and thus is able to purchase basic food and non-food items, therefore the proposed cash modality is both timely and justified. Under this activity, PIN will provide MPC to 3,000 beneficiaries transferred via a remittance system. The amount of cash assistance is calculated per each beneficiary, thus all beneficiaries living in a HH will be covered. The standard financial allocation per beneficiary is 970 UAH (33USD) per month during 3 months, following the Cash Working Group recommendations. Based on current experience of MPC implementation projects, PIN will select the beneficiaries based on previous assessment data and verification process, which includes community awareness sessions and information sharing by posters, leaflets, notices, etc., and provision of preliminary lists of beneficiaries by local authorities and NGOs. During the final selection stage the household composition, status, income, health and ability status, shelter and food needs will be considered. A special attention will be paid to beneficiaries with disabilities to ensure their full access to the benefits of the MPC project. For this purpose PIN will initiate an additional modality to facilitate help to this category of beneficiaries whereas a group of local active community members will be involved to provide such beneficiaries with food and non-food items procured at their request and render other necessary assistance. The following households will be prioritized: single parent women headed households; isolated elderly women; male headed households with 3 or more children. It is expected that 70% of beneficiaries will be women and girls; approximately 10% of beneficiary households will be headed by single mothers; 20% will be children (50% girls) while 35-40% will be elderly people (approx. 65-70% women). The MPC delivery will be based on 4 rounds, which will follow the structure below:1) January – March2) April – June3) July – September4) October – DecemberThe PIN’s Assessment, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit will execute monitoring and evaluation to ensure the applicability, accuracy and efficiency of the proposed activity over the course of the project and in the post intervention period, utilizing strong and harmonized processes in terms of outputs, methods, outcomes and impact assessments. PIN and other NGO active in the area will divide the intervention zone to provide meaningful assistance. PIN will build a database of selected beneficiaries to cross-reference them with other humanitarian actors to avoid any risk of overlap. PIN will conduct regular monitoring activities through both the management and the M&E functions. Field checks will be made, randomly interviewing beneficiary who have received assistance through the project. Post-distribution monitoring will be conducted via structured phone interviews to collect qualitative feedback on the relevance of aid and beneficiaries’ experience of receiving it. Furthermore, PIN M&E Unit will carry out PDM surveys reviewing beneficiary experiences on the whole aid process (from beneficiary identification to aid delivery). Findings of the monitoring reports will be disseminated to relevant clusters and other stakeholders.
