Project Summary

Provision of health services in South Sudan has been greatly affected by the instability experienced in the country since 2013. Health budget allocation by the government has been less that 2% consistently for the last few years and that has greatly affected the provision of basic health and nutrition services to the vulnerable populations.
South Sudan remains one of the countries in the world with very bad health indicators with the maternal mortality rate being at 789/100000 live births, infant mortality rate of 62.5/1000 live births and child mortality rate of 96.4/1000 live births (WHO 2018). This are way above the recommended numbers by the WHO.
Around 1.3 million people in South Sudan were affected by malaria in 2018 and 95% of South Sudan is endemic of malaria. 17 of the 89 counties in south Sudan have had confirmed measles outbreak in 2019 and the infection cycle is not yet broken. This can be attributed to very low routine immunization coverage which stands at less than 50%.
Access to health facilities is also very difficult with only 44% of the total population in South Sudan reached with health services according to the SARA survey 2019. South Sudan has a total of 989 functional facilities against a population of 12 million individuals, this makes the facility population ratio above 10000 individuals/ health facility which is recommended by WHO.
The utilization for of maternal and child health services in south Sudan remains very low with only 17% of the pregnant mothers attending to the ANC 4th visit (UNICEF 2018)
This project will therefore improve the utilization of health services in the community by provision of mobile health services to vulnerable and affected populations in Yirol East.
WAV will give the minimum health package with the following activities
1. Mobile outpatient consultations
2. Provision of ANC and PNC services
3. Provision of immunization services
4. Reproductive health services
5. Health education services to the vulnerable populations
6. Management of common illnesses and especially the 5 leading causes of morbidity
7. Capacity building of the health staff to ensure that they have the skills on infection prevention and control, IDSR, and CMR
8. Referral of the SAM and MAM cases to the nutrition centers
9. Referral of cases of GBV and Sexual abuse to treatment centers
10. Weekly EWARS reporting and monthly 5W reporting to the health cluster.
The project will reach a total of 10000 populations of which will be 3000 returnees 1000 IDPs and 7000 Host communities

1 - Needs

Health needs in south Sudan are immerse and that can partly be attributed to weak health systems that include health financing, health human resource, health infrastructure and insufficient medical supplies.Yirol East county has occasional flooding ,inter and intra-communal conflicts and cattle raiding that affect health service delivery in the county and causes massive displacement of the populations.According to REACH 2018, the leading cause of morbidity in the county was malaria and acute watery diarrhea with GAM rates of 22.6% way above the WHO emergency threshold.There is underutilization of ANC services and immunization services in the county with less than 17% of the pregnant mothers having ANC 4th visit (UNICEF 2018)Access to health facilities is also very difficult with only 44% of the total population in South Sudan reached with health services according to the SARA survey 2019. South Sudan has a total of 989 functional facilities against a population of 12 million individuals; this makes the facility population ratio above 10000 individuals/ health facility which is recommended by WHO.Yirol East has experienced intercommunal clashes and cattle raiding that displace communities and make it even hard to reach the health services and only 1 PHCC is serving the whole county.Increased malaria cases attributed to the rainy season. This has increased the morbidity rates and increased risk to mortality. It has also been one of the counties that were affected by suspected measles vaccine and according to the IDSR report week 4 2019. This can be attributed to the unavailability of the cold chain facilities and routine immunization coverage. It was also noted that there was very low availability of health service delivery due to unavailability of health facilities in the county.Open defecation and water contamination that increases risk to waterborne diseases in the county has a detrimental effect on the disease burden in the county. There will be need to improve health education on key health messages and also early health seeking behaviors.

2 - Activities

1. Mobile outpatient consultations: That will increase the access of health services to the vulnerable and affected populations in Yirol East County. This will reduce morbidity and mortality rates.2. Provision of ANC and PNC services: The provision of this essential health services will greatly reduce the maternal infant and under five mortality rates that can be directly linked to unavailability of ANC and PNC services.3. Provision of immunization services: Occasional disease outbreaks in the county and especially measles is linked to unavailability of EPI services.4. Reproductive health services: this will help reduce transmission of sexually transmitted infection in the country5. Health education services to the vulnerable populations: health education will sensitize the communities on risks and behavior change communication to enhance early health seeking behaviors among the communities 6.
