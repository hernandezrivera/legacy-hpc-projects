Project Summary

The overall humanitarian situation in Somalia is already grave, due to the country having endured decades of conflicts, insecurity, drought, famine and various disease outbreaks. The 2020 Humanitarian Response plans (HRP) estimates 5.2 million people (42 per cent of the total population of 12.3 million) are in critical need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 19 percent from 4.2 million in 2019. This situation is compounded by 2.6 million internally displaced population (IDP) living in over 2,000 congested IDP sites in Somalia. Furthermore, the country’s nascent social protection sector is not equipped to manage significant shocks to household’s livelihoods. Thus, in the midst of the COVID-19 response, there is a need to ensure that the women and children can continue to access health facility and community-based services.
The continuous need assessments with stakeholders in Banadir, Lower Shebelle, Galgaduud and Mudug during ongoing interventions by CISP indicate that basic health services are needed by the vulnerable conflict affected populations, including urban and rural IDPs and host communities. Health care provision remains dismal with huge gaps in facilities offering maternal and child health and nutrition services including deliveries, vaccination, treatment of childhood illnesses and micronutrient supplementation. This is attributable to the overburdened health capacities, services disruption in conflict and disaster and population influx (IDP and Returnees).The challenges have exposed vulnerable populations to the risk of communicable disease and other morbidities. CISP seeks to build on her previous experience in Health specific and sensitive integrated programming to step up provision of lifesaving health in mentioned areas.
In view of the above CISP will ensure continuity of delivery of essential health care services focusing on maternal neonatal and child health services with COIVD-19 emergency response initiatives such as Minimizing morbidity and mortality secondary to COVID-19 through capacity Building of health care workers and preventing and addressing the impact of human consequences of the outbreak by ensuring continuation of essential life-saving preventive and curative services at Health facilities where CISP operates.
CISP will also put in place measures to limit human-to-human transmission and protect individuals from exposure to COVID-19 In line with current COVID-19 pandemic response, CISP will promote application of standard precautions such as respiratory and hand hygiene measures in Elder health facility as well as implementing additional precautions (including contact and droplet precautions and airborne precautions for aerosol-generating procedures) for any cases where COVID-19 infection is suspected with special focus on congested areas such IDP camps. Administrative controls and policies as well as environmental and engineering controls will be put in place for the prevention and control of transmission of the virus. Extra hand washing facilities and triage section will be set up the hospital. As waiting rooms can be a source of transmission for respiratory illness. CISP will ensure there is adequate ventilation in all areas in the healthcare facility and that cleaning and disinfection procedures are followed consistently and correctly.
The project targets a total of 62,816 under 5 children (32,036 girls, 30,780 boys), 35,334 WCBA and 10,000 men to address health needs by providing outpatient consultation in 6 MCHs, in patient care services in the 2 Hospitals, integrated management of childhood illnesses, immunization, maternal health services, emergency reproductive health screening of acute malnutrition and referral and health education. Some services, targeted areas women and children, including IDPs and returnees, will also be targeted and get services through 6 Mobile facilities linked to existing static health facilities for better service coverage.

Mitigation Measures

The target population faces continuous displacements due to armed conflict between SNA/AMISOM on one side and the Militant group on the other. The target population also faces forceful recruitment of young boys to the militia of the militant groups, although some local communities fought back and resisted they could not sustain and were ultimately defeated like what happened in some areas in Mudug region. As per the current situation the only way to mitigate is to families to send the young to government controlled districts and for long term solutions is to liberate those areas from the iron grip of the militants. The risk involved lack of gender equity and social inclusion: This will be mitigated CISP will prevent exclusion and inequality by ensuring equal participation of all people, including individuals at risk of exclusion and ensure they reach their full potential, both in terms of its economy and its level of governance. Social Inclusion will be promoted through: •When hiring incentive staff equal opportunities will be given to all irrespective of their social background, gender and disability. • Equal representation in the Community Health Committees (CHC) and Community Development Committees (CDC). • Training Health staff on do no harm. The proposed project will equally target women, boys and girls, particularly those from vulnerable families affected by ill health and drought whose vulnerabilities are exacerbated by the worsening drought, water scarcity and displacement into IDP settlements where children live in extremely precarious conditions with only limited community support. This project will support the MOH to identify the staff with optimum qualifications and experiences taking into account the different capacities and needs of men and women. As well as assigning equal opportunities, tasks and responsibilities to both men and women. Community Based workers selection and trainings will also consider gender sensitivity including both men and women. All this will ensure gender issues and equity is addressed. – CISP will prioritize safety &dignity, and avoid causing and any unintended negative effects of its intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and Psycho-social risks. CISP will ensure people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services like people disabilities and the elderly.
