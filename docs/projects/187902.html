Project Summary

The project endeavors to implement complemented activities in the humanitarian field in western rural of Aleppo province. This intervention will strengthen permanent protection assistance and services in IDP sites through the establishment of facilities inside and/or in close proximity to IDP sites and make a contribution towards Life-saving humanitarian assistance and protection to the most vulnerable people with an emphasis on underserved areas with high severity of needs depending on the expansion of the activities of two existing protection centers WGSS specialized in GBV in Atareb and Kafr Karmin villages.
The project includes the following activities:
- Forming of three mobile teams to provide of psychological first aid (PFA), information/awareness, individual and emergency case management support, life skills program, GBV services, referrals to GBV services and other services. The teams will cover the Daret Azza and Atareb sub-district in rural Aleppo in addition to the surrounding camps. (every sub-team will consist of two mobile teams, two social workers and case management workers).
- Support the case management activities (Case fund) for 100 survivors. This assistance will be provided in kind and cash. The estimated amount for each beneficiary is 100 USD. Based on Cash Assistance in GBV Case Management Guidance Note June 2019.By GBV-sub cluster.
- Engaging 3760 women and girls with vocational activities in WGSS and ten point in camps.
- Provide 950 men, women, girls and boys with disabilities reached with protection services to specialized and non-specialized services like (peer-support, awareness raising, skills development, PSS, case management and Ambassador's programme to increase PwD participation in humanitarian action).
MASRRAT has been in the protection sector for seven years. MASRRAT is a focal point to provide GBV services in Jebel Saman district.Where MASRRAT has full access to target project sites in Aleppo countryside and has exllent experience in cross-border assistance as per the requirements of protection services.
MASRRAT works as a partner with Ihsan organization (since 2015 ) to implementing a (ongoing) project funded by UNFPA in the western rural Aleppo.Where the project supports Gender-Based Violence (GBV), the project satisfies the beneficiaries and the community in the target area.
Also, MASRRAT has qualified and well-trained staff regarding the protection of children and women.That includes protection coordinator in Turkey who is expert and trained in carrying out the protection projects as well as the coordination and PSS specialists also in Turkey supervise on technical staff.
Which MASRRAT makes has experience, capacity and commitment to successfully implement the work plan.
MASRRAT will provide specialized services by expanding the work static facilities in two locations (Kafr Karmin and Atarib) and outreach teams. Specialized services include case management, psychological support and referrals including enabling access to health services for gender-based violence. Activities will also include conducting recreational activities, vocational training like ( literacy , First aids, Hairdressing, Draw , Turkish , Sport and English ), life skills activities, individual and group counseling (non-specialized), psychological first aid (PFA) and awareness activities through direct contact initiatives, and the topics of these sessions may include one or more of the following topics: Information about GBV related services, information on GBV, gender equality, domestic violence, child marriage, etc., including engaging men and boys in preventing GBV activities.
As a first step, the activities of the project will be announced, and registration will be opened for beneficiaries that meet the vulnerability criteria.
Provider training for humanitarian actors’ non-specialists trained on GBV Basics and risk mitigation. MASRRAT protection staff will coordinate with other sectors inside MASRRAT

4 - Needs

According to VOICES FROM SYRIA 2022, the most common form of violence mentioned by women and girls in NWS was IPV and family violence, digital/online violence, restrictions of rights and movements, denial of education - linked to early marriage and child labor, deprivation of inheritance especially widows, sexual exploitation andharassment - especially at work and for rent and early marriage continues to be mentioned. women and girls with disability are most commonly subjected to physical, psychological, and social violence: including IPV, psychological violence by the family, public bullying in school, social stigma, and neglect. in addition, widowed women are at increased risk for Forced re-marriage and forced abandonment of children in some cases,movement restrictions by family members, and higher levels of social and community violence -stigma, shaming. MASRRAT conducted need assessment to determine the best practices and most suitable methodologies to support the families in protection areas in Jebel Saman in rural Aleppo where the KIIs mentioned that: women and children are the most vulnerable, especially when it is related to the families' needs and the lack of work opportunities and the absence of many breadwinners, and 70% of women Headed-households suffer from lack of access to the basic food needs and non-food assistance especially older woman or women with special needs.35% of reviewed families have people with disabilities in a range of 1 person per family.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will support 36514 beneficiaries affected by the Syrian crisis in Atareb and Daret Azza - sub-districts. MASRRAT trained specialists will provide specialized services, structured and recreational psychosocial support (PSS), life skills sessions, awareness sessions, Psychological First Aid (PFA) and dissemination of information about services to HC and IDPs. The specialized services include case management, individual and group counseling, and referrals. Initially the following vulnerability criteria will be adopted as conditions to registration:-Vulnerable IDP women, adolescent girls and boys either facing or are at risk of discrimination and GBV and deprivation of services, which includes persons with disabilities (PWDs). -Single-headed households, the elderly and PWDs facing neglect and deprivation of services. - Women and girls with chronic disease, psychological disorders -Pregnant or lactating women-elderly/disabled family members. MASRRAT MEAL team will conduct regular visits to places of service provision related to gender-based violence as well as the survivor satisfaction surveys that will be conducted during the visits.Regarding the outreach activities, they will be monitored by the monitoring staff who will record the daily numbers of the sessions in addition to the number of beneficiaries through the activities tracking table, in addition to conducting focus group discussion with the participants in the awareness activities.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: 1-Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Lack of quality services also lead to fewer disclosures and lack of trust in servicseLikelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigating measures: 1. Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centred services 2. Promotion of SOPs roll out for all staff of organizations involved in the referral . Protection risk: Women and adolescent girls with child-bearing responsibilities might have difficulty accessing services in the WGSSLikelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Adaptation of GBV messaging to ensure sensitivity to the context and culture Protection risk: Lack of support from family members to allow women and girls to travel to WGSS and access servicesLikelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Thorough gender analysis prior to start of programming. Equip all WGSS with child-care services. Provide transportation support for persons with disability. Include persons with disability in all conducted assessments at the level of WGSS and/or community to understand their needs and gaps in their access to services


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

MASRRAT will recognize the risks faced by beneficiaries, and the difficulties they face in reporting harm they experience by the organization will not tolerate our staff, partners or associates to engage in or support any type of sexual exploitation and abuse or child abuse. The principle of ‘best interests’ will guide our actions in response to concerns and breaches of this policy. MASRRAT understands that, in order for the PSEA/Child Safeguarding policy to be well understood and effectively implemented, it is essential that there are high levels of awareness regarding the policy and that staff and others are clear, confident and competent in putting the policy into practice. Disseminate and promote the policy widely. Copies of the policy will be made available and accessible to all stakeholders, both in hard copy and electronically in order to ensure that the policy commitments, principles, and practices are publicized and that everyone is clear about MASRRAT position in relation to PSEA and safeguarding children. MASRRAT mobile teams, will encourage the beneficiaries to claim their rights and will follow up the issues with the project management in Turkey. Furthermore, the affected population will have access to the intervention services equally as appropriate with the need and without any discrimination. People with disabilities, women, and elders have been accessed during the needs assessment process and involved in the discussions in the community of intervention.

Effect on expected Results: Very High. RiskManagement Strategy/Mitigating Measure: MASRRAT would be conducting more meetings and coordinate with those organizations. Risk : Changing the security situation that interferes with the implementation of the project.Likelihood of Risk Occurring: Medium. Effect on Expected Results: high. RiskManagement Strategy/Mitigating Measure: We will establish a response plan that can be activated according to the situation. Designing the activities in a flexible way where we can change the way of conducting the activity according to the situation so as to achieve the wanted results. and we can focus on more suitable activities like doing PFA . Stopping the activities for a short period.
