Project Summary

Plan International conducted a job market scan at Teknaf to identify market-relevant skill development training for girls, boys and young women. The target people for the skills development will be selected from the extremely poor households in the host community and from the Rohingya Camps. The age of the target people is from 18 to 24 years. For the host community, Plan Internation will use the curriculum and books from the Bangladesh Technical Education Board (BTEB) which is equivalent to the grade 1 of the NTVQF competency standard. The training will be for 360 hours based on the nature of the trade course. The major areas of skills development found in the market scan are driving, mobile phone servicing, industrial sewing operator, para-vet, computer literacy & hardware, beauty parlour, hospitality & tourism, food and beverage etc.
To implement the skills development training in the host community, Plan International will follow 3 strategies because there is no Technical Training Center of BMET in Teknaf nor Cox’sbzar. Firstly, Plan will establish a temporary training centre for a few of the courses like driving and industrial sewing operators, computer literacy and hardware, and freelancing. Already Plan International successfully implemented this type of training in Teknaf. Secondly, the target people will be admitted to the training centres in Cox’s Bazar. Plan International’s partner UCEP Bangladesh has established a training centre in Cox’sbazar recently where Plan will expand its partnership with UCEP for arranging training in Cox's Bazar. The major courses would be electrical and electronics, plumbing, hospitality etc. Plan International will also establish a partnership with the renowned Tomy Mia’s training centre for food and beverage course. The courses will follow the curriculum and books of BTEB including certification. The project will also explore, the opportunity for skills training locally at the department of youth development and women, women & children affairs and the ICT division of the Government (there is 2 popular government TVET programme SIEP and learning & earning). Thirdly, Plan International will follow a mentor-mentee approach for the target people who are unable to join the training courses outside of the union based on their family situation. However, Plan International will ensure the curriculum of BTEB to the mentors. After successful completion of the training, the target people will be placed in a paid job or enterprise. As 85% economy of the country is informal, therefore, the project will follow the approach of job and enterprise development. For the improvement of their livelihood, the young entrepreneurs will be linked with MFIs for further capital to expand their businesses.
To implement the skills development training in the camps, the project will arrange temporary training sessions inside the camps in the selected courses. The trade courses would be electric maintenance, knitting, tailoring, handicrafts, etc. However, the project will ensure a competency-based curriculum and books of BTEB for the quality of the training. After training, there will be an assessment. The youth in the camps will be supported to start a small business inside the camps.
For the coordination with the Government, the project will follow the curriculam and books of the Bangladesh technical Education Board. Already Plan International has a working relations with BTEB which will be further explored.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The job market scan identified that there is a huge dropout of adolescent boys and girls in Teknaf which is referred to the information of BANBIAS. Due to dropouts, the youth does not have the appropriate skills-set to enter the job market. There is a market trend for going abroad by the young people in Teknaf. Remittance is one of the major foreign currency-earning sources of the Government. At present, there is no TTC (technical training centre of BMET) standard in Teknaf. However, the priority of 8FYP of the Government focuses the technical skills education for the youth. There is also demand for skilled workers locally, nationally and abroad. Due to a lack of technical skills, the boys, girls and young women join in low-paid work in an informal environment. Therefore, skills training is selected so that girls, boys, and young women can join in dignified work with market-relevant skills.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project is built based on integrated approaches with social cohesion and an education program. Social cohesion-related activities increase leadership capacities and understanding among the youth. To avoid overlapping with other organizations, Plan International will implement the project in the selected camps. Plan International will coordinate with the CiC in implementing this project. In the host community, the existing NGOs will be consulted for avoiding overlapping also. Plan International will continue coordination with the sectors/WGs for better coordination. Plan International is taking part in the camp-level coordination with other organizations. Plan International is continually working to ensure multi-year funding which has already started in Telnaf and will continue up to 2024. Therefore, the proposed project is fully aligned with the rationalization plan.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

As a global child rights organization, protection is one of the priority issues in every project of Plan International. For the staff, training on protection is mandatory. The project included specific activities in the areas of protection. Before, every event like a meeting/training, safeguarding will be assessed. Plan International Bangladesh has the tools for the protection of the target people from any harmful situation which will be used regularly. .

3.2 - Gender

The project will work for agency building of girls and young women by disseminating information on leadership and capacity development. A family-based approach is used in the project including the use of specific tools that ensure the participation of the girls and young women in a decision-making process at the household level. The project will include 80% of young women so that they have regular income from their job/enterprises as well as control over resources. The project ensured men's engagement and diversification for gender transformation where role sharing will be encouraged. Plan International has comprehensive gender marker tools which included agency of the girls/young women, diversity, enabling environment, male engagement etc. which will be used in the project annually. Based on the report, activities will be reviewed to ensure gender transformation. Plan International is also working nationally on the area of decent working and fear of violence. The policy-level changes

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

As per Plan International's policy, girls and boys below 18 years are treated as children. Plan International follows strict child protection mechanisms in developing and implementing all the projects. All the staff in Plan International including partner NGOs are trained in safeguarding. Plan arranges periodic reviews on safeguarding among the staff. However, the protection-related activities are equally applicable to external stakeholders, visitors and consultants.

3.4 - Diversity

The project will follow diversity to include persons with disability and excluded people. It is expected that 8% PWD will be included in the project. Again the project will select the target people from the bottom layer of the population by arranging PRA sessions and wealth ranking. The women headed and members with PWD in households will get priority in selection.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) is an active commitment by Plan International Bangladesh. Plan International inform people in the camps and the host community about their rights and entitlements, and participation in decisions that affect them. The courtyard session is a platform in the project where the target people express their opinion which has been included in the design of the project. The income empowers the youth, especially girls and young women and creates space for control over resources. Awareness raising on disaster preparedness is part of the project. The project ensures services from the government offices for better resilience.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the DRM and climate change issues are embedded in the project. The target people are oriented on the preparation and recovery mechanism from the courtyard sessions fortnightly. As a risk mitigation plan, the target people who will start the enterprisees are linked with the MFIs for savings and loans so that they may use it as further capital after any disaster. As a global humanitarian organization, Plan International has a well-equipped DRR response team who are ready to respond immediately after any disaster. There are 2 warehouses of Plan equipped with Non-food items. Plan is responding to the victims of Sitrang in Teknaf in 2022. The project included plantation activities along with the kitchen garden as a long-term impact on climate change.
