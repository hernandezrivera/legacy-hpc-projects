Project Summary

The project will support the sustainable reintegration of returnees and resident community in Um Baru, Kornoi and Tina localities in North Darfur through targeted livelihood and wash interventions.
This action will focus on targeted villages of return and will contribute to:
- Increase the availability of safe water and improved hygiene practices
- Restore and enhance basic production capacities as well as improving and diversify household livelihood options

Needs

Since 2015, a total of 101,424 persons returned in North Darfur state. A general push towards Return, Recovery and Reintegration activities has been experienced at both local and federal government, as well as UN agencies and major donors. Over the last few years over 36,966 people have returned to North Darfur, many more are expected to return from Chad (IOM, 2018). The returnees are although returning to areas which have been abandoned by years, with infrastructures which were heavily damaged during the war, and in most cases returning to villages which have no basic infrastructures. Access to basic services in the areas of concern is overstretched, especially on water accessibility and livelihood resources. Key findings from COOPI KAP surveys conducted in Um Baru, Mellit and Malha include that water availability is always below 15 liters per person per day (9.15 liters), during the dry season the average time to reach a water source is mostly between one and three hours and waiting time at the water source is regularly between one and three hours. Guaranteeing basic services and above all ensuring the provision of water for human consumption and production is a key priority in the identified areas or return and settlement of IDPs. Despite the good rains, poor seasonal progress in pastoral and agro-pastoral areas of parts of North Darfur are still projected at crisis phase (IPC Phase 3). Findings from the recent CFSAM indicate 2017/18 cereal harvests were significantly below average, mainly due to prolonged dry spells during the rainy season. State-wide cereal production is estimated at 61,000 MT, which is approximately 36 percent lower than the five year-average and less than 20 percent of the above average-production.Despite economic and political measures set to reduce government expenditure, the shortage of foreign currency continues to be the major driver of the deteriorating macroeconomic situation. This has led to extremely high prices of imported and locally produced food and non-food items. The extremely high cost of fuel and other agricultural inputs is anticipated to negatively impact the coming harvest.

Activities

The project envisioned to support a total of 7,249 people living in returnees villages of Kornoi, Tina and Um Barou locality. Result 1) Improved availability of water and natural resources to enhance livelihoods productivityActivity 1.1: Feasibility studies and rehabilitation of one hafir in Um BaruActivity 1.2: Feasibility studies and construction of permeable rock check dams (30 feddans) in Um Baru Activity 1.3 Construction of water spreading bunds (30 feddans) in Kornoi and Um BaruActivity 1.4: Creation and training of 3 Natural Resources Management Committees Result 2) Women and youth empowerment through micro-finance and IGAActivity 2.1: Establishment and training of 6 women and Youth VSLAActivity 2.2: Small business development skill training to 6 women and Youth VSLA membersActivity 2.3: Support winning business plan of VSLA through start-up capital
