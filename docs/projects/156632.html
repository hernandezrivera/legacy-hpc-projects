Project Summary

Due to the prolonged conflict, many Syrian families are left vulnerable to the cold weather during the winter months. Many are unable to purchase basic winterisation items after multiple instances of displacement. This project will assist approx.1000 households with winterisation items according to the NFI MEB or typical winterisation NFI items. Furthermore, 150 beneficiaries will receive training on NFI related issues. WRG will target beneficiaries in areas with high host, returnee and/or IDP figures and select beneficiaries from the most vulnerable groups. The project will last 6 months and be due to assist beneficiaries at the beginning of winter 2019 (with project preparations starting in July, and the project ending in December 2019).

Needs

While market access appears to be gradually improving and NFI items are available in markets, many essential, humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining basic items continue to be unaffordable for many people. 4.4 million people require NFI assistance in Syria. It is the second year in a row that the total number of people in need of NFI assistance has reduced, with this decrease in people in need reflecting some minor improvements in market functionality in certain areas. Access to NFIs has become on the whole more stable over the last year, however, “needs remain substantial, both in terms of humanitarian life-saving needs, particularly those among recently displaced IDPs requiring core, supplementary and seasonal assistance, as well as longer-term needs, among those communities requiring resilience-oriented support to reduce dependence on external assistance and enhance the ability of families to withstand future shocks and stresses”. (SNFI HNO 2019 Draft) Affordability of winterisation items has remained a challenge to PiN, and is an issue affecting people in stable areas, such as Lattakia, Hama and Tartous, in addition to those in areas directly impacted by hostilities, such as Idleb. Populations in areas which experience cold conditions during winter continue to need seasonal support, with communities surveyed reporting heating-related shelter deficiencies (lack of heating and insulation) as the most widespread issue. The need for clothing & hygiene items were also among the most reported NFI needs across the country. This project will meet the winter needs of approx 1000 households in multiple locations, and train 150 beneficiaries on NFI related skills. Distribution will happen through local partners and will be in line with MEBs and sector guidelines. The most vulnerable will be targeted, including but not limited to host, returnees, the elderly, people with disabilities & pregnant/lactating mothers. This project seeks to address identified needs of WGBM across all age groups. Most often male and female heads of households are responsible for providing adequate provision to the family in winter. Heads of Households usually, but not always refer to young and middle aged adults. However, Heads of Households can be minors in addition to the elderly, and they can also be single. Each has unique needs. For males their responsibility to provide adequate winter provision to the family often relates to warmth (heat, gas) and shelter needs. For female heads of households it typically means clothing, warmth (blankets, bedding) and sanitary items. Young children and children can most often be affected by the cold winters due to their ever changing sizes and their need for frequent clothing changes. Older people are additionally highly impacted during cold months with degrading health and inability to preserve body heat. They may be more likely to put the needs of younger individuals above their own, thus reducing their ability to cope with winter conditions. Pregnant and breast-feeding mothers have distinct needs during winter as they care for those around them. Specifically in winter when families live in close proximity to each other to preserve warmth (often resulting in 2 families sharing the same home, and overcrowding), privacy and SGBV concerns for women, girls, boys and men are heightened. However, of these groups during these months, women (especially single and adolescent), girls, mothers (especially heads of households), babies, people with disabilities and the elderly) face increased levels of need and exposure to risk. They should be consulted and prioritised in all programming. Sources:HNO 2019

Implementation of activities

World Relief Germany (WRG) will work closely with several partners in multiple locations. Beneficiaries will be targeted according to need and vulnerability. Partners will distribute winterisation items according to the sector MEB, or using previous Winterisation NFI examples, being aware of protection risks for women, girls, boys and men. Monitoring and evaluation activities, including a PDM will take place appropriately.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat - Mitigating Measure: A) Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members 1. Agree to minimum assistance package across sector.2. Assess and validate any changes before they occur.3. Identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions. B) Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees 1. Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. 2. Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. C) Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion) 1. Sector partners ensure that their staffs have read/are aware of the Code of Conduct.2. Sector partners develop a range of measures and internal procedures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation and act accordingly. D) Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety) 1. Well organized distribution to avoid overcrowding.2. Training to staffs on establishing and managing distributions.3. Pre-consultations with communities. 4. Ensure access to distribution points by all groups taking into consideration people with specific needs. 5. Application of checklist at distribution sites. E) The risk towards the humanitarian nature of the intervention for the presence and activities of military and non-humanitarian actors. 1. Pre conciliation with community leaders F) Lack of accepted documentation may unfairly exclude certain population. 1. People are informed about their rights to receive assistance regardless of the documents they have.2. Training of the distribution staff about the rights of affected population to receive assistance. 3. Working with local religious and community leaders in order to ensure alternative documentation or approvals for those without formal documentation.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk/Threat & Mitigation Measures: 1. Conflict resumes in project area: Due to the fluid nature of the conflict it is not impossible for fighting to resume in multiple areas. However, the majority of the implementation areas have been stable for a significant period of time now and are deemed to stay that way for the foreseeable future. Should conflict resume, partner, staff and beneficiary well-being would be paramount and any project activities would be delayed or ceased should there be any danger to stakeholders. 2. Partner cannot complete project: This project has been selected specifically and tailored in order to meet the ability and capacity of partners. Support has been and will continue to be given in order to equip them with everything they need to implement the project. 3. Government/authorities close/delay project: It is not unknown for authorities to close/delay projects. This project will be implemented only full approval from SARC and any relevant GoS Ministries. Many partners have experience in this sector. Open communications exist between WRG and government authorities.
