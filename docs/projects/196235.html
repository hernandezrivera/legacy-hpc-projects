Project Summary

The humanitarian intervention intends to address the dire needs of communities affected by flood and sub national conflict through provision of emergency shelter and nonfood items in Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar, Koch, Panyikang, , Ayod, Nyirol, Fangak and Pigi counties. According Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) (2022) an approximately 2.4 million people in South Sudan are in needs of NFI across 10 state.
IRNA conducted between September to October 2021 in Fangak and Pigi counties revealed that, up to 85%of the total population displaced with limited access to shelter and non-food items and experienced few protection concerns among others. IRNAs and households surveys, conducted by Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) and other humanitarian partners in Panyijiar, Leer Mayendit and Ayod, confirmed, displacement of thousand households to the highest grounds without standard and light shelters. Sub national conflict continue to caused mass displacement and worsened humanitarian situation in the target locations. Communal violence exacerbated by the cattle raiding and revenge killing, continued to be worsening and increased suffering in children, women, men, boys and girls. This eventually triggered Lack of enough shelters and non-food items to enable affected persons live a dignified life. It becomes a matter of greater concern and requires to be sufficiently address and mitigated. Women, elderly, persons living with disability and children are compromised to high risks of water borne diseases: Malaria, Typhoid, cholera and e.tc. The total number of persons affected are 10,812 HHS translated to 64,872 individuals and can be broken down as follows: Leer=8109 Koch=8109 Ayod=8109, Fangak=8109, Panyijiar=8109, Mayendit=8109, Panyikang=8109
This humanitarian situation in the counties mentioned, as evidenced by IPC report 2021, has worsened as result of men made and natural catastrophes, leaving more vulnerable to make up their living. Community coping mechanisms has been weaken, as other options are not attainable. Their houses were submerged, cattle either raided by gunmen or killed by the flood, agricultural crops damaged, households items lost and agricultural lands washed away. The project will provide shelter and non-food items for displaced people in situations of emergency and transition in an ability-challenged accessible, gender-responsive manner address the needs of the affected persons through provision of emergency shelters&non-food items and indeed consider community awareness using tailored messages through establishment Complaint feedback mechanisms and encourage participatory inclusion of all population groups in decision-making processes throughout the project cycle. Formation of AAP committee will be formed and trained to in order champion the accountability work and ensure each population group is represented in the decision making process throughout the period of project life.

1 - Needs

Displacement of population and the aftermath looting of their properties, harassments and abuses of communities, have tangled humanitarian situation in Unity and jonglei states. Crises associated to the natural disasters or flooding and subnational conflict reinforced by revenge killings, left many people vulnerable. Heavy rainfalls supported by flooding from the river Nile and its tributaries, redoubled and increased water volume, which eventually submerged human shelters and the subsequence losses of households items, during long walk to the highest ground and nearest islands. Few GBV and protection concerns were reported to have posed threats to the dignity of women and girls. Returnees coming homes are facing land challenges worsened enough by the presence of secondary occupants in the lands. Rule of law , or judiciary system to redress GBV and land issues, in this country, has been paralyzed due to huge presence of small arms in the hands of bandits and unauthorized elements. HNO 2022, revealed 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021 and 1.9 million women, girls, boys and men, will further suffer from the lack enough shelters while 2.3 million will need NFIS. A continuation and increase in sub-national violence reported in many states across the country, including Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile,will likely continue to disrupt livelihoods and trade and contribute to an increase in humanitarian needs in 2023. IRNA carried out by Hope Restoration South (HRSS) with humanitarian partners, in Mayendit and Leer between June and July 2022, found that half or more shelters owned by community, were submerged. While civil war and communal violence intensified amid disaster and compromised lives of children, elderly, women, girls and men to higher vulnerability, including protection risks. Water borne diseases such typhoid, malaria and cholera are conceivable and exceedingly threatening. Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) and other static partners in Panyijiar county also conducted IRNA, which highlighted 85% of the total population were displaced by floods and conflict and without shelters and Nonfood items and experienced few GBV and protection concerns. In this contextual analysis, Humanitarian situation described in Jonglei and unity states, is very big concerns which needs humanitarian intervention to address the dire needs through provision of emergency shelters and nonfood items to the disaster and conflict affected persons. These items must be flood kits such as: plastic sheets, blankets, mosquito nets, bundle of rubber ropes and reusable facemask to mitigate virus infections during distribution exercises. Hope Restoration South Sudan(HRSS) will form accountability to the affected population committee(AAP) committee members in efforts to increase community participation in the humanitarian response. Committee will be drawn from population groups to form AAP committee members. Hope Restoration South Sudan(HRSS) will establish complaint feedback desk in and around distribution points to track beneficiaries feedback about the satisfaction and dissatisfaction felt about modalities employed during the process. The feedback will inform the program team to adjust modalities or reform process and realign it in favor of beneficiaries complaints. Hope Restoration South Sudan(HRSS) will address Lack of independence instruments to address the stalemates arising illegal occupation of lands and return it to rightful owners. The project will coordinate with relevant authorities and other actors to support the rule of laws and ensure that land crises in the project locations addressed once and for all.

2 - Project activities

Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) will conduct ES/NFI need analysis with involvement of other static partners to establish ES/NFI needs of the targeted population and identify project beneficiaries. Shelters and NFI needs will be done to justify evolving needs and inform the pipeline request as per shelter &NFI tools and methodologies. It will also take up community consultations needs assessment at the project location which will target community representatives of population groups in its entirety. These will include; representative of local authorities, affected households, opinion leaders, cultural leaders or local chiefs, church leaders, representative of persons living with disability, persons living HIV&AIDS, Women leaders, youth leaders and other stakeholders in the targeted locations to work as focal and entry point to the project beneficiaries which allows smooth implementation of the project activities. Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) will form AAP Committee in each location and trained AAP members in all project locations. Members of (AAP) will be drawn from community representatives taking into consideration issues to do with gender balance in the selections; Hope restoration will also establishment and strengthening of complaint feedback Desk: The Complaint feedback desk will track beneficiaries feed backs about the level of satisfaction in regards to service provisions at the site. HRSS will identify,register and verified target beneficiaries of individuals in all locations. following the shelter and non-food items cluster selection criteria. Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) will develop distribution plan, which will involve selection of distribution site and establish distribution timeline and share beneficiaries and distribution of SNFI will address critical SNFI needs of the affected population. NFI SFP, ES/NFI coordination team and pipeline team. Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) will champion community engagement awareness campaigns through enumerators, community mobilizers and l HRSS will under take Post Distribution Monitoring(PDM) assessment in all the target location.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Hope Restoration Sudan (HRSS) will form accountability to the affected population committee (AAP) committee members to compliment coordination and accountability work throughout the project cycle. Accountability to the affected population(AAP) committee members shall be drawn from all population groups such as: women leaders, representatives of persons living with disability or permanent illness, church leaders or religious representatives, representatives of youths, local chiefs, local authorities, of vulnerable members including persons with disabilities, women and girls, older persons and persons with chronic illnesses, cultural leaders, opinion leaders or teachers and representatives of persons living without effective community links. Hope Restoration South Sudan(HRSS) will hold consultative meetings with community representatives: representatives of the affected households, local authorities, opinion leaders, cultural leaders or local chiefs, representatives of persons living with disability, women representatives, religious leaders, and other stakeholders in the project locations to serve as focal and entry points for activities implementation. Hope Restoration South Sudan(HRSS) will engage in consultative meetings, in each location. There will be participants per location. The participants will compose of 2 beneficiaries’ representatives, 3 women representatives, local authorities, opinion leaders, church leaders, youths, representatives of persons living with disability per location and 1 host community representative. These meetings are meant to mobilize the stakeholders and create awareness to the beneficiaries about the scope of the project, expectations and share what is agreed upon the criteria for selecting and targeting the beneficiaries. The meetings will be facilitated by the SNFIs project manager, SNFI Project Officer assisted b SNFIs Assistants officers and community mobilizers in all the locations. The stakeholder mobilization meeting will last for 6 days in all the locations. Hope Restoration South Sudan(HRSS) will also observe Covid-19 protocols of physical distancing of sitting 2 meters apart, no hand shakings, no hugging, washing of hands with soap and water.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

First thing first; Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) staffs sign professional code of conducts and policies, which involves: Prevention of sexual (PSEA) exploitation and abuses, child safeguarding and conflict of interest. Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) orients all volunteers or all incentive staffs on PSEA which considers sexual exploitation as grave misconducts treats with zero tolerance when arises. All staffs, volunteers including AAP committee members and enumerators involved in the distribution, will undergo induction and orientation, before taking up the duties. We will also consult with population groups to decide over important decisions such as distribution sites time and date. Distribution will kick off in a broad daylight and separate queues will be arranged for girls, boys, women and men to avoid sexual abuses assaults and harassment. Distribution will take place in an area very far military barracks and its associates such as: armed youths and other unruly bandits posing threats to the dignity of girls and women receiving NFIS

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

HRSS will use Community-Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) approach which is a system blending both formal and informal community structures, where individuals will be able and encouraged to safely reporting. Local communities will be involved in developing and approving the CBCM so that the structure is both culturally and gender-sensitive. The mechanism should have multiple entry points, allowing both beneficiaries and staff the opportunity to report at the organizational level – internally through the network’s and field agency focal points at the community level. Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) will ensure that inclusive participation of all stakeholders and in particular the beneficiaries at each stage of this project implementation. Participation will be enhanced through conducting participatory needs assessment in an inclusive and participatory manner and consultations of key beneficiary representatives in decision making processes. Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) will establish complaint feedback desk in and around distribution point to track beneficiaries’ feedback on the modality employed and how they felt satisfied or dissatisfied about the assistance and the process. However, feedback from the project beneficiaries’ inform the project team or the organization to adjust as per beneficiaries’ views and consume as a lesson learned.
