Project Summary

CARE proposes an integrated approach to sexual and reproductive health and gender-based violence that will reach 81,263 people living in Camps 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 and Palongali Host Community in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh. The project will provide sexual and reproductive health services and undertake prevention and response to gender-based violence activities. The project activities are informed by CARE’s on-going gender analysis and operational expertise and are adapted to meet the differing needs of men, women, boys and adolescent girls.

Needs

Since August 2017, a massive refugee influx into Bangladesh has occurred as a result of violence in Rakhine State, Myanmar. The total refugee population has reached 914,998 people (UNHCR, September 2019). Over the past two years, humanitarian actors have sought to meet their basic needs. However, significant needs remain unmet due to the scale of the crisis and insufficient funding. This means that households are left highly vulnerable, as many lack essential services in what is now the largest refugee camp in the world, Cox’s Bazar. The refugees remain without access to a durable solution to their current circumstances, with neither the opportunity to return safely to Myanmar nor the right to work in Bangladesh. This is causing increasing tensions and security concerns at community and government levels, affecting refugee and host communities (Ground Truth Solutions, June 2019), as well as NGOs’ ability to operate. Women and children, together making up 80% of the total camp population, are particularly vulnerable in the camps (JRP, 2019). The vast majority of reported GBV cases are against women aged 18-60 and take place at the survivor’s residence (CARE, July 2019; GBVIMS, September 2019). Adolescent girls are particularly restricted in the camps, and require targeted services to ensure their meaningful participation (ISCG gender profile, March 2019). One third of households in the camps are identified as having specific needs (UNHCR, August 2019), with female-headed households, the elderly and those with people with disabilities requiring targeted assistance in accessing services to meet those needs. Recent assessment targeting refugees indicated, 57% were unable to identify any of the GBV service points, while 85% mentioned ‘Majihis’ as their preferred point of referral for cases of sexual violence showing a need for more GBV interventions in the camps.The 2019 JRP is currently 34% funded. While the protection sector is 28% funded. This leaves critical gaps in ensuring the health and security of vulnerable populations in the camps. The 2019 JRP highlights that gender-based violence (GBV) prevention and response, as well as sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services are essential to keeping women and girls safe and healthy in the refugee camps. Women largely continue to give birth at home, with low levels of awareness and interest in SRHR services including family planning, particularly amongst women (CARE, July 2019). There is common acknowledgement of the need for integrated services, including GBV and SRH (JRP, 2019; Joint agency gender analysis, 2018). While there are now more delivery facilities available in the camps, significant cultural barriers continue to prevent women and girls from travelling to static clinics, particularly for SRH services. In addition, the region suffers from a harsh monsoon, unstable terrain, and deteriorating roads and pathways. These challenges further prevent women and children from receiving required health and protection services. Beneficiary accountability is embedded in all CARE's emergency response and interventions. CARE’s feedback/ complaint mechanism has been developed in line with CARE's quality and accountability framework and operational context. CARE raises awareness and training on PSEA to staff, volunteers, beneficiaries, visitors and others. CARE equally displays PSEA IEC materials in the offices, WGSS and distribution centers with clear instructions on how and where to report PSEA cases. Internally CARE has a strong PSEA policy as well as reporting and investigation mechanism.
