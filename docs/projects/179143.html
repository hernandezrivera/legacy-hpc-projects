Project Summary

HNAP is a joint-UN assessment initiative, which tracks displacement and return movements, conducts sector and multi-sectoral assessments, and monitors humanitarian needs inside the Syrian Arab Republic. The project will contribute to the coordination sector by providing effective coordination support at hub and WoS levels, facilitating a timely and evidence-based response informed by enhanced needs assessments and information management with a focus on strengthening the response capacity of national humanitarian actors.

4 - Needs

More than 10 years since the start of conflict, the humanitarian needs and locations of the affected population inside Syria have been clearly identified and prioritized geographically, demographically and by access status. Resident population, IDP, returnee and humanitarian need datasets are available and regularly updated at community-level across Syria (9,024 communities). HNAP has been the most widely used source of data to inform the humanitarian community’s strategic planning and response. This project continue the collection of time-sensitive, impartial, geographically specific and independent data that will be shared with UN clusters and humanitarian partners, through the following:1. IDP, returnee and resident population data (critical for the humanitarian community to monitor population movements on a monthly basis, have updated population figures monthly and twice a year to guide programming),2. Conducting multisectoral needs assessments, providing sectorial-relevant data that is disaggregated by sex, age, and disability as well as by other indicators of vulnerability.3. Supporting the Humanitarian Program Cycle with data on specific thematic areas, such as the intentions of IDPs in Syria or socio-economic conditions of returnees within a given year through regular household surveys and nationwide assessments of disability prevalence.4. Support to sector-specific needs assessments (e.g. WASH, Shelter, education, etc.).5. Regular Assessments of COVID-19 mitigation measures, access to services and monitoring of transit points status.6. Ad-hoc assessments in areas affected by critical developments.

5 - Implementation of activities

HNAP conducts regular assessments and surveys using primary data collection methods, including direct interviews with local key informants (KIs), household surveys, and direct observations at community level throughout the Syrian Arab Republic. It is now the sole source of information for more than 85% of communities inside Syria (compared to approximately 80% last year). The following activities will be conducted as part of this project: • Daily, monthly, quarterly and needs-based data collection across all communities in Syria (100% coverage); • Data entry, cleaning, and analysis; • Production of reports based on collected and analysed data; • Circulation of products with humanitarian partners, including OCHA, UN agencies, NGOs and donors via email and through HNAP’s password-protected website; • Providing IDP and Population taskforce and CCCM cluster with updates on population movements (monthly), as well as baseline figures disaggregated by population group (quarterly). On an annual basis HNAP provides sex/age disaggregated data to the population task-force which is used for the HPC cycle. • Coordination with humanitarian partners on a regular basis; • Conducting needs assessments, as well as sector and multi-sectorial needs assessments in order to feed the HNO and HRP • Training of field staff and humanitarian partners on needs assessments, household surveys and thematic or location-specific assessments; • Focus on returnee data, with emphasis on developing regression model to better predict return movements. • In relation to COVID-19, HNAP will continue regular assessments with relevant stakeholders.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: 1. Physical safety of respondents/humanitarian staff in asking sensitive questions and working in dangerous locations. 2. Risk of staff being exposed to primary and secondary trauma without resources or training for self-care. 3. Risk of receiving distorted information due to communities' expectations of a specific type or level of response, as well as the communities’ perceptions of humanitarian staff expectations of the information required to be received. 4. Compromised quality of trainings due to the lack of opportunities for face to face trainings and measuring the impact of trainings which are delivered remotely. 5. Lack of awareness of the importance of protection/GBV mainstreaming among the staff; 6. Protection concerns of individuals due to collection of personal data during interview/assessments. Mitigation Measures: 1. Do No Harm considerations will be integrated into survey/assessment questions; IPs to conduct daily security briefings informing enumerators of high-risk areas and areas to avoid; 2. Training of staff in survivor-centred approach and basic psycho-social first aid (PFA), and duty of care for local partners budgeted; 3. Enumerators to continue to provide consistent information to communities on the scope of the assessment to make intent clear; 4. Delivery of trainings and capacity building initiatives will be strengthened by training key resource people to act as trainers/facilitators inside Syria; 5. Enhance the sensitization on Protection and GBV mainstreaming through mandatory trainings. Ensure adequate assessment approaches and interviewing techniques are applied; 6. Providing personal information is always optional. HNAP data is always anonymized; no personal information is shared.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: 1. The security situation in Syria is the most prominent risk for this project. Changes in the situation and actors controlling some areas might affect the access to some locations and could also impact the personal security of the field staff. Assumptions: 1. Security conditions in Syria will continue to be conducive for enumerators to carry out assessments without facing personal security risks. 2. The security conditions will also continue to be conducive for communities to engage in answering assessment questions and feel safe to do so. 3. Key informants will continue to provide information which is representative of the needs of all community members including men, women, boys, girls, persons living with disabilities and elderly persons. 4. Effective engagement with communities and completion of assessments will contribute to a more appropriate and effective humanitarian programming for the WoS response. Mitigation measures: 1. The security situation will continue to be actively monitored by team leaders in coordination with the field coordinator of implementing partner with close coordination with program management in Gaziantep. 2. Data is always encrypted to minimize risk faced by enumerators. Kobo and other tools are used to avoid dependence on paper forms and there is no storage of data in any form at the field level.3. All newly hired enumerators are given self-care training to build their capacity in dealing with issues arising from being exposed to primary and secondary trauma. This training is included as part of the induction and provided by the field coordinator, team leader and support staff in Gaziantep. For current staff, refresher courses are also provided.
