Project Summary

The action aims at strengthening health care facilities (HCFs) preparedness and support resilience building in the face of C-19 outbreak, protracted threats and chronic vulnerability especially in terms of WASH and Health. This project is aligned with the WASH allocation strategy, which aims to ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely-managed access to suﬃcient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians through the construction/ rehabilitation of WASH facilities in HCFs (handwashing, latrines, sewer system) and the provision of environmental and personal cleaning kits in HCFs”. WASH in HCFs is prioritized as a necessary input to achieve all global and national health goals especially those linked to Universal Health Coverage.
The actions will support directly the public health by enforcing the main HCF to face overload due the C-19 outbreak, to control the spread of C-19, and decrease the mortality rate in-out patients and protect the very high risky patients and people from being infected. This also will give the main HCF extra capacity to handle with all types of people not only C-19 patients. It will ensure that no patient is deprived of receiving medical services due to the health sector's preoccupation with responding to C-19. The project will ensure a sustainable access to safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene for all. The project responds to UNICEF strategy 2016-2030, aiming at every health facility should have safely managed reliable water, sanitation, hygiene facilities and practices that meet staff and patient needs by 2030. Furthermore, this project will contribute to the Strategic Objectives 2 and 3 of the HRP 2021. This project will work for mitigating threats and improving preparedness for potential outbreaks. Effective sanitation, hygiene and infection prevention measures are one of the ﬁve objectives of the WHO Global Action Plan on antimicrobial resistance and minimizing the risk of health care-associated infections (HCAIs). Lastly, this project will increase IPC measures in HCF's, respecting WHO/UNICEF/JMP (Joint Monitoring Program) indicators enhance the resilience of vulnerable people increasing their access to safe, affordable and reliable WASH services inside the most vulnerable health institutions in Gaza.
The proposed project envisages rehabilitation of the WASH facilities inside the Ten main

Hospitals in the Gaza Strip (GS). The speciﬁc objective is to enforce sustainable improvements in the WASH and services in HCF. The project will target Ten HCFs: 1) Indonesian, 2)AlShifa, 3)Shohada AlAqsa, 4)European, 5)Nasser, 6)AlNasser Pediatrics (NPH), 7)Ophthalmic (OH) 8)Al-Rantissi specialized (RSH) 9)Psychiatric (PSH) 10) AlNajjer. The project will improve the access of quality WASH and Health services for an average 1,480,000 people served by the ten hospitals annually as per the health cluster HeRAMS Dashboard including 260.000 Children, 720,000 Female and 500,000 Male including 261,000 chronic patients. The priority needs and interventions of the project were built based on key informant interviews (KIIs) with personnel in MoH and the Ten hospitals conducted jointly by WW-GVC and Qatar Red Crescent (QRC), according to gender and protection approach. The needs include: 1) improved access to safe water through rehabilitation of the aged water infrastructure upgrading the water systems including wells, desalination plants and others 2) Rehabilitation of sanitation services for staff and patients, 3) Rehabilitation and Upgrading of hygiene and environmental cleaning infrastructure including installation of medical waste collection area, 4) capacity- building activities targeting health and non-health providers on WASH, WASH in health Emergency preparedness and response plan (EPRP) and infection prevention and control (IPC) with provision of essential supplies, equipment

1 - Needs

The health system is struggling to cope with the ongoing inﬂux of C-19 outbreak, while infrastructure, human resources, and the referral system remains only partially functional after 14 years of blockade while the recent acute emergency events have required prioritisation of emergency interventions. In 2019, PWA has witnessed a deterioration of the WASH infrastructures in institutions, mainly in public HCFs (HNO 2020). WW-GVC in cooperation with UNICEF and by ECHO fund conducted a WASH assessment in 2019 targeting 21 MoH run HCFs in the GS. The study used triangulation of qualitative and quantitative information. It used face-to-face interviews with key personnel in each HCF including the medical and/or nursing director, admin director, IPC committee member and head of engineering and maintenance department .The assessment was based on WHO/UNICEF/JMP indicators for water, sanitation hygiene, environmental cleaning and medical waste, highlighted critical deﬁciencies in WASH services and infrastructure, which endangers the quality of health services, especially for immunocompromised patients. The assessment enforced the needs of sustainable improvements in the WASH infrastructures inside HCFs with the need to establishing a WASH in health capacity building and monitoring system. The data were analysed in line with indicators that assessed the availability and quality of WASH services showing that the majority of services in ten HCF, is limited, while other is basic. and showing the following: 1) JMP Core indicators for availability of basic WASH services in HCFs, 2) Advanced indicators from WASH in HCFs (WHO WASH FIT), 3) Investment’s costs in the WASH infrastructure (an estimation for the cost of investments in WASH infrastructure rehabilitation). it included KII with key personnel in the (MoH), (PWA) (CMWU). More over KII with key personnel in Al Nasser medical compound’s four hospitals were conducted in Sep 2020 by WW-GVC and QRC to agree on priority emerging WASH needs. Indonesian Hospital serves an average of 120K annually in N.Gaza, Alshifa is covering Gaza and the referral cases by all governorates, it serves an average of 350K annually. Shohada Al-Aqsa is the main Hospital in M.Area serving 170K, European, Nasser and Alnajjar Hospitals are serving an average of 450K annually. NPH & RSH are the main pediatrics hospitals in Gaza & referals from N.Gaza and M.Area serving an average of 108K (53000 girls, 55000 boys) annually, including Neonatal Intensive care unit (NICU), Paediatrics ICU, haemodialysis and the only oncology center in the GS noting that women are the only companions of children in the hospitals. OH is serving an average of 132K annually (64920 women and 67080 men mainly chronic patients with non-communicable diseases). PSH is the only hospital providing inpatient psychological services for an average of 5000 cases annually (2450 women, 2550 men). Adequate WASH conditions in the Ten hospitals are fundamental to reduce the preventable causes of morbidity and mortality by minimizing the risk of HCAIs, improve staff satisfaction, patient care-seeking behaviour, especially in C-19 outbreak. The priority needs to be addressed are taking into consideration the special needs of staff, patients and companions mainly women, children and PWDs as follows: (i) Unsafe drinking and domestic water due to poor water quality (high salinity) which is eroding the aged water infrastructure and damaging the desalination plants beside the non-availability hot water in the critical departments and non-availability of regular water quality monitoring system (ii) Lack of segregated and enough toilets for staff and patients, women and men as well as non- availability of adapted toilets for PWDs and children in any of the 10 hospitals, which plays a signiﬁcant role in promoting gender-based violence (GBV) against women. According to interviewed women in the design phase of the project, it was indicated that lack of separated toilets would contribute in increasing the risk of forms of psychological and physical abuse. For instance, the substandard bathrooms were found to undermine privacy, safety and security that increases the risk of women being subjected to abuse, which also negatively impacts the overall sense of safety and dignity for women and PWDs accessing toilets in these hospitals. (iii) Poor hygiene and environmental cleaning infrastructure especially in inpatient departments, daily care department, outpatient departments with lack of designated patient waiting areas. (iv) Lack of safe and protected designated areas for medical waste collection and storage which is of high public health concern especially in the situation of C-19 outbreak, (v) Lack of WASH in health EPRP, except Alshifa Hospital, and regular capacity building programs for health and non-health providers on WASH and IPC. Further, WW-GVC conducted WASH in Health EPRP in Al-Shifa Hospital in 2020. The assessment outlined the capital WASH interventions that need to be implemented so as to extend the operational options of the hospital and avoid being limited to some solo or only-option scenario that could be leading to major WASH services shutdown or signiﬁcant interruption which in turn may lead to health services interruption

2 - Activities

The project intends to improve access to WASH Services inside key public health institutions in Gaza governorates through enforcing sustainable improvement in WASH infrastructure inside Ten main HCF’s Ten hospitals in Gaza target all governorates served and contributes to improve the public health conditions and provide more digniﬁed services of all Gazan people especially women, children, elders and PWDs. At the start of the project, a detailed needs assessment will be conducted in cooperation with QRC and MoH in order to prioritize and conﬁrm needs and speciﬁcations. WW-GVC’s vast experience on implementing WASH and capacity building projects will target the ten hospitals through: 1) (WW-GVC) Upgrading water systems services including Rehabilitation of 13 water wells, rehabilitation of 20 desalination plants and installation of one new desalination plant with a capacity of 60 m3 in NPH, taking in consideration international and national standards for brine water management. The intervention will include installation/rehabilitation of 75 solar heating systems for critical departments inside the HCFs, such as the intensive care units (ICUs), operating theatres and patients’ areas. The action includes provision of water quality testing instruments to engineering and IPC departments, such as pH, TDS and free chlorine testing kits, that will permit to monitor quality and safeness of the water supply system.2) (WW-GVC) Rehabilitation of sanitation services in the 10 HCFs including maintenance of 327 already existing toilets and construction of 4 new toilets, and 46 toilets will be adapted for PwDs. The works will take in consideration the clear segregation and equipping of toilets to meet the needs of women, men from staff, patients and companions as well as the special needs of PWDs and children as recommended by the JMP, in order to improve the access to safe and equitable WASH services, decrease the risk of GBV cases, and prioritize safety and dignity of the under-served and most vulnerable groups (women, children, PWDs) . For GBV component: it is in line with HRP objective 1: “The rights of Palestinians living under occupation, including those living under the blockade and other restrictions, are protected, respected, and promoted in accordance with IHL and IHRL” 3) (WW-GVC) Construction of 10 safe and protected medical waste collection and storage zone for the ten hospitals, and rehabilitation/construction of 501 hand washing basins and/or alcohol-based hand rub wall mounted dispensers. This will include rehabilitation inside inpatient, daily care, pharmacy and pharmaceutical store and outpatient areas with provision of patient beds, companion´s resting chairs, ventilation, paintings, essential supplies and equipment to maintain IPC standards. (4( (WW-GVC) Develop WASH in health EPRPs in 9 hospitals to guarantee that the WASH preparedness actions are undertaken at the HCFs with the aim to ensure resilience and response mechanisms able to deal with potential disasters. The activity will also include conduction of Capacity building for 565 health and non-health key staff on WASH in health EEPRP in 9 hospitals. (5.(QRC) is a leading INGO in health and WASH sectors and will be responsible for implementing interventions to address gaps in hygiene and environmental cleaning conditions through provision of 1695 environmental and personal cleaning kits and personal protective equipment (PPE) and supplying of 1297 cover mattresses. The hygiene material support and dignity kits will consider the speciﬁc hygiene needs for women and girls particularly teenagers, pregnant, elderly and women with disabilities .6)(QRC) will establish a regular based auditing system through assigning and training focal persons in each hospital concerning IPC measures and break the transition of C-19 based on guidelines and WASH C-19 Response Guidance. Advocacy for WASH in Health inside HCFs will include hosting an event for 5th of May (the world hand hygiene day) targeting health and non-health staff. Intervention will be including development of the necessary IEC materials and a video ﬁlm to document all phases of the project from the design till the ﬁnal stage and advocate for the WASH in health. In the initial phase of the project, coordination will be established with the WASH in health working group which is led by PWA and facilitated by WW-GVC and WASH cluster with technical support from the MoH and WHO to ensure coordination, monitoring and following up the implementation of the activities.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The no harm principle is assured by the whole implementation of the project to prevent any negative impact of the action and guaranteeing the safety and dignity for the beneﬁciaries. WW-GVC and QRC will ensure the addressing of the needs of the beneﬁciaries since the ﬁrst phase of in depth assessment, considering special needs for women, girls, boys, elderlies and special patients. All the activities proposed will be designed according to JMP standards and will meet the needs of the patients of the hospitals, guaranteeing a positive effect on the quality of the service and an improvement on the condition of the beneﬁciaries themselves. The action will enhance the privacy and safety of women, through segregation of toilets between men and women and securing doors, having a great impact on reduction of risk of GBV cases, addressing child protection, and supporting the wellbeing of elderlies and people with disabilities.The installation of safe and protected medical waste collection and storage zone will ensure the proper storage of medication and will reduce the risk of infection inside the clinics. Furthermore, the action will consider different needs, priority and cases in the provision of medical equipment guaranteeing items that match with meaningful access and that respect the cultural values of the PwDs, children and caretakers, women and men. Besides, training and awareness session will be tailored based on the result of the initial in-depth assessment to address the main problems risen up by the same beneﬁciaries. Medical and non medical staff will be trained on WASH in health Emergency preparedness and response plan and IPC training and on job coaching to guarantee the sustainability of the action and empower the capacity of the medical complex to cope with emergency. Save of the environment will be considered with proper protective measure as the rehabilitation of the ﬁre central ﬁreﬁghting system and the stormwater drainage system. Upgrading water systems services and rehabilitation /installing desalination plants will ensure clean water to all the patients decreasing the risk of hygiene infections and the presence of back up source during emergencies.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

WW-GVC and QRC have conducted a thorough context analysis in order to prioritize the targeted interventions that will impact the lives of the most vulnerable population in the GS. The chronic emergency situation has shifted the humanitarian needs to prioritize interventions to respond to the emergency on the expense on other vulnerable groups: people with chronic diseases including children, women and PWDs other than trauma patients. Based on the WASH assessment conducted by WW-GVC in partnership with UNICEF in 2020, 80% of the HCFs doesn’t have adapted toilets for PWDS and 100% doesn’t have adapted toilets for children even in pediatric departments. While the clear segregation of toilets between women and men (patients and staff) with a signage was not ensured in more than 80% of the assessed toilets. Such specification is representing the WASH in health sanitation core indicator for the WHO-UNICEF joint monitoring program. The continued deterioration of WASH facilities in HCFs raises serious concerns about resources, the ability to respond and to deal with emergencies, which have serious consequences on the health status of patients, especially children, women, the elderly and PwDs. The rehabilitation /installation of the desalination plants will ensure clean water with a consequent positive impact on beneficiaries’ health in terms of morbidity and mortality and on cost of services due to prolonged hospital stay. Thus, the proposed project was designed to ensure the improvement access of women, men, girls, boys and PWDs to WASH service inside the hospital. The new standards will meet the core indicators for WASH in Health as defined by the WHO- UNICEF joint monitoring program taking in consideration the necessary needed interventions to improve access of women, children and PWDs to WASH services. Also, the needs assessment on which the project was built took in consideration the needs of women staff and staff with disabilities (who represent 2% of the staff working in the hospitals) regarding the availability and number of sanitation services including to construction of new sanitation services. The action also takes in consideration not only the patients but also the companions who are in the majority women to ensure safety and dignity during the treatment journey and hospital stay through rehabilitation and upgrading of the hygiene and environmental standards inside the inpatient and outpatient areas. Active participation of women staff and patients will be ensured in all the capacity building activity and community empowerment trough a feedback mechanism and by several key informant interview to detect the different needs, role and dimensions of the targeted beneficiaries.
