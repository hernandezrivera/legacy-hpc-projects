Project Summary

Education is not only a basic human right for all but also plays vital role on poverty reduction and development of social life. Formal Education Network for Private Schools (FENPS) has a vision of a society where all children have access to quality education and are not subjected to any abuse. Its mission is to uplift children and allow them to grow succeed and realize their full potential through guidance, education and training in secure, caring environments that are conducive to learning. FENPS currently runs Education in Emergency projects in which over 3,000 conflicts and crisis affected children (48% girls) are benefiting in Banadir region. FENPS project will contribute to durable solution for IDPs through providing children with sustained and continued education and helping vulnerable community receive safe learning environment for their children.
However, vast numbers of Somali children are still encountering barriers to quality education and necessities of their basic human rights because of one reason or the other. The goal of FENPS program is to take a role in addressing the particular needs of especially vulnerable groups in society, for example young girls in general, children belonging to internally displaced people, refugees, returnees, the disabled and poor children from deprived backgrounds, who often lack education, health care, protection and livelihood opportunities.
The specific purpose of this project is to reach 4,500 vulnerable out of school children (50% girls) through construction and rehabilitation of classrooms, building the capacity 100 teachers (65 male and 35 female) and 112 community education committees (56 female and 56 male), provision of teaching and learning materials, WASH facilities, safe drinking water and emergency school feeding, conducting awareness on hygiene promotion and child protection mapping activities at 16 life-saving temporary learning spaces in IDP settlements in Banadir and Lower Shabelle regions of Somalia.
The FENPS program is aligned with HRP 2020 and its activities complement to recovery and resilient national plans that humanitarian and development partners are implementing with the leadership of Somali government.

Project Indicators

PROBLEMS:Education Cluster needs assessment conducted in June 2019 report that over 12,316 children (5,542 boys and 6,774 girls) were out of school in 83 IDP camps assessed in Banadir region.FENPS survey conducted in June 2019 at 6 IDP camps in Marka and Qoryoley of Lower Shabele region reported that over 6,630 school age children (50% girls) in the visited areas were lacking access to education. There was no clean drinking water in the visited camps except little fetched on human back about 2 to 3 kilometres far from the IDP settlements. Education challenges and barriers reported were different from one child to another but all have had hard to access to quality education in their locations. The main barrier to access education reported by the interviewed children included lack of school fees, work for the family, far distance to school, girls taking care siblings for their mothers, lack of school or lack of teachers in their areas.UNICEF Somalia Humanitarian Action for Children 2019 highlights that 3,566 children, including 569 girls, reported to be victims of violence committed by parties to conflict between January and September 2018. August 2019 report issued by Save the Children also indicates that there were verified abductions that affected about 2,500 children over half of which happened in Somali in 2018. The proposed project will directly support 4,000 children (2,000 girls and 2,000 boys) to help receive access to lifesaving quality education and protection opportunities in the crisis affected areas in Banadir and Lower Shabelle regions of Somalia.BENEFICIARIES:The target beneficiaries of the project are 4,500 school children (2,250 boys and 2,250 boys) who consist of 3,850 internally displaced and 650 vulnerable cost community to gather with 212 adults including 100 teachers (65 male and 35 male) and 112 community education committee members (56 male and 56 female). Mitigation Measures

For the issues related to protection of civilians, exclusion and marginalization and protection mainstreaming, FENPS will take concrete activities that will remove all barriers to quality education and basic human rights in the affected community (i.e. FENPS will prevent any interference or barriers to gender parity or exclusion of vulnerable groups). Formal Education Network for Private Schools (FENPS) will implement the program giving highest consideration to gender equality and risk mitigation in the all areas of the project stages. FENPS will provide education to 4,500 children (2,250 girls) at secure learning environments that are near to the residents of children. FENPS will consult with children especially adolescent girls and other beneficiaries of the project, and discuss with them about the safety of their travel to and presence at school. FENPS will also make first aid kits available/equipped in schools, and will train teachers on first aid including psychological first aid and safe school standards to prevent abuse and keep children safe in education. FENPS will also carry out background checks of teachers and other new personnel working with children, and will assign child protection focal point ( 1 male and 1 female) for each school who will be responsible child protection mapping for schools and supervision of children during breaks and where possible to and from school. FENPS will ensure that design of teaching and learning materials preserves the safety and dignity of girls and boys. It will ensure provide separate, secure and private latrines for boys and girls and separate facilities for students and teachers. The affected community will be engaged in the project activities while FENPS will give them mentoring and consultation to actively participate in decision-making processes of the project activities, promote their ownership sense and improve the relevance and sustainability of education response. Furthermore, FENPS will create inclusive environment where women, men, boys, girls, adolescents, children with disabilities, teachers and parents are involved in and consulted with all about project related issues to identify and respond to their education needs.The personnel of FENPS, volunteers, teachers, vendors and other persons involved in this project will be given child protection and child safe guarding guidelines. Each of these stakeholders will sign an agreement and safe guarding Code of Conduct with FENPS and responsible person from FENPS will supervise these people on the implementation of safe guarding and child protection in the protect phases.In addition to that, FENPS will conduct training workshops and awareness rising activities on cross-cutting issues that enable students and parents should know how to report any cases of exploitation and abuse, including sexual abuse. FENPS will ensure all staff, implementing partners and volunteers working with students, teachers sign and adhere to a humanitarian Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries and receive training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA)
