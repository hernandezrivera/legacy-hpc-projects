Project Summary

The project's overall objective is to contribute to strengthening the capacity of essential Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services for the most vulnerable population affected by the ongoing crisis in the Gaza Strip.
This is in line with the objectives and the framework of the OPT country 2023 HRP strategy, the WASH cluster HRP objectives, the needs identified in the humanitarian need overview (HNO), and Oxfam's recent assessments.
The project complements the HRP 2023 strategy objectives including objectives 1,2, and 3, as it promotes ”the rights of Palestinians living under occupation, including those living under the blockade and other restrictions, are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL)(Objective #1). Also, it contributes to meeting the basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation through the provision of quality basic services and contributes to improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL (objective #2 ). In addition, the project addresses objective #3, as it supports the capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome the protracted crisis, including environmental threats.
Furthermore, this project is aligned to the WASH cluster HRP Objective 1: "Palestinians living under occupation access safe and adequate WASH services with dignity", objective 2: "Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinian", and objective 3: "Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the most vulnerable Palestinians to reduce threats and cope with emergencies and shocks".
The project specifically aims at providing an appropriate, integrated WASH response for a total of 134,315 individuals (30,715 men, 30,487 women, 34,466 boys, and 38,647 girls) of vulnerable populations in the areas of eastern parts of the Gaza Strip to enhance sufficient access to WASH services. Targeting the most vulnerable groups namely; women, children, girls, and boys in fragile and critically under-served communities,
the project will be implemented with the full support of and cooperation with key local partners namely: the Palestinian Environment Friends (PEF), Palestine Save the Children Foundation (PSCF), and Al-Azhar University.
Project design, implementation, and learning will be conducted in full cooperation with key actors including the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA), Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU), Ministry of Health (MoH), Ministry of Education (MoE), and WASH sector.
After its completion, the project is expected to rehabilitate and conduct required maintenance for 9 water wells, 3 sewage and WASH facilities at 100 HHs, 3 healthcare centers, and 9 schools. In addition, the project will install 1 stormwater system, conduct a water rights campaign, provide hygiene kits, disseminate IEC materials. and develop and publish a research study on wastewater treatment issues.

1 - Needs

WASH vulnerabilities in the Gaza Strip have been driven by the ongoing blockade, Israeli restrictions, the internal Palestinian divide, May 2021 and August 2022 escalations along with the consequences of these factors. According to HNO 2023, 47% of the people in need (PIN) in oPt live in Gaza Strip and more than one-third of them are living below the poverty line. Additionally, 60% of the PIN in the Gaza Strip live in Gaza, Khan Younis, and Deir Al Balah governorates. Based on the 2022 WASH cluster vulnerability mapping, there are significant shortages of adequate water and sanitation services at the community and household (HH) levels. More than 5,500 households in Gaza Strip are not connected to the sewer networks. 41,000 households are not connected to water networks and more than 35,000 people in Gaza Strip can access only to less than 50l/c/d of domestic water. At institutional level and according to the institution's gap analysis conducted by the WASH cluster in 2021, healthcare facilities (HCFs) and schools in Gaza Strip suffer from the insufficient capacity of WASH facilities to meet the number of service users nor MoH and MoE standards. More than 72% of the HCFs don’t have functional toilets for persons with disability (PWDs) and 23% of them don’t have separate toilets for men and women. Only 23% of schools in Gaza strip have functional drinking water taps. This results in overcrowding the existing facilities and undermining hygiene practices. 794,850 Gazans reside in low-lying areas at risk of flooding events during winter due to heavy rains and overflow of stormwater facilities and sewage pumping stations. And recently, due to climate change, flooding has become more frequent and have caused significant losses of assets, disrupted access to livelihoods and services, and exacerbated the risk of waterborne diseases. Women bear the brunt of the hardship in flooded homes, as they are expected to continue fulfilling their domestic duties under these adverse conditions. The proposed WASH priority interventions were identified based on the 2023 humanitarian needs priorities, Oxfam's WASH strategy 2021-2025, Oxfam's gender analysis (July 2021), Oxfam's rapid needs assessment carried out in August 2022, and WASH cluster Vulnerability Map updated in August 2022. Those interventions were also identified as a result of coordination efforts with the WASH cluster areas focal point, Palestinian Water Authority (PWA), Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU), Ministry of Health (MoH), Ministry of Education (MoE) and Oxfam's local partners. The identified WASH interventions are classified at 3 levels: community, HHs and institutions levels. WASH along with flood mitigation and prevention actions are as follows: 1-Community-level: CMWU and Oxfam agreed to prioritize 10 critical public WASH facilities (9 wells, 1 sewage pumping station) that serve five vulnerable communities in the Gaza strip (Al Qararah, Bani Suhaila, Al Fukhari, Abasan Al Kabera, Wadi Al Salqa). In addition, they agreed to rehabilitate, and upgrade the water network at Deir Al Balah where the average domestic water supply is less than 50 (l/c/d). On the other hand, they prioritized the construction of a sewage network at Al Fukhari and a stormwater network at Abasan Al Jadida where the people are highly exposed to public health risks. PEF with Oxfam’s support carried out a technical field assessment in September 2022 to identify the specific needs of each targeted facility. The results of 6 FGDs (3 women and 3 Men) which were conducted in the targeted communities with 48 participants (15 women and 33 men), confirmed their need to rehabilitate the prioritized WASH facilities. Doing so will ensure adequate access to WASH services and will contribute to facilitating care responsibilities and house chores for women and children as they bear the brunt of having access to WASH services at HH level. 2-HHs level: Following a discussion between Oxfam and the MoSD, PWA, Action Against Hunger (AAH) , WeWorld-GVC, Islamic Relief (IR), and Save the Children in August 2022, households with outdated sanitation facilities in Alhekr community in Deir Albalah, AlShejaeya in Gaza and Alfukhari in East of Khan Younis were identified to be targeted. This is based on Oxfam and Palestine Save the Children Foundation (PSCF) rapid assessment conducted in August 2022 where 4 FGDs (2 women and 2 Men) were carried out in the targeted communities with 36 participants (20 women and 16 men) and 2 KIIs conducted with the community committee's representatives. Two of the FGDs were conducted with female-headed households (FHHs) and PWDs (1 female and 1 male). The assessment highlighted the need to improve access to adequate water and sanitation services at HH level for 300 families, 40% are FHHs and include PWDs. The assessment also found that the majority of the population has poor hygiene practices, especially in terms of water handling and hygiene measures during and after flooding Moreover, Oxfam's gender analysis (July 2021) indicated that women are mostly affected by the lack of access to safe water and sufficient sanitation services, reporting serious concerns about their children's hygiene, health, and safety. 3-Institutions level (Health care facilities and Schools): Considering the WASH in HCFs Vulnerability Mapping and WASH in schools assessment conducted by the WASH cluster during the 3rd quarter of 2022. Oxfam and PEF, in coordination with the MoH and MoE, conducted in-depth assessments during September 2022, for 3 HCFs and 9 schools serving the targeted vulnerable communities. The assessment illustrated the need to intervene in the 3 identified HCFs and the 9 schools by providing maintenance and rehabilitation works for domestic water and wastewater networks, stormwater collection systems, and WASH facilities

2 - Activities

The project will respond to the needs of targeted male and female beneficiaries in the targeted vulnerable areas, including children, the elderly, and people with disabilities. Furthermore, the proposed WASH action will provide a comprehensive and consistent contribution to strengthen people's access to dignified WASH services at each target vulnerable community. The rehabilitation and maintenance work for the critical public WASH facilities and networks will include consistent works on HHs, schools, and HCFs levels to improve the access of targeted people to sufficient WASH services. It will also include implementing flood mitigation and prevention actions to contribute to strengthening WASH preparedness and response capacity. Doing so will contribute to alleviating the burden on vulnerable women and children. In parallel, the complementary hygiene promotion and public awareness campaign will be designed and carried out in a participatory manner with the targeted community committees and HHs to enhance women, men, girls, boys, PWDs, and elderly knowledge and awareness, including outbreaks considering the COVID-19 experience. While designing and implementing Water Rights Campaign (Water Blockade) will promote access to safe and adequate WASH services with dignity. This will include the development and dissemination of policy/ advocacy materials by Oxfam and research related to new Wastewater treatment technology in partnership with Al-Azhar university. That will use the existing studies and evidence to highlight the lack of people’s access to humanitarian services, especially in poor and crowded areas, and how it affects their health, in addition, to recommending further actions for national and international actors. Oxfam will be responsible for the implementation of the action in partnership with PEF, PSCF, and relevant stakeholders to ensure access to systems, procedures, and standards to achieve effective and high-quality delivery. Oxfam's particular added value will be in its ability to bring together multiple stakeholders, local authorities, and end-users through its active participation in the WASH cluster and engagement with related stakeholders. Oxfam will also ensure action delivery in accordance with the agreed action plan and budget in compliance with the donor programmatic, visibility, reporting, financial, and procurement guidelines. Oxfam will be responsible for developing and implementing the water rights campaign considering the available and missing evidence about the impact of lack of access to water in poor and crowded areas on public health. Al-Azhar university will lead the development of wastewater treatment research in coordination with Oxfam through it is Master’s degree program. This will provide advice and support on Wastewater treatment, public health, gender, and protection-sensitive project implementation. PEF and PSCF will lead the implementation of the community mobilization actions, the beneficiaries' selection process, the technical engineering assessment, and the tenders’ announcement in local newspapers in compliance with the donor, Oxfam, and their own procurement regulations and guidelines. PEF and PSCF work closely with vulnerable communities in diverse locations, including men, women, children, youth, and PWDs. They have extensive experience in WASH and access to sustainable and safe water, and sanitation services in the Gaza strip. PSCF and PEF were fully engaged in the project's design, needs assessment, and community consultations. Both organizations will implement the project in coordination with Oxfam with joint responsibility for M&E activities and stakeholder engagement. Also, PEF will be engaged in the implementation of outputs 1.1, 3.1, and 4.1. While PSCF will be engaged in the implementation of output 2.1. On the other hand, Output 5.1 will be jointly implemented by Oxfam and Al-Azhar university. 3 - Indicators

Based on Oxfam’s Common global Approach to MEAL and Social Accountability (CAMSA) the project will integrate a comprehensive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) framework. CAMSA is an approach based on minimum requirements for MEL and Social Accountability aligned to Oxfam’s program quality efforts and provides guidance, tools, and methodologies including gender analysis and assessment of gender outcomes. Oxfam and its partners are committed to the EU data protection rules represented by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and will enforce Oxfam and its partners’ staff members to strictly consider its rules in all data processing and beneficiaries' databases including accountability mechanisms. Oxfam is also committed to participatory MEAL in which partners, service providers, project stakeholders, and beneficiaries have active and significant involvement in monitoring and evaluation actions through continuous and regular consultations throughout the entire project cycle. All targeted groups, project beneficiaries, and stakeholders are considered influencers in the design, implementation, and results of all monitoring actions including needs assessments FGDs, baseline/end-line surveys, project reviews, and evaluations. Monitoring of project actions will be conducted at two levels: Quality Monitoring: Oxfam’s MEAL staff will lead the design of the project intervention logical framework, theory of change, MEAL planning, MEAL actions tracking, project internal and external evaluations, and learning. Technical Monitoring: Oxfam WASH technical team and partners will ensure the quality of implementation in line with technical standards, action plan, and budget as well as baseline and end-line data collection. Oxfam and partners will conduct the direct implementation of the project actions and day-to-day follow-up by their field teams and experts. The MEAL framework will promote gender-sensitive collaborative and effective, results-focused, and accountable MEAL and social accountability practices throughout project implementation. End-line Assessment: the online assessment aims to identify the post-intervention status of the project's outputs and indicators. 4. Learning Event: A final learning workshop will be conducted to present and disseminate the key findings of the assessment actions including the baseline, HH verification, and the end-line. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The action, in alignment with Oxfam programming parameters, will be premised on the grounds that the lives and dignity of all women and men beneficiaries, both direct and indirect, must be protected. The main humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration, especially the do-no-harm principle. This is particularly important in the Gaza context, where social and political instability and conflict are evident in many areas with conservative protection norms. Explicit plans to reduce risks, and to competently mitigate and respond to any incidents, including safeguarding training (i.e. on sexual harassment, discrimination and exploitation, and child protection) will be elaborated by Oxfam and monitored, adjusted, and refreshed if the context changes.Oxfam is committed to following an inclusive approach in the design, implementation, and monitoring of assistance to respond to vulnerability in all its forms and reach all marginalized people (including children, youth, older people, people with disabilities, ethnic groups, and others who are marginalized due to their social status). This entails observing and working towards:Participation: We will systematically engage with all affected people and encourage active and meaningful participation of the most marginalized, including people with disabilities, to ensure that their views and capacities are reflected and accounted for in all aspects of the response including assessment, design, delivery, and monitoring and evaluation.Data: We will collect, fully disaggregate, and utilize data for different population groups in order to provide robust evidence to design, revise and learn from programming that is reflective of and appropriate for the people and needs identified.The action responds to Oxfam's and PWA's needs analysis where women highlighted the need to be engaged in community committees and municipality activities due to their severe lack of representation in public life. Community mobilization sessions on gender, water, and sanitation will be implemented, encouraging the participation of men and women community leaders. These sessions will also serve as an awareness-raising tool to sensitize households and the community to accept women's participation. This will contribute to mitigating any risk/ harm that women could face as a result of this newfound participation in public life. Gender will be mainstreamed, ensuring that the needs of men and women, girls and boys, are addressed in line with Oxfam's gender in emergencies minimum standards and gender mainstreaming strategies. This action's response is grounded on the fact that all women's and men's lives and dignity, including people with disabilities, youth, children, and the elderly, both direct and indirect, must be protected. Local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement and ongoing consultation with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the project, thus enhancing community engagement and reducing any potential protection risks or exclusion at the community level. Building the capacities of targeted communities to improve their self-protection mechanisms will be ensured by conducting orientation sessions to inform and consult with targeted communities about services that will be provided and safety measures and access modalities. It is worth mentioning that preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence and coping with stressful humanitarian situations. Supporting women's self-esteem and confidence will be positively reflected and will enhance the roles of each member of the household and will result in the overall improved well-being of the family, especially children as care receivers.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As part of the main principles of protection mainstreaming in all of Oxfam's humanitarian work, special attention is paid to achieving equal and impartial access to aid. This will be done throughout the different stages of the project; the selection of targeted communities is premised on concrete and updated needs assessments confirming the vulnerability and need. Similarly, selecting the beneficiaries follows a clear process with selection criteria and presents an equal opportunity for all eligible to benefit. Gender and age are also taken into consideration, meaning that women, men, youth, elderly people, people with disabilities, and children will be able to benefit from the intervention as fit for their needs without any discrimination based on their gender, age, or status. Using a combination of a community outreach approach, virtual platforms for information sharing, and actual work in the field will also address any obstacles related to the spread of outbreaks that could hinder access to aid. Having a feedback mechanism available and accessible for all the targeted community will facilitate knowing people's concerns and comments that could highlight obstacles to access, whether physical or related to the processes of implementing the intervention, and would inform the response to ensure equality and impartiality.
