1 - Needs

A functional common feedback mechanism (CFM) is a cornerstone of Accountability to Affected Population (AAP). Part of the CFM is having a functional Hotline in the form of a toll-free, country-wide number that affected populations can call to obtain information on humanitarian assistance programs; submit their feedback; and, get referrals to the humanitarian organizations that are best-suited to handle their issues. According to the Multisectoral Need Assessment that took place in July 2021, the findings found that 23% households in the Gaza Strip with an income of less than 200 NIS per person reported not receiving any assistance in the past 6 months. As well, 57% of the surveyed households in the Gaza Strip and 71% of the surveyed households in the West Bank reported their satisfaction of the received aid. In addition, 10% of surveyed households in the West Bank and Gaza Strip reported that women and girls avoid areas because they feel unsafe in specific areas.The above findings demonstrate the need for CFM to reach the people in general and those who are in need to understand their needs, concerns, follow up on their inquiries, complaints and address them.The CFM will enable the broad humanitarian community to collect feedback directly from the affected populations; better understand the needs on the ground; and, take better informed decisions. The CFM will systematically receive calls, queries, complaints, and requests from the affected population with the goal to address such calls taking account of the different needs within communities from an age, gender, and diversity perspective. CFM will refer cases to a partner or sector coordinator when additional information or follow-up is required. This may cover information needs, complaint/negative feedback, and case referral (as per agreed upon guidelines).Referrals are intended to be timely, accurate safe and secure method to ensure relevant pathways for information sharing are available in the right modality to the appropriate partner for resolution of issues.In emergencies precedent has shown that many humanitarian organizations’ in-house feedback mechanisms become compromised and/or inactive, best practice recommends using a single phone number as an entry point for queries from affected populations. Hence, a coordinated mechanism built on international humanitarian architecture allows members of the affected population easier access to answers, speeds up the resolution of issues and allows greater access for humanitarian decision-makers, including Government, thereby improving aid delivery efficiency.The CFM will be accessible for all Palestinians in Gaza Strip and West Bank, including East Jerusalem.

2 - Activities

For the CFM will enhance and support the HRP objective #1 through the following activities: - Establish a network connection with international and national organizations and clusters members to be part of the CFM in order to receive referrals for AAP queries received through the toll-free hotline. - Monthly meetings will take place with CFM members’ or focal points to provide key information and feedback to CFM- Sensitization activities about the common feedback , mainly referral, mechanism through different communications channels including distribution of leaflets/posters, SMS messages, and face to face sessions.- Referring the inquiries/requests/complaints to the specialized agencies for their follow up.- Conduct Needs and Trends analysis to be provided to the Inter-Sector Coordination group WFP will engage with humanitarian stakeholders through the clusters and individual agencies and organization to understand the humanitarian needs, projects, specificity of the affected populations and based on that modify and update the CFM. The referral procedures will be prepared and discussed in consultation with the humanitarian stakeholders and will be linked to other functional CFM. This CFM will complement the CFM related to GBV and PSEA (handling sensitive cases) to ensure proper referral and follow up. A specific SoP will be developed depicting the links between the two CFMs, the referral of the cases, the follow up, the case management and the close of such sensitive cases.The CFM include a functional hotline where calls are received, ticketed, and filed. Based on the discussion with Humanitarian stakeholders, a focal point per cluster will be identified to receive the tickets based on the requested need/ follow up. A ticket with details about the need, inquiry about a request and details of the caller will be sent to the designated focal point, the focal point should follow up this ticket and provide feedback to the caller and WFP. Based on the follow, either the ticket will be closed, or it will be referred for further follow up. WFP will generate on regular basis reports from the system to be shared with concerned stakeholders/clusters on the number of calls received, categorized by the Region, type of call, needs, inquiries and will indicate the number of calls that were addressed and closed (based on the response from the focal points).

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The Complaint Feedback Mechanism (CFM) supports the gathering, production and sharing of information required for ensuring feedback from beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries are received, heard and actioned. The CFM activities support partners to address key protection needs of vulnerable people in Palestine, and by promoting protection mainstreaming in programming. The CFM does not provide any cash or in-kind tangible assistance, however it is a service provision that is aiming at strengthening the humanitarian community’s accountability to affected population. Service provision by the CFM is delivered through meetings, workshops, and analysis, in cooperation with partners.

8.1 - Project needs assessment

No

If not checked, please explain why

No specific needs assessment was done to assess the need for common feedback mechanism (CFM). However, having a functional CFM is a requisite to ensure Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP). Accountability to affected people is at the heart of humanitarian action. Accountability cannot exist unless organizations create the safe space and opportunity for people to provide feedback about programmes and services, or to complain about its quality, relevance, and timeliness, or the respect and dignity shown in its delivery.Humanitarian actors and the UN Mission at large are committed to AAP to achieve results and for the manner in which programmes are implemented for two principal reasons: 1) from a right-based perspective, affected people have the right to be actively involved in the decisions that affect their lives; and 2) engaging affected people in programme decisions contributes to a more effective design, programming and implementation of the assistance. Basing programmes on the preferences of affected people helps ensure that needs are correctly identified and understood and that programmes are designed in a manner appropriate to the context. Engagement with affected people can also help flag problems in programme implementation early allowing for adjustments to take place.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The CFM facilitates collective accountability to affected populations and consolidates limited resources that are currently only available to selected humanitarian organizations individually while putting the affected populations at the centre of humanitarian and development response.
