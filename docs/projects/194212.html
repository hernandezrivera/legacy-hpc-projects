Project Summary

In line with the CCCM cluster strategy, ACTED will continue to provide humanitarian coordination and CCCM integrated response to displacement affected population in Juba former PoC sites and will provide mobile support in hard-to-reach areas in response to displacement shocks. Due to natural hazards, localized and state conflicts, displacement waves remain high in South Sudan with 1.64 million people currently being Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in need of CCCM assistance according to the South Sudan HNO 2022. The trend has been increasing in 2022 following massive natural hazards such as floodings in Jonglei state, leading to the multiplication of displacement sites.
As South Sudan has been subject to displacement waves since its creation, and particularly following the 2013 civil war, some IDP sites have existed for several years with residents not willing to return to their areas of origin and ongoing humanitarian aid. To address this issue, ACTED aims to provide a more sustainable solution and support these IDP communities to become more empowered and increase their integration into host communities settings. Following a strategy implemented since 2021 in agreement with CCCM cluster, ACTED handed over camp management responsibilities to the community representatives and local authorities, especially Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) in Bor and Juba former PoC camps in 2022. In 2023, ACTED will strengthen social cohesion between IDPs and host communities in Juba to foster peaceful coexistence and will provide ad-hoc support to community representatives if needed to ensure a smooth transition.
In parallel, ACTED provides emergency CCCM assistance in response to displacement shocks across the country. Through a well-established coordination system with REACH, the Inter Cluster Coordination Group, the Needs Analysis Working Group and the clusters, ACTED has access to timely information on displacements, allowing for a rapid multi-sectoral intervention whose core is CCCM services. Within its CCCM rapid response, ACTED aims to provide life saving coordination activities among humanitarian actors and build local communities’ capacities to self manage the sites.
Therefore, ACTED will continue implementing CCCM interventions in line with the following cluster objectives: CO1: Improve living condition of IDPs by facilitating safe, equitable and dignified access to cross-sectoral humanitarian services, including CCCM CO2: Enhance the protective environment, respond to vulnerable population with priority on addressing protection gaps and strengthen the predictability and effectiveness of multi sectorial interventions at site level CO3: Support vulnerable IDPs to recover from the crisis by strengthening community self-management, build resilience to promote solutions for displaced people in sites.

1 - Needs

According to the South Sudan CCCM Cluster HNO 2023, 1.7million people are currently IDPs in need of CCCM assistance across South Sudan. After a sudden shock, IDPs often gather in informal displacement sites in extremely precarious conditions, most of the time without access to basic services and information, and with limited decision-making power on issues that affect their daily lives and their future. These gathering places are often characterized by an increased exposure to security risks and protection needs, particularly for the most vulnerable groups. Poor living conditions; lack of financial resources and limited access to basic services can lead to outbreaks of disease, increased prevalence of GBV or domestic violence, and tensions between population groups within the informal camps. Likewise, overcrowding could lead to challenges in maintaining safe levels of hygiene and sanitation. This is of particular importance in 2022-2023 as neighbouring Uganda is currently facing an Ebola outbreak that could spill to South Sudan. Continued political uncertainty, coupled with the increased risks of climate change has also largely resulted in the inability of the international community to return these IDP’s to more suitable locations and an unwillingness from the camps inhabitants to leave. Regarding Bor and Juba former PoC camps, ACTED further reinforced the local authorities’ resources and abilities in Jonglei and completed a full handover of the Camp management responsibilities to the Community Members Committees and Camp administration role to RRC and community representatives in Juba IDPs Camp 1 and 3 and Bor camp in 2022.

2 - Project activities

In line with the CCCM cluster strategy for 2023, ACTED will continue to implement its CCCM interventions through mobile and semi static responses.In 2023, ACTED will aim to strengthen peaceful cohesion between Juba camps and the peri-IDP areas to support IDPs integration within host communities. ACTED might also provide ad-hoc support to the new camp managers to ensure a smooth transition. In parallel, ACTED’s CCCM mobile teams will address the needs of displacement affected populations in camp like settings and informal settlements with a focus on quality and information-driven interventions that help people adapt to shocks and prepare to transition towards self-reliance. ACTED will utilize mobile responses in response to critical needs for the provision of life-saving support in emergency scenarios, specifically in hard-to-reach areas, where service provision and partners presence is limited. In particular, the response includes: - Restructuring and capacity building of governance through Community Management Committees. - Communities identify prominent risks for site maintenance, planning works, and contingency plans - mapping of service provision and site profiles to be shared with humanitarian coordination forums - Protection Focal Points are trained in the Community to establish a referral pathway to be maintained after the intervention. - Communities are supported through a CLI to improve livelihoods. - Local partners are trained in coordination and service monitoring. In addition, ACTED mobile team will pilot a response to areas of potential and actual returns to improve governance and resilience. ACTED will conduct cash-based interventions through CFW modality when possible. CFW can be used for a variety of activities including site maintenance, community centre construction, dyke construction in line with the identified needs per location. Moreover, while taking into account the nature of the CCCM response which remains a rapid response mechanism, ACTED is seeking to bridge its current emergency response to a longer response towards the restoration of self-reliance of displaced affected communities. While continuing to use its current mechanism to respond to the immediate needs as currently implemented, ACTED would like to expand the scope of its emergency mechanism toward longer-term, early recovery response to support the communities through their path to enhanced self-reliance. In this framework, ACTED would like to deploy more robust package after the first response with a deeper involvement of governance structures through for example the formation of community based-structures and conditional MPCA for a longer period of implementation.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

ACTED will continue working to ensure the accessibility of its Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM). For that purpose, ACTED will identify additional complaints collection mechanisms on top of the ones generally used (help desk, CRM lines and boxes) in order to ensure that the most vulnerable community members can share their complaints safely. Some of these mechanisms will include active collection of complaints and increased field presence of members of the Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU), as well as presence of female members in CRM desks. During mobile interventions, ACTED teams will set up Community Based Complaints Mechanisms (CBCMs) in order to ensure the sustainability of complaints management by community members after the intervention.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

ACTED’s AME and program teams will join efforts in order to ensure that beneficiaries are sensitized and aware about the CRM, its scope and functioning. For this purpose, ACTED AME team will conduct awareness raising sessions at the beginning of project activities, CRM visibility will be displayed during each distribution, ACTED program teams will also sensitize and refer beneficiaries to the CRM and the AME team will collect data on CRM awareness during assessment in order to ensure a close monitoring and identify needed actions.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The Camp Management Committees who are trained under the project activities receive specific trainings on PSEA and are expected to disseminate this information to the communities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The AMEU will categorize complaints from 1 to 5 depending on its level of sensitivity. Although the CRM will remain an independent system managed by the AMEU, level 1 and 2 complaints including feedback and request for assistance will be shared with the PT in a timely manner. This way, the PT will be able to respond to beneficiaries’ feedback and adapt project assistance if necessary. The AME team will monitor the closure of complaints and will ensure that relevant action is taken.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Within the former PoCs, ACTED establishes and supports inclusive community governance mechanisms. These groups will be consulted throughout with a focus on ensuring all minority groups, as well as specific interest groups such as women and youth, are included in consultations and project design. ACTED’s mobile interventions all include a protection officer to ensure training of leadership, identification of referral mechanisms and identification of PSNs. However, in mobile interventions which are often remote, it has previously proved challenging to identify protection partners able to take referrals. Therefore, ACTED is increasingly collaborating with protection specialists to run joint missions where possible. The RSRTF project looks to address the risks related to dispute on land ownership and intercommunity conflict over access to resources. The project design will be very closely coordinated with UNMISS and dependent on the prevailing security situation, to ensure that the project will support HLP rights of affected populations and do no harm. Living in insecure settlements exposes displaced women and girls in particular to GBV. Site maintenance activities will therefore be designed with a GBV angle in mind. Furthermore, women and girls will be prioritized by vulnerability criteria for shelter/ NFI interventions (e.g. torches). ACTED’s interventions will target not only IDPs, but also the host community, returnees and local polity (known collectively as a displacement -affected community) with mobile CCCM activities. Where multi-sectoral assistance is targeted, vulnerable host communities will also be considered under vulnerability criteria. ACTED has previously experienced low utilization of CRM in mobile responses. This will be mitigated through clear and consistent communication with the community, as well as establishment of a community-based complaints mechanism (CBCM).

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Climate change is one of the major issues in South-Sudan as flooding massively affects populations. Floods exacerbate the vulnerability of communities and force people to flee their houses, increasing their basic needs. Given the climatic situation in South-Sudan, ACTED will ensure in each of its interventions to strengthen the resilience and self-reliance of communities to reduce the impacts of recurrent shocks. As an example in this project, ACTED will strengthen the communities to adopt strategies to increase their resilience to climate and disaster-related shocks/stressors, including flooding, through participatory, community-driven planning and resource management.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Within the scope of the project, ACTED is planning a direct implementation of protection activities such as capacity building, awareness-raising, and strengthening referral pathways. Moreover, ACTED interventions will include scoping assessment and safety audit to improve safety and protection of populations through site improvements/maintenance activities, and to evaluate the needs of people displaced - and particularly those related to protection risks such as sexual and gender-based violence with limited access to basic services. Once completed, these assessments will be followed by feedback and advocacy with the humanitarian community to seek positioning of humanitarian actors. ACTED will incorporate protection training, covering key topics and issues identified through the scoping assessment and safety audits, providing participants with a more comprehensive understanding of how to respond within the specific areas of intervention.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Based on solid field experience, ACTED will address the specificities of displaced populations in South Sudan to meet the needs of vulnerable forcibly displaced communities. This approach will allow to support the populations with a response to help them organize, structure themselves and meet their needs. The mobile team will distribute lifesaving non-food items availed from the pipeline; and mobile will strengthen governance structures, building capacity of the community, local partners, and authorities; as well as site maintenance to ultimately deliver a holistic approach to emergency response.
