Project Summary

This project intends to contribute to the protection of civilians from explosive devices in central Mali, through the delivery of emergency risk education and awareness to affected-communities, as well as assistance to survivors of explosive hazards, indirect victims and persons with disabilities from other causes depending on the findings following the needs assessments within the 10 circles (Macina and Niono in Segou and Mopti, Bandiagara, Bankass, Douentza, Koro, Djenné, Tenenkou and Youwarou in Mopti region). This will involve setting up a community mechanism for the identification of victims and referral for a holistic management of accident cases, by the medical evacuation of the injured, their emergency medical care, psychosocial support, physical rehabilitation and support for socio-economic integration. Risk education activities will consist of raising awareness among the populations of the most at-risk areas and informing them of the behavior to adopt in front of suspicious objects.

1 - Contexte

Mali is facing a multidimensional crisis since 2012 which particularly affects women and children, as they are daily exposed to violations of their fundamental rights and repeated attacks on their dignity. Among the people in need of protection in 2020, we count 1,308,775 children (640,095 boys and 668,679 girls), and 1,160,611 adults (590,659 women and 569,952 men) and 102,891 elderly people (52,569 women and 50,322 men).In terms of vulnerability towards explosive devices, some groups within the popualtion are particuarly at risk, namely children, Internally Displaced Persons, returnees and host communities, road users, pastoralists, nomads, as well as farmers. This contamination with explosive devices leads to a risk of disability and loss of life. It also restricts the freedom of movement of populations, which on the one hand limits their access to basic social services and means of subsistence, including access to water, food and shelter, and on the other hand hinder their ability to regularly and reliably pursue their income-generating activities. From January to June 2020, there have been 100 accidents linked to IED or mines in Mali, resulting in 55 deaths and 219 injured. In 2019, the total number reached 194 accidents, which caused the death of 140 persons and injured 329 others. The important ratio of deaths per accident can be explained by the limited capacity to quickly evacuate and deliver emergency assistance to victims. Most of the accidents are located in the region of Mopti (52,5% of the accidents recorded from April to June 2020).Considering the recent dynamics of the conflict in Mali, it is expected that the presence of explosive devices will not decrease in the near future and that accidents will remain frequent in different part of the country and especially in Mopti region. In addition, the legacy of several years of conflict generated a significant level of contamination in some areas, which is putting the civilians particularly at risk of accidents, potentially contributing to more deaths and injuries.In addition, the illicit proliferation of small arms and light weapons (SALW) and the risks associated with it further exacerbate the violence and aggravates the consequences of inter and intra-community conflicts, the number of victims of which continues to increase. Support for these victims then acts precisely to protect vulnerable populations in the central (Mopti, Ségou) and northern (Timbuktu, Gao, Kidal and Ménaka) regions, particularly affected by conflicts.
