Project Summary

South Sudan Development Agency (SSUDA) is a National NGO supporting most vulnerable people in hard-to-reach areas in Upper Nile. SSUDA is currently the State Co-Focal Point for NFIs/Shelter for Upper Nile and implementing NFIs/Shelter in most of the 13 Counties. Due to increased life threatening humanitarian needs in these locations, SSUDA seeks to provide lifesaving gender and aged specific ES/NFIs to 30,000 most vulnerable women, men and children from IDPs, Returnees and host communities that will reduce morbidity and mortality of these conflict affected communities. The project is in support of S/NFI Cluster Strategic Objective 1 and in addition will contribute to reduction of protection threats and incidences in Luakpiny/Nasir, Fashoda, Baliet, Maiwut, Ulang, Longuchuk. Some of the location targeted are most affect by current floods and the impact will be felt longer. The intervention includes both Gift in Kind for 24,000 and 6,000 Cash Transfers. SSUDA will have two static teams in Ulang and Malakal who have the capacity to respond in Eastern and Western Upper Nile respectively covering all the target counties. There is also one mobile team based Juba that will support and complement the static teams as needs arise. SSUDA will maintain regular communication and coordination with other Clusters and partners especially CCCM to avoid duplication and share synergies in areas they target or they would want support particularly in POCs/Humanitarian Hubs such as in Fashoda. SSUDA will consult with UNHCR and DRC supporting People with Special Needs in specific areas to avoid double targeting. SSUDA will also participate in the ICWG meetings for coordination and integrating of activities from different sectors.
SSUDA will conduct assessment/verification of and respond to life-saving needs for women, men and children being displaced, returnees, protracted IDPs and most vulnerable host communities by distributing NFIs and emergency shelter items and conduct PDM. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be conducted at the end of distribution to assess the effectiveness and efficiency of the distribution. Special consideration to women and female-headed households, households with children under 5, the elderly and critically ill. Community awareness in the target locations will be carried out to inform the community about the distribution to ensure all beneficiaries are informed about the dates, time and location. Youth will be mobilized and sensitized to support elderly and people with special needs to establish shelters. To increase ownership and sustainability, SSUDA will facilitate and train community committees (50% women) to work hand in hand with staff to deliver the project effectively. SSUDA will work closely with other organization to ensure distribution is done concurrently with other interventions such as WASH, SGBV, and FSL. Since most beneficiaries are affected by conflict and facing HLP related challenges, SSUDA will work with local authorities to create awareness and advocate HLP’s rights. In addition, SSUDA will collaborate with community leaders to ensure that of LHP rights are addressed whenever they arise. SSUDA as the State Co-Focal Point work with SFP to facilitate and support monthly Cluster meeting at State level to strengthen coordination and timely reporting of gaps. SSUDA will in consultation with SFP organize workshops for NNGOs Cluster partners/members so that they familiarize with Cluster’s tools and guiding principles while mainstreaming Protection that cover “DO NO HARM Principle, SPHERE, standards, Accountability to the Affected Population and Conflict Sensitivity.

1 - Needs

Five years of conflict has left almost 4.2 million people displaced internally and outside the country. While the intensity of the conflict may have reduced recently, and clashes have been contained to certain degree, vulnerable people mostly women and children will continue to experience the impact of the conflict through 2020 and beyond (National Framework for Return, Reintegration and Relocation of Displaced Person – July 2019). According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (NHO) 2019, 274,431 individuals will require support for NFI/ES in 2020. An increased number of displaced persons is expected to return to their original homes. Vulnerability has been exacerbated by the current floods that has displaced more than 1 million people across the Country (Presidential Declaration of State of Emergency – 29th October 2019) Expected returnees’ settlements in 2019 will result in a need for immediate NFI/Shelter assistance. Alongside the returnees, many people are still living in protracted IDPs camps and PoCs in pathetic shelter conditions. Furthermore, due to the magnitude of the impact of conflict, the host communities are nearly as vulnerable as returnees and IDPs are, with limited coping mechanisms especially on shelter/NFIs (UNOCHA Snapshot June 2019). Most IDPs in Upper Nile continue to live in deplorable conditions while many returnees who arrive home after long period of displacement find their shelters burned or damaged and possessions looted. The situation is aggravated by the ongoing abnormal rains and floods that has affected half of the country and will continue for the next few months (South Sudan Metrological Department Update, Aug, 2019). The returnees trek for long distances back home and most of the time have no capacity to carry their belonging. The markets in most parts of Upper Nile are dysfunctional and even if a few are functional the affected populations have no capacity to purchase household items. The only coping mechanism is to put up under the tree. But with the torrential rains it is not possible to sleep under trees. Grass are having also been submerged. Lack of shelter and NFIs increases the risk of life threatening water-borne and other communicable diseases as well as safety challenges of women, girls and children under 5. Many of the returnees and IDPs put up in open spaces especially under the trees in their host communities who cannot cope up with accommodating huge numbers returning back. A joint Humanitarian mission to Abroc (Fashoda, County) in August 2019, reported that there are over 50 returnees coming back daily from Sudan, this trend was noted from June 2019. According to the report, Shelter has been, the main challenge. The health centres in Abroc had reported malaria and watery diarrhea as the main health implications (Aburoc Mission Report 13 – Aug, 2019). The situation is not different in Baliet where the entire resident was displaced during the conflict and now returning back with completely no basic households. In Nasir County, in addition to influx of returnees and existing IDPs, in the month of August 2019, flooding has caused fresh displacement of nearly 15,000 HHs in most of the Payams including Kuetrengke, Jikmir, Dhorading, Maker, Koat, Wanding and Kier (Coordinated Flood Disaster Assessment in Nasir County, Upper Nile State – Aug, 2019). The affected families are sheltering in higher grounds with no shelter materials. The grass which is the main shelter materials have been covered by water while NFIs have been washed away by floods. In Ulang there has been constant flow of thousands of returnees from Ethiopia as well as frequent inter and intra-ethnic fighting in the neighboring counties forcing people to flee to Ulang County (IRNA to Ulang, Ulang County 8 – 13, May 2019). Ulang and Luakpiny are among the 27 counties most affected by floods (Presidential Decree - State of Emergency 29th October, 2019).

2 - Activities

• Communities engagement – SSUDA will conduct community inception meetings with community leaders and local authorities in the target locations. The inception meetings aim to inform the communities the key objective of the project and mobilize their support. SSUDA will facilitate the selection of community support committees to assist Implementation plans will be shared and reviewed and expectations managed. The meetings will be opportunity to set pace for community to actively involved in all stages of the project while promoting Accountability to Affected population. Mainstreaming of HLP through awareness and advocacy will be carried out.• Gender and age specific needs assessment - men, women and children in the target counties will be assessed for NFIs and emergency Shelter needs using participatory rapid assessment, identification and verification of beneficiaries in consultation with local stakeholders. The assessment will start with identification and training of local enumerators who will work with Community support committees to collect quality information on the NFI, Shelter needs of women, men and children. The information will also capture other needs which SSUDA will use for mainstreaming and link with other interventions such as protection and WASH. • Identification of Beneficiaries – Household level beneficiary selection will be carried out through a vigorous process based on Cluster recommended selection criteria taking into consideration the target group, displacement status and vulnerability categories. Female headed households, child headed households, disabled or chronically ill people will be prioritized. As household level assessment data would have been conducted for needy caseloads in target locations. Cash Vouchers will be used in in areas where markets are functional and will be done after market assessment is carried. • Distribution of in-kind NFI kits– SSUDA will receive in-kind NFI kits from the NFI/ES Cluster Pipeline (IOM) comprising of blankets, mosquito nets, kitchen and cooking kits. SSUDA is planning to train and help household to establish simple energy saving stoves using new technologies. This will contribute to reduction time and protection risk of women and girls in search of firewood. SSUDA will acquire the non-food items (NFIs) through the shelter/NFI cluster pipeline and transported to a temporary storage facility by the Logistics Cluster. SSUDA will be in charge of ensuring that, there is a local temporary storage facility in each county. After the items are delivered to the field storage, SSUDA transport the items to distribution points in each payam • Sensitization of NFI Voucher beneficiaries - In Maiwut and Nasir, SSUDA has conducted market analysis and found that NFI and Shelter vouchers is appropriate. SSUDA will sensitize selected beneficiaries on use of vouchers with information provided on their value, how to use them and list of approved vendors. • Distribution of NFI/ES vouchers – Upon completion of market surveys, SSUDA will engage local venders of ES/NFIs and set up a voucher system where selected beneficiaries can use vouchers for ES/NFI items. The voucher system will be implemented in way that does not compromise safety of the beneficiaries. Female beneficiaries will be protected to ensure their safety. • Post Distribution Monitoring – SSUDA will carry out PDMs within a month of each NFI distribution intervention. • Cluster Coordination – SSUDA as the State Co- focal point will collaborate with the SFP to organize State level cluster meeting, through this, SSUDA will identify other agencies responding in the same locations and closely work together to avoid duplication of activities.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

SSUDA has put in place all mechanisms to ensure that the rights of the beneficiaries are protected and respected. SSUDA is very strict with any action intended to violate the rights of beneficiaries either by staff or anyone assigned to work or act on behalf of SSUDA, this includes all staff and volunteers signing Code of ethics to adhere to the values of the organization. SSUDA ensures that all complaints emanating from beneficiaries are appropriately addressed.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

SSUDA understands and believe in the Kampala Convention and International Guiding Principles for IDPs that promotes the protection of the specific needs of internally displaced persons due to displacement, and to address vulnerabilities arising from the situation of displacement as well as to achieve durable solutions for them

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project is directly linked to the government systems. The project will contribute to the recently approved National Framework for the Return, Reintegration and Relocation of Displaced Persons: Achieving Durable Solutions in South Sudan. It is also contributing to the South Sudan Strategic Development Plan of 2018.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project aligned to the recently approved National Framework for the Return, Reintegration and Relocation of Displaced Persons: Achieving Durable Solutions in South Sudan. It is also contributing to the objectives of the Revitalized Peace Agreement

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project is planning to integrate the Disaster Risk Reduction activities in which SSUDA is the national focal point for the Global Network of Civil Society for Disaster Reduction. SSUDA is planning to mainstream Disaster Risk reduction in all its programmes. One of the specific activities is the promotion of the use of energy saving stoves and community awareness on environmental issues that will significantly contribute to environmental protection and preservation.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

SSUDA as a national NGO will be present in the location for long and has various funding streams over multiple sectors to further support the most vulnerable communities to cope with any emerging shocks. SSUDA is working to integrate S/NFI with other projects such as Quick Impact project, Peace building and DRR activities that SSUDA is implementing in these locations. SSUDA’s activities promote and contribute to peaceful coexistence and enable communities to harmoniously access emerging markets to acquire goods and reduce over dependence on relief items. At the end of the project activities, SSUDA expects that the communities’ resilience capacity would be enhance to cope with future shocks. SSUDA will ensure active participation of the affected population and local NGOs during the implementation period to ensure the community is involved in management and decision-making through frequent engagement and establishing local structures that will go a long way to exist beyond the project period. The capacity of the local structures will also be built through skills and knowledge transferred.
