Project Summary

The SPEDP 2021 humanitarian response intervention will be implemented in 25 schools in 3 Counties in Central Equatoria and 2 in Upper Nile States. The target population will comprise of school children, youth, the disable, women, IDPs, returnees and host community. This project will apply a 2 prong approach; combination of activities in objective 2 with activities in strategic objective 3. The strategic objective 2: having the WASH Component; rehabilitation of segregated gender latrines and objective 3: to enhance the capacity of education system’s response of the school community and education actors to mitigate the impact of emergency in their midst and mitigative measures on the transmission of COVID-19 pandemic on learners, teachers, and communities in Counties of Central Equatoria and the Upper Nile States. This response plan will be a supplement to SPEDP’s 5 year ongoing health project in the selected locations of Kajokeji, Morobo, and Terekeka in Central Equatoria State. In Renk and Melut, SPEDP has successfully completed a Health intervention in these locations in May 2020. SPEDP has also been implementing school feeding programme in Renk and Melut in 2018/2019 including in Manyo and Fashoda. This has enabled it build excellent rapport with the local authorities on ground and now very much conversant with the cultural, geo-political and socio-enonomic status of the area. The break down of the targeted population of of 10,426 (5316female/5110male) will be as follows; 10,322 (5,264girls/5,058boys) will be school children and 104 (52 women/52men) will be adults who are either internally displaced persons, host communities, returnees, and people with disabilities living in the 5 Counties. These targeted people will benefit from a number of activities that include; rehabilitation of gender-segregated latrines and provision of handwashing facilities and detergents to the 25 schools, training of PTA/SMC members on their roles, training of local education officials in the effective management of schools, rebuilding of positive psycho-social support of the targeted school local authorities and to lessen the disaster risk by changing people’s attitude into active and positive school Managers to enhance increase in registration of learners. In addition, this project also intends to provide communities with suitable IEC prevention and mitigation of Covid-19 messages, lifesaving skills, and inclusive education that will bring all children aboard regardless of their backgrounds. Lastly, the project intends to wage a “back to learning” campaign to raise awareness and encourage a massive return to school and to build the resilience of the children, parents or and guidance in Morobo, Kajo-keji, Terekeka, Renk, and Melut Counties in Central Equatoria and the Upper Nile States to cope with challenges in their communities in South Sudan. The project will also target the 25 schools and offer emergency-affected children and youth with counseling to change their attitudes and behavior from negative effect of Covid-19 to positive attitudes while in the school environment. The local education officials and PTA/SMC members will also be trained to increase their maximum decision-making recovery from the emergency effect and become stronger in the management of the emergencies and their effect in schools. SPEDP as its policy demands, collaborate with local government authorities and NGO partners on ground on different thematic area that are in line with project and deemed to enhance results of this project. These organization will include among others; ARC, World Vision, Titi Foundation, TERM, UNICEF, Plan International and NRC.

1 - Needs

The 22 years of war in South Sudan left behind numerous needs due to the devastating results behind it; killing of estimated 4 million South Sudanese, 2 million believed to be refugees in neighboring countries, 2.2 million lived as internally displaced. The 2013 and 2016 conflicts that came on the hill of independence of South Sudan in July 2011 worsened the situation. South Sudan is currently undergoing numerous socio-economic and political challenges. All sectors of society; economy, infrastructure including the education system have been affected. There is high displacement, high mobility of people, lost livelihoods, destroyed infrastructure, properties and social amenities like schools and hospitals. The country faces a collapsing economy; high inflation, continued tribal wars, cattle rustling and floods displacing thousands of people making HHs fail to meet their needs including feeding their children. In the2021 HNO Report, 8.3 million people representing 2/3 of the total population of South Sudan; 9.6% up from the number in 2020 are estimated to be in need. Despite the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, the country still faces awful humanitarian crises; 1.62 million people still continue to live in IDP Camps and 125 thousand are in POCs with inadequate basic services. The education cluster estimates that 3.4 million people need education in the country; 51% of those are women, 99% are children where 15% are children with disabilities. UNESCO Education Study Report (2020) states that over 2.5 million children are out of the school of who 75% are girls. The appearance of COVID 19 Pandemic has made the education situation even worse. The closure of schools in March 2020 left thousands of children to remain at home causing many children to be psychologically affected due to the long stay. Parents also lost their livelihoods and reduced their coping mechanisms due to the COVID 19 lockdown making them unable to meet their needs including supporting their children should schools open. Most schools are likely to struggle to open due to challenge of meeting the SOP requirements for COVID 19 pandemic. Unsegregated latrines are other factors affecting high attendance particularly of girls in schools. SPEDP plan to implement this project in 25 schools (5/County) in 5 Counties in 2 States; Terekeka, Kajokeji and Morobo in Central Equatoria and Renk and Melut in Upper Nile. These Counties all fall under IPC Phase 4 level and have high similarities; share borders with international borders, high cross border movements, influx of returning refugees, long distances from the Centre and have extreme weather conditions. In an assessment conducted in Kajokeji by ARC, SCA, Titi Foundation, TERM and SPEDP, it was found that 4 schools have been occupied by soldiers. In Morobo, out of the 89 schools before the war, only 7 are currently functional; dilapidated toilets and shared between girls and boys. In Melut and Renk like in Morobo, Kajokeji, and Terekeka, the number of returnees is high putting high pressure on the functional schools and are a risk of spread of COVID 19. The absence of non-functional governance structures (PTAs/SMCs) in these schools due to displacements, mobility of people or closure of schools is another challenge. SPEDP, therefore, plans to address these identified needs/challenges; spread of COVID 19 in schools and communities through SOP awareness creation and provision of handwashing facilities and detergents, address the psychological effect of COVID 19 in the communities and around the 25 schools, the broken toilets by carrying out rehabilitation of these latrines and create hygienic clubs and gender-segregated latrines, the absence or non-functional governance structures in the schools through the formation and strengthening of these structures through training of the PTAs/SMCs. Other needs to be addressed include mobilization of learners and communities for higher registration and attendance of learners.

2 - Project activities

The activities that make up this project that SPEDP intends to implement address the identified needs in this project. These shall include the following; (i). Identification and selection of the 5 schools in each of the 5 Counties in the 2 States (ii). Mobilize the community and their leadership in and around the selected schools towards awareness creation, involvement, and ownership of this project and in the launch of the project in each of the 5 Counties where this project will be implemented (iii). Strengthening of the governance structure in the selected schools; training of 130 (66 women/64men) selected PTA/SMC members in the 25 schools located in the 5 Counties. The training will focus on equipping the PTA/SMC members with general knowledge of their roles. (iv). To conduct Psychosocial support and disaster risk reduction mechanism sessions to 21(11women /10men) local education officials from the Counties and Payams where the 25 schools are located. (v). To provide cross-cutting emergency life-saving and life-sustaining skills and inclusiveness training to 12,500 emergency-affected children and youth in the 25 schools supported. (vi). To create awareness, prevention, and mitigation measures on COVID 19 SOPs in all the 25 schools and communities surrounding the schools to reduce the spread of the pandemic. (vii). To develop, print, and distribute appropriate Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials in form of posters, leaflets, and banners in English, Arabic, and local languages and 100 T-Shirts. SPEDP will group the beneficiaries into 30 members per group and the formed groups will identify a leader who will coordinate necessary training with SPEDP and project field staff members. (viii). Rehabilitation 10 broken down latrines to create gender-segregated latrines in the 25 schools (ix). To procure and supply 75 pieces of 100 liters hand washing facilities (water tanks, water tank stands, and basins) and hard and liquid soaps (detergents) - soap, sanitizers, to be placed at the gates of the selected schools to encourage regular handwashing. These handwashing facilities will be placed at the entry of the schools for incoming learners and teachers including visitors to the school and at the latrines. The training conducted it is hoped, will in turn make the project sustainable after the project comes to an end. The trained beneficiaries it is hoped to thereafter continue to train and conduct disaster risk management, Psychosocial support skills in the schools and in the neighborhoods. SPEDP team members will engage and coordinate joint response missions with stakeholders and the Cluster. (x) Formation and training of 1 hygiene club in each of the schools supported (xi) Run cross-cutting emergency engagement for 10,275 children and youth by building their self-confidence and self-efficacy through softer life skills such as; Communication, problem-solving, decision-making, and critical thinking skills to enable them to engage and advocate on issues that are important.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SPEDP adheres to the internationally accepted “DO NO HARM” and “SPHERE” protection principles which ensure avoidance of exposing beneficiaries to further harm as a result of the actions of the project being implemented in the community. And, to ensure complete impartiality in people’s access to assistance in proportion to needs, no discrimination, and protection of people from physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion. SPEDP will ensure that people claim their rights, have access to available remedies, and recover from unforeseen effects of abuse. SPEDP will also ensure that protection risks and benefits are considered in this project to all beneficiaries. A community-based protection approach or meaningful engagement will be promoted. Ideal leadership of crisis-affected communities to identify protection risks, self-protection capacities, joint prevention, and mitigation schemes will be paramount in this project and will be linked to the SPEDP Protection Department and actors so as to seek and mainstream an age, gender, and diversity approach that aims at equal enjoyment of rights by all crisis-affected people and to integrate this into accountability framework in the project. Aware of the UN Secretary-General's Bulletin on Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (ST/SGB/2003/13), SPEDP Project Staff Members will strengthen the PMCs and community engagement and create awareness-raising sessions around PSEA with women, girls, boys and men in the project areas as well as with other stakeholders and a feedback mechanisms for receiving complaints from communities set up. SPEDP will identify and support feedback channels that communities prefer while ensuring that feedback mechanisms are sensitive to age, gender, and diversity (AGD) and accessible to women and girls, children, persons with disabilities, and other persons at high risk. Besides, SPEDP will also coordinate complaint collection and responses with other partners within these proposed project locations and establish clear tasks and duties (who will do what, where, and how).

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Through the Project Management Committees (PMCs) established in the project, communities will be encouraged to work on the principle of “leaving no one behind,” non-discrimination, and inclusiveness in the implementation of this project. This Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) created will take a special interest in identifying and collecting opinions from the physically challenged members of the beneficiaries in the project. The PMCs will deliver a mechanism that enables the most vulnerable members of the beneficiaries and community to provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities of these beneficiaries in relation to the project in a manner that is inclusive, safe, non-discriminative, non-threatening, and accessible to all. This initiative is intended to promote beneficiary accountability and feedback and response mechanism to individuals who are physically challenged in the project. SPEDP will use this to measure the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints of most vulnerable members e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls. The PMCs will also be encouraged to ensure easy access possible for returnees, IDPs, and host communities regardless of their age and sex. SPEDP plan to use multiple information dissemination channels to bring about easy access and information leverage. This will include multimedia platforms including radio and SMS. These platforms shall be identified and where necessary and appropriate used to disseminate information on inclusiveness. Mechanisms that take into account opinion of women folks shall as well be consulted for their opinions for inclusion in the project implementation process. This will be done through various fora including monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by the PMCs. The Project Management Committees will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and work plans for the inclusion of the information collected into the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

SPEDP will provide beneficiary information about the project and feedback mechanism or complaint mechanism by involving every stakeholder in the implementation of the project cycle right from the start of the project. This will also be done through the project launch or inception and meetings. All rights or opinions of the beneficiaries at all levels of the project will be fully acknowledged and recognized based on national and international settings through the contact mechanisms above. The Project Management Committee (PMC) complaint and feedback mechanism will be established in both Juba and field offices in order to register complaints from the community and give immediate feedback to the complainers if it is a case that can be handled at the field level but if not, the case will be referred to Juba. This will ensure that the rights of the beneficiaries are given priority during inception and implementation periods. Non-discrimination and meaningful access to assistance and services will be encouraged: all beneficiaries in the project will be accorded equal opportunity to access information on the project from the start of the project implementation. Concern for the safety and dignity of children, youths, women, and men for example will be central in this project. Prioritizing the safety and dignity of beneficiaries will entail a reduction of risks in all the project activities. A range of Beneficiary Communication and Accountability mechanisms to strengthen community engagement and feedback throughout the life of the project shall be established. This too shall be developed during the project’s inception phase and will be the primary responsibility of the Project Management Committee to implement, with support from SPEDP project staff. A core component of this will be the regular dissemination of project-related information and updates to community members who are beneficiaries and those who may not directly benefit from the project.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback information will be collected through meetings and interviews with the stakeholders in this project. The opinion of the beneficiaries will be collected during project field monitoring. SPEDP will organize community committees made up of diverse representatives from the student body, PTA/SMCs, Teachers, and project staff members to work together to record and handle complaints and feedback especially from the beneficiaries' regarding the quality of project implementation. SPEDP will approach and apply the principle of equity perspective and advocate for equal participation of different groups of beneficiaries. This will be based on the cluster reporting system or mechanisms. The project will put in place a monitoring and reporting system, progress, best practices, and lessons learned will be presented in cluster meetings based on the 5Ws formats developed by the cluster. The Project Management Committee (PMC) formed will be briefed on their roles and on the keeping of confidentiality. Regular meetings with staff and beneficiary representatives shall regularly be conducted to review records and in deciding on action needed. Feedback collected will immediately be addressed and included in the implementation of the project. That is, the Feedback Project Management Team will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and work plans accordingly in real-time. Information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the PMC through scheduled consultation meetings, which in so doing will ensure functional two-way communication. Project Management Committees will also publicize and manage complaints and response mechanisms which will allow communities to provide feedback between scheduled community consultations, and anonymously report grievances and complaints. The Project Management Committees will use the project-specific risk-based protocols to respond to any complaints.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

SPEDP will ensure that universal access to quality training, distribution of IEC, and campaign through establishing community engagement responsibilities with the stakeholders and beneficiaries who shall be the main focus. This is aimed at creating a strong accountability strategy to the SPEDP’s policy framework, ensure a reduction of inequality based on the principle of equal human rights for all citizens, and preventing the adoption of subjective notions of fairness, which might be the outcome of political negotiation in which most disadvantaged are often voiceless. The project will ensure non-discrimination and meaningful access to assistance and services. The implementing team will seek meaningful engagement with the local authorities, SSRC, Education cluster, and UNOCHA to ensure that activities are only implemented in locations where the safety of beneficiaries and implementing team is guaranteed. All beneficiaries and other stakeholders will be central in decision-making concerning their own protection and welfare. Special attention will be paid to ensure the participation of and consultation with the women and girls who are the most vulnerable members of society who may often be excluded from decision-making processes. To maximize the linkages between social protection and access to education and to improve the effectiveness of both Education in Emergencies and protection, it is imperative that gender-sensitive measures are integrated into policy and program design and implementation. The project will use Initial Rapid Needs Assessment (IRNA) that revealed a number of important lessons which can be used to inform policy dialogues on Education in Emergencies initiatives in project locations.
