Project Summary

This project is targeting Returnees, host community and IDPS living in Aweil South , Aweil West,counties in Northern Bhar-El-Ghazal State , Pibor and Akobo Counties in Jonglei State and Yei county in Central Equatoria . Specifically the target beneficiaries are a total of 30,000 men, women, girls boys and persons living with disability ,Persons With Specific Needs and the elderly, who are at high risk of gender based violence, death, injury and life threatening diseases like HIV/AIDS, STIs and reproductive health related illnesses aggravated due to floods ,violence, famine-risk (IPC 3,4 5 ), COVID 19 and economic woes . The primary purpose of the project is to address compounding effects of converging factors i.e severe food insecurity /loss of livelihoods, economic decline, protection threats and lack of services which contributes and aggravates the prevalence of GBV. The HNO 2021 classified Aweil South, Akobo and Pibor counties as a severity phase 5 catastrophe meaning that access to basic humanitarian needs and services is critical therefore CIDO will adopt an integrated approach and coordinate with WASH, Health, MHPSS, FSL, Education and Nutrition partners to prevent protection risks and to provide timely GBV response services to men, women, boys and girls affected by multiple shocks.The response is strategic to the attainment of Cluster objectives and align to activities, indicators, and outcomes of the HRP 2021. CIDO project staff are trained in GBV in emergencies and adheres to the AAP principles as well as PSEA principles while delivering the activities at the project sites The project activities focus on mitigating GBV risks, preventing and responding to GBV cases through; WGFS programming, Case management / Individual PSS, community Outreach and dialogues, legal services, EMAP and safe referrals. It is expected that by the end of the project period, lives of vulnerable women and girls will be saved, they will have increased access to multi sector services , life skills and positive coping mechanisms entrenched in them.It is anticipated that through EMAP boys and men will be better informed on GBV prevention mechanisms in emergencies and will make meaningful sustainable contribution to the prevention of GBV .

1 - Needs

According to the South Sudan HNO 2021, Gender-based violence is pervasive in South Sudan. As a result of structural gender inequality and unequal gender power relationships, with women and girls and especially those who are internally displaced or living near cantonment sites, or with a disability continue to suffer from GBV. Flooding, armed conflict in pockets of areas, sub-national violence and COVID-19 are escalating the risk of GBV including Conflict Related Sexual Violence (CRSV) and sexual exploitation and abuse. The condition also compounded reduced access to sexual and reproductive health services for women and girls. Measures adopted to curb the spread of COVID-19 like lockdowns have also contributed to a rise in GBV incidents. In 2021, 2 million people are estimated to be affected by GBV attributed to COVID pandemic and subsequent limit access to services. According to IPC analysis November 2020 indicates that Six counties, including Akobo, Aweil South and Pibor fall under the catastrophic category(IPC 5). Based on nationwide October 2020 FSMNS+ assessment, the risk of GBV is identified high as 49 per cent of surveyed households expressed concern on GBV and 34 per cent of households travel more than 30 minutes to fetch water. Similarly, 49 per cent of affected women and girls who are in need of services have no access to GBV services and only 12 per cent of the community have awareness on available GBV services. With this trend women and girls living in Aweil South and Aweil West are likely to be trapped in a web of transactional sex in desperation to provide food for their children. Based on the 2020 GBV IMS data analysis, 29 per cent of GBV survivors in need of safe house did not obtain the service because of lack of safe houses. Reach assessments of January 2021 reported an increase in Intimate partner violence in Yei county and rape cases perpetuated by men in uniform. IRNA assessment for October 2020 indicated massive abduction, revenge killings and forced early marriage of women and girls attributed to subnational violence among Pibor and Akobo communities .Increased risk of GBV coupled with gaps in response services necessitates an urgent need for strengthened primary GBV prevention, integration of GBV risk mitigation in other sectors, and scaling up GBV response services. Provision of safe housing, dignity kits and livelihood support continue to be a high priority. The continuous conflict dynamics and natural disasters perpetuated women’s vulnerability and exposure to GBV. As such, many women and girls stay in the PoC sites, transitioned to the IDP camps because of concerns about sexual violence and other forms of GBV. Formal and customary laws on marriage, land and property rights, among other support discrimination against women and afford them few legal rights.This project intends to fully coordinate with sectoral partners and promote integration to ensure girls, women boys and men access other basic services . CIDO will conduct joint outreach campaigns for GBV prevention messaging, and psychosocial services within the GBV minimum package per IASC guidelines and coordinate with health partners to provide SRH, CMR and MHPSS services.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During AAP sessions , CIDO team and committee on AAP and GBV will provide information on PSEA to all beneficiaries including how to identify and report PSEA cases. The beneficiaries will be made aware that vendors , CIDO contractors, CIDO staff and volunteers are all required to sign codes of conduct and commit to protect beneficiaries against sexual exploitation and abuse. IEC materials on PSEA will be sourced from the Lead (UNFPA) translated and distributed in all project locations.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CIDO will establish a Complaint and Feedback mechanism and establish Help desk during dignity kit distributions. There will be Community communication and engagement (CCE) before the project begins to harmonise the expectations of the community in regards to the project activities vs the available services. During CCE , consultations will be made with communities to establish committees which will oversee AAP issues. The committee on AAP will ensure that voices of the most vulnerable are heard .The team will be careful to provide opportunity to people living with disability, women and girls to air their views in local languages through separate FGDs and ensure that they are included in various leadership roles like being part of the complaint committees.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community Initiative for Development Organization (CIDO) is committed to ensuring that frontline staff engage in timely and proper Community Communication and Engagement (CCE) while being guided by the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) guidelines all through the project implementation. CIDO will conduct entry and exit meetings prior to the end of the project implementation to have discussions with not only community leaders but also directly with all targeted beneficiaries who are women, men girls and boys to capture their interests and recommendations and asses the vulnerability. In addition, CIDO will conduct awareness sessions on AAP rights and how to use the established Complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFMs). The AAP committee composed of community members representing men, women, elderly, youth , people with disability will be established to ensure communities rights and dignity is respected by all stakeholders.There will be an open-door policy, complaints, response, and feedback mechanism (CRFM) boxes, hotline and CRFM desks set up at each distribution site where beneficiaries will be given opportunity to air their feedback to the project team through using both leaders and open-door policy. The hotline will be linked directly to the management. This will facilitate Communication, transparency, and Community full engagement.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CIDO team will compile feedback from the committees and beneficiaries and present to the management in Juba who will confirm the grievances and compliments . Feedback on how the complaints have been addressed on a daily basis on ground will also be shared with the management and will be used to define further actions to improve service delivery. There will be project review meetings where project staff and management will analyze the usefulness of the CRFM , information gathered and how the system can be made better to encourage all beneficiaries to use it freely. Feedback from beneficiaries will be used to improve programming and adjust weaker areas accordingly.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

CIDO will ensure all staff are trained on data management and security. The management will provide lockable safe boxes in all field locations for keeping confidential files and encourage the use of passwords to protect soft copies of data.CIDO will coordinate with HLP partners to conduct rapid HLP assessments with focus on the immediate protection needs of affected people and the potential risks they face, including their access to different shelter types, security of tenure, including risk of forced eviction. Assessments at this stage may also be able to get an overview of the level of damaged/destroyed HLP and any (secondary) occupation trends. This will define ways of engaging the local authority and raising awareness on HLP rights among crisis-affected populations, including the services available to support claiming such rights (e.g., legal assistance, dispute resolution, women property rights) and local rules and regulations on HLP matters (e.g., rental/lease agreements,). CIDO will also consult with all groups within affected populations to identify most effective and appropriate methods of awareness-raising Community engagement and communication including with the local leadership in all aspects of the project will mitigate external interference likely to spark conflict. CIDO will adhere to principals of protection and the Do no Harm policy while engaging with all stakeholders .CIDO will work closely with county security organs and local authority to prioritize safety of staff, lives and property of community members through local dialogues and peaceful negotiations.To promote beneficiary access to CRFM mechanisms, CIDO Project team will use Community Communication and Engagement (CCE) strategies while being guided by the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) guidelines all through the project implementation. CIDO will put in place AAP committees in all project locations to monitor and promote rights of beneficiaries as far as access to quality services is concerned.To mitigate the risk of explosive hazards in the project locations, CIDO will coordinate with Mine Action partners through sharing data in subnational and national cluster meetings and use their expert recommendations to advise on safety of both beneficiaries and staff.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

CIDO team will coordinate with other sectoral partners to ensure beneficiaries are aware of the services available and the referral system/pathways. Notably referral systems and frameworks contain vital information on Health, Psychosocial support/Mental Health, Legal, livelihood, safety etc and contacts of service providers. Joint activities will be carried out with partners in all project locations which will include safety audits, dissemination of referral pathways and outreach messages conducted at all NFI distribution sites. Women facing HLP issues will be assisted and referred to HLP partners and legal actors for further support.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

This will be achieved through coordination with other sectoral partners to integrate disaster risk mitigation strategies known to cause displacements and aggravates food insecurity like conflict and floods. CIDO will coordinate capacity building activities on life-skills, coping strategies and durable solutions with partners while promoting actionable risk mitigation strategies like building flood control structures (dykes), conflict sensitivity and promoting peaceful co-existence among communities with active involvement of women as stakeholders This project will coordinate with FSL partners to conduct joint distributions of fishing kits, Survival kits and essential NFIs with clear messages on durable solutions. CIDO will refer women and girls for livelihood support to the FSL partners. In addition, CIDO will conduct joint outreach messages with other sectoral partners on community led disaster risk mitigation , early warning and response mechanisms and protection of livelihoods from loss.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

CIDO will participate in Joint interventions with WASH, FSL, S/NFI and Nutrition to provide cross-sectoral basic services . The project team will use the existing referral pathways to refer beneficiaries to other sectoral services in circumstances where the services cannot be provided. In addition, the project will make use of gender analysis reports to adjust activities accordingly and meet the needs of both men and women, boys and girls in dignity and safety.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

• The project will conduct Joint assessments and joint outreach campaigns with other sectoral partners in Health, Nutrition, WASH and FSL to provide clear messaging on the linkages of sectoral services to reducing malnutrition among children under 5 years. The activities will also allow communities to provide useful feedback to be used by partners in advocacy for nutrition.• CIDO team will make use of the referral system and ensure that malnourished children who accompany their mothers to WGFS centers are referred appropriately for services.
