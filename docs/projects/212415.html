Project Summary

The nutrition team Is comprised of 2 doctors, 4 nutrition assistants/nurses, 8 nutrition OTP daily workers, 2 IYCF counselors, 6 IYCF daily workers, other support and technical staff. They will continue the CMAM activities in the locations strategically selected in 2023 based on high numbers of beneficiaries and low service coverage. The locations include: Washokani, Serekaniye, Tel Hajar, Tel Beydar, Tel Barak, Sfaia, Tweina, Al Gharra, Areesha, and Gweiran. Community mobilization activities are conducted to increase awareness about malnutrition and Samaritan’s Purse OTP program. Daily workers trained by the team will be continuing awareness and patient referrals in respective communities. The OTP/TSFP team consists of a lead doctor, nutrition assistants, and additional daily workers to support operations. The team conducts weekly malnutrition screenings in each location using both MUAC and weight for height (WFH) measurement. Children presented with acute malnutrition are enrolled in the OTP program and receive RUTF based on WHO simplified protocol. The lead doctor is responsible for assessing children, providing medications in alignment with WHO’s CMAM guidelines, referring patients with complicated severe malnutrition to inpatient care at a stabilization center, and individual IYCF education with IYCF key messaging. All enrolled beneficiaries are followed up weekly or bi-weekly according to the national guidelines for MAM and SAM follow up until a maximum of 16 week stay in the program. The team closely follow up with all cases to achieve optimal outcomes defined by recovery rates and defaulter, and death rates.
Acute malnutrition among children under five years (CU5) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) will be reduced through a community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) intervention which includes provision of ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF), referrals to inpatient care for children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM), and educating caregivers on Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices through mother to mother to support groups (MtMSG).
The program will deliver IYCF education in two different modalities; short IYCF messaging to caregivers during patient visits and in-depth IYCF counseling and support through MtMSGs. All primary caregivers who accompany children to OTP/TSFP sites for nutrition screening will receive IYCF messages from a dedicated promoter. Mothers of children who are admitted to the treatment program are followed up at the community level through MtMSGs. Each group will meet weekly for 12 weeks and will focus on peer discussions of IYCF knowledge, challenges, and provide breastfeeding support. The team will also join with other organization to conduct the mass awareness campaign in conjunction with the World breastfeeding week in August 2024. Additionally, the IYCF will conduct nutrition fairs in selected communities to promote positive behaviors among the caregivers. These nutrition fairs will target other male and female household members such fathers and grandmothers as well. This scale-up in IYCF promotion is in keeping with the goals set forth by the NES NWG to provide a holistic approach to the deteriorating nutrition situation.

Protection Risk Analysisa)Staff or patients assaulted en route to/from the mobile unit locations; b)Risk to beneficiaries related to the receipt of ready-to-use therapeutic food (nutrition program) and/or assistive devices and/or medication, the perceived economic value of which may make the beneficiaries or their families targets within the community. c) Risk to beneficiaries around inequitable access to programming for females and children, due to cultural context which gives preference to adult males as recipients of programming and/or programming commodities. d) Risk to female beneficiaries of forced pregnancy in order to have them qualify for nutrition programming benefiting pregnant and lactating women. All staff involved in the project are trained in managing and/or referring specific gender and protection concerns and have an operational awareness of protection risks/issues. A safeguarding focal point is assigned, and the complaints/feedback mechanism means that feedback is captured from remote/off-site locations and acted upon. PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategyRisks identified: • Contracted doctors violating PSEA policy; • Beneficiaries exploited by SP staff members, partners, and/or vendors. Samaritan's Purse mitigates PSEA risks with the following measures: • Background checks on all personnel, safeguarding training for all staff, protection messaging displayed in the clinic, clear reporting system for violations. There is an HR and complaints processing department to support this risk mitigation. • A safeguarding focal point is assigned, and the complaints/feedback mechanism means that feedback is captured from remote/off-site locations and acted upon. • Reporting mechanisms for each project. • Hotline. • Email hotline.Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures• Security situation or border restrictions cause the team to lose access to NES for a period of time. This is mitigated through the CoH staff and SP staff being able to continue activities in the interim period. • COVID-19 cases amongst health worker staff, SP or partner. Continued training and enforcement of infection prevention and control protocols. • Pockets of higher risk area due to tribal tension and ISIS sleeper cell activity. Mitigated through security assessment of the area to identify processes and protocols and coordination with local community actors and members. • Consistent provision of medication and consumables. The supply chain has remained relatively stable as SP procures all medications and consumables locally within NES. Samaritan's Purse has been able to successfully mitigate medication and supply shortages resulting from supply chain instability by setting up contracted agreements for the supply of 6-9 months' worth of medication from several local vendors.
