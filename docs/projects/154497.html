Project Summary

The project aims at ensuring the continuity of quality health care services for most vulnerable people in
KRI, Ninewa Governorates and northern - central governorates of Iraq through the provision of comprehensive curative quality health services for Iraqi IDPs hosted in Ashti and Arbat camps and the provision of rehabilitation and social reintegration services for amputee patients referred from all the governorates of KRI and Iraq to the EMERGENCY Rehabilitation Center in Sulaymaniyah. The first component of the project focuses on the provision of essential packages of health services to IDPs (2822 men, 3304 women, 3391 boys and 3691 girls) depending on humanitarian aid, in close coordination with the stakeholders working in the area. Comprehensive health care services include the provision of free health care (curative and preventive) and referral system for emergency and elective cases. The second component focuses on the provision of medical support and rehabilitation services to people (3797 men, 763 women, 1199 boys and 241 girls) who have been heavy injured in most conflict affected areas, where Mines and IEDs are still causing several victims even after the end of fighting.
This component will be implemented in close collaboration with the functional Rehabilitation Centers of the Iraqi governorates, as well as with the local authorities (MoH and DoHs). The project strategy covers the gaps of the local capacity to respond adequately to the humanitarian crisis and combines short terms activities (provision of services) with long term ones (training on the job and capacity building of local staff) in order to ensure the sustainability of the action, in the sight of a successful hand-over to the local authorities. An accurate needs assessment, as well as a long experience in the country, has permitted to identify the health needs of the beneficiaries and to define the activities in order to cover the specific necessities of every category of beneficiaries: women, girls, men and boys, elderly and disabled people.
A handover plan will be written in collaboration with Sulaymaniyah DoH in order to ensure a smooth exit strategy as well as the continuation of the activities. Monthly meetings will be organized in order to define the handover steps and to increase the involvement of DOH in the management of Ashti PHCC.

Needs

Even after the military operations against the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL) have ended, Iraq still require strong efforts on humanitarian aid: over 1.800.000 of IDPs are still in need of assistance, while the population still present in the post conflict-affected areas, requires medical support rehabilitation services.The situation of IDPs is extremely volatile and constantly under modification. IOM (October 2018) estimates that more than four million people have returned to their home since 2014. The conditions facing returning families vary enormously: some return areas are contaminated by explosive hazards, public infrastructure and private housing have been destroyed and damaged in at least half of all retaken areas and movement of returnees coming back to their home did not occurred as expected. The Central and Northern Governorates concentrate most of those who remain displaced: difficulties in returning to the habitual residence are different, but those who remain in displacement are often the poorest or the most vulnerable. IDPs already living in Sulaymaniyah camps are still in need to be supported by humanitarian assistance, as any prospective of return or relocation is not likely to happen in the next future. A recent survey conducted by REACH in August 2018 shows that 84% of the IDPs living in Sulaymaniyah governorate will remain in the current location in the next 3 months. If we extend the analysis to 12 months more than 45% of IDPs has no intention to return. Ashti camp hosts the biggest population of IDPs in Sulaymaniyah governorate while Arbat is the second biggest camp in the area. According to the last camp manager data (October 2018), Ashti camp hosts approximately 11.500 persons (2385 families) while the Arbat one hosts round 1,700 individuals (370 families). 53% of the camps’ population is composed by people under 18 years old (28% male and 27% female). The male component represents 49% of the total camps’ population while the female one represents 51% of the total. 0.67% (83.12% affected by physical disability and 16.88% by mental disability) of the camp population is disable and in need of specific assistance. Considering the actual living conditions and the external constraints that those populations are facing, ensuring the continuity of the primary health medical care in these camps is essential to not creating gaps in the humanitarian assistance. According to EMERGENCY medical data the incidence of different categories of patients are the following: acute conditions (73,1%), chronic condition (8%), nursing care (follow up such as growth monitoring, breast feeding, special cases, referrals 18,9%). This typology of care needs to be provided 24/7 by qualified staff, able to idenitfy the specific medical needs and vulnerability of the different patients' groups.Beyond the situation in camps, the situation in some areas, in particular in Ninewa and Kirikuk continues to be dramatic even after the end of the conflict: thousands of homemade explosives, mines and booby traps are still present. They are buried alongside roads, hidden in farm fields, scattered around schools and inside homes. The immediate consequence of the conflict on the civilian population is an increasing need concerning the rehabilitation of injured people who got amputation. In fact, after the treatment received in the hospitals, trauma patients are often sent back to camps or to their homes, where services are inadequate or absent. Follow up of these patients is difficult due to high number of security checks that restrain movement of people. Also, people movements are possible only if authorized by official documents released by recognized medical authorities or organizations, which makes necessary to develop a structured system for the follow up of patients living out of areas under the Kurdish control. The high number of trauma injured reported are at risk to become a new vulnerable part of the community who has lost forever the possibility to return to their living condition before the beginning of the conflict. Only in Ninewa governorate are registered more than 4400 (668 victims of the recent war). The difficulties of the rehabilitation centers in Iraq to respond to the needs of those individuals represent one of the major gaps in the country that can have dramatic consequences on the mid and long term. The aim of EMERGENCY is to cover this gap ensuring a referral service from most affected area (Mosul) to Sulaymaniyah and a complete and comprehensive treatment to the amputees: provision of free of charge and high quality ortho – prosthesis, physiotherapy, hospitalization, psychosocial support and social reintegration activities. All the activities will be implemented paying a particular attention to the specific age group as well as to the gender dimension.

Activities

- PHCC fully operational in Ashti camp: EMERGENCY provides primary, curative, chronic and preventive health care within PHCC for the people living in Ashti and Arbat IDPs camps. A triage post settled in Arbat IDPs camp with 2 nurses present 24/7 permits to identify, stabilize and transfer the patients, according to the needs, to Ashti PHCC or to the secondary and tertiary health centers in Sulaymainiyah. The Center also provides gynecological and midwifery services for women, in addition to a vaccination and growth control program for children, antenatal and postnatal care. In order to ensure the access to the most vulnerable people, a referall service is available also within the camp for elderly, severe chronic patients and disable people. EMERGENCY also holds training courses on the job for the local medical staff. In 2019 EMERGENCY aims at ensuring qualitative health services to almost 5000 patients per month (41% male, 58% female, 24% under 5 years old and 41% under 14 years old); - Referral system for secondary and tertiary care including specialized services: emergency and elective cases are referred to secondary and tertiary healthcare facilities of the region for investigation and hospitalization. The patients are stabilized in the PHCCs and then referred to secondary and tertiary healthcare facilities by ambulances or minivan accompanied by a nurse. In 2019 EMERGENCY aims at ensuring this service to almost 280 patients per month (19% urgent referral and 81% elective referral); - Community health worker teams are in place and promote preventive, promotive and curative services: EMERGENCY has implemented a health promotion program by training Community Health Promoters who work in the Centres and in the camps to promote health awareness; particularly with regard to the most critical issues in the camps, such as infectious disease prevention, and hygiene. The principal target of this activity will be the women, considering their educative role in the family.- Access to free of charge referral services for amputee patients living in Mosul area in need of ortho - prosthesis: in collaboration with DoH Ninewa and the Mosul Rehabilitation Centre, the patients are registered, assessed and prepared by a team composed by physiotherapists and surgeons. When the patients are ready to receive prosthesis are referred to Sulayminiyah through a referral system supervised by EMERGENCY and implemented by a local NGO in charge of the preparation of the documentations necessary to ensure a smooth and safe transfer. In 2019 EMERGENCY aims at ensuring this service for more than 300 amputees (4% girls, 20% boys, 13% women and 63% men). - Reception of the amputees in the Sulaymaniyah Rehabilitation Centre: The Sulaymaniyah Rehabilitation Centre ensures to the beneficiaries of the project their hospitalization in the hostels (one for men, one for women and one for children) of the facility, guaranteeing food, cleaning services and patients hygiene materials. According to the specific medical conditions of the patient, the Centre will prepare specific alimentary regimes: diabetic, hypertensive, hypotensive, etc. Moreover the Centre will accept that patients belonging to the following categories are accompanied by one relative:• Children (below 14 years old);• Female patients;• Bilateral amputations;• Diabetic patients or patients suffering from a chronic disease;• Wheelchair patients. In 2019 EMERGENCY aims at ensuring this service for more than 650 patients coming from all the Iraqi governorates.- Fabrication and fit of free of charge and high quality ortho - prosthesis for amputees: The expert and high qualified ortho - tecnicians working in the Sulaymaniyah Rehabilitation Center will provide a complete care to the referred patients. This process will take between 24 to 72 hours, according to the type of prosthesis. In 2019 EMERGENCY aims at delivering almost 800 ortho - prosthesis (86.30% for male patients and 13.70% for female patients). 84.52% lower limb prosthesis (72% below knee and 28% above knee) and 15.48% upper limb prosthesis (88% below elbow and 12% above elbow);- Provision of physiotherapy and rehabilitation sessions: The patients will be assessed and evaluated by the physiotherapy team, in order to start ad hoc process of physiotherapy and rehabilitation sessions: exercises, training on correct prosthesis use, maintenance of the prosthesis and health education (hygiene, bandaging, home exercises, etc.). The duration of this activity will vary from 1 week to 2 weeks, according to the type of disability and the physical condition of the patient (age, sex, etc.). In 2019 EMERGENCY aims at ensuring more than 3300 physiotherapy sessions to the patients.

Protection Risk Analysis

The major protection risk to which the IDPs population in Ashti and Arbat camps can be exposed is the deterioration of security conditions that prevent the PHCC to be regularly opened and accessible. Another risk is the insurgence of epidemic outbreaks in the camps, because of the deterioration of the hygiene conditions. On the other side the deterioration of the security conditions and the strengthening of security control can prevent a smooth and safe referral of patients to secondary and tertiary health facilities. The last point can affect also the transfer and movement of amputees to Sualyaminiyah rehabilitation Centre as well as the mobility and monitoring visit to Mosul.

Mitigation Measures

Regarding the first risk mentioned EMERGENCY will guarantee the continuity of the project's activities implementing mobile clinics provided by DoH and managed by EMERGENCY medical staff. Regarding the second risk, EMERGENCY will implement epidemic surveillance comprehensive of early warning system. In case of epidemic disease outbreak EMERGENCY will comply with its contingency plan for specific epidemic (such as cholera) and local contingency plan applied at national level.Finally regarding the last point EMERGENCY will advocate in collaboration with the protection cluster in order to ensure the essential health service to the most vulnerable people.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Considering the specificity of the activities proposed, the involvement of the beneficiaries in the design of the action is indirect. However, for EMERGENCY is very important to implement mechanisms of mutual feedback in order to improve the quality of the services provided. EMERGENCY will implement the following mechanisms of involvement and feedback:- A complain box were the patients can directly address their complaints and their suggestions,- Scheduled meeting with the camp managers and the director of the Rehabilitation Centers,- Mission of monitoring and evaluation conducted by the health promoters of EMERGENCY inside theCamp,- Mission of monitoring and evaluation conducted by the Sulaymaniyah Rehabilitation Centre in the area of provenance of the patients.These mechanisms implemented in the past were used for the definition of needs and the design of the proposed action. These mechanisms will permit also to monitor the satisfaction of the beneficiaries of the proposed action during all the implementation period as well as to evaluate the impact of the intervention. The involvement of the national staff will be more direct and essential for the definition of the trainings' topics.Finally, EMERGENCY will ensure the accountability to the affected population throughout IDP Call Center, CwC, RPAs, monitoring missions and RNAs.
