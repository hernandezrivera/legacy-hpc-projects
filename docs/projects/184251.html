Project Summary

IOM WASH is proposing to contribute to lifesaving of access to safe and equitable basic WASH services to overall 281,400 beneficiaries from vulnerable Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) and host-communities in multiple regions in Somalia.
Specifically, the project will target 168,840 Non displaced people (children 104,681; women 27,014; Disabled 25,326; and elderly 11,819) and 112,560 displaced populations (children 69,787; Women 18,010; Disabled 16,884; and elderly 7,879)
IOM will rehabilitate 60 strategic water sources for multi-use (human and animals) and further 55 community berkads (water reservoirs) that will provide continued, equitable, safe, sufficient and sustainable water to regions, targeted by the cluster, prone to natural disasters.
IOM further proposes to construct 3,300 new emergency sanitation facilities (following cluster specifications) targeting populations without access to basic sanitation and hygiene.
The project will also strengthen the capacity of the Federal Government of Somalia and Federal Members States, community leaders and water committees/technicians for better coordination with government authorities and communities and sustainability of the constructed/ rehabilitated WASH infrastructures. In addition, the project will nurture community participation in good hygiene practices through training and community sensitization campaigns.
The project is aligned with protection mainstreaming to ensure safety and dignity of affected populations without causing harm. Affected populations and all relevant stakeholders including men, women, boys, girls, elderly and people with disability will be involved in decision making throughout the project activities/cycle. Furthermore, the project will be a part of an IOM integrated emergency response program and resilience building, where possible, efforts will be made to integrate IOM health, nutrition, shelter, CCCM and protection activities.

1. - Projects activities

-Provision of equitable, safe, sufficient and appropriate emergency water supply (with temporary distribution systems) to 115,374 people (40,381 girls; 34,612 boys, 23,075 women and 17,306 men) in vulnerable communities.-Rehabilitate 60 strategic existing water sources systems for multi-use to provide continued, equitable, safe, sufficient and sustainable safe water to 104,118 people (36,441 girls; 31,235 boys; 20,824 women and 15,618 men)-Construction/rehabilitation of 3300 emergency sanitation facilities (with internal locks) targeting 66,598 people (23,309 girls; 19,979 boys; 13,320 women; 9,990 men) who do not have any access to immediate provision of basic sanitation and hygiene. Different age and gender groups (women, girls, men, boys), persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalized individuals will be consulted to collect accurate information about their specific requirements and preferences for the location and design of the sanitation facilities.-Rehabilitation and improvement of 55 communal water berkads (sizing, cover, lining, water extraction) to provide safe water to 61,908 people (21,668 girls; 18,572 boys; 12,382 women and 9,286 men). -Dissemination of key hygiene messages, including COVID-19 preventive messages, and distribution of hygiene kits (inclusive of menstrual hygiene management items, through hygiene promotion campaigns targeting individuals (girls, boys, women, men), groups, households and institutions (schools). These will be conducted by trained hygiene promoters (50% men and 50% female) and mass media communications to 281,400 people. -Provide capacity building to 600 (300 men and 300 women) water committees, local technicians, community leaders and local authorities on proper operations and maintenance of water services.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Using a voucher system is cost-effective and allows for timely response since the transportation and logistics costs are lower. It provides beneficiaries with a greater choice of what they want to spend and increases their dignity by eliminating long queues at water points. Voucher system further allows money to be spent locally which will have a multiplier effect on local economic activities by increasing production in other sectors such as agriculture and livelihoods. Vouchers will be handed out based on needs. Vulnerable members of the community (including female headed household, elderly, people with disabilities) will be included to ensure inclusive distribution.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

By using a voucher system for hygiene kits and the transportation of water, it is expected to generate demand for products and services which will catalyze the growth of the local market. When required, market assessment will be conducted, to see how the local market can be strengthened more effectively.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Risk that marginalized groups do not participate in consultations:a. IOM will gather data (age, sex, diversity) of communities and beneficiaries for effective and informative programming. This will help identify vulnerable and marginalized persons for planning, inclusion purposes and engagement b. A systematic outreach process will be done to pre-register marginalized/minority vulnerable groups to avoid exclusion c. Efforts will be made to ensure that information regarding provision water will be disseminated to all members of communities (including women, girls, boys, men) through appropriate communication channels such as radio d. An inclusive feedback mechanism that will include all beneficiaries will be put in place which will help to evaluate selection criteria and overall services gaps. e. In all water collection points, a mechanism will be established that will give priority to those who cannot stand in line for long periods, such as older persons, persons with disability, unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating women4.2 Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance or violence during/after registration or distribution: a. Water locations will be put in central, visible places and not more than 500 meters away from settlements. Location will be decided following consultations with women and girls. This will help to protect them from risks of all forms of GBV when accessing services b. Separate toilet facilities with internal locks and secondary enclosures will be constructed for women and men with clear labels c. In locations where threats are anticipated, frequency of hygiene kits distribution will be modified and door-to-door distribution considered, to avoid sexual abuse and extortion while carrying kits. d. Close coordination with Shelter/NFI partners to put solar light installations at WASH community centers to deter any risk of violence against women and girls e. Distributions of water will be done during day light to allow beneficiaries who often women and girls reach home on time.
