Project Summary


UNIDOR seeks to strongly advocate and conduct protection assessments to enable timely and appropriate emergency programming to the most vulnerable populations. UNIDOR already has presence in Leer, Mayendit, Koch, Panyinjiar, Jur River, Cuiebet, Yirol West and Pariang. In Leer, Mayendit, Panyinjiar and Koch.and has static presence in Jur River conducting Health activities, Nutrition in Cuiebet and Yirol west and WASH in Pariang. UNIDOR operates in a multi-sectoral approach and provides a protection lens in all its programmes. There are already established bases in the selected locations and this will make it easier for integration of activities within sectors, promoting value for money and already fostered relationships with authorities and communities. The protection department will only focus on hiring staff and implementation.UNIDOR, will try and gather as much information as possible from different sources in an effort to triangulate it to identify protection risks faced by communities. There is an open recognition that some locations, even within close reach from our existing static field sites, are not covered by our existing and ongoing project activities.
The protection team will be hired and deployed at the different field sites where they will assess the most critical and high priority needs of the affected communities. While UNIDOR already has a static presence in the chosen locations, the proposed project activities will focus on locations under-served by the existing projects. The teams will comprise of one Team Leader per location and three Officers with knowledge and technical knowhow on Child Protection and Gender Based Violence. A technical Specialist in Protection is currently managing the GBV and CP projects and this will be an added advantage in terms of competence and effectiveness.
Methods used in the assessments will include Focus Group Discussions, Key Informant Interviews and Direct Observation to identify major protection risks. The target group will include Host communities, IDPs as well as returnees. In all assessments, UNIDOR will ensure that the issue of age, gender and diversity will be taken into account when assessing needs. UNIDOR will coordinate with the Protection Cluster to confirm the assessment locations and ensure that assessment reports are submitted for them to inform partners to respond, wherever possible. The assessment results will also be shared with other partners on a need to know basis. Urgent protection needs identified will be responded to with the purpose of increasing safety and security of affected populations facing imminent danger or risks. Activities will include awareness raising campaigns, advocacy, information dissemination, service mapping and development of referral pathways, with the assistance of Sub Clusters, Task Force and Technical Reference Groups. The assumption is that behavioral key messaging targeted at key groups promotes a transformative social change which helps reduce violence and its impact on communities. Awareness raising will seek to address key issues associated with GBV in the community, with men and boys also included to promote their roles as agents of social change.
The team will also focus on protection mainstreaming to other sectors. Existing Community Based Networks will be mapped and trained to enable them to prevent and respond to violence. Meetings with other service providers, law enforcement agents and other community based child protection networks will be done in order to review the quality of care that is provided within the referral pathway and identifying possibilities for strengthening service provision. UNIDOR is already a member of the Protection Cluster, Child Protection Sub Cluster, Case Management Task Force, Child Protection Strategic Advisory Group, PSS Working Group and the Co- Chair for the Caring for Child Survivors Technical Reference Group. UNIDOR will continue reporting monthly and give updates to Clusters.

1 - Needs

According to the (HRP 2019), the recently revitalized peace process promises to offer new opportunities in 2019 for South Sudan’s women, men and children. However, South Sudan remains in a serious humanitarian crisis due to the cumulative effects of years of conflict and violence against civilians, which have destroyed people’s livelihoods and forced 4.2 million people to flee their homes. The number of people who require humanitarian or protection assistance in 2019 remains high at seven million, just like 2018. Some 2.2 million children are out of school and nearly 600,000 pregnant and lactating women will be acutely malnourished in 2019. Gender roles have changed as women take up the role of bread winners in the family by moving in search of food, water, firewood and petty jobs which expose them to exploitation, sexual violence and abuse. Girls have been forced to marry and at times they have also multiple forms of violence and have limited support systems and access to information. As a result of the early marriages, girls have faced a lot of medical complications which sometimes has resulted in their deaths during delivery. UNIDOR will continue building capacity and provide training and guidance to the already existing Community Based Networks to prevent and respond to child abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence against children. The main threats facing vulnerable populations have shifted little since 2018, with targeted attacks, conflict-related sexual violence, forced recruitment, abduction, forced displacement, deliberate destruction of property and family separation remaining key areas of concern. As such, the main needs in terms of general protection, child protection, GBV and UXO remain the same. A key difference from 2018 however involves prioritizing a focus on addressing resilience capacity building and housing, land and property issues. In a similar vein, protection needs remain.According to the 2019 HRP, despite large-scale humanitarian assistance, more South Sudanese have been pushed into hunger than ever before. Over 60 percent of the population, some 6.1 million people, were dealing with severe food insecurity during the peak of the lean season over the summer months, with a decrease to about 4.4 million by the end of 2018. The scale and severity of this food crisis is expected to bleed into 2019, with crises deepening in areas where people are already dealing with enhanced vulnerability due to persistent displacement, conflict and lack of access to markets and/or humanitarian services. According to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC), Phase 5 “Catastrophe” status, Leer and Mayendit are also included. Children remain at particular risk of critical acute malnutrition. There is therefore need to prioritize their needs.Displaced people are also likely to continue returning home in all locations as they begin to generate greater confidence in the peace agreement. However for now, the scale and flow of such movements remain difficult to project. According to a Situation Report dated 11/10/2019 and a UNOCHA snapshot dated 21/10/2019, floods caused by torrential rains have also worsened the living conditions of South Sudanese people in many parts of the country in the past few months. Mayendit and Panyinjiar Counties have also not been spared. This calls for urgent assessments and it is expected that there will be increase in the number of displacements, family separation, need for urgent assistance hence the need to have financial, human and material resources to meet the needs of men,women,boys and girls.

2 - Activities

Inorder to address the identified needs, the following activities will be conducted:-Conducting static and mobile protection monitoring and assessments to inform protection and overall humanitarian response. The in-depth assessments will assist in strengthening and establishing referral pathways and will also help in measuring the type of service, availability and quality of the service. Creating referral pathways will ensure a well-coordinated protection response that is both age- and sex-sensitive. -Providing advice to agencies providing material aid and food distribution on protection mainstreaming in project locations. Identification & prioritization of vulnerable individuals during registration and distribution. UNIDOR will be directly involved in ensuring the centrality of protection at the distribution sites. UNIDOR will also team up with other partners providing IPMT in providing resource persons and staff during RRM and joint assessments. -Conducting protection mainstreaming, peace building and risk mitigation trainings for staff and other partner agencies and local community structures. -Facilitating monthly meetings between beneficiaries, security actors and humanitarian actors. -Conducting capacity building workshops and trainings for community members to strengthen their capacity to prevent, protect and respond to violence. -Conducting advocacy and awareness raising campaigns and public drama sessions on protection to advocate for people’s rights, strengthen prevention and response mechanisms and ensure a sustainable means to tackle harmful social norms. -The project implementation also be closely monitored to ensure targets are achieved in a timely manner and in a quality manner.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The project will ensure that protection is mainstreamed into other sectors.This will be done through trainings and sessions.Emphasis will be on the principles and how other sectors can ensure that communities are not exposed to risks because of humanitarian actions. UNIDOR will make sure that interventions planned strive to reduce their exposure to GBV and other forms of violence.Activities will be conducted at safer places which are within reach of the target groups and all assessments will be done during day time.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

UNIDOR will ensure that the project adheres to the SPHERE standards and the Do No Harm framework.During all the project phases, the team will ensure that they seek to identify and elevate the needs and voices of the most vulnerable and marginalized community members. During assessments,will participate freely and will be allowed to to identify specific protection risks that different groups face, identify underlying causes and emerging concerns, assess the local capacity for response and solicit solutions from within the community itself. UNIDOR will ensure adherence to policies on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), by putting in place an effective and anonymous complaints mechanism to allow and encourage individuals to report instances of SEA easily, confidentially and safely. A joint complaint and feedback mechanisms for any other matter will also be available and clearly communicated to all beneficiaries prior to any activity and involvement.Project planning will draw heavily upon beneficiary's experiences of security, safety and risks through consultation to identify key areas of programming
