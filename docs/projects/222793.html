Project Summary

According to HNRP 2024, an estimated 9 million South Sudanese will need humanitarian assistance due to critical needs. The acute food insecurity and malnutrition crisis is worsening, exacerbated by economic instability, climatic shocks such as widespread flooding (a 10% increase since 2023), and protracted conflicts and insecurity. The influx of returnees and refugees from the Sudan conflict further exacerbates the situation, straining an already fragile country. Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people faced Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or above) levels of acute food insecurity. Of these, 1.71 million people faced Emergency (IPC Phase 4) levels, and 41,000 faced Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) levels. It is estimated that 10,000 residents of Malakal County (Upper Nile State) and approximately 31,000 South Sudanese returnees from Sudan's ongoing conflict are at Catastrophe-level. Compared to the same period last year, this represents a 500,000-person increase in the number of people facing Crisis or worse (latest published IPC report 2024).
Rubkona County hosts more than 177,000 IDPs across at least 13 sites in Rubkona Town, Bentiu Town, and Rotriak. The sharp population increase over the past decade is attributed to displacements resulting from conflicts, climatic shocks, or poor living conditions in the surrounding areas. The county also received an additional 46,062 returnees from the Sudan crisis between April and August 2023. Due to floodwater left over from 2021, community participation in subsistence livelihoods (agriculture and livestock rearing) significantly reduced (REACH Emergency Food Update, November 2023). In Aweil North, widespread flooding resulted in the rotting of crops, notably sorghum, legumes, and vegetables, which has negatively impacted harvests (IRNA Aweil, North September 2024).
The loss of crop production due to prolonged dry spells, infestation by fall armyworms last year (2023), unprecedented flooding the year before, and worsening inter-communal conflict worsened the Food Security and Livelihood Situation in Abyei. Communities resorted to gathering wild fruits as coping mechanisms. Major trading centers of Southern Abyei were razed and looted during the most recent clashes, including stored food and markets.
Old Fangak Centre is heavily overcrowded, with 3 to 4 households often sharing a single home compound. Internally displaced persons (IDPs) have been integrated into the host community across three residential areas: Hai 44, Hai Matar, and Hai Muzafin. Due to limited space and flooding, there is no farming around the settlement. The flooding has affected 2,377 households, totaling 14,262 individuals, according to the RRC.
In line with HNRP and Food Security and Livelihood Cluster objectives, this project will focus on Objective CO2: Emergency Agriculture and CO3: Resilience Building Activities. Through this project, Coalition for Humanity will target 58,850 households (353,100 individuals) to improve food security and livelihood across 17 priority 1&2 counties. Segregated by gender: 58,000 male, 59,700 female, 117,000 boys, and 118,400 girls.
Coalition for Humanity envisages to implement the following activities integrating strong protection mainstreaming and prioritization of people with special needs including women and children.
1. Community mobilization and sensitization of households
2. Identification, registration verification and distribution of quality livelihood kits to targeted households.
3. Formation and training of lead farmer groups in every county on identification and reporting of early warning signs of pests, diseases and floods.
4. Training of Agricultural extension workers, per county on pest and crop disease control e.g. detection, prevention & eradication.
5. Provision of farm level support to farmers in every county on improved agricultural practices/modern farming methods.
6. Provision of emergency livelihood support to farmers through distribution

2. Project activities

Coalition for Humanity envisages to implement the following activities integrating strong protection mainstreaming and prioritization of people with special needs including women and children.1. Community mobilization and sensitization of households2. Identification, registration verification and distribution of quality livelihood kits to targeted households.3. Formation and training of lead farmer groups in every county on identification and reporting of early warning signs of pests, diseases and floods.4. Training of Agricultural extension workers, per county on pest and crop disease control e.g. detection, prevention & eradication.5. Provision of farm level support to farmers in every county on improved agricultural practices/modern farming methods.6. Provision of emergency livelihood support to farmers through distribution of crop kits, fishing kits and fast maturing vegetable seeds.7. Training community animal health workers (CAHW) on livestock diseases, prevention & treatment especially quarantine measures on zoonotic diseases e.g. RVF, Anthrax, Hemorrhagic septicemia (HS), black quarter among others that are common in these counties.8. Mobilization and sensitization of communities and identification of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs)9. Collection and distribution of Livestock Kits to the CAHWs and regular replenishment.10. Conducting monitoring, surveillance and reporting of diseases in the target areas.11. Formation and training of AAP committees12. Conduct safety audits and GBV risk analysis
