Project Summary

The proposed livelihood and resilience activities include Activity1: Provision of support with Agricultural inputs (Seeds, tools, other inputs) and training; this will be delivered through provision of assistance to produce nutrition rich crops, legume and vegetables, timely to the refugees who have access to farming land and 25% of the target will be from the vulnerable host communities members living with them in the targeted locations. Activity 2: Provision of support with livestock supplies and services (vaccination, treatment, deworming, concentrate animal feed) to the refugees who own livestock; 25% of the target will be from the vulnerable host communities members. Activity 3: Provision of support for Income Generation Activities (IGA) ' such as support with donkey carts, restocking, vocational training', women training on food processing and conservation. Activity 4: The beneficiaries will also be supported with environmental interventions such as production, distribution and planting of multipurpose trees’ seedlings and training of communities’ members especially women on production and use of Fuel Efficient Stoves.

1 - Needs

In Sudan, agriculture and livestock breeding are the main sources of livelihood for more than 70% of the population including refugees. Therefore, support with agriculture and livestock inputs and supplies, along with capacity development for the refugees and the vulnerable host communities in agriculture and livestock production is a priority need as well as strengthening the agricultural extension services and transfer for the targeted beneficiaries. Small-scale home gardens and restocking with small ruminants (milking goats and sheep) are needed to provide opportunities for women headed households as well as to address issues related to malnutrition, especially for children who will be able to access animal protein from the milk, which will be obtained from the goats/sheep. Access to extension services need to be strengthened to increase value-added opportunities for farmers, which go hand in hand with awareness raising on existing services and how to access them. Based on the above analysis, refugees as well as the host communities living with them should be provided with agriculture and livestock inputs, supplies and training to restore their production capacity to become self-reliance.
