Project Summary

The project aims to support the WASH response of the most vulnerable displaced populations in Northwest Syria through implementing the following set of activities:
i) Provision of emergency WASH services to 40 IDP sites (informal camps, planned camps, and reception centres) regularly supporting 80,000 individuals (40, 800 females, 39,200 males, 10,400 young children (<5), 28,000 children (5-17), 33,600 adults (18-59), 8,000 elderly (>59), 22,400 persons with disabilities). Activities include the provision of water trucking, solid waste management, desludging, care and maintenance of latrines, provision of water storage tanks, garbage containers, and WASH NFI kits, as well as hygiene promotion sessions.
ii) Construction/rehabilitation of sewage networks, including the construction of septic tanks to provide an initial treatment of wastewater produced in informal camps. This specific intervention will target 15 informal IDP sites supporting 30,000 individuals (15,300 females, 14,700 males, 3,900 young children (<5), 10,500 children (5-17), 12,600 adults (18-59), 3,000 elderly (>59), 8,400 persons with disabilities).
iii) Enhanced water quality assurance, operation, and maintenance of water supply systems through the provision of solar energy in eight IDP sites, supporting 22,000 individuals (11,220 females, 10,780 males, 2,860 young children (<5), 7,700 children (5-17), 9,240 adults (18-59), 2,200 elderly (>59), 6,160 persons with disabilities).
iv) Implementation of COVID-19 response activities in collective centres and planned camps through the provision of water trucking and the distribution of COVID-19-adapted hygiene kits. In total, the COVID-19 response will target 80,000 individuals (40, 800 females, 39,200 males, 10,400 young children (<5), 28,000 children (5-17), 33,600 adults (18-59), 8,000 elderly (>59), 22,400 persons with disabilities).
Overall, the project is supporting the following strategic objectives set out by the WASH Cluster:
-Support to water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria,
-Deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services, and improve hygienic behaviour and practices of the most vulnerable people.
In total, the project aims to target 110,000 individuals individuals (56,100 females, 53,900 males, 14,300 young children (<5), 38,500 children (5-17), 46,200 adults (18-59), 11,000 elderly (>59), 30,800 persons with disabilities) in Idleb and Aleppo governorates.

4 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (2021), there are 4,167,415 people in need of WASH services across Idleb and Aleppo governorates. Of those displaced in NWS, 28% live in tents (IDP Report Series 2020, Shelter Conditions), most of which are situated in informal camps without adequate water or sanitation infrastructure. Without sanitation infrastructure, significant amounts of sewage waste from these camps run into open streams, creating breeding sites for mosquitos, phlebotomine sand flies, and other water-borne vectors. In response, IDPs discharge wastewater (sometimes untreated) into open channels or excavate open cesspits and cover them with plastic sheeting. In other cases, wastewater is desludged and trucked to agricultural land without being treated. This method can impact the food consumed by IDPs, leading to health hazards such as diarrhoea. Moreover, a recent WASH Cluster HH level survey has shown that 78% of people stated that they did not have access to sufficient water during the previous month. Women and children are estimated to make up 76% of the population, while 25% are estimated to be persons with disabilities. These groups are particularly vulnerable to any reduction in WASH services and are likely to face increased protection risks as a result (OCHA NWS Sitrep – February 2021). Compounding the already high WASH needs in NWS, the spread of COVID-19 raises life-threatening risks to IDPs’ health and hygiene. Lastly, fluctuating fuel prices can provide barriers to access to safe and secure water (e.g. water supplied via trucking), thus increasing the need for water facilities which can be maintained and operated through independent energy systems (particularly solar).

5 - Implementation of activities

IOM will work with established partners and coordinate with the WASH Cluster and other stakeholders to ensure needs are met without duplication. For the emergency WASH services, IOM’s IPs will carry out a competitive bidding process to identify contractors for water trucking, desludging, and solid waste management. For water trucking activities, IOM will conduct daily monitoring on chlorination activities and water quality testing through third part monitors. A KAP Survey will be carried out at the start and end of the project to better inform the hygiene promotion curriculum and the emergency WASH programming. For the rehabilitation and construction of sewage networks, IOM’s IPs will carry out technical assessments for each site, including topography studies. For the operation and maintenance of water supply facilities, IOM will work with an experienced IP to procure and set up solar panels to maintain newly established water facilities in eight planned camps. Lastly, for the COVID-19 activities, IOM and its IP will distribute hygiene kits to planned camps and collective centres and provide emergency water trucking to collective centres. For all infrastructure activities (sanitation and water facilities), IOM will pay attention to HLP dynamics and ensure land ownership is identified prior to any infrastructure works, and agreements are signed with landowners and local councils. Third Party Monitoring engineers will conduct regular visits to all locations to assess the progress and ensure adherence to BoQs and designs, as well as identify any issues and propose corrective actions. Projects will be monitored through IOM’s M&E unit who will conduct post-distribution satisfaction surveys with beneficiaries.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

For the provision of water trucking, there is a risk that the quality and quantity of water does not meet the SPHERE standards. To ensure the provision of safe water, regular water quality monitoring and testing will be carried out and water quantity tracking will take place to meet the requirements. Another risk associated to the provision of water is that distribution points and times can pose protection risks for women and girls. To mitigate this risk, community consultations will be held to agree upon the timing of water distribution and water tanks will be installed in line with SPHERE standards to ensure a minimum distance of travel for beneficiaries. For all in-camp activities, there is a potential risk of congesting the sites through the movement of water-trucking, desludging, solid waste management, and provision of solar energy for water facilities. This issue can be mitigated by consulting/engaging the community regarding the services modality/ frequency, truck routes and schedules. Specifically, on the removal of solid waste from the sites, there are associated health risks for those handling the waste. This risk can be mitigated through the provision of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) and ensuring the safety regulations are abided by. Similarly, for the construction/rehabilitation of sewage networks, PPE will be provided to ensure IP staff or contractors are not exposed to unsafe chemicals.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

For all activities, the risk of SEA by IPs and their sub-contractors exists during the physical implementation phase. For this, key mitigation measures include having all IPs ensure that their staff have read and signed their Code of Conduct, have taken part in PSEA trainings, and have a dedicated complaint mechanism in place. Additionally, IPs will ensure the presence of staff during all distribution activities, particularly during water deliveries by contractors, paying special attention for female- and child-headed households to enhance protection of beneficiaries.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

There are several risks which are beyond the project’s control, these include: access restrictions due to the shifting of frontlines/active fighting, aid diversion by armed groups, loss of assets through looting or bombing, non-renewal of the UNSC resolution, inflation of market prices (particularly fuel). The project will take the following measures to mitigate the abovementioned risks: regular communication with IPs to report any security incidents of attempted interference or shifting security risks. In case of sustained insecurity and inaccessibility, IOM will conduct needs assessments to determine alternative locations in coordination with the WASH Cluster and project donors. On aid diversion and loss of assets, IOM will invest in sound security measures in warehouses inside Syria and train staff on how to handle attempted aid diversion. In cases of conflict-induced damages to partners’ warehouses/trucks, IOM will ensure IPs provide timely incident reports to its access unit and share the information with other actors on the ground, donors, and clusters. Lastly, on the inflation of prices, IOM will conduct regular market monitoring. Moreover, IOM will be piloting a solar project to decrease reliance on fuel for the running of water facilities.
