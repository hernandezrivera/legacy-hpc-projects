Project Summary

This project aims to support livelihood stabilization, income recovery, and contribute to early recovery and strengthen resilience of the conflict affected communities, focusing young adults, old adults, and people living with disabilities, through multiple livelihoods interventions such as short-cycle business management training, cash grants, access to microfinance schemes, coaching and other income-generating activities.
This project is an 11 months programme to assist about four thousand eight hundred and seventy eight 4,878 of the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community members in 13 selected geographical areas across the BAY states.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Northeast Nigeria that has endured for over ten years has eroded and destroyed public infrastructure, and access to basic services remain inadequate especially due to the continued influx of internally displaced persons across Borno Adamawa and Yobe, and has also placed additional pressure on existing services. In the most severely affected Borno state and two adjacent states (Adamawa and Yobe), an estimated population of 8.4 million people (59% children, 22% women) are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022. According to the HNO and HRP 2021, an estimated 2.1 million people are in need of early recovery assistance as they suffer worse loss of resilience and drastic lesser livelihood opportunities. Of these, 62% are women who are unable to cope and have seen their resilience undermined by the protracted conflict and socio-economic consequences of the pandemic and about 1.8% are living with disability. More precisely, by population groups, 13% are IDPs, 21% returnees while 65% are host communities across the BAY States. The 37th round of the Displacement Tracking Matrix (IOM – August 2021), estimated the number of IDPs to be 2,005,545 (81% women and children). The number of displaced people increased by 10,490 individuals (1%). Out of the total number of displaced people, 1,633,829 IDPs are currently in Borno, 209,322 in Adamawa and 162,394 in Yobe, while 740,595 returnees are in Borno, 829,594 in Adamawa and 183,295 in Yobe (DTM R37). Schools have been forcibly occupied and used as IDP camps and where camps have been relocated from schools, parents and children have found it difficult to go back to the same schools. In consequence, 78% of households reported insufficient income to meet their basic needs and 20% reported being obliged to adopt negative coping mechanisms, such as the sale of productive assets and food borrowing. Amongst CVA recipients, more than half (52%) reported remaining livelihoods-related needs even after accounting for the humanitarian aid received. This was most likely in Borno, where 63% of CVA recipients reported ongoing livelihoods needs, compared to Adamawa (57%) and Yobe (19%). Tensions rise in host communities where the displacement phenomenon is straining the already-scarce resources, furtherly eroding livelihood and income opportunities. Out of 61 LGAs in the BAY states, 22 are in severe need concerning the lack of sufficient and reliable income which forces them to adopt negative coping mechanisms. Moreover, erratic climatic patterns with recurrent severe floods, NSAG attacks, and overcrowded settlements are limiting and damaging basic public services, such as health and sanitation facilities, waste management services and markets. On average in the BAY states, Over 30% of people do not have access to basic services in the community. In view of CNFs objectives, the IDPs, returnees and host communities need and deserve an opportunity to transition from a humanitarian context, construct their livelihood systems, restoring access to public services, while strengthening their resilience for building and sustaining positive peace in the locations of return. In this context, CNF decided to focus on improving the short-term and long-term livelihood opportunities of vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities paying special attention to the needs of displaced women and their families.

2 - Activities

In-line with the sectors identified gaps and geographical area of targeted response, our activities is structured to cater for the Early Recovery Sectors across the BAY state with focus around Mafa, Konduga, Pulka, Dikwa, and Mungono in Borno, Mahia, Magadali, Michika, Mubi North, and Mubi South in Adamawa and Geidam, Yusufari and Yunusari in Yobe. Our core activities are centered around first three activities of the sector strategic objectives which are: To support livelihood stabilization and income recovery, Provide emergency employment opportunities and Cash-for-Work activities, Deliver vocational training and business skills development and provide support for small scale business establishment (start-up grants or in-kind grants), and Improve community saving, increase access to credit through creation and capacity building of community cooperatives, Village Saving and Loan Associations (VSLAs) and other relevant mechanisms. We will be targeting a total of 4,878 individuals across the identified geographical areas in the BAY state, 582 in Borno, 368 in Adamawa and 3,928 in Yobe. We plan to utilize the support of the able bodied young and old adults through cash for work modality and to further support reduce the negative coping strategy. In community mobilization, project staff will be visiting and holding consultation meetings with the community leaders on the project also get their buy in. CNF will proceed to identify, select and register the beneficiaries which will be done close with the ER&L sector, community leaders in accordance to the set criterial and beneficiaries will be given identification cards upon successful registration to authenticate their participation in this programme. The outlined trainings that informed each activity will be grouped into batches and scheduled per time along the beneficiaries as each set will have their own instructors/facilitators in line with the trade areas of the beneficiaries and of course in their most preferred vocation. Facilitated by competent trainers who are experience in these areas, there will be community level training on saving and loan scheme (CSLS) that will expose them to simple culture of working in teams/group, saving and loaning to themselves. The vocation course for the beneficiaries will be for 6 months to ascertain successful completion before the seed capital will be released which will support the trainees to purchase some initial materials. We shall follow up and ensure the trainees are facilitated to join the Village and savings association (VSLA). We take all standard management practices with high esteem. therefore, this project will provide accountability, transparency, and adherence to humanitarian principles.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

CNF aims to contribute to the HDP-Nexus through a development lens not only taking into account early recovery but also with a core objective to strengthen reliance through livelihood stabilization for IDPs, returnees, and host communities in the identified conflict-affected areas across the BAY states. As partners, and also with the support of the community heads, we intend to achieve the aforementioned by providing vocational training, business skill development, cash-for-work activities, support for a small-scale business establishment to foster livelihood stabilization, economic development, social cohesion, and an improved community-saving mechanisms.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CNF will ensure that protection mainstreaming is realized through regular consultation with the target communities, training and capacity building of staff on PSEA effective reporting channel case management modalities. While encouraging the use of the existing VSO hotlines and emails for reporting cases, suspicions around the violation of primary actors right, we will also, always display the various contacts for reporting cases of protection, fraud, abuse and how primary actors can seek redress at all community meetings, in our offices and partner offices. We will also encourage the use of community score cards to gauge community appreciation of our work and its quality, effectiveness and impartiality of the services we are providing.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure accountability to affected populations, Cosmic Nudge Foundation (CNF) will set up functional and contextually appropriate feedback and accountability mechanisms in such a way that the beneficiaries can confidently and safely relay their complaints, dissatisfaction and suggestions of project implementation.These will be through the use of suggestion boxes that will be fixed at strategic locations within the affected communities, the use of complaint help desk, a toll-free line and a WhatsApp channel where beneficiaries may relay their complaints comfortably. These feedback mechanisms will ensure to provide the beneficiaries with timely and appropriate responses while maintaining the principle of confidentiality.
