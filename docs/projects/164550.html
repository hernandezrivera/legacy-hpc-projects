Project Summary

According to a wash assessment conducted in 2019 to inform the 2020 HNO, 42 per cent of the Syrian population is relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their needs. The proposed action aims at enhancing lifesaving and life sustaining WASH responses to the acute needs of vulnerable people left unaddressed due to significant destruction and limited humanitarian capacities in Eastern Ghouta (EG), Hama, Dara’a and Ar- Raqqa. Most targeted locations have a 4 or a 5 score on PIN vulnerability scale map, and those with a lesser score (3) are in the vicinity of areas prone to conflict and potentially source of new displacement thus humanitarian needs.
In terms of WASH, TGH action is based on a strategy which combines emergency response and exit strategy i.e. from water trucking services to installation of durable systems aligning itself with the two first pillar of the water sector strategy. Following the coordination with other WASH humanitarian actors, water trucking (WT) has been planned for locations where no humanitarian organization has plan for restoring the access to water and where TGH is already providing water such as Dara’a. TGH, as an active member of the WASH cluster, will inform WASH partners and line authorities of its plans and advocate for their intervention. In the meantime, the proposed intervention will allow responding to the current widely spread consumption of unsafe water, pumped in unsafe sources with no treatment systems. Communities not served with public water networks are in general at higher risk of unsafe water consumption, use less water than people well served via free/low cost water networks and spend significantly more of their incomes on purchasing water. Such factors pose significant public health risks and have negative cascade effects on communities in terms of public health, inequality and poverty. The action defines a clear exit strategy limiting WT to an emergency response phase. In accordance with TGH principles, WT is envisaged as a last resort and will be implemented in support to other longer-term alternatives such as rehabilitation of boreholes (BH) and water systems and provision of Reverse Osmosis (RO) unit in areas where above standards level of nitrates were reported. In Hama and Dara’a, TGH will rehabilitate damaged water systems without engaging in WT, as authorities and private entities already ensure the trucking of water, a situation towards which local communities could develop some resilience. The proposed action will complement TGH current activities in rural areas of Hama, Damascus and Dara’a allowing filling identified gaps and building and scaling up from TGH achievements to date, benefiting to a broaden humanitarian response in Syria. In addition, by targeting the Hama governorate, the action will contribute to the larger contingency plan anticipating potential large return movements from the neighboring governorate of Idleb. The targeted locations have been selected with regards to TGH current operational capacity, which guarantees an immediate start of the action complying with the demand for rapid answers to the identified needs. Protection mainstreaming will be ensured, guaranteeing a meaningful access and a sound accountability, further fostered by the proposed community-based approach and protection risk analysis and mitigation measures in every step of the project. A specific attention will be given to gender. The project will reduce gender risks by providing water within homes and consequently not exposing women and girls to risks when fetching water. Noteworthy is that, although not fully developed under this sector specific project description, the targeted locations have also been selected to be part of a more comprehensive response (WASH, Education, Shelter, Protection Livelihood). The synergy ensured by TGH between its several sectors of intervention aims at increasing the impact of the activities described below.

4 - Needs

The needs to which the action aims at responding have been assessed in the course of the implementation of TGH projects in Hama, Rural Damascus and Dara’a. TGH geographical coverage in the 3 Governorates allowed gathering information from local stakeholders from which the list of potential interventions has been drawn. Locations were pre-selected on the basis of objective criteria. TGH engaged in the assessment of needs at household level and among key informants and communities. This questionnaire allowed appraising the targeted communities’ vulnerability. TGH project is directly drawn from it. In November 2019, TGH also managed to conduct a multi-sector assessment in the Governorate of Ar-Raqqa (Ath-Thwrah sub-district). After the government of Syria recaptured North Hama, a field assessment was also made in newly retaken areas of Muhrada district. WASH facilities of the 4 targeted areas have been severely affected by the conflict, be it due to intensive shelling or to sabotage from armed groups leading to severe disruptions in the provision of water, as well as to an important deterioration of water quality. This is confirmed by the figures of the 2020 WASH sector PIN analysis. Across southern Syria, many communities are completely dependent on WT to cope with their basic water needs. TGH assessments confirm it in both EG where TGH is the main provider of trucked water and Dara’a, where WT is handled by communities at a high cost. WT however neither suffices to cover water needs nor is sustainable. In addition, water is being distributed through public water tank which holds protection concerns. The overall water scarcity leads people to resort to harmful practices or consuming water from shallow wells which water tests have proved polluted. Consequently, TGH action aims at providing emergency response trough the provision of water trucking but also more durable solutions through the rehabilitation of water systems and sewage network.

5 - Implementation of activities

TGH will collaborate with water relevant authorities and SARC to avoid duplication and ensure compliance with national standards. Act. 1 Repair and rehabilitation of water systemsAct 1.1. Repair and rehabilitation of water systemsThe rehabilitations of 8 water systems aims at expanding the access to sustainable supply services where access to safe water is generally below standard in EG, Dar’a and Ar-Raqqa. Act 1.2. Installation of a Reverse Osmosis unit as an exit strategy for water trucking: Due to the high nitrates level in Otaya, BH water cannot be used for drinking or cooking purposes. The RO Unit will make the produced water compliant with the standards for drinking water. This will allow stopping TGH water trucking in Otaya and 4 neighboring villages (Shafuniyeh, Hosh Elfara, Nashabiyeh and Rihan) through local and cheaper water trucking.Act 2. Distribution in schools of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotion: This activity will complement the activities ensured in TGH ongoing projects in Rural Damascus and Rural Hama, for which implementation arrangements with SARC volunteers are already in place. TGH will tailor the hygiene promotion messages to each specific context. At the end of the sessions, hygiene kits will be distributed to attendants. Act 3. Improved access to humanitarian lifesaving/ emergency WASH facilities and services Act 3.1. Provision of clean water through water trucking to 10 localities of Easter GhoutaAct 3.2. Support the functioning of existing sewage network: TGH will do the maintenance of Da’el high pressure pump truck (Dara’a). It will allow unblocking all clogged sewage lines in Da’el and some surrounding municipalities such as Hrak or Alma, where similar problems have been assessed.M&E: TGH will set up a monitoring system which will allow an improved monitoring of indicators. It will be used for random cross-checks and KI during field visits. In addition, results of small-scale surveys and focus groups will be organized.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Act 1: A) Unequal distribution of services. Likelihood: Low. Impact: Medium. Mitigation measures: the selection for rehabilitation, jointly with key local stakeholders, of pre-existing water systems which used to guarantee an equal access to the resource. B) Inadvertently empower or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors & risk of theft of materials/chemicals. Likelihood: very low. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: Given the critical situation with regards to water, the risk of looting of equipment is considered as low; it will be lessened by the coordination with water Authorities and by a final formal handover.Act 2:A) Inequality in distribution: Likelihood: Low. Impact: Medium. Mitigation measure: distribution will be organized following HP sessions at schools. Community based committees are currently being established in support to TGH education activities and will guarantee that all access the proposed assistance including OoS. B) Gender insensitive contents verities of kits with inconsistent items: Likelihood: Low. Impact: Medium. Mitigation measure: TGH will follow the recommendation of the cluster and previous PDM.Act 3: A) Movement of trucks in congested areas, potential exposure to explosive hazard, health risk associated with garbage removal. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigating measures: consult community regarding services modality, truck routes and schedule; establish routes and schedule for trucks that use wider access routes; avoiding school areas during the time children travel to / from school; safety training with truck drivers / operators; ensure staff involved in those activities wear appropriate personal protective equipment.B) Inequality in distribution. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High Mitigation measure: geographical dispatch of water tanks and the coordination with other actors, complaint mechanism. Exit strategy set up to guarantee equal access to water.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: Security level does not deteriorate to an extent that prevents staff to be deployed. No major change of the current balance between parties at conflict leads to major upheavals. No major security incident targeting TGH staff. TGH authorization to operate in Syria remains granted. Syrian laws ruling humanitarian organizations remain favorable. Field access remains granted. No major increased inflation allowing TGH to implement activities with the budget allocated.Risks: (A) Local authorities suspend TGH authorization to operate (B) access to one or other area is suspended due to military operations or increased attacks (C) Access to beneficiaries is limited by local authorities (D) New population displacement in and/or around project areas (E) Security incident targeting TGH staff (F) Attempts from backlisted entities to bid for services, goods or work contracts (G) Sexual harassment from staff or individuals in relation with TGH action (H) Attempts to fraud and corruption by staff (I) Diversion of the provided assistance bringing in inequality towards the assistance (J) Inflation prevent TGH from implementing all activities planned Mitigation: A: Maintaining of good relationships with national counterparts. B: Targeting of various regions to allow adjustments. C - Local authorities’ openness to individual selection of beneficiaries proven through previous programs D - Enforcement of TGH security procedures and training if staff on these relates to risk E - Systematic check of potential suppliers and contractors through FinScanF- Endorsement by all TGH personnel of TGH anti-abuse policy & strict enforcement of this policy G- Endorsement by all TGH personnel of TGH anti-fraud policy & whistle-blowing mechanism H- Adoption of systematic cross-checking mechanismsI- Early contacts with relevant stakeholders prior to the implementation of the action Involvement and empowerment of community members (J) Secure additional funding and monitoring of prices.
