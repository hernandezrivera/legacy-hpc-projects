Project Summary

Zimbabwe is among countries worst affected by drought in Eastern and Southern Africa. According to inter-sectoral needs assessment undertaken in 2019 from October November, 465,000 women are in need of maternal health services in the 16 most drought-affected districts in Zimbabwe. Identified districts are Bulawayo Urban, Chitungwiza, Harare Urban, Buhera, Chipinge Rural, Makoni, Mutare Rural Mutare Urban, Mazowe, Goromonzi, Hurungwe, Zvimba, Chiredzi Rural, Gokwe North, Gokwe South and Gweru. The affected districts have higher needs for maternal health services compared to other drought affected districts.
Pregnancy is a critical period during which maternal nutrition has a major effect on a mother’s and baby’s health. Lack of adequate nutrition of good quality and quantity during pregnancy have adverse impacts on both the mother and her foetus. Maternal malnutrition increases the risk of gestational anaemia, hypertension, miscarriages and foetal deaths, pre-term delivery and maternal mortality. For newborn, it can cause low birth weight, foetal intrauterine growth retardation that may have long-term consequences on newborn development and quality of life. The deteriorating economic environment has led to industrial action by health service providers (nurses and doctors) leading to a serious human resource crisis in many parts of the country, thus constraining access to health care services, especially maternity services. This is further aggravated by the aging and malfunctioning health care equipment in most health facilities. The prevailing drought coupled with the deteriorating economic environment is likely to lead to constrained household income. The limited resources available will result in relegation of SRH from the household priorities; diverting resources towards maternity and health care needs such as transportation, consultation fees and medicines to other immediate and more pressing needs such as food. This limits access to health care services and the much needed ante-natal and post-natal care, thus predisposing women to complications as normal contact with health service providers is disrupted. Pregnant and lactating women are also exposed to GBV due to reduced household income.
This project seeks to strengthen management of Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal care services in health facilities within the respective districts. The project will in addition, strengthen life-saving primary health care services by supporting Maternity Waiting Homes for high risk pregnant women in focus districts. Priority population groups for the lifesaving interventions will be women of reproductive age (15-49yrs) with a special focus on pregnant women and adolescent girls.
The specific objective for the interventions is to decrease excess maternal mortality rate by 20% in 465,000 women in child bearing age in the identified 11 rural and 5 urban districts in Zimbabwe.
Activities include
1. Rapid Health facility assessment and identification of women at high risk of developing obstetric complications
2. Supporting the developemnt of SRHR in emergency Operational Guidelines
3. Procurement, pre-positioning and distribution emergency RH kits, equipment and essential medicines
4. Strengthening referral system to minimise delays in receiving skilled birth attendance
5. Support health facilities to offer comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care and cope with increased patient burden as a result of humanitarian crisis
6. Strengthening of health workers’ knowledge and skills providing Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for SRH in humanitarian/emergency
7. Strengthen Maternity Waiting Homes’ capacity to provide antenatal care; serving as safe spaces for high risk pregnant women.
8. Provide technical support and quality assurance in the delivery of emergency obstetric and neonatal care services

This data will be used as baseline from which progress in project implementation will be measured.
