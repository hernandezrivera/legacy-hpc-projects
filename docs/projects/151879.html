Project Summary

This project aims to increase self-sufficiency and subsistence farming capacity of the most vulnerable households in Donetsk region to cover their immediate basic needs by providing small size agricultural grants, training and advisory services.
Needs assessments have been conducted (see chapter needs).
500 small grants for agricultural purposes with related training and advisory activities will be provided over the course of the year to selected beneficiaries residing within 20 km zone of GCA.
Dorcas aims to reach following targets: 500 households assisted
(1250 of individuals), basic business planning and agricultural training provided to at least 500 beneficiaries in Maryinka district, Donetsk region, GCA.

Needs

Below, Dorcas will highlight the needs for different activities in the sectors FSL. All information is based on DORCAS field information as well as UNOCHA, UNFPA, OHCHR, OSCE, REACH reports and materials and concerns the 5 districts along the line of contact (LoC) in Donetsk region (Government Controlled Areas (GCA)). Dorcas’ own data collection and interventions:In 2018 Dorcas collected data on over 5600 individuals that have been screened for the possibility to receive various kinds of assistance, include agricultural input grants and training and multipurpose cash grants. Dorcas data collection system uses over 20 criteria, including sex, age, displacement, marital status, unemployment, health conditions, income, etc. The data is gender- and age disaggregated to help establish individual vulnerability and target the most vulnerable for the FSL intervention within the HRP project. Within the current FSL project (Ukraine Joint Response 4) DORCAS noticed that in the majority of cases our agricultural input grants and training strengthen coping strategies and resilience of the beneficiaries, and improve their diets and household income. Needs and Vulnerable groups, gender and age analysisAt the contact line between the fighting parties in Eastern Ukraine, regular, almost daily shelling and fighting continues. It has a destructive impact on the food security and livelihoods of women, men, boys and girls near that contact line. Below an overview of the types of needs in the FSL sectors, followed by a gender and age analysis.Vulnerable (gender and age) groups:Analysis of the main FSL-related indicators (food consumption score, livelihood coping strategies and food security index) shows that in both GCA and NGCA, the most vulnerable groups remain single headed households with children, elders (60+) (mostly those living alone), chronically ill and disabled people, female-headed households as well as households with no active employment – and especially households headed by people aged 40-60 (with no employment). When looking at poor and borderline FCS, the proportion of households featuring some type of vulnerability (such as chronic illness, disability, single headed household status, three or more children etc.) is 29%, almost double compared to households with no vulnerabilities.Elders (60+) have traditionally been the most vulnerable age group, however in 2018, an increasing proportion of the households headed by people aged 40-60 report even bigger difficulties in relation to food and livelihoods. This is mainly a reflection of the high proportion of unemployed people within this age group who are heads of household. For households headed by someone who is unemployed and aged 40-60, the level of food insecurity is 37%. This includes sometimes male-headed households in this age-group that are not the traditional target group for humanitarian assistance. For male-headed households in this age group unemployment frequently goes together with addictions.It is estimated that 30% of female-headed households have poor and borderline levels of food consumption and therefore continue to be more vulnerable than male-headed households at 11.4% in GCA. Women-headed households, especially those with children, are among the most likely to adopt negative coping strategies and suffer from food insecurity, which may be linked with lower income, less employment options and higher dependency rates. Some women and girls experiencing domestic and other forms of violence, including the disabled and elderly, are forced to remain in unsafe living environments because there are no alternatives and marital relations are a survival strategy. Women often function as the main caregivers for their children. Children lack access to after school activities and their social life is very limited and mundane in a rural areas targeted by the project. Sometimes children are being abused by farm work inside their families.Dorcas’ adapts interventions.

Activities & outcomes

For the last four years DORCAS has been operating in Donetsk region (GCA), within the closest proximity of the contact line by providing multipurpose cash, agricultural grants, psycho-social support and other help to the most vulnerable population locally including the internally displaced persons. Dorcas leads a humanitarian consortium funded by the Dutch Ministry of Foreign Affairs which mainly implements MPC programmes in Donetsk and Luhansk regions. Dorcas has developed a consortium wide PDM system. Dorcas is an active member of the Cash Working Group and the Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster and coordinates with INGOs and local partners. Dorcas has strong relationships with other INGOs that implement or have implemented cash programmes like Terres des Hommes, Save the Children and Help Age International. They complement each other and refer beneficiaries to each other. To help local residents improve their situation, in 2018 Dorcas provided cash grants for agricultural assets, which, together with intensive training helped people increase income generation. 150 grants of 300 EUR have been provided to selected households in order to improve their self-sufficiency and improve their agricultural activities. All beneficiaries have received training on business planning, agricultural development as well as advisory services including site visits to larger farms.Dorcas operates a hotline and within the existing project in 2018 has carried multiple psycho social support and protection meetings with local beneficiaries and population at large. Amount of the grant is based on our priori experience and provide a household with a substantial startup for food improvement, like getting 100 poultry and feeder for the season.Activity 1: To provide 500 agricultural grants (400 EUR each) to selected households (1250 individuals in total) from the current list of the most vulnerable MPC beneficiaries in 2019 (250 grants in April and 250 grants in August). Similar time frame would be kept in 2020 as well.Dorcas runs an assessment collecting gender and sex disaggregated data using existing database of MPC beneficiaries and applicants. Dorcas will use following vulnerability criteria in line with FSL cluster guidelines. Dorcas will provide grants to 500 households. The value of the grant is based on prior DORCAS experience and recommendations of the FSL cluster. Outcome 1: Increase in production and improved food security of beneficiaries via support. 500 agricultural grants (400 EUR each) provided to selected households (1250 individuals in total). Half of it will be provided in Spring and another half in the beginning of Autumn. Activity 2: To provide training of beneficiaries on basic business planning, market access and agricultural activities (40 workshops organised) as well as 500 hours of advisory services. Outcome 2: At least 500 beneficiaries increased their understanding of business planning and agricultural management through workshops and advisory services.We run beneficiary focus groups before, during and after the intervention. Monitoring and evaluation mechanismMonitoring and evaluation mechanism is in place and consists of baseline and endline studies of the beneficiaries. Beneficiary selection and verification process involves household visits and interviews by our field officers. Focus group discussions are planned at the beginning and later stage of the project Implementation in order to improve project implementation, impact and sustainability. KOBO system is used extensively for M&E data collection and analysis. Both quantitative and qualitative data is being collected and analyzed during the process. M&E process supervision and coordination responsibility lies within the FSL Manager. Costs for external project evaluation by DORCAS HQ are included in the project budget. M&E findings will be included into the final report of the project.
