Project Summary

As the Syria crisis moves into its twelfth year its impact continues to blight the present, and the future prospects of its population, with the impacts felt most negatively on the lives of vulnerable civilians across the country. In response to these needs, People in Need's (PIN's) project proposes to contribute towards saving and sustaining lives of targeted conflict-affected communities in Northern Syria through the provision of food assistance (multiple modalities). Responses between immediate needs and resiliencebuilding will be adapted according to continuous assessments and analysis of the local contexts within a flexible and responsive framework.
Activities will be underpinned by emergency preparedness and response plans developed in coordination with relevant clusters, working groups, and partners – to promote timely, efficient and joined-up responses that seek to maximize geographic and sectoral coverage and minimize gaps. PIN will also focus upon assisting vulnerable, conflict-affected to meet basic food needs. Activities will target the most vulnerable households in communities chronically affected by the adverse impacts of the conflict, using common assistance modalities and selection criteria, per the recommendation of the working groups.
PIN will plan to reach beneficiaries with monthly food assistance (food baskets, food vouchers, MPCA), agriculture vouchers, agriculture business grants, and bakery support hrough reduced-price bread on a community level through providing contracted bakeries with 50% of flour and 50% of yeast to reduce the price of the bread price up to 37% in comparison with the free market bread price. Beneficiaries are targeted from both IDPs and host community members, meeting eligibility and vulnerability criteria to avoid creating tensions within and across communities. Selection criteria was elaborated in consultation with the affected communities, as well as based on evidence from previous project monitoring and community feedback obtained through the Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), and include low-income families from the following groups: Femaleheaded households (FHH); Child-headed HH; Elderly-headed HH; HH with disabled and chronically ill members; HH with high dependency ratio; Unemployed male and female youth; Poor farming HH. PIN is aware that needs are different amongst gender and age groups, and other vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities. PIN aims to provide the most needed assistance by employing a wide spectrum of different activities to meet the distinct needs, establishing beneficiary selection criteria to prioritise the inclusion of those most vulnerable, and adapting its assistance delivery to promote access for the most vulnerable people. PINs programmes are coordinated with local authorities at various levels – from cross-border to hubs to district and village levels. PIN engages in regular dialogue with authorities to ensure transparency, accountability, and protection mainstreaming; including consultations and briefings on needs, humanitarian principles, PIN procedures, and beneficiary selection at projects' planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation stages.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

According to the HNO 2024, 15M people need food and agriculture assistance with 44% being children and 28% being people with disabilities. 12.1M Syrians are facing food insecurity and another 2.9M are at risk of becoming food insecure. This is an alarming increase of 1M in comparison to 2022, demonstrating a dire need for food assistance. Not only that the purchasing power continued to weaken following the COVID-19 pandemic which has contributed to the depreciation of the Syrian pound, but also problems with the supply chain and deliveries following Russian invasion of Ukraine worsened the food security of HHs in Syria. Additionally, the climate crisis is making it more challenging for farmers to produce agricultural goods to support the population. PIN is committed to ensuring inclusivity, ‘’do no harm’’, and accessibility throughout the project. The selection of women, children, people with disabilities, and the elderly as primary target groups is grounded in a deep understanding of the distinct vulnerabilities these populations face in conflict-affected settings. Women, often disproportionately impacted by conflict as well as social barriers, confront elevated risks of gender-based violence, encounter obstacles to accessing essential services and have much less opportunities to sustain income. Prioritizing their needs is not only a matter of addressing immediate challenges but is also critical for advancing gender equality, community resilience, and long-term sustainable development. Children, being amongst the most vulnerable in conflict zones, experience disruptions in education, psychosocial trauma, and heightened exposure to various forms of exploitation such as home retention for girls due to safety and culture-driven reasons or child labour where school-aged boys are the primary group impacted. By bringing strong emphasis on the needs of children, PIN aims to safeguard their well-being and contribute to their holistic development. People with disabilities encounter unique challenges, including discrimination and physical barriers, limiting their access to critical services. Tailoring our interventions to address their specific needs aligns with the principles of inclusivity, ensuring that humanitarian assistance is accessible to all members of the community, regardless of ability. The approach is embedded in all PIN policies and technical manuals that govern the implementation of all the activities under this action. The elderly, often overlooked in crisis responses, face heightened health risks, isolation, and increased vulnerabilities. By specifically targeting this demographic, the action recognizes the importance of addressing their unique needs, fostering social cohesion, and contributing to a more comprehensive and effective humanitarian intervention. The intervention will be carried out in consultations with community members using a participatory and inclusive approach.
