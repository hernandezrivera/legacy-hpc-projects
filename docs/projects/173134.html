Project Summary

Under the proposed project, RI will deliver comprehensive primary health care services to 302,100 (128,058 ND, 57,626 ED, 116, 416 BN) IDPs and vulnerable host communities in Mellit, Sara-Umra, Kekbkabiya, localities located in North Darfur; and in Abujabra, and Bahr al-Arab localities in East Darfur; and Damazine, Roseries, Kurmuk, Geizan, and Bau localities in Blue Nile State.
RI will provide a comprehensive intervention to support health systems, clinical support, and medical supplies to improve access to quality healthcare for vulnerable people living in the targeted areas. Under this funding, RI provides support including pharmaceuticals, medical consumables, equipment, incentives, and running costs for health facility function and maintenance. Through this package of support, RI enables consistent service delivery through a network of primary health care centers, mobile clinics, nutrition sites, and hospitals, linked by a robust referral system. This aims at improving the quality of service, and in increasing the number of static clinics RI aims to improve access. Community outreach is a key modality for health access as a means of increasing disease awareness and disseminating information about services. RI will continue to strengthen the outreach teams – the Village Health Committees (VHCs), the CHWs, and the village-trained midwives (VTMs)
To improve service quality and strengthen the healthcare system and service delivery, RI will provide capacity-building training to health facility staff and CHWs on diagnosis and treatment; EPI; and sexual and reproductive health. This targeted skills-building program will improve disease surveillance, detection, and response (with emphasis on diarrhea, malaria, and ARI) through consultations and enhanced CHW community engagement. RI will also support health staff to submit Early Warning Alert and Response System (EWARS) reports on a daily and weekly basis. Strengthened coordination with line ministries and partners (particularly through seconded MoH clinical staff) and regular information sharing will improve the overall quality of services. RI will also support referrals of all emergency obstetric care (EMOC) cases to secondary units. In order to ensure that clinic facilities are fully functional and in suitable condition for eventual handover to SMOHSD, RI also proposes continuing minor rehabilitation support to targeted facilities.

1 - Needs

As per the HNO 2020 report, 6.3million people are in need of health services. In North Darfur, new and protracted displacement due to conflict, chronic poverty, under-development, and recent economic crisis impede access to basic services and disrupt livelihoods, exposing vulnerable communities to high levels of food insecurity and malnutrition. In Sudan, nearly 2 million people remain internally displaced, following decades of conflict. The majority are living in camps and settlements where conditions remain dire, and the root causes that led to their displacement remain unaddressed. The core problem facing these conflict-affected communities and vulnerable host communities is high morbidity and mortality, driven by low quality and coverage of primary health services, poor maternal and child feeding practices, poor access to quality WASH services, high rates of malnutrition, and poor food security and limited livelihood opportunities. As WHO 2017 report shows, sixty-three of every 1,000 Sudanese children will die before the age of five, well above the global average of 39. The last Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (2014) showed a neonatal mortality rate of 44/1,000 and an infant mortality rate of 69/1,000 in North Darfur, both are above the national average. The WHO Q1 2017 Health Resources Availability Mapping System (HeRAMS) also shows a health facility functionality rate of 67%. The 2018 HNO reports a slightly lower percentage at 64% functionality due to either staff shortages or poor physical infrastructure. Without ongoing support in infrastructure, staffing, and service provision, these facilities would almost certainly collapse and leave the population without basic and lifesaving healthcare. The capacity of SMoHSD to maintain infrastructure and facility equipment and supplies is limited, and qualified technical staff are scarce and require significant capacity building and oversight. Based on past and current programming, RI notes this is particularly true for remote areas, which are considered undesirable postings since they are hundreds of kilometers away from town without easily accessible and affordable modes of transportation. Without these two factors, basic service provision will quickly slip; for example, midwives are not provided a salary or incentive by the government, and, given their critical role in the provision of reproductive health care, services will likely become unavailable. While more recent data is unavailable, the Q1 2017 HeRAMS shows a dire picture in terms of human resources for health, which is unlikely to have improved due to the economic crisis. The HeRAMS indicates 4 medical officers/assistances, nurses, and midwives per 10,000 population, far below the WHO threshold of 23/10,000. Adding to these challenges, communicable disease morbidities and disease outbreaks are a serious concern in all the states targeted by this action. In 2019, cases of acute watery diarrhea (AWD), measles, and malaria were reported across the proposed target locations. More than 219 cases of measles were reported in Zamzam camp (B) (RI facility data, 2018) and, due to heavy rains in 2018, malaria morbidity rose to 22%, beyond the 15% emergency threshold in North Darfur state (Sudan Health Cluster, 2018).

2 - Activities/Outputs

RI will support 11 PHC facilities to deliver primary health care services to the IDPs, host communities, and vulnerable populations across North Darfur, East Darfur, and Blue Nile States. The key activities will include (but are not limited to) direct outpatient consultations (focus on women and children); diagnosis and treatment of communicable diseases; provision of reproductive health care including antenatal care, safe delivery, postnatal care, family planning, and medical response to sexual abuse cases; provision of immunization to children under five and reproductive-age women; training and payment of incentives to seconded health workers from the SMOH; rehabilitation, re-equipping and restocking of the health facilities with medicines, medical equipment, supplies, and furniture. Below is the list of key activities the project aims to implement:• Consultation services on communicable disease, RH, EPI, and mental health and injuries• Pharmaceutical supply to target facilities• Medical equipment and furniture supply to target facilities• Distribution of LLINs • PPEs and IPC materials supply to target facilities• Rehabilitation and maintenance of health facilities • Support to the referral of complicated cases to secondary centers• Training of facility and community health workers on several health topics

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

RI’s programming takes into consideration that in situations of conflict-related displacement, women and girls, as well as men and boys, are often subject to heightened risks of GBV. The legacy and consequences of violent conflict can also increase intra-family violence due to a number of factors: individual and social trauma; changes in gender roles and in notions of masculinity; generalized impunity and breakdown of the rule of law; and increased tensions and frustration caused by economic stress, challenging living conditions, and the lack of opportunities. RI takes these gender dynamics into account during the design of its programs. RI ensures that all interventions are aligned with all applicable international standards, including but not limited to the GBV Guiding Principles and the Child Protection Minimum Standards. RI has also rolled out its own Child Safeguarding Policy across the region in order to reinforce existing policies such as Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) within RI and therefore increase and/or maintain a protective environment for beneficiaries, including children attending all RI-supported services. All staff receives mandatory training on this policy.Promoting equitable access to health services will ensure that the project is able to reach the most vulnerable members of the targeted communities. Nearly all health indicators are gender-disaggregated, and it is expected that over 50% of beneficiaries will be women. Special attention will be paid to mothers and PLW through reproductive health services. CHWs will identify people with disabilities or the elderly that require home visits for treatment.
