Project Summary

This project seeks to reduce nutrition related morbidities and mortalities in the 6 counties of upper Nile state through prevention and provision of improved treatment of acute malnutrition. The project will target South Sudanese in upper Nile State living as refugees, internal displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and other vulnerable populations including people with disabilities. Specifically, the project targets children U5 years with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), U5 children with Moderate Acute malnutrition (MAM) and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) with MAM.
In line with Nutrition Cluster Strategy for 2024, the project has three objectives:
1. Increase equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services to prevent malnutrition among children, adolescents, and women 6 counties of Upper Nile State by the end of 2024
2. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW in 6 target counties of Upper Nile State
3. To improve nutrition and health-related knowledge and practices that prevent malnutrition of pregnant women and girls and new mothers, caregivers and community members through social and behaviour change communication strategies
4. To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2024
RI will provide nutrition-sensitive services through early screening at the community level using the Family MUAC approach and timely referrals, increasing access to MIYCN services, deworming and Vitamin A supplementation as well as improving the access to integrated nutrition, health, WASH and FSL services. Gender sensitive approaches will be utilised in the project to ensure equitable access to nutrition services. Though Nutrition sites integrated services will be provided to prevent and treat malnutrition. RI will link this nutrition project to the health, food security and WASH projects RI is implementing in Maban, Melut, Longechuk, Maiwut, Ulang and Nasir to ensure that children and women access a safe, equitable and dignified comprehensive package of essential services through the nutrition sites and integration of early childhood development as stimulation activities to prevent malnutrition. RI will strengthen nutrition surveillance mechanisms through accurate and timely reporting, participating in SMART surveys, monitoring, and analysis of data for early and objective decision-making. Protection mainstreaming will be integral across the project cycle to ensure that the services are provided in safe spaces and conditions for children and women
In Maban, Melut, Longechuk and Maiwut, RI as the Lead partner will be directly implementing a comprehensive package of nutrition services. In Ulang and Nasir, as part of RI integrated Mobile Medical units, RI will provide mobile nutrition services in hard to reach areas of the counties in collaboration with the Lead Nutrition Partners. In the later, the treatment commodities will be provided through the Lead partners and the data from the outreaches will be shared through the Lead partner in line with the Nutrition cluster strategy. To further localize the RI’s response and to ensure sustainability, RI will strengthen the County Health Department (CHD) responsibilities to oversee the nutrition interventions, to coordinate the response with various stakeholders at the county level and provide stakeholder’s capacity building on the new national guidelines on management of malnutrition and maternal, infant, and young child nutrition.

1. Needs

The high levels of acute food insecurity in South Sudan remain worrying, with the most severely acute food insecure populations significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation and high food prices), conflict and insecurity including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan causing forced displacement, low agricultural production and a reduction in humanitarian assistance. Between July 2023 and June 2024, an estimated 1.65 million children between 6-59 months are expected to suffer acute malnutrition, and 870,000 pregnant or breastfeeding women are expected to suffer acute malnutrition.In the Upper Nile State (UNS), the impacts of the Sudan crisis, combined with flooding and displacement, continues to impact pre-existing vulnerabilities and coping capacities of affected populations. Upper Nile is among the most food insecure states between September and November 2023 with more than 56% of the population food insecure, with Maban, Melut, Maiwut, Longechuk described as being in IPC Phase 4. The intensified sub-national violence, and three consecutive years of widespread flooding are the main reason for the worsening of acute malnutrition and food insecurity in those counties. The recent Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS+) findings estimated the under-five GAM rate above the threshold in all proposed locations (Maban 17.5%, Longechuk 21.9%, Maiwut 13.0%, Melut 28.9%). As indicated above poor IYCF is one of the contributing factors for the high rate of malnutrition in South Sudan. The most recent 2023 FSNMS+ indicated that among the families reached in Upper Nile, only 39.3% of children aged under 6 months of age were exclusively breastfed, while only 53% of children age 6-8 months were fed solid/semi-solid foods. The composite indicator of quality and quantity of complimentary feeds provided (minimum acceptable diet or MAD) to children 6 to 23 months shows a disturbing situation in which only 3.7% of children 6 to 23 months of age received the MAD. In the targeted areas, poor dietary diversity, and exclusive breast feeding along with the lack of timely initiation of complementary feeding at the age of 6 months, continues to be the most concerning IYCF behaviours. Displacement, insecurity and the economic downturn have compounded the impact of historically low coverage of water infrastructure. Despite the fact that major waterways were replenished by the long rainy season, only 39% of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs countrywide, dropping to 34% in rural areas. The areas have low infrastructure coverage with 49% of households reported relying on surface or unprotected water sources. In the context of severe food insecurity, poor sanitation in communities aggravates the burden of malnutrition through the spread of faecal-oral diseases. Poor personal hygiene contributes to the spreading of water-related diseases, including diarrheal diseases. One of the major contributing factors to acute malnutrition in South Sudan is a high prevalence of diseases. According to the IPC 2023 preliminary report, the high disease burden contributes to up to 25% of the estimated malnutrition. Malaria, diarrhoea, and respiratory tract infections are the most prevalent disease in children. Despite the high burden of disease, the health system of the country remains very weak and, has deteriorated further over the past few years due to the continued conflict and prolonged flooding. Women, children, the elderly and persons with disabilities are particularly vulnerable and most frequently face limited access to healthcare. According to the HNO 2022, only 8% of the 1,869 reported health facilities in South Sudan are providing the full national package of health and nutrition services (BPHNS). Extremely low immunization coverage (49% Penta3 coverage) has made children more vulnerable to preventable diseases

2. Project activities

Relief International will continue its high-quality nutrition services in the catchment areas of Maban, Melut, Longechuk, Nasir and Maiwut counties. In four counties of Maban, Melut, Longchuk and Maiwut, as lead nutrition partner, RI will directly integrate the nutrition activities with the WFP/ UNICEF funded nutrition project. The project will also adopt the use of RI run Integrated Mobile Medical units in Longechuk, Maiwut, Nasir and Ulang to provide mobile nutrition services to the hard to reach areas of these counties. In Nasir and Ulang, Mobile nutrition services will be provided in collaboration with the Lead Nutrition partners in the counties, who will provide treatment food and collate nutrition data. Nutrition activities will be in line with the nutrition cluster strategic and sectoral objectives. The services will focus on both preventive and treatment of malnutrition in compliance with national guidelines. Key activities are aligned to the sector objectives and strategy which includes:Infant Young Child Feeding and Behaviour ChangeIn Maban, Melut, Longechuk, Maiwut, Nasser, and Ulang Relief International will implement IYCF and use the National MIYCN Guidelines to govern the dissemination of IYCF messaging. IYCF activities will be integrated into each level of contact beneficiaries have with staff throughout the CMAM programs as well as mother-to-mother/Care Groups to provide sufficient opportunities for knowledge acquisition and to promote behavior change. Child-friendly spaces will be used as an opportunity to support mothers, in particular in the violence-affected areas project locations. Management of Acute MalnutritionRelief International proposes to continue screening and treating children under 5 years and PLW for SAM, and MAM through the provision of the CMAM program (static, mobile, and outreach sites) in up to 32 locations. The community mobilization activities will rely on the development of networks of Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) in all six Counties and emergency response as well as Care Groups in Maban refugee camps. Preventative measures to mitigate the risk of developing acute malnutrition will be implemented, and will include; activities of behaviour change communication targeting maternal, infant, and young child feeding practices (noted above), participation in the National Immunization Days (NIDs), as well as periodic campaigns of mass distribution of Vitamin A (children aged 6-59 months), and deworming with albendazole (children aged 12-59 months). In addition to these preventive activities Relief International will provide BSFP to vulnerable young children and pregnant women. IntegrationRelief International has multisector projects in the proposed locations and will integrate the proposed projects with the WASH package, health package (RDT and measles vaccination; provision of amoxicillin, and deworming according to the CMAM protocol, management of co-existing illnesses; referral to the stabilization center); and FSL linkage will be implemented in the patient’s best interest and according to the directives of the South Sudan Nutrition Cluster.CoordinationRelief International will closely work with the Ministry of health, County Health department, UNICARE, GOAL and other partners, and the nutrition cluster coordination at the national and sub-national levelsThe Emergency Response: Relief International will respond with nutrition interventions to the Sudan Crisis in Renk, Melut and Maban, and any other emergencies that may come in upper Nile state as needed. The actual response will depend on the needs and gaps identified in the Needs Assessments.
