Project Summary

Years of violent conflict have left millions of South Sudanese facing one of the world’s most devastating, complex and deep-rooted humanitarian crises in recent history. The compounding effect of repeated waves of violence and an increasingly fast economic decline have pushed the number of people uprooted since the start of the conflict to 4 million, with roughly 1.4 million estimated to be internally displaced. In response to the growing and shifting needs in South Sudan, ACTED proposes to extend the scope of its current response through rapid interventions to reach some of the 84% of internally displaced people (IDPs) living in precarious conditions outside of the Protection of Civilian (PoC) sites. ACTED will build on its experience in Camp Coordination and Camp Management in PoCs and camp-like settings across the country, to launch a CCCM Mobile Team able to respond to sudden IDP emergencies and crises. In a context where prioritisation is becoming ever more complex and there is a disjunct between data and response, ACTED will ensure that this response is coordinated, and that sustainability is built within through a grassroots approach focusing on community capacity building and self-management. ACTED will also continue its historic commitment to the residents of Bor and Juba PoCs by continuing to provide Camp Management and Camp Coordination (CCCM) services geared towards the improvement of living conditions of more than 34,000 residents. Services will include amongst others day-to-day coordination and service delivery monitoring and site maintenance activities. A special focus will be put on improving communication and community engagement and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP). A core component of the approach proposed will be to strengthen the community governance structures and capacity for self-management. The unique nature of the PoCs – this “72-hour solution” to a 5-year situation – has generated increased frustration amongst its residents. As Camp Manager, ACTED strongly believes that it is our responsibility to ensure that our response is guided by fundamental humanitarian principles as well as ensuring the right to life with dignity which include considerations beyond basic survival. ACTED therefore proposes to provide recreational spaces for people to carry out sports and craft activities, where they will also be able to organise group discussions on themes chosen on a weekly/monthly basis (e.g. protection-related topics, GBV, etc.).
As the scale and geographic spread of the humanitarian crisis increases, it also becomes more important than ever to have a realistic, regular and predictable country-wide needs monitoring mechanism and strong coordination structures to trigger cost-efficient ad needs-based interventions. Secondly, in hosting the Camp Management and Camp Coordination (CCCM) cluster co-lead, ACTED endeavours to ensure a strong contribution in working toward a coordinated response and advocacy at the highest levels of the humanitarian architecture to ensure that the voice of displaced people is represented and heard.

Needs

Since 2013 South Sudan has been plagued by a civil war, with thousands of people fleeing the conflict and seeking refuge in Protection of Civilian (PoC) sites in Bentiu, Juba, Bor, Melut, Malakal and Wau. Deteriorating security and fighting since 2016 continues to drive displacement, along with resource-related conflict (such as cattle raiding), and the high level of food insecurity, with 6.1 million severely food insecure in 2018. As a result, more than 2 million have sought refuge in neighbouring countries, and 1.9 million are estimated to be internally displaced: among them roughly 216,631 individuals (12% of IDPs) are sheltered in PoCs, with others living in collective centres (1%), informal settlements (3%) or amongst host communities (84%) (HRP 2018). It is anticipated that PoC populations will remain in 2019, with returns movements resuming only if there is stability and confidence among the PoC population of physical safety outside the sites. Bor and Juba PoCs continue to experience significant rainy season flooding. Serious protection concerns continue in and out of the POCs, in particular: lack of freedom of movement; threats to physical security; SGBV; and over-crowding. Juba: In 2013, following the outbreak of violence in Juba, civilians sought protection at the UN House UNMISS base in Juba. In 2014, PoC3 was opened to relocate IDPs sheltering in the former PoC2 at the Tongping UN base. During the July 2016 fighting in Juba, a new wave of displacement occurred, with IDPs seeking refuge in the PoCs. In PoC1 and PoC3 the current population corresponds to 32,113, biometric registered people, resulting in only 21.5m² per person in PoC3 only 12.1 m² per person in PoC1 where overcrowding is more significant, meaning that both sites fall well below the SPHERE Standards. The risks created by a lack of adequate space are manifold. These include but are not limited to increased threats to health due with poor hygiene practice being common place and a lack of permanent drainage systems as well as higher likelihoods of fire rapidly spreading across sites due to there being no room for maintaining fire breaks. 3,079 individuals voluntarily moved to Mangateen collective site between August and September of 2018. Bor: Following fighting in 2013, people from both Nuer and Dinka tribes sought refuge inside the UNMISS base situated outside Bor town. While Dinka IDPs eventually left the PoC, most of the Nuer population remained, fearing for their safety. As of today, there are 2,505 individuals living in the PoC. In an assessment conducted by ACTED in December 2017, 88% of IDPs interviewed stated that they would not leave the camp as they did not feel safe outside the PoC. ACTED will continue to work toward and support durable solutions for the site’s population but undoubtedly, this will not see the majority of people move from the site in 2019. CCCM Mobile Team: An overall lack of coordination and capacity to deploy effective assistance to IDPs located in remote areas or suddenly displaced has stressed the need to deploy a CCCM Mobile Team capable of responding to the most urgent need for coordination and information management from site to state to national level, thus ensuring that humanitarian actors can effectively communicate with beneficiaries to understand their needs and assist them, and that agencies and organisations can improve coordination to strengthen their response. Following discussions with the CCCM Cluster and partners, Akobo and Rimenze have been identified as potential areas of intervention, as various assessments have indicated that there are 3,615 IDPs HHs in Akobo (ACTED and WVI), and a high numbers of IDPs in Rimenze living in a spontaneous settlements, lacking access to water food. As the CCCM Mobile Team is inherently designed to respond rapidly to sudden influxes of IDPs, or IDPs in hard-to-reach areas, tailored needs assessments will be conducted to identify priority areas of intervention.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

A community based participatory planning (CBPP) approach will be employed throughout the project to ensure that the affected population and beneficiaries are informed and included throughout all stages of the project, particularly regarding the objectives and activities of the project. In addition, the CBPP will ensure that the beneficiary selection process is transparent and accepted by the community in each of the project locations, with the most vulnerable populations targeted. The CBPP approach will ensure that the project is accountable, accepted, and owned by the targeted population. In the PoCs, ACTED will constantly engage and communicate with the various community leaders and committees to disseminate key information regarding services and events, as well as to discuss any issues or concerns for the community.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are context specific, as they take into consideration the different contexts and dynamics of each project location. Messages are also tailored towards the specific community or committee that is being engaged, with specific messages and information targeted at specific groups, particularly the most vulnerable groups including persons with disabilities, persons with special needs, and women and children.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

ACTED will ensure that protection mainstreaming is included throughout all phases of the project. A focal point for protection and gender complaints will be established in each location, to address the targeted communities’ concerns and queries regarding protection and gender equality. Furthermore, during verifications, distributions and assessments/ monitoring, ACTED will ensure that the specific needs of women and girls are taken into consideration and prioritized. As the Camp Manager, ACTED will coordinate with the protection partner in each camp to ensure that protection is being mainstreamed, and that protection issues are being addressed. ACTED’s staff have all been trained on protection, gender equality and sexual exploitation, including on how to identify and address any issues or concerns, and in addition will be supported with technical guidance by ACTED’s Protection Manager based in Juba. Moreover, ACTED has a strong code of conduct and policy on sexual exploitation and abuse which all staff are required to review, agree to and sign.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The project is based off assessments conducted in the targeted locations (Bor and Juba PoCs) that involved a range of respondents, not just community leaders. In addition, at the onset of the project, further assessments and community consultations will take place, especially regarding the CCCM Mobile Team, to ensure that the targeted location, approach and beneficiaries are in line with the needs of the community. Community consultations are a key part of ACTED's CCCM approach, to ensure that the beneficiaries needs and wishes are being met and respected, and that the services provided within the camps meet certain minimum standards.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with a dedicated Accountability Officer based in Juba will set up a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) in all project locations, and oversee reporting for the project to ensure beneficiaries can objectively report complaints and feedback. Standardized CRM tools, including set SOPs and sensitivity levels, as well as the training of project staff on collecting and responding to complaints, and sensitization of the community in accountability practice will be utilized. All complaints will be compiled and reported on each month and the Accountability Officer will ensure that follow up is conducted by the relevant people. The CRM will also ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between ACTED and the community, and improve the quality of our programmes. Complaints can be lodged through complaints desks/ boxes set up in the camps, and/ or at distribution and project sites, over the phone to the designated CRM hotline, or directly to ACTED's staff. The beneficiaries and local community through awareness sessions, as well as the CBPP process. One of ACTED's main activities as Camp Manager is to provide and strengthen a community CFM (Complaints Feedback Mechanism) within the camps, which is utilised and trusted by the camp community.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Accountability to affected populations and community engagement will be considered at all stages of the project. A community based participatory planning (CBPP) approach will be employed in all locations to ensure that the beneficiary selection process is transparent and accepted by the community, with the most vulnerable populations targeted. As outlined above, the CBPP will ensure the transparency, accountability, acceptance and ownership of the project for the targeted community. Project Management Committees will be established in each location to ensure that there is ongoing communication and coordination between the beneficiaries and ACTED. The various Community Committees in the PoCs will monitor the project activities from the beneficiaries' perspective, and provide feedback and identify issues through meetings with ACTED. In addition, assessments will be conducted over the course of the project, and will serve as another form of AAP by evaluating the effectiveness of the project in addressing the beneficiaries’ most immediate needs, according to the beneficiaries themselves, as well as their level of satisfaction with the project and ACTED staff. The assessments will consist of representative surveys, as well as key informant interviews (KIIs) with beneficiaries to gather a more in depth body of information. Moreover, throughout the intervention ACTED will utilise assessment tools and mechanisms developed with the CCCM Cluster and INGOs, and will rely on its CCCM Mobile Team supported by ACTED Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) to develop short reports highlighting assessments results.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

As CCCM Manager, ACTED will actively engage with GBV and protection partners to refer identified protection cases and ensure overall implementation of protection activities, including liaising with partners to address protection issues on site, as well as ensuring that older displaced persons are included in tracing and re-unification activities. Furthermore, ACTED will preposition NFI kits within each managed PoC, in order to respond to urgent protection needs while waiting for a protection partner to take over, thus ensuring a swift response to residents’ beneficiaries. Through its CCCM Mobile Response ACTED will assess the presence of potential protection partners: if partners are already in place, ACTED will advocate to refer identified protection cases and ensure overall implementation of protection activities. However, as ACTED in line with the CCCM Cluster aims to target hard-to-reach areas lacking overall humanitarian support, it is very unlikely that protection partners be already present in targeted locations. Therefore ACTED through the CCCM Cluster at state and country level will advocate to enhance a protection response and ensure that protection needs are addressed.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

ACTED will continue to promote, monitor and advocate for safe and non‐discriminatory access to facilities and services by all beneficiaries. Furthermore, by implementing light site maintenance activities, ACTED will make sure that the overall safety and accessibility of targeted sites are improved and that particular attention is given to ensure that people with special needs can access services made available. Help for elder persons, people with disabilities and the chronically ill will be made available through assistance provided by outreach workers helping directly, or casual labour hired during the day to assist with these vulnerable groups when needed. The presence of outreach teams will also ensure that vulnerable people unable to participate in community meetings receive up-to-date information on activities, meetings, distributions, etc., implemented by ACTED and partners present in the camps. This service – compounded to radio messaging in Bor PoC – will reduce the risk of exclusion of the most vulnerable. The CCCM Mobile Response will guarantee that information on services and facilities is widely distributed throughout the sites in ways that do not rely on literacy, and that facilities are not located in unsafe areas within the sites.ACTED will rely on the CRM/CFM, on the Community-based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) for PSEA established by UNICEF and on outreach teams, to identify protection needs and potential barriers that residents face in the camps. Moreover, the anonymity granted by the CRM/CFM and CBCM allows people to report any protection concerns to ACTED, which then refers them to the protection partner for action (the CBCM in particular will enable beneficiaries to report on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse).

ACTED will cooperate with all Committees to identify beneficiaries’ needs and aspirations and develop activities accordingly – with a particular focus on building specific site maintenance structures capable of responding to the needs pf PSNs. In both the PoCs and while carrying out a mobile response, ACTED will provide protection and PSEA trainings to Committees and NGOs, having a dedicated PSEA Programme Manager with a strong background in Protection who will develop trainings material to be adopted by the CCCM Mobile Team. All ACTED staff will work in respect of and compliance with ACTED Code of Conduct and PSEA Policy.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring aims to measure the access of beneficiaries through specific questions about their ability to access the project sites and distributions, as well as their perceived freedom of movement/ restrictions, and reported access to various services, including local markets through the various monitoring surveys and interviews.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring measures the safety of beneficiaries through specific questions regarding their main safety concerns, experiences, and perceptions of their own safety, and if/ how these have changed over the course of the project, through the various monitoring surveys and interviews.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring aims to measure the dignity of beneficiaries through specific questions regarding their feelings and perceptions of their dignity, and if/ how these have changed over the course of the project, and whether they feel like their needs have been prioritised, through the various monitoring surveys and interviews. The questions will also focus on how the beneficiaries perceived the project approach and activities contributed or not to improving their dignity. The project monitoring will utilise the beneficiary satisfaction tool.
