Project Summary

LCED will target 30,000 individuals (6900 men, 10620 Women, 5412 boys, 7068 girls) with cash and in-kind modality (30%) in Mundri West, Tambura (WES), Yambio, Torit (EES), and Yei (CES), and only in-kind (70%) in Mundri East and Morobo Counties (CES), due to lack of functional markets. The vulnerability matrix include IDPs (48%), returnees (42%) and host community households (10%, DTM round6). Beneficiary targeting will be guided by the SNFI Cluster criteria of multiple vulnerabilities; PLWs, children <5, the elderly, PWN/PWDs, high number of dependents, combined with multiple displacement, lack of ties with the local community/purchasing power, and GBV risk associated with S/NFI needs. LCED will scale-up its cash-for-work modality, initially implemented in Yei County as pilot project, targeting 200 women/youth for training in Fuel-Efficient Stoves (FES) construction.
The choice of modality is informed by LCED previous experience in the target Counties, and the need to scale-up CBI as an avenue to recovery&resilience. 3 successful CBIs were implemented in Yei and Mundri West, while market assessment findings from Tambura (LCED, Mar19) revealed cash feasibility and cost ineffectiveness of road transport for in-kind assistance.
To implement CBIs, a $225,000 multipurpose cash fund will be needed for procurement of shelter materials (poles/grass/tools) and NFIs from the local market, allowing 1100 vulnerable families to repair their partially/totally destroyed homes, and ensuring adequate shelter/protection and dignified life. This activity will require unskilled/semi-skilled labor, since in remote areas framing material is not accessible to vulnerable beneficiaries due to protection concerns, hence the project will include a small CFW component targeting 200 individuals for training in shelter construction by the LCED local shelter expert, with secondary benefits for host communities (financial empowerment and conflict risk reduction). HLP and CCCM partners on mobile response will be engaged to minimize conflicts over land and shelters, as well as using an integrated response for camp-like settings in order to benefit groups of IDPs, following the HLP due diligence guidelines.
Multipurpose cash voucher distribution (restricted/unconditional) will include an initial $70 installment and a second one of $30, allowing households to purchase SNFIs as per their priority. Financial service providers will be mapped out in the project sites, and distribution facilitated by LCED staff. Female heads of household will be prioritized, to ensure financial empowerment of intended people and GBV risk reduction.
For in-kind response, pipeline items will be requested upon approval of S/NFI analysis report by the SNFI Cluster coordinators, and delivered to agreed distribution sites close to the beneficiaries allowing access, targeting 3,700 vulnerable households in areas without access to local market or local shelter material. Of those, 1,750 HHs in long-term displacement will be targeted with shelter repairing/NFI replenishment, considering the 6 month life-span.
To measure the project success and take evidence-based decision on the course of action, continuous monitoring and end of line evaluation (third party monitoring) will be conducted jointly with community members/local leaders as part of a Feedback and Complaint Mechanism.
Coordination with the S/NFI cluster through the SFPs on by-monthly basis, and with other humanitarian partners utilizing humanitarian hubs in Mundri, Torit, Yambio and Yei in order to take part to joint responses, will minimize duplication, and promote integrated response, gender and protection mainstreaming, GBV referrals, risk mitigation, and incorporation of HIV/AIDS and EVD/cholera messaging where possible. As alternate WES SFP, Greater Mundri SiFP and DFC focal point for UN OCHA, LCED will lead humanitarian interventions through the cluster methodology, lead humanitarian coordination and refer need/gaps to ICWG.

1 - Needs

According to the DTM round 6 (Sept 2019), 1,271,487 people have returned to South Sudan since 2014 (7.4% increase since DTM roud5), of which 30% previously displaced outside South Sudan. 51% of those have returned since the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS). Additionally, 1,465,542 individuals have been mapped as IDPs, of which 5% previously displaced abroad. 210,463 IDPs and 129,144 returnees are located in CES, 63,589 IDPs and 155,158 returnees in WES, 69,339 IDPs and 98,190 returnees in EES (DTM round 6, 2019). Many face considerable barriers to return as poor conditions in their settlements of origin; inadequate shelter conditions and construction materials, basic household items and absence of functional markets (REACH, Aug 2019).In Western Equatoria, 195,000 individuals are in IPC phase 3 (crisis) and 70,000 in phase 4 (emergency) as per August 2019, (IPC Sept 2019). According to REACH Situation Overview (Sept 2019), IDPs and returnees appeared to face insecure shelter solutions, less solid structures being the main shelter solution (rakoobas, tents, improvised shelters) and with condensed coping strategies, while the host communities frequently reported use of more solid ones that are being shared with IDPs/returnees. LCED’s findings of the recent market assessments and previous response feedback, reported displaced people having abandoned all their NFIs during the displacement, hence they are facing shortage especially of saucepans/cooking pots, mosquito nets, plastic sheets, sleeping mats, blankets, and solar lamps, also due to their lack of purchasing power. In Mundri East, Mundri West, Yambio and Torit Counties, current coping mechanisms include staying in temporary shelters (tents) or sharing congested huts with neighbors/relatives, not sustainable for the already resource constrained host HHs, raising overcrowding & GBV concerns. FGDs and interviews with the communities prioritized immediate needs in terms of cash, construction materials, cooking utensils & bedding material. Majority of respondents reported being unable to repair their houses without financial support, due to lack of source of income. In Yei, Mundri West, Yambio, Torit and Tambura Counties, it is possible to purchase basic shelter material in adequate quantity (covering material, framing material, ropes, etc), while Morobo and Mundri East do not have access to a functional market and would have to rely on in-kind assistance. At-risk groups include Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), children under 5, the elderly and Persons with Special Needs/With Disabilities (PSN/PWDs), due to their higher vulnerability to environmental hazards related to lack of access to essential SNFIs, inability to collect shelter material, and higher vulnerabilities to protection and GBV risks, especially for women and girls. This justifies LCED’s targeting criteria of most vulnerable households, considering the groups just mentioned, who cannot afford to access shelter materials and moreover community members are not willing to support at a free cost (consider that labor rates are $4/day for unskilled labor and up to $8 for skilled). Finally, findings from various FGDs revealed that the extent of damage on S/NFI differs, which will guide the cash voucher value. Multi-purpose cash support will be preferred where possible, as part of a dignified approach to allow freedom of choice in the item selection as per household priority (including personal items like clothing) and for repair kits. Such modality increases the speed of implementation, integrated response and reduces the likelihood that surplus kit items will be sold, for a smaller amount than could have been saved and used for the purchase of other much needed items.

2 - Activities

LCED will use cash and in-kind modalities in order to target the most vulnerable 30,000 individuals in the Greater Equatoria Region, with ES/NFI assistance. For the cash modality, activities will include;(1) Community consultation including protection risk and context analysis, establishment of complaint feedback mechanisms and needs/market analysis(2) Beneficiary identification(3) Market/Price monitoring – to update the prices due to fluctuations in the currency (4) Mapping of service providers for Cash-Based Intervention (cash voucher modality)(5) Community sensitization on cash modalities (vouchers, CFW) supported by community selected volunteers on source of funding, intended purpose, transfer value, CFMs(6) Provision of cash assistance (vouchers) through the selected service provider and then LCED staff(7) Training in Fuel-Efficient Stoves (FES) and shelter construction, and provision of cash under the CFW modality(8) M&E; Rapid monitoring after cash distribution, M&E (after 1st installment) by the M&E staff through household visits and FGDs with beneficiaries and later after a month (final installments) a post distribution monitoring conducted to measure impact and effectiveness. LCED may use third-party monitoring/evaluation, by involving a cluster partner, as well as an external consultant to measure effectiveness, efficiency and impact of the intervention, ensuring transparency and quality reporting.(9) Documentation and reporting to the cluster (LCED M&E data flow chart), exit activities (case studies, photos, documentaries). For in-kind assistance, activities will include; (1) S/NFI analysis, including protection risk analysis, involving at least 2 S/NFI partners and one GBV partner, and conduction of community consultations (total duration 2-3 weeks). The analysis will also include analysis of the existing land rights systems in place and risk of conflict over HLP issues, through discussion with local authorities, host community leaders, HLP and S/NFI partners.(2) Community consultations and establishment of complaint feedback mechanisms (CFM)(3) Formation and training of Village Representation Committees (VRCs, including women/youth/PWD representatives, IDP and host community leaders, and sub-chiefs; 50% female). Training will be on their role and responsibilities during activity implementation, including beneficiary selection, distribution, CFM, and mediation in cases of disputes, especially over land and property, using the appropriate channels.(4) Beneficiary registration and verification, including CFM(5) Request and transportation of ES/NFI from S/NFI cluster core pipeline(6) Local procurement of framing material (wooden poles, bamboo bundles) and roofing material (grass) wherever possible (7) Secondary transportation and distribution of pipeline items and shelter material, including GBV sensitization, and HIV/AIDS, EVD and cholera messaging, through the collaboration with GBV/protection and health partners in the project locations.(8) M&E and reporting: Rapid monitoring at the time of distribution, in order to record feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries concerning the distribution modalities and type/quality of items received, and PDM 4 weeks after distribution. LCED will use third-party monitoring, by involving a cluster partner, as well as an external consultant to measure effectiveness, efficiency and impact of the intervention, ensuring transparency and quality reporting.(9) Documentation and reporting to the cluster (data flow chart), exit activities (case studies, photos, documentaries).

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

LCED will ensure that all activities are gender sensitive and incorporate elements which allow the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). All parties involved will be informed of LCED PSEA policy and Code of Conduct, and new staff/volunteers will be trained in order to minimize and GBV risks. During the activity implementation, all-female FGDs will be conducted by a female staff jointly with a GBV partner, to identify and address gender specific needs, and encourage vulnerable and voiceless groups to actively participate in the design and implementation of projects, including beneficiary selection and shelter design (with the LCED local shelter expert), among others. The Village Representation Committees formed will emphasize women representation to break the gender cultural norms of stereotyping women for income-generating tasks, and will act as Feedback and Complaint Mechanism.Prioritizing vulnerable women and girls with distribution of solar lamps and kanga, shelter construction and CBIs will allow to prevent/mitigate SEA cases, considering the high risk of exchanging sexual favors for good and service. More so, gender-balance will be ensured while hiring local/temporary personnel, in order to provide a source of income to both genders.Partner coordination and joint missions will be emphasized to minimize overlapping/duplication, share resources and information. For security updates, LCED will work closely with the local authorities, SFPs and other implementing partners to share information. The distribution sites’ selection will be done in consultation with local leaders, women and PWDs, to identify and suggest safe and accessible distribution sites and schedules to prevent any security and SGBV risks related to access. To reduce risk of conflict between host community and beneficiaries, 10% of most vulnerable host community HHs will be targeted for assistance, and local procurement of shelter material will be done from the host community.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Community consultations will be conducted at the inception phase of the project, including FGDs with representatives of vulnerable groups (women, youth, elderly, PWDs), in order to make sure that views, challenges and needs of the vulnerable groups are addressed.Also, in each distribution location LCED will form a Village Representation Committee, including representatives of vulnerable groups, IDP/returnee and host community leaders, and sub-chiefs. The VRCs provide great insight for activity planning and project design, Complaint and Feedback Mechanism, as well as context-specific information, changes, and early warning in case of conflict. They will work with LCED in order to identify the most vulnerable households to be targeted by in-kind or cash-based interventions, based on the shared vulnerability criteria, and will be responsible for ensuring that all stakeholders are aware of the priorities for beneficiary selection, and providing feedback to the community about the project activities. They will also assist the LCED team in the distribution of S/NFI and assist the LCED team by mediating in case of discontent among the population in regards to the prioritization of the beneficiaries, quality of items, and any other complaint raised and be part of the project monitoring team.For quality assurance and guidelines’ adherence, a Complaint and Feedback Mechanism will be in place, in consultation with the community to allow beneficiaries to provide feedback on project activities including the conduct of staff. This will be supplemented by continuous monitoring, PDM and end line evaluation (third party monitoring) that will be conducted jointly with community members/local leaders to assess satisfaction, timeliness, quality and impact of our intervention. Data collected will inform action during the activity implementation, while lessons learnt will be shared with the different stakeholders including the S/NFI cluster to inform and improve its future work.
