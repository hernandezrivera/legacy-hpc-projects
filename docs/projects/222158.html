Project Summary

Care for Children & Old Age in South Sudan (CCOSS) will scale up provision of lifesaving WASH and Nutrition response to women, men, girls, and boys affected by multiple shocks and hazards in South Sudan with critical water, sanitation, and hygiene services, items, and facilities. CCOSS through this project aims to provide lifesaving emergency water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion services for conflict affected and flood displaced, vulnerable host communities and people in disease prone (high GAM famine and outbreak related) areas including protection related actions to mitigate GBV, strengthening of communities’ resilience to cope with the impact of emergencies and recurrent shocks.
Critically, this project builds on significant knowledge of CCOSS on the ground and the trust with targeted communities that have established a strong foundation. This emergency WASH project will comprise of assessments including safety audits, preparedness, resilience, frontline emergency WASH response and cluster coordination. Care for Children & Old Aage in South Sudan will thus ensure that there is emergency lifesaving support by providing access to WASH services as per Sphere and South Sudan WASH Cluster standards.
CCOSS will reach the most vulnerable populations with an integrated package of life-saving services that includes WASH and nutrition. Integrated WASH and nutrition interventions will focus on delivering a minimum package of services at the nutrition centers, at the household of patients, and in the community. Nutrition activities at household level include context specific WASH ‘small doable actions’ and establishing means for minimum standards of WASH. The WASH ‘small doable actions’ include handwashing with water and ash, disposal of feces including child feces and treatment and storage of water.
CCOSS programmes will be informed by a conflict-sensitive approach, with a strong focus on social cohesion, embedded in an analysis of socio-political-ethnic dynamics as the main drivers of violence. In its approach to service provision, CCOSS will be accountable to affected populations, will ensure equitable representation of men, boys, women and girls in all community feedback and complaint mechanisms, and will target the most vulnerable people, including returnees, internally displaced persons, survivors of gender-based violence, and persons with disabilities. Men, women, boys and girls will be engaged throughout the project cycle. We will secure appropriate means (community desks) for affected populations to provide feedback and to complain about their experiences and perspectives on our programmes and responses, including on sensitive issues. These mechanisms will ensure that information is regularly collected, analyzed, integrated into decision-making and acted on, and that the results are communicated back to people in a meaningful dialogue.
The project will assess, identify and respond to protection risks and vulnerability of affected populations and communities by ensuring safe and reliable mechanisms are in place to access assistance and to raise concerns about sexual exploitation and abuse issues, and other protection issues such as gender-based violence. The project staff have been trained on PSEA, the project volunteers such as hygiene promoters will also be trained on PSEA.
This project will target 35,000 individuals (Men:4200, Women: 9800, Boys: 6300 and Girls: 14700) in Abyei Administration Area and Twic (Warrap). Per county, the numbers are as follows: - Abyei 21000, and Twic 14,000.

1. Needs

WASH access throughout South Sudan remains critically low with 64 percent of households either lacking access to an improved water source within 30 minutes or reporting protection concerns to access these sources and 73 percent of the country practicing open defecation. During rainy season, most counties in are affected by heavy and flash floods which continue to strain access to the very limited basic resources. Flood induced displacement limit access to WASH infrastructure and places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites. The limited access to WASH services impacts people across all population groups, but the needs are the most severe among the newly displaced population, and in areas hosting returnees, IDPs and host communities. Poor access to WASH services contributes to the high levels of food insecurity with an overlap of WASH Cluster priority counties with critical needs and areas reporting high GAM rates. Water-borne diseases, including diarrhea, contribute to negative nutritional outcomes and contribute to the risk of famine. In addition to WASH infrastructure needs in nutrition treatment centers, mothers and caretakers of malnourished children require access to hygiene information and non-food items to practice safe hygiene in support of nutrition improvements. High WASH severity can be attributed to a heavy reliance on surface water, lacking sanitation facilities and the repeated presence of insecurity in these locations. Women and girls face increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water, using communal latrines and accessing menstrual hygiene products. Additionally, appropriate and dignified washing locations remain as critical needs. High open defecation rates also expose women, children and people living with disabilities at higher risk of violence and discrimination, particularly in IDP sites and other crowded settlements. The absence, presence or placement of WASH facilities is a driver of population movement and conflict over land and property in some areas and requires careful management to ensure a conflict-sensitive approach that strengthens social cohesion.
