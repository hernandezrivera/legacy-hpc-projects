Project Summary

Despite above-average national cereal crop production and improved livestock production following a largely favorable Deyr (October-December) rainy season, up to 1.3 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through mid-2020. (FEWSNET/FSNAU, 03 Feb 2020) Humanitarian food assistance needs are high across Somalia, where Stressed (IPC Phase 2) and Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes remain widespread. An estimated 3.5 million people in rural livelihood zones, urban areas, and IDP settlements need food assistance. Furthermore, a long-term decline in rainfall performance and preliminary climatological research suggest an elevated likelihood of below-average rainfall in the March to May 2021 season. Two consecutive poor seasons would likely result in rapidly worsening acute food insecurity in 2021. (FEWSNET, 31 Aug 2020). Furthermore, Somalia farmers could face a double jeopardy of the second attack of desert locust even as the country struggles to combat the COVID19 pandemic. the situation remains very critical as more swarm’s form and a new generation of breeding has started. According to the FAO October report, 2020 indicates that mature adult groups and swarms are present and breeding in the northeast and in central areas of Mudug and Galgaduud. Hatching and band formation have commenced in the past few days and more is expected in the coming weeks. The indirect effects of the COVID-19 pandemic on food security within Somalia are more visible, driven by the global economic downturn, supply chain disruptions in the Arabian Peninsula, and social distancing measures. International and local flight restrictions and port closures in Dubai disrupted import and export trade activities, while money vendors report steep declines in both external and internal remittances. Declines in business activities have affected the airline, construction, road transport, hospitality, petty trade, miraa/khat trade, and education and health services sectors. Protracted conflict across most of southern and central Somalia continues to periodically cause loss of life and assets, suspend cropping activities, disrupt trade and population movements, and impede access to humanitarian assistance. According to data collected by ACLED, the number of conflict events in Somalia in the first six months of 2020 is similar to the number of conflict events recorded in 2019 (+2 percent) and similar to conflict recorded over the preceding five years (-1 percent). Conflict and weather hazards continue to be the primary drivers of population displacement across Somalia in 2020. According to UNCHR, approximately 2.6 million people are internally displaced in a protracted situation in Somalia as of April. While some herd growth has occurred, many poor pastoralists are still unable to afford all basic non-food needs from the sale of livestock and livestock products. Households routinely allocate a high portion of the income from livestock sales to debt repayment, accumulated during recurrent droughts. To boost the food security of the poor pastoralist and IDPs, TOWFIIQ will distribute $60 unconditional cash transfer, which is 60$ of MEB for a period of 3 months to 1,200 poor pastoralists, IDPs and marginalized communities in Cadaado and Dhuusamareeb. Benefiting around 7,200 people (1296 men, 1440 women, 2160 girls, 2160 boys, and 360 disabilities) including minorities and IDPs. The project will give priority to women-heads households to maintain the dignity of women against the risk of GBV, HHs with disabilities and malnurished children & women, and poor of the poorest households.

Project Activities

Activity 1. Conduct two workshops at the inception of the project in Cadaado and Dhusamareeb districts, each 100 participants (50 men and 50 men) from community stakeholders including local authorities, women groups, disabled people, and minorities to consult and familiarize them with the project objectives, activities, feedback, selection criterion, and complaint mechanisms for accountability & transparency. Activity 2: Provide unconditional Cash Transfer (UCT) through mobile transfer to improve the nutrition and immediate food security to 1,200 crisis affected IDPs & Host Communities HHs in Galagaduud region (Cadaado and Dhuusamareeb districts), benefiting around 7,200 Provide unconditional Cash Transfer (UCT) to improve the nutrition and food security to 1,000 IDP & Host Community HHs in Bari, and Hiran regions benefiting around 6,000 (900 men, 1200 women, 1800 girls, 1800 boys, and 300 disabilities) for a period of 3 months depending on the severity of food insecurity phases, vulnerability, and seasonality of the livelihoods. for a period of 3 months depending on the severity of food insecurity phases, vulnerability, and seasonality of the livelihoods. The project will give priority to poor women-headed households, households with disabilities, households with malnourished children and women, minorities and poor of the poorest households in Cadaado and Dhuusameeb districts of Galgaduud region.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Due to ongoing conflict Alshabab militia can easily reach the targeted villages, thus the security issue will not allow us to distribute food items in a such insecure remote areas.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

This project is designed to stimulate Dhusamareeb markets, as pastoralists will buy food and other items from Dhusamreeb markets which currently remain dormant due to decreased remittance from Somalia diaspora as a result Covid 19 problems.
