Project Summary

This project aims to support health and social institutions (hereafter referred to as SIs) in the field of WASH in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts to enhance safe and qualitative service delivery to conflict-affected communities by providing hygiene kits, WASH-related equipment and hygiene items.
A well-functioning WASH infrastructure is crucial for the health and wellbeing of the conflict-affected communities in Eastern Ukraine. The protracted conflict has not only caused direct damage to this infrastructure but has also aggravated the tight funding situation, a situation which has been further affected by the COVID-19 pandemic. This threatens to disrupt services but also increased the risks SI staff members are exposed to.
In order to alleviate the pressure on these facilities, IOM will distribute COVID-19 hygiene kits, including information leaflets on COVID-19 protective measures and hygiene practices, to 175 SIs. As IOM is currently responding to 175 institutions in NGCA/GCA, IOM will approach current receiving institutions to see if top-up kits are required. In addition, WASH-related equipment will be procured, delivered and installed at 80 SIs to facilitate their work and reduce the risk of infections. As elderly people are particularly vulnerable and underserved, the project also foresees the distribution of adult diapers to 125 SIs. Hygiene kits are in line with cluster guidance and will include cleaning tools, disinfection material in line with WHO recommendations for COVID-19, washing powder/fluid to allow turnover of communal use items (linens /plates/cutlery) and some personal use items identified by SI that assist in the management of individuals with mobility issues and those who are bed ridden to make hygiene management easier. These will enable the facilities to maintain the increased cleaning schedules that are advised by WHO and local legislation to help mitigate against the spread of COVID-19.
Identification of institutions in GCA will be done through coordination with WASH cluster primarily with secondary coordination with health clusters in relation to IPC based on relevant cluster gaps analysis. These locations and institutions will be further coordinated with regional and local authorities to ensure that most in need locations from the oblast perspective are being considered. Sanitary equipment procured will be determined through a needs assessment at the institution level to ensure that it is necessary and appropriate. Hygiene kit content will be based on cluster guidance and advised through PDM results and revised needs assessments at the facility level. In NGCA, given the restrictions, IOM will leverage IP knowledge of the operating environment and needs assessments where possible to advise on both hygiene kit content review and sanitary equipment provision.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in the ECA, originating from more than six years of armed conflict, has only grown more acute since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic. The situation regarding the provision of essential services such as water supply, adequate heating, wastewater disposal, and sewage in key health and social institutions (SIs) remains a key concern. Social institutions typically suffer from a lack of funding which impacts their ability to sufficiently cover their hygiene and sanitation needs. This is particularly pronounced in NGCA where there has been a substantial reduction in funding as a result of the conflict and the economic embargo has affected supplies. The identified lack of hygiene products in SIs does not only pose a risk to the health of the staff members but also facilitates the spread of COVID-19 to beneficiaries and threatens the disruption of services. HNO 2020 highlighted the detrimental impact COVID-19 is having on an already weakened health system with WHO highlighting the specific threat to the elderly and people with pre-existing medical conditions. The risk of spread in institutions, which often manage these vulnerable groups, and the need for support to mitigate against this is reflected in both the WASH and health clusters’ key humanitarian needs 2020. These concerns over the inability to maintain adequate hygiene standards was reflected in IOM COVID-19 WASH assessments conducted with 130 institutions in GCA and IPs in NGCA in July/August 2020. All institutions assessed indicated substantial gaps in hygiene consumables, diapers and sanitary equipment (washing machines/dryers/disinfection cabinets/autoclaves/disinfection packs, etc). The need was confirmed by the heads of SIs in Donetsk and Luhansk oblast (GCA) supported with handwashing stations during the PDM interviews over the phone in August 2020. Furthermore, IOM discussions with regional line ministry representatives in both the Ministry of Health and the Ministry of Social Policy confirmed the need for continued hygiene assistance particularly in 2nd/3rd line response SIs, as funding was being directed towards 1st line COVID-19 response facilities. WHO guidance on IPC of epidemic and pandemic prone acute respiratory infections in health care facilities (2014) indicates the need to wash hospital linens regularly and at high temperatures to help reduce the risk of bedding/clothing acting as vectors. IOM WASH assessments both in GCA and NGCA highlighted a high need for appropriate sanitary equipment for washing/disinfecting as existing equipment is often outdated or absent, both of which reduces the institutions’ efficiency in cleaning reusable items (bedding/medical clothing/patient clothing, etc). Due to COVID-19, institutions are turning over these items more frequently due to the concern of COVID-19 spread which requires higher capacity equipment. IOM’s latest Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) report on hygiene kits distribution in NGCA, conducted in December 2019, showed that most assisted SIs have huge needs in hygiene items. Representatives of 73% institutions mentioned the high relevance of the assistance provided in IOM kits due to the inclusion of hygiene items relevant to patients with mobility issues and those who are bed ridden. IOM’s current COVID-19 response includes hygiene kit distributions to 176 institutions in GCA and NGCA. Following cluster guidance, the current level of response is sufficient to cover the needs of the institutions for 3 months. With the continued threat of COVID-19, IOM is looking to replenish stock of targeted institutions. With the elderly constituting nearly 40% of the ECA population, special hygiene needs in the form of diapers is highly requested across medical and social institutions. IOM’s COVID-19 WASH assessments (July/August 20) highlighted a diaper gap across all medical facilities and social institutions assessed.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

To respond to the needs of social institutions in the context of COVID-19, IOM intends to continue hygiene consumable support in line with cluster guidance and provide sanitary equipment support based on pre-assessments but to be confirmed with additional assessments in target areas. This support will improve the conditions of stay of patients/residents and ensure adherence to sanitary standards. Priority will be given but not limited to facilities with infectious wards for the treatment of patients with COVID-19 in NGCA, 2nd and 3rd line medical facilities, IDP centers and social institutions specifically targeting the elderly, people with disabilities, individuals with mental health issues, homeless shelters and referral shelters from IOMs protection programme Where possible, IOM intends to integrate its WASH response to maximise input at the institutional level by providing SIs with hygiene kits, diapers and sanitary equipment and targeting, where possible, the same institutions benefiting from WASH rehabilitations will be provided with hygiene consumable assistance. As IOM has already run both hygiene kit and sanitation equipment provision (content based on WASH cluster recommendation, IPC partner discussion and assessments with facilities) at the institutional level, IOM PDM results will contribute towards the base package and further confirmed/altered with additional assessments with targeted institutions. Outcome: Patients/residents of social institutions in Donetsk and Luhansk are able to maintain adequate sanitary standards in light of COVID-19. Output 1: Targeted SIs in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts are better equipped with hygiene consumables, including adult diapers, to support conflict-affected communities to mitigate against the spread of COVID-19 Activities for output 1:• Assessment and identification social institutions; although in line with cluster guidance, IOM may include additional items as identified as essential through gender/age assessments;• Procurement and distribution of hygiene kits, diapers and other hygiene consumables for medical and in/outpatient facilities; • Procurement and distribution of hygiene kits, diapers and other hygiene consumables among selected social institutions; • Post-distribution monitoring of support provided.Hygiene kits will be comprised of personal and general use hygiene consumable items. The list of the items to be included into the hygiene kits are in line with WASH Сluster recommendations with the addition of some core items (including disinfectant, hand sanitiser, cleaning items mops, gloves), identified by IOM during previous WASH interventions as essential. These additional items have been endorsed by the WASH Cluster during COVID 19 kit composition consultations and include personal hygiene items to help facilities maintain the general hygiene of patients and equipment (disposable diapers/rubber undersheets). To promote hygiene practices together with the hygiene kits, IOM will endeavor to provide hygiene information packs as well. Output 2: Targeted SIs are better equipped with sanitation equipment to facilitate their support to affected communities and mitigate against the spread of COVID 19 Activities for output 2:• Identification of institutions through stakeholder and cluster engagement• Assessment of targeted institutions;• Procurement and distribution of required sanitation equipment (for example, washing and drying machines, disinfection cameras, etc) through an open competitive process; to be further distributed and installed in SIs; • Post-distribution monitoring of support provided.To ensure accountability to the population, IOM operates a number of feedback mechanisms which allows beneficiaries to contact IOM by phone, email and through a global accountability platform. Information on the mechanisms are distributed to beneficiaries of IOM projects and a dedicated team are responsible for investigating claims and providing feedback.
