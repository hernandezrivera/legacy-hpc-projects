Project Summary

This integrated Health, Nutrition and WASH intervention is meant to provide essential, life-saving and life-sustaining assistance to the floods and conflict affected populace of Middle (Barsaanguni) and Lower Juba to reduce the acute humanitarian needs and excess mortality and Morbidity.
Malnutrition is one of the most persistent humanitarian challenges in Somalia despite concerted efforts to address it. The results of 33 separate nutrition surveys between May to July 2018, indicate that the overall nutrition situation in Somalia is at sustained levels despite improved food security, reduced disease outbreaks and sustained humanitarian interventions. Nearly 954,007 children are expected to be acutely malnourished, including 173,594 who are expected to be severely malnourished during the next one year period (Sep 2018-Sep 2019). The surveys indicate Critical levels of GAM (15-29.9%) in 12 out of 33 populations surveyed. The sustained nutrition situation is attributed to high morbidity, sub-optimal IYCF practices, poor hygiene and sanitation situation and low coverage of immunization. However, despite the improved humanitarian situation, the positive outlook and gains made, humanitarian assistance needs to be sustained to support the people in need since these gains has not translated into improvement in the nutrition situation and categorically the nutrition situation remains of serious concern. This intervention will provide enhanced access to integrated curative and preventive nutrition services including but not limited to: Regular screening and identification of acutely malnourished child and PLW's, provision of therapeutic and supplementary support, establishing strong referral pathways for those requiring in-patient stabilization care, promotion of optimal child feeding and care practices, provision of essential Vitamins and Micro-nutrient supplements to both children-boys and girls and PLWS's, promotion of appropriate hygiene and sanitation practices, uptake of immunization services in both the static and the mobile centers to reduce the high morbidity cases emanated from poor hygiene practices and vaccine-preventable diseases.
This Intervention is also geared to provide life saving emergency primary health services including maternal, neonatal and child health support through both static and mobile health clinics in floods and conflict affected locations of Barsaanguni in Jilib,-Bulo gaduud, Yontoy and Wirokoy in Kismayo districts,-Booda, farwamo, marrer and Raxoole in Jamame to scale up disease outbreak surveillance system for early cases detection and timely responses; accelerate response to epidemics and communicable diseases outbreaks through joint field monitoring and supervision, regional and state health cluster coordination meetings; enhance the capacity of health workers for effective emergency responses and disseminate health and hygiene information on the prevention and control of AWD/cholera outbreaks.
A technical team of men and women comprised of: Health manager, Health supervisor, Qualified nurses, Auxiliary nurses, Pharmacist, Mid-wife, Health promoter/crowd controllers and a vaccinators will be recruited to support this intervention. The Health supervisor will direct day to day Supervision of the project Implementation coupled with routine on-job trainings where applicable.
The Health manager will overall guidance and support to all teams, collate and compile weekly and monthly progress reports, share it with the donor, MOH and the Health Cluster through HMIS. To ensure gender equality and mainstreaming 50% of the project team will be female. No discrimination or exclusion based on gender, minority ethnic, religion or belief will be taken lightly. Child protection and safeguarding policy will be signed and adopted by every staff member and no 1-1 lonely interaction with a child. Health & Nutrition centers will have gender sensitive and gender friendly facilities. All vulnerable members ( men, women, boys, girls, child-headed HH, single women, older persons and Female headed HHs) in the project are will be targeted in the WASH, Health and Nutrition services without discrimination or exclusion.
It is estimated that, about 2.9M people need urgent WASH assistance in Somalia in 2019. Among them:
• IDPs: 1.5M
• Non-IDPs/Host communities: 1.4M
• Population in rural drought hotspots: 0.5M
• Population living in AWD/Cholera hotspots: 1.2M
• Natural disaster affected/vulnerable (un-responded): 0.5M
• Women and children, and man in need of protection in relation to WASH: 2.1M
Use of unprotected water sources, open defecation and failure to wash hands with soap at critical times contributes largely to frequent AWD/Cholera outbreaks. More that half of the population in Somalia have no adequate access to sanitation services and only a third of the population practice hand washing with soap at critical times, and according to the WASH cluster access to an improved water source remains below 40%, with large variations from one region to another. To address the worrying WASH situation in the target area, the project project aims to construct/rehabilitate water sources and establish sustainable management structures, desludge latrines, increase knowledge in good hygiene and sanitation to reduce AWD/Cholera as well as address protection risks that women and girls face as they access the WASH facilities.

Needs

Somalia has been suffering from civil conflicts, droughts, flooding and humanitarian crisis for more than two decades. Public health services and other basic amenities in South Central Somalia have been in a dire state since the collapse of the central government in the early 1990s. Middle and Lower Juba region has been vulnerable to extreme shocks such as drought and famine exacerbated by insecurity and civil conflict. 90% of middle juba region is still under the control of non-state actors. However courtesy of the joint forces part of the locations in Jilib and Jamame were recently liberated but the local communities still remained landlocked with no any humanitarian support, and as a result, Malnutrition (Under-nutrition), poor immunization coverage and disease outbreak remain alarming levels in this locations. In addition, the current fighting between Al-Shabaab and SNAF assisted with AMISOM forces in most regions in South Central Somalia has aggravated the situation by causing new displacements. With modest hopes of greater stability in Somalia, some IDPs and refugee Returnees from neighboring countries are spontaneously returning to their areas of origin especially lower Juba. The presence of high number of returness has aggravated the growing needs of humanitarian assistance and the protracted deadly AWD/Cholera cases in Lower Juba. According to latest report released by FSNAU, reveals,1.5 million in crisis or worse require immediate lifesaving assistance. Somalia experienced outbreaks of measles, diarrheal disease (AWD/Cholera) and vaccine-derived polio, as well as malaria in 2018. The health system remains fragmented and under-resourced and is ill-equipped to provide the life-saving and preventative services is inevitable. GAM rates is well above emergency threshold of 15%. nearly 954,007 children are expected to be acutely malnourished, including 173,594 who are expected to be severely malnourished during the next one year period (Sep 2018-Sep 2019).

Activities

SFH make this appeal to provide life saving emergency primary health services including maternal, neonatal and child health support through both static and mobile health clinics in flood affected locations in Kismayo districts such as Bulo gadud, wirkoy where SFH constructed the 1st ever hospital outside kismayo and Barsannguni Location in Middle Juba, scale up disease outbreak surveillance system for early cases detection and timely responses; accelerate response to epidemics and communicable diseases outbreaks through joint field monitoring and supervision, regional and state health cluster coordination meetings; enhance the capacity of health workers for effective emergency responses with a particular focus on the following specific deliverables: -Provide curative (including treatment of AWD/Cholera) and promotive health services to 70,000 beneficiaries (35,000 girls, 21,000 boys, 10,500 women, 3,500 men) for IDPs and flood affected populations.-Vaccinate 5000 children under the age of 5 and 2000 women of child bearing age from vaccine preventable diseases -Strengthen the referral system to Bulogadud and Kismayo General Hospital for patients in need of secondary healthcare services including malnourished children that need stabilization center (SC).-Conduct awareness raising activities to raise awareness of common communicable diseases with a particular focus on AWD/Cholera, childhood illnesses, maternal health, malaria and malnutrition-related morbidity.- Regular identification of acutely malnourished children and PLW's-Therapeutic feeding for the treatment of acute malnutrition cases( OTP/SFP).-Micro-nutrient support for the vulnerable groups ( <5 and PLW's) -IYCF support to caregivers of sick children. -10 new Shallow wells constructed and 30 rehabilitated- Construction of 700 Emergency and Institutional latrines with hand washing facilities. Hygiene and sanitation promotions services by training 100 CHPs. Hygiene Kits 4000 HHs in emergency.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

1. Minority or marginalized communities will be included in all consultative meetings regarding the WASH facilities.2. Sensitization gathering will be conducted and introduce project objectives, selection criteria for various components and the entitlements for all 3. Confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms will be set to ensure the information reaches all 4. Follow up and subsequent meetings will be hold for all target communities.5. Hotlines and free radio sessions will be used to air the project deliverables.6. Project committees will be selected from the all representatives of the target communities.7. Health and Nutrition services will be accessible to boys, girls, men and women, minority or majority without any discrimination based on gender or ethnic background.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

-Women and girls will be involves in the constructions of the WASH facilities.-Gender sensitive facilities will be constructed in such a way that the latrines will have inside lockers and menstrual hygiene facility.-50% of the WASH committes will be women.-Child headed household will be considered for the kits distributions to avoid age segregations.-Skilled deliveries and IYCF sessions will be conducted by female health workers.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

-SFH is a locally based Humanitarian organization and enjoys full support and acceptance by the local communities- this will help us receive timely information any threat to staff or vandalism of facilities.-We limit visibility in sensitive areas to reduce any possible risk.-Project and village level committees will be established to ensure communal and organizational linkages
