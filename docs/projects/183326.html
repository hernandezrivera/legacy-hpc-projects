Project Summary

Lebanon faces an unprecedented economic, health, political and humanitarian crisis. Over 55% of Lebanese (ILO) are living in poverty, impacting their access to healthcare. The public health system faced challenges to provide drugs, equipment, and services prior to the crisis. The current subsidies on medicines in USD is already being removed, putting hospitals and patients in imminent danger to their lives and continuity of services.
RMF and Project HOPE, a US based NGO with over 60 years of experience in Global Health and Emergency Response, will support the Bcharreh, Tannourine, Sir Denniye, and Ehden Governmental Hospitals (with both Sir Denniye and Ehden hospitals located in mountainous areas), and three private hospitals (located in coastal densely populated areas) in Koura, Minnieh-Dannieh, and Zgharta districts, with essential medication. The initiative aims to meet the medicine needs of the hospitals to cater to vulnerable Lebanese and migrants, for a duration of 11 months.
The partnership is based on previous joint experience in securing 66 boxes of medical supplies through 7 health facilities and NGOs. 100,000 items of PPEs were distributed to 4 hospitals impacted by the Beirut explosion in August 2020, one NGO in Beirut, and 4 hospitals and dispensaries in North-Lebanon, to protect health care workers from exposure to COVID-19.
RMF will sign MoUs with the hospitals as recipient of medicine, and RMF as distributor / monitoring entity ensuring that medicine are provided in priority to the most vulnerable, and to segments not benefiting from health insurance or Social Security.
As per existing communication with the hospitals, yearly medicine needs are already identified at each private hospital’s pharmacy. Public hospitals directly coordinate with the Ministry of Public Health MoPH. RMF Project Manager and recruited Pharmacist will coordinate with MoPH and the UN Health Sector Working Group (UN HWG) to validate the list of requirements ensuring there is no duplication with existing initiatives, and that the listed medicine are considered essential.
Project HOPE will leverage its international procurement platform to procure the needed medicine upon each hospital’s needs and medicine consumption frequency, as it brings a wealth of experience in Global Supply Chain Management having delivered over 2 billion USD in medical commodities around the world. All pharmaceuticals will align MoPH and WHO essential medicines lists for Lebanon.
RMF will also prioritize procuring medicine from local manufacturers should local supply chains be active or restored. RMF will manage customs’ clearance administrative procedures.
RMF will use its online vulnerability assessment form and scoring sheet based on the Ministry of Social Affair’s vulnerability indicators. Applications are in both Arabic and English. Each patient (Lebanese or migrant) requiring procured medicine will have an application filled, and if scoring validates vulnerability, will be prioritized, through direct communication with the hospital, to receive the medicine for free, tracked through an attributed case number preventing system abuse in getting medicine from more than one source per month, averting medicine trafficking.
Procured medicine will be temporarily stored at RMF’s warehouse (respecting proper storage temperature and other environmental factors). A routing system will be developed along with a distribution schedule. RMF will ensure the distribution of procured assets, and will update MoPH and the UN HWG with periodic distribution reports.
RMF has access to fuel and good connections with suppliers. RMF’s warehouse benefits from increased power supply through renewable energy, preventing the interruption of operations within the current fuel crisis.
RMF will integrate Project HOPE’s procurement platform as sole procurer and distributor, with capacity building in supply chain management from Project HOPE throughout the initiative, ensuring sustainability.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

RMF will distribute 11 months’ needs of essential medicine for 7 hospitals in Batroun, Koura, Bsharri, Minnieh-Dannieh, and Zgharta through:- Signature of MoUs with targeted institutions- Usage of RMF’s online vulnerability assessment application and scoring sheet- Gathering of needs of medical facilities, upon each hospital’s requirements and distribution frequency- Validating the list of requirements with both MoPH, and UN HWG, ensuring that there is no overlap or duplication with other initiatives- Develop proper routing and delivery schedule to ensure the timely supply of essential medicine. The routing will be shared with targeted hospitals to ensure proper coordination- Procure needed medicine through Project HOPE’s international procurement platform, or through RMF direct procurement for locally produced medicine- Coordinate with key local actors on customs administrative clearance of procured items- Ensure proper storage of procured assets, and timely delivery to targeted facilities- Periodically inform MoPH representatives and UN HWG on medicine supply updates, while adhering to the UN Interagency reporting procedures- Track beneficiaries’ receipt of medicine through an assigned case number, to prevent system abuse and medicine trafficking- Adhere to Project HOPE’s procurement platform to ensure the ability to procure medicine internationally and distribute locally through supply chain capacity building from Project HOPE.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Protection, gender equality, and social inclusion are mainstreamed in RMF’s response to ensure that the needs of women, men, girls and boys are achieved. RMF initiates community dialogue with key stakeholders and municipalities to identify risks and suggest solutions to address rights violations to prevent and mitigate risks. Signed MoUs with targeted hospitals will comprise ethical causes related to direct contact with beneficiaries. RMF will ensure that procured medicine are distributed in a mindful manner to all vulnerable beneficiaries without any discrimination of the basis of gender, age, religion, or nationality.RMF will provide awareness sessions to hospital staff on equality principles, and will disseminate a hotline through which beneficiaries may report any instance of SEA, GBV, or non-equal treatment. RMF staff are trained to receive input from marginalized communities and will ensure proper referral to RMF’s internal case management unit for proper case handling.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

An appropriate feedback/complaint mechanisms will be put in place to make sure that affected populations are able to reflect their feedback as well as report on any type of complaint. A dedicated hotline will be activated for this initiative, managed by a trained hotline respondent able to provide logistical feedback, as well as ensure proper referral if needed. The delivery schedule will be shared with targeted medical facilities for feedback. Updates on procurement and shipping will also be periodically provided to facilities’ focal points. RMF will also consider avoiding community events disruption (for example, weekly prayer) in the route planning. Finally feedback is solicited in an ad hoc manner, through ongoing interaction during the implementation of the activities. RMF will release a survey compiling beneficiaries’ input on what is deemed essential medication. Highly recommended items not included in the estimated hospital needs, will be added for procurement.
