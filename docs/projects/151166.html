Project Summary

The Mudug region in Puntland and Sool region in Somaliland are among areas classified under in IPC 3 and 4, which are considered as hotspots in need of urgent nutrition and health support interventions. They have Critical prevalence of acute malnutrition (≥15% GAM) or 10.7 percent or more of children have Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) below the 125 millimeters (mm) threshold affected by food insecurity. Despite recent improvement, an estimated 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be in Crisis or worse through December 2018 according to findings from the post-Gu seasonal assessment conducted in June and July 2018. With predicted forecast of average to above-average Deyr (October-December 2018) rains, are expected above-average off-season harvests, and favourable market conditions are expected to further improve food security conditions through December 2018. However, acute food insecurity remains prevalent among pastoral populations who lost most of their livestock and became destitute during the 2016/2017 drought and Cyclone Sagar; riverine populations affected by flooding in April and May 2018; and others experiencing large-scale, protracted displacement due to conflict and natural hazards. These groups are expected to face food consumption gaps or engage in unsustainable coping strategies to meet their food needs, and humanitarian assistance is required to prevent further deterioration of their food security and nutrition situation.IFRC will work with Somali Red Crescent in Puntland and Somaliland to improve access to food by providing food vouchers for vulnerable groups and providing certified seeds and farm inputs for land preparation and irrigation . in partnership IFRC and SRCS will build resilience of these communities affected by recurrent drought, floods, civil conflict, and a changing climate through natural resource management and trainings on CCA. The proposed interventions will be geared towards improving food and nutrition status of the targeted communities to reduce vulnerability, malnutritional rates and enhance their resilience to future shocks. The proposed interventions will seek to support 1,500 pastoralists and agro -pastoralists households, with a special focus on households led by women, households with young nutritionally vulnerable children and households with older persons or people living with disabilities in Puntland and Somaliland. The project will target areas which have potential for irrigation utilizing seasonal flood waters and diverting water from seasonal rivers. The project will combine a mixture of household level, and group or community level initiatives for sustainable food, vegetable, fruit and fodder production to tackle food insecurity, malnutrition as well as in the long run potentially provide alternative incomes from sale of fruits, vegetables and fodder, and contribute to environmental rehabilitation. In addition to food and fodder production, the project will support strengthening of capacity to reduce risks from disasters, as well as support in building capacity of Somalia Red Crescent Society and communities on livestock protection and rebuilding of livestock after emergencies guided by LEGS in Somaliland and Puntland

Needs

Assessed Needs:According to variouse reports, assessmnts and JMCNA report, they point to increasing levels of food insecurity with households reporting abnormal household hunger rates, reliance on coping strategies and a reduction in the quality and variety of food. Additionally, findings indicate decreasing levels of household economic resilience, as evidenced by a reported reduction in household income sources, rising food prices, and a reported lack of resources to purchase food as a key access constraint despite the improved food sitaution. This is due to the impact of 2016/2017 drought that left most households vulnerable with their source of livelihoods destroyed. The project will aim to improve immediate access to food by offering food vouchers to vulnerable families and conditional cash transfers towards repairing infastructures that contibute to improved food assesst. With the aim of restroirng livelihoods related to food access, we will invest in training farmers on importance of drought resilient crops, rotational farming and provide certified seedlings and farm inputs for irrigation and small scale farming.The project will also aim to protect livestock through fodder crop production, tree planting, rehabiltation of rangeland, water sources, trainings on LEGS. In improving communities prepardness and resilience IFRC will train SRCS staff and volunteers on Climate ChangeAdpatation and support in contexualising these trainings for the affected communities.The capacity building of community will improve communities prepardness and reduce vulnerability to natural shocks. Due to the continious increase in malnutrition rates in these areas, nutrient – dense food production as well as a focus on fodder production will be be emphasised. The project will seek to increase household income through cash grants targeting households led by children, women and people with disabilities or older persons. The cash grants will be linked to IGAs for sustainability.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

SRCS has continued to coordinate its interventions with the goverment and other actors in the area to ensure complementarity and sharing of learning on suitable resilience building actions. Other partners including UN Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and the local Government Ministry of Agriculture in Puntland and Somaliland. These actors will be included in the coordination mechanism of the project. In particular, the project will work with FAO and the Ministry of Agriculture to ensure animal health aspects are strengthened in the target area to enhance productivity. The project will also tap into the technical capacities within government line Ministries in areas like fodder, fruit and crop management, as well as natural resource management to ensure local authorities and actors are involved to create ownership and enhance sustainability.The project will promote learning and information sharing on hunger resilience through building strong relationships with other partners in resilience work in Puntland and Somaliland. IFRC Somalia and the regional food security adviser will provide technical assistance to SRCS to enable the project meet its intended objectives. IFRC will ensure the project intergrates with other programmes such as health and WASH to ensure its addressing the root causes of the issues addressed in these two prgrammes. Hence there will also be strong collaboration and coordination withthe varios goverment authorities to ensure the programme is providing durable solutions. Forums will be organized where the SRCS other key stakeholders will share knowledge, experience and lessons learned during the life of the project. In the long run opportunities for joint initiatives to promote food security and nutrition towards hunger resilience will be identified and promoted for a collective approach. SRCS will stregthen the capacities of volunteers to ensure they are welled trained to support in the implementation of the project activities and the core humanitarian principles so as to ensure the communities are at the centre of the activities for ownershipand participation. All volunteers will be from the targetted communities to ensure continuity after the project and linkage with other actors in the locations to avoid duplication.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The main risks are:-i) Conflicts : Particularly in Mudug region there has been recurrent conflict in the region which forced many people to be displaced and joining to the IDPs camps creating proctection concern.(ii) Drought: The drought affected communities lost totaly their livestock which is their livelihood means and people started to migrate to bigger town created fragmented families needing protection.(iii)Poor Quality of Shelter: Is the security concern for the IDPs residents. Most of the IDP shelter are made of meterials such as plastics, sheets and cloths and are lack of doors which are easily torn and ripped increasing the vulnerability of women and girls and theft their properties. (iv) Lack of latrines: Most IDP residents don't access latrines and people go for open field deification which is risk for women and girls to be ripped in the outskits of IDP sites. (v) Far distance for water points: Most of the water points in the IDPs locate somehow distance places which sometimes create protection issues especially at night for the women and girls and children to fetch water from the water points. Family separation: Particularly the vulnerable families, the children as sent to the IDPs camps whilst the adults remain in the livestock during the droughts or travel to urban areas in search of work. Mitigation measures are: Through increased food production ,cash grants and fodder protection will support in protecting households assets and reduce the separation of displaced families. Fodder production will protect livestock especially the young claves and those breast feeding, hence ensuring continuity of milk production at household level. By rehabilitating existing water points the project will seek to reduce the distance women and girls travel to fetch water especially for household consumption. Cash grants will target the most vulnerable households to support them in addressing key protection issues such as enhancing their security where they live.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Older persons are often forgotten while women headed household are more vulnerable as they have to seek for livelihoods as well as attend to the children who are young to fed for themselves. Older persons headed household face the challenge of supporting themselves as well as those who are caring for them. the main risks they face is the distances they have to cover to access aid and the frequency of having to do so. Project terminologies will be contextualized and translated into the local language to ensure all beneficiaries understand the objectives of the project and are aware of their entitlements especially for the older persons. Different communication tools will be used such as posters, radio and pictograms to promote equitable and impartial access to all. Food distribution and rations will take into consideration gender and age of most of the beneficiaries. Rations provided will be considerate of gender, age and distance to be covered and security of the beneficiaries. The project will set up accessible confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms to encourage feedback with a view to improve the project. The feedback mechanism will also take into consideration age, gender and literacy levels. SRCS will ensure volunteers are grouped into different age groups, gender and clans represented in the community to ensure all age groups are considered and the most vulnerable groups. are targeted first.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Main impacts of armed conflict and violence are;-(1) Violation of Human rights: The above two regions experience recurrent conflicts based on mainly borders, range-land and water. During the conflicts the people are killed including the fighting parties, and civilians, the people are deliberately displaced, women are ripped and children are abducted. The project will seek to enhance communal assets to increase cooperation and rebuild trust among these communities. (2) Displacement: Host communities will be involved in identifying priorities for the IDPs to ensure the new arrivals do not stretch their current living conditions and they too are considered where possible in project activities. (3) Psychological impacts:The project will coordinate with other programmes offering these support to ensure they are addressed. SRCS health programme provides these service. (4) Sexual and Gender Based Violence: When there are conflicts the sexual and gender based violence spread and women and girls become susceptive to sexual violation.5- Physical destruction: The armed conflicts result in destruction of private and public properties, communication systems, food stocks, natural resources, water supplies, sanitation systems etc.6-Impacts on Economy: The huge amounts of money used to buy the weapons during the conflicts can support the livelihoods of millions of families. Hence, the programme will coordinate with other actors to advocate for more support to go towards rebuilding livelihoods and supporting sustainable development for communities affected by conflict.7- Impacts of Children and Women: During the conflicts the most displaced people are women and children and face particular hardship conditions:- Targeting will ensure women and children are prioritised.
