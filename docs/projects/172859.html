Project Summary

Operating in Libya remains challenging, with numerous security, logistical and access constraints. Strong and effective sector coordination mechanism is required to ensure humanitarian action is timely, effective & meets the needs of vulnerable people.
Collaboration with national/International Humanitarian actors and Human Rights organizations are critical to ensure that assistance is provided safely and respects people’s dignity. Functional sectors and inter sector coordination group support humanitarian actors with coordination, information management, needs assessments, advocacy for access, safety
and protection. It will benefit the entire humanitarian community to effectively and timely reach the most vulnerable people in Libya, as well as to sustain and extend their members’ operational reach and impact.

1 - Needs

The sector approach aims to address gaps and to increase the effectiveness of humanitarian response by building effective and efficient partnerships. It ensures that international responses to humanitarian emergencies are predictable, accountable and have clear leadership role ensuring a balanced division of labour among organizations, and their roles and responsibilities in different sectors. Sector approach in Libya has become overly process-driven and in order to enable timely delivery, efficiency and effectiveness in the process, sectors need to return to their original purpose and refocus on strategic and operational assessment, gaps analysis, planning, and results. Therefore, there is a incontrovertible need to strengthen their capacity and provide enough resources allowing them to fulfill their six core functions: support service delivery, inform the HC/HCT’s strategic decision-making, plan and implement sector strategies, monitor and evaluate performance, build national capacity in preparedness and contingency planning and support a robust advocacy.UNHCR chairs Protection, shelter and NFIs, Cash and market working groups in Libya and have limited financial resources for coordination activities. In order for UNHCR to fulfil its obligation as sector lead for Protection, Shelter/NFIs and Cash, there is a need to have dedicated sector coordinators who will coordinate and lead the respective sector. Similarly, an information management office (IMO) is required to support the three sectors. The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) provides information and analysis on the humanitarian needs, humanitarian consequences, critical needs and gaps in addition to impact of war on economy, public service, social system and more importantly on people and their lives. The Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA), and Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) significantly contribute to the HNO and therefore the need to undertaking these assessments regularly is extremely important. These assessments not only contribute in analysis of situation and needs but are considered useful source of information to guide the humanitarian intervention in Libya. not only for the is a need for undertaking these assessments to humanitarian intervention.

2 - Activities

The overall objective of the activities is to ensure humanitarian needs and protection concerns of people in-need are addressed in a comprehensive, professional, timely and well-coordinated fashion. The proposed activities aim to provide adequate resources to the three sectors, namely Protection, Shelter/NFIs and Cash, to enable them to fulfill their core functions and their role in the ISCG. In order to achieve that, UNHCR will ensure to have dedicated coordinator for Protection, Shelter/NFIs and Cash sectors as well as sufficient information management support to deliver in a timely and effective manner. Main activities under this project proposal include: - One Snr. Shelter/NFIs coordinator to lead SNFIs sector;- One Snr. Protection coordinator to lead Protection sector;- One coordinator for cash and market working group;- One information management officer for the above three sectors; - One Arabic translator/interpreter to assist the three sectors in the translation and interpretation ensuring active participation of NGOs and government institutions in the coordination activities; and- Undertaking multi sector needs assessment and joint market monitoring initiative assessments

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.1 (a) Ensure complaint mechanism and related SOP are in place and are working effectively and efficiently. (b) Identify other possible barriers to reporting (c) Increase awareness raising among people of concern on use of feedback and complaint mechanism. (d) Conduct NFI distribution exit interviews field monitoring visits movement restrictions permitting. (e) Ensure follow up on complaints/cases received. 4.3. (a) Promoting peaceful co-existence and social cohesion in the communities through implementation of quick impact projects. (b) Promoting communications with communities using different platforms. (c) advocacy with government for its proactive engagement in the delivery of aid to ensure assistance are delivered on the basis of needs and ensure that power dynamics doesn't exclude certain groups from assistance. (d) Ensure that sector interventions are undertaking in a safe, dignified and conflict sensitive approach taking in to account any risks identified during the needs assessment. 4.10. (a) Person of concern (women, men, boys and girls) will be actively engaged in the needs assessment, project design and implementation. (b) Different age, gender and groups will be engaged in the focus group discussion, identification of needs and how humanitarian assistance could equally be distributed to the most vulnerable groups in need of assistance. (c) Engage project beneficiaries in the post distribution monitoring, feedback mechanism and evaluation of projects. (d) Engaging project beneficiary in project monitoring mission, meetings where progress of projects are reviewed and discussed. (e) Proactively engage with communities through established communication with communities mechanism to ensure listening and reaching out to different age and gender groups. 4.14. (a) General awareness raising in the communities about humanitarian aid. (b) Awareness through posters, leaflets, group discussion to inform beneficiaries that humanitarian aid is free of charge and equally available to eligible persons. (c) Ensuring access to complaint and reporting mechanism through establishment of hotlines, call center, complaint box and dedicated email address. 4.23. (a) Engaging communities in the design of project and building capacity of communities to ensure sense of ownership and sustainability of the projects. (b) Liaising with line ministries to include people with need into the long-term development programmes. (c) Ensuring that people with specific needs are included in the social welfare system. (d) Liaising with developmental actors to include beneficiaries of the humanitarian projects into the long-term development project/programme. (d) Liaising with Government and none government development actors to included beneficiaries of humanitarian project into livelihoods and vocational projects ensuring self-reliance. (e) Ensure that existing coping mechanisms are carefully identified as part of the multi-sectoral participatory needs assessments and duly taken into account in the design and the delivery of protection and assistance services needed.(f) Strengthen existing coping mechanism through relevant capacity building measures such as training and the provision of whatever material support needed.(g) Promote access to public services, social protection mechanisms, vocational training and income generating activities/job opportunities. (h) Ensure that assistance is provided on the basis of the actual needs identified and in accordance with the applicable minimum standards in terms of fairness, equity and transparency. (i) Conduct post distribution and protection monitoring to measure the impact of humanitarian interventions on the protection and well-being of the assisted populations and their existing coping mechanisms, and take any corrective actions deemed appropriate to guide future interventions.
