Project Summary

The project is designed for promoting cohesion and recovery by reducing shelter vulnerabilities of residents, host communities, returnees, and IDPs for both insides of camps by restoring basic infrastructure to mitigate the risks of oods and outside of camps by building transitional sheltering units (TSUs) and rehabilitation of damaged houses.
The main achievement for the proposed interventions is to enhance the situation for the current shelter in order to improve the living conditions for those IDP’s who are living in the informal settlements, self-settled sites, and managed camps in order to avoid the negative
effect of the weather uctuations along the different seasons which might lead to ood and losing their shelters, in addition, to enhance the privacy for the targeted bene ciaries
to ensure their protection and safety as well. This achievement can be achieved by conducting the following activities:
a. Provision/Replacement for the worn tents.
b. Conduct Ground, internal and external insulations for the tents in the IDP’s sites. c. Construction of community infrastructures in camps (drainage, ground preparation/leveling of the site, etc.)
d. Provision for seasonal assistance such as stove, heating fuel, thermal blankets, and winter clothes either in-kind or as Cash vouchers restricted to be used to maintain the access for the service.
e. Provision for NFI kits which includes essential items to the IDP’s such as kitchen kits. f. Providing a transitional shelter unit solution (TSUs) that in a digni ed shelter would contribute to the elevation and promotion of the living conditions of IDPs. g. Rehabilitation/repair of houses/apartments.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, 4.2 million people in northwest Syria, some 2.7 million people are displaced, and some 2.8 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance. Sharp devaluation of the Syrian Pound in 2020 and an ongoing economic crisis are further eroding the resilience of both local and host communities. The impacts of these developments are entrenching existing and creating new humanitarian needs, increasing reliance on timely and principled humanitarian assistance, including to avoid the adoption of negative and harmful coping strategies. The large number of displacements is severely exacerbating shelter and accommodation shortages across northwest Syria. According to most recent data, the most urgent need and challenge for the newly displaced population is shelter. The majority of newly displaced families live out of camps and stay in the collective shelters or rented houses or with host families within the city. Many families are seeking shelter in un nished buildings, many of which have no doors or windows and lack the basic sanitary services. Thousands of families are reportedly temporarily accommodated in public buildings, schools and mosques, and relying on local charities to provide immediate assistance including cooked meals. According to ATAA need assessment, Hundreds of other families are reportedly sheltering in un nished houses, shops and other sub-standard buildings, as well as in open spaces including public parks, with no access to basic services. Overcrowding, lack of space and lack of segregated WASH facilities have also reportedly led to serious privacy and protection concerns for women and girls and resulted in a notable increase in forced early marriages among displaced girls as a coping

5 - Implementation of activities

Based on the type of the mentioned intervention activities (Provision/Replacement for the worn tents, infrastructures, Provision for seasonal assistance, Rehabilitation/repair of houses/apartments, construction transitional sheltering units TSUs) The deep needs evaluation will be started by conducting a Community Level Assessment for those communities and IDP’s Sites which are located in the targeted area using the information in the RNA which have been conducted earlier by ATAA in order to identify the gaps and needs for the response taking into consideration the dynamic changes in the humanitarian context with keeping the flexibility of tuning the intervention according to the need. While the project will be targeting the most vulnerable people in both settings In-Camp and also Off-Camp the project once be approved will be started by coordination with the SNFI Cluster in order to avoid any potential overlap with the other active humanitarian actors. The guidelines which are developed by the clusters and the SoP’s for the related programs which are developed by ATAA will be taken into considerations during the design of the activities. The most vulnerable communities and IDP’s sites will be de ned using the vulnerability criteria which is recommended by the clusters along with the developed selection criteria by ATAA. After finalizing the identification for the targeted communities/IDP’s sites, a technical evaluation will be conducted for the targeted areas in order to prepare the BoQ’s taking into consideration the PSEA, GBV, AAP, Transparency, and other cross-cutting issues in order to ensure the community engagement especially for the Women, PwD’s, Children, and elderly benefeciaries

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

This project was designed in a manner that enables all gender and age groups, in addition to PwD to access the project services. ATAA will reflect the age and gender of Project beneficiaries in the beneficiary lists. A referral system will be in place in coordination with Protection Cluster to refer any GBV case and provide needed support. Moreover, a feedback and complaint mechanism (complaint boxes, WA number, email) will be established and shared with all Project beneficiaries to ensure ATAA can receive any Project-related complaint, including PSEA and CSG related complaints. ATAA will train all project staff on its protection policy, including the child safeguarding policy and the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse policy, and when ATAA will conduct the training to the camp managers, it will include PSEA training and CSG. ATAA also will raise the awareness about PSEA, in coordination with PSEA Network as Ataa is an active member in PSEA Network, led by IOM. - ATAA will mainstream PSEA policy including training of ATAA`s personnel in addition to signing ATAA protection policy and COC by all of ATAA personnel, volunteers, partners, suppliers, services providers, and all relevant persons. - Activating the complaint mechanism including protection incidents and penalty procedures. - ATAA will assign a PSEA focal point to ensure the implementation of all protection principals, prevention any abuse, and to conduct the protection training and awareness-raising sessions related PSEA and CSG topics, with coordination and supervising of ATAA Protection Program.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks and Mitigations: 1- Overlapping, losing some locations due to other NGOs intervention, loss of access. - will perform multilevel coordination (Cluster, Field) - coordinated with all the stakeholder during the design phase 2- Risks related to HLP and propriety conflicts - Ensure validation of the documents during the project - communicate clearly with all the stakeholders - Involved the camps management committees,LCs,KIs in the process in case of any conflict 3- Because of the conditions on the ground that may force some beneficiaries to displace. - ATAA will be monitoring all updates on the ground and if we noticed that any of the beneficiaries displaced we will change the selected beneficiaries and choose other beneficiaries who meet the criteria in the same location. 4: Increased number of cases infected with COVID - Training ATAA teams about best practices including social distance, sterilization and wearing face masks. - Providing teams with PPE kits (gloves, sterilization and face masks). - Printing awareness posters on food baskets about COVID-19 and best healthy practices to avoid it. - ATAA distribution teams will organize beneficiaries and make social distance between them during commodities distribution. 5- Sexual exploitation and abuse of affected community members by anyone associated with the provision of aid. This risk will be mitigated by strict measures and the awareness of the field staff on this topic and by signing codes of conduct of all staff relating to sexual exploitation and abuse, and reporting via established reporting mechanisms of any concerns or suspicions regarding sexual exploitation and abuse, and investigation if necessary (hotline).
