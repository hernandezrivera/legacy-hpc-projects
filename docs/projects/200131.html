Project Summary

As part of the intervention, RI will improve nutrition outcomes and practices towards breastfeeding, infant and young child feeding (IYCF), hygiene practices,
optimal nutrition for pregnant and lactating women (PLW), children under two (CU2), and adolescents through skilled IYCF counseling with lactation specialists, mother to mother support groups, raising awareness and education though c,ommunity health workers’ (CHWs) outreach activities, and malnutrition screening in the field and at the supported PHCCs.
These activities will be implemented across Bekaa, Beirut, Mt. Lebanon, South (Saida) and North for vulnerable Lebanese, Palestinian refugees in Lebanon (PRL), and migrant communities.
Prolonged, economic deterioration, social and political tensions, and major food
shortages continue to undermine food security and access to basic needs and services, limiting access to nutritional diets and knowledge of nutrition practices. Households
across the country have been pushed into severe economic difficulties, are unable to seek or obtain the critical nutrition services required, have limited access to necessary nourishable foods, are limited in movement due to major fuel shortages, and have limited employment prospects. Such circumstances mean households are prioritizing other needs and children and PLWare placed at increased risk.
RI will continue to coordinate with other nutrition actors (i.e. IOCC, Mercy USA) to ensure a strong referral mechanism for malnourished cases. This will be done across all areas of intervention and will include referral and follow up on malnourished cases identified through screening at the community of primary health care centers (PHCCs) to the malnutrition treatment centers. Moreover, RI will support four malnutrition treatment centers in North by providing monthly incentives to healthcare providers involved in malnutrition treatment and covering consultation fees and transportation fees for patients identified as malnourished.
Aligned to the nutrition sector strategic objectives, RI will contribute to addressing the
growing humanitarian needs, by targeting 38,211 individuals, made up of children, adolescents, and PLW

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Activity 1.1: RI will recruit five lactation specialists (2 in Beirut / Mount Lebanon (BML), 2 in North and Akkar, and 1 in Bekaa) to provide skilled IYCF counselling, awareness raising, and referral services on optimal maternal nutrition and IYCF for PLW and caregivers of children aged below 23 months.Activity 1.2: RI’s Community Health Workers (CHWs) will be trained to provide awareness sessions on proper nutrition and IYCF in BML, South, North and Akkar, and Bekaa. Activity 2.1: RI will enhance the community outreach component as part of the package of services offered by RI’s supported PHCCs through school health and nutrition activities. Activity 3.1: RI will establish mother to mother support groups and provide individual counseling to PLWs through Lactation Specialists at PHCC and community level; Activity 3.2: RI’s CHWs will screen children between 6 and 59 months and PLW for acute malnutrition by measuring the MUAC and refer individuals at risk to malnutrition treatment centers in BML, South, Bekaa, North and Akkar, and will follow up on referred cases to ensure they are able to access adequate services; Activity 3.3: RI will strengthen screening for malnutrition at PHCC level by training the triage Nurses and Midwives at the PHCCs on anthropometric measurements and growth monitoring of children between 6 and 59 months old visiting the PHCCs and PLWs. RI will follow up and monitor the screening in these PHCCs; Activity 3.3: RI will strengthen screening.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Protecting participants from harm will be the center of RI’s interventions. RI will offer services to eligible groups regardless of their nationality and gender following clear selection criteria and outreach components to prevent exclusion of any vulnerable group from obtaining assistance. The feedback, complaints, and response mechanism (FCRM) will ensure and reinforce protection during the proposed activities as it will be emphasized to the targeted communities for reporting any complaints, including safeguarding concerns. Moreover, staff will be hired taking into consideration gender balance and will be trained on gender-based violence (GBV) safeguarding, and accountability to affected population (AAP). Such training will not be limited to only RI staff and will also be provided to PHCCs considered under this proposed intervention.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

RI adheres to the Interagency Standing Committee’s (IASC) five commitments to AAP focusing on 1) Participation and Inclusion through CHWs selected from the targeted communities, outreach activities, surveys while focusing on regular 2) Information sharing and Transparency in disseminating relevant and timely information on its activities at PHCC and community level along with available services at RI and other agencies level through its referral's mechanism. The 3) FCRM is set with clear pathways relevant to complaints and feedback received through accountability channels, collected/secured by accountability team and shared on a need-to-know basis in relevance to feedback, complaint sensitivity levels. Findings will serve in 4) Program Adaptation through data collected under FCRM and MEAL activities reflecting participants' feedback on services provided and shared with the program team during regular meetings and lessons learned events in order address participants' needs.
