Project Summary

Women with disabilities often face additional, severe disadvantage due to discriminatory norms and perceptions of their low value and capacity. They are at more risk of abuse and have little access to rehabilitation. Persons with disabilities face attitudinal and physical barriers and are often excluded and not consulted. Lack of experience of field staff to interact with people with disability further exacerbates their failure to access essential information. Older persons face restrictions to participation (like language or literary barriers) that prevent them from accessing information services. Gender Equality and Social Inclusion will be a significant cross cutting issue, ensuring marginalised groups (including women, girls, older persons, people with disabilities and LGBTIQ+) actively participate in the Activity. The project will incorporate strategies to address gender equality and social inclusion issues as follows:
• Particular attention paid to ensuring meaningful participation and input from particularly vulnerable women and girls, e.g. providing girls and women with disabilities with reasonable accommodation, assistive devices, and other means to access activities, equally contributing to participation processes.
• Data disaggregated by gender, age, and disability to monitor participation of marginalised groups.
• CBM-CDD will proactively capture systematic feedback of beneficiaries with disabilities and set strategies to remove barriers accordingly.
• Female rehabilitative service providers and volunteers from the host community employed who speak the same language.
• All staff receive refresher training on CBM/CDD gender and safeguarding/child protection policies and codes of conduct.
The project will create access to rehabilitation in primary health care. While there are now multiple health service providers operating in the camps and host communities, rehabilitation service provision for people with different medical conditions, including various Neurologic (e.g., stroke, spinal cord injuries), Orthopedic (Osteoarthritis, Cervical Spondylitis), and Pediatrics Conditions (Cerebral Palsy) is limited to the availability of a few organizations only representing a significant gap. The provision of accessible Home Based Rehabilitative (HBR) services will improve the health outcome & quality of life of people with disabilities in the camps and host communities. The proposed rehabilitation services include Physiotherapy, Occupational Therapy, Speech & Language Therapy, Eye Screening, Hearing Screening, provision of assistive devices (including orthosis and prostheses,) & home adaptation. The project will make strong links with other (primary) health care providers to create a strong referral system for those patients who need primary health care services.
CBM with local partner the Centre for Disability in Development (CDD), will implement the programme based in camp 15, camp 16, and mobilize to other unreached camps upon request & agreement by mainstream health actors. In the host community, CBM & CDD will implement the project in the Jaliapalong Union. The project will be gender inclusive and will cover all age range people while ensuring added focus on women with disabilities.
Recognizing the significant gap in rehabilitation services that exits in the camps & host communities, the project will advocate for integrating rehabilitation components within the existing healthcare system and share example of good practices on Health & Rehabilitation. Among the community peoples, persons with disabilities specially women with disabilities are more vulnerable and deprived from services. By considering this issue, CDD will form Rehab team by equal number of male & female staff.
Considering frequent natural disasters, the project also includes a 5% contingency budget based on a risk compliance contingency plan followed by the CBM crisis modifier approach align with Mobile Medical Team (MMT) during emergencies.

1 - Identified Needs

The service mapping conducted by the Age and Disability Working Group (ADWG) has identified that rehabilitation services remain important gaps in several camps of the Cox’s Bazar, and that these services continue to be provided by specialized agencies and not integrated into the activities of mainstream health actors. It is estimated that more than 50% of the Rohingya population in need of rehabilitation are not reached by these services. The World Health Organization’s rapid Assistive Technology Assessment (rATA) was conducted by CBM and REACH in May 2021. Results of the rATA indicate significant need for assistive devices still exist in the camp. Overall 1% of individuals reportedly have their needs met in terms of assistive products, with 51% of individuals having unmet needs. In the host community, while there are no rehabilitation services available in from the government or mainstream health actors, CBM-CDD, HI, HAI and SARPV provide rehabilitation services for people in need in certain areas of Ukhia and Teknaf. Rehabilitation is an essential component of multidisciplinary health care for preventing complications related to other health conditions, improving function following an injury, and to optimize the impact of other health interventions. Significant gaps in early rehabilitation in the camp and host community is putting the Rohingya and host community populations at risk of developing further complications and negatively affecting their longer-term health prognosis. Gaps in access to rehabilitation services have led to a scenario where patients have not received timely following up following hospital admission or medical consultation. In areas where rehabilitation services are delayed or not existent, secondary complications such as contractures, shoulder subluxation and pressure sores have arisen thereby having a permanent impact on the individual’s health and functional mobility and increasing rate of re hospitalization. Based on CBM-CDD analysis of client case load in past & current areas of operations, the top three major neurological conditions for which gaps in rehabilitation exist are Stroke, Guillain-Barre Syndrome and Traumatic Brain Injury and for musculoskeletal conditions are Prolapsed Intervertebral Disc, Osteo and Rheumatoid Arthritis and Upper and Lower Limb Amputations. Timely rehabilitation and provision of assistive devices is essential for improving muscle strength and range of motion for effective ambulation, transfers and participation in activities of daily living. Rehabilitation is also essential for pain management and positioning, while caregiver training can help to reduce burden on caregivers. As the majority of caregivers tend to be women, gaps in rehabilitation increase the burden on caregivers and further limit the opportunities for women to participate in social and economic life. Lack of rehabilitation and assistive devices in the camp and host community continues to present barriers for persons with disabilities to access humanitarian services including other health services, as well as education and employment, thereby continuing the cycle of poverty and exclusion. According to the REACH research report on "Age & Disability Inclusion Need Assessment," 12% of the population of camp has some form of disability, with 2% of the population aged 2-4 years, 3% of the population aged 5-17 years, and 20% of the population aged 18-99 years having some form of disability. Certain barriers were increasingly reported with increasing age, often appearing to particularly affect women older persons & women with disabilities.
