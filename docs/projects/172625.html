Project Summary

The project will provide life-saving interventions for the vulnerable IDP, returnee, and resident households in Shattaya, Kass, Al-salam, Katela and Merching localities in South Darfur state to prevent further losses of their productive assets and enhance their capacity to access, through agricultural based livelihood interventions, sufficient nutritious food and to diversify their food basket and improve their income

1 - Needs

As far as South Darfur State is considered as area recovering from conflict and displacement PODR seeks to implement according to the Humanitarian-Development-Peace (HDP) nexus, which can help to reduce conflict, meet humanitarian needs, promote IDP return and reintegration, and transition communities for longer term recovery and development. Much FSL programming in Sudan is in post-conflict or apprehensive environment and actively contributes to peacebuilding outcomes, where land and water resources are scarce, such conflict-sensitive programming helps avoid future conflict over natural resources.As Stated in the HNO, Food insecurity remains alarmingly high in Sudan with increased and protracted displacement, economic decline and inflation, and high food price hikes exacerbated by the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic and the high inflation rates. The lockdown measures to prevent the spread of COVID-19 significantly decreased commodity movement, market function and cross-border trade, and compromised livelihoods and daily labor opportunities, reducing household purchasing power and food access of the vulnerable population.Based on the PROJECTION UPDATE FOR OCTOBER - DECEMBER 2020 PERIOD, Nittega locality in South Darfur as one of the 11 localities in Sudan that shifted from from Stressed (IPC Phase 2) to Crisis (IPC Phase 3) while Kass, Shattaya, Alsalm an Katela localities are in stress IPC Phase 2, Its noteworthy that Katela, Alsalam and Shattaya localities have the highest % (above 50%)of population who are in Phase2. Although Kass locality has only 25%in Phase two and 5% in Phase3 but this %s are higher when considering the number of population Kass which highly populated locality,The IPC analysis recommended that Scaling up diversified livelihood programs for improved self-reliance, resilience building and social protection for all vulnerable communities classified in IPC Phases 2, 3 and 4, with special support for reconstructing and restoring livelihood assets. the following priority have identified by PODR:• Essential inputs (seeds, tools, ploughs, animal concentrate feed, mineral licks, veterinary medical supplies and milking goats) • Summer season crop p of cereals and nutritious crops (grains, legumes and vegetables)• Supplementary feed and female productive goats • De-worming and basic veterinary protection;

2 - Activities/Outputs

•Procure and distribute essential inputs (seeds, tools, ploughs, animal concentrate feed, mineral licks, veterinary medical supplies and milking goats) to restore and improve small scale agro-pastoral production;•Enhance summer season crop production of cereals and nutritious crops (grains, legumes and vegetables which provide nutritious food for humans as well as fodder for livestock)•Increase goat milk production through provision of milking supplementary feed, female productive goats and training on improved husbandry practices to households who suffer from Malnutrition;•Foster systematic de-worming and basic veterinary protection of milking animals;•Provide training to farmers and herders to enable them to optimize the use of these inputs;•Provide training to the members of the Village Livestock Committees and Village Agricultural Committees to consolidate their roles and responsibilities in managing crop, livestock and natural resources;•Monitor the project activities at output and outcome levels;

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The Food insecurity and humanitarian situation in Sudan has deteriorated rapidly in the first half of the year 2020. The devastating combination of recurrent climate shocks, conflict, economic downturn, disease outbreaks, exacerbated by the consequences of the COVID-19 pandemic and floods, has driven rising food insecurity levels across the country. In addition, the lockdown measures to prevent the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic significantly decreased commodity movement, market function and cross-border trade, and compromised livelihoods, daily labour opportunities, reducing household purchasing power and food access of the vulnerable population.The project aim to avoid, minimize or reduce any unintended negative consequences or impact of their assistance or interventions and are committed to a ‘do no harm’ approach to programming
