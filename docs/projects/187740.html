Project Summary

The targeted proposed areas face enormous humanitarian challenges due to protracted crisis in Syria spanning over more than ten years, profound gender and social inequities, underdeveloped livelihood systems and shattered social and physical infrastructure. child morbidity and mortality rates are very high in the targeted selected areas of Hassakeh, Deir Ez Zor and Ar Raqqa provinces, resulting from low access to health care services, poor service-quality and inappropriate and delayed health care-seeking behaviors due to low recognition of illness-related danger-signs and failure to seek pre-emptive, timely treatment.
Thus, the ultimate aim of this project proposal of health and care program is a sustained decrease in common prevalent diseases incidence, waterborne disease outbreaks, new-born and child morbidity and mortality in programme beneficiaries.
The objective of this project is to contribute to improving and strengthening emergency health response through comprehensive community based health intervention with a focus on improving health of children under five years of age including new born babies through provision of treatment for common childhood diseases which can save many young lives., in addition to enabling targeted communities and individuals to better increase control over their health, providing information, mobilizing and supporting for the adoption of illness-prevention behaviors children under five, as well as encouraging optimal utilization of available primary health care services are important in making sustained changes in the challenging context of intervention areas. treatment for common childhood diseases can save many young lives.
Project area: This project will carry out through:
a. A health pediatric clinic in Hassakeh city to provide health services to the most needy displaced children U5 living in Hassakeh city and its rural areas.
b. A health pediatric clinic in Deir Ez Zor city to provide health services to the most needy displaced children U5 living in Deir Ez Zor city and its rural areas.
c. A health pediatric clinic in Sabkha town to provide health services to the most needy displaced children U5 living in Sabkha town and its rural areas.
Beneficiary criteria and number:
The project beneficiaries are crisis affected children under 5 years of age, pregnant and lactating mothers, new born and adolescent girls.
These activities will target 15,400 children U5 in three governorates, of which 6,000 children in Hassakeh, 6,000 children in Deir Ez Zor and 3,400 children in Al Sabkha and its rural areas.

4 - Needs

A recent study conducted by Al Bir Wa Ehsan Association assessment teams in the targeted areas in Hassakeh, Deir Ez Zor and Ar Raqqa revealed that the main challenges in providing health services to crisis affected population are: a lack of awareness; a lack of access to facilities; lack of human resources capacity; and poor quality of services. In addition, the special attention needs to be given to the needs of children under five years of age including new born. Health facilities are underutilized mainly due to unavailability of health personnel, poor quality of care. These conditions are exacerbated by lack of female health care providers in basic health facilities, which has a serious and negative bearing on women’s health care, especially their reproductive health needs. The assessment revealed that there is au urgent needs from health sector actions to prevent and treat the infectious diseases of early childhood in particular children under five, and there is a need to work together to prevent premature mortality in children under five.As of the assessment that it is recommended to target 15,400 children U5 in three governorate who are the most in need to benefit from the urgent health services. Bearing in mind that this proposed figure represents 27% of the children U5.It is proposed that the plan of program intervention to be taken place during 2022 targeting 15,400 children in three governorates and also to be carried out in 2023 targeting 15,000 children.

5 - Implementation of activities

In order to understand the implementation strategies we have to take into account different hurdles that have halted the provision of health care. It is vital to understand the fact that these packages are central for low-income areas of intervention where child health indicators are still not up to standard. Some of the known reasons for the poor health indicators in these areas include low levels of education, poor awareness regarding health care services, lack of infrastructure to support health care, deprived sanitation and hygienic practices, poverty, dearth of access to communities as a result of protected crisis and poor acceptance of health care services as a result of cultural and social practices and beliefs. The implementation strategies thus have to tackle with these issues in order to successfully provide the health care package and improve childcare outcomes. We have devised the following strategies that can be useful in such scenarios: Identification of needs, setting up the infrastructure, provision of essential health care to child U5 including childbirth, empowering the targeted community through education and awareness and monitoring and evaluation.The estimation cost of the activities of this project proposal has been $499,176 to be implemented in 2022 and around $500,000 is the activities cost that to be implemented in 2023.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

BICA is committed to mainstreaming the protection considerations at all stages of the project cycle. The project is deigned to make the concerns and experiences of women, girls, boys and men an integral dimension of it. BICA field team will select community volunteers from both genders and rely on their feedback and inputs throughout the implementation period. Men and women will also be separately consulted with regards to the day-to-day logistical considerations, such as regarding the most appropriate locations for gender-segregated distributions points. As a result, women and men have an equal the right and opportunity to participate in decision-making. The risks of PSEA will be identified through publicly discuss and address ongoing violence where men and women will be given a chance to express their different concerns, experiences, needs and capabilities through gender-age including persons with disabilities and survivors of GBV as identified. The assessment phase will highlight different gender- age specific gaps, such as unequal access assistance and services for men and women, in addition to likely protection risks for women, boys and girls. And as prevention measures that: 1. Employees sign code of conduct related to sexual exploitation. 2. Training of employees on procedures related to sexual and material exploitation. 3. Posters that include the complaints numbers. 4. Assigning a specialized team to follow up and verify complaints in order to respond immediately.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security risk: the security is unpredictable although the areas of intervention is enjoying calm security situation, Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: BICA closely monitors the changes in the overall security situation in targeted areas and adjust accordingly its operations, adapting its protocols, systems and contingency plans, to minimize safety and security risks for its personnel and beneficiaries. Fraud risk: Aid diversion, fraud, and corruption. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: diversification of supplier, triangulation of information, standard monitoring procedures in place, security plan and risks assessments tables updated and followed. Financial risk: Substantive devaluation of USD against the Syrian pound. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: The eventuality of significant changes in the value of the Syrian currency will be closely monitored and, if materialized, timely communicated with OCHA office in Qamishli and options discussed. COVID 19 risk: Vulnerable households including persons with disabilities are mostly affected and in need of support. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: Support the beneficiaries to implement the health protocol and the preventive measures against the challenges posed by COVID-19 and to adhere to wearing masks.
