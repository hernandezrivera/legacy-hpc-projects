Needs

Since 1991, the security situation in Somalia has remained volatile through armed conflicts occasioned by clan hostilities and lack of functional government. The emergence of terror groups has worsened the already fragile operating environment of the NGO community. Somalia remains among the most dangerous countries in the world for aid workers. For the last three decades, the country has consistently remained among the top five in absolute number of critical incidents (killings, kidnappings and severe injuries) and, in aggregate in the last two decades, it is the number one in ratio of incidents per number of aid workers deployed. In order to improve the safety of their staff, NGOs need to enhance their threats awareness, vulnerabilities awareness, context information and understanding, risk mitigation measures, impact mitigation measures, capacity for crisis management, individual safety and organizational safety. The safety of the NGO staff is fundamental to ensure that they efficiently and effectively deliver on their mandate and remains accountable to the vulnerable women, girls, boys and men in Somalia. Timely information sharing on security incidents, threats and risks increases awareness on safety actions that can be taken by the staff of the NGOs operating in areas affected by hostilities

Activities

1. Information sharing and analysis INSO provides a number of services to inform on and analyze the changing context and the threats involved through; a. Reports (flash reports, weekly incident list, biweeklies, quarterlies, advisories, special reports, area briefs): These unique reports compile the key security incidents of a given period for the whole country, analyze the data and provide critical advise to NGOs. b. Maps (areas of influence, access, geographic, town maps) : They are highly valued by NGOs, the areas of influence maps help understand which armed actors are present in a given area. Similarly, INSO access maps are a reference for the whole humanitarian community to synthesize the difficulties in accessing an area, staying there and delivering. c. Briefings (biweekly and ad hoc): The biweekly security briefings to NGOs provide a forum to openly discuss about the key events and security threats in all the regions. However, the ad hoc briefings, upon request of NGOs, help in the planning of operations, understand the threats specific to the location and profile of the agency, and tailor their security management to their needs. 2. Training: a. The Hostile Environment Individual Safety Training (HEIST) provides the trainees aid workers to be deployed in Somalia the tools to deal with specific situations that they may confront in the field. b. The Country Directors Training improves the security management of the country Director's organization, tailored to the Somali context.3. Support to Crisis Management In case of critical incident (e.g. abduction, medical evaluation) INSO supports the NGOs on the management of the crisis, especially on the first 72 hours by setting up the crisis team, acting as expert adviser, helping in data gathering and connecting with critical stakeholders.
