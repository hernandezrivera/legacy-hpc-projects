Project Summary

UNHCR’s CCCM activities are aimed at enhancing the protection and response of displaced population through camp coordination and camp management activities. UNHCR will target 500,000 including displaced IDPs, vulnerable host community and returnees living in informal and self-settled sites across 21 districts in Somalia which includes: Kismayu, Afmadow, Dhobley Luuq, Belethawa, Baidoa, Diinsor, Kahda, Daynile, Beletweyn, Jowhar Dhusamareb, Guri’el, Adado, Abudwak, Galkayo, Garowe, Gardo, Bossaso, Hargeisa and Berbera.
According to Somalia’s Humanitarian Needs Overview, conflicts, recurrent climate shocks, disease outbreaks and increasing poverty are devastating largely the already poor urban and other host community members and also internally displaced populations. Despite progress made in recent years, the impacts of these shocks continue to affect the living conditions of some 2.2 million displaced people who are already in sub-standard living conditions as they continue to reside in informal settlements or camp-like settings.
As per the detailed site assessment findings (DSA) and site verification assessments conducted by the CCCM cluster, more than 80% of sites are informal settlements established on private land with the majority of sites located in urban areas. The lack of access to land coupled with a weak land tenure system, multiplicity of land ownership, lack of adequate land disputes systems, weak judicial systems to seek redress, and a lack of proper national land policy further exacerbates the vulnerability of IDPs. This has led to frequent incidents of forceful evictions pushing IDPs into worsened situations requiring both emergency and prolonged assistance. IDP returns are extremely limited as many displaced families have lost livelihoods and are reliant on humanitarian services.
In addition, (REACH, JMCNA) indicate 11% of all IDPs in Somalia are classified as currently at risk of being evicted from their shelters due to lack of land tenure security therefore UNHCR through inter-cluster efforts of both the Protection and the CCCM clusters will continue to advocate on prevention of evictions and to ensure that displaced populations have access to a dignified relocation where necessary.
The living conditions in IDP sites across Somalia are challenging due to lack of sufficient services that meet minimum standards and according to joint multisectoral assessment conducted indicate that majority of IDPs cannot receive humanitarian service because of existing service gaps.
Aligned to the Centrality of Protection strategy, UNHCR will ensure that gender empowerment and meaningful inclusion for minority groups, people with disabilities, age and gender is mainstreamed across all its activities. UNHCR will work closely with leaders from minority and marginalized groups to ensure fair and equal representation in the governance structures at the IDP sites. The project will also ensure women, girls, youths and elderly persons are well represented and are taking part in decision-making process within IDP sites.
Through enhanced Risk and Community Engagement strategies such as Communication with Communities, UNHCR co-lead CCCM will ensure PoCs have access to information about different humanitarian services available within the site hence closing the gap between the community and service providers. UNHCR will ensure that IDP communities have access to reliable and updated COVID-19 information through applying MoH/WHO RCCE contents alongside ensuring maintenance of other CCCM mechanism including Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), service monitoring, community participation through camp committees, safety audits and site improvement and maintenance activities among others

UNHCR interventions in 2022 will continue to be guided by the AGD approach and the UNHCR AGD Policy (2018),, aimed at promoting gender equality and inclusion of vulnerable groups in the response and which focuses on six areas of engagement that comprise the framework for achieving accountability to persons of concern. All UNHCR interventions will be vulnerability based and participatory in nature - ensuring that all the different AGD groups are included in the response. For the purposes of analysis and programming, to the extent possible, the data collected by UNHCR will be disaggregated by age and gender and by other diversity considerations. Available data on protection risks and capacities will also be shared with relevant stakeholders at the interagency level to inform advocacy efforts and responses tailored to the needs identified. UNHCR has established hotline phone numbers designed to receive complaints and feedback from persons of concern on a range of protection issues. In addition, UNHCR implementing partners also maintained their own internal complaint mechanisms, which are designed to receive complaints and feedback, this will be continued in 2022. UNHCR and partners will continue to regularly share with the concerned communities the details of those existing mechanisms making sure the information reaches all AGD groups in the community. UNHCR will continue to ensure that all programmes implemented for PoCs are designed taking into consideration the principles of AGD, promote protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. That will also be achieved through continuous organizational learning, capacity building, adaptation of strategies and approaches to program implementation. AGD participatory assessments will annually be conducted by all UNHCR Sub Offices in Somalia with all age-groups, gender and diversity. The findings of these participatory assessments will feed into UNHCR Multi-Year programming for 2023 and beyond, and it will enable both UNHCR and its partners review 2022 Programming, identify the gaps and use a multi-sectoral approach to further improve the lives, protection and assistance in terms of access to protection and basic essentials like Health, Education, shelter and livelihood support.
