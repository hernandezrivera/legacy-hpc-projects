Project Summary

The overall objective of Mine Action Area of Responsibility (AoR) coordination is to facilitate information sharing and coordination in support of the designated Libyan authorities and communities that are impacted by explosive hazards. The aim of the Mine Action Sub-Working Group (MASWG) is to help reduce, and if possible, eliminate the threats posed by the use and presence of explosive hazards to civilians, including internally displaced persons, returnees, migrants, refugees, humanitarian workers, and other relevant government and stabilization partners, through Mine Action activities.

1 - Needs

Following the 2011 revolution, Libya was reported to possess the largest unsecured stockpiles of arms and ammunition in the world. The conﬂict resulted in uncontrolled access to damaged and destroyed weapons and Ammunition Storage Areas (ASAs). Since the revolution Libya has experienced several outbreaks of heavy ﬁghting in populated areas, leaving many areas contaminated with explosive hazards, including unexploded and abandoned ordnance (UXO/AXO), landmines, booby traps and improvised explosive devices (IEDs). The threat from explosive hazards was further exacerbated by the outbreak of hostilities in April 2019 which was centered on, but not limited to, the capital Tripoli. As all parties to the conﬂict made use of explosive and heavy weaponry in densely populated areas – often in a disproportionate and indiscriminate manner – UXO and other explosive hazards have injured, maimed, an killed hundreds of civilians, including children.IEDs continue to pose a threat across the country, driven by the availability of explosives derived from high explosive bombs, missile warheads or artillery shells. Vast numbers of booby-traps, mines and IEDs were encountered following the Libyan National Army’s withdrawal from southern Tripoli. These explosive hazards present an unprecedented threat to returning IDPs and Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) operators alike, as highlighted by the high number of casualties – over 200 since May 2020. Accordingly, in the 2020 MSNA, 44 percent of returnee HH in Tripoli reported Explosive Hazards in their neighborhood, compared to nine percent of non-displaced. Explosive Hazards are also named as the top-ranking security concern among returnees in Tripoli and neighboring Al Jfara.Explosive hazards are an overarching protection concern, posing a threat to civilians in terms of safety, impeding mobility, and blocking access to livelihoods, essential services and infrastructure, including to homes, roads, schools, and health facilities. According to the 2020 MSNA, households (HH) reported Explosive Hazard contamination in their neighborhood in 17 out of 22 Mantikas, with Tripoli, Benghazi, Sirte and Misrata ranking the highest. Until these hazards are systematically cleared they will continue to pose an immediate threat to the life of anyone in their surroundings in terms of safety, mobility, and access to services and livelihoods. It hinders the safe return of IDPs, restricts access for humanitarian workers and government institutions, and complicates early recovery processes. An estimated 505,000 people are in need of mine action assistance as a result of widespread contamination from explosive hazards due to the protracted conﬂict, particularly in urban areas. Low awareness among the general population, coupled with the frequently concealed nature of contamination, and underreporting, often result in an underestimation of the actual scope of the problem.

2 - Activities

Mine Action refers to the full range of activities that aim to reduce the impact of explosive hazards, that threaten the lives of civilians. The Mine Action comprises ﬁve complementary groups of activities: a) Risk education (RE); b) Survey and clearance of explosive hazards, i.e. survey, mapping, marking and clearance of mines, ERW, and IEDs; c) Victim assistance, the provision of specialized services, including physical rehabilitation, mental health and psycho-social support, and reintegration, for persons with disabilities including survivors of explosive hazards; d) Stockpile destruction; and, e) Advocacy for the full implementation and universalization of international frameworks and instruments. A number of other enabling activities are required to support these ﬁve components of mine action, including: national capacity building, information management, human skills development and management training, quality management and the application of effective and appropriate safety standards. Not all the elements and components of mine action, as deﬁned in the previous paragraph, may be applicable at all times in Libya. The Mine Action Sub-Working Group (MASWG) is the coordination forum for the Protection Sector’s response to explosive hazards in Libya. The main purposes of the MASWG are to: a) Assist in facilitating a coordinated, coherent, and effective mine action response and reporting that contributes to the achievement of the objectives of the Protection Sector, the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), and OCHA in the context of humanitarian crises; b) Share information to advance the rights of individuals and affected populations within Libya and inform strategic decision making.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

NA, direct beneficiaries of this project are the member organization of the MASWG. Indirect beneficiaries are Libyan and non-Libyan civilians in Libya. Accountability to affected populations will be integrated during project design, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation. Face-to-face Risk Education activities, by deﬁnition, require close personal interaction with the affected population. Information and preferences communicated by local residents, both men and women, will be taken into account when prioritizing and targeting areas for clearance. Clearance will be determined and carried out in close consultation with local authorities, local communities and the LibMAC. Emergency hotline numbers, that are being operated by MASWG members or it’s partners, including the LibMAC, will be communicated to local populations. The emergency numbers serve to receive reports about explosive hazards, as well as to receive feedback about the services delivered by the respective operator. Quality Management visits will be carried out by the LibMAC and supported by UNMAS Libya to ensure that all interventions comply with International Mine Action Standards and the Gender guidelines for Mine Action Programmes and are in line with PSEA policies and the United Nations Code of Conduct.
