Project Summary

Recognizing that the southwest region of Cameroon continues to have hundreds of thousands of individuals affected by crisis and in need of humanitarian assistance, DRC is proposing a multi-sector humanitarian response to meet the most urgent needs of affected populations. At present, responses are largely ad hoc and insufficient to provide a comprehensive response for immediate needs. Through WASH, emergency shelter and NFI support, vulnerabilities will be mitigated in the immediate term for those permanently or chronically displaced by conflict in highly vulnerable, hard to reach areas. The proposed action aims to address some of the most critical gaps in assistance through the provision of cluster standard shelter, NFI and WASH assistance to 4,200 households.
The crisis is threatening to produce a lost generation of children who have been highly exposed to violence and conflict, without any form of education, and are caught in an environment where community and family structure degradation is pervasive, leading to high needs for psychosocial support. DRC will expand on the demonstrated success of its current child protection programming to continue to meet the social and emotional needs of children affected by conflict through community-based child protection programming, and individual child protection assistance. This will produce an improved protection environment for over 3,000 children in the immediate and medium term.
Case management remains a critical response gap across the northwest and southwest regions of Cameroon reducing the capacity for successful referrals of child protection cases. DRC will integrate dedicated case management capacity into its child protection programming to ensure follow-up capacity on cases referred for further protection support. In addition, DRC will train other responding organizations to provide case management within their response to vastly improve the coverage of services where feasible. This will ensure that the protection environment for children is improved in the longer term.
As a result of this action, DRC will be providing over 4,200 households with a comprehensive response to alleviate their vulnerabilities and reduce reliance on harmful coping, support over 3,000 children in receiving critical psychosocial support, and improve the protection environment for children through the development of a network of organizations/individuals trained in case management.
The crisis in the southwest has created diverse and widespread protection needs that are in critical need of being chronicled and addressed. DRC has had dedicated protection monitoring and response capacity in the southwest since January, 2019. The proposed action will allow DRC to expand coverage of monitoring and response, building the capacity for evidence based advocacy, and improving the protection environment for individuals and communities in the longer term. Protection needs will be addressed through psychosocial support including psychological first aid, with immediate protection cases being supported through individual protection assistance. Protection monitoring and reporting will allow DRC to continue to gather important information on the protection environment in highly vulnerable communities informing both advocacy and response.
As a result of the proposed intervention, crisis affected households will have better capacity to meet their basic needs, and the protection environment for both children and adults will be improved in the longer term.

Besoins / Needs

Cameroon is currently struggling to cope with three competing crises that have put millions of people into humanitarian need. In the northwest and southwest regions, over half a million people have been displaced since the start of the conflict, and needs continue to climb as the conflict enters its fourth year without a resolution in sight. Despite this, the crisis remains critically underfunded, which compromises the capacity of responding agencies to provide a timely and comprehensive response. OCHA and cluster reports indicate that over 400,000 people are in need of NFIs, and over 300,000 people are in need of shelter. This in addition to the 1.3 million people in need of WASH support across Cameroon, meaning that the need to provide a response at scale has never been greater. OCHA’s humanitarian snapshot from October, 2019 reports that over 450,000 people remain displaced and over 80% face protection issues. Without immediate support, protection needs will continue to rise as populations exhaust coping mechanisms. The Child Protection AoR has indicated that 100% of IDP and host community children have been adversely affected by the crisis and are in need of psychosocial support as a result. DRC has been collecting primary data on emergency needs since January, 2019. Evidence overwhelmingly supports the need for improved multi-sector response. Protection monitoring is ongoing and has highlighted the multitude of risks that crisis affected individuals and households face including arbitrary arrest, extrajudicial killings, destruction/burning of homes, movement restrictions and more. Protection data across 11 months of protection monitoring demonstrates that protection risks and violations are not localized and time-bound, but extend throughout the southwest and are creating widespread individual and community level protection needs, as evidenced by hundreds of key informants.DRC has also been conducting focus group discussions with over 240 children, adolescents and caregivers to understand the needs of children as a result of the crisis, as this information is largely absent in the response so far. Full analysis is found in a dedicated report available upon request and indicates that it is clear that children have been drastically affected by the conflict in the southwest region, and are in need of significant psychosocial support. High levels of exposure to conflict between state and non-state actors, significant incidence of sexual violence and abuse against children as young as 8 years old, children as young as 9 forming associations with non-state armed groups, and widespread family separation are a few of the trends found throughout DRC’s FGDs in highly affected communities. Years without access to education have robbed children of a healthy environment for cognitive development, and unless the crisis reaches a resolution soon, the risk of losing a generation to the impacts of conflict will rise. DRC’s multi-sector needs assessment tool has been used to gather the information from over 400 households about their needs during the crisis. Data indicates that 33% of respondents are permanently displaced, with 67% frequently displacing into the bush to avoid conflict. Over 80% reported that they are unable to return to their place of origin due to conflict, with lack of livelihoods and shelter also reported as reasons for inability to return by over 60% of respondents. For those that are frequently but not permanently displaced, the majority are moving an average of 1-2 times a week, indicating that for both permanently and chronically displaced, shelter is a need. 79% indicated that they do not have enough eating utensils and 70% indicate they don’t have enough pots. 84% of respondents indicated they do not have enough beds, with 50% indicating that they are sharing bedding as a response. DRC's primary data collection evidences the need for comprehensive and multi-sector responses across the southwest region.
