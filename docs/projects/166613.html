Project Summary

The project seeks to mitigate the secondary impacts of COVID-19 on vulnerable households through cash transfers in large cities of Zimbabwe (Harare, Mutare, Bulawayo). Due to the extended lock down and movement restrictions urban households who subsist in the informal economy are disproportionately affected as it is taking time for the informal economy to be fully opened. More so some of the beneficiaries have been affected by the demolitions that were effected by the city councils while others have their value chains being disrupted by the movement restrictions internally as well as cross border trade.

1 - Activities

Outcome 1 key activities1.1 Cash distribution to vulnerable households approximately 20 000 beneficiaries.1.2 Carry post distribution monitoring to assess effect of cash distribution on livelihoods1.3 Market monitoring Description of project Activities1.The project will seek to assist 20 000 people (4500 households) from urban communities with high numbers of households under the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) level 3 through cash transfers to increase their ability to purchase food needed to meet household daily consumption needs and suffering from the secondary impacts of COVID-19. These communities were based on the vulnerability mapping as identified in the revised revised HRP which took account of the effects of Covid-19. Beneficiary selection will be participatory looking at intersecting vulnerabilities of food insecurity and covid -19. The beneficiaries will be selected from the community using settlement level household ranking processes. The communities will lead the process to ensure that the project reaches out to the most vulnerable food insecure households in need of assistance. Distribution will be done through mobile money transfers or CIT that will be continuously monitored for effectiveness and to ensure that households receive the value of the transfer.Post distribution tools will be developed to determine the effects of the food insecurity situation on the rights of the children especially girls as well as the benefits of the project implementation on bridging the game. The Post distribution Monitoring will be done two weeks after each distribution cycle to assess utilisation and monitor and other protection issues arising from project implementation.The post distribution will validate the level of satisfaction of the targeted beneficiaries from the intervention by measuring the ability to purchase desired balanced diet food items from the distributed cash. Market monitoring will also tack the market performance following cash injection into the community to ensure that value for money is achieved and beneficiaries are able to access all the food items they desire at the expected costs. Outcome 2 Child Protection in emergencies. Key Activities2.1 Training of CPC structures on emerging protection issues related to Covid-19 and alternative reporting mechanisms available during lock down.2.2 Training of CCW on PSEA and sensitisation of communities on the PSEA and reporting channels2.3The project seeks to directly work with girls, boys, women, men and the traditional leadership to strengthen protection mechanisms during the emergency. Activities will be aimed at collectively identifying protection risks during the emergency and risk mitigation. The most vulnerable groups of children that have been identified include the girls, Child Headed Households (CHH), Children with Disabilities, children from marginalised communities and out of school children. A Rapid Assessment on the situation of children (RA) conducted by Plan International Zimbabwe on the effects of COVID-19 on children have noted the rise of cases of abuse and GBV. Furthermore, the RA indicated a high likelihood of children (in particular boys) engaging in high risk economic activities further compromising their protection and security. These risks include but are not limited to illegal migration to neighbouring countries, and theft. The RA also indicated that as a result of food insecurity and secondary impacts of COVID some children may not return to school when eventually school opens as a coping strategy for lack of tuition fees as households will be looking at satisfying their food security needs. By addressing food insecurity at household level and supporting children and their families with their most immediate needs, children will be supported with a more protective environment. Plan International proposes its CPiE intervention to be modelled around empowerment of local child protection mechanisms.
