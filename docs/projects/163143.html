Project Summary

Child Protection Sector estimates that 1,206,804 girls, boys, adolescents and women will be in need of protection services as a result of humanitarian hazards affecting Zimbabwe.
UNICEF in partnership with several Civil Society Organisations, child protection networks, networks for specially vulnerable children, government departments including Department of Social Welfare, Judicial Service Commission, Victim Friendly Unit, Legal Aid Directorate will require additional resources to target more than 422,000 in 35 priority districts in the country. The multi-hazards include possible floods, hails storms and mudslides, diarrheal disease outbreaks, prevailing food insecurity induced by drought and hyperinflation on basic food commodities. UNICEF – as cluster lead has made an situation analysis with regards to child protection risks and caseloads. Data from the Victim Friendly System indicates more than 20% increase in cases that have gone through the system compared to the same period in 2018. A 24% increase in reported child abuse cases and a 20% increase in reported child sexual abuse for 2019. According to the Legal Aid Directorate of Zimbabwe, quarter 3 of 2019 saw the highest caseload of children in contact with the law needing legal aid. Most cases reflect the increasing number of children in street situations trying to survive by committing petty crime. The country’s Civil Court also recorded a 50% increase in claims for child maintenance support submitted to court system, mostly by single mothers. Child placements in residential care has increased by 31%.

1 - Activities

Implementation of (IDTR) identification, documentation, family tracing and assessments, reunification with family, kinship care, community foster care or placed in interim care. Provision of PSS including life skills training for adolescents through Child Friendly Spaces and community based approaches Strengthen the referral pathway for child protection cases including management of sexual exploitation and abuse cases in the emergency context Training of Child Protection Actors including community based child protection mechanisms on PSEA, Capacity building for community child protection mechanisms and, community leaders. Community sensitization on child protection in emergency Provision of Survivor friendly and community-based feedback mechanism. Provision of Child Friendly reporting and feedback mechanism (Toll free Child helpline, suggestion boxes including interactive sessions) Skills training for caregivers - through Peer to Peer (P2P) Mother to Mother (M2M) mentor-ship sessions. Provision of supplies for child protection in emergencies. Provision of specialized services for children with special needs including children with disabilities, children living with HIV and children in contact with the law. Support national and sub-national level child protection in emergency coordination
