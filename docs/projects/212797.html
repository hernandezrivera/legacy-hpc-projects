Project Summary

Needs:
In 2024, it is estimated that approximately 6.8 million people (19 per cent increase from previous year) in Syria will require shelter support and 6.1 million people (8 per cent decrease from previous year) will require NFI support.
Across all shelter and settlement contexts, access to NFIs to support adequate sleep quality, living space and personal warmth, and domestic activity (cooking and hygiene) remains a significant need, exacerbated by the increasingly challenging economic situation and devaluation of the SYP.
Approximately 10 million people are without sufficient and appropriate winter clothing and safe and sufficient heating devices to maintain living space and personal warmth. This, coupled with the deterioration of shelter integrity and increasing shelter adequacy issues (limited electricity/lighting and poor insulation from heat/cold), exposes households to harsh weather conditions with a high risk of loss of life, particularly during winter.
This drives the need for repeat year-on-year emergency orientated unsustainable humanitarian shelter and NFI assistance across the northwest and north-east. This comes in the form of tent replacement, household, and site level improvements, and amplifies the need for increased funding and advocacy toward enhanced and more dignified transitional shelter and settlement solutions for the most vulnerable.
Consequences of the problem:
• Increase of illnesses due to the extreme weather conditions.
• Increase of dropouts as the children do not have for an education year sufficient clothes to attend school, especially boots.
Coping mechanism:
• Dropping out of school is also a copying mechanism.
• Trying to recycle old clothes.
Intervention:
Through the proposed project, Oxfam plans to enable the most vulnerable families in 3 governorates of Idleb, Deir Az’zour, and Rural Damascus to have access to the essential non-food items (Winterization kit) through the communities’ preferable modality.
Oxfam will target 4,000 HHs, at least 30% of which will be women headed households This will benefit a total of 20,000 individuals in need. Oxfam will make sure to engage the communities in the selection of the most vulnerable families. Therefore, Oxfam will form a community committee where at least 30 to 50% of participants will be women. The committees will be responsible of identifying the most vulnerable families through agreed criteria, the criteria will be clear to the community in order to avoid any misunderstanding and/or tension about the targeting. Oxfam will follow its internal procedures to do the verification process, impact and market prices will be monitored regularly. Worth noting that the targeted sub-districts fall under winterization severity scale 3 or higher and will be covered with WASH services and hygiene items as well.
Protection Risk Analysis
Risk: Due to access restrictions& limited funds, Oxfam cannot always adopt a blanket distribution, and at the same time is unable to directly identify beneficiaries eligible to NFI in alignment with its vulnerability/selection criteria, which generates a risk of selection bias, inclusion/exclusion errors and community perceptions of unfair eligibility criteria or discriminatory practices. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High MM: community-based targeting and selection; Risk-informed selection criteria; multi-layer verification of the list of selected beneficiaries; Programme data is disaggregated per gender, disability, and status i.e., displaced, returnee, host, so red flags are identified if any. Oxfam, in 2024, will also review its conflict-sensitive framework to ensure building on the connectors and not exacerbating dividers throughout the project cycle.Risk: limited options of sites for distributions & bureaucratic/admin impediments that further condense the timeframe for implementation, could lead to compromising on essential safe& inclusive requirements e.g., access ramps for PWD..
