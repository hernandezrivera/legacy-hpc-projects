Project Summary

COOPI proposes to implement the lifesaving food security and livelihood strengthening project in Kaga LGA in Borno State and Gujba, Gulani, Jakusko, Potiskum, Nengere and Yunusari LGAs of Yobe state, to support vulnerable Internally Displaced Persons (IDPS), vulnerable Host Communities and vulnerable returnees to improve food security and strengthen their resilience by supporting agricultural production. The inclusion of all categories (IDPs/Host/Returnees) as targeted beneficiaries will avoid conflicts in the communities as no category of population will be excluded and the selection will follow vulnerability criteria.
The project will provide assistance to 38,500 individuals meaning around 7,000 households(Based on average household size of Yobe state ), with food assistance through e-vouchers targeting 100% of food basket based on the prevailing MEB data that will be conducted in the first phase of the project, this intervention is proposed for a project duration of 12 months. Based on COOPI previous experience on implementing similar projects in similar contexts and taking into account the most operationally effective timelines(including assessments, beneficiaries’ selection and closure of the activities) and the intention to reach a significant number of beneficiaries, 10transfers are proposed to be completed during this time frame covering the lean season and the period of stress on the household food security
At the same time COOPI will foster resilience of the communities through distribution of agricultural inputs to the most vulnerable households in the farming seasons, whilst ensuring a close collaboration with the relevant government institutions for extension services, training and monitoring to enhance optimum agricultural productivity and production.
Beneficiaries will be trained on climate-resilient agricultural techniques and effective post-harvest techniques including storage mechanisms and processing. COOPI will adapt the approach to the specific agro-ecosystems of Yobe and Borno State (with a focus on improved soil management, improved eﬃcient use of rainwater, irrigation and ground water resources, and of use of organic options for fertilizer, and farmer-managed natural forest regeneration and/or tree nurseries).
Beneficiaries will be linked to accessible improved seeds and other high-quality Inputs via cash-based vouchers. Selected beneficiaries will receive agricultural input cash-based grants for use in buying improved seeds, inputs and tools (where appropriate) in local markets. Arrangements will be made in advance by COOPI with vendors, whose stocks and established presence will be screened and monitored by this proposed project. COOPI will pilot a combination of methods to determine the most effective market intervention. Cash grants will cover the complete cost of improved seeds and high-quality inputs and seek to establish vendors in the marketplace. The outcomes of cash-based action will be to help rebuild input supply markets by partnering with private sector input suppliers and other actors to improve stocking and services to farmers.
Gender shall be mainstreamed by making sure that all data are age and gender dis-aggregated, all projects and activities will be informed by a gender analysis, equal engagement of women and men using their preferred channel of communication on the activity design and implementation as well as the provision of feedback and finally we shall ensure to have a minimum of 30% women personnel at the field level
across operations and activities whilst Facilitating women and men’s equal access to PMC decision making
structures.

1 - Needs

According to the HNO 2019, across the three affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY), 7.1 million people are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2019 out of the total population of 13.4 million. Over eighty per cent of internally displaced people are in Borno State, the epicenter of the crisis, and over sixty per cent are living in host communities, making it harder to access them with assistance and putting additional pressure on the already stretched resources of these communities. One in four of the internally displaced people are under five, and 80 per cent are women and children. According to the Nigerian Humanitarian response strategy for January 2019 to December 2021, a reduction in the number of people in need in 2019 at a time of ongoing conflict and new displacements must be understood within a context of bolstered and sustained humanitarian assistance and service delivery that has helped to stabilize many communities. Maintaining humanitarian support is therefore critical to ensure that the situation does not deteriorate and people do not slip back into crisis. An estimated 1.7 million people in the BAY states are currently estimated to be food insecure and considered to be facing crisis or emergency situations (CH Phase 3 or 4)20.. The security situation also impacts access to fuel and energy for cooking food, with 85 per cent of women and girls interviewed during a joint assessment reporting heightened protection risks when collecting firewood. IOM displacement tracking matric reports that over the Round 28 reporting period, a nominal increase in numbers of internally displaced people (IDPs) was observed but hostilities and increased insecurities continued to restrict coverage of DTM assessments – in-line with the previous round of assessment that were published in May 2019. In addition to the ongoing conflict, communal clashes also played a part in increased population mobility. The influx of IDPs into host communities exacerbated a situation of already scarce resources.The Cadre Harmonise (June 2019) confirmed that an elevated number of households continue to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity and humanitarian needs remain high for the more than 1.7 million IDPs and poorest host populations. Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes are expected to remain widespread throughout northeast Nigeria through early 2020. Outcomes in inaccessible areas are likely similar or may be worse than neighbouring accessible areas, and there remains a risk of Famine (IPC Phase 5) in these areas. Extreme levels of acute food insecurity, including Famine (IPC Phase 5) are also possible in a worst-case scenario in which conflict shifts and significantly restricts humanitarian access and household movement referencing FEWSNET food security outlook for June 2019 to January 2020 also reports that the recurring attacks by insurgents, persistent insecurity, and military operations have restricted movement in the northeast of Nigeria. Similarly, farming, trade, transhumance, and other livelihood activities have been affected significantly, limiting access to basic services. Humanitarian food assistance to the northeast has declined during the recent year. Funding gaps remain a major constraint to adequate provision of assistance in the northeast of Nigeria. Despite the main seasons harvest coming soon, due to restricted access to land for most households in the NE, harvest will be substantially below average for most of the households. In line with the above context, identified needs by the sector, priorities and objective for 2020, the project seeks to deliver life-saving food assistance to the most vulnerable IDP, returnees and host community households. In parallel, the project aims at fostering resilience of vulnerable communities by providing them with agricultural inputs and training to improve agricultural production. Inclusion and protection will be mainstreamed.

2 - Activities

Activities or outputs Food assistance • Community participation meetings to select the most in need vulnerable households, ensuring participation of the most vulnerable groups within the target communities and equal representation of man and women • Generate beneficiary data/ distribution lists • Vendors selection through clear criteria: geographic proximity with the communities, capacity and quality of storage, quantity and variety of food items. Vendors will be required to have a bank account and a safe. • Cash based transfer to the most in need households in line with the FSSWG and the CWG harmonized guidelines • Conduct PDM and establish an effective complaints handling and feedback mechanism • Routine monitoring of protection/GBV concerns in food assistance activities to ensure safety and dignity of beneficiaries accessing assistance • Routine monitoring and community sensitization • Price monitoring/ continuous market assessments Farming kits distribution • Selection of the beneficiaries • Distribution of farming kits (seeds, fertilizers, tools) to the most food unsecured households • Training on agricultural production • Monitoring of the correct utilization of the tools and the techniques Indicators and targets. Assumptions:• Harvest prospects: Main harvests in the area will be substantially below-average following attacks and resultant population displacements in the area that have reduced the farming population and restricted farming activities. In the proposed areas, cultivation activities occur only within the territories of the LGA headquarters. Restrictions on cultivation of tall growing crops are further limiting the main harvest prospects in the area. Similarly, land access constrains, inputs such as fertilizer and IEDs will continue to restrict the level of cultivation in the area.• Household stocks: Household stock levels will increase during the main harvest, though stock levels will be below average following the anticipated reduction in the main harvest. Household stocks will deplete faster than usual, one to two months earlier than the typical lean season period and most households will resort to markets for food earlier than normal.• Market functioning levels: Due to continuing attacks and insecurity, market function is expected to remain at a maximum of 40 percent, most markets in the northeast will continue to function below the pre-conflict period through May 2020.• Sources of income: Agricultural labour opportunities during the main harvest and dry season cultivation, construction work, firewood sales, petty trading, craft making, cap knitting, and labour migration will provide limited income for the vulnerable population in the area. Income sources and wages will generally remain below average during the period. • Coping strategies: Households will continue to engage in intense labour opportunities, petty trading, and firewood sales to earn limited income and to access food. Others will solely depend on limited humanitarian assistance for food. Women will engage in domestic work in better off households and children will be encouraged to beg to earn income and food.

7.3 - In which LGA are you planning to undertake Nexus activities?

In the project locations of Potiskum, Damatutu and Geidem where COOPI is partnering EU supported resilience program, and those in LGAs COOPI shall ensure to work with the FSS and OCHA local coordination to identify and select gap locations to avoid duplication and to enhance the complimentary actions of other agencies conducting resilience building interventions. In liaison with the SEMA to work support the government's strategic plan and to avoid friction between the government agencies and COOPI programs as proposed. COOPI has established presence in Yunusari and Potiskum LGAs Yobe state, and recently closed out projects in Jakusko, and Kaga LGA Borno state, hence a referral and linkage system shall be established to enhance the resilience of this FSL intervention by linking beneficiaries to complimentary projects from COOPI where co-location is envisaged. The beneficiaries’ selection process will be as following: 1. In order to ensure a relevant targeting of the most vulnerable people, a geographic targeting shall be used to identify the LGAs with poor food security indicators as published by the 2019 WFP EFSA and Cadre Harmonise reports for Yobe state: following two criteria: • 20% or more of the district population severely food insecure; or • Districts with a total number of 1,000 or more IDPs/returnees. • District level estimates of proportion of population with severe food insecurity are extrapolated from the WFP EFSA and FEWSNET Cadre Harmonise level IPC results based on geo-statistical analysis. IDPs/Returnees data is from the most recent IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix published monthly.2. a census of all the population in the targeted area will be done, including household composition and household socio-economic situation.3. Once this data collected, the selection process will be based on food security criteria, by using indicators such as FCS and CSI.4. In parallel, in order to ensure a well acceptance of the program and an adaptation to the context of the targeting process, the participants (including IDPs, host communities and community leaders) will be involved through focus groups and committees, in the definition of the socio-economic criteria. The vulnerability criteria will be established in collaboration with the community, through these focus groups, with an emphasis on persons with specific needs. These vulnerability criteria can be: widow, elderly, disabled people, household with no income. 5. Finally, the selection of the most vulnerable households according to the thresholds previously defined will be done. The weighting of the socio economic criteria is done with the community. Past experiences have shown that household headed by widow, disabled and elderly are usually considered as the most vulnerable households by the community. This community-based approach enables selection of beneficiaries while preventing or limiting frustration from people not included in the final selection.
