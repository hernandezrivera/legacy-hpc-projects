Project Summary

In wards 3 and 5 of Whykong, and 7 and 8 of Nhilla, Oxfam will operate through our partner NGO Forum, in close cooperation with local government stakeholders. Together, we will tackle the challenge of low availability of water during the dry season through the installation of rainwater harvesting ponds and solar-powered pond sand filters. This should not only provide greater water security, but also greater equality in terms of water access between the refugees and the host community.
We will tackle the solid waste problem through running “Clean Host Community Hygiene Campaigns”.
We will work with the community to construct new latrines, with a community-led scale up including inclusive features learned from the social architecture approach: women, girls and disabled people will all have their needs understood.
In Camp 22, Oxfam will hand over to an as-yet to be decided partner (UNICEF is currently selecting the new partner), but we will continue to operate in the camp during 2022 to ensure a responsible handover.
Oxfam currently operates the surface water treatment plant and water distribution system which currently supplies all of Camp 22. As an area without good groundwater availability, this is a crucial piece of infrastructure and even more so during the dry season. This will be handed over to a partner but during the handover period we will remain active, running the plant jointly with the new partner, to ensure that this is done responsibly and that all users of the systems have equitable access which meets their needs.
In terms of our hygiene promotion work, we will continue this in one block of Camp 22 (preferably block C). We will use this to demonstrate the approaches and techniques and campaigns that Oxfam has been successfully developing to allow the adoption by others. This will include our “Mum’s magic hands” handwashing campaign and a complete menstrual hygiene management project (kit distribution, training and education).
We will not construct any new latrines in Camp 22, but will adapt existing latrines according to the needs of women, girls, elderly and disabled users. We will support with the design of a faecal sludge treatment plant, using lessons learned from our plants in Ukhiya.
In terms of sold waste, we will be supporting a partner for six months in the whole process of recycling, composting, reuse and disposal (in the UNDP central landfill). We will promote household-level segregation, and be tracking community attitudes towards this.
The “Community Perception Tracker” methodology and tools will be used and promoted in all areas to increase accountability to the affected populations. Oxfam’s aim is that this flagship programme will also be adopted by others.

1 - Identified Needs

Camp 22Camp 22 is very water stressed due to insufficient groundwater availability. All year round surface water is of poor quality, but this becomes an even bigger problem during the dry season when most sources dry up and water can only be stored in some limited places suitable for dams.Lack of space for latrines in camp 22 means they are often not well adapted to vulnerable people’s needs. There is a need for ongoing maintenance as highlighted in the joint sectors needs analysis. Host communityOver 30% of people in Teknaf do not have access to adequate water for domestic needs. Inward 3 & 5 of Whykong union, people depend on seasonal shallow and surface water sources exposed to contamination. Over 37% of people do not have sanitation facilities at HH level it is much higher in Teknaf especially inner remote communities in Whykong Union. Despite good improvement among host communities in hygiene practice, they were less consulted and or involved in the design and delivery of WASH assistance, much less for vulnerable groups (gender, age, disability)
