Project Summary

The proposed HOPE360 project aims to respond to the Camp Coordination and Camp Management critical needs for the protracted and newly displaced families in informal sites of MMC, Jere and Konduga Local Government Area (LGA), as well as other areas where the needs would require CCCM interventions. The project, which will be implemented under a 12 month period, will cover a total of 1,401 households; 6,615 individuals (2,800 M; 3,815 F) affected by the ongoing conflict and different waves of displacements with a CCCM response.

1 - Needs

The year 2019 witnessed a constant and increased trend of new displacements in Borno state, as highlighted by the regular DTM assessment reports. Considering the current context, additional displacements of populations to IDP sites are expected in 2020. Meanwhile, the assessment conducted by NRC in MMC/Jere sites, along with coordination with the CCCM sector, confirmed a major gap in the provision of CCCM services in those areas. Approximately 182,202 households (836,520 individuals) have been identified living in a camp like setting across 273 sites in the BAY states according to DTM site assessment Round 28, out of which 138 are located in MMC and Jere. Of the 273 sites, 139 sites, all of which are informal spontaneous sites, neither have a Site Management Agency nor any CCCM support. The majority of them are within the LGAS of Jere and Maiduguri, where 50 and 40 sites respectively are lacking any CCCM support or activities. Is most of the sites, there is no site facilitator to collect feedback from the community and raise their needs to service providers. The lack of CCCM services in Maiduguri and Jere correlates with lack of humanitarian actors and important gaps in the provision of other services. As per the shelter DMS/CCCM multisector tracker report released in September 2019, MMC and Jere witness critical level of shelter gaps, including damaged shelters or households sharing shelters (nearly 4,500 households in Maduguri and 2,900 in Jere). Maiduguri is also the second LGA with the highest number of households in need of NFI (11,022), whereas Jere is the LGA with more number of sites (11) without functional and accessible health facility on site or nearby. Needs are also critical in the targeted areas regarding food security assistance, with large number of families not benefitting from food distributions (nearly 6,000 in Jere and 3,000 in Maiduguri). In those sites, the degradation or lack of proper communal infrastructures, accessible facilities (for example latrines or showers) and maintenance increase the risk of protection concerns for the inhabitants in sites, in particular for women and girls, who constitute the majority of the displaced population and are at risk of Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV). Other groups with specific needs, like elderly or people with disability are also facing additional protection risks. In sites where CCCM actors are not present, community groups and governance structures are not supported and therefore do not have proper interlocutors to provide feedback, raise their needs and concerns. These gaps in CCCM assistance compounded by the regular influx of IDPs which aggravates congestion, puts high pressure on the existing resources and infrastructures. This threatens to create social tension and additional protection risks for the displaced population. Without proper preparedness and mitigation plan, these conditions can result in fire outbreaks and destruction of shelters and site infrastructure. During the rainy season, IDP settlements are also at high risks of heavy floods and destructions of facilities and shelters. The coming rainy season also poses a risk of epidemics and there is a high probability of an outbreak of sanitation and water related epidemics in the existing conditions. It is therefore imperative to design a CCCM response for improving coordination and provision of basic services in those settlements. CCCM presence will also facilitate advocacy towards sectors to ensure that humanitarian actors are targeting those sites and that adequate assistance is provided. CCCM will also be critical to ensure basic site planning and site improvement for reducing risks related to congestion and hazards, as well as improvement of site infrastructures for mitigating protection risks towards camp residents. The active involvement of community structures and leaders to identify problems and design solutions would also be paramount to the success of the project.

2 - Activities

Based on the gaps identified, the project will address the following needs; 1. Reinforce coordination mechanisms to improve access to services for targeted populations through: - Regular meetings with service providers and community representatives, circulation of comprehensive service mappings. - Regular service monitoring exercises to identify gaps and duplications in service provision. - Referral mechanisms and facilitation of referrals of beneficiaries to specific service providers. 2. Improved communication with communities and access to information through: - Information and awareness sessions, outreach activities conducted by community based workers. - Complaint and feedback mechanism based on the community’s preferred channels in order to install a two-ways communication and enable the community to provide feedback on the assistance. 3. Support community engagement mechanisms through: - Selection and support of community and governance structures, including support to site facilitators in each site. - Training community members on problem identification and solving and support community on developing community-driven projects. 4. Support to safety committees with tools and training on safety mappings exercise that will be integrated in a preparedness plan, to enhance the capacity of the community to respond to hazards such fires, floods, etc. 5. Conduct site improvement activities and maintenance of site infrastructures to improve safety and protection for camps’ residents.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The proposed CCCM response will ensure involvement of the targeted population at all stage of the project. HOPE360 will establish a complaint and feedback mechanism that will be designed according to the communities’ cultural preferences following a participatory approach. It will allow people to provide and receive feedback about services received in the sites through different channels. This will build upon the lessons learnt in HOPE360’ complaint and feedback mechanisms that are implemented during ongoing programming, WASH, Shelter, livelihoods and food security, information, counselling and legal assistance and education. HOPE360 employs the four components – Information sharing, participation, M&E, and a beneficiary complaints and feedback mechanism (BCFM) of the Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) framework, to ensure downward accountability to beneficiaries.
