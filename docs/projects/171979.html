Project Summary

With the assumption of temporary localised school lockdowns on both sides of the contact line, Save the Children (SC) will build on previous phases of Schools as Zones of Peace project implementation to expand support to boys and girls with specific focus on the schools along the contact line. The approach of setting up School Safety Committees (SSC) consisting of parents, children and teachers to identify safety risks, train them on child rights, child participation, gender equality etc. and implement action plans to increase the safety of the school will be implemented. Recognizing the higher severity of needs in NGCA as per the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview, SC will increase its presence through local partner which has experience of implementation of education projects in NGCA.
Special attention will be given to protective measures against COVID 19 and Safe School reopening following expected waves of localised school lockdowns. SC will also support distance learning by strengthening teachers’ capacities to deliver online classes. Recognizing differences in local response to COVID-19 and barriers to accessing remote education, a school-by-school approach will be taken to support children (including children with disabilities) access learning safely (whether in person in line with the Safe Back to School Practitioner’s guide or remotely). These options could be supplying teachers with equipment to deliver classes online or print learning materials for children who cannot access the internet, hygiene kits, awareness raising etc.).
Teachers will be trained to provide in different topics to ensure they have knowledge and skills to ensure children and their own wellbeing and to contribute to resilience building and disaster risk reduction processes. Parents, teachers and children’s capacity will be built on child rights and inclusive education (for children with disabilities, including those injured in the conflict), in particular for children who might have been left behind during distance learning.
In the context of COVID-19 where it is highly likely that cases of domestic violence and violence against children has increased during and following lockdowns, SC will build use its experience to support existing referral mechanism recognising protective role of education.
Save the Children will continue delivering trainings on the Safe Schools Declaration and Guidelines to Protect Schools and Universities from Military Attack in Armed Conflict to civil military officers and Ukrainian Army officers. SC will continue looking for opportunities to deliver online innovative Safe Schools Declaration trainings. SC will continue advocating on setting up of the inter-ministerial taskforce consisting of key ministries in charge of the implementation of the SSD. SC will support the Government in clarifying their policy on the use of schools and universities during armed conflict as part of the SSD national implementation plan to be adopted by the GoU. SC will contribute to global advocacy mechanisms by submitting report to relevant treaty monitoring bodies and working with the UN Ukraine mission in global fora.
In 2021 SC will support transition of the Education Cluster functions to relevant stakeholders on both sides of the contact line through consultations and capacity building events.

1 - Needs

Children and adolescents affected by the armed conflict in eastern Ukraine are not only faced with persistent threats to their physical and mental well-being, they also run the risk of losing their futures. Attacks on education has a profound impact on the learning and overall development of nearly 660,000 school-aged boys and girls. Besides, the conflict impacts teachers’ and school staff's wellbeing and/or ability to work effectively, as reported by school administrations in 58% of schools in rayons along the contact line in GCA. The situation has been further compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic that led to a complete lockdown of education. Limitations of distance learning modalities left thousands of boys and girls on both sides of the contact line without access to education. Advocating for the implementation of the Safe Schools Declaration (SSD) remains a priority. Its implementation is the subject of continued and consistent efforts from the government including those ones such as setting the inter-ministerial working group. Schools in conflict affected areas of Ukraine are exposed to hazards, major environmental (ecological) and industrial risks that may be triggered by the conflict as was estimated by 3P consortium. To avoid negative long-term consequences in the socio-emotional development of conflict-exposed boys and girls and adolescents, emphasis must be placed on nurturing their resilience through positive peer relations, interdependent values, healthy self-esteem and emotional connection to their school while working to restore normality to their communities . Further chances to participation and expressing their views in school would also contribute to strengthening resilience of children and adolescents. In more than a third of education facilities along the contact line in GCA, teachers have no access to Psychosocial Support (PSS) In GCA, 36 % of teachers needed additional capacity building or pedagogical support to address the impact of conflict on education, and most of them point to the need for capacity building in psychosocial support . Due to the COVID-19 pandemic additional capacity building for teachers on how to use distance learning modalities has become rampant: 24% of teachers in GCA indicated their absence of the relevant technical skills to organise distance learning . The conflict has significantly deteriorated the economic situation of families residing in the affected areas, as a result of economic decline and unemployment. Based on the 2019 REACH trend analysis, 27% of households in GCA are unable to afford all school materials for their children. In the 0-5km area, this indicator is even higher, 44% in rural and 36% in urban areas . Save the Children’s 2020 assessment “COVID-19's impact on children in Eastern Ukraine” surveyed 145 children (85 girls and 60 boys) in conflict affected areas. Results about distance learning vary between adults and children: the former report higher levels of absence of distance education for their children (up to 20% in urban areas). The responsibility of home learning disproportionately fell on mothers. 14% of children reported knowing some children who did not return to school after the quarantine and summer, however further research needs to be conducted to understand the reasons. A large proportion of children with disabilities asked for PSS (35% vs 8% for children without disabilities).
