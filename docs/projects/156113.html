Project Summary

Extension services for olive oil value chain: this activity will start by August till February depends on the need, and it will provide 270 farmers (Idleb) with the following services:
• Monitoring the olive fly, then Spraying Pesticide and foliar fertilizers (to control pray oleae insect and high nitrogen potash fertilizer to get extra virgin olive oil and maintain the vitality of olive trees)
• Harvesting services to practice the technical conditions related to getting a high quality of fruits, where there will be 10 working groups will be contracted to commence the harvesting during Nov/Dec
• Olive Oil Extraction to practice the technical conditions related to getting a high quality of oil.
• Pruning service to maintain the vitality of trees and promoting the vegetative growth
• Capacity building & raise awareness in terms of the integrated management of olive.
Extension services for dairy production value chain: providing 35 women groups (Idleb -Aleppo) with equipment and materials for processing (Yoghurt, Cheese, Kuresha, Yogurt). In addition to capacity building & raise awareness in terms of the technician production of dairy products.
Vaccination campaign for livestock - Mobile clinics for livestock - ToT for para-vet:
• Train trainees in Idleb and Aleppo through TOT for para vet activity and choose the qualified trainees to work in the mobile clinics and oversee the vaccination campaign Enterprise support for dairy production
• Provide women groups with equipment and tools necessary for dairy production or support them with the cash amount needed to procure these materials
Apprenticeship and linkage with labor market:
• Support business grant that related to olive and dairy production women groups
• The BNFs should submit the business plan and the review committee will review and assist these plans
CBOs, Cooperative or entity support to provide continue vet services and olive farmers
• strengthen\supporting or establish CBOs for dairy production and olive farmers in terms of quality control.
Support market functionality through improving purchase power
• supporting the HHs with purchasing “voucher / in-kind “olive oil in order to improve the market purchasing power and support the farmers

Needs

According to HNO 2019 Some 9.8 million people are estimated to be in acute need of early recovery and livelihood support. • Over 50 per cent of basic social infrastructure in Syria is now either destroyed or non-operational. • An estimated three in four people of working age are not involved in any income-generating activities. • Syria has seen the proportion of the population living in extreme poverty (less than US$2 per day) double from almost 34 per cent pre crisis to approximately 69 per cent today. Close to seven years of conflict have severely stretched the capacity and resilience of people living in Syria to cope with shocks and stresses brought about by the crisis. The loss of jobs and livelihoods, the depletion of productive assets and savings, increasing debt, the destruction of civilian infrastructure, large-scale displacement and the disruption of social services have all contributed to increased economic hardship that have eroded people’s ability to cope. The deterioration of basic services continues to undermine the viability of productive sectors, feeding a vicious cycle of unemployment, diminishing resources and resulting in increased levels of poverty. Deprived of the productive tools required to meet their basic needs and in the absence of statesupported basic services, many households are resorting to improvised solutions to address their needs. On the basis of this analysis, the Early Recovery and Livelihoods (ERL) sector estimates that 13 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance of whom 9.8 million are in acute need. This compares to a 2017 figure of 13.8 million, of whom 10 million were in acute need.

Protection Risk Analysis

Although all Syrian population has been heavily affected by the current crisis, due to traditional local norms, women are exposed to additional protection related risks. All types of violence are cited as occurring in opposition areas of Syria, with domestic violence, sexual exploitation and child marriage as top three. While sexual exploitation is somehow new, child marriage and domestic violence are often normalized and ingrained in the culture. Women and girls are exposed to structural and societal risks as the lack of safe spaces and public transportation, limited freedom of movement, poverty, unemployment, displacement, stress associated to war, gender role change, lack of documents. All these poses enormous pressure, men feeling they failed in protecting and securing a good life for their families. With men perceiving women as a threat to their control due to gender roles change, domestic violence has increased in Syria. Whereas men actions are explained as a result of stress, women are often blamed for provoking the violence or allowing it to happen. Parental violence against children has also been reported as increasing. Displacement and living in camps are noted as being particularly unsafe environments for women/girls due to the lack of privacy (in tents or in latrines), overcrowding and mixing of people, poverty, financial desperation, and lawlessness.The WoS Protection analysis reveals that 17 sub-districts in Idleb and 23 In Aleppo are facing severe, critical and catastrophic situation regarding protection. While some services are available, GBV cases are less addressed and the assistance is focusing mainly on survival pathway support and less on community-based interventions, mandatory for mind-set change. Women and girls occupy a central role in WATAN’s strategy for 2018-2023. Part of the development approach, WATAN seeks to empower women to become more resilient in the conflict setting while securing their safety at all levels. When designing the project, WATAN has applied all its security protocols to ensure that interviewed GBV survivors and leaders of communities are not exposed to any risk for their involvement. For this purpose, WATAN and its partners have a set of policies and procedures to be respected when working with GBV cases.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Risk: Deterioration of the security situation or escalation of conflict/natural disaster preventing access to the project site. Mitigation measures:- Contextual information is gathered through staff on the ground and fed into rapid feedback mechanisms to be established.- In case the situation requires temporary suspension or adjustment or permanent suspension of activities in a given area, the consortium, through the PMU will immediately inform the NMFA and discuss options for re-programming. 2- Risk: Threat of warring parties Mitigation measures:- During the start-up phase, WATAN develops solid security management plans to mitigate risks for beneficiaries and staff, and joint scenario-based contingency planning are conducted at the level of implementation area. - WATAN Security department coordinate and constantly monitor the security situation. 3- Risk: Corruption and diversion of assistance.Mitigation measures:- Operating livelihoods programming with a cash transfer component in conflict-affected areas, particularly where various armed actors have a presence, is considered to be a high-risk activity, with the potential for assistance to be diverted. WATAN proposes the following mitigation measures to minimize those risks:- Common principles for remote management and monitoring procedures are developed during the current pilot phase inception phase by their relevance for and application through the staff will be reviewed periodically. - WATAN staff are trained in accountability, and, wherever possible, feedback and complaint mechanisms are established. - WATAN have well-established financial and support systems. - Contingency plans take into account the risk of corruption or aid diversion and detail measures to be taken should the situation arise. - Third-party monitoring and where possible, peer monitoring mechanisms are established to produce information for triangulation. 4- Risk: Conflict around access to project support:Mitigation measures::- Common do-no-harm approaches are developed, and staff and local partners are trained on them. - Discussions and awareness raising sessions are organized for local stakeholders, and community members to identify and address perceptions around resource allocation and related risks. - WATAN commit to open and transparent communication. - The value of support packages is standardized across each of the geographical implementation areas. - Local communities and municipalities are strongly involved in the identification and monitoring of CFW schemes. - Local communities and municipalities are strongly involved in the identification and monitoring of CFW schemes. - Standard operating procedures are developed for CFW interventions, agreed upon and monitored by a third party.
