Project Summary

Since 2018, the security situation in Burkina Faso drastically deteriorated with the
expansion and intensification of activities of non-state armed groups (NSAGs), exploiting
pre-existing inter-communal tensions. A further escalation was observed in 2020 and 2021, with a
greater toll on human lives and on vulnerable populations. The non-state armed groups
(NSAGs) are fragmented, with multiple internal players; and there are increased external
players linked with the regional dynamics (i.e. Central Sahel countries). This has led to
massive population displacements and has had a severe impact on already vulnerable
populations.
As of October 2021, over 1.4 million persons have been displaced and new displacements are reported daily. The security situation has had repercussions on the overall food and nutritional security of the country. Food security and nutritional situation has been impacted by the growing crisis affecting
vulnerable populations.
The unprecedented humanitarian emergency will left 2.6 million persons in need of
humanitarian assistance during the 2022 lean season – according to the last Cadre Harmonisé.
Health services, schools and markets have been closed due to growing insecurity in
several parts of the country, seriously affecting the population’s ability to sustain healthy
lives. COVID-19, and measures taken by the Government from March 2020, added another layer of
vulnerability.
Humanitarian access to vulnerable populations in need of assistance is restricted by
threats posed by non-state armed groups, counterterrorism operations, the presence of
Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs) planted on roads and the damage or destruction of
key road infrastructure (bridges targeted in attacks in the Sahel region for instance). In
addition, access is hindered by restrictive counter-terrorism legislation preventing
negotiation with NSAGs. Many humanitarian actors have left areas which remain
impeded by serious access constraints. To safeguard neutrality and the perception of it,
humanitarian workers in Burkina Faso do not use military escorts, particularly since the
national Security Forces (FDS) remain the primary target of armed groups.
In order to support the planned humanitarian response and enable access to the populations in need and project implementation sites, UNHAS aims to provide a safe,
effective and efficient passenger air service to the entire humanitarian community and
their implementing partners, principally NGOs, UN agencies, red cross/crescent movement,
donor organizations and diplomatic missions.
UNHAS will be used to deliver assistance to hard-to-reach areas were people are the most vulnerable.
Another crew is available since 2021 to allow 7/7 days coverage. Depending on the level of resources, another helicopter could be available to increase the operations in hard-to-reach areas.
The service is accessible to all humanitarian actors upon registration transporting personnel or
cargo aimed to support targeted populations.

2 - BESOINS, ACTIVITÉS ET RÉSULTATS ATTENDUS
2.1. Décrivez les besoins, activités et résultats attendus

Considering the deterioration of the security situation, UNHAS aims to provide a safe,effective and efficient passenger air service to the entire humanitarian community andtheir implementing partners, principally NGOs, UN agencies, red cross/crescentmovement, donor organizations and diplomatic missions. Considering the evolvingsituation in Burkina Faso, the United Nations Humanitarian Air Service was launchedofficially in late 2020, with a first flight in October following the acquisition of thenecessary flight authorizations from Civil Aviation Authority. This came as a processstarted in March 2020, with an assessment, from WFP Aviation security, to assess thefeasibility of a potential service in Burkina Faso, upon Humanitarian Country Teamrequest. This determined the intention to deploy a helicopter, as it would allow for moredirect approach to the centre of interest, not requiring any special airstrip infrastructurenor large use of the armed forces to ensure the security of the area. The use of ahelicopter allows improved access during the rainy season compared with fixed wingor road transport; serviceability of the helicopter is less impacted by sodden groundconditions during the rainy season. The service allows to mitigate thehumanitarian access constraints faced by humanitarian community in accessinghard-to-reach of the country. Populations in these areas are suffering from amultifaceted emergency – insecurity causing massive displacements of populationsand natural/climate-related shocks affecting those most vulnerable. Food andnutritional situation is critical in a country, where approximately 1.4 million of thepopulation has been forced to displace. Two platforms have been put in place to manage the service:- UNHAS UserGroup Committee (UGC) meetings composed of NGOs, UN agencies and DonorRepresentatives. The role of the UGC is administrative, provides feedback to UNHAS'Chief Air Transport Officer (CATO) to help ensure high service quality and maintains aservice-oriented approach to supporting the partners. The UGC are used as a forumwhere the users of the service can voice their views on operational matters, includingsuggestions with regard to destinations or other changes to the weekly flightsschedule.- Periodic Steering Committee meetings chaired by the HumanitarianCoordinator and composed of some representatives from INGOs, UN Agencies andDonors to define operational requirements and air transport priorities, also to discusstopics of a strategic nature such as funding, and give guidance on UNHAS policies.Upon the need and decision of the Steering Committee, UNHAS currently offers differentweekly flights rotations and special flights all departing from Ouagadougou airport. Theservice is accessible to all humanitarian actors upon registration. Furthermore, partnership is ensured at local, regional and national level through the different platforms. For each serviced location, a partner has been identified locally to serve as a focal point for security-related aspects. Relevant analysis and consultations is ensured in advance and during flights to ensure that a location is accessible.As UNHAS increased its capacity since October with an additional crew, 744 passengers flew with UNHAS in November 2021 - compared to 515 in October and 391 in September. UNHAS also conducted medical evacuation flights in 2021.
