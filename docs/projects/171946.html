Project Summary

The project “Hygiene, Nutrition and Winterization as Cash as well as Psychosocial Support for Conflict-Affected People in need along the Contact Line, Eastern Ukraine” (2019-2021) aims to improve food security, hygiene conditions, preparedness to winter and psychosocial well-being of conflict-affected populations in the 5-km zone along the 'contact line' in Eastern Ukraine (GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts).
Whereas the project’s food, hygiene and winterization components are implemented by ASB Country Office Ukraine, the psychosocial support (PSS) is provided by ASB’s local partner, Samaritan Union of Ukraine (SSU). The project is currently funded by the Federal Foreign Office of the Federal Republic of Germany
Within the framework of the food security component of the project, vulnerable beneficiaries will receive cash for food assistance.
ASB will target primarily households of older men and women, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, persons aged 40-60 with no employment as well as men and women with compound vulnerabilities. Depending on the availability of funding and food needs, the cash for food will be provided during the first half of 2021 or throughout the year.
Since ASB Country Office Ukraine is already operating in the area, it has immediate capacity and access to implement the planned activities also in 2021. Moreover, ASB has secured funding to implement the cash for food activities in the first half of 2021. The planned activities will be coordinated with other FSLC partners and follow the principles of do no harm, disability inclusion and accountability to affected populations (AAP). In the time of COVID-19 pandemic, all activities will follow the COVID-19 Ukraine Assistance Delivery Protocol.
The project activities contribute to the FSLC Objective 1: “Ensure immediate access to food for the most vulnerable people affected by conflict and/or СOVID-19”.

1 - Needs

The conflict in Eastern Ukraine continues to have a negative impact on the socioeconomic situation in Ukraine, particularly in the areas adjacent to the ‘contact line’. As identified by the Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster (FSLC), assistance under Cluster Objective 1 (“Ensure immediate access to food for the most vulnerable people affected by conflict and/or СOVID-19”) is needed by approximately 210,700 people. Out of these, 8,100 people reside in the 5-km zone along the ‘contact line’ in Luhanska and Donetska GCA. The food security situation in 2020 has deteriorated in comparison to 2019. Thus, whereas the proportion of moderately and severely food insecure households in the 5-km zone near the ‘contact line’ was estimated at 9% and 0% in 2019, now, similar to 2018, it is 13% and 1% respectively (REACH Humanitarian Trend Analysis, July-August 2020). Moreover, the residents of the 5-km zone (44% of households in rural areas and 26% in urban areas) experience problems with access to food markets (REACH Humanitarian Trend Analysis 2020).Food insecurity is more often experienced by vulnerable groups, for example, women, older people, persons with disabilities and chronic illnesses. Within the 20-km zone (GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts), 74% of households reported having at least one vulnerability, with the proportion being even higher (76%) for the 5-km zone (REACH Economic Security Assessment, March 2019). According to the REACH Humanitarian Trend Analysis (2020), 74% of households in the 5-km zone along the ‘contact line’ in the GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts are headed by women (in comparison to 69% in the 5-20-km zone). The study also shows that, within the 5-km zone (GCA), the proportion of moderately and severely food insecure households is higher in rural (19%) than in urban (11%) areas. For households headed by older women (60+), the level of food insecurity is even higher: 15% in urban and 25% in rural areas). According to the needs assessment by HelpAge International conducted in rayons near the ‘contact line’ in the GCA of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts (August 2020), 96,58% of older people (75,11% women) reported having a need in food assistance. Moreover, 68,54% of respondents indicated food as the first priority amidst the COVID-19 pandemic (“Humanitarian needs of older women and men in Government Controlled Areas of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, Ukraine”, August 2020). The food needs were even more pronounced in spring 2020, when 97,74% of older women and men were in need of food assistance, whereas 94,66% of respondents indicated food as the first priority (“Older women and men needs assessment in COVID-19 pandemic” by HelpAge International, May 2020). This data shows that older women are particularly prone to food insecurity, especially during the COVID-19 pandemic. Moreover, the situation may further deteriorate. Thus, according to the FSLC’s overview of HRP-2020, the research conducted by the M. Ptukha Institute for Demography and Social Studies of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine indicates that the share of households with a food expenditure share of over 60% (estimated at 17% in 2019) is expected to reach 22.1% by the end of 2020. Furthermore, household vulnerability increases in winter, when high heating costs negatively affect food security of particularly vulnerable households (FSLC’s Guidance Note on Winterisation (2017-2018) in Food Security). So, both the primary and secondary data indicate a need in provision of food assistance in the areas close to the ‘contact line’, particularly during the COVID-19 pandemic.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Purpose: To meet food needs of vulnerable conflict-affected populations in Eastern Ukraine.Output: vulnerable beneficiaries along the 'contact line' received food assistance.Outcome: target beneficiaries improve their food security situation.Activity 1. Beneficiary identification Given the multisectoral nature of the project (food, hygiene, winterization), the beneficiary selection methodology is based on a combination of food security indicators and general vulnerabilities. Since vulnerable households often prioritize their food needs over other expenses, the beneficiaries identified for cash for food will automatically receive cash for hygiene. The beneficiary assessment will be undertaken in coordination with the FSL Cluster, local authorities and humanitarian agencies operating in the area. ASB will ensure that beneficiaries meet vulnerability criteria identified by HRP 2021.The assistance will be provided based on specific needs of men, women, boys and girls, including their food security and vulnerability status. The food assistance aims at the household level, so all gender and age groups will be covered. However, given the demographic and socioeconomic profile of the vulnerable population in the 5-km zone along the ‘contact line,’ women will particularly benefit from the project activities. To illustrate, girls and women represent 66% of cash for food and hygiene caseload within the framework of the current ASB’s project.The beneficiary assessment will take place in Avdiivska and Toretska municipalities, Bakhmutskyi, Yasynuvatskyi and Mariinskyi raions in Donetska oblast as well as in Novoaidarskyi, Popasnianskyi and Stanychno-Luhanskyi raions in Luhanska oblast. A hotline feedback mechanism is available to ask questions, request assistance, provide feedback and lodge complaints. All complaints are recorded in the Complaints Registration Database for follow-up. At the end of each distribution cycle, ASB will conduct the post-distribution monitoring (PDM). Households who were the most vulnerable during the initial stage of beneficiary selection, will be re-assessed and, if needed, included into the next caseload. This may eventually reduce the overall number of unique beneficiaries. All assessments, including PDM, are designed to allow age, gender and disability disaggregation.Activity 2. Distribution of cash to cover basic food needs There will be 2 distribution cycles of cash assistance in 2021. In each caseload, up to 3500 beneficiaries will receive cash assistance to cover their food and hygiene needs:• Caseload 1: January-June 2021• Caseload 2: July-December 2021Cash transfers to 3500 beneficiaries will take place once in 2-3 months, covering food and hygiene needs for the respective period. The proposed cash intervention will follow the FSLC’s September 2018 recommendation (UAH 825 per person per month), covering 75% of minimum daily food needs (1,575 kcal). ASB may change a number of beneficiaries if the FSLC revises cash for food value or in case of significant exchange rate fluctuations.If the security situation deteriorates drastically, ASB will consider changing the target area or the target group to respond to new humanitarian needs. If local markets are disrupted, ASB will consider switching partially or fully from cash to in-kind modality.

3 - Indicators & targets

FSL Cluster Objective 1. Ensure immediate access to food for the most vulnerable people affected by conflict and or СOVID-19. • % of people in targeted receiving food, cash transfer and voucher assistance (target: 100);• # of affected people who receive adequate food/cash responses, in a timely manner (target: 7000);• % of households with improved food consumption (target: 75);• % of beneficiaries reporting that they are aware of the complaint mechanisms (target: 70);• % of project beneficiaries who are female (target: 56);• % of project beneficiaries who are older people (target: 35);• % of project beneficiaries who are persons with disabilities and/or homebound (target: 15).Based on the interim monitoring data from the ASB’s ongoing project “Hygiene, Nutrition and Winterization as Cash as well as Psychosocial Support for Conflict-Affected People in need along the Contact Line, Eastern Ukraine” (2019-2021), the above indicators are likely to be achieved also in 2021.As demonstrated by the results of the interim PDM on the project component of cash for food and hygiene (March 2020), the overall satisfaction of beneficiaries with the provided assistance is very high (99%). More than 95% of respondents said that the assistance was available, and it was safe for them to receive it. Also, 80,1% of PDM respondents said that they fully knew how to provide feedback to ASB through different channels (phone, e-mail or field staff). Also, as a result of 8-month assistance, 77,6% of beneficiaries improved their food consumption scores. The project beneficiaries who were supported by ASB between 1 January and 30 September 2020 demonstrate significant vulnerabilities in terms of age, gender and disability. Thus, among the recipients of cash for food, 56% are older people, 66% are women, 14% of the caseload are boys and girls, 23% are persons with disabilities, and 8% are IDPs. Since the markets are functioning in the 5-km zone along the ‘contact line’ (GCA of Donetska and Luhanska oblast), ASB plans to provide cash for food assistance in 2021. The PDM study (March 2020), confirmed that cash modality is in line with the preferences of affected populations: 95% of surveyed beneficiaries indicated cash as the preferred aid modality. For the population in the 5-km zone near the ‘contact line’ (GCA of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts), the most preferred modes of delivery for cash assistance are bank account (48%) and post office (32%) (REACH Humanitarian Trend Analysis 2018). Thus, the modes of cash disbursement used by ASB (Oshchadbank and UkrPoshta) are also in line with the preferences of affected populations. Due to the home delivery of cash by Ukrposhta, older people and persons with disabilities do not need to travel to bank to withdraw their cash grants, so the protection risks are minimized.The PDM findings and regular feedback from beneficiaries through ASB hotline and other complaint and response channels feed the redesign of ASB’s current and design of future project activities.
