Project Summary

This proposed project is designed to address the shelter and NFI vulnerability in the Rohingya camps and the host community Area. IRB will assist the Rohingya and adjacent host communities of Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila by providing durable and sustainable shelter support. Optimum shelter locations will be selected in coordination with the CIC, UN agencies, and the shelter sector. Based on the gap analysis and subsequent discussions with the shelter sector and other actors, IRB is planning to implement shelter and NFI support activities in Camps 2E (Block-D, & E), Camp 2W (Block-A, & B), Camp 3 (Block A, B, &C), Camp 4 (Block B, D & F) and Camp 7 (Block E, F & G). Host community activities will be implemented in 03 unions (Haldia Palong, Jalia Palong, Ratna Palong) under Ukhiya upazila and 02 unions (Whykong & Nhilla) under Teknaf Upazilla. To address the needs of vulnerable populations, IRB will continue providing shelter and NFI materials to the affected host community vulnerable people in Ukhiya and Teknaf. IRB will support Transitional Shelter Assistance (TSA) to increase the shelter's durability, minimize the risk of shelter damage, and provide technical assistance and porter support for the EVI families in the Rohingya camp. IRB will organize training /orientation for the beneficiaries before distributing the TSA materials. Training will focus on elevating treated bamboo columns off the ground and installing proper pad footing. Training will emphasize DRR and climate-resilient measures such as building household-level drainage, bracing corners, installing tie-down kits, and fire protection in cooking areas. As part of shelter care and Maintenance and distributions, IRB will provide Porter support and construction labor support for verified HHs. In addition, 3200 HHs will receive PSEA and GBV training. 1250 Transitional Shelter (TSA) will be built for the most vulnerable families in Rohingya camps. Among them, 250 TSA will be built in Camp 3, 250 TSA in Camp 4, 225 TSA in Camp 2E, 225 TSA in Camp 2W, and 300 TSA in Camp 7. IRB will provide Care and Maintenance materials to 3400 households in five camps (650 HHs in Camp 3, 650 HHs in Camp 4, 600 HHs in Camp 2E, 600 HHs in Camp 2W, and the rest of 900 HHs in Camp 7. The project also aims to provide 3200 emergency shelter kits for emergency response or natural disasters like cyclones, heavy rainfall, landslides, fire incidents, earthquakes, etc. This includes the construction of 600 emergency shelters in Camp 3, 600 emergency shelters in Camp 4, 600 emergency shelters in Camp 2E, 600 emergency shelters in Camp 2W, and 800 emergency shelters in Camp 7. Besides this, IRB will construct 203 Mid-term shelters (MTS) for the most vulnerable families of the respective five Camps. Households will be identified and verified by SMS and then technically assessed by IRB. The project will build 55 permanent shelters for the most vulnerable families in the Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila. These shelters will be constructed by vendors and community people will be involved in construction monitoring. Among the 55 HC shelters, 30 shelters will be constructed in Ukhia Upazila (Haldia Palong union-10, Ratna Palong union-10, & Jalia Palong-10) and 25 shelters will be constructed in Teknaf Upazila (Whyknog-13, Hnilla-12). NFI distribution will be completed in UNHCR-supported camps according to the S-CCCM sector’s guidance, recommendations, and needs. IRB has a plan to distribute NFIs among 5000 (900 in camp-3, 900 in camp-4, 900 in camp 2W, 900 in camp 2E, and 1400 in Camp 7) extremely vulnerable Rohingya families living in respective 05 camps and another 1500 (300 HHs in Ratna Palong, 300 Jalia Palong, 300 Haldia Palong, 300 HHs in Whykong, and 300 HHs in Nhilla Union) NFI packages for the vulnerable households in the host communities in Cox’s Bazar district. This proposed project is designed for JRP -2025 (January 2025- December 2025), and continuity will depend on donor funds availability for 2026.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

About 1,010,298 (including 50,000 new arrivals) Forcibly Displaced Myanmar Nationals (FDMNs) are residing in 33 camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas of Cox’s Bazar district. As of 31st October 2024, the Joint Response Plan (JRP-2024) for the Rohingya refugee response is 53% funded, with USD 452.1 M received out of an appeal of USD 852.4M. In the Shelter-CCCM sector, USD 23.4 M (14.87% of the required USD 157.6 M) has been received as of 31st October 2024. There are funding gaps for ongoing activities at the FDMN camps. Islamic Relief Bangladesh (IRB) has identified 13 blocks under Camp 2 East, 2 West, 3, 4, and Camp 7 for expanding Shelter/NFI interventions through active engagement in the rationalization process and coordination with UNCHR, ISCG, and sectors. The FDMNs are fully dependent on the assistance provided by the humanitarian community. The 2023 Multi Sector Needs Assessment found that 72% of HHs report damage in their shelters with 41% unable to improve them, mostly due to a lack of humanitarian support. Shelter repairs are essential as the alternative shelter options await large-scale approval. The most commonly reported shelter issues were leaks during rain (39%), damage to walls (35%), and minor damage to the roof (35%). Overall, 97% of the households reported NFI needs, most commonly blankets (45%), kitchen sets (53% of the households), and mosquito nets (58% of the households).In the camps, shelters are exposed to cyclic household levels risk of floods, landslides, fire, and cyclones. Temporary materials such as bamboo and tarpaulin have a limited capacity to resist weather impacts and thus require regular repairs and replacement. The use of adequate material (treated bamboo, good quality tarpaulin) along with the design, site plan, and proper technical details for the materials connections, can improve the lifespan of materials if properly followed. Training for the beneficiaries on how to repair and maintain their shelters is also one of the essential elements of shelter assistance to ensure less dependency on humanitarian support. Following the gap analysis, four types of activities are the most shelter needs in the camp context. New improved shelter construction, Regular repair and maintenance support, Emergency shelter materials distribution, and Household-level site development works. Improved shelters include transitional shelters, Mid-Term shelters, Steel frame Shelters, and Shelters with TSA support. As different materials are being used for the different types of improved shelter, the quality and quantity also differ, and most of them still require regular repair and maintenance. According to the gap analysis from the Shelter-CCCM sector, 52% of the refugee households informed that they received shelter assistance last year, while 48 % did not. According to ISNA, 75% of households got shelter repair & maintenance last year. 43% shelter repair & maintenance & 3.39 % new shelters will be required for 2025. Out of 33 camps, in 22 camps, the percentage of receiving shelter assistance is more than 50%. About 52% of shelters that were assessed had shelters standing on a plinth. 53% of shelters did not meet this standard for treated bamboo and visible signs of infestation, 15% met it partially, and only 22 % of shelters were constructed with treated bamboo. Considering the sustainability & durability of MTS and TSA shelters, there are requirements for Treated bamboo and pad footings for all MTS and TSA support in the respective 13 blocks under the proposed five camps. Household level Site Development work is also needed. While providing the Improved Shelter Materials, there is a need for Site Development in every household. “Shelters need to be improved” is considered as the GAP for household-level Site Development activities. These activities include slope protection, footpaths, and drains, among others. On the other hand, Permanent shelters and NFIs are the most demanding needs for the host community.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will apply protection principles across all stages to safeguard girls, women, children, and vulnerable groups. Islamic Relief Bangladesh (IRB) is already providing shelter and NFI interventions in the same camps where this project will also be implemented. Every shelter activity will be guided by a protection lens, with staff well-oriented in protection mainstreaming to recognize and mitigate protection risks. Our protection mainstreaming involves integrating protection principles to ensure access, safety, and dignity in FDMN camps. To ensure accountability, Islamic Relief has established complaints and Feedback mechanisms for affected populations to assess interventions, voice concerns, and seek resolutions. This project will ensure information about PSEA, including rules and code of conduct for humanitarian workers has to exhibit clearly in a variety of formats in distribution points. Distribution point staff and volunteers will be trained on PSEA referrals and the agency’s code of conduct. IRB shelter project will consider extremely vulnerable rights holders, and consult HHs on specific changes required in their shelter to ensure accessibility. Include porter and construction labor support to extremely vulnerable FDMN HHs. IRB will arrange training on gender, GBV, women’s/human rights, code of conduct, and PSEA for all staff and volunteers of the shelter/NFI support project. This project will emphasize the importance of privacy and dignity during shelter construction, constructing new shelters or reinforcing existing shelters, consult individual households for a ventilation design that meets privacy needs, and include women and adolescents in the consultation. Through adherence to "Do No Harm" and "Leave No One Behind" principles, the project aims to create a safe, inclusive environment that addresses protection risks and empowers rights holders within the project framework.

2.3 - Age & Disability

This shelter and NFI support project will emphasize the needs of extremely vulnerable individuals of different ages such as adolescent girls, elderly people, and persons with disabilities during the implementation of Shelter and NFI activities including emergency and non-emergency shelters, and NFI distributions. As part of the shelter and site planning, it is considered the specific risks faced by women, girls, and persons with disabilities. For example, during shelter kit distributions, the locations of distributions will be taken into consideration for the different access needs of the rights-holders. The project will prioritize age, diversity, and disability inclusion across all activities to ensure that every rights holder has equitable access to shelter & NFI services. During the project implementation, the team will actively include persons with disabilities in all project activities and ensure they are aware of relevant risks, similar to other rights holders. Our project team will collect SADDD (Sex, Age, and Disability, Desegregated Data) information for this project’s activity implementation. Targeted activities will be developed for various age groups; for instance, youth-specific shelter volunteer involvement will address the unique needs and interests of young people within the project. Additionally, the project will aim for at least 3% of all rights holders to be individuals with disabilities, ensuring meaningful participation and representation.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The shelter project will prioritize and provide the Shelter/ NFI assistance considering the right holder's needs. Ensure accountable practice shares communications about distribution dates, locations, modality, selection criteria, and package materials with SMS and CiCs. To ensure accountability to the affected population, the Project will orient staff and volunteers on the Complaints Feedback Mechanism (CFM). The MEAL team will actively gather community feedback through FGDs, and consultations. To strengthen the Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM), the Project will deploy CFM boxes and provide accessible contact information in local languages at key community locations. This will enable beneficiaries to voice concerns effectively. To empower the community, the Project team will involve community representatives in every initiative of project implementation. In addition, the monitoring and evaluation process will involve relevant stakeholders to work together on improving the project. This approach will strengthen community involvement and ownership. IRB’s field team will regularly monitor the affected population’s perception (including adequacy, quality, timely information provision, and engagement in decision-making) through existing Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) and perception surveys. The Project team will ensure that all common messages and information education communication (IEC) materials are available in English and Rohingya languages. For age, gender, and disability inclusion, the Project will ensure that all demographic groups are included, and their specific needs will be addressed at each step of AAP planning and implementation.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The project will orient and ensure that 100% of project staff (frontline staff, enumerators, volunteers, senior management) and related personnel (including vendors, suppliers, and third parties) receive mandatory PSEA training (including regular refreshers). We must mainstream PSEA in all of our project activities and stages of the project cycle to ensure proper prevention. IEC materials on PSEA will be displayed at dedicated camp offices/ distribution points. A designated PSEA focal person (in each camp) will be available for community members and rights holders to report cases and receive immediate support. A robust mechanism will be in place to address and respond to PSEA issues effectively. As a member of the PSEA network in Cox’s Bazar, IRB will adhere to all guidelines provided by the network and follow its protocols closely. IRB will ensure any SEA allegation, investigate properly, and take necessary actions in line with standard operating procedures (SOP) for SEA complaint referral in the Rohingya response. We will identify risks that must be addressed with the technical support of the PSEA network. PSEA awareness materials (such as posters) including key PSEA messages, and the available CFM will be visible to beneficiaries. The project will ensure that 100% of project beneficiaries will be aware of PSEA and reporting mechanisms. IRB will also ensure that 100% of project staff receive training on how to receive and refer SEA disclosures safely, respectfully, and confidentially. This will ensure safe, accessible, confidential, culturally appropriate, and victim-centered reporting channels for sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment, tailored to beneficiaries' needs and preferences. All contractors, vendors, and third parties engaged in this project will sign the code of conduct and abide by PSEA principles.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

In Cox’s Bazar, the frequency and intensity of environmental hazards, exacerbated by climate change, pose significant risks. This project aims to enhance emergency preparedness and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) efforts while addressing climate change and environmental protection in FDMN camps In refugee camps, floods being the primary driver, recorded the highest number of disaster displacements and faced escalating climate-related threats, impacting camp life, safety during irregular movement, and prospects for return. Rohingya camps are vulnerable to extreme weather events, with urgent climate mitigation and disaster preparedness measures needed to mitigate risks such as cyclones and landslides. Climate change adaptation and mitigation activities are incorporated into the project, including disaster resilience and adaptation activities i.e. household-level slope stabilization work, protection wall construction, disaster resilient shelter construction, pad footing, metal bracing, treated bamboo, etc. This project also aims to reduce social and environmental detriment by providing environment-friendly and climate-resilient building materials to protect from cyclones, heavy floods, landslides, earthquakes, dots, soil erosion, etc. Community people will be aware of the consequences result of climate change and its effects through community awareness sessions. Regular awareness sessions with the community will focus on building resilience to potential climate and environmental risks, empowering individuals to take proactive steps in protecting their surroundings during emergencies. This project provides emergency disaster-resilient shelter kits for the affected and suffering rights holders to settle, mitigate & adapt to climate changes, especially in climate hazard-prone areas, exacerbating the risk of climate-related movement on a larger scale.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, in the event of emergencies within the camps, the project team will coordinate closely with the Emergency Preparedness and Response Unit to ensure a swift and effective response and provide joint and coordinated support to the affected community. To enhance community resilience before, during, and after emergencies, the project will conduct shelter damage assessment, and provide shelter support that informs community members of disaster risks that may intensify in crises. These will include guidance on emergency response during disasters, reporting mechanisms, and safe access to services, helping to reduce vulnerabilities associated with emergency conditions. Through these efforts, the project aims to support the Rohingya community with safe shelter facilities. To ensure emergency preparedness and response (EPR), the project has kept a provision of contingency funds.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project actively engages youth aged 15 to 24 in various volunteer activities to support the FDMN communities. Youth will participate in shelter construction and site development works. They will also join community consultation meetings alongside local representatives, promoting a shared commitment to shelter support. Through capacity-building training and consistent shelter construction activities, youth will be empowered to play a key role in contributing their shelter construction within their communities. Additionally, youth will take part in beneficiary mobilization activities that will aim to equip young people as proactive contributors to a safer living environment. Above all, the project has a dedicated team including community mobilizers, field facilitators, and community volunteers who will contribute and engage in joint response in any emergency that occurs in camps. This project will create participatory approaches that actively engage youth in project design, implementation, and monitoring, ensuring their voices are heard and recognized. To reflect the youth’s participation in the project cycle, this project will focus on cash-for-work initiatives during shelter construction. The project will develop age-appropriate youth engagement strategies. IRB will ensure specialized training for staff and volunteers who work directly with adolescents and youth (15-24 years old) on how to effectively and appropriately communicate with this age group.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project promotes resilience by providing shelter materials, beneficiary built their shelters after receiving training from IRB’s shelter team. This project will focus on regular shelter repair and maintenance, site development, and DRR support. The improved shelter designs will not only provide critically needed fire and weather resistance but will also consider the local availability of the proposed materials and technical capacity for safe installation. Resilient shelters will ensure cost savings and the sustainability of the refugee response in the context of reducing humanitarian funding. In addition, this project will enhance the capacity of the refugee community along with strengthening camp governance will promote refugees' self-reliance and resilience. All the project activities will empower refugees to gradually reduce dependency on external aid and enhance their capacity to cope with future challenges. This project will help the beneficiaries become self-reliant.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the influx exacerbated issues for a region already suffering from poverty, lack of livelihoods, and limited access to infrastructure and basic services, leading to tensions over jobs and resources in host communities. Considering the severity of the affected people, this project aims to provide host community development activities for increasing social well-being & reducing the suffering of affected people. Disaster-resilient shelters will be constructed for the most vulnerable host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila. Dignified NFI items will be provided through this project which include mosquito nets, blankets, health hygiene kits, and pitchers. Primarily, the UNO office will provide a host community permanent shelter beneficiary list in consultation with the DC office.
