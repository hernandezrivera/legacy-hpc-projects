Project Summary

Aligned with the objectives of the 2024 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for South Sudan, IMC's healthcare interventions are designed to; Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality healthcare services including maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services to approximately 996,627 crisis-affected populations in 5 states across South Sudan including Central Equatoria, Western Bhar El Ghazal, Unity, Upper Nile, and Jonglei States. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic-prone diseases; Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.
From 2022 to 2023 IMC expanded its areas of operation to include Unity State, thus, increasing the cumulative number of supported health facilities from 23 to 75 across all IMC sites. To achieve the above objectives, IMC will continue to provide primary and Secondary health care services integrated with Nutrition, SGBV and MHPSS across its 75 supported health facilities in the states of Central Equatoria (Juba IDP Camps, Khor Williang PHCC), Western Bahr El Ghazal (Wau IDP camp), Jonglei (Canal/Pigi County), Upper Nile (Malakal PoC and County, and Akoka, Balliet and Panyikang Counties), and Unity State (Panyijar, Koch, Leer, and Mayendit Counties). IMC projects will continue to provide essential care services including Integrated Management of Neonatal and Childhood Illness (IMNCI), outpatient consultation for communicable and non-communicable diseases, endemic diseases, trauma, injuries, community-based education at the facility, community events, gatherings, and house-to-house visits.
IMC will contribute to the Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) at static health facilities and community mobile outreaches while Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) services will continue to be provided at both the primary and secondary healthcare levels.
In line with the existing structure of the South Sudan health system, IMC will continue implementing health projects through primary and secondary healthcare facilities, Mobile outreaches, and community-based interventions to ensure access, availability, and utilization of appropriate essential health services.
IMC will ensure access to treatment and care for chronic diseases like diabetes, hypertension, and other cardiovascular diseases, and asthma. Trauma and injury-related patients and potential mass-casualties will be provided with good services in all IMC proposed project locations. With the expanding capacity of IMC in the field of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), IMC mental health specialists and officers will ensure appropriate identification, intervention, referral, and regular follow-up will be provided for persons with mental, neurological, and substance use disorders. Mental health and general health staff will receive training in mhGAP-HIP guidelines, including detection and referrals.
Provision of integrated mental health and psychosocial support services, including psychiatric emergency services for in- and outpatients in all static health facilities through skilled MHPSS Workers. IMC and MOH MHPSS workers trained on mhGAP-HIG will conduct regular field visits to follow up on all cases treated in facilities to ensure the quality of case management and provide with routine supportive supervision by field mental health officers, senior mental health officer, and MPHSS specialist. The facilities will also be supported with essential psychotropic medicines. MC will provide Community health education and social-behavioral change communication for disease prevention and health promotion activities; main activities will include providing community health workers training to deliver appropriate education and awareness activities on crucial health, nutrition, and hygiene messages.

1. Needs

The 2023 humanitarian needs in South Sudan remain concerning when compared to 2022. Gaps in access and use of health services persist and drive unsatisfactory healthcare indicators. The neonatal, infant, and under-five mortality rates remain among the highest globally at 39.63, 63.76, and 98.69 per 1000 live births respectively (2023-2027 HSSP). Current estimates indicate that South Sudan has a maternal mortality ratio of over 1,223 per 100,000 live births – one of the world's highest maternal mortality ratios (MMR) (MMEIG, 2022). The MMR translated into about 10 deaths daily in 2020. Available data shows that the main causes of maternal deaths in South Sudan were postpartum hemorrhage (16% of cases), obstructed/prolonged labor (13.3%), and antepartum hemorrhage (12.9 %) (Ministry of Health, 2013). A shortage of human resources and skills, referral constraints, financial constraints, challenges with availability and last-mile distribution of supplies, floods, and insecurity characterize the weak health system at both facility and community levels. The impacts of these factors on the health system are further worsened by the Sudan conflict which has resulted in an influx of over 500,000 refugees and returnees into already vulnerable communities with very weak health systems. It is estimated that only 44% of the population in South Sudan live within reach of health facilities and have consistent access to primary care services. According to the 2022 HNO, insecurity is the main barrier to accessing health services in South Sudan, and according to the multisectoral need assessment conducted by IMC in December 2022, Lack of transportation, long distance to health facilities, high cost of transportation, inadequate medicines, and shortage of health personnel were the main barriers to access and utilization of care in Canal Pigi where IMC remains the only organization providing health services for the whole county According to the need assessment mentioned above, there is still a substantial health services gap in IMC operational areas that has led to low MCH indicators, the survey showed a 48% home delivery, and 59% ANC attendance rates, including unavailability of some health services in remote areas, limited referral options, poor drug storage facilities and shortage of essential medicine and basic equipment. These gaps have made it difficult to meet additional health needs posed by the influx of returnees, conflict, and flood displacement. In terms of disease burden, it has been shown that preventable communicable diseases continue to be the top causes of morbidity and mortality among the general population, especially among the vulnerable internally displaced people within the camps. This is revealed in the analysis of IDSR, and DHIS2 data which showed that malaria, acute respiratory tract infections, and diarrheal diseases remain the top causes of morbidity and mortality within South Sudan. Malaria continues to be the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounting for 64 percent of all morbidities and 45 percent of all mortalities in South Sudan (HSSP 2023-2027). Based on IMC Semi-Annual data (1st April 2023 - 30th September 2023) obtained from IMC-supported outpatient clinics, communicable diseases (Malaria, Diarrheal diseases, and Acute respiratory infections) accounted for 81% of all OPD consultations. IMC data from the Juba and Malakal IDP camps shows an increasing trend of communicable disease consultations, accounting for 66% of OPD consultations in 2021, 77% in 2022, and now 81% of all OPD consultations in 2023. This indicates the significant disease burden posed by preventable communicable diseases in South Sudan, especially among the displaced population that benefits from IMC health services in Juba and Malakal IDP camps. The 2023 increase in communicable disease consultations might be related to the influx of over 500,000 refugees and returnees fleeing conflict in Sudan.

2. Project activities

Curative and Prevention of communicable diseases: IMC will provide outpatient services in all IMC-supported health facilities and mobile outreach to treat communicable diseases, especially the leading communicable diseases, including Malaria, diarrhea, and acute respiratory infections. Using the Integrated Disease Surveillance and Reporting mechanism (IDSR) IMC will closely monitor diseases of public health importance through community outreach and the Boma health workers. Together with the MoH, data collected will be analyzed and relevant action taken in coordination with the health cluster and WHO. Non-Communicable Diseases: IMC will provide consultations for NCDs, prepositioning and distribution of essential medicines for NCDs in accordance with MOH clinical guidelines will liaise with the WHO in the training of healthcare providers on the prevention and management of NCDs as per national guidelines,24/7 clinical care for people with priority mental, neurological and substance use disorders in the static health facilities, and referrals from MMUs to health facilities, as well as provision of consultations and care for trauma-related injuries. Sexual and Reproductive Health: IMC will ensure the provision of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) of life-saving sexual and reproductive health services, including Antenatal Care, BeMONC, CEmONC, health facility delivery, postnatal care services, family planning services, and STI prevention/protection. IMC will invest in improving on the quality of care provided in all sites, targeting better IPC (Infection Prevention and Control) measures, upgrading maternity and operating theatres for CEMONC interventions, facilitating referrals for higher levels of care, distribution of clean delivery kits to all pregnant women in their third trimesters during mobile units outreaches; provision of CMR (Clinical Management of Rape) services; prepositioning of PEP Kits; provision of FP (Family Planning) services with special focus on family planning counseling, and reproductive health outreach through CHWs.MHPSS: Provision of integrated mental health and psychosocial support services, including psychiatric emergency services for both in- and outpatients in all static health facilities through skilled MHPSS Workers. IMC's MHPSS workers trained on mhGAP-HIG will conduct regular field visits to follow up on all cases treated in facilities to ensure the quality of case management. IMC and MoH clinical staff will be trained on selected priority mental, neurological, and substance use disorders, and will be provided with routine supportive supervision. Community Health Services: Community health education and social-behavioral Change communication improves disease prevention and health promotion activities. IMC will partner with the MoH at all levels to provide training for community health workers on providing appropriate education and awareness activities on key health, nutrition, and hygiene messages. Promote referrals of pregnant women to health facilities for delivery and postnatal services. Provide Information, Education & Communication/ Social, and Behavior Change (IEC/SBC) materials on key messages for dissemination at the community level.Emergency Preparedness and Response: IMC has an IDSR system on the ground for early identification of disease outbreaks. IMC will increase the number of community health workers and form an Emergency Response Rapid Team (ERRT), that can quickly respond to health emergencies/potential outbreaks, including Cholera, AWD, Measles, COVID-19, Ebola, and conflict-related injuries/wounds, as well as to natural calamities, such as flooding. Higher Level of Care: IMC will continue to provide advanced emergency obstetric and newborn care, trauma care, trauma surgery, and rehabilitation for major injuries and basic surgical care at the Operating Theatres located in Malakal POC, Malakal Teaching Hospital, and Khor Williang Health Facility.
