Project Summary

Education has been at the heart of the conflict in NWSW Cameroon, and over 90% of schools have closed for several years over the course of conflict. In basic education, the gross pre-school enrolment rate dropped from 39% in 2017 to 36,8% in 2018. This decline was due to the instability in the south west and North West regions which was at its peak with serious disruption in the functioning of several schools. In 2020, only 23% of primary schools were operational (HNO, 2021). Attacks on pupils, school personnel and infrastructures in the North-West and South-West had a devastating impact on access to learning institutions. In addition, deaths, injuries, psychological damages abductions, and threats caused by the attacks resulted in a decline in student attendance and education quality.
As of now, the space for education has opened up, and children can now access learning in formal institutions. This is the case of Tombel which is a buffer zone for it 72 neighboring villages. Internally Displaced Pupils who have excitedly gained access to formal education have caused severe overcrowding in these schools. Due to this increase access of pupils in school, teacher pupil ratio has increase with pupil per classroom ratios of up to 200 children (HN0, 2021). This has greatly caused a drop in the quality of learning. Though data on learning levels is not available, the majority of children are severely lacking in foundational learning skills after having missed up to five years of schooling
To date, education projects in NWSW Cameroon have largely focused on access to education over quality of learning. The approach of this project, which aims to improve access to education but also ensure that children are acquiring foundational skills that are life-saving and life-sustaining in a conflict-affected context, is therefore innovative.
Secondly, education projects implemented in this context to date have often relied on distance teaching and learning and the use of technology such as radios and tablets. These approaches are not sustainable given the low numbers of households with access to technologies in Tombel. TaRL is innovative in its use of local resources and capacity for teaching and learning – an approach which is both more cost-effective and more sustainable in the context where the absence of state capacity means communities have a critical role in re-establishing education, and where building community capacity is crucial to improving learning at scale. Teaching at the Right Level is an appropriate approach for addressing learning loss at scale.
NADEV seeks to improve the quality of education learners receive by introducing the Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) methodology. TaRL offers a simple, scalable and high-impact solution with the proven potential to address critical gaps in life-saving, life-sustaining learning for these children. Teaching at the Right Level is an innovative intervention in the complex context of Cameroon, as it (i) shifts focus from access to schooling to access to learning; (ii) offers a localized, low-cost approach to introducing learning in hard-to-reach conflict-affected areas; and (ii) has the potential to galvanize the acquisition of foundational skills at scale for children who have been out of school for up to 5 years. It will be one of the first projects to measure learning outcomes in this context. This is of particular interest to members of the NWSW Education Cluster, particularly for local actors able to take TaRL to hard-to-reach areas where there are no alternative learning opportunities.
Again, teachers and students in formal school settings also face barriers to effective teaching and learning. Teachers struggle to meet the varying ability levels in their classrooms, a key challenge given the high rates of displaced and conflict-affected children who are re-entering the formal school system after missing several years of schooling, and the varying levels of education.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Education is the gateway for every child to realized their future purposes but this is not so in the south west and North West regions of Cameroon. Due to the crisis, most children have lost up to 5 years of schooling. As a result of this, most families have been displaced to buffer zones leading to overcrowding in many schools thereby causing a huge drop in learning quality. The space for education has opened up, and children can now access learning in formal institutions but majority of children are severely lacking in foundational literacy and numeracy learning skills. Also, these pupils have inadequate basic materials, teachers are insufficient and do not have appropriate didactic materials, those of them who are volunteers do not have remuneration. Teachers do not master the appropriate methodology to address this challenge, they tend to go along with the more performing pupils with the risk of leaving many of the pupils behind. In this context, the quality of education is compromised and the schools may even close. So, TaRL is the gateway to reduce these gaps by improving the foundational skills of 720 learners,were we are going to 432girls and 288boys for the project,24 teachers(8 males and 16 females),3 teachers per school will be trained on the TaRL methodology and will receive stipend at the end of the month.This project will seek to work with 90 learners in 8 schools making of total of 720 learners that will improve on their foundational skills at the end of 4 months. Activities• Community entry• Sensitise school heads on project and selection of beneficiary• Enrol 720 pupils in catch up foundational classes• Train 24 teachers on TaRL methodology• Provide basic learning materials for 720 pupils• Provide teaching materials for 24 teachers• Provide daily snacks to learners• Purchase and distribution of teaching and learning material. • Provide monthly stipend to teachers.• Monitoring and evaluation
