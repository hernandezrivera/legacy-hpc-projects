Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to increase the resilience of vulnerable, low
resilience farming households in the ARA in the Gaza Strip to prepare for, withstand and
adequately recover from IHL violations that impact their ability to sustain an independent,
dignified and productive living. This project will target the most vulnerable farmers /women whose productive assets are in need of protection/restoration in order to facilitate economic
recovery. Agriculture based livelihoods will be supported and promoted by increasing
agricultural production and productivity among low resilience female and male led
households in the ARA through the rehabilitation and reconstruction of key agricultural
infrastructure and assets. In doing so, this project will contribute to socio economic
stabilization and will increase the resilience of farming households and communities
through increased income and subsequent reduction in vulnerability and aid dependency.
This project contributes to Strategic Objective 3 of the HRP 2018 2020,And Food Security Cluster objective 1 "The resilience , and food security and productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored, enhanced and livelihoods protected". and is planned as
a one year project as there is a pressing need for such an emergency intervention. This
one year intervention will contribute to socio-economic stabilization and will increase the
resilience of farming households and communities through increased income and
subsequent reduction in vulnerability and aid dependency.

1 - Needs

Farmers in the ARA are constantly affected and suffering from limited access to their lands. The recent events in Gaza and particularly the Great March of Return (GMR) are causing damages to the land of ARA farmers. According to the Ministry of Agriculture, about 400 farmers have been directly affected due to the (GMR) demonstrations since March 2018, and about 20% of arable lands are still not planted. Low resilience farmers in the ARA typically lack the financial resources required to rehabilitate agricultural land in order to resume normal levels of cultivation. An additional restriction faced by farmers in the ARA is that placed on the height of crops -only crops up to 80 centimeters in height are permitted within the ARA by the Israeli authorities. As a result, farmers are prevented from replanting fruit trees removed during land leveling incursions by Israeli forces and must depend solely upon vegetables and other crops, which yield a lower return in markets, for their income. When compared with perennial crops (trees), annual crops (e.g. vegetables, cereals and legumes) require significantly more work and services on a daily basis, a role that is performed mainly by women and children. Women are also typically responsible for selling the crops at market. Taking this into consideration, improving the production and productivity in the ARA will result in a lesser work burden on women coupled with higher income for them and their families. Low resilience farmers in the ARA are characterized by limited access to resources and public services, low daily income (average NIS 9/USD 2.5 per adult per day) and a high percentage - often over 50 per cent - of income spent on household food consumption. Moreover, according to the findings of a field assessment conducted by FAO in September 2017 in response to Gaza’s deepening electricity crisis, farming communities in the ARA in the Rafah and Khan Yunis governorates reported that input costs for planting one dunum had increased by 25 percent as a result of the increasing electricity deficit, leading to a reduction in the area planted by 40 50 percent. Overall, low resilience farming households are more exposed to protection risks, have limited capacity to absorb and respond to shocks, and are more likely to employ negative coping strategies than their more resilient counterparts. Women farmers are most impacted by these as they bear ever increasing levels of responsibility both inside and outside the home. According to the Gender Alert Report (2015) by UN Women and OCHA, women’s limited share of assets makes them increasingly vulnerable during difficult economic times. Overall, the work of rural women in the agricultural sector is under recognized and women farmers are considered to be “helpers” regardless of the level of responsibility they have for the household’s agricultural work. Data from PCBS indicate that 18.2% of economically active women work in agriculture and constitute 30% of agricultural employees in Gaza, thereby masking biases that exist with regard to recognizing the contribution of rural women in agricultural labour including as unpaid family workers in this sector.

2 - Activities

As FAO’s primary government counterpart, the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) will be consulted and closely coordinated with before and throughout project implementation. Through this project, FAO will provide time critical, in kind and technical support to targeted farmers and women farmers through the rehabilitation and valorization of land and greenhouses, rehabilitation/reconstruction of critical water facilities and provision of fruit fly traps. In order to restore and protect agricultural production and productivity in the ARA, FAO will rehabilitate 1 000 dunums of arable land for the purpose of cultivating vegetables and other field crops (to be determined by analysis of environmental and physical parameters). Each targeted dunum will be provided with essential nutrient elements in the form of organic fertilizers and basic chemical fertilizers. A further 200 dunums of greenhouses will be targeted for rehabilitation with in kind inputs by way of essential plastic sheeting, insect proof agricultural nets and the required wood paneling for a fixing the sheeting and nets. And support the treatment and prevention of the olive knot disease outbreak by targeting 500 out of 3,850 dunums of olive trees on Northern Gaza governorate as well as conducting a campaign with farmers in North Gaza and Gaza about the best practices on preventing the spread of the disease further.All project activities have been designed in consultation with the communities to be targeted by this project in order to respond to the needs expressed by the targeted communities which were subsequently verified by FAO field staff through continuous consultations, field visits and daily interactions. Prior to project implementation, in each of the targeted communities Local Committees, engaging beneficiary representatives, local authorities and the MoA, will be formed to participate in the beneficiary selection process, to provide support during distribution of inputs, and to assist the FAO project team with follow up activities. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention proposed by FAO aims to support marginalized and vulnerable farmers whose livelihoods are under the threat. The selection of farmers is solely based on needs and the most food-insecure and does not consider any other difference or discriminatory factors. The interventions proposed under this project do not put beneficiaries in risk, however, FAO aims to ensure that all efforts will be made to prevent and minimize as much as possible unintended negative effects of the interventions. As part of the project inception phase, FAO will develop a risk log identifying all potential risks, their probability and ways of mitigation. The assessment and analysis of risks will be followed by a coordinated application of resources to monitor, minimize and control the probability and/or impact of risks. External risk factors will continuously be considered in order to ensure an adequate understanding of the context of FAO operations during the implementation of the project. Through FAO’s participation in the UN Country Team (UNCT) and Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), the development of the political situation in the WBGS will regularly be monitored and reviewed in line with project objectives. FAO contributes to policy-level initiatives to protect Palestinians from further reduction in access to land, sea and livelihood resources (e.g. water and agricultural inputs) owing to the political situation, through its participation in the UNCT Commendation Group and HCT Advocacy Working Group. All advocacy efforts will be conducted under the banner of the UN in the WBGS to ensure that FAO’s mandate is not compromised.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

FAO’s approach provides a framework for addressing and integrating issues such as gender equality, protection, the inclusion of the aged and people living with disability, and the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse by FAO and partner staff. Throughout the process of beneficiary selection, disability and age will be a factor taken into consideration. Beneficiaries with this vulnerability will be prioritized. The project also seeks to engage with targeted stakeholders and mainstream FAO’s commitments in each phase of the project cycle. FAO maintains its commitments through regular stakeholder engagement and participation, two-way communication, transparent disclosure and a grievance mechanism.
