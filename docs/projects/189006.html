Project Summary

Through the proposed humanitarian responses, International Medical Corps (IMC UK) will provide integrated lifesaving nutrition service to conflict affected and displaced communities in Juba IDP Camps, Panyijar, Malakal and Canal Pigi Counties. With the aim to reduce suffering, morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable populations, IMC will increase equitable access and utilization of comprehensive treatment and preventive nutrition specific services, which are essential for addressing key drivers of acute malnutrition affecting children and women in the aforesaid project locations. IMC UK will also continue to address prevention of malnutrition in children under two years of age through promotion of appropriate Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition Feeding (MIYCN) practices. The service will be delivered through static and outreach sites for remote and hard to reach areas in the targeted project locations. The proposed response will include all components of Community Management of Acute Malnutrition, including Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP), Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP), Stabilization Center (SC) and community mobilization activities. International Medical Corps UK proposes to operate 22 OTPs, 22 Targeted supplementary feeding programs (TSFPs), and 2 SCs across the project sites. IMC UK will work with the Ministry of Health (MoH), UNICEF and WFP to secure timely provision of nutrition supplies, which are necessary for nutrition service delivery. IMC will reach approximately 105,899 individuals, of which 50,831 are IDPs. All project activities will be implemented in line with adapted guidelines for nutrition services delivery in the context of COVID-19 provided by the Nutrition Cluster.
IMC UK will monitor the nutrition status of the population and the nutrition response through routine nutrition program data, quarterly MUAC screening campaigns and population-based SMART Surveys. IMC UK, with its already established Nutrition Surveillance Team, will undertake nutrition assessments in its operational locations and support to undertake in nutrition assessments in Priority counties as well to provide robust information on the nutrition situation of a given population, this information will be key in responding effectively to nutrition needs in the project area.
Additionally, IMC UK will reinforce and promote access to integrated nutrition, health, WASH, protection and FSL services provision in the project target areas through geographical integration, activity integration, and integration through common approaches to reduce the burden of malnutrition. IMC will also closely work with Nutrition partners in the target location to prevent overlap of the services and enhance services availability through referral and integration. This project aims to enhance nutrition-sensitive interventions that will ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services; to further decrease the prevalence of acute malnutrition, and ultimately, to contribute to the prevention and reduction of morbidity and mortality associated to malnutrition in South Sudan. IMC will also support hygiene interventions to improve nutrition outcomes and reduce risks of hygiene-related morbidities. Nutrition services will be considered as potential entry points for GBV survivors seeking assistance, with nutrition staff trained on GBV referral pathways and other key GBV concepts. The project protection focal points will perform safety audits and assessments of nutrition sites. Safety audits are one of the key approaches used to identify potential GBV related risks at the program sites using a tested tool. The project will conduct safety audit assessments bi-annually at the health and nutrition sites.

1 - Needs

Malnutrition needs are greatest in Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Eastern Equatoria state, where the number of acutely malnourished children under-five and PLW caseloads are highest, and also Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15%. According HNO, an estimated 2 million including 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and 676,000 pregnant and lactating women, are expected to be acutely malnourished and will need treatment in 2022. The key drivers of acute malnutrition are multifaceted, including severe acute food insecurity, sub-optimal feeding practices, high prevalence of disease, inadequate sanitation conditions and hygiene practices, and limited access to health and nutrition services driven by sub national conflict, floods and COVID-19 related disruptions. Panyijar County, with 120,033 population, continues to face numerous challenges, including insecurity due to cattle raids and revenge killings; the negative impact of COVID-19; high prevalence of malnutrition and chronic food insecurity; increasing morbidity levels particularly malaria, watery diarrhea, and respiratory tract infection diseases; high incidence of gender-based violence, and continued yearly flooding from the Nile River. Findings of the most recent SMART survey conducted by IMC in December 2021 in Greater Panyijar, estimated GAM prevalence at 15.7% (12.5-19.5 95% CI)) and SAM prevalence at 3.3% (2.0- 5.6 95% CI) based on Weight-for-Height. MIYCN care practices continued to be sub-optimal in this location. The breastfeeding indicators were found low in Panyijair County based on WHO recommendation. In Oct 2021, IMC conducted an Integrated Anthropometric and Mortality SMART survey in Malakal PoC, the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) among the surveyed population was estimated at 17.8 % (14.4↔21.8 95% C.I), and the prevalence of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) was 3.2 % (1.9↔5.2 95% C.I) Therefore, the nutritional status among the IDPs in Malakal PoC was classified as very high. The high levels of malnutrition among the population were due to poor access to food, high morbidity burden, exclusion of WHZ admission criteria due to COVID-19 and regular influx of IDPs. On IYCF indicators, the breastfeeding indicators are commendable in Malakal PoC based on WHO recommendation. However, the introduction of solid or semi solid foods is still low which is 75%. Similarly, SMART survey conducted by Concern Worldwide in December 2019 in Juba IDP indicated as critical based on the WHO standards. The GAM prevalence in Juba IDP camps based on weight for height z scores and oedema was 18.3% [14.6  22.6, 95% C.I.], and the SAM prevalence was 3.3% [2.0  5.2, 95% C.I.]. According to the survey, the exclusive breastfeeding rates of 44% was quite below WHO recommended level. Humanitarian needs are most concerning in Canal/Pigi, where GAM rate was reported to be >15% of the threshold according FSNMS result. IMC’s 2019 SMART survey report in Canal/Pigi indicated a prevalence of GAM of 24.3% (19.5-29.8 95% CI), and SAM prevalence of 6.7% (4.6- 9.7 95% CI) which is indicating critical. MIYCN care practices in the county continued to be sub-optimal, with an exclusive breastfeeding rate of 16.7%, minimum dietary diversity among 6-23 months old children of 15.1%, and minimum acceptable diet of 12.6%. The protracted conflict further exacerbated the prevalence of acute malnutrition due to the reduced ability of under 5 children, PLW, and chronically ill persons to meet their nutritional needs due to displacement, increased morbidity caused by disease outbreaks, and lack of access to clean water and sanitation facilities. Hence, immediate response is needed – both curative & preventive – to avert a possible further catastrophe and spike to IPC Phase 5 (famine).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Through this response, IMC will engage the affected population to participate fully in the project design and implementation to ensure communities have clarity about the project, and have their say on the project’s objectives, activities, location, and their entitlements. Feedback and Complaints arising from the communities will be processed, analyzed, and discussed during program management meetings. The meetings will develop responses and action plans to address the complaints and feedback raised. These responses will be provided through the AAP committees in each project location. In addition, the information will be keyed into a complaints and feedback response database for analysis and learning. During monthly/quarterly program review meetings, the information from the database will form part of the meeting reflections and will inform necessary programmatic corrections.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IMC will further strengthen and/or put in place various CBFRM mechanisms that will ensure the most vulnerable members of the community are catered for. IMC ensures accountability to affected population is incorporated from the design phase by consulting affected communities, with equal representation from women, girls, PWDs, elderly, etc., on their specific needs and vulnerabilities, that should be adapted by the project. During project implementation, the project will use different channels, such as AAP monthly meetings, suggestion boxes, hotlines, house-to-house visits, etc., to inform affected communities on their rights and entitlements, and mechanisms available to them to share feedback and complaints. The project will also consult communities on their preferred feedback modalities, and support set up of these channels where they are not functional. The mechanisms will be robust to ensure collection of all forms of complaints including cases of PSEA. Feedback and complaints coming from the various channels will be analyzed, and patterns identified to inform program adaptation where necessary. In addition, regular community consultations will be conducted to ensure participation of communities in the ongoing implementation and M&E activities. These will include assessing level of satisfaction with services provided, unmet needs, identification of lesson learned and recommendations for project improvement. Finally, IMC will monitor community’s perception of and satisfaction with service provision through integration of a set of AAP indicators into the SMART and KAP surveys.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IMC in South Sudan has developed country-specific guidance to protect beneficiaries from exploitation and abuse in humanitarian crisis. This Guidance complements IMC’s global safeguarding policies. All IMC employees have to undergo a training on PSEA and sign a pre-employment commitment against sexual exploitation and abuse, as well as report any suspected cases. IMC will conduct regular awareness raising sessions with the community on PSEA through QAAPCs CNVs, CHWs and other community structures. The project team will incorporate PSEA messages into CBFRM activities, especially during monthly AAP meetings with the community. The project team will also ensure posting/distribution of IEC materials at strategic locations in the community, including how to report PSEA cases. Regular sessions at the health facilities and other services point will be done on a monthly basis. Community focal points will be empowered through trainings to be able to pass on key PSEA messages, jointly with IMC project teams. For sensitive cases that arise, communities will be sensitized to report to the field site manager/designated focal point. The field site manager is responsible to log in the complaints into IMC reporting system, Ethics point, for further investigation. Communities will be sensitized to report any sensitive cases directly to Ethics point reporting systems for investigation. Ethics point contacts will be shared during community sensitizations.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IMC will ensure accountability to affected populations during implementation of this project. Mechanisms such as community feedback and complaints handling systems will be in place to ensure accountability to the affected population. IMC nutrition response is designed to work closely with the communities from project inception through implementation and monitoring of results. The project is going to provide beneficiaries with information about the assistance to be provided. IMC will ensure active consultations with the beneficiaries or affected populations during onsite monitoring, which allows generation of feedback from the beneficiaries. At the onset of the project, IMC will conduct community consultations and sensitizations on the types of nutrition intervention needed to implement, which takes into consideration gender and age group needs. During the nutrition treatment days, mothers will receive explanation about the program, its importance, the distribution process of supplies, and the importance of using therapeutic supplies for its sole purpose. Beneficiary feedback will be received through already existing Quality Accountability to the Affected Population Committees (QAAPC) in all the project sites. Similarly, IMC will conduct beneficiary feedback assessments at the household level that will gauge how beneficiary feel about the project, and their overall satisfaction on the services they received from IMC. IMC will also involve the County Health Department in field visits, giving them chance to meet with beneficiaries and directly get feedback regarding the program. IMC will also organize meeting with Payam leaders on a quarterly basis to inform them on program achievements and challenges and seek support in community mobilization and awareness raising on nutrition-related matters. Additionally, IMC utilizes community-based feedback and response mechanisms (CBFRM) to solicit feedback and receive complaints. IMC’s CBFRM procedures provides guidance on: (1) effective community consultations – building upon community preferences on how to raise concerns and provide feedback; (2) major steps on managing feedback and complaints and how to explain our actions and decisions to the communities (including vulnerable and marginalized people); (3) Clear informed consent - IMC will provide all necessary information on how to manage feedback and complaint process, explain people’s rights and entitlements, as well as key steps that IMC will take to keep confidentiality of sensitive complaints, and (4) Routing and management of sensitive complaints, including SEA and safeguarding, including, protection measures and investigation pathways. CBFRM is aligned closely with the organization’s broader safeguarding and accountability policies; together, these procedures help ensure the systematic use and management of the feedback and complaint handling process across all field locations and programs. Building on the existing feedback and complaints mechanism, IMC will ultimately promote community participation on management and use of the feedback and complaints through community learning meetings, community Help desks, Post distribution Monitoring, AAP meetings and field registers as a safe and user friendly channels to collect, process and respond to the complaints according to IMC and community’ preferences, as well as disseminate information about the project.During program implementation, IMC will continue to enhance these already existing community accountability structures in the interventions guided by IMC South Sudan CBFRM country local procedures. IMC will share relevant program information including information on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure meaningful participation. Regular information on the program performance and monitoring results, staff code of conduct and PSEA reporting lines staff will also be shared. 8.11. Mitigation Measures.

• IMC will maintain alertness on the security situation in all its health facilities and project locations, in close coordination with the camp/community leaders, local and national authorities, and UN agencies. IMC will also maintain its 24/7 security guards across all its supported health facilities, which also help in assessing the daily security situation together with IMC Site Managers and Security Director. IMC has also Security Risks Analysis (SRAs), Standard Operating Procedures (SoPs) and Contingency Plans in all its project locations, which are being updated bi-annually, or as need arise.• IMC will engage the entire community, including marginalized /minority groups, women and girls, youth and elderly people in all stages of the Nutrition program through conducting community consultations, beneficiary feedback assessment, monthly meetings with Quality Accountability of the affected population (QAAP) committees, joint needs assessment, planning, monitoring and review workshops. • IMC will continue to implement all planned activities in full equity, without any discrimination based on race, color, origin, religion, age, gender nor disability. Particular attention will be given to children (boys and girls), women, and men with disabilities and old caregivers (both men and women) in service provision to reduce further suffering, as well in case they have other complications that need specialized care. Full access and preventive activities will also integrate persons with disabilities, elderly and youth. Special adjustments are being done by IMC to accommodate the special needs of PWDs and elderly, such as doing house-to-house visits/consultations to them; ensuring that health facilities are accessible to patients with movement constraints and physical disabilities, supporting rehabilitation where necessary such as ramps and grab bars, among others.• IMC will mitigate GBV risks by scaling up of safety audits at the nutrition centers to identify and address women and children’s protection/safety needs and ensure that nutrition services and centers are not avenues for GBV. IMC ensures that multi-sectoral referral pathways are in place in all its project locations, especially for survivors of protection incidents. Monthly safety audits are being conducted, which results are shared to and discussed with relevant national and local authorities and other NGOs to draw timely, actionable solutions, and provide targeted support to the survivors.• To avoid abnormal population movement for the purpose of getting assistance (humanitarian assistance as a pull factor), IMC will bring the services closer to the beneficiaries, such as setting clear criteria for selecting sites and at the same time increasing number of sites to increase coverage and access. The service provision to the beneficiaries will be on a weekly basis to reduce frequency of beneficiaries’ movement. Treatment will be conducted in health and nutrition facilities, free from military or armed personnel, away from military bases. In addition, the treatment will start in the morning hours and close before dark to allow mothers reach home safely. If allocation has many beneficiaries, then more days will be allocated to serve each section or block to allow enough time to reach and leave the site. • IMC will further strengthen existing and/or establish Quality Accountability of the affected population (QAAP) committees and other CBFRM modalities, which were selected by the community, to ensure that everyone has equitable and easy access to context-specific complaint and feedback mechanisms.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

IMC incorporates protection principles in its all its programs and its daily work by prioritizing the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries served. Consultations and other activities will take place in secure areas that are also easily accessible for groups who are extremely vulnerable and/or with limited mobility. IMC healthcare and nutrition workers will be trained with a focus on gender-based violence prevention and survivor-centered treatment core concepts (clinical management of rape), considering the challenging environment that poses high barriers to vulnerable people, especially women and girls, in terms of reporting GBV cases, as well to counter the general lack of information regarding this topic. IMC’s multi-sectoral health, nutrition and protection project will integrate GBV medical and MHPSS services to survivors, including counselling, management and proper referral for survivors of GBV, including rape survivors. All staff will be trained on protection, GBV Core Concepts and safe referrals. Service mapping and medical referral protocols are in place across IMC’s health facilities and project locations to ensure meaningful and safe referrals, including ensuring patient information shared with third-party service providers is only done so after informed consent of the patient is secured. Furthermore, IMC provides integrated mental health and psychosocial support services, including psychiatric emergency services for both in- and out-patients in all its supported health facilities. Other PSS interventions that IMC provides include psychoeducation, support groups and peer support sessions for the most vulnerable groups, including community leaders, women, children, GBV survivors and PWDs of the displaced and conflict-affected population in the target sites. These are conducted through small group discussions, tea sessions and community outreaches with the aim of bringing vulnerable individuals and group of people together, create social opportunities for their families and care givers of persons with psychosocial disabilities, and the wider community structures, which are tailored to ensure promotion of psychosocial wellbeing is achieved and the human rights of socially disadvantaged vulnerable persons are promoted. In addition, IMC will implement/pilot Self-help -plus (SH+) in all its project locations. SH+ is a stress management intervention based on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) principles, for use by persons and group of people experiencing psychosocial distress and other stress-related conditions.

Through an integrated and comprehensive health, nutrition, FSL,WASH and protection services delivery, and through working with partners, IMC will ensure optimal and sustainable service quality for acute malnutrition management, as well as improving MYCN practices through nutrition education, counseling, facilitation of support groups, complementary feeding and kitchen gardening. A sustained delivery of such interventions will then significantly contribute to the decrease of proportion of populations facing IPC 3, 4 and 5.
