Project Summary

Ten years after independence and three years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, people in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions (South Sudan HNO, 2022). Their situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education and water, sanitation and hygiene and health services. In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. An estimated 2 million people including 1.3 million children under the age 5 and another 675,548 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be acutely malnourished in the year 2022 (Nutrition Cluster 2022). Acute malnutrition has been a protracted problem in South Sudan and one that requires sustained investment for life-saving nutrition service delivery. Based on findings of Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) round 27 of 2021, Budi county had a Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate of 15.4% (SAM=4.0%), Kapoeta East County had 16.6% (SAM=1.8%) while Pibor had a GAM rate of 21.6% (SAM=4.9%). In the same vein, the severity scores shared by the nutrition cluster 2022, all the three counties had a severity score of 5 (high concern). The nutrition situation in all the three counties is expected to deteriorate significantly as a result of unprecedented levels of food insecurity, high morbidity, outbreaks of diarrhea and other illness, poor infant and young feeding practices as well as limited access to services. Those who are malnourished are more vulnerable to common and infectious diseases such as cholera, malaria, diarrhea diseases, acute respiratory infection and measles. Continued scale up of treatment of acute malnutrition targeting the current and future caseloads is of a high priority. GREDO proposes to initiate emergency nutrition response and scale up nutrition activities in Kapoeta East, Pibor and Budi Counties. GREDO seeks to enhance efforts to reduce malnutrition including promoting the broader goals of improving knowledge related to MIYCN practices and health seeking, establishment of kitchen gardening for food diversification as part of the nutrition-sensitive agriculture agenda as well as strengthening active case finding and integrated community outreach program. In order to have high impact response, SAM and MAM management need to be complemented with preventive responses targeting vulnerable and at risk children and PLWs including those with disabilities. This proposal seeks to address nutritional needs of children <5yrs and PLWs affected by acute malnutrition in Budi, Pibor and Kapoeta East counties through both OTP and TSFP. GREDO will be using both static and mobile/outreach response approaches in the three counties provide equitable lifesaving nutrition interventions to women and men, boys and girls at existing sites while increasing and continuing program reach to underserved locations through outreaches. This will include integration of cluster priorities such as malaria testing, NFI distribution, WASH supplies into CMAM and MIYCN activities. GREDO targets to provide quality nutrition services to 5500 children with SAM (1500 in Budi, 2500 in Pibor and 1500 in Kapoeta East), 16000 children with MAM (3000 in Budi, 10000 in Pibor and 3000 in Kapoeta East) and 17500 PLWs (3000 in Budi, 10500 in Pibor and 4000 in Kapoeta East). The project will as well target 20000 caregivers for MIYCN, 40000 children with VAS and 35000 children for deworming.

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview, an estimated 2 million people in South Sudan will be at risk of acute malnutrition, including 302,078 children (148,018 boys and 154,060 girls) with SAM, 1,039,214 children (509,215 boys and 529,999 girls) with MAM, and 675,548 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition (AM-PLW). There is a slight overall increase of 100,000 people (5.2 per cent) in 2022 compared to 1.9 million people who needed treatment for acute malnutrition in 2021. Acute malnutrition has been a protracted problem in South Sudan and one that requires sustained investment in life-saving nutrition services and the scale up of prevention actions to address the major causes of malnutrition. The key drivers of acute malnutrition are multifaceted, including food insecurity, sub-optimal feeding practices, high prevalence of disease, inadequate sanitation conditions and hygiene practices. Based on the severity scores shared by the nutrition cluster 2022, Budi County has a severity score of 4 (high concern), Kapoeta East County has a severity score of 2 (low concern) while Pibor County has a severity scores of 4 (high concern). The nutrition situation in all the three counties is expected to deteriorate significantly as a result of unprecedented levels of food insecurity, high morbidity, outbreaks of diarrhea and other illness, poor infant and young feeding practices as well as limited access to services. This necessitates continued provision of nutrition services through existing OTP and TSFP sites and scaling up services through outreaches. The areas of Ngauro, Loudo and Nagishot Payams in Budi County can be considered hard to reach considering its mountainous topography which has led to a decline in the nutrition situation affecting consumption, access to services and severely disrupting life-saving activities pushing communities deeper into a nutrition emergency. The situation is even worse for Jie Payam in Kapoeta East County in which a recent IRNA report (March 2022) established that there have been displacements within the Payam with the main drivers for this being: Lack of food due to failed rains, poor agricultural practices, and reliance on pastoralist livelihoods; scarce/ very low access to water for household and livestock use (The only water dam in Jie is drying up and water is too dirty for human consumption triggering seasonal migrations that exposes them to conflict with other communities); Insecurity due to cattle raids and child kidnappings orchestrated by the neighboring Murle (in Pibor County) leading to lose of livestock incomes and fear to engage in agricultural and other income activities in the fields. Lack of schools in Jie Payam has perpetuated illiteracy and limited knowledge to engage in meaningful/productive occupations, lack of health facilities on the ground has resulted in moderate malnutrition cases among children and elderly; and families are already facing disintegration due to food burden and the risk of elderly, children and the physically challenged being abandoned is a reality as the energetic population flee to other locations seeking survival. Thousands of people in are likely to face severe hunger due to lack of rainfall and unexpected crop failure according to situation on the ground in Pibor County as the first phase of harvest has gone without food crop yields. The ultimate result of this will be reduced access to food and other services pushing families deeper into malnutrition. Treatment of acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM) continue to be one of key need for saving lives of most at risk children < 5 years and PLWs in these counties. In order to have high impact response, SAM and MAM management need to be complemented with preventive responses targeting vulnerable and at risk children and PLWs.

2 - Project activities

To address the aforementioned needs, GREDO will carry out several activities including but not limited to the following: Active case finding and referral of malnourished children (both SAM and MAM) and PLWs into appropriate nutrition programs for appropriate care. For active case finding, GREDO will adopt the family MUAC (in the context of COVID 19); Active social mobilization to encourage communities to send malnourished children to the nearest nutrition sites. Treatment of both severe and moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among PLWs. Training health and nutrition care providers on management of acute malnutrition. Increase access to IYCF interventions to mothers and caregivers through provision of MIYCN services in nutrition sites and the community. Test and treatment of malaria among children with SAM.Rehabilitate 10 nutrition sites (Nahochod, Kadumakuch, Ngauro, Tulugi, Kathac, Hibongorok, Haliba, Ngatuba, Kapeta and Ngathilani) and ensure quality service delivery is provided through strengthening referral system and linkage through other nutrition services and sectors. Rehabilitate 5 nutrition sites in Pibor with 3 outreaches in Lekuangule in the greater Pibor County. Establish 8 outreach nutrition sites in Jie Payam in Kapoeta East CountyTimely, accurate and credible information sharing of program activities with relevant stakeholders. Ensuring safe and dignified participation by beneficiaries in the program activities. Implement complaint and feedback mechanisms. Conduct safety audits and ensure GREDO’s nutrition sites are safe for her beneficiaries. Conduct one a SMART survey in Budi County and one in Kapoeta East County. GREDO will be implementing minimum package of Integrating Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) within existing health facilities by providing routine treatment using simple medical protocol to SAM and MAM children under 5 and Pregnant and Lactating Mother’s (PLW’s). In addition, GREDO will work towards integrating Health, WASH and FSL into Nutrition targeting both static and mobile outreaches. GREDO will provide these lifesaving nutrition services in collaboration with MoH, partners and local authorities through supporting initiatives for the decentralization of TSFP sites, increasing awareness through community mobilization. GREDO will in essence: - Carry out door-to-door screening (with the adoption of family MUAC in the context of COVID 19) of children and PLWs in the community for malnutrition and routine screening at the health facilities; Advocate for IYCF/MIYCN Practices for prevention of Malnutrition. HRP funding will enable GREDO to provide targeted emergency nutrition supplies and services to a MAM caseload of up to 16000 for under 5 children and 17500 PLW’s, and to a SAM case load of 5500 for the year 2022. GREDO will train CNVs and MIYCN groups from host community to acquire skills and knowledge on Integrated Management of SAM/MAM and MIYCN so as to provide quality nutrition services.Existing health facility staff will be capacitated for integration of Acute Malnutrition management into health programs especially on malaria screening and referral of malnourished children under 5 and PLWs to the OTP/TSFP interventions. GREDO Project focal persons will undergo the mandatory IASC Gender Marker online assessment to ensure clear understanding of best practices of gender in the humanitarian context.GREDO's Health and nutrition staff including clinicians, nutrition assistants, nurses and midwives will be shared in this programme to increase cost effectiveness and strengthen output results.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Humanitarian crises have different impacts on the levels of nutrition available to women, girls, men and boys. Gender inequality for women and girls hampers their ability to access adequate and consistent amounts of nutritious food to meet their own needs as well as those of their families. Prevailing social norms mean that where food is in short supply, women and girls are likely to reduce their food intake, while men and boys are favored. During a humanitarian crisis, the links between nutrition and risk of GBV can become more pronounced. Constraints on women’s mobility can also hamper their access to food distribution sites. Pregnant or lactating women may be disproportionately affected by under-nutrition due to their increased physiological nutritional needs. Single men and boys who have been separated from their families can also be at risk of under nutrition if they are unable to cook or access food distribution. Increased availability of nutritious food coupled with improved access to adequate health and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services reduces levels of acute and chronic malnutrition for women, girls, men and boys. GREDO will seek to effectively integrate gender equality into her nutrition program to achieve the following goals: i) protect the right to security, nutrition and dignity for all and build safer communities. By meeting the nutrition needs of all women, girls, men and boys, programmes reduce the need for crisis-affected people to engage in risky coping strategies such as reducing their nutritional intake or undertaking transactional sex. This in turn reduces associated risks of GBV, exploitation, social stigma, unwanted pregnancies and sexually transmitted infections such as HIV/AIDS. ii) Improve outcomes for children born in crisis contexts. Prioritizing nutrition support for pregnant and lactating mothers and advocating breastfeeding practices (through the MIYCN component of the project) provides the best nutritional and developmental prospects for children born in crisis contexts. In addition, helping families to meet their household nutrition needs can reduce the practice of forced child marriage for girls, a coping strategy to access more food for girls and their families. iii) Respect the right to meaningful participation. Consulting women, girls, men and boys without discrimination on the provision of nutrition services and facilities upholds rights and ensures appropriate service provision. For example, consultation can lead to the creation of infant and young child feeding centers that provide private spaces for breastfeeding mothers and offer a safe haven from harassment and violence. In addition, GREDO will disaggregate project data to understand and measure women and girls’ participation in project activities. GREDO will strengthen leadership and meaningful participation of women in the project by ensuring that women are actively involved throughout the project period. Both men and women will be given equal opportunity to employment and it is expected that at least 50% of all recruited staff to be women. GREDO will ensure that at all times women and men (and girls and boys, as applicable) will participate equally in the implementation, monitoring and evaluation of gender-transformative food security and nutrition policies, programmes and projects;GREDO will advocate for women and girls to have increased leadership and decision-making power regarding food security and nutrition in households, communities and societies; GREDO will ensure its activities do not expose the beneficiaries to sexual abuse and exploitation by ensuring shared amenities are segregated by gender and that GBV survivors supported and referred to GBV response services through nutrition sites
