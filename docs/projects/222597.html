Project Summary

The Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC), will deliver specialized emergency legal protection assistance to displacement-affected populations in climate and conflict impacted areas in South Sudan. This intervention will address the urgent legal needs of individuals and communities who are highly vulnerable due to displacement, conflict, or disasters. It will focus on providing timely and targeted support to mitigate protection risks such as eviction, the loss of legal documentation, housing, land, and property disputes, and the denial of fundamental rights and services. The intervention will reduce barriers that often exacerbate the precarious conditions of target populations, exposing them to further risks, including exploitation, marginalization, and barriers to accessing services.
NRC's approach is centered on ensuring displaced individuals and host communities understand and assert their rights in the face of legal and administrative challenges. Through legal empowerment , capacity building, and advocacy, the intervention will strengthen the capacity of target populations to navigate complex legal systems. A core component of the program is the issuance or recovery of civil documentation, such as birth certificates, ID cards, and property deeds, which are essential for accessing services like healthcare and education. In parallel, NRC will actively engage with local authorities and stakeholders to resolve disputes, prevent unlawful evictions, and advocate for the inclusion of displaced populations in decision making including at the onset of emergencies.
The intervention will prioritize those most at risk, including women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities, who are disproportionately affected by displacement and often face systemic barriers to accessing justice. Legal assistance will be provided in coordination with other sectors to address overlapping vulnerabilities, ensuring holistic support that meets especially immediate and builds the resilience of communities to meet their longer-term needs. Additionally, mobile legal aid teams and community-based paralegals will be deployed to reach remote or undeserved areas, enabling access for those who might otherwise be excluded from legal support mechanisms.
The intervention also integrates holistic support mechanisms by coordinating legal assistance with psychosocial support, case management, and cash-based interventions. This approach aims to address overlapping vulnerabilities, such as economic pressures and trauma.
By addressing immediate legal needs and strengthening the resilience of displaced populations, NRC's emergency legal protection assistance will contribute to reducing vulnerability, enhancing dignity, and fostering stability in fragile settings. The intervention aligns with humanitarian principles, emphasizes accountability, exclusivity, and respect for human rights.

1. Needs

Displacement-affected populations face a range of urgent and critical needs that require specialized legal protection interventions. One of the most pressing issues is the prevention of forced evictions, which threaten the stability and safety of displaced individuals and families. Many are at risk of being unlawfully removed from their land, necessitating legal assistance to negotiate with landlords, community leaders, and authorities to secure their housing rights and prevent eviction. Additionally, disputes over housing, land, and property (HLP) rights are common among displaced populations. These conflicts often arise from unclear ownership, lost documentation, or competing claims, making it essential to provide support for resolving such disputes and ensuring equitable access to housing and property. For those displaced by disasters, securing access to land is a vital need. Negotiating with authorities, host communities, and private landowners is critical to ensure that displaced people can access safe and legally recognized areas for shelter and livelihood activities. Without such agreements, displaced populations face insecurity and instability, which hampers their ability to recover from crises. Another urgent need is access to civil documentation, such as identification cards, birth certificates, marriage records, and property deeds. These documents are essential for accessing healthcare, education, and social services, yet many displaced individuals lose their documentation during crises or face administrative barriers in obtaining replacements. Addressing these challenges is crucial to restoring their legal identity and ensuring they can exercise their rights. Displacement also exacerbates vulnerabilities among specific groups, including women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities. These groups face unique legal challenges, such as gender-based discrimination, and barriers to inheritance. Tailored legal assistance is necessary to address these issues and ensure their protection. Moreover, displaced populations often face exploitation and abuse, including fraud and exploitation due to their precarious legal and social status. Providing legal remedies and raising awareness of rights are essential to safeguarding them from further harm. A significant need is raising awareness among displacement-affected populations about their legal rights and available legal pathways. Many lack knowledge of their rights or how to navigate legal systems, which leaves them vulnerable to exploitation and exclusion. Community-based legal education and counseling can empower individuals to assert their rights and access justice. Furthermore, administrative challenges, including complex and exclusionary processes, frequently hinder displaced populations from accessing legal protections and essential services. Simplifying these processes and providing legal support to navigate them are critical to reducing barriers. Finally, many displacement-affected people reside in remote or undeserved areas, where legal assistance is scarce or non-existent. Mobile legal aid teams and community-based paralegals are essential to extending legal services to these populations, ensuring no one is left behind. Addressing these interconnected needs is vital to protecting the rights, dignity, and stability of displacement-affected populations, enabling them to recover from crises and rebuild their lives.

2. Project activities

The project focuses on protecting children and supporting displaced populations through key activities, including mental health support, case management for cash aid, community awareness, and training local authorities. It provides legal assistance for housing, land, and property disputes, facilitates access to civil documentation, and supports vulnerable groups like women and children facing systemic barriers. Additionally, the project raises community awareness to prevent exploitation, establishes legal protection desks, simplifies administrative processes, and advocates for inclusive policies. Mobile legal aid teams and trained paralegals ensure outreach to underserved areas, guided by specialized assessments to address specific needs. # Access to Civil Documentation: -Support in obtaining or recovering essential documents such as birth certificates, national IDs, marriage certificates, and property deeds to facilitate access to essential services -Addressing administrative barriers that prevent access to documentation.# Support for Women and Vulnerable Groups: -Legal assistance for women, children, and marginalized groups facing systemic barriers to justice. -Addressing gender-specific legal challenges, such as inheritance and custody disputes.# Protection Against Exploitation and Abuse: -Strengthening community awareness of legal rights and procedures to reduce exploitation risks. -Establishing legal protection desks # Resolution of Administrative Challenges: -Assistance with navigating complex administrative processes to access legal protections. -Advocacy for streamlined and inclusive bureaucratic procedures for displaced populations.# Reaching Undeserved and Remote Areas: -Deployment of mobile legal aid teams to ensure access for communities in hard-to-reach regions -Training and supporting local paralegals to extend legal assistance within communities.#Assessments -Conduct and facilitate specialized assessments# Forced displacement driven by conflict and climatic disasters -Legal counselling for individuals and families facing imminent eviction and encroachment on their land.-Collaboration with authorities to prevent encroachment.# Resolution of Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) Disputes: -Assistance with disputes over land ownership and property rights. -Advocacy for equitable access to housing for displaced individuals.# Negotiation for Land Access: -Facilitating agreements to secure land access for disaster-displaced people. -Advocacy with authorities, host communities, and private landowners to ensure safe and legal land use.
