Project Summary

NCA plans a multisectorial intervention for better impact in the conflict affected communties of Central and South Darfur states: In IDP camps, vital water and sanitation infrastructure will be renewed and complemented: sustainable and permanent handovers can only be done if the overall condition of the water and sanitation infrastructure is good. Striving towards sustainability of WASH services in the camps by creating organizational structures and gradually handing over of responsibility will be the priority. NCA will focus on training and strengthening of the committees to ensure ownership and sustainability of WASH services
In rural areas: NCA will provide sustainable water and sanitation services to targeted populations to meet their basic needs while increasing their self-reliance. A multi-sector approach to tackle malnutrition combining WASH, Health and Food security and economic empowerment activities will be developed. The NCA DP plans for multiple-use water services where it will work with their partners to improve access to water resources. This will aim at addressing drivers of conflict between farmers and pastoralist and hence communal conflict due to scarcity of water.
With the Health and Nutrtion: NCA focuses on increasing and sustaining quality health and nutrition services for the most vulnerable communities of Darfur. To this the sector will improve the quality of primary health care (PHC) services delivered at supported facilities through on-the-job training, and continuous programme monitoring. The NCA DP renews its commitment to work together with the State Ministry of Health (MoH) and national partners to build local capacity for sustainable service delivery of primary health care (PHC) in targeted areas. The DP will augment the role of local partner in providing health services and will support local partner to take the full responsibility of managing and implementing activities in H&N. The main focus in the first year will be on building the capacity of local partner, and on transferring some responsibilities. In the following years, the DP role will be confined to providing technical support and mobilization of fund. NCA will work with UNHCR to preposition ES/NFIs to reach newly displaced peoples.
In 2021, prevention and treatment of SGBV and traditional harmful practices will be central to the Health and Nutrition sector. As a result of displacement, IDPs and South Sudanese refugees experience a heightened risk of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) with women and girls disproportionally affected. This coupled with the ongoing socio-economic situation in Sudan, will force the victims to negative coping mechanisms. Gender-based violence continues to be a major concern in Sudan with 1.8 million people in dire need of SGBV prevention, mitigation and response. Women and girls continue to suffer disproportionately from SGBV, poverty and violations of basic rights due to insecurity. NCA plans to reach a total of 50,000 people with different GBV prevention activties. The targets constitute IDPs and host communities. NCA will work on awarness raising on GBV, creating refferal linkage, support legal and psycosocial support for survivors and build local capacity on GBV prevention and awarness. NCA is strengthening the GBV response and putting a strong emphasis on the sector with a new team- where an international GBV advisor joined and working on setting up a solid GBV response on the ground. NCA conducted a baseline GBV assessment in Nyala and Zalingie- both on the state of service delivery and attitude/practice regarding GBV. The assessment result showed a huge gap in both Central and South Darfur states, when it comes to basic services for survivors aswell as worrying attitude towards GBV- with an overall acceptance and practice around GBV.
All in all: NCA plans to reach about 220,000 people of IDPs, returnees and host communties.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian needs persist in Sudan. Displaced communities are badly affected by the lack of basic health and nutrition services as well as access to productive resources for livelihoods. They are also influenced by other factors including food insecurity, malnutrition protection risks, and inter-communal violence and conflict. Rising food prices have decreased households’ purchasing power and reduced access to food among the most vulnerable groups. According to the latest IPC, between January-March 2019 about 5.76 million people in Sudan are projected to be food insecure. This includes a considerable number of newly food insecure people in urban and peri-urban areas. According to the 2019 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP,) there are 4.4 million people in Sudan in need of humanitarian support. The figure has further increased in 2020, and according to the latest Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) of 2020, Some 9.3 million people – 23 per cent of the population – will need humanitarian assistance in 2020. The same report outlines that incidents of fighting have reduced considerably in recent years, the situation of people displaced due to decades of conflict remains unresolved. Some 1.87 million IDPs and 1.1 million refugees and asylum seekers continue to need humanitarian assistance and protection support, both in and out of camps and within host communities . Pockets of armed conflict continue in Darfur, and sporadic inter-communal conflicts also continue. across Sudan, basic services are lacking, and natural disasters, like floods, affect people each year. But it is a deepening economic crisis, following years of stagnation and little investment in already-weak public services, that is driving worsening food insecurity, deteriorating healthcare, and other needs across Sudan. Throughout Sudan, most people – 58 per cent of households – cannot afford a basic daily food basket. Over 2.7 million children suffer from acute malnutrition. Medical facilities across the country are not functional due to lack of essential drugs. For over a decade now, Sudan has faced a period of conflict, displacement and marginalization which has resulted in several challenges to the existing H&N systems throughout Darfur. Central Darfur has been targeted for this project because of the existing need to support vulnerable IDPs and host community members. Through the NCA health facility mapping as well as the World Health Organization (WHO) HeRAMS exercises in 2018, it is clear there is a large gap in health services exists in the region. Limited food access, poor maternal and child health and feeding practices, poor hygiene behavior and the limited services currently available in the camps and host communities are the main challenges to the IDPs and host communities in Central Darfur. Health facilities have limited logistical capability, medical supplies and equipment to reach those in urgent need of healthcare in IDP camps as well as host communities. The low motivation of health care providers due to low salaries and absence of payments for some staff such as midwives is the other contributing factor for the poor performance of health facilities. As a result, most IDPs and poor host communities are caught in the downward spiral of poverty– ill health–low productivity, particularly affecting women and children. There are no alternate options available to the community such as private clinics- except for the primary health care clinics run by humanitarian organizations such as NCA. There are no health facilities near NCAs target locations which also eliminates the potential for duplicated efforts in the area. The lasting impact of conflict between the Government of Sudan (GoS) and rebel groups has continued. Accoding to NCA's own assessment, services in SGBV in Darfur is scarce. There is a high persistance of traditional harmful practices- with about 89% of FGM practice in Darfur.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

activities are designed with the environment in mind. There will be drainage ditches to support with water overflow into backyard gardens which will inevitable enhance vegetable production in homesteads and also help to avoid stagnant water; breeding grounds for mosquitos (malaria risk).In WASH, communities and sanitarians actively participate in the latrine site selection to make sure there is a reasonable distance between water wells to avoid contamination of water sources from the latrine pits. To this, dry waste collection and disposal has been designed in accordance to the SPHERE Standards. Regular cleaning and awareness campaign are organized to sensitize communities and improve environmental hygiene.The FSEE team will encourage people to prepare farmyard manure or compost as a means of fertilizer. This practice will improve upon the soil fertility as well as enhance the physical and microbial environment of the soil. Moreover, Natural Resource management is central to the FSEE sector; whereby activities such as Hazard Vulnerability Capacity Assessments will be conducted to identify main Vulnerability of communities and plan for activities in Natural resources management to improve resilience. Community Action Groups will be strengthened and capacitated to undertake activities aiming at improving the natural resources.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The NCA DP programme will follow the ACT child protection (CP) and CAFOD Child Protection (2015) policies to ensure children are protected and treated equally irrespective of race, religion, gender, age or abilities. NCA also follows the Code of Conduct for the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and NGOs in Disaster Relief (CoC) for staff and partners with guidance on appropriate and expected standards of behaviour, including with children. NCA & partners will during the first quarter of 2021 complete CP safety audits to assess gaps that might need to be addressed to ensure that children are protected and included in all aspects of decision-making affecting their well-being. NCA will strive to ensure safety, inclusiveness and protection of children in camps and host communities. The DP will create awareness to staff, visitors and other stakeholders about child protection protocols to make sure that the wellbeing of children is secure and their right to access basic services is understood. The DP additionally strives to create child friendly spaces where children (including any unaccompanied children) can come together, play and talk comfortably. The multi-sectoral programmes are designed to address the immediate and strategic needs of children. NCA has a strong GBV sector with an international GBV advisor leading the overall implementation as well as national officers who are based in both Darfur and Khartoum.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

NCA as a HAP certified humanitarian organization, is committed in involving target communities and already exist committees in assessing needs and the programme teams are available to listen to and address complaints. Disaster Risk Reduction Committees (DRRCs) are formed and are with other community structure fully involved in providing the assistance to their community after sharing the assessment finding report. In addition, a baseline survey and feasibility study will be conducted to provide benchmarks upon which progress are monitored and evaluated at different levels in involvement of the target beneficiaries. In addition to, the sector holds discussions with IDPs, returnees and refugee’s community leaders for transparency purposes during assessments, verification and distribution of NFIs and shelter assistance (cash grant). The programme staff will conduct monthly consultation meetings in target areas with community leaders and DRRCs to consult with them on the verification results and together we agreed on data so as to start distribution process. The participation of community members ensures that the whole process of beneficiary selection is transparent and understood by all. All staff on field trips produces trip reports on return from trips indicating achievements, challenges faced and proposed solutions to problems faced. Periodic progress reports on implementation are produced for all monitoring visits made. These include weekly, monthly, quarterly and annual reports. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) is carried out 15 days after distribution day to identify whether the items met the beneficiaries’ needs or not as well captured their recommendation on the quality of the NFIs. EPRU participates effectively in monthly ES/NFIs and Refugee Working Group coordination meetings, where humanitarian actors updated their planned and implemented activities.NCA will use existing complaints mechanism at place (PDM, focus group discussion and camp coordination meetings chaired by UNOCHA), NCA is accountable to target beneficiaries and other stakeholders (UN agencies, INGOs, National NGOs and HAC) in sharing information and the achievement. NCA used PDM as one of the complaint mechanisms hear the voice of beneficiaries, their change of needs and their recommendation and NCA advocate accordingly. NCA share the findings of PDM with ES/NFI sector on state and Khartoum level.
