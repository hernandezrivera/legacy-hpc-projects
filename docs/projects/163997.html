Project Summary

Silk Road through this project aims to make Girls, boys, youth, women and persons with disabilities and other vulnerable members of the community feel better supported through access to improved protection resources as well as the vulnerable women, youth, persons with disabilities and others have a sense of greater empowerment through participation in life skills and in leading community events.
Silk Road Organization will establish nin safe spaces in A'zaz, Al-Bab, Jarablus, Afrin, Daret Azza, Harim, Dana, Ma'btali and Haritan for people who will be involved with the project and will be managed by Silk Road Organization team to providing protection activities i.e.: PSS, life Skills & informal activities for (Age of the late adolescents and adults ) as per each age group requires such as regular drop-in sessions which include different recreational activities for (children with age from 10-14 years old) that will create the emotional balance to individuals reflecting that to the family and community levels.
Safe spaces will be the place to manage and initiate a community projects (Community projects will be resulted according to the LS ( Life skills) sessions and beneficiaries engagement to decide the most important community project shall be done in their area such as Solid waste collection, Hygiene campaigns, Back to learn awareness sessions and flyers distribution, Afforestation campaigns or any other projects) which will increase the linkages and the access to the community by beneficiaries aiming to re-integrate Internally Displaced People with the host communities that will lead to promoting the positive coping mechanisms.
This project also will provide the informal education sessions aiming to cover the gap between peers which is created due to the displacements resulting the negative coping mechanisms such as child marriage, recruitment and labour while life skills courses to women and youth strive to burden the risk towards them that came due to ignoring women and youth category abilities of being leaders and engaged with the communities.
Our community outreach teams in the field will inform the communities about safe space activities and eligibility and encourage drop-ins and request lists from local councils /civil local actors beside a comprehensive questionnaire (Vulnerability assessment to identify eligible participants) will be designed containing many questions that will help us to identify eligible participants (project activities, emergency and referrals) and to understand the protection risks, durable solutions or needs of the affected population.
Silk Road will also facilitate participants the people with disabilities by equipping each centre with all needed equipment that can help them to enter the centre such as installing of the disabled handrail, Disabled Trails and special WASH equipment in WC also we can encourage the families and communities to come to the centre. Moreover, the recreational activities will take into consideration people with disabilities through simple recreational activities designed for them such as providing them with a range of items like coloured paper, string, buttons, mesh, paper clips that you can think of then learning them to make a collage, with a design of their choice in addition to reading and painting as well. With supports such from the family, community and the organization, PWD can choose to participate in a full range of community leisure and recreation activities based upon their personal interests and desires.
Local authorities and field team will be informed of the service mapping to enable them to direct affected people to the humanitarian community. Silk Road will follow all these cases and will provide the best communication with Protection cluster and other clusters as well in order to ensure timely in providing assistance through mapping services.

4 - Needs

Children throughout Syria continue to face pervasive violence and a range of protection risks. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of killing and injury, recruitment and use in hostilities, torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence, attacks on schools and hospitals and denial of humanitarian access by parties to the conflict. The crisis has deepened poverty, fueled harmful coping mechanisms and increased the risk of a suite of child protection concerns such as child labour and child marriage, putting adolescent boys and girls at particular risk. Child labour and child marriage are reported in all 14 governorates of Syria in 84 per cent and 69 per cent of assessed communities respectively, depriving many of their childhood and basic rights, including the right to an education. Access to basic rights and services are further jeopardized for children with disabilities and children that cannot access official birth certificates. In Syria, it is estimated that 5.3 million children are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection needs. Child protection concerns that have been identified are related to documentation, child labour, early marriage and psychosocial stress. The number of children with disabilities has also increased with limited access to the services they require.As assessed by the ongoing Silk Road protection monitoring programming, through case studies, interviews with key informants in the community, participatory assessments and field observations, the protection situation in the sub-prefectures of targeted subdistricts continues to be critical. In rural areas, Vulnerable people the new IDPs remain at high risk of lack of safety and security beside them concerns from a range of risks, including eviction, exploitation and abuse, overcrowding, poor access to services, and unhygienic living conditions.

5 - Implementation of activities

Activity 1: Conducting vulnerability Assessment: Silk Road will conduct a vulnerability assessment to identify the most vulnerable, in need of protection support and or emergency support. Activity 2: Establish and maintain the three Safe Space in the target location for girls, boys, youth, men and women. Two safe spaces will be established based on clear selecting criteria ِ(Accessibility and safety within the centres). Activity 3: Recruiting, training and managing coaches/ Protection Assistant on our Policies and regulations “Child Protection, Child Safeguarding, CoC…etc. Activity 4: Regular drop-in sessions. Silk Road will provide Cultural & Recreational Activities for both Male and Female of all age( Learning to draw/carve, learning how to using household waste such as paper, cardboard and plastic to create a useful and unique product, Embroidery and knitting, fun games like Chess, music and games for children under 10 years old etc. Activity 5: Providing psychosocial support activities to Female and Male aged 15 to 24 years old beside Parenting sessions that will be provided to the Women above 25 years old. Those will be regular sessions dealing with 3 pillars of PSS (Communication, social and emotional) in addition to providing parenting sessions. Activity 6: Distribution of dignity kits for the most affected female above 19 years. Activity 7: Engage the Protection cluster referral pathway to refer beneficiaries with specific needs beyond the scope of Silk Road provision. Activity 8: Creating broader social mobilization and awareness on child and women protection issues Activity 8: Mapping of services and referral pathway. Activity 9: Life skills courses Activity 9: Supporting women and youth-led community projects.For this project, there will be a dedicated M&E officer who will work closely with the field team supported by the M&E manager to apply the relevant M&E tools such as FGDs , attendance sheets, pre- and post-assessments and community surveys.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1- Code of Conduct and/or Child protection incidents; Silk Road has a COC and Child protection and child safeguarding policies in place that have been signed by all the staff focusing on the frontline workers in prior of starting direct activities with beneficiaries, including all procedures for reporting directly to the head office. in order to increase their awareness to the cases they might face in the field and know correct practices and procedures they have to follow and reduce the risks on themselves, the affected beneficiaries and the organization as well with their correct reaction.Risk 2- Discrimination between the beneficiaries: Silk Road Organization staff are well trained to be professional in involving both categories of beneficiaries (IDPs and host community) in the same PSS sessions that will create a smooth communication channel between them and increase the communication flexibility to share their emotions and support each other (Peer-to-Peer support).Risk3 - Content of message is not fully adapted to the social and cultural environment and creates misunderstanding or even tensions and has repercussions on the organisation implementing the campaign or /and the Media chosen is not the most effective in the specific environment/ community:- Silk Road will consult with population groups and communities on the most effective way of delivering messages; consult existing assessments on communication to communities (MSNA); resort to the knowledge of local counterparts and social workers in the communities.Risk4 - The population targeted by the intervention is put at risk as a result of the location of the facility; lack of consideration for the accessibility to services and lack of consideration of contextual information on the location and the local dynamics. The safe spaces will be established based on clear selecting criteria (In terms of accessibility the centres shall accessible to all beneficiaries houses in selective and known areas.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:1- A safe environment allows implementing the activities by having safe access to the safe space by beneficiaries and staff (low airstrike and less fighting between armed groups)2- The curricula are used will be engaging and suitable for all age categories for children, women and youth Risks:Risk1 -Security incidents affect project activities (beneficiaries, staff, safe space and/or relevant facilities): Silk Road field team has been trained security conflict dynamics analysis and they have the full rights to have the decision on the ground. A security officer is in place to follow up with the team with all relevant support in terms of building the field team capacity, following up their issues and analyzing the field situation in a regular and incident basisRisk2 -Personnel with critical skills needed for the project cannot be found: Silk Road will overcome this risk by: - Re-process if necessary- Broader advertising- Use local ads via local councils- Evaluate strengths and weaknesses well in applicants- Provide training for new employees after assessing what they need.Risk3 -Inflation, especially the cost of equipment, furniture and etc, may result in higher than planned prices. If prices spike too high, then Silk Road may not have the budget to complete the project / Market system disruption. - Silk Road will monitor inflation and prices and include this risk in its contracts with service providers. If inflation rises, they will work with the donor to re-align the budget and program accordingly. - We will include the ability to ensure procurement as a criterion in the contractor's bid analysis. - We will Consider using fixed prices and placing this risk in the contractor's contracts. - We will Conduct a strong and constant market monitoring before starting any local procurement to have early warning of potential lack of supplies, and also map out alternative supply.
