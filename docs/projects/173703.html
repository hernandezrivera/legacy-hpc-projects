Project Summary

The project will target the most vulnerable IDPs in Jere LGA. Through multiple livelihoods interventions, SIF will address the lack of economic opportunities of targeted beneficiaries, deteriorated by the prolonged displacement. Two hundred and fifty women will be selected to participate in the vocational training activities, which will be conducted through a local institute. The training will develop beneficiaries’ capacities to engage in small income-generating activities, including petty trading, cap making, food processing and preservation and tailoring. In addition, their abilities to manage savings and loans will be supported through specific training and coaching. Finally, the project will provide start-up kits to the participants, in order to support their income-generating activities. Since this project component will involve vulnerable women, their awareness on Gender-based Violence (GBV) matters will be raised in addition to the vocational training package. Fifty vulnerable men will also be targeted with cash for work (CFW) activities. SIF will integrate the CFW to its ongoing WASH activities, and will engage its WASH contractors to hire vulnerable IDPs to participate in the works. If needed, additional on-the-job training will be provided to the participants of the CFW activities, for more sustainable project results.

1 - Needs

According to the September 2020 DTM report from IOM, more than 2.1 million people are still internally displaced in North-East Nigeria and around 1.5 million of them only in the state of Borno. This number is increasing from 2% since the last report of June 2020 by conflict-induced food insecurity and severe malnutrition. The difficulties of displaced families were further aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, which reduced significantly their livelihood opportunities. More importantly, the COVID-19 pandemic further exacerbated gender inequalities, with women especially suffering the movement restrictions and the reduced access to basic services. The child labour and child marriage rate is increasing due to the reduced economic opportunities, as families are forced to adopt these negative coping strategies. In addition, women are even more prone to the risk of sexual and gender-based violence, as their vulnerability is increased and access to services is endangered. The SIF Programme team conducted preliminary discussions with the communities neighbouring Farm Centre IDP camp in Jere LGA at the beginning of November 2020, to understand the livelihood situation of the displaced families and to identify specific risks to address. The findings of these discussions indicated that most of the IDPs in the LGA are women and children, with a generalized lack of resources to cater for their needs. Due to the lack of means, women often engage in transactional sex and begging, while children are forced to work. The IDPs with whom SIF team was able to discuss, expressed the preference to remain in their community of displacement and to rebuild their livelihoods there. In this context, SIF decided to focus on improving the short-term and long-term livelihood opportunities of vulnerable IDPs, paying special attention to the needs of displaced women and their families.

2 - Activities

The project will be articulated in two components: vocational training for vulnerable women and cash for Work for vulnerable IDPs.VOCATIONAL TRAININGIn order to address the economic needs of the most vulnerable displaced women and to reduce the adoption of negative coping strategies, SIF will engage selected women in vocational training, developing their ability to reach self-reliability.To ensure community buy-in, SIF will involve relevant stakeholders to conduct beneficiary targeting. Community leaders, local authorities, humanitarian partners and community representatives will contribute to the identification of the vulnerable families. SIF team will then conduct a door-to-door assessment of the most vulnerable displaced women. Priority for targeting will be given to those with more than one dependant, without any source of income, with disabled or ill dependant(s), willing to participate in the vocational training activities, basic skills to follow the training and women facing protection risks (those facing trauma, disabled women or GBV survivors). The training program will provide a sustainable economic empowerment component and skills acquisition component. Through a transparent procurement process, SIF will select a qualified supplier to provide the technical training. The supplier will deploy expert facilitators, who will conduct a theoretical and a practical training. Among the theoretical principles, the beneficiaries will also improve their skills on how to gain market access to sell their products and how to manage their savings to invest in their activities. The selected income generating activities will include petty trading, cap making, food processing and preservation, and tailoring. In order to capitalise on the access to vulnerable women, SIF staff will also conduct GBV awareness, to enable women to recognize and mitigate GBV risks. At the end of the trainings, the participants will receive a start-up kit to initiate their income generating activities, while SIF staff will conduct a close monitoring to support them to face possible challenges, as well as continue to raise awareness on GBV matters.CASH FOR WORK In coordination with SIF’s WASH project, vulnerable beneficiaries will be targeted to participate in the construction of the boreholes and the sanitation facilities. People with disabilities will be prioritised according to the types of tasks identified. Only beneficiaries who are found not to have any source of income or insufficient sources of income for their family size will be selected for the project. Additional vulnerability criteria will include skilled beneficiaries whose livelihoods have been negatively impacted by the COVID-19 emergency and those with more than three dependants. Based on the bill of quantity received from the contractors, SIF will select eligible tasks to be completed by vulnerable IDPs, and will be engaging directly with them through an agreement, to distribute cash in hand according to the number of days worked. SIF will provide safety equipment for the beneficiaries and, if further training is needed, will engage the contractor to conduct on-the-job training for the participants.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Since SIF is committed to ensuring protection considerations are always present in its interventions, the needs assessment planned for January 2021 will prioritise the voices of women and people with disabilities, as they are the main targeted population. Similarly, equal representation of all the marginalised groups will be ensured in all community structures involved in the design and the targeting phase of the project. In addition, SIF will ensure safe access to project facilities and services for these marginalized groups.The beneficiaries will be receiving information sessions related to SIF’s procedure for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), which will target especially the women participating in the vocational training. Regular training on this topic will also be delivered to the project team to reduce the risk of SEA.The project plans on targeting only vulnerable IDPs, which might spark community tensions. For this reason, SIF field team will be sensitised to social stability issues, in order to identify possible community tensions at an early stage. If these circumstances materialise, SIF might consider the possibility to target vulnerable host community members in the project.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The initial discussions SIF conducted with the communities where it is present were meant to have a first overview of the main needs of vulnerable communities facing prolonged displacement. However, SIF is planning to conduct a thorough needs assessment to identify specific areas of intervention. The needs assessment will be conducted in January 2021 and it will include community consultations. SIF will mobilise the community from the very beginning, to ensure information on the project is shared and understood. The communication with the communities will include the targeting process, the details of the planned assistance as well as the complaint and feedback mechanism. A Community Mobiliser will be specifically trained on the complaint and feedback mechanism of the project, and they will be in charge of registering, processing and responding to the complaints. They will work closely with the MEAL team to ensure beneficiaries’ feedback is included in project learning. Complaints will be collected through a helpdesk before and after the training sessions, through the activation of a hotline as well as through the implementation of regular focus group discussions to solicit the opinion of specific groups who might not have access to the other modalities.
