Project Summary

This project will provide support to the extremely vulnerable, conflict-affected girls, women, boys, and men in Ayod and Fangak who are exposed to gender-based violence and protection risks. CAO has a presence and filed offices in Ayod and Fangak. CAO access Fangak and Ayod through UNHASS, water transport, and during dry season Ayod can be accessed by road. This project programme will includes provision of comprehensive case management to the identified and registered GBV cases, provision of psychosocial support ( PSS), identifying and registering women, men, boys and girls with protection concerns and supported through case management and referral services, conducting referral services to women, men, girls and boys with protection concerns, provision of individual, group, specialized PSS and other related services at the women and girls friendly spaces, establishing women and girls friendly spaces (WFGs) ,Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience to the identified women men, girls and boys, Distribution of dignity kits to women and adolescent girls of the reproductive age as part GBV risk mitigation measure and addressing differential needs, Provision of awareness-raising to women, men, girls and boys on the GBV preventive measures, the project will target 15,000 (3,500 women, 3,500 men, 4,000 girls and 4,000 boys) with the total budget of $300,000.

1 - Needs

Women, Men Girls, and boys in South Sudan are disproportionately affected by ongoing conflict, High levels of displacement of about around 1.8 million internally displaced, an estimated number of 400,000 people to have been Women, Men Girls, and boys in South Sudan are disproportionately affected by ongoing conflict, High levels of displacement of about around 1.8 million internally displaced, an estimated number of 400,000 people to have been killed, and 2.5 million fled to the neighboring countries, especially Uganda. leaving Women, Men Girls, and boys in psychological distress. Women, Men Girls, and boys are exposed to gender-based violence such as rape, sexual abuses, exploitation, early marriages sexual assault The 2022 HNO states that Protection concerns remain high, with people impacted by violence having limited access to justice and the rule of law. In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. Women and children require lifesaving protection services. CAO aims to build a more holistic approach to GBV, integrating MHPSS with other sectors like Wash, education Neutrino, health, and FSL.CAO also aims to strengthen community-based support mechanisms Women and girls have also reported sexual assault, rape, and other abuses when walking long distances to collect water, firewood, and wild fruits. In the recent IRNA assessment that was carried out in Feb 2022 Ayod county in which CAO was part of the team, There was several findings that was raised through the focal group discussions and the key informant interviews that was conducted to the different stake holders such as church and opinions leaders, chiefs , women leaders, youth and the women group, it was noted that among reported GBV incidents 98% of survivors were women and girls. Incidents included physical assault by an intimate partner (37 percent), sexual violence (18 percent), and emotional abuse (25 percent). It was also reported that women and girls face gender base violence on they way to collect water, firewood and wild fruits, however, GBV remains underreported, with survivors facing multiple barriers to accessing services. Many traditional and formal protection mechanisms have broken down, and conflict, violence, and displacement have significantly increased the vulnerability of women and girls to various forms of GBV. Increased risks, gaps in GBV service provision, and a response constrained. The COVID-19 pandemic necessitate urgently and strengthened GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and response services. The conflict has destroyed many support systems for survivors and impacted access to services; as such, there is a high need for continued support to GBV response, prevention, and social norms transformation and risk mitigation.In communities, there is a need for women and girls to have safe spaces for sharing their experiences and accessing support. GBV interventions need to be integrated into all sectors of the humanitarian response as well as engage communities in harmful social norms transformation to address barriers and drivers of GBV
