Needs

Due to its geographical position, Haiti is highly exposed to natural and humanitarian disasters related to earthquakes and hurricanes. The country is classified as the most vulnerable in the Latin America and Caribbean region, and the 14th most vulnerable country in the world. The entire country is at risk, but the departments of Artibonite, Centre, Grand'Anse, Nippes, Nord-Est, Ouest, Sud and Sud-Est are the most at risk of being affected by natural disasters. In 2016, the country was hit by its strongest Hurricane in 52 years, causing the internally displacement of 175,500 people and affecting an estimated of 2.1 million people. Two years after the passage of hurricane Matthew, people affected still identify shelter as their most important unmet need. Most recently, on October 6th, 2018, a 5.9 magnitude earthquake struck 20 km north of Port-de-Paix (North-West department) which was followed by two aftershocks of magnitude 5.2 and 4.2 on October 7th, 2018. According to the Directorate of Civil Protection (DPC) report, 17 people were killed and 427 were wounded across the North West, North and Artibonite Departments. The earthquake caused significant damage to infrastructures and the DPC estimates that 261 institutional buildings and 29,433 homes were damaged, and that 2668 homes were completely destroyed, with most of the damage being recorded in Saint Louis du Nord, Port de Paix et Gros Morne. Coordination structures for the shelter/NFI sector need to be maintained in 2019, in order to strengthen the harmonization of emergency preparedness and response of humanitarian and state partners, taking into account lessons learned from events taking place in 2018 and during previous crises. IOM co-leads the Shelter/NFI Working Group (WG) with the UCLBP and maintains strong links with the Direction de la Protection Civile (DPC), the Ministry of Social Affairs, the Ministry of Interior the Entreprise Publique de Promotion de Logements Sociaux (EPPLS) and local authorities in the targeted areas. As such, the proposed activities are designed in line with the Government’s response strategy and build upon IOM’s on going activities in Matthew’s and the October 2018 earthquake affected areas. IOM’s activities aim to provide timely and lifesaving assistance to those affected by the October 2018 earthquake and to the extremely vulnerable people still in need of assistance as a result of Hurricane Matthew.The proposed action seeks to reduce the vulnerability of the population affected by sudden catastrophes by providing shelter support and promoting multi-sectoral approach. Furthermore, the action incorporates key strategic priorities, fully aligns with the Government's response strategy, with all activities being conducted in the context of capacity building of state authorities, including the Unité de construction de logements et bâtiments publics (UCLBP) at the national level and local authorities at the commune and neighbourhood level. IOM will maintain effective linkages with the activities carried out by Shelter partners and will establish linkages with shelter initiatives in Haiti. The proposed action is composed of the following three pillars:1. Shelter/NFI Coordination 2. "Build Back Safer" Capacity Building and sensitization3. Core Houses trainings4. Cash for rent

Activities or outputs

Proposed project Activities include: 1. Shelter Working Group Coordination: As co-lead of the Shelter/NFI WG, IOM aims to maintain and continue on going activities through close coordination and collaboration with the partners of the Shelter/NFI WG in promoting early recovery through the provision of shelter solutions tackling those affected by the October 2018 earthquake and residual displacement of populations affected by sudden catastrophes, and providing technical advice and guidelines to partners. The Coordination will ensure information gathering and analysis on ongoing shelter response activities including those driven by the government and trying to survey self-reconstruction managed by the population. Coordination will be decentralized at departmental level. The North, North West and Artibonite Departments, Grand Anse, Nippes, Southwill be considered priority areas. 2. "Build Back Safer" Capacity Building: As SWG Co-lead agency, IOM seeks to continue to provide technical support to actors which include consortium partners involved in the shelter response and to promote the BBS approach. The BBS promotion will build on the activities implemented in 2017 and 2018. Key actors with ongoing projects will be important stakeholders in the implementation of the strategy. The response strategy encompassed training related activities, sensitization and governance related activities. The set of the activities will be implemented nationwide with focus on the most prone disaster areas . In addition to that, the most prone disaster areas will be sensitized through further diffusion of the established BBS key messages. The key strategic element of the sensitization activities is the implication of DPC,the Haitian Red Cross, with the aim of increasing the actors potentially able to diffuse BBS messages through their networks. By involving DPC and Haitian Red Cross in the project activities the action will also promote a broader multi-hazard DRR approach. At the same time the action will ensure that the shelter sector will be included in the overall DRR agenda in Haiti, linked to the National DRM System. Through the Shelter Working Group, all shelter partners Involved in DRR activities will be asked to include the shelter component, at the same time the SWG coordination will be more involved in DRR activities coordination processes. 3. Core House Training: The SWG coordination's facilitate trainings trough partners and professional schools in order to promote BBS reconstructions. Through the interaction of the technical stakeholders (including International Organizations, local relevant organizations and local technical authorities) and by sharing existing knowledge, training tools and practices, the action will support the technical improvement of ongoing constructions while producing at the same time a core house training module. Cash for RentCash solution are promoted to provide immediate support to people affected by earthquake 2018, a multi sectorial approach is recommended to implementing partners. This proposed intervention will aim to provide direct housing solutions to the situation of those affected by the natural disasters. The households under particular precarious conditions will benefit from the provision of a rental subsidy cash grant, allowing them to move into the community and home respecting SPHERE standards, to be covered during 6/12 months. The principle aim is to end prolonged displacement by providing targeted beneficiaries with assistance to expedite their return to their community of origin or choice. To facilitate a well-organized approach that provides assistance to facilitate returns of the displaced households based on individual household needs, the following steps are proposed: Registration/Needs Assessment, Grievance Mechanism, Identification of Appropriate Housing Solution, Rental Subsidy Cash Grant,Ongoing Case Management, Camp Decommissioning and Follow-Up Visits.
