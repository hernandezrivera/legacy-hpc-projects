Project Summary

The project total number of beneficiaries is 54,942. This number is dis-aggregated as (Idp 13,735 – host population 38,460 – returnees 2,747) 53,821 children (28,283 Boys and 25,538 Girls) 1,121 (532 women and 589 men) (Male 28,872, female 26,070). BINAA aims to reach 53821 children (28283 Boys and 25538 Girls) in approximately 160 schools or temporary learning space (TLS) and support them with fuel, heaters in addition to the learning supplies (backpack, school in box kit and recreational kits), develop the capacity of 1121 (532 women and 589 men) teachers through training sessions during the school year, and awareness campaigns in targeted communities on back to school and dropout. The project also aims to rehabilitate 50 damaged schools (including the maintenance of windows, doors and ceilings, water facilities, rubble removal and lighting) and equip the 50 schools with the necessary furniture (desks, tables, boards, laptops and projectors). The project also includes preparation of child friendly spaces in 50 schools, and operate the 50 schools to deliver quality education services to the children in these schools by securing educational staff incentives, schools’ operational costs and coal for heating during winter.
Gender has been inbuilt within the design of the project by ensuring that both men and women are targeted by this action. In particular, girls and boys will be targeted to ensure their access to adequate education services. Representation of both men and women to ensure their voices and particularly those of women are heard in consultation and awareness campaigns matters that are pertinent to their gender. People with disabilities will be targeted as well through adding disable-friendly facilities such as handrails, ramps and toilets.
In tracking and monitoring the results accrued from the project, the project has developed gender dis aggregated indicators to demonstrate the extent to which both girls, boys, men and women have benefited from the action.

Needs

The humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2019 states that there are now 5,970,000 million children and youth from pre-school to secondary-age (including more than 100,000 Palestine refugee children) and over 121,000 education personnel in need of education assistance inside Syria. In the 2017/2018 academic year, a total of 2.1 million children, or 36 per cent of school-age children (aged 5-17 years) are estimated to be out of school and an additional 1.3 million are at risk of dropping out. Around 180,000 education personnel, including teachers, left the education system, which has negatively affected the quality of education for all children. Hostilities have seen over one in 3 schools damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for shelter and other purposes. Schools, accounting for an estimated 33 per cent of collective centers, were reported as the most common building type used for IDP sites in Syria. Increased internal displacement has put the current infrastructure under enormous pressure and highlighted the need for school rehabilitation.Because of the massive displacement movements, nearly 2 million displaced children face disruptions in their studying years and the attendance at schools is sporadic and fragmented, and many children receive classes at inappropriate educational levels. Displaced children suffer from discrimination in host communities struggling to accommodate new students. BINAA provided full education services to more than 125000 children during 2018. Through the intervention fuel, educational materials, school support bags and recreational kits. In order to increase access to Life saving education for war affected children in Syria, BINAA will complement all responses to sustain students' involvement in the learning process by providing school with fuel, heaters in addition to box materials and recreational kits to 58000 children in elementary, middle and high school, including children from IDPs and host communities, and rehabilitate, operate and follow up the education process for 50 schools, and provide psychosocial support and mine risk education, and provide water and sanitation facilities as they are key interventions to achieve this goal.BINAA conducted rapid consultations with the educational directorates and selected sub-districts conforming with HNO needs to be targeted with high severity scale and will conduct a field and detailed surveys to identify the target villages, the target schools in each village. The number of students in each school and the numbers of the education staff in each school. The required supplies will be secured and then distributed to the target schools.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk-01: Interference from armed groups or the Camp management in the works. The likelihood of occurrence is high and its impact is high as well. this risk will be monitored by Binaa field staff in addition to the report received from the security department. Clear agreement with and consent form signed by the civilian management bodies. However, such kind of risks when happen the activities should be held and the incident will be reported to the relevant stakeholders. Risk-02: Unintentionally, misapplying Do no-harm principle: the likelihood is low and the impact is high. This risk will be monitored basically by Binaa M&E team. Community mobilization in addition to ensuring proper and adequate need technical and verification process. The verification process should cover both side of the intervention, vulnerability in addition to the technical needs assessment. Feedback and complaint mechanism is adopted to contribute in solving the issues. Risk-03: Access restrictions to the targeted areas active fighting: the likely hood of occurrence is moderate and its impact is high. The risk will be monitored by Binaa field staff in addition to the report received from the security department. Contingency plan in addition to reasonable exit strategy is prepared. Once this type of risk is happened the all works will stalled in the low-security locations and being prepared to apply either the contingency or exit plan. Risk-04: GBV, sexual abuse or facilities are not disability-sensitive in the rehabilitated schools’ facilities. the likelihood is low and the impact is high. This risk will be monitored basically by Binaa M&E team. Referral path system and Feedback and complaint mechanism is adopted to contribute in solving the issues.
