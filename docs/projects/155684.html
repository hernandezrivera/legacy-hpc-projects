Project Summary

The eastern regions in Cameroon continue to face a complex and unprecedented humanitarian crisis with growing insecurity in the Lake Chad Basin region, the conflict in the Central African Republic (CAR) and the significant deterioration of food security and nutrition situation.
The main goal of WFP nutrition programme in the Eastern Regions is to contribute to reduction of under-nutrition and associated levels of mortality by 2020. WFP through the implementation of the nutrition programme under its Country Strategic Plan(CSP) will facilitate access to appropriate nutritious foods for vulnerable groups to prevent or treat malnutrition and thus makes a crucial contribution to saving lives and strengthening physical resilience to shocks in affected communities.
Therefore, as a nutrition intervention in the Eastern regions, WFP will prioritize integrated approaches for prevention and treatment of acute and chronic malnutrition targeting 62,400 children aged 6–23 months through the blanket supplementary feeding programme (BSFP) in targeted health districts and 7,600 moderate acute malnutrition(MAM) children aged 24–59 months in food-insecure areas with high malnutrition rates for 12 months in 2019.

Needs

Across the country, the 2014 Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS) found stunting to affect more than 31 percent of children under five, 13 percent of them severely. 34 percent under 59 months in the North were stunted, 38 percent in Adamaoua and 36 percent in the East. SMART survey in September 2017 situate all three regions close to the 5% poor nutrition situation with global acute malnutrition rates of 6.5%, 4.5%, and 4.8% respectively for the North, Adamaoua and East regions. The SMART survey also estimated that three quarters of stunted children in surveyed areas were in the North and Far North regions. The prevalence of food insecurity is 11% in the North region, 9% in the East region, and 7.5% in Adamaoua region (Food Security Monitoring System(FSMS), Feb 2018). The proportion of households with poor food consumption remained relatively stable in Adamaoua (1%) but still high in the North (9%) and East (5%). In Adamaoua region, the economic vulnerability was particularly notable in February 2018 (25%, of which 12% extremely vulnerable) compared to October 2017 (17%, of which 5% extremely vulnerable). In the East, economic vulnerability deteriorated with 13% of affected households in February 2018 compared to 10% in October 2017. The use of livelihood strategies remains high in the East (30% in February 2018). With this situation, the vulnerable population, particularly children aged 6 – 59 months, are severely exposed to the danger of malnutrition. Therefore, WFP’s nutrition intervention to prevent and treat the Moderate Acute Malnutrition(MAM) is required.
