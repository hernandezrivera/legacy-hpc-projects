Project Summary

In order to improve primary health care services to conflict affected population in the targeted area in Aleppo, SAMS through this project will provide primary health care services for both IDPs and host communities, laboratory services and implementing vaccination activities. The project will help to support salaries of medical and mon-medical staff and secure medication at the facility. All needed running costs will be covered to ensure that medical services are provided for the vulnerable people. Community health workers will implement in facility and outreach visits to the nearby host and IDP communities to share health awareness messages and implement screening for malnutrition.

Needs

Now approaching its eighth year of crisis , the overall scale and complexity of humanitarian needs of people in Syria remain staggering in terms of magnitude and severity with an estimated 13.2 million people who are in need of health assistance in Syria. Females make up 72 per cent of the PiN. Vulnerable groups include 5.2 million women of reproductive age, 2.2 million children under 5 years old, and 2.3 million people aged over 50, (HNO 2019).Across Syria health-related needs continue to be driven and exacerbated by insufficient access to healthcare due to a combination of damage and destruction of health facilities, insufficient human resources, and imposed access constraints. According to the WHO Surveillance System of Attacks on Healthcare (SSA), 137 targeted attacks on health care took place in Syria during the first 10 months of 2018, resulting in a reported 97 deaths and 169 injuries, (World Health Organization. Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care. See ssa.who.int for the Public Dashboard), compared to 122 verified attacks in 2017, reportedly resulting in 73 deaths and 149 injuries, (Violence against Health Care report by the Turkey Health Cluster). Attacks and resource constraints impact the functionality of health facilities. September 2018 Health Resource Availability Monitoring System (HeRAMS) data indicated that across Syria 46 per cent of health facilities were either non-functional or partially functional, with 167 facilities completely destroyed, and a further 419 facilities partially destroyed and in need of rehabilitation. The national ratio of fully functional Primary Healthcare Centers (PHCs) of 2.28 per 50,000 populations is above Sphere Standards, but the distribution is highly unequal, with 101 of 270 surveyed sub-districts reporting having no fully functional PHC, and an additional 9 sub-districts not meeting minimum standards. Nationally, less than one functional hospital bed is available per 1,000 populations, which barely below Sphere standards and very low when measured against a global average of 3 beds per 1,000 populations. As for PHCs, availability of and access to hospital services is not distributed evenly across the country, (Whole of Syria Q3 2018 consolidated HeRAMS).UNICEF/WHO estimates that in 2019, about 577,219 newborns will require routine immunization across Syria, with an estimated 320,000 children between 13 and 59 months of age not fully vaccinated during their first years of life. According to the severity scale, Al Zarbah sub-district get score five, which indicate to critical problems in this area. The severity of humanitarian needs was assessed through a rigorous set of protocols, whereby all available data collected on indicators addressing access to services, availability of services, conflict intensity, and vulnerable populations contributed to the final severity score. (Health sector input, HNO 2019) Based on HNO 2019, the total people in need for health services in Zarba are 77,297, they are disaggregated as following: 40,967 Female, 36,330 Male, Young Children 20,870, Children 17,778, Adults 33,238 and 5,411 Elderly in addition to 155 persons with disabilities (the disaggregation is based on SAMS estimates). This project aims to increase the access of Primary Health care services for Women, men, children, adolescents, youth and elderly persons to the health in Zarbah sub-district, by sporting Alzarbah PHC Center, it's wroth mentioning that Alzarbah is PHC level 3 and according to our PHC records 23% of the project target are adolescents and youth.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Sexual Exploitation and abuse by Facilities staff toward beneficiaries. Possibility: high. Effect: high. Mitigation measures: 1. Set PSEA Policy and effectively implement the policy in Hiring the staff and oriented them on the policy, training them, and make sure every staff sign the policy, 2. Effective complaint mechanism in place, 3. Awareness raising for beneficiaries on PSEA, 4. Clear complaints channels for beneficiaries and distribute brochures and leaflets in all places in the facilities (waiting rooms, entrance, Etc.).Risk: Sexual and Gender Based Violence in the facilities between beneficiaries. Possibility: high. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Put cameras in the entrance and every floor of the facilities and corridors, 2. Use or establish separated toilets for men and women, 3. Establish spacious waiting rooms to host huge number of patient. 4. Put keys for the empty rooms if any. Risk: Facilities are inaccessible for the disabled patient and elderly persons. Possibility: moderate. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Build ramp for disabled people at the facility entrance, 2. Make sure of the availability of logistics supplies needed for elderly persons and people with disabilities. Risk: impure or spoiled medications and supplies that could be harmful to beneficiaries. Possibility: moderate. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Procure medication with long time validity (taking into consideration the transportation time for these medication and deliver it to the facilities then to beneficiaries to be used), 2. Procure the medications from reliable sources, 3. ensure cold-chain transportation for drugs requiring low temperatures, 4. Regular monitoring of the storage conditions of the medications and the validity date.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Control by unaccepted groups / entities or GoS. Mitigation measures: Anti-Terrorism Check (ATC), Pause / Cessation; Information security SOP; Strengthening the relations with the local communities; Relocating the activities locations.Risk: Attack by GoS and its allies / others.Mitigation measures: Selecting fortified locations; Security assessment (site & building); Evacuation SOP; Sourcing alternative locations, in case of need for relocation.Risk: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salariesMitigation measure: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salaries; Repeating the trainings regularly to ensure that all new staff delivering it; Adopt appropriate salary-scale to retain the staff as possible.Risk: Abduction (kidnapping)Mitigation measure: Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Security Briefing; Counter Abduction; Abduction Management training for staff and partners.Risk: TheftMitigation Measures: Safety and security upgrades for SAMS facilities, including CCTV cameras and guards; Photo verification of received shipments, signed waybills, and notification if any missing items noted in HF count within 72 hours; Review of stock/inventory records during monitoring visits; Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Quarterly physical inventory of warehouses; review of monthly consumption reports; Security Briefing for staff and partners.
