Project Summary

Throughout the proposed intervention, critical support will be provided to 600 institutions including healthcare institutions, social institutions and schools demonstrating high needs in hygiene items on both sides of the contact line (400 in GCA, 200 in NGCA). In addition, life-saving assistance will be provided to 2000 households prioritising the most vulnerable groups of population. The project will employ the TGH innovative and effective e-voucher modality ensuring geographic coverage, easy access, rapid distribution and real-time monitoring and few contacts between individuals which is extremely relevant in the current context.
The overall objective of the proposed project is to mitigate the negative effects of the COVID-19 and the protracted conflict by reinforcing the protection of the most vulnerable and at risk population in eastern Ukraine. The specific objective of the project is to reduce risks related to WASH and infectious diseases for residents, patients, pupils, personnel of institutions and for the most vulnerable conflict-affected people in eastern Ukraine through enhanced access to hygiene and awareness raising.

TGH draws its action on: (i) previous and ongoing projects including current TGH-emergency COVID-19 response project (PDM), (ii) needs assessment conducted by TGH in NGCA (February 2020), (iii) a context analysis in NGCA conducted by CF HADC (October 2020). The suggested response is the “COVID-19+ response” that takes into account: (i) the overall needs of vulnerable households and institutions affected by the conflict in essential hygiene items as well as (ii) the COVID-19-related needs.
The defined project objectives will be achieved through the following expected outcomes:
Outcome 1. Critical hygiene items are made available for healthcare, social and educational institutions for COVID-19 Infection Prevention and Control (GCA, NGCA). The project envisages the provision of critical life-saving hygiene assistance for three months for 600 institutions (300 Healthcare institutions (HCIs), 150 Social institutions (SI) and 150 Educational institutions (EIs) including as follows: GCA: 200 HCIs, 100 SIs, 100 EIs; NGCA: 100 HCIs, 50 SIs, 50 EIs. In line with the pioritization criteria (HRP 2021), priority will be given to underserved areas, isolated and hard to reach areas, rural areas, conflict-affected areas and COVID-19 cluster areas. Information sessions on the prevention of infectious diseases will be conducted in EIs in SIs in GCA and information materials on the same subject will be distributed in NGCA, if the context allows.
Outcome 2. Life-saving protection of the most vulnerable conflict-affected groups of population is ensured through the provision of essential hygiene items (GCA and NGCA). 2000 most vulnerable conflict-affected households (circa 4,600 individuals: 1,978 women, 1,127 men, 722 girls, 773 boys), 4,600 individuals among whom about 414 are estimated being persons with disabilities (PwD) will benefit from improved access to hygiene during three subsequent months. Specifically, 2,300 on each side of the contact line. The project will prioritise persons with multiple vulnerabilities (see in Activities).
Under Outcome 1 and 2, the project will employ its e-voucher modality and TGH four partners will be engaged in implementation and monitoring in GCA while assistance will be provided in-kind jointly with TGH and CF HADC in NGCA.
Outcome 3. Accountability to affected populations (AAP) is enhanced. AAP is an integral part of the project. Representatives of the affected population and beneficiaries will be included in all stages of the project cycle. A project Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) will be established as part of the overall TGH CRM and will have multiple feedback channels to ensure better access and inclusion. Protection of beneficiaries will be mainstreamed throughout the project through provision of tailored assistance.

1 - Needs

With the high proportion of elderly, population living with chronic diseases such as multidrug-resistant tuberculosis, cardiovascular diseases, diabetes and human immunodeficiency viruses, Ukraine is particularly vulnerable to the COVID-19 (REACH, KAP, GCA, August 2020). The situation represents particularly worrisome challenges for the already weakened healthcare systems in Ukraine. 72% of primary, secondary and tertiary facilities in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts GCA have only limited availability of hand sanitizers (REACH, Rapid Assessment of Health Facilities, April 2020). The overwhelming majority of healthcare institutions (75%) and households (96%) surveyed by TGH in July 2020 reported that they did not receive other support in hygiene apart from TGH (TGH PDM, contingency project UNICEF).Further, the onset of the COVID-19 and related restrictions has had dramatic consequences on the economic situation. The increased unemployment rate (9% nationwide, circa 14% in the east), the substantial rate of informal employment (circa 30%) and limited social safety nets, along with the rising prices have had worrisome consequences on the population (Preliminary Humanitarian Needs Analysis, 2021, September 2020). In addition, increases of prices of hygiene items were reported (7% rise in February-April, ACCESS/NRC, Rapid Market Assessment, April 2020). According to the Knowledge Attitudes and Practices (KAP) Assessment conducted by REACH in August 2020, hygiene items are too expensive (14% of respondents). Households with members with a disability reported significant difficulties in affording hygiene costs. Likewise, a higher proportion of rural residents and families with elderly or family members with disabilities reported that prices for hygiene items being too high (REACH, Ukraine COVID-19, KAP, August 2020).TGH PDM demonstrated that the average income per beneficiary per month was 1570 UAH which represented 74% of the official minimum subsistence level (2118 UAH/month/person) and only 41% of the actual minimum subsistence level (3799 UAH/month/person defined by Ministry of Social Policy, January-April 2020) (TGH COVID-19 Project GCA, July 2020). In September 2020 average income of TGH home-based care beneficiaries who are elderly and people with disabilities was 2,754 UAH per household, with 85% of beneficiaries having income inferior to the actual minimum subsistence level.Further, TGH survey held in September 2020 demonstrated that hygiene represented only 11.3% of households’ expenses far behind food (35.9%), health (32.6%) and shelter (19.6%) leaving few disposable cash for hygiene. Households along the contact line are thus deprived of access to essential basic hygiene. The low income levels explain the narrow share allocated to hygiene products. Specific groups such as older persons are particularly at risk. Most of them do not afford buying preventive products against the COVID-19 (78.64%, HelpAge assessment on elderly, May 2020, GCA). 82% of them (including 85% women) require basic hygiene items (89%), urological pads (39%) and diapers for adults (21%). Unsatisfactory hygiene practices including the use of unhygienic menstrual hygiene materials as well as a lack of access to basic hygiene products such as soap, laundry detergent, toilet paper, shampoo were reported in GCA (14%) and NGCA (13%) (WASH cluster HNO 2020). According to needs assessment carried out by TGH and “Variant” in NGCA (12 locations, Donetsk oblast, February 2020), the major issues faced by the communities were: high prices (28.3%), severe emotional state (17.9%), low salary (17.3%), lack of paid work opportunities (10.9%) and access to services. The types of aid crucially needed and prioritized by the respondents were: humanitarian assistance for food, hygiene and clothes (82.8% for the 66+, 78.3% for the 50-65, 29% for the general population). Elderly people of 66 and above expressed high demand for food and hygiene (82.8%) and health care (69.6%).

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome 1. A 1.1.: Selection of institutions. Identification will be conducted in cooperation with local and regional authorities, clusters, and via CF HADC in NGCA. In GCA, institutions will be supported in within 0-40 km area from the contact line. Priority will be given to underserved areas, isolated and, hard to reach areas, rural areas, conflict-affected areas and COVID-19 clusters areas (prioritization criteria, HRP 2021). Within its current projects in NGCA TGH and its local partner “Variant”, collaborate with healthcare institutions and the partnership will be in charge of identification of institutions. A 1.2.: Distribution of e-vouchers/in-kind kits for hygiene. In GCA TGH will employ the e-voucher modality and rely on its networks of over 110 local shops. In NGCA kits will be distributed in-kind by CF HADC and procurements will be done by TGH in GCA. The COVID-19+ hygiene kits were devised by TGH based on the WASH cluster kits and adjusted to cover other urgent needs of institutions. The amounts range according to the type and size of institutions (see Indicator section). A 1.3.: Information on the prevention of infectious diseases in educational in social institutions. In GCA children and personnel of 200 EIs/SIs will additionally benefit from sensitization awareness events on hygiene practices and COVID-19 preventives measures. In NGCA in view of restrictive context only leaflets on COVID-19 prevention measures will be provided. Outcome 2. A 2.1.: Selection of beneficiary households. Selection of households will be done based on WASH cluster criteria in coordination with local authorities and actors working in the same areas. In NGCA selection will be conducted in partnership with CF HACD. Final selection will be made based on a scoring system and cumulative vulnerabilities. Preferences will be given to households with: low incomes, using negative coping strategies, multiple children, members with disabilities, chronic diseases, pregnant/lactating women, single-headed households, IDPs, guardians of children, persons with special needs and isolated elderly. A 2.2.: Distribution of e-vouchers for hygiene and hygiene in-kind kits. In GCA distributions will be done through TGH e-voucher modality. 1 round for each household is envisaged including family kits and PSN kits. The response aims at covering urgent needs of beneficiaries in hygiene basic for 3 months. The amounts of e-vouchers/kits range according to the size of families. Kits have been extended to include a wider list of items aimed at covering the needs of the populations affected by the ongoing conflict as well as to prevent and respond to the COVID-19 pandemic. In NGCA kits will be distributed in-kind by CF HADC and procurements will be done by TGH in GCA. Home deliveries will be organised to physically impaired individuals. Information leaflets on COVID-19 prevention and project leaflets encompassing TGH and CF HADC CRM will be provided to the beneficiaries. The COVID-19 Ukraine Assistance Delivery Protocol will be strictly followed as required.Monitoring will be implemented based on TGH Results-based Monitoring (RBM) framework. Baseline data collected at the project start will be used as a reference to measure project outcomes. Recommendations from PDM will be taken into account. In GCA, TGH 4 partners will be engaged along with TGH. In NGCA, TGH Project Officer based in Donetsk will oversee the implementation of activities. Outcome 3. A.3.1.: Project monitoring and learning. Results of PDM, best practices and lessons learned will be regularly shared with all relevant stakeholders. A.3.2.: Establishing a project Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM). Beneficiaries will be consulted in all stages of the project. A project CRM will have multiple feedback channels to ensure access and inclusion. TGH has a Safeguarding Policy for the Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and all staff will receive PSEA training.
