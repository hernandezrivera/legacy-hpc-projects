Project Summary

Overall, the humanitarian crisis in Somalia is among the most complex and longstanding
emergencies in the world. Malnutrition remains a challenge for the Children and Women of Somalia.
According to the FSNAU nutrition report, 2020 post GU results show favorable outcomes yet for nutrition it is estimated that in 2021 around 2.3 million people (1.14 million for treatment services and 1.16 million for preventive services) in Somalia will require both lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition support and this number includes children under the age of five and pregnant and lactating women and girls as well.
Cumulative vulnerabilities, including those related to gender, disability and age poses a risk that undermines the livelihood and underscores the need for an equitable humanitarian response.
Women and girls, especially those with disabilities face additional challenges as well as heightened risks
of gender-based violence in Somalia. Based on a report commissioned by Ministry of Women and Human
Rights Development (MoWHRD) it was revealed that children with disabilities face challenges in terms of
access to nutrition sites. The nutrition cluster hence commits to ensuring gender analysis in all interventions, as well considering women and girls for nutrition interventions. These vulnerable population groups are more exposed to the risks of malnutrition due to the prevailing “triple threat” i.e. COVID 19 pandemic, continued floods and desert locus. COVID 19 pandemic had an adverse effect on the service delivery whereby access of population of health and nutrition facilities was compromised.
The impacts of this triple threat not only led to aggravation of the the shocks but also rendered the vulnerable groups to the causes of malnutrition such as food insecurity decreasing purchasing power and morbidity due to water borne diseases and low immunization services. Similarly, as a result of floods there were reported displacements that further exposed the population to poor livelihood conditions. The main contributory factors, therefore, include the high number of children effected by acute watery diarrhea, malaria and the concurrent reported low coverage of measles vaccination and vitamin A supplementation (FSNAU). Responding these protracted crisis will eventually contribute to minimize and reduce the morbidity and mortality of the affected communities with special focus on children under 5 years and pregnant and lactating women through the provision of life saving emergency nutrition services by establishing Fixed and mobile OTPs supported by a network of Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs). The interventions are in line with the HRP of 2021 for Somalia that has components on responding to the severe malnutrition and crisis affected population that has priorities set by the Humanitarian Technical Clusters and Somalia Humanitarian Fund (SHF) for emergency interventions. INTERSOS is proposing in considering to the country strategic plan of nutrition response to scale up its interventions through supporting 1 fixed facility and 2 mobile OTPs in every district with a network of CNVs in Bay and Middle Shabelle in which INTERSOS has been providing emergency response for more than two decades. INTERSOS were running integrated health and nutrition projects into all above regions, in the meantime, there are three ongoing projects targeting g into Jowhar, Mahadaay of Middle Shabelle through provision of OPD consultations, vaccinations, Reproductive health, Screening of U5, PLWs, OTP admission and SC for all referred severe malnourished children. INTERSOS will engage 20 CVs in every district of implementation. The Nutrition Package includes screening of under five children and PLWs immunization (for both routine and supplementary vaccines), deworming, vitamin A supplement. To ensure protection mainstreaming, staff will be trained in identification of protection concerns are referral for specialized interventions.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement Some of the major risks facing the targeted population includes incidents of Gender-based violence (GBV), especially in IDP sites. Abuses against civilians, including widespread sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), limitations on the freedom of movement, social exclusion of marginalized communities, forced evictions and secondary displacement remain a pervasive feature of the conflict in targeted locations. INTERSOS will ensure meaningful representation of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, persons with disabilities) and play a key role in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination. Through community, engagement and provision of services at community level will actively ensure to include all target beneficiaries in an equal opportunity to serve for and provide the interventions.INTERSOS will collect and use data disaggregated by age, gender in a sense of understanding the served beneficiaries with their specific age groups and gender to mitigate in the upcoming projects to integrate the lessons learnt during the project implementation. INTERSOS will identify groups who face difficulties accessing services (e.g. women, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons, persons fromminority groups, persons belonging to marginalized groups) and work with community leaders, religious leaders and service providers to support equal and safe access to services without discrimination. INTERSOS will identify the power dynamics in the area by focusing on who has access to service, who manages the facilities and who is vulnerable and marginalized. INTERSOS will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by Identifying and prioritizing the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized and use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups. Considering this when determining locations for interventions. Furthermore, INTERSOS eld staffs will undertake training's on protection mainstreaming with the support of INTERSOS Protection department. Mitigating the challenges to complaint and feedback mechanism by putting complain suggestion boxes, free hotlines to pass messages and calls for all beneficiaries in an equal manner to provide their concerns with highly confidential system. The projectwill Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to the needs specically by consulting women, men, boys, girls, youth, persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalized groups to understand their needs, coping strategies and preferences for protection services.Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect the targeted population, to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. INTERSOS will set up accessible, confidential and locally understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improving programming, understanding community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct eg.Feedback box in centers. A strong feedback mechanism will enhance the capacity ofINTERSOS Staff to prevent challenges and address areas of the project weakness thusimproving risks management in relation of the delivery of services.
