Project Summary

DRC proposes to undertake a life-saving and sustainable assistance to Disaster-Affected Communities (DACs) through provision of emergency and sustainable WASH interventions (water, sanitation and hygiene). Areas that are mostly affected by droughts, floods and armed conflict will be targeted in the emergency interventions. DRC shall target areas within its intervention and where DRC has presence. DRC has field offices in Mogadishu, Hargeisa, Baidoa, Dollow, Kismaayo and Beletweyne. There are also several small offices across Somalia which will support the proposed interventions. Proposed key activities to be implemented include water trucking through water voucher, rehabilitation of strategic water sources, supply of fast-moving parts for pumps and generators targeting boreholes, construction of emergency latrines and other related WASH activities. DRC defines DACs as people who are internally displaced and are affected by drought and other factors and returnees seeking for reintegration. Therefore, DRC targets IDPs, returnees, refugees and host communities. DRC understands that the primary displacements in Somalia is as a result of natural disasters and man-made conflict. However, secondary displacements are now a common reality due to either military offensive or forced evictions by government. These displacements are the key driver of humanitarian WASH needs in Somalia. IDPs are currently residing in urban and Peri-urban informal crowded settlements, with insufficient access to WASH services that raise protection and health concerns. Therefore, DRC will ensure that, where IDPs settle they get immediate assistance of life-saving interventions. These will include water trucking where there is no safe water source, emergency latrine, sanitation and hygiene kits and rapid hygiene promotion. On the other hand, DRC targets Host Communities - to ensure peaceful coexistence between the local population and target groups and also because many of them are equally vulnerable and in need of humanitarian assistance. Refugee returnees are also targeted with durable solutions in which case DRC will focus on governance and overall management of water and sanitation facilities. In this case, DRC will ensure there is a robust market system in place for continued service provision. For the case of water, DRC will encourage existing water source owners – both private and government, to extend water services to the settlements of DACs. For the case of sanitation, DRC will encourage a market-based approach – looking at creating demand for latrine construction and building capacity of the supply side to ensure enterprise-creation and therefore sustainable services beyond the project period. Hygiene promotion in durable solutions will include messaging and house to house sensitization of safe hygiene practices with emphasis of hand-washing at critical times, safe handling of food, and environmental sanitation. Specific to cash-based interventions (CBIs) and vouchers, DRC proposes to have water vouchers instead of blanket water trucking. DRC has experience in using water vouchers in previous projects. For WASH NFIs (hygiene kits and sanitation kits), DRC will prefer to give cash to households so that they can buy items of their choice. In both voucher and cash, DRC will engage a strict beneficiary selection process based on targeting the most vulnerable households. The beneficiary selection criteria shall be discussed with settlement/camp committees and agreed on. Then vouchers will be designs based on household codes and loaded with cash to collect water for the period. For hygiene kits, each household will receive cash to buy items and this will be followed with a PDM to assess how the cash was used. For sanitation kits, risk analysis will be undertake to determine if WASH committees can buy the items or in-kind distribution is done by DRC.

Project Activities

DRC will target both people with life-saving emergency response and recovery to durable solutions to water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) needs. Sustainable access to water:DRC proposes borehole drilling with full compliance to cluster guidelines. Drill two (2) strategic boreholes to supply water in Baidoa and Dollow towns. DRC also proposes to rehabilitate eight (8) boreholes targeting water supply to IDP settlements and host communities. This will include provision of spare parts (Solar pumps, generators) construction or rehabilitation water elevated water tanks and extension of pipeline system to provide sustainable water sources to the affected populations. On shallow wells, DRC will target construction/rehabilitation and improvement of hand-dug wells. DRC targets 20 shallow wells in Jowhar, Baletweyne, Baidoa, Awdal and Gedo.Under rainwater harvesting, DRC will improve berkads (sizing, covering, lining and water extraction). This activity will target areas with groundwater scarcity in Northern Somalia such as Sool, Saanag, Hiran and central Somalia in Bay regions. DRC also proposes surface runoff water extraction through construction or rehabilitation of balleys (water pans) through rainwater harvesting. DRC targets one (1) water pans complete with treatment works and supply to IDP camps and urban supply network. This activity targets Baidoa town.Other major activity under water, is installation of treatment works (small/medium scale) including sedimentation and disinfection. DRC targets to install one (1) small-scale water supply systems including pumping, power supply through an hybrid power grid (solar and generator). As well as construction of elevated water tank and distribution system in Dollow from the Daua River intake. All sustainable water projects will establish water management committees to ensure proper maintenance of water services. Where private boreholes are targeted, DRC will ensure an MOU is signed between DRC, borehole owner and government. Emergency Water Supply:DRC will undertake emergency water trucking with temporary distribution systems for 60-90 days. DRC will target most vulnerable households by use of water vouchers to access water trucking. This will ensure transparency and value for money. DRC also will promote Household Water Treatment and Safe Storage (HWTSS) especially for people targeted with water trucking and other temporary water systems through pipeline extension/rehabilitation of existing water supply system. DRC will also monitor water quality at household and community levels. For wells, disinfection through chlorination will be done. DRC will also distribute Jerry cans for transport and storage of water.Training of water management committee to build local governance capacity targeting at least 30% women in operation and maintenance in all target locations. For sanitation, DRC proposes construction/rehabilitation of sanitation facilities. This will include latrines build with solid walls and locks at doors; desludgable latrines for vulnerable IDP populations, host communities and returnees living in camps and urban informal settlements. Design of the latrines will be done in consultation with respective beneficiaries considering the culture of the people and access by different segments of the community. DRC will also ensure that all latrines are fitted with hand-wash facilities. DRC will fabricate and install metallic hand washing facilities at strategic locations that can easily be accessed by all community members so as to promote hand washing at critical times.As part of promoting sustainable sanitation in Somalia, DRC proposes to establish simplified Fecal Sludge Management (FSM) systems. DRC will pilot this in Dollow and Baidoa based on a formative study carried out in 2019.Other activities proposed include cleaning campaigns (waste removal and disposal); provision to sanitation tools; distribution of hygiene kits and carrying out hygiene promotion campaigns.

Mitigation Measures

1a. Protection of civilians: Exposure to landmines or explosive remnants of war - DDG is a department of DRC and has developed community safety tools used to create awareness of beneficiaries to landmines and ERWs. 2a. Exclusion and marginalization: Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance - DRC has strict selection criteria for beneficiaries that identifies marginalized groups as vulnerable groups. DRC has trained field teams in ensuring that these groups are targeted. DRC has also sensitized field staff in following the organization’s code of conduct which if breached can lead to disciplinary action.3c. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services - DRC will ensure that they take services close to the beneficiaries. In WASH programming, water extensions will be done and emergency latrines will target ut most five households to reduce risk of exposure to SGBV. 3d. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Delivery of humanitarian assistance affects peaceful coexistence and spark intercommunity conflict over access to resources - DRC criteria specific to targeting IDPs, Refugee Returnees and vulnerable Host Community, a Do No Harm approach that is seen to promote peaceful co-existence of Displacement-Affected Communities (DACs). DRC will ensure the selection/targeting criteria is shared with WASH/IDP management committees while DRC staffs will ensure that the selection criteria is strictly followed. The committees must be selected based on AGD criteria to ensure all affected groups are involved in the intervention and their voices heard. 4a. Protection mainstreaming: Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areas - DRC will ensure that land occupied by IDPs is allocated by government or that the landowners agree to accommodate them for a reasonable time to remedy any forced evictions. For protracted IDPs seeking durable solutions, DRC will ensure that they have land allocated by government or they have land ownership documents like title deeds that guarantees them reasonable lease of 5+years before any WASH infrastructure is installed or constructed. 4b. Protection mainstreaming: Beneficiaries risk exposure to violence during registration/distribution - DRC will ensure that there are IDP committees who organize people in order to reduce chances of scrambling during distribution. During distribution of WASH NFIs (sanitation and hygiene kits), DRC will also ensure that vulnerable groups (women and children, PLDs and elderly) have special queues to minimize physical injuries or that they have delivered to their places of settlement.4d. Protection mainstreaming: Barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanisms. DRC has a robust complaints mechanism system that uses both sms and voice data to capture beneficiary feedback. There is a dedicated staff under MEAL who gets the feedback, analyses and shares with concerned sector leads to respond to the questions or feedback being provided by the beneficiaries.

Please provide further explanation

DRC has a strong design approach in durable solutions with a strong focus on re-integration of IDPs and returnees with the host community. Accordingly, DRC will ensure that conflict analysis becomes part of project design ensuring that different needs of displacement-affected communities are considered throughout the project. To ensure that the project brings durable solutions, DRC will focus on governance and overall management of water and sanitation facilities. In this case, DRC will ensure there is a robust market system in place for continued service provision. For the case of water, DRC will encourage existing water source owners – both private and government, to extend water services to the settlements of DACs. For the case of sanitation, DRC will encourage a market-based approach – looking at creating demand for latrine construction and building capacity of the supply side to ensure enterprise-creation and therefore sustainable services beyond the project period. Hygiene promotion in durable solutions will include messaging and house to house sensitization of safe hygiene practices with emphasis of hand-washing at critical times, safe handling of food, and environmental sanitation. For any permanent infrastructure, like boreholes, water tanks and kiosks, DRC will ensure that the land on which they are installed have right legal documents or at least ownership that is recognized locally by community or government. DRC will ensure there is a clear exit strategy on the project and that this will be inbuilt into the project design to ensure that the community will be able to sustain the running of the WASH facilities rehabilitated or constructed under this project way after the intervention. First, DRC will establish a project implementation unit in the target community whose role will be to represent the community on project design. For water point rehabilitated or constructed, a water management committee will trained on operation and maintenance, spare parts supply chain, and preventive maintenance procedures. The local authorities will also be capacity-built on issues of governance and oversight onmanagement of community water schemes. For projects where DRC will partner with a private water source owner such as a borehole, DRC will ensure an MOU is signed between LA, borehole owner and DRC. The MOU will spell the roles of each party with DRC's role lasting until the end of the project period. DRC will help the water management committees and private borehole owners in coming up with key performance indicators to be used in monitoring water service provision. The community and private borehole owners will be capacity built to develop a strategic plan for their schemes. DRC will also impart skills on water savings, water allocation across different users and resource management to ensure sustainable development.
