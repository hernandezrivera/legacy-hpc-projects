Project Summary

The project will provide food assistance to 48,800 vulnerable CAR refugees in camps located in Adamaoua, East and North regions. The food Assistance will be delivered to beneficiaries through monthly cash based transfers throughout the year 2020.

Besoins / Needs

The Far North Region has been experiencing increasing humanitarian crises mostly caused by Boko Haram armed groups. IDPs affected by the crisis are struggling to survive in difficult conditions abandoning all their properties and livelihood assets and living with host families in various safer locations in villages or towns, putting enormous pressure on already scarce community resources and the local economy. The most affected divisions of Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Tsanaga and Mayo Sava are the most food insecure areas of the far north region. The presence of over 400,000 displaced people mainly IDPs (63%), returnees (23%) and out of camp Refugees (11%) has put more pressure on the natural resources and other economic assets of these areas. The arrival of IDPs in food-insecure areas has severely affected host communities. Boko Haram attacks have disrupted the economic, agricultural, and pastoral activities in the Far North region, causing a depletion of livelihoods and household assets. Therefore, the existing chronic vulnerability and structural food insecurity have been aggravated.The region is predominantly arid and regularly hit by drought and seasonal floods. The most recent floods in October 2019 left about 40,000 people displaced and above 500,000 hectares of cultivated farmland destroyed. The October 2019 Cadre Harmonisé highlighted that 1.3 million people in the Far North are food insecure. Out of this number, 232,934 people are severely food insecure, which means that they have limited or no access to sufficient, nutritious food required to live a healthy life. The Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWS NET) reports that vulnerable families in the conflict-affected areas of the Far North Region are experiencing ‘Stress’ level of acute food insecurity due to flood and pest-related crop damage, and also attacks by Boko Haram. As food insecurity affects differently men, women, girls and boys of various ages, Food assistance will prioritize the most affected group . In addition access to land, resources, income-generating activities; credit, seeds have an impact on the economic situation of the household and on food security. During Distribution special attention will be paid on households with people specific needs and necessary support will be provided taking into consideration of the situation of the head of the household (whether male or female or child); the presence of a person with specific needs (pregnant woman, elderly person, disabled person etc.) in the household also has an impact on food security. During post retribution monitoring attention will placed on the role played by men, women, girls and boys including decision-making on food consumption and food security in the household. Affected people will be closely involved in various stages including beneficiaries targeting process, food distribution also through complaints and feedback mechanisms they could freely express their views and concerns on the project implementation, that is considered for projects adjustment.WFP together with local authorities and partners, conducts assessments to identify IDPs to be considered in the projects based on vulnerability criteria. These criteria include household size, presence of under-5 children, the elderly and handicapped, women-headed households, assets. Women and men will be equal participants, decision-makers and beneficiaries to enhance resilience and promote reconciliation and social cohesion between host and displaced communities.
