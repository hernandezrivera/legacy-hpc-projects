Project Summary

Care for Children & Old age in South Sudan (CCOSS) will provide life-saving shelter and NFI assistance to the persons affected by conflict and natural disasters such as flooding in Abyei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Lakes and Warrap. Newly displaced population especially those affected by recent conflict and population affected by flooding as well as returnees will be prioritized. Where feasible, cash modality will be implemented especially for emergency shelter assistance.
Within the framework of shelter and Non-Food Items (NFI) Cluster objectives and sector strategic goals, this project is initiated aiming to contribute in developing shelter solutions and NFI assistance. The purpose of this project is to ensure that vulnerable people affected by crisis have access to basic domestic items and receive several shelter solutions. The provision of timely shelter and NFIs will help newly and, protracted IDPs, returnees and other affected populations to restore their dignity and reduce exposure to potential risks associated with protection and health.
The project will consider pregnant women, chronically ill people, elderly, unaccompanied minors, and people with disabilities as highly vulnerable groups. Likewise, the project aims to support resilience of returnees and integration of IDPs through early recovery activities by supporting durable solutions and the transition from dependency to self-reliance.
The project will target 52,000 individuals disaggregated as Men 9360, Women 21840, Boys 6240 and Girls 14560, distributed as follows Abyei 10400, Aweil East 14040, Aweil North 4680, Aweil South 3640, Cueibet 6760 and Twic 12480. These localities have a high severity ranking of people in needs as identified by the shelter-NFIs cluster.
The project will ensure that emergency shelters address the emergency and immediate shelter needs in order to provide protection from life threatening diseases and restore dignity to all persons of concern. This will be achieved by ensuring IDPs, returnees and vulnerable communities have access to basic domestic items and several shelter solutions (emergency and transitional) that provide privacy, security, protection from the elements, and a space to live and store belongings in a dignified manner. Also, the project will focus on supporting transition, recovery and reintegration of returnees, vulnerable and protracted households and IDPs through provision of appropriate and environmentally friendly transitional shelters and provision of shelter construction materials for self-building or supported with Labor costs for vulnerable households with lack of capacity to implement. Additionally, IDPs, returnees and vulnerable communities will receive essential NFI kits and/or distribution of emergency life-saving shelter items. Also, Community awareness on the impact of Shelter/NFI activities on the environment and mitigation measures will be reinforced. Addition to this, the project will implement constructive training program on alternate building techniques and technical guidance for effective shelter delivery. Furthermore, the project will support prepositioning of NFIs and materials to support timely response. Moreover, the project will ensure gender and environmental markers and other cross-cutting issues have been addressed through ensuring that the needs of all beneficiaries are addressed. CCOSS will inform and consult affected population at each stage of the project and will establish complaint and feedback mechanism. The project will ensure synergy with other sectors to ensure synergy in the intervention and to avoid duplication of services.

1. Needs

The key drivers of Shelter and NFI needs in Abyei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Lakes and Warrap are conflict related internal displacements, returnee influxes, flooding and food insecure. In addition, displaced population living in protracted situation also need periodical shelter and NFI assistance. The intensity of the new clashes and their indiscriminate nature is now causing widespread devastation to housing, household assets and public infrastructure, prompting many to leave their homes. Those who are internally displaced require Shelter and NFI assistance urgently, as many, including children, women, elderly, and others with specific needs, find themselves crammed into overcrowded public buildings, or forced to sleep outdoors exposed to severe weather and threats. As a result of clashes, flooding and related displacements, the People in need (PIN) of shelter and NFI cluster is on the increase. Inter-Agency floods Assessment Response Report for Abyei that was conducted between 23rd September – 27th September 2024 established the following. Most of the shelters in those floods-affected areas collapsed down by the heavy rain and severely submerged in water leaving thousands of the families in need of temporary or transitional shelters and basic non-food items, such as blankets, cooking utensils, and hygiene kits, particularly to the displaced households living in temporary setting along the main roads. The report recommended that there is need for provision of basic non-food items, such as blankets, cooking utensils, and hygiene kits, particularly to the floods-affected households living in temporary residing along the main roads. A total of 3,478 HH, (20,868 Individuals) were affected by floods in Abyei. In Aweil East (according the IRNA conducted between 29-31 August 2024) the total approximate households affected by the flooding in August 2024 is 28,923HH amounting to 99,732 individuals. Of the demography, 15,670 (51,301 individuals) are severely affected, 7,646HH (28,183 individuals) moderately affected and 5,282 (20,248 individuals) minimally affected respectively. Currently, the affected population are sharing the already constrained and limited resources with host communities, which is further increasing the level of vulnerability across the county. In Aweil South, the IRNA conducted between 14-16 August 2024, carried out by partners, were able to verify and estimate total number of affected households 14,217 (85,302 individuals) by flood in the whole county. Among those affected population there are returnees who are integrated within communities. The most vulnerable groups affected by the disaster are children, pregnant and lactating mothers, old aged persons and people with disabilities. Shelter and NFI needs arising from key drivers mentioned earlier are expected to continue in 2025. Some of households in IDPs camps live in shelters that are not adequate and do not provide protection from weather elements and privacy. Generally, population affected by conflict and flooding lose all their belongings and shelter requiring complete NFI kit and emergency shelter assistance whereas those affected by flooding require partial NFI assistance and shelter repair and shelter reconstruction assistance. Immediate provision of shelter assistance is critical to ensure privacy and safety, mitigate GBV risks, and safeguard displaced populations from harsh environmental conditions to preserve their health amid the rainy season. Provision of essential household items will be crucial for restoring basic daily activities. The targeted caseloads lack access to essential lifesaving NFI items and are often unable to support themselves through communal connections or coping mechanisms through other locally available resources.

2. Project activities

1. Engage the community in a way that stimulates active participation and promote accountability to the affected populations. This will be through focus group discussions; setting up complaint and feedback mechanism (feedback desks in the community) prior to project implementation; formation of committees. This will enhance the community’s dignity while increasing their ability to respond to new shock and build on existing skills.2. Conduct needs assessments, participate in inter-agency rapid assessments to identify felt needs prior to any shelter solutions and NFI assistance 3. Conduct market assessments in locations with functional markets to identify the availability of Shelter and NFIs in the markets, assess the supply chain of the items, check the quality of the NFIs and identify the suppliers/ vendors of the products, identify the risks of the CVA modality and suggest mitigation measures. Depending on market assessment, the project team will identify the type of cash- based interventions; conditional or unconditional. Local traders will be identified and selected through a competitive process following standard operating procedures.4. The response for shelter solutions and NFI assistance will focus on provision of shelter solutions and core life-saving items (in-kind or cash modality) to returnees, newly displaced IDPs, protracted IDPs and vulnerable communities. Those to be supported by cash will be depended on market functionality. This is aimed at addressing the emergency shelter and NFI needs for affected populations 5. Shelter upgrades (In-kind or cash) for returnees /protracted IDPs/Host Community and people in secondary displacement. 6. Conduct training targeting beneficiaries to improve the skills on construction methodologies and techniques in order to achieve an effective shelter delivery as a durable shelter solution7. Construction / rehabilitation of shelter through owner driven approach. 8. Establishment of Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM).9. Monitoring of quality service provision – PDM will be conducted two weeks after the distribution is finalized. The format for the PDM will include conducting focus group discussions, random sampling for interviews. This activity will ensure fast, lifesaving response to the right people, enhances ability to use S/NFIs for supporting results bases health, protection, livelihood and hygiene needs and reducing overlap to reach the truly vulnerable. 10. Mainstream HLP AAP/CCE and conflict sensitive approaches throughout all stages of S/NFI response through conducting conflict analysis, sensitizing local chiefs on women rights especially about land, avoiding manipulation of distribution of S/NFIs by remaining neutral and practicing “do-no-harm” principle. This activity will reduce harm to communities and build resilience by strengthening existing systems and community dynamics. 11. Mainstream GBV throughout all stages of S/NFI response – this will be done through conducting gender safety audits, ensuring that distributions are done in an open space, during the day, and close to the camps. This will ensure gender and protection risks are addressed directly, mitigating the chances of exacerbated vulnerabilities
