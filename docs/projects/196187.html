Project Summary

Throughout 2023, VO plans to implement comprehensive food security interventions by applying a variety of modalities (In-kind, Cash, and vouchers) to address the needs of the different groups with proper modality.
The proposed project will effectively improve the food security status of the assessed food-insecure people through life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance by improving food consumption, dietary diversity, and avoiding negative coping strategies. the planned activities to be implemented during 2023 are the following activities:
1. Emergency Food assistance distribution for HHs of crisis-affected IDPs through proper modality (Cash - In Kind)
2. Regular Food assistance per modality (in-kind, Cash, Voucher) for HHs
3. Provision of free and subsidized bread for IDP individuals and host communities.
Moreover, in terms of the implementation activities, Violet is planning for cash and voucher modalities whenever possible for regular food assistance to ensure market revival in the targeted location, taking into consideration market functionality/proximity/capacity, also the functionality of financial service providers in the targeted location. the value of the voucher will be according to FSL and CWG recommendations for food assistance, which is currently around 65 USD per HH based on their last update. In the meantime, VO will consider an in-kind modality for regular food assistance where there are low-functionality of markets in the targeted areas to increase food availability in the targeted locations; the weight of the in-kind food basket (when applied) will be based on the WFP recommended food basket composition and FSL cluster response packages.
Regarding beneficiary targeting, VO will still consider blanket distribution to the camp's residents based on the current cluster recommendations and applying fair eligibility and vulnerability criteria regarding food distribution off-camps, taking into consideration the gender balance and targeting the most vulnerable groups such as women-headed households, child-headed households, elderlies headed households, families with big size numbers and people with special needs and chronically ill members, as well as households with pregnant and lactating women, Moreover, during the design and implementation of projects Violet will ensure the design of different activities to better inclusion of PWDs and female beneficiaries in the project activities.
Based on the situation/context analysis, VO will ensure considering the best suitable modality for all of the food assistance; then if voucher, then Violet will ensure proper and enough vouchers distribution and redemption period, and if in-kind, then the distribution will be through setting distribution points in the targeted location based on recently updated VO distribution SOPs during COVID-19 context.
Out of the total project budget (1047000), no amount has been secured yet for the 2023 year; however, 560,000 USD is in the pipeline.

3 - Needs

According to food security and agriculture sectoral analysis in the HNO 2023, an estimated 15 million people will need food and agriculture assistance, representing 68 percent of the total population. At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure, including all 2 million people living in camps who are deemed to be 100 percent food insecure, and 2.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity, an alarming increase of people at risk who could shift to become food insecure during 2023 if the crisis continues at same worsening levels. Also, it is important to note that the sharp decline in crop production and the entrenchment of food insecurity during the 2021-2022 cropping season was driven by not only weather anomalies but also other drivers, including issues with water scarcity and availability and disrupted services. The decline in agricultural production output was not only limited to rainfed food production systems but also irrigated systems such as cereals, legumes, barley, and vegetables. Substantial harvest losses combined with chronic economic deprivation and displacement have contributed to mounting food insecurity and associated negative food and livelihood coping strategies for vulnerable Syrians. Moreover, The WFP remote food security monitoring and recent FSL assessment in Syria (FSA,FSLA) estimate that 70% of Syrian HHs are food insecure, with the highest concentration in Idleb (74%), Quneitra and Al-Raqqa (68%), Aleppo (68%).

4 - Implementation of activities

VO aims to respond to emergency needs by the implementation of different activities with an appropriate modality based on the needs, BNFs preference availability, and functionality of the markers, however; Prior to the implementation, VO will engage the BNFs, and project stakeholders in the project design and implementation to ensure the local ownership of the project. Thus, VO will sign MOUs with the LCs demonstrating their roles and responsibilities. Moreover, VO will ensure close coordination with the FSL cluster and humanitarian actors in the field to avoid potential overlapping.During the implementation, VO will ensure the BNFs' protection and do No Harm principle by consulting the BNFs on the distribution location, Timing and arranging the priority ques for elderlies, women, and PWD, but also through training the staff on the Organization code of conduct and PSEA policy. To avoid the tension between the communities and different groups, VO will include a % of the most vulnerable HC members in the project but also will announce the project criteria and make sure it's clear to the BNFs and activate the CRM mechanism.For the CVA modality, VO will ensure the market is functioning prior to the distribution in order to avoid the harm that may occur to the non-BNFs

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk of discrimination if the service is provided to a specific group and others are excludedLikelihood: Medium; Impact: HighBefore implementation, VO conducts assessments and selects vulnerable communities based on severity; selection criteria (eligibility/vulnerability) are transparently communicated through the AAP team. AAP team has access to service mapping to communicate information with communities about alternative services.2) PWD or at-risk groups might be excluded from assistance or distribution.Likelihood: Low; Impact: HighInformation sharing and distribution points are suitable for PWDs; conduct AAP sessions to inform about requesting special assistance (transportation/delivery) for PWD/elderly/pregnant BNFs; consult with stakeholders to identify excluded beneficiaries, and train staff on inclusion.3) Risk of GBV to and from or in overcrowded distributionsLikelihood: Medium; Impact: HighSelection of suitable distribution (time, location, accessibility), referral of special cases, avoid long queues and allow clear space for waiting, operate with multiple distribution sites.4) At-risk groups do not receive needed servicesLikelihood: Medium; Impact: MediumCoordination with relevant sectors to avoid duplications, collaboration with local protection team to ensure equitable access to services, consult BNFs in sex-segregated FGDs, systematic use of WG Questions as a tool to include disability in the selection, considering intersectionality in targeting


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

VO targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements.Potential risks:1) Request of favors, e.g., SEA, for selecting BNFs for the servicesMitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return. In any case, emphasize complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as an alternative, and ensur appropriate staffing during any distribution or service provision.2) SEA misconduct from staff to selected beneficiaries or staffMitigation: ensure regular staff training on PSEA and how to engage with beneficiaries, communication of PSEA policy and complaints mechanisms to BNFs, ensure diverse and gender-balanced project and MEAL teams, clear visibility of staff, avoid overcrowded distribution sites and allocate clear waiting areas, identify at-risk households and provide special services e.g. transportation or delivery, participation of protection team in the distribution, if neededViolet will conduct sex-segregated FGDs and KIIs to ensure participation of women and girls during all phases of the project, especially in design of the activities and monitoring


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: activities implementation is stopped by violence or interference by the opposition groupsMitigation 1: monitoring the security situation and reporting criminal violence along its main delivery routes and in its areas of operation.Risks 2: fees might be collected by the LCs or camp management in return for assistance leading to Aid diversionMitigation 2: Ensure the humanitarian nature of the project by communicating the red flags with the BNFs and community leaders. In case of aid diversion, the activity will be suspended, and the incident will be reported to OCHA. stakeholders will be involved to facilitate the activities and get their supportRisk 3: markets are not functioning properlyMitigation 3: VO confirmed the markets are functioning, and the security situation allows the implementation. In case the situation changes, VO would consider relocating the areas of intervention where the required conditions are met.Risk 4: Diversion of cash when using (MTA)Mitigation 4: VO disposes of 3 FWA with MTAs for all CBR activities, and its currently in the process of contracting with new MTAs. the MTAs will be vetted by an ATC check (in both UK and USA ATC platforms). Risk 5: Tension might occur between the HC and IDPs if targeted BNFs of cash assistance (in USD) are only IDPs, Particularly with SYP devaluation and the Covid-19 outbreak, when HC HHs/non-BNFs are economically vulnerable, and they lose their purchasing power.
