Project Summary

Violence against women and girls is the most pervasive human rights violation in the world. Twenty-eight years after the Beijing platform for action, despite unprecedented momentum and commitments to eliminate violence, prevalence has persisted in Cameroon. This persistence is notably in Nomadic communities, Hard to reach communities and Minority communities in Humanitarian and conflict settings particularly in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon, a problem persistence which has lasted now for 7 years. Despite the efforts of the Ministry of Social Affairs, NGOs, operational partners, and the ministry of Public Health in Cameroon, distinct barriers still exist which are
1) Lack of an efficient strategy for Protection of vulnerable groups, SRHR and SGBV service delivery in this target missed out and underserved Communities especially in the persistence of the ongoing humanitarian concern.
2) Lack of concrete strategies for community outreach. These challenges highlight the need for culturally appropriate strategies that not only emphasize service delivery but also incorporate engagement, advocacy and capacity building of local actors to engage communities, dispel myths, and bring a community-focused perspective that would help nurture a collaborative dialogue that resonates deeply within communities and counter the barriers to Protection, SRHR and SGBV responses posed by misinformation, skepticism, and fear and bolster target response coverage generally and protection particularly.
The existence of these barriers has forced women and girls in these target communities to shoulder disproportionate opportunities to their Health outcomes, livelihood, Economic potentials, seriously limiting their life and initiatives in Cameroon. This undermining is worsened by existing ongoing humanitarian conflict, road networks to hard to reach communities, the nomadic nature of some community settings and cultural barriers limiting also the contribution of women and girls to local development and allows their livelihood initiatives tampered adversely. In the face of their numerous challenges, the nature of the communities also limits their access to resources including technical resources which impedes their productivity, exacerbates post-harvest losses, and widens barriers to accessing high income value markets, worsening their efforts to self-promotion in supporting local development initiatives.
In response, GLOCIR is proposing a 24 months comprehensive approach addressing systemic barriers, foster an enabling environment conducive for women's sexual and reproductive health, Protection, livelihood, entrepreneurship and participation in the economy in these various settings proposed by reinforcing the technical capacities to access to essential knowledge, networks, and platforms they need to launch and scale successful entrepreneurial initiatives. The intervention will further provide livelihood seed capital and will restructure local women led entrepreneurial initiatives into promoting the development of value chains giving them technical, Business and production access penetrating and marketing their products to high value purchasing markets. This 24 months intervention will ensure that woman have access to health responses with key components of Persons living with HIV (PLWHIV), ANC medical bill coverage for vulnerable women, Protection and livelihood seed capital provision, mentorship, partnerships and access to funding that will enable them to contribute and participate in local development, increase income, improve living conditions, protect themselves, Build resilience and transition to autonomy and be economically empowered benefitting the community at large by enabling them to contribute meaningfully, support, participate and promote local development.
