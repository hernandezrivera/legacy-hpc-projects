Project Summary

Human Trafficking is defined in the UN Convention on Transnational Organised Crime as “the recruitment, transportation, transfer, harbouring or receipt of persons, by means of the threat or use of force or other forms of coercion, of abduction, of fraud, of deception, of the abuse of power or of a position of vulnerability or of the giving or receiving of payments or benefits to achieve the consent of a person having control over another person, for the purpose of exploitation.
Nigeria has been identified as a source, transit and destination country for VOTs subjected to forced labour and sex trafficking. IOM has documented that internal trafficking occurs within Nigeria mainly for purposes of domestic labour and sexual exploitation, while international or cross-border trafficking occurs for purposes of forced labour, domestic servitude and sexual exploitation.
In North-East Nigeria, the influx of IDPs at camps in Borno state has increased vulnerability to trafficking, smuggling and other forms of exploitation. Generally, IDP camps in North-East Nigeria have been known as hotspot areas for human trafficking as well as for irregular migration.

1 - Needs

In Jere, MMC, Konduga, Kala Balge and Ngala, we have a total of about 997,077 IDPs, returnees and host communities in POC sites and host communities in critical need of general protection based on protection sector PiN generated. They are almost entirely reliant on the provision of basic services by humanitarian actors. Need based assessment, beneficiary/vulnerability screening was conducted in coordination with concerned stakeholders to inform the planning and design of subsequent activities. Most importantly with intent to create awareness support on trafficking in persons and to ensure informed, evidence based planning and implementation capacity at community level across IDPs site, returnees, host communities in MMC, Jere, Konduga, Ngala and Kala balge. The protection sector priorotise activities through assessment and report from the field and through consultaions with the humanitarian and beneficiary communities. Needs and gaps are identified through coordination with partners and findings are presented in sector meetings. Base on these findings, the sector identifies priority locations and activities that the sector can support and carryout. Coordination with other sectors and harmonization of the sector strategy was carried out through the ISWJ. Further coordination with the partners is made to avoid duplication of responses and to ensure that partners are capable of implementing in target locations.

Providing assistance, including cash-based assistance to victims of trafficking in persons or those at heightened risks of violence, exploitation, serious neglect or discrimination, in order to reduce their vulnerability and the resort to negative coping mechanisms.4. Raising awareness and supporting duty bearers, including local authorities, to reduce the risk of violence, exploitation, serious neglect or discrimination, as well as to identify persons who have suffered such abuses, or at heightened risk, and to make appropriate referrals for prevention, protection and prosecution of traffickers.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Our work often put BOAID personnel and associates in positions of power or influence concerning the communities we work with. As a humanitarian aid organization, we are committed to creating and maintaining an environment that promotes its core values of human dignity and prevent the abuse and exploitation of its employees, partner and beneficiaries.BOAID has a duty of care towards the POCs and must not abuse power and influence that might have over the lives and well beings of the beneficiaries of its program. We will sensitize the communities on BOAID core principles relating to prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse.The following core principles of behavior applies to all BOAID personnel including staff, volunteers, community member, interns and consultants while under contract with BOAID and at all times, this includes both within working hours and outside standard working hours.1. Sexual exploitation and abuse by any staff: in BOAID constitute acts of gross miscunduct and are therefore ground for termination of appointment.2. Sexual activity with children (person under the age of 18) is prohibited3. Exchange of money, employment, goods or services for sex, including sexual favours or other forms of humiliating or degrading behaviour is prohibited.4. Any sexual relationship between those providing assistance and protection from BOAID with the person receiving assistance is prohibited and will attract a penalty of dismissal.BOAID personnel are obliged to adhere to this standard of behavior, any violation of this standard constitute serious misconduct. Failure to comply with these standard or failure to report sexual exploitations and abuse is grounds for disciplinary action. This action may include termination of contract or dismissal.
