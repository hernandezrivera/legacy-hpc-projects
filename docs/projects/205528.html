Project Summary

ARDF's proposed project will provide comprehensive, lifesaving protection prevention and response interventions to the floods, Sudan Crisis and conflict affected communities of Renk, Melut and Aweil South Counties. The proposal will target 4,000 beneficiaries (1000 boys;1000 men;1000 women;1000 girls) in Aweil South County and 10,672 beneficiaries (1974 boys, 2436 girls,2852 men and 3410 women) in Renk and Melut Counties. ARDF proposes to implement comprehensive, lifesaving SGBV prevention and response activities by providing quality and timely, age appropriate, case management and psychosocial support to GBV survivors.
The proposed project seeks to implement General Protection prevention, response and risk mitigation activities by providing quality and timely, age appropriate, case management and psychosocial support to GBV survivors. Further, the proposed project will provide cash assistance to the most vulnerable women and girls visiting WGFSs who are facing financial challenges to facilitate them in accessing multi-sectoral GBV services. Priority will be given to survivors who require clinical management of rape (CMR) and survivors of Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) so that they can continue to visit health facilities, access for legal aid and they can also access the nearest available centers and safe spaces that provide PSS.
This proposed project is designed to improve responses to cases of GBV and enhance the ability of survivors to recover from GBV incidences. ARDF will provide individual psychosocial support for women and girls in established women and girls' safe spaces in the targeted locations. The project's proposed activities will also include Strengthening Psychosocial Support services and collaboration with MHPSS working group, GBV awareness and risk mitigation measures, Strengthening the implementation of primary GBV preventions interventions, Strengthening coverage of dignity kit distribution and programming as part of risk mitigation, GBV preparedness and contingency planning and provision of livelihood support, through the provision of need based capacity building trainings to GBV actors and frontline service providers and strengthening GBV case management and referral pathways. The activities in the established WGFS will be conducted using the guidelines from the ministry of health and World Health Organization (WHO) on COVID-19 prevention and will include maintaining social distance, wearing of masks and washing hands with soap. ARDF also intends to strengthen gender-based violence prevention through EMAP and community care programs to reduce GBV risks on men, women, boys and girls.
The targeted locations have been selected because of the prevalent needs stated in the HNO 2023 and appearing in category 5 and 4 in the severity mapping as well as ARDF's long standing presence and GBV programming in Renk, Melut and Aweil South Counties through the ongoing World bank Project (CERSHP) reaching the two counties of Upper Nile state (Renk and Melut counties) with integrated Health/ GBV services to the host communities, Forced Returnees and Refugees affected by the ongoing Sudan crisis. Through Consortium with Relief International and funding from UNCHR, ARDF is supporting two mobile medical and protection units in Renk transit site and Paloch site who are providing health care and PSS services to the Refugees and Returnees affected by the ongoing Sudan Crisis. ARDF is supporting Aweil South with Integrated Health package mainstreaming GBV services to the vulnerable. In consortium with Help International and funding from BMZ, ARDF will be from January 2024 start a 3-year Health/WASH/GBV Transition Development Assistance (TDA) project targeting Aweil South, Aweil Centre, Aweil North and Aweil North Counties of Northern Bhar El Ghazal state.

1. Needs

In South Sudan, the UNMISS report (2023) report cites violence, abuse and exploitation as the greatest protection risks to women, girls, boys and men including the disabled and the elderly reflecting continued gender inequalities exacerbated by the conflict and the prolonged crisis in South Sudan. The GBViMS statistics (2023) reports that there has been an increase in GBV cases reported in the first half of 2023 compared to the same period in 2022. During the same period 97 per cent of reported cases in South Sudan affected women and girls, and 21 per cent of survivors were children, of which 79 per cent were adolescent girls. Physical violence, commonly by an intimate partner or someone known by the survivor, continues to be the most common form of GBV, accounting for 42 per cent of reported cases with cases now increasing to the current influx of returnees and Refugees from the ongoing Sudan Crisis. The increased presence of mixed populations such as returnees and IDPs has exerted pressure on the few services available in these locations thereby exacerbating protection risks and increasing the need for GBV prevention and response interventions according to Renk IRNA report September 2023. The GBViMS statistics (2023) further indicate that 97% of the reported GBV incidents survivors were females (women and girls) with intimate partners accounting for more than half of the alleged perpetrators. Access to GBV services for the targeted locations remain a major challenge owing to the limited availability of GBV prevention and response services. A multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted in August 2023 in Renk County indicated that GBV is more prevalent within the households and mostly perpetrated by intimate partners. In addition, majority of the community members who participated in the key informant interviews and FGD acknowledged that the prevalence of GBV was high in their communities as they mentioned to have witnessed GBV related incidents perpetrated almost on a daily basis. This is largely attributed to the deep-rooted cultural beliefs, attitudes and perception about women and girls that contribute significantly to unequal power dynamic among men and women, hence resulting to normalizing violence against women within the Agro-pastoral communities. In addition, fear of retaliation through revenge killings was mentioned to be a major barrier in accessing the available services by survivors. Across the four counties the GBV situation has further been exacerbated by the insecurity situation, flooding and COVID-19 pandemic.
