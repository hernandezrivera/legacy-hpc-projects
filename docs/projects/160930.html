Project Summary

This project seeks to improve the psychosocial well-being of women in Leer County. A network of mother-to-mother ‘Cascade Groups’ is supported by staff trained in appropriate modalities of mental health and psychosocial support. Child-friendly spaces complement Medair’s integrated programming in Leer

1 - Needs

The crisis in South Sudan is considered to be primarily a protection crisis. Protection concerns related to conflict and violence continue to be the primary drivers of displacement. As of October 2018, nearly 2 million people were internally displaced (HRP 2019), living in formal and informal settlements, host communities and in hard-to-reach areas. Prolonged conflict continues to impact the physical and mental wellbeing of vulnerable populations. 2300 cases of Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) were reported in the first half of 2018 HNO 2019). The recurring conflict and brutal violence in Leer County that began in 2014, with exacerbations continuing into 2018, has included killing, rape, abduction and loss of homes and livelihoods for thousands of people, as well as large-scale displacement.There is a growing body of reports regarding the extent of the trauma experienced and witnessed by the population within Leer. These reports describe an alarming picture of an almost uniformly traumatized population that has suffered individual, family and community losses. Up to 90% of people interviewed lost at least one relative in the violence and 75% of interviewees indicated that their family was experiencing at least one symptom of trauma (Protection Cluster report 2016). Focus group discussions conducted in 2016 showed that 66-100% had experienced/witnessed physical violence by a stranger and 25% had experienced/witnessed sexual violence by a stranger. Assessments by protection and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) actors in Leer in 2018 and 2019 paint a similar ongoing picture of SGBV and trauma, and also reflect the severity of the current MHPSS needs in this area.Given the high exposure to potentially traumatic experiences there is a need to support the mental health psychosocial well-being of this population, through the provision of MHPSS services. If appropriate services are not provided as a preventative, response, risk mitigation and resilience measure, the ability of the population to cope with further shocks will be severely compromised. Medair’s protection programming in Leer will be integrated alongside its ongoing nutrition, health, and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) programme.

2 - Activities

MHPSS • Regular supervision of the MHPSS team by the MHPSS Project Manager. • Equipand supervise two Psycho Social Support (PSS) Officers to support 13 PSS Assistants inprovision of psychoeducation and life and stress management skills to Cascade Groups, a community-based network of women engaging in peer-to peer psychosocial support and behaviour change communication messaging and activities within their communities (general protection).PSS staff trained and supervised in the provision of Psychological First Aid (PFA),torecognise mild mental health needs, and link beneficiaries with appropriate services aswell as trained in Community Based Psychosocial Support (CBPS) and Life Skills tostrengthen and build resilience. . • Train at least one PSS Assistant per nutrition site tolead integration of ECD activities into the cascade group network and facilitate nutritionsite activities including Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) and discussions with groups ofmothers on coping mechanisms. • Provide training to all PSS Assistants (Care GroupPromoters) and Lead Mothers (Cascade Group Volunteers) on leading peer-to-peersupport groups, SGBV awareness and referrals, Behaviour Change Communication (BCC)and provision of psychosocial support and referral. • Provide capacity building to existing WASH,health and nutrition staff on generalPSS basics such as PFA, supportive communication skills, self-care, stress management . • Continue to implement a peer-to-peer group module onPSS topics for use in cascade group networks, for example positive coping mechanisms,how to stay safe and to reduce stigma with regard to mental health and SGBV. • Increasecommunity awareness of available mental health and SGBV services through CascadeGroups. Consider use of focus group discussions (FGD) and/or barrier analysis toinvestigate the factors influencing uptake of such services. • Revise supervision systemwith quality checklists for supervision of PSS activities including CFS at nutrition sites,cascade group PSS sessions and Care Group Volunteer group PSS meetings withneighbour women, and PSS Assistant follow up home visits for women needing moresupport. • Conduct a pre and post qualitative cascade group participant’spsychosocial wellbeing survey on randomly selected peer group participants. • Link PSSfocal points with community nutrition workers for periodic home visits. • Recruit, train,equip and supervise up to two Health Managers and two Health Officers to detect,diagnose and manage selected mental health conditions as per mhGAP-HIG guideline. •Co-ordinate with protection, psychosocial and other GBV actors to establish confidential,safe mutual referral pathways as appropriate and feasible. • Actively participate in andcoordinate with other actors in the MHPSS working group at Juba level.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

8.4 Given the sensitive nature of SGBV within the South Sudanese context Medair continues to work with communities to build knowledge and acceptance of SGBV services. Medair partners with like-minded organisations through links at cluster and local level to continue to try and improve referral services and support to survivors of SGBV. Nutrition and health staff are trained in PFA and additionally health staff receive training in SGBV and CMR. 8.10 Medair uses a community based approach to programming in Leer. Peer-to-peer support groups are operational at community level as are Cascade Groups, providing support to local women in the community. Women are less likely to need to travel to access services. They are able to access Medair MHPSS services with a neighbour woman or a peer-to-peer support worker if they have security concerns

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Medair’s peer-support ‘Cascade Groups’ provide a natural route through which the views of thecommunity are continually engaged. In addition, Medair consults regularly with community leaders and other key stakeholders to elicit their opinions and advice regarding the initiation of or new developments within interventions. Medair recruits staff locally wherever possible, creating a further network of local contacts through which community feedback is sought. Additionally, Medair conducts regular ‘knowledge, practice and coverage’ evaluations, using local staff to conduct these exercises which elicit information from communities regarding health needs and behaviours. Barrier analysis is utilised to engage community participation in overcoming obstacles to accessing services and practicing healthy behaviours.
