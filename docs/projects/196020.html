Project Summary

In line with the South Sudan 2023 HRP and the FSL Cluster priorities and cluster objectives, in 2023, the Food Security and Livelihood Cluster aims (1) to provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations facing IPC phases 3, 4 and 5; (2) to enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing, and livestock support; and (3) to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability both to absorb shocks and adapt to a changing context, especially in relation to climatic, economic and conflict related risks. Agency for Child Relief Aid (ACRA’s) project is aimed at providing food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations in IPC 5, 4 and some in 3 target Counties of Fangak, Ayod and Uror of Jonglei State, Maiwut and Longechuk Counties of Upper Nile State and Budi County of Eastern Equatoria State. The project aims to improve the food security and livelihoods of IDPs, returnees and host communities through provision of timely and appropriate main season agricultural inputs/kits (crop and vegetable seeds, and fishing kits), (linked to cluster objective 2), capacity building & training, asset building and market support and training in improved agronomic and fishing practices, (linked to cluster objective 3). ACRA will also provide emergency assistance and livelihoods support to save lives and avert total collapse of livelihoods in the target Counties. The provision of agricultural and fishing inputs/kits, training in agricultural production and fishing is expected to improve the food production and in turn the food security of the target households (HHs) thus supporting them to recover from crisis and placing them on an accelerated path to early recovery and resilience to acute climate shocks.
ACRA seeks to support 8,725 HHs (52,350 individuals) in the targeted Counties with agricultural and livelihood kits. The kits will be procured and distributed directly by ACRA and some will be secured from FAO pipeline. Training on improved agronomic and fishing practices, marketing and group dynamics will be done through model farmers and fisher folk who will undergo a Training of Trainers (ToT) and subsequently train the farmers and fisher folk in their communities. The knowledge gained from these trainings will improve the agricultural and fishing practices of the target HHs thus improving food production, addressing their immediate food security needs and building their resilience to future shocks. The provision of agricultural inputs and fishing kits and subsequent enhancement of agricultural production and fishing is expected to contribute to improvement of the Household Diversity Score (HDDS) and Food Consumption Score (FCS) of the target HHs
ACRA will employ a community-based beneficiary selection approach which will entail establishing Project Management Committees (PMC’s) to lead the selection of eligible beneficiaries based on the beneficiary selection criteria. Vulnerable HHs with severe food insecurity including female, child, disabled/chronically ill headed HHs; persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS and survivors of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) will be targeted in this project.
ACRA will hold regular meetings with the PMC’s and community members and conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) to get their feedback on the project implementation. ACRA will ensure that their feedback is incorporated in the implementation of this project and design of future projects. Further, to promote accountability to the affected populations, ACRA will use the community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues or concerns. 1 - Needs

In 2023, the FSL Cluster will target 6.0 million people (excluding refugees), providing humanitarian life-saving assistance to 4.6 million and capacity building and training to 1.0 million South Sudanese people, in line with its objectives. According to HNO 2023, an estimated 8.2 million people, including refugees and Abyei, are likely to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2023 lean season (May – July). According to IPC analysis, it projected that 62.7% of the population in South Sudan who will be facing severe food insecurity. According to December 2022 to March 2023 IPC analysis, 703,000 people in Jongolei State will be in Crisis (IPC3) while another 559,00 people are classified as emergency (IPC4). In Eastern Equitoria during the same period, 354,000 people are classified as IPC3 while another 160,000 people are in emergency (IPC4) while in Upper Nile 622,000 people are in IPC3 while 239,000 are classified as IPC4 (emergency). According to the IPC analysis, it is expected that in April- July, 2023, Eastern Equitoria, 51.3% people will face Crisis, Emergency & Humanitarian Catastrophe while 74.2% and 69.6% will experience the same humanitarian crisis. Between July 2022 and June 2023, an estimated 1.4 million children under five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition based on analysis and estimations from the results of the SMART nutrition surveys, Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) and program admission trends. 33,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Akobo and Fangak of Jonglei State. Between July and October 2022, 57% of the Counties in crises (IPC AMN Phase 3) or worse) are from the Greater Upper Nile region (Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity states) – Jonglei State with nine Counties (Akobo, Ayod, Canal/Pigi, Fangak, Pibor, Nyirol, Uror, Twic East and Duk); and ten Counties (Melut, Baliet, Renk, Maban, Malakal, Manyo, Fashoda, Ulang, Nasir and Maiwut) classified as worst affected. Continued conflict and instability in the country combined with flooding and the impact of COVID 19 have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement. Low crop production caused by prolonged dry spells, floods, crop pests and diseases, and shortage of seeds and agricultural tools as well reduced access to livestock products as animals migrate in search of water and pasture, (HNO, 2022). This is compounded by already existing factors of low seed and tools access, monoculture, flooding, dry spells, low area under production, lack of land use planning, weak state institutions and poor governance. Deterrents associated with limited and lack of means for income generation and limited access to markets, have been worsened now due to insecurity and poor road conditions as a way to cope, households (mainly female-headed HHs) engage in negative strategies such as reducing number of meals, eating less preferred foods, sending children to beg and selling livelihood assets, (IRNA October 7th 2022). All the ACRA target Counties will likely experience a further deterioration in food insecurity during the April to July 2023 period. During the lean season of April to July 2023, the target Counties are expected to be (IPC Phase 4) emergency food insecurity. As per the IPC report, the key drivers of food insecurity include reduced food availability due to flooding, dry spells, crop pests and diseases, insecurity and displacement of farming households which affected most agricultural activities. The economic crisis and the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic continue to make it difficult for many households to access food from markets because of loss of sources of livelihoods, reduced income, high food prices coupled with high inflation rates in the country. Therefore, immediate scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to save lives and avert total collapse is required especially in the ACRA target Counties in IPC4 in Jongolei and Upper Nile States.

2 - Project activities

1.1. Community-Based Participation and Planning (CBPP): The CBPP exercise will focus on the full participation of selected community planning teams, who in participatory ways, will identify community needs, suggest some recommendations and draw the final community’s plan. A range of participatory methods will be used to guide the planning process. This exercise will be conducted at the Boma level. ACRA will then establish beneficiary’s selection committees (BSC) and project steering committees (PSC) 1.2. Formation and training of BSCs and PMCs/PSCs: ACRA will facilitate the formation of the Boma-level PMC’s. Each PMC will comprise of 10 members (5 women, 5 men) who will be trained in beneficiary selection criteria and monitoring of the project implementation. The PMCs will also be trained in protection and mainstreaming cross-cutting issues across all activities with a particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to the affected population. 1.3. Beneficiary selection: ACRA adopts participatory, vulnerability and rights-based approaches in identification, verification and registration of target beneficiaries. ACRA will furthermore strengthen community communication to ensure beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project, beneficiary criteria, and how to provide feedback or complains to ACRA. The PSCs with guidance form ACRA PMC’s will identify and register the beneficiaries according to ACRA’s vulnerability criteria for targeting food-insecure women/child/chronically ill-headed HHs, People with disabilities, widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned, Survivors of GBV. 1.4. Distribution of complementary cropping and livelihoods inputs: ACRA will Protect and stabilize livelihoods for 8,370 vulnerable HHs (female/male 51/49%) (46,800 individuals) through provision of Livelihoods inputs (seeds and tools plus fishing gears and distribute Crop kits (Maize, Sorghum and Cowpea), vegetables (Amaranth, Collard, Okra, Tomato, Onion, Watermelon, Pumpkin and Maloda) and Fishing kits (hooks, monofilament and spools of twine). This will also improve food availability and income from sale of surplus vegetables 1.5. Formation and training of farmers and fisher folk groups: ACRA will facilitate the formation of farmer and fisher folk groups. ToT training on improved agronomic practices, fish preservation, marketing and group dynamics will be provided. The trained farmers and fisher folk will then sensitize and build the capacity of the other farmers and fisher folk in their communities. Training on crop disease control including training of farmers and surveillance on Fall army worm (FAW) and Desert Locusts control through Community based surveillance and Early Warning. Training farmers on grain/seed harvesting, storage and support through traditional granaries and hermitic bags. Training on post-harvest management and market linkages. Fisher folk training in good fishing techniques, preservation, processing and marketing and on the use of fishing kits received. 1.6. Support provision of veterinary services through community based initiatives to 20,000 livestock. 1.7. Provision of livelihoods skills training: Female-headed HHs and fisher folk supported through training on IGAs. IGA’s will be linked to the other two activities of fishing and vegetable production on market linkages. Some markets in Jonglei, Upper Nile and EES are functional and ACRA will target this market-based activity in these areas 1.8. Facilitate formation of VSLA groups and supporting with start-up kits and supporting small scale farmers on group formation, value addition to their produce and linking them to markets and agro-input dealers and other value chain actors 1.9. Monitoring and reporting: ACRA will also conduct post distribution monitoring after every distribution to ascertain the appropriateness of the response.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

For the people with special needs, whose access to feedback and complaint mechanism is limited, ACRA will adjust the community-based complaints and feedback mechanism paths to provide a platform for beneficiaries with special needs to voice any issues of ACRAs and feedback on the project activities. ACRA will set up help desks and encourage vulnerable community members to share any feedback they have on the project through face to face meetings. The most vulnerable members will form part of the PDM sample to ensure that their voice is heard. Help desks will be erected in FSL and WASH sites to provide a good opportunity for two-way dialogue. In case of beneficiaries with disabilities with limited mobility, ACRA will visit households with the help of the hygiene promoters and food monitors/ enumerators to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate, community hygiene promoters, complaint committee and enumerators will be encouraged to document the complaints and feedbacks since they are selected from within the community and are familiar with the local language. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the complaints and feedback mechanism, as well having the opportunity to provide feedback directly to ACRA program staff or monitoring team. Feedback and complaints can also be shared through local chiefs, FGDs, hygiene promoters, food monitors/ enumerators, water user committee, FSL enumerators who are part of the community and are participating in all implementation activities. Depending on what people feel comfortable with, they can share the feedback personally or via trusted persons.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Prior to initiating project activities and delivery of assistance, ACRA will conduct an inception meeting with the target beneficiaries and stakeholders to inform them about the project scope, target criteria and their entitlements. Similarly, during the inception meetings and during regular consultations with the community, ACRA will inform the beneficiaries about the feedback mechanism, their right to assistance and complaints. Depending on type of assistance, context and target population profile, ACRA ensure that the banners with information on complaint channel and feedback mechanism are displayed broadly in the target areas and at the distribution points. ACRA will endeavor to provide accurate, timely and accessible information to beneficiaries about every aspect of the project including the planned activities, beneficiary entitlements, timelines and how to share feedback or report complains to ACRA. Community mobilization will take the form of regular community and bilateral meetings. ACRA will also place banners and signposts with project details at strategic points in the community. ACRA will actively involve beneficiaries in identifying their priorities and solutions to their challenges to ensure that the voices and ACRAs of all groups are considered during the project’s design, planning and implementation

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

ACRA staff will be responsible for championing good practices and maintaining an organizational culture that prioritizes and safeguards all against SEA. Managers at all levels (Both Juba and Field level) have responsibilities to support and develop systems that maintain an environment that facilitates implementation of this Policy and which prevents SEA. During the community mobilization and sensitization meetings, ACRA will provide beneficiaries with information on how to safely and confidentially report SGBV/SEA if and when it occurs. ACRA will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues of ACRAs and feedback on the project activities. In line with ACRA's Safeguarding Policy on PSEA, ACRA will inform the target communities about ACRA's staff responsibilities and the expected code of conduct. At the start of the project, ACRA will develop a plan on how information about PSEA will be disseminated. In all its communication activities, ACRA will re-emphasize that all the beneficiary entitlements are provided freely and that they should immediately report if and when they’re asked to do or provide anything in exchange of the project’s entitlements. ACRA will work closely with community leaders and stakeholders and will sensitize them on how to report any SEA cases that they come across. The PSEA information will be part of information campaign conducted at the beginning of the project implementation. The messages will be given by ACRA officers (including women) to groups of beneficiaries during FGD baseline studies, KII, education campaigns. PSEA will be important part of training of community members engaged in the project and volunteers.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

To ensure effective records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, ACRA team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as the use of complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. ACRA will also organize project performance and quality review meetings with staff, beneficiaries, and partners to assess progress towards the set objectives and work plans, to monitor project performance and quality of activities based on monitoring and beneficiary feedback data, to identify risks and set risk management strategies during implementation. A project evaluation will be carried out at the end of the project and the process used to measure the achievement of project outputs and outcomes then documented for the record. ACRA will take immediate and appropriate action upon receipt of any report or complaint about SEA allegations, ACRAs or suspicions, or retaliation related to SEA. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and will be used to adjust project activities. The records of complaints and feedback is kept and regularly analysed by MEAL team in Juba, whose officers track each case and advise/support the programme team. The implementing programme team, under supervision of HoP will conduct regular review meetings and adjust the project implementation to according to constructive feedbacks received.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Mitigation Measures While ACRA has recently conducted a country-wide operational, security and programmatic analysis, ACRA will conduct a further in-depth risk analysis to ascertain the operational, programmatic and security related risks alongside the corresponding mitigation measures before commencement of the project activities. ACRA has identified the following protection related risks and analysis 8.1: Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services (Likelihood is low and impact is high). To mitigate this risk, ACRA will ensure that registration is conducted and assistance provided as close to the beneficiaries as possible. ACRA will work with existing community structures and local authorities to ensure that appropriate and locations are identified for project activities so that beneficiaries do not walk for long distances for registration or to receive assistance. PMC's will assist in crowd control during project activities. Activities will be implemented in line with the seasonal calendar to ensure accessibility to project locations. Inputs will be distributed and trainings conducted prior to the rainy season during which some locations are either cut off or inaccessible. 8.5: Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities (likelihood is low, impact is high). As a mitigation measure, ACRA will adopt participatory, vulnerability and rights-based approaches throughout the project life-cycle from the onset of the project, consultations, identification, verification and registration of target beneficiaries. Under the guidance of ACRA, the PMC’s will identify and register the beneficiaries according to ACRA’s vulnerability criteria for targeting food-insecure women/child/chronically ill-headed HHs, People with disabilities, widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned, Persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS, Survivors of GBV etc. This will ensure the prioritization of assistance towards those who need it most. 8.6: Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms (likelihood is low, impact is medium). To mitigate this risk, ACRA will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues or concerns. ACRA will strengthen community communication to ensure beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project, beneficiary criteria and how to provide feedback or complains to ACRA. This will be done through regular meetings with the target communities and where possible use of IEC materials to disseminate information in the target communities about the CRM. 8.8: Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources (likelihood is low, impact is high). ACRA's mitigation measure for this risk entails developing and sensitizing the local authorities, PMC's on the beneficiary selection criteria. The beneficiary selection process will be monitored by ACRA staff to ensure objectivity and impartiality. PDM's will be conducted to ascertain the objectivity of the beneficiary selection process and appropriate corrective measures will be implemented in case of any issues identified.
