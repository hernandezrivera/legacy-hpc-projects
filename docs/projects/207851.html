Project Summary

This project aims to deliver early childhood care and development with basic education for 4- to 18-year-old children through diverse interventions. 3960 children have been targeted from the host community, and 480 children have been targeted from the Rohingya community. To reach the targeted learners, 11 locations in Teknaf and Ukhiya have been selected from the host community. Total learners of 4 years old are targeted at 1320 (female: 660 & male: 660) and 5–14 year old learners are targeted at 2640 (female: 1320 & male: 1320). Whereas in the Rohingya community, total learners aged 4 targeted 240 (female: 120 and male: 120) and 15–18-year-old learners targeted 240 (female: 240) at camp 13. Total nos of teachers is set at 91 (71 for Host and 20 for Rohingya).
As a result, the following actions will be taken:
Access: To guarantee play-based preparation support for the establishment of a play-based integrated ECCD center for 4-year-old children. Here, there will be integration between the sectors of nutrition, WASH, child protection, and health. 1320 children in total will fall into the 4-year-old age range. There will be 11 community-based learning facilities (CBLF) with 22 classrooms where 22 host teachers will facilitate them.
To give our children, ages 5 to 18, access to a facility for informal education. The B2S (Back to School) program will be introduced to out-of-school children, and the curriculum and teaching strategy will be developed in accordance with their needs. There will be 2640 students total at Teknaf and Ukhiya who are aimed at providing quality education. To facilitate the students, 22 centers will be required, with 44 classrooms with 44 host teachers. For the Rohingya camps at Camp 13, total numbers of children targeted are 480 age level 4-18 years. Therefore, 20 CBLF needs to be established. 10 CBLF will be dedicated only for girls aged 15-18 years (total Rohingya teachers targeted 10 and host teachers targeted 5), and separate 10 CBLF will be established for children aged 4 years and 10 Rohingya teachers will be facilitated.
Equity and Inclusivity: Teachers, staff capacity building on equity and inclusion, addressing CwD needs in the classroom using IEP, gender and inclusion-responsive training for teachers and SMCs, inclusive sports and recreation, rehabilitation support through referral to cater to the needs of children, engagement of OPDs and self-help groups, and accessibility actions at school and community. Increasing the enrollment of adolescents in schools and providing accessible facilities for adolescent learners.
Quality Learning: Initiatives with teachers will be taken to build capacity for quality teaching and learning. Where necessary, logistical support is offered. Teachers, staff capacity building on equity and inclusion, addressing CwD needs with IEPs, gender and inclusion responsive training, inclusive sports and recreation, community engagement support Separation incentives will be supported on a contextual basis for the adolescent girl's continued behavior.
Community Engagement: Participation in the community will compel the children to stay in school. Parents' conscious effort will be necessary for children under the age of 4 to grow and develop. The key task in this regard will be community engagement activities like parent meetings, SMC meetings, PTA, mother awareness sessions, SRHR awareness sessions, etc. Total nos of community members target 5830.
System Strengthening: A community feedback mechanism will be developed. Meetings with the SMC and PTA can be strengthened by active participation. Workshops with a variety of stakeholders, including the Upazila Education Office Sectoral cooperation with numerous stakeholders will help this program continue over time. Along with strengthening the educational system at the community level, this project will also establish and implement an inclusive school improvement plan and enhance the school's monitoring and tracking system.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

According to the information provided by the education sector, there are clear signs that the children in Ukhiya and Teknaf who are in early childhood, primary, and secondary education are out of school to some extent, and there is a gap in providing quality education. To emphasize early childhood education, 5361 children aged 4 years old have been targeted out of total 17868 children from the host community. More assistance for early childhood has to step up with high-quality activities and interventions in order to boost implementation. Establishing an integrated ECCD program can therefore be a solution for offering services in education integrating health, hygiene, protection, and nutrition. Similar to this, a sizable number of school-aged adolescents were discovered not attending school in the Ukhiya and Teknaf areas. There are 1,66,382 populations in total for the age group 5 to 14 years, of which 25,424 have been targeted as 15% of the total. There is a clear support gap that needs to be filled in order to give these children high-quality assistance. With B2S (back-to-school curriculum), the informal education system will be the answer for keeping children in school and ensuring their access. Some needs that are still present in Ukhiya and Teknaf are recognized from the J-MSNA (https://reliefweb.int/report/bangladesh/joint-multi-sector-needs-assessment-j-msna-bangladesh-rohingya-refugees-may-2021) that was held most recently. There is a lack of funds to cover tuition and other related costs, even after accounting for inflation. Second, there is a COVID-19 infection risk while traveling or at school, and third, the children have fallen behind too much.For children to return to school, to decrease dropout rates, to limit the risk of child labor, and to enroll adolescent children, there is a need for awareness-raising and community involvement. The biggest obstacle to children acquiring an education may be a lack of money or availability of both in-person and remote instruction. Project activities must take physical education and distance learning initiatives into consideration.For children between the ages of 4 and 5, attention must be paid to nutrition education, hygienic upkeep, and neat and tidy activities, as well as the development of the parents' skills and capacity for childcare at home. Children under the age of four are particularly excluded from educational opportunities because enrollment in the host community begins at age 5.The education of adolescent children, especially adolescent girls, should also be taken into consideration. Contextual incentives must be identified, parents and community leaders must be in constant communication, and support for menstrual health services must be offered. Inclusion is also another crucial component. All children must have access to school. Children with disabilities (CwD) must find and adapt disability-inclusive TLM (Teaching Learning Materials), pilot a tool to assess the learning outcomes of children with disabilities, and create an action plan for all institutions that addresses the intersection of gender and disability. There is no alternative to arranging a series of dialogues and conversations between the host community and Rohingya people to assure societal harmony. The educational systems in Rohingya-host communities require a sustained and comprehensive approach. In order to enhance service delivery for both the host and Rohingya communities, there is also a need to improve collaboration between government and non-governmental groups. Under the direction of the education sector, resolving issues such as learner duplication, integrated services, and inter-agency or organization cross-learning sharing for quality implementation is equally vital.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This project emphasizes rationalization principles when choosing the geographic regions and takes into consideration zero duplication while carrying out the interventions and providing assistance for the children in order to ensure sincerity and accountability. Addressing the Education Gap among ECD Learners: The inclusion of 1320 ECD learners in the project demonstrates a crucial understanding of the importance of early childhood education. ECD lays the foundation for future learning, and providing access to quality ECD programs is essential for the overall cognitive, social, and emotional development of children. The adoption of the integrated approach has been prioritized. In order to meet our goal, we have focused on the ECD sector and just the host communities (Ukhiya and Teknaf), where there is a great demand and learning deficit in ECD, and bridging the ECD learners with primary grade learners. Catering to the Educational Needs of Primary and Secondary Students: The project also addresses the educational needs of a significant population of primary and secondary students from the host community. By enrolling 2640 students in these levels, the project ensures that children continue to receive education beyond the early years and have the opportunity to complete their primary and secondary schooling.Supporting the Host Community: The focus on the host community is particularly noteworthy, as it highlights the project's commitment to not only addressing the educational needs of the Rohingya refugee population but also supporting the local Bangladeshi community. This inclusive approach promotes social cohesion and fosters a sense of shared responsibility for education.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project is intended to guarantee participation by including CwD inclusion, community involvement to reduce dropout rates, activities to minimize child labor, and ensuring enrollment of teenage children, particularly female children. A child protection risk assessment is also planned to close the gap, and appropriate action will be taken based on the situation. This project intends to spread information on child protection as part of an educational program, such as separation and trafficking prevention or mine-risk education. The project will create shared referral systems with child protection professionals. This project will make sure that the learning environment is physically secure, including having first aid supplies on hand and teachers who are trained in first aid, well-known emergency evacuation procedures, equipment and facilities in good condition, and staff restrooms that are separate for boys and girls. Positive discipline and non-violent classroom management techniques will be promoted through project activities.

3.3 - Age & Disability

By putting the following tactics into practice, this education initiative in Cox's Bazar that serves ECD, primary, and secondary students from the host community ensures inclusiveness for all ages and disabilities: Allow children to enter and exit the education system at different points in time to accommodate their individual needs and circumstances. Provide accelerated learning opportunities for older children who are out of school or have fallen behind due to disruptions in their education. Implement UDL principles to create a learning environment that is accessible to all learners, regardless of their abilities. Assistive technology: Provide assistive technology, such as hearing aids, wheelchairs, and adaptive learning software, to support learners with disabilities. Train teachers to effectively teach students with disabilities, including strategies for accommodating different learning styles and needs. Provide early identification and intervention services for children with disabilities to help them reach their full potential. Additionally, creating a safe and supportive learning environment where all children feel welcome and valued, initiating culturally sensitive and respectful of the diverse backgrounds of the learners, and engaging with the community will ensure that the education project is responsive to the needs of all children, including those with disabilities and those from different age groups. By implementing these strategies, the education project in Cox's Bazar can ensure that it is inclusive of all children, regardless of their age or disability. This will help to create a more equitable and just education system that benefits all members of the community.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Stakeholder' engagement includes SMC bodies of the school with PTA (parents teachers association), engagement of OPDs/Self-Help groups for community sensitization and accessibility actions in decision-making, and feedback mechanisms to be practiced more, which allow them to understand feedback channels and give feedback without facing difficulties. KII, FGD, etc. would be held with the community to accelerate the process and get their issues resolved. At each school, a feedback mechanism box will be established. A common feedback platform will be utilized for any inter-sectoral issues that come from community feedback. For program implementation, intra-sectoral coordination will be followed so that no duplication of service delivery will be confirmed, information sharing will be unique, and community engagement through diverse activities can be effective.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Mainstreaming PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) in an education project in Cox's Bazar involves embedding PSEA principles and practices into the project's design, implementation, and monitoring. This ensures that PSEA is not just an add-on but an integral part of the project's overall approach to safeguarding children. The organization will develop PSEA guidelines that clearly define PSEA, outline the project's commitment to preventing SEA, and establish procedures for reporting, investigating, and responding to SEA incidents. The project will conduct a risk assessment, from which potential risks of SEA will be identified, and develop mitigation strategies to address these risks. This project will ensure that all staff members, including teachers, administrators, and non-teaching staff, are trained on PSEA principles, prevention measures, reporting procedures, and their roles and responsibilities in protecting children. Implementing measures to create a safe and supportive learning environment where children feel comfortable and empowered to report SEA incidents This includes establishing clear communication channels, promoting open dialogue, and fostering a culture of respect and inclusion. This project will designate a specific person or team to be responsible for overseeing PSEA implementation within the project. This focal point will have the authority and resources to effectively implement PSEA measures. Incorporate PSEA messaging and activities into the project's curriculum, teacher training, and community engagement initiatives. This ensures that PSEA awareness and prevention strategies are embedded throughout the project. Develop a clear and accessible reporting mechanism for children and adults to report SEA incidents without fear of reprisal. This mechanism should ensure that reports are handled confidentially and promptly. Regularly monitor the implementation of PSEA measures and evaluate their effectiveness in preventing SEA incidents.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Given that the area is vulnerable to disasters, it intends to provide an opportunity for the organization of capacity-building training, staff orientation, and community mobilization in accordance with governmental authorities. In order to mitigate climate change, the project will start planting trees across the neighborhood and closely collaborate with the DRR sector to adopt and sensitize the neighborhood with effective communication trees and functioning DRR plans. With community stakeholders, this initiative seeks to provide DRR training in 13 batches to every union council in Ukhiya and Teknaf. Additionally, teachers will receive DRR training so that pupils can understand it and follow the functioning DRR plan. With the creation of a communication tree and a DRR contingency plan, 53 schools will be covered. Climate and environmental issues will be discussed and spread through a variety of community sessions, such as parents’ meetings, PTA meetings, SMC meetings, etc.
