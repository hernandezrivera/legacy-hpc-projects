Project Summary

This projects aims to reach 79,028 beneficiaries, (63,933 children below 5years and 15,095 above 5years) in six Counties of Juba, Lainya, Ikwoto, Budi, Baliet and Panyikang. Beneficiaries comprise of children and adults, both male and female inclusive of persons with disability. The proposed target accounts for 33% of nutrition cluster targets for these locations.
Health Link intends to implement varies activities to achieve reduction in malnutrition related morbidity and mortality in south Sudan through achievement of nutrition cluster objectives as below:
Sectoral objective 1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2021.
To achieve the above objective, HLSS intends to:
• Provision of MIYCN/ IYCF counselling services both at facility and community level.
• Community Awareness and Sensitization on malnutrition causes and prevention.
• Mass nutritional screening for all children under 5 in the community
• Strengthening family MUAC Screening.
• Awareness and Sensitization on COVID -19 prevention and reporting.
Sectoral objective 2: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2021.
• Establishment of 7 mobile teams, one in each county except Ikwoto that will have two teams; one in Katire and the other in Mohina.
• Treatment of severe acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months.
• Identification and referral of children with severe acute malnutrition with complications.
• Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59m months and PLWs.
• Training of health and nutrition and nutrition workers on CMAM and MIYCN with major focusing on case detection and referral for community health workers.
• Training of nutrition worker on COVID 19- prevention during service provision.
Sectoral objective 3: To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2021.
Health link south Sudan with support the attainment of the above objective through:
• Participation in national nutrition surveys.
• Routine submission of NIS reports on to the national database.
• Routine surveillance and reporting of diseases of epidemiological importance.
• Training of Health and Nutrition workers on Emergency response.
Sectoral objective 4: To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2021.
HLSS intends to implement the following to achieve the above objective:
• Screening and testing for malaria for all children with SAM.
• Training of Health and Nutrition workers on Malaria testing and treatment.
• Support existing facilities to establish kitchen gardens at nutrition sites for demonstration.
• Support national nutrition surveys in locations of implementation.
• Establish a community complain and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities by placing a suggestion boxes in the OTP/TSFP sites, initiating community focus group discussions with members of the community etc.
• Training of Health and nutrition workers on GBV, case identification and referral
Provision of WASH kits to children admitted diagnosed with SAM
• Provision of LLITN for all children admitted with SAM for malaria prevention.
• Training of CNV and Nutrition workers on provision of ECD
• Establishment of ECD corners at static nutrition sites to support child stimulation.
Theses activities will be implemented in collaboration with CHD, SMOH, National MOH and implementing partners of Health, WASH, Protection and FSL among others in accordance to MOH-SS guidelines.

1 - Needs

The Malnutrition situation in South Sudan remains Serious with Overall Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels at 12.6%, a 2.4% points short of the 15% emergency threshold. In Central equatorial, GAM levels have consistently on the rise from 8.2% in 2018, to 9.6% in 2019 and then to 15.3% 2020 (FSNMS R24 Dec 2019 and FSNMS R25 Feb 2020). Proxy GAM rates are estimated at 16.04% and 5.88% in Juba and lianya counties respectively(Nutrition Cluster Feb 2021). Juba has remained fairly security wise except mangalla Payam facing a complex emergency as vulnerable populations are forced to seek refuge following persistent flooding in Jongole state and later conflict rose between their supposed host communities of Mundari and Bari where the population has thought refuge. Although implementation of the revitalized peace agreement is in full swing, the multidimensional conflicts continue in some counties e.g. Baliet, Panyikang, Lianya among others. Lainya currently hosts IDPs from neighbouring kenyi, kupera &Wuji payams that are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection due to ongoing political/intercultural conflicts. (21Jan2021 Assessment Report). In EES, Proxy GAM rates are estimated at 16.77% and 19.55% in Ikwoto and Lafon respectively (Nutrition Cluster Feb 2021) classified as serious malnutrition levels. This area anciently known as a food basket for the country changed following 2013 insecurity as communities fled to neighboring countries for refuge, this led to reduction of food supplies. Likewise in UNS, the recent December 2019 FSNMSr25 revealed extremely critical GAM levels (16.4%) in Baliet County.Based on the Consolidated Approach to Reporting Indicators of Food Security, 69.2% of households across South Sudan were food insecure in December 2019, high levels of food insecurity are driven by continued insecurity which caused population displacements and livelihood disruptions. Floods have greatly affected the locations of Lafon and baliet county throughout 2020, submerged thousands of homes and devastated livelihoods leading to crop destruction, reduced planting impeding households access to various food sources. Furthermore, high prices of food commodities and sustained depreciation of the South Sudanese pound against the US dollar has reduced the purchasing power of vulnerable households. This has suffocated livelihoods in families and children under5 are hit hardest by this catastrophic situation. Displaced populations suffer more constraints in accessing lifesaving nutrition services, with under 5 most vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases as Immunization coverage for vaccine preventable diseases have persistently remained low and increased measles risk for children under1 (WHO Report Sept 2018). Recurrent measles outbreaks and/or upsurge of infectious diseases i.e. Acute Watery Diarrhoea/cholera, malaria, meningitis and hepatitis E compounded by flooding and food insecurity presents increased susceptibility to Malnutrition related morbidity and mortality. The health care system is over stretched due to current Covid-19 pandemic with observed second wave far above 100 cases per day (MoH, Daily Updates, February 2021). To bridge the gap, HLSS intends to roll out a strategic nutrition response by establishing 7 mobile teams to deliver cost effective lifesaving nutrition CMAM packages in 6 counties, this will target locations of: -Mangalla in Juba County, -Ibaahure, lodomiok and lohirang in Lopa county, -Romula, Mohina, Madwany and Ifere in ikwoto county, -Ryangnum, Benythiang, Baychan in Baliet County, -Pakwa and nyibol in Panyikang County and Lainya town in Lanya County. These are locations not reached by nutrition services and beneficiaries walk over 2 hours to the nearest OTP/TSFP site. This proposed nutrition response project is meant to be integrate into ongoing Health, nutrition, Protection and ICCM projects run by health link in these locations.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HLSS has PSEA Policy and reporting mechanism in place and implemented at all her project locations. which include hot line for reporting, email address for ethics and integrity. Core protection activities are described clearly in the logical framework. Additionally, in cross-sectoral activities and implementation, protection mainstreaming will be applied as follows: - During distributions OTP/TSFP days, the nutrition personnel with protection team will routinely conduct the safety audit survey which will advise the programme team on necessary adjustments. - HLSS staff members and temporary workers in the field (such as nutrition promoters and volunteers) understand and sign the Code of Conduct, PSEA policy and Child Protection. These policies are made available for the beneficiaries in a widely understood form such as images and posters. - Entitlements to beneficiaries are clearly visible at the distribution points and these are communicated to the community. The static and outreach sites will be selected closer to beneficiaries to minimize the risk of exposure of violence because of a distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services. - HLSS will include women, men, boys and girls for community engagement activities to minimize intercommunity conflict over access to limited livelihood activities. - The protection officer will be responsible for the verification/establishment of the referral pathway and to refer cases to the nearest active service providers. - The beneficiaries will be informed and trained on the case reporting mechanism, provided information on the access to or awareness of referral services for survivors of GVBS, Child protection incidents. Advocacy on HLP and supporting the facilitation of discussions with key stakeholders and community. - Well-established, understandable and widely known complaint & feedback mechanism will be part of all activities. HLSS will further ensure that the poster containing the hotline and email address for reporting will be put in all the service points and educate the beneficiaries on the use. - The M&E team will measure whether beneficiaries feel safe accessing the services before, during, and after implementation. Throughout the intervention, HLSS commits to the collection of age, gender and diversity disaggregated data. - HLSS will further ensure that the poster containing the hotline and email address for reporting will be put in all the service points and educate the beneficiaries on the use. - HLSS further conducts PSEA training to all staffs and this is conducted bi-yearly every year and every staff is to adhere to the polices and there is zero tolerance of PSEA for all staffs at work place and as well during service provision for the beneficiaries. - HLSS further has embedded PSEA in all staff contracts and code of contacts which all staffs sign from the time of joining the organization and as well whenever staff’s contract is being renewed. Therefore during service delivery HLSS will ensure to conduct awareness to the beneficiaries making sure that all the services are provided at no cost, and they are never attached in return of any goods from them in order to receive the said services, secondly HLSS will ensure to aware the beneficiaries on the importance of reporting any staffs that has asked them in return of the services they are receiving or attached conditions of sexual in nature for services to be received and letting them know what ever is reported will be treated with confidentiality and managed in safe manner by investigation team.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HLSS will ensure that PWDs are always considered with 5% of all services to be provided and thus separate feedback meetings will be conducted with them and responses acted accordingly throughout the implementation period. Further HLSS will ensure service delivery sites support the vulnerable women and girls and their feedback captured and incorporated in all the activities. HLSS will ensure that the project design will factor all categories of beneficiaries even those that are not literate and they will be engaged in other hand activities. To ease communication, groupings will be made based on gender and disability and special translators will be used to pass information/feed back to such vulnerable groups. More than one language will be used in meeting to ensure inclusion of all beneficiaries. Posters and images will be printed and displayed on the walls communicating different messages for those who are not literate to get information. Reports will be easy to read tailored and presented in form of picture, graphs and charts among others. Existing community structures such as Boma Health Initiative, mother support groups, Community nutrition volunteers and community mobilizers will be used to share feedback as they already have constant communication mechanism with the beneficiaries. Awareness messaging will be ongoing through out the project period through various media such as radio talk shows and/or jingles , public address and health education talks

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

HLSS with its current projects have a strong AAP mechanisms established in all the field locations and this has proved effective with the implementations in line of engaging the communities and their leaders in the project activities right from the inception of the project. For every project that is approved for implementation, HLSS conducts an orientation meeting with the stalk holders at different levels informing them of the new project in place, duration, the key deliverables, and the role of the community in relation to the project. Posters are pinned on the wall at the service delivery points with images relating to the project so as to cater for those unable to read. And at all service delivery points, HLSS has established a help desk and suggestion boxes for beneficiaries to raise any concerns /complaints and to be able to seek clarity and guidance in regards to the project. Feedback meetings conducted with beneficiaries (women, girls and boys including men) at service points, client satisfactory survey as well conducted with beneficiaries to ensure they are engaged on the various services they receive and well HLSS factors their feedback during this sessions. Exit feedbacks are always been conducted with the beneficiaries when they report to receive supplies. These post distribution feedback meetings with beneficiaries help in keeping the community engaged and feedbacks collected to improve on the service delivery. Monthly AAP feedback meetings conducted by HLSS with the community which basically looks in to the concerns and responses of the feedback received from the community in the course of the month. For any concerns that are urgent, the organisation makes gets in touch with the affected beneficiaries immediately. HLSS will ensure to have all these rolled out in the intended proposed locations for this project to keep the community engaged throughout the project implementation period.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback that HLSS will receive from beneficiaries during the project implementation shall be adapted during indicator reviews and as well factored during implementation at all service delivery points. Feed backs are from beneficiaries are gathered through engagement meetings, help desks, suggestions boxes and review meetings. HLSS analyses the feedbacks shares their responses with the beneficiaries, and adaptations for the project are made as follows.- For whatever has worked well and is appreciated by the beneficiaries, HLSs, will strengthen the strategy/activity, and keep track for future planning for proposals and replication to other locations.- For any strategy that does not go well, discussions are made during the meetings and consultations are made to identify what is workable within for the community without compromising on the quality of services. Some strategies or activities may not be applicable in some locations due to context and cultural difference which will need consistence sensitization and reinforcement.- Best practices shared by beneficiaries are also replicated and shared in cluster meetings.- Awareness and sensitizations and routinely conducted and incorporated in to the health education packages to clear misinformation and miscommunications.- For any feedback that are not within the mandate of HLSS to address or adopt in to the project implementation, this will be share with the necessary departments or stakeholders for their response will be given to the beneficiaries as soon as available.Such adaptations based on beneficiary feedbacks have helped HLSS in meeting the needsand right of the people, improved relationships with the community and instils the spirit of project ownership.
