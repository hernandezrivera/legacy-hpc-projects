Project Summary

The project aims to distribute 100 Food Baskets for "food insecure people" displaced to Al-Bab Sub-district (monthly distribution for 12 months), Priority will be given for households with specific vulnerability criteria (e.g. HH headed by women, People with special needs)
The Selection process will be coordinated with FSL Cluster and Al-Bab Local Council and will start in January 2022, Food baskets will be standardized according to FSL Cluster technical recommendations.
Needs Assessment was carried out for 100 Households in December 2021, which reached many neighborhoods inside Al-Bab city.
The targeted beneficiaries according to the needs assessment are:
- 94 IDPs / 6 HC (Total 100 families)
- 25 HHs headed by female / 58 HHs with PSN / 17 HHs under poverty line
- 98 Men / 106 Women / 146 Boys / 141 Girls (Total 513)
25% of the project fund is secured so far, 75% of the fund is planned (expected) to be secured in April 2022 (During Ramadan 1443H)

5 - Implementation of activities

SO 1.2 Provide the assessed food-insecure people with monthly food assistance through appropriate modalities Seeds of Hope will utilize its logistics capabilities in Al-Bab city (warehouse, transportation, work permit ...etc.), including a group of volunteers who will contribute to project implementation. Quality Control for Food Items Seeds of Hope will buy the Food Items from trusted suppliers (who are capable of providing attested health certificates), in addition, samples of the Food items purchased will be taken to a third party for analysis, to ensure no toxic material or any other harmful substances do exist in the shipment delivered to support food security of beneficiaries. Key Activities: 1.a.1 Needs Assessment/Analysis 1.a.2 Intervention logic Definition 2.a.1 Check availability/quality of items in local markets 2.a.2 Evaluate differences between alternatives / Select Item Category 2.a.3 Negotiate with Suppliers about quantities and delivery / Select Suppliers 2.a.4 Tender or solicit 3 quotations from qualified suppliers 2.a.5 Evaluate offers according to procurement policy / Select best bid 2.a.6 Sign procurement agreement with the supplier 2.a.7 Supply/Delivery process 2.b.1 Procurement committee inspect the conformity of the items (specs and quantities) 2.b.2 Official receipts from supplier according to financial and administrative policy 2.c.1 loading packaged Food Items 2.c.2 Transporting Food Baskets to the destination (camps/neighborhoods) 2.c.3 Distribution

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat 1: Risk of theft of Food baskets or inputs given, Likelihood: medium, Impact: high, Mitigating Measure: Agencies provide feedback and accountability mechanisms to different groups of users; work closely with all partners on the ground, Monitoring: Agency ﬁeld monitoring and consultation with communities; Proper stock management system in place. Resources: Dedicated staff member and good storage management. PSEA Risk/Threat 2: beneficiaries sexually harassed or abused in exchange for Food baskets, Likelihood: low, Impact: high, Mitigating Measure: Consult community throughout program cycle to determine the safest mechanism for handing over the food baskets, Monitoring: Enhance communication: (i) distribute brochures with Food baskets including hot-line numbers for reporting PSEA incidents (ii) avoid 1 to 1 connection with beneficiaries (SOH front-end team will consist of 1 male and 1 female at least), and (iii) provide training for SOH field staff, and ensure they commit to humanitarian principles and code of conduct signed after recruitment. Protection Risk/Threat 3: Exchange process for Food baskets at a lesser value may expose females to harassment by buyers, Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium, Mitigating Measure: Consult community throughout program cycle to determine the reasoning behind the sale/exchange of assistance and solicit their feedback with regards to solutions, Monitoring: Enhance data collection: (i) align PDM tool with global standards;

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The project took into consideration the following risks (beyond the project's control): 1- Mass displacement from Idlib to Aleppo based on conflict escalation (SOH contributes actively with local stakeholders during crises and has plan B to shift operation to Turkish/Syrian borderline). 2- Markets may be un-accessible because of insecurity (modalities will be changed if necessary according to local security conditions/challenges). 3- Some humanitarian actors may conduct uncoordinated distribution for households targeted by our project (coordination with the local council and FSL cluster will take place before implementation).
