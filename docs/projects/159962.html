Project Summary

The dynamic and multi-faceted nature of the South Sudanese displacement crisis has created significant challenges for the delivery of humanitarian aid. Accessibility and security issues within South Sudan have impeded a systematic understanding of Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) and nutrition needs in many areas of the country, and have created difficulties in establishing a clear and unambiguous system for prioritizing the delivery of aid, thereby limiting the effectiveness of humanitarian planning and limiting the potential impact of donor funding. As this crisis continues to expand, evolve and spill into all the states, it has become increasingly important to fill information gaps to inform a more effective humanitarian response and plan for immediate life-saving WASH and nutrition activities and contingency planning for durable solutions.WASH and nutrition situation in the country remains critical , (IPC) analysis for Aug 2019 show that 48 counties are in IPC 4 and 12 counties in IPC 5 out of the 78 counties of South Sudan. Aweil east leads with 117,000 people in crisis and 6,000 in an emergency thresh hold, Pibor has 102,000 population in crisis and 31,000 in emergency thresh hold, and Pariang has 64,000 in crisis and 6,000 in an emergency situation CRADA will conduct life saving WASH/Nut activities aimed at meeting the emergency critical needs of IDPs,Host communities and returnees in Aweil east,Pibor and Pariang Counties to mitigate the dire need.Most of the WASH facilities are in deplorable conditions and are broken down ,further increasing the burden of the already vulnerable population.About 45 per cent of the 51 SMART surveys conducted in 2018 reported critical nutrition situations with global acute malnutrition (GAM) prevalence of 15% and above WHO emergency thresh hold.Critical levels of Global Acute Malnutrition(15.0%-29.9%) in Aweil east, Pariang and Pibor ,Considering this, it calls for an intermediate intervention with an integrated approach to address these needs.This project will ensure provision of clean and safe drinking water for children admitted with SAM and MAM in OTPs and stabilization centers,WASH integrated interventions starting from the OTP/SC will target the couple'mother/care taker -malnourished children' until the house hold level aiming to break the vicious circle of diarrheal diseases/nematods, with malnutrition addressing also small stock open defecation affecting the young children,comprehensive water point management for cattle and small stock requirement during the dry season, with promotion of appropriate hygiene practices to prevent animal open defecation down to the house hold level.The project will ensure that the mothers of the children admitted are supplied with MHM kits and trained on hygiene management.There will be sensitization on the minimum WASH package activities to the health/Nutrition workers in the counties to ensure that communities receive accurate information.WASH facilities in the nutrition centers and stabilization centers will be rehabilitated to ensure that the objectives of this project are well met.There will be rehabilitation of latrines in OTP centers and health facilities to ensure that the already malnourished children do not contact other WASH related diseases which may further deteriorate their health.This project will distribute house hold package for famine prevention for children admitted for SAM treatment and discharged from functional OTP, This project aims at providing timely,equitable access to safe and sufficient water to the vulnerable and affected population in these counties. CRADA will work hand in hand with WASH and nutrition cluster together with other actors in these counties to ensure that the most vulnerable population are well served.

1 - Needs

To improve access of WASH services to the most vulnerable population through provision of safe water, improved sanitation and hygiene, Training and mitigate WASH'Nut cases to children admitted with SAM and MAM through an integrated response approach in Aweil East, Pariang and Pibor Counties and to Integrate WASH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package. The specific needs for this project include determining the current WASH needs in these three locations and finding out the causes behind a great level of negative WASH and nutrition impact and find ways of improving access to WASH and nutrition services through provision of safe water, sanitation and hygiene and to mitigate WASH related malnutrition cases to the most vulnerable population focusing on the provision of life saving interventions, and prevention of acute malnutrition through implementation of prioritized and integrated multi-Sectoral responses. This project will target a population of 20,000 in the three counties at an average package of $15 translating to a budget of $300,000. Aweil East, Pariang and Pibor counties are among the most stricken counties in South Sudan. Through May-July assessment conducted in these locations by REACH it was evidenced that 78% of households did not have access to a latrine, only 23% of assessed households reported having soap at the time of the assessment and a mere 6% of respondents reported washing their hands at all critical times. Generally with water alone (65%). The findings of the report point to the fact that activities aiming at inducing behavioral changes with regards to WASH practices and attitudes should be implemented in Aweil East, Pariang and Pibor counties particularly with regards to hand washing practices, open defecation and treatment of water. Pariang county- conflict in the area has displaced many households, most of whom are living with family members. The coexistence of refugees, IDPs, and host community members in Pariang County is at times strained and stretches resources with very few safe water access points, Poor hygiene, which leads to infections .This applies to Pibor and Aweil east as well .
