Project Summary

Since the 15th of April armed conflict spiraled between the SAF and the RSF leading to mass displacement, huge causalities and complete collapse in the basic service sector( health, water, energy and food) . According to the 2023 HNO the four most significant risks identified are conflict, disasters associated with natural hazards, disease outbreaks, and economic deterioration. this project targets 40,000 people with Basic WASH services in Darfur, Gadaref, Khartoum, Port Sudan, Aljazirah and South Kordofan states. The project will ensure that women, men and children have access to information and awareness raising on the safe household water treatment and use of proper sanitation facilities. Women, girls, boys and men continue to be affected by protection related WASH issues specially now in light of the recent events and rise in protection concerns and reports of increasing violations, with gender based violence occurring due to insufficient and unsafe access to water and sanitation facilities. • Sustain access to safe water,
sanitation and hygiene improvements and awareness is critical to save thousands of lives
of children and women. Excretal and poor disposal coupled with lack of proper good
hygiene practices characterized by rampant open defecation leaves a large portion of the
population at high risk of preventable water related diseases such as cholera and AWD.
UPO will ensure that women, men, girls and boys are consulted in the decision making and have equal access to safe water supply, sanitation and hygiene services to
prevent potential public health risk as a result of using unprotected water sources poor
sanitation and hygiene practices. • The consequences of poor water, sanitation and
hygiene are stark, with more boys and girls dying from water and sanitation related
illnesses such as diarrhea; this exacerbates the health of malnourished boys and girls and
opportunistic infections like pneumonia and cholera that have been reported in parts of
Darfur and Kordofan. Inadequate access to safe water points also has implications on
women and the education of girls in particular as they have to spend considerable periods
fetching the scarce commodity when they should be in school. UPO believes that through
provision of safe water, sanitation and hygiene services to these vulnerable communities
that will cover aspects of social mobilization, rehabilitation of water points, sanitation
facilities, emergency hygiene promotion, enhancing capacity of the communities and
resilience. UPO will ensure active involvement of women and men in decision-making in
the provision of safe and appropriate water, sanitation and hygiene services. UPO uses and focuses on gender sensitive programming and modalities by designing all activities through gender roles frameworks and gender matrix frameworks to ensure safe and inclusive participation and sustainability of the project outcomes by creating a change theory in the communities gender norms and harmful practices and promoting healthy habits and co-existing spheres.

1 - Needs

the ongoing tribal conflicts and the floods have increase significantly the number of IDPs this year 2022 . The immediate influx of IDPs is increasing pressure on few shared WASHinfrastructures/facilities among the IDPs and the vulnerable host community especially inJebel Mara area .Despite the provided humanitarian services in camps, rural areas inDarfur and Kordofan , major gaps in WASH sector remained unsatisfactorily responded,the newly displaced people mostly women and children are in lack of urgent WASHservices. Only 2.4 3 L/P/per day in most of the visited locations. UPO assessments werejointly conducted by HAC in South, East, North and Central Darfur, to contribute to acommon understanding of WASH needs in Golo. In general, the assessments reported thatwith the exception of few (IDPs) typically walk anywhere from 8 11 Km to collect water.This poses significant risks, particularly for women and girls, who often bear the brunt ofabuse and physical and/or sexual violence given their vulnerability to armed groups andmale aggressors when traveling to and from remote water sources. At the same time, thewater collected is commonly untreated as it is derived from Hafir, ponds, , while opendefecation continues to be widespread – compounding the vulnerability among displacedand conflict affected communities to chronic illnesses and disease outbreaks. Theassessments carried out to date show that approx. 90% of surveyed host and IDPhouseholds lack access to latrines and practice open defecation along Hafir where wateris collected for household use and human consumption. Further, hygiene practices andsolid waste management require critical attention by continuing to reinforce the capacityof existing hygiene committees, in addition to forming new ones where necessary.Meanwhile, the training of hygiene promoters/volunteers at the camps and gatheringslevel needs to be intensified alongside the provision of hygiene kits. UPO thereforeproposes activities that will help improve the health, hygiene and sanitation practicesthrough behavior change. Overall, the findings reveal that approx. 80% ofthe communities surveyed lack basic access to sanitation facilities, adequate protectionmechanisms are largely absent for women and girls to safely access boreholes, and thecombination of overuse of existing water facilities and unavailability of spare parts, alongwith typically limited maintenance, precludes the proper functioning of water facilities.This trend, in turn, contributes to numerous water facilities being abandoned, with themajority of displaced and conflict affected community members (resorting to unprotectedsurface water from rivers, Hafir ponds and swamps)

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

UPO will conduct environment impact assessment before the project starts, to identifythe potential risks, impact as well mitigation measures to be applied both duringconstruction and operation phases of the project. the key issues UPO will look intoduring the assessment are as follows 1- Soil erosion and contamination. 2- Waterpollution. 3- Solid waste generation. 4- Public safety and health 5- Conflictingdemands for water use. For each of the identified impacts, mitigation measures havebeen suggested as follows: a) Avoidance of major impacts: major impacts aregenerally considered unacceptable, ones that would endure in the long-term or extendover a large area; UPO will ensure effective mitigation measures b) Reduction of majorand moderate impacts to as low as reasonably practicable (ALARP) by planning,designing and controlling mitigation measures. This implies that mitigation measureswill be applied until the limitations of cost-effectiveness and practical application havebeen reached. c) Implementation of good practices for impacts rated as minor, in orderto ensure that impacts are managed within good reason
