Project Summary

The prevalence of the socio-political crisis within the North West and South West regions of Cameroon has brought the inhabitants of both regions face-to-face with the realities of insecurity, extreme hunger, extreme GBV, destruction of property and sources of livelihood as well as an ever-increasing death toll. In effect, the socio-political crisis has resulted in mass exodus from both regions into the more stable regions of Littoral and the West. According to UNHCR’s locations of persons of concern December 2020 report, both the West and Littoral regions host over 75,000 IDPs each (a figure that has considerably swelled in 2021 following prolonged instability in both crisis regions). Added to the 711,056 IDPs reported by UNHCR as of January 2021 from both the North West and South West are 321,886 IDP s from the crisis hit Far North region of the country, bringing the total number of displaced persons within the country to a whooping 1,032,942 people within the country.
This massive displaced population is inclusive of children and other youths of school going age who present as vulnerable education seekers in their host communities, forcing educational institutions to morph into structures that can effectively accommodate this massive influx of students. This has seen the creation and/or upgrade of unilingual French schools into Bilingual schools capable of meeting the needs of the English-speaking immigrants. However, this upgrade is most often not accompanied by relevant structural and human resource upgrade in these facilities, resulting in increased pressure on the available educational resources and poor service delivery to both host community children and IDPs.
Many IDP children of school going age are unable to access education due to a wide spectrum of hindrances inclusive of financial, social and language barriers. For IDP students who actually make it into schools, language barriers, unresolved trauma, PTSD, insensitivity of both students and instructors, accompanied by adaptation pressures make knowledge acquisition very difficult for these students. This is due to the fact that schools lack the appropriate institutional resources (structural and human) to meet the needs of these IDP students coming in from the crisis zones and living in relatively unstable (both physical and mentally) homes. Little attention is placed on the psychological needs, and even less is made towards easing the adaptation process in view of enhancing language acquisition processes, and allocation of any form financial and material viability. Against this backdrop, effective realization of education for IDP children is next to impossible.
In view of bettering the position held by IDP children within host communities in the French regions, there is a need to enhance the structures, mechanism and processes put in place to realize the education of IDP children with respect to building capacity and development/implementation of research/evident based approaches for effective realization of IDP children education. In this regard, a cataloguing of all necessary direct stakeholders (inclusive of educational institutions, PTAs , relevant governmental/non-governmental bodies and other community led initiatives) and development of a harmonized approach to the position held by IDP children, as well as capacity enhancement initiatives for local educational institutions are vital. It is for these reasons that the project was developed to realize inclusive and safe academic environments for IDP and host community children within the Littoral and West region.
