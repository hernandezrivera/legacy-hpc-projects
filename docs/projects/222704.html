Project Summary

According to the Nov 18, 2024 IPC report, 22% of 996,179 population in Western Equatoria are severely food insecure, with 19.2% facing crises level of acute food security (IPC 3) and 2.8% at emergency level of food insecurity(IPC4). Tambura is one of the worst affected areas in terms of food insecurity, with 25.2% of the 122,913 population classified under IPC 3 and 4.8% under IPC 4. In Yambio, 14.9% of the 173,924 population are at IPC 3 level of acute food insecurity. The population of refugees in Makpandu refugee camp in Yambio are the worst affected, making 47.95% of the food insecure population in Yambio County. Intertribal conflict between the Azande and Balanda tribes in Tambura has left over 37,559 people displaced, without access to adequate humanitarian assistance and recovery support. Education for children has been disrupted by the conflict, leaving many children out of school in Tambura. 2250 newly relocated Sudanese refugees from Renk into Makfandu face catastrophic living conditions characterized by limited access to food, health and psychosocial support and education. The decline in humanitarian funding has severely affected service delivery for refugees in Makpandu, with WFP significantly reducing food rations for refugees in the camp.
The proposed intervention will provide lifesaving food assistance and livelihoods support to the most affected IDPs and host community to enhance their recovery and resilience. The project will provide unconditional cash assistance to 1500 (525m, 975f) households to meet their basic needs. Agricultural inputs and training to enable 500 (175m, 325f) IDPs engage in emergency food production will be provided. The project will support livelihood initiatives aimed at enhancing income capacities and assets of 550 (193m, 357f) affected households, with special focus on supporting small scale business enterprises for vulnerable women groups and GBV survivors and provision of poultry. Additionally, the project will facilitate access to quality education for 1000 (500m, 500f) children affected by the conflict in Tambura by providing basic teaching and scholastic material support, payment of incentives for teachers and training of school governance bodies to ensure quality control and assurance.
In order to maximize the impact of the project at household and individual levels, the project will be implemented in an integrated and complementary manner with other ongoing protection initiatives by JRS, UNHCR and World Vision in Tambura and Makpandu (Yambio). JRS will focus on addressing food security, protection and education gaps in Tambura and Makpandu in close coordination and collaboration with World Vision, UNHCR, Commission for refugees affairs (CRA) and office of Relief and Rehabilitation Commission in Western Equatoria and county level. The action will particularly complement JRS emergency lifesaving and protection interventions currently active in Tambura, ensuring more people are reached with education and protection services in the target locations.

1. Needs

According to the Nov 18, 2024 IPC report, 22% of 996,179 population in Western Equatoria are severely food insecure, with 19.2% facing crises level of acute food security (IPC 3) and 2.8% at emergency level of food insecurity(IPC4). Tambura is one of the worst affected areas in terms of food insecurity, with 25.2% of the 122,913 population classified under IPC 3 and 4.8% under IPC 4. In Yambio, 14.9% of the 173,924 population are at IPC 3 level of acute food insecurity. The population of refugees in Makpandu refugee camp in Yambio are the worst affected, making 47.95% of the food insecure population in Yambio County. Intertribal conflict between the Azande and Balanda tribes in Tambura has left over 37,559 people displaced, without access to adequate humanitarian assistance, services and recovery support according to the interagency needs assessment conducted in May and July 2024. The conflict caused massive destruction of lives, property, shelter and disrupted access to basic services for the entire population. Women faced pervasive levels of GBV linked to sexual violence and exploitation amidst inadequate protection and psychosocial support services, leaving them mostly traumatized and exposed to the risks of developing post-traumatic stress disorders. People injured in the conflict lack access to physical rehabilitation services commensurate with their needs. Tambura used to be one of the breadbaskets for Western Equatoria, but this potential has been significantly eroded by the conflict as agricultural and livelihood activities are severely affected. Education for children has been disrupted by the recent conflict, leaving many children out of school in Tambura. Teachers faced low teaching morale due to lack of payment, causing significant impact on quality of education for children in the County. About 2250 newly relocated Sudanese refugees from Renk into Makfandu face catastrophic living conditions characterized by limited access to food, health and psychosocial support and education support. This is in addition to 13,533 refugees from other nationalities such as Congo, CAR and Burundi. The decline in humanitarian funding has severely affected service delivery for refugees in Makpandu, with WFP significantly reducing food rations for refugees in the camp. In August 2024, JRS conducted a rapid needs assessment in Makpandu camp and host community. The findings of the assessment showed increased access barriers to food for refugees/IDPs in the camp, inadequate protection services for GBV survivors, poor health services and increased access barriers to education services for children due to limited access to teaching and scholastic materials support. The worsening economic conditions and lack of opportunities for livelihood development complicate recovery efforts, leaving refugees and IDPs more vulnerable and exposed to negative coping mechanisms and protection risks such as GBV. This situation is compounded by limited number of humanitarian agencies providing services in Makpandu. World Vision and UNHCR are the only agencies operating in the Makpandu camp, providing limited protection and camp management services in the camp. Finally, the spontaneous return of refugee returnees from Congo, CAR and Sudan to Western Equatoria increase pressure on already limited humanitarian resources, leaving unmet needs. Currently, there are over 26,280 refugee returnees in Western Equatoria who are integrated in the host communities. The government public service and social protection system have been severely weakened by the effects of conflict and economic collapse. Therefore, JRS seeks funding to provide integrated emergency livelihood and education support to 3683 vulnerable IDPs and returnees/host community in Tambura and Makpandu in Yambio County.

2. Project activities

JRS seeks funding to provide integrated emergency livelihood and education support to 3683 vulnerable IDPs and host community in Tambura and Makpandu camp in Yambio County, Western Equatoria. This will be achieved through the following list of measures: Outcome 1: The action will contribute to improved access to food and income needs of 2550(893m, 1657f) IDPs and host community in Makpandu camp (Yambio) and Tambura County. This will be achieved through provision of unconditional cash assistance to 1500 (525m, 975f) vulnerable households to meet their food and basic needs; provision of emergency agricultural inputs (seeds, tools) and technical agronomic training to 500 (175m, 325f) farming households to engage in emergency food production; poultry re-stocking for 300 (105m, 195f) households; and support for 250 (88m, 262f) households to engage in small scale incomes generation activities/businesses to boost their resilience. Outcome 2: The project will contribute to increased access to quality and equitable education services for 1000 (500m, 500f) children in Makpandu camp and Tambura County. This will be achieved through provision of teaching and scholastic materials support to 1000 (500m, 500f) economically vulnerable school children; distribution of sanitary pads to 500 girls and awareness creation on sexual reproductive health to increase class attendance and participation in learning activities; provision of monthly incentives to 84 primary school teachers (50m, 34f) to improve teachers' morale and quality of education for children in Tambura; and strengthening capacity of school governance bodies (PTAs) to improve schools management, development, and quality control and assurance.
