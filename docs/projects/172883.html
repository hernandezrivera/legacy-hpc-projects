Project Summary

The project aims to provide immediate live saving and integrated basic services assistance of WASH, Health and Nutrition, to 307,655 refugees and host community including 79,655 South Sudanese, 58,000 Non South Sudanese refugees and 70,000 newly arrived Ethiopian refugees. The host community target is 20%.
Currently Sudan host approximately 1,110,000 million refuges individuals including over 49,000 newly arrived Ethiopian refuges. Many refuges face protracted displacement and new arrivals continue enter Sudan from neighboring countries. Refuges are highly vulnerable and need multi sectoral interventions to address their vulnerabilities and needs while in asylum, and to maintain and fulfil their rights. They are mostly coming to Sudan with poor health conditions with little to no personnel belongings with them.
and who are in urgent need of protection, WASH, nutrition, shelter & health support. The majority of the affected & target population are women & children. Over 50% of the newly arrived Ethiopian refuges are women & children who are arriving in poor health conditions after walking days to cross in to Sudan and have little to no personnel belongings with them. Refuges are in urgent need of protection, WASH, nutrition, shelter & health support.
The project will seek to provide live saving interventions & sustain services of WASH, Health & Nutrition through safe, clean water & adequate sanitation facilities, quality primary health care, preventive & curative nutritional interventions. The services will be provided in a holistic & collective manner to enhance peaceful coexistence between refuges & host community & improving the peoples’ wellbeing. Gender, protection &
accountability will be mainstreamed throughout the implementation. CIS with national partners will work in coordinated manner to provide the needed support, staff will participate & attend coordination meetings at different levels to provide assistance in coordinated & collaborative manner.

1 - Needs

Sudan host over 1,110,000 refugees & asylums (protracted and new arrivals). CARE target areas host big numbers of refuges including Khartoum, South Kordofan, East Darfur, Kassala and Gadaref States. Refuges are highly vulnerable and need urgent live saving assistance to address their vulnerabilities, needs and un met gaps. 52% of the registered households are female headed and 1% are child headed. Despite progress made significant gaps remain & exacerbated by funding gaps, economic crises including shortages of fuel, cash, medicines and fragile health system. Over 30% of the refuges are living in camps where they are relay on assistance provided through humanitarian partners. Although progress has been made to achieve minimum assistance standers in camps, gaps remain across sectors. The majority of refuges children remain out of schools, water availability and latrines are below standards with high open defecation reported and access to health services is inconsistence associated with supply gaps in basic medicines and lack of qualified staff. There is a high food insecurity, this compounded by limiting access to income generation opportunities and prevalence of child labor, early marriage and other child protection issues as well as high risks of SGBV. In Eastern Sudan, there are over 150,000 refugees and asylum seekers living in 9 camps and out of camps settlements in Kassala and Gadaref States in protracted refugee situation and they need humanitarian assistance. They lack access to sufficient services and infrastructure, with WASH, Health, Education facilitations need urgent rehabilitation and expansion. They are expose to protection risks and exploitation including SGBV by the criminal networks who are active in Eastern Sudan and involved in human smuggling and people trafficking. Also, in other parts of the country (Khartoum, Darfur) thousands of refugees are living in poor situation, seeking for livelihood opportunities and humanitarian assistance. New arrivals continued to arrive through different crossing points along the borders of South Sudan, Eritrea, Ethiopia, CAR etc.it is expected that in 2021 around 40,500 refuges will arrive from neighboring countries in 2021 and will need humanitarian assistance in Early November 2020 armed conflict erupted in Ethiopia in Tigray’s region. Accordingly, Sudan received big influxes of refuges who arrived in poor health situation, non or less personal possessions and frighten seeking safety, security and live saving humanitarian assistance. There is gap in all sectors and there is high need to provide humanitarian live saving assistance of all sectors and in all areas including entry points, transits locations, reception centers and settlements. (For more information please refer to the Refugee Response Plan Chapter and the Ethiopian Response Plan).CARE will provide refugees with needed assistance in East Darfur, South Kordofan, Kassala and Gadaref States.

2 - Activities/Outputs

WASH: Construction households latrines and bathing shelters; Solid waste management (cleaning tools, disposal sites, and cleaning campaigns); construction of blocks of VIP latrines; Hygiene promotion (promoters recruitment, training; awareness raising campaigns, and IEC materials); construction and rehabilitation Water facilities; support operation and maintenance of water facilities; chlorination of water sources; water quality testing; water user committee’s formation/revival & training; Distribution of WASH NFIs (Jerrycans, Potties); Provide basic primary health care services (consultation and referrals). Health & Nutrition: Construction/rehabilitation of health facilities and equipping them; Basic primary health care services (consultation and referrals); Health outreach and surveillance services (training of health workers and awareness-raising); CMAM services; Mothers Support Groups and nutrition outreach workers (training and outreach services); Outreach services (awareness, screening, and defaulter tracing. Activities will cover80% of refuges & 20% of host community from the target to meet the ongoing and unmet needs. The implementation of the activities will be done in close coordination and collaboration with Government of Sudan's Commission of Refuges (COR), Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC), UNHCR, related ministries and other national partners in each state. This will help to avoid duplication of efforts & resource misuse. CARE will attend coordination meetings through Refugees Consultation Forums & refugees working groupsat Federal & States levels and strategies and plans will be shared so that responses are needs based & timely. CIS will support response monitoring & analyze progress through regular reporting & share information on targets & activities on regular bases & as required.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

CARE recognizes that water and sanitation, health and nutrition response programs andlivelihoods diverse action interventions do have impact on the health of the public,environment and natural resources. Interventions to preserve and protect the environmentwill be included from the analysis, design phase, implementation, monitoring & evaluation of eachprogram intervention. The mitigation measures could include but not limited to: analysisof the potential impact of key interventions and discussion with community leaders andsector experts; regular joint water and sanitation facilities monitoring and supervision;emphasis on environmental education and capacity building for increased environmentalawareness among beneficiaries and implementing partners; solid and liquid wastemanagement; actions to avoid water pollution due to excavation, backfilling andconcreting, hauling and damping of earth materials; controlling formation of stagnantwater pool to avoid soil erosion and breeding mosquitoes, use sustainably energy sources particularly Solar system that last long. Health and nutrition facilities measures include cleaning campaigns, quality and monitored incinerators, and refresher training to health professionals, WASH experts and community volunteers. Youth, women, men and others will be organized into water user’s committees, hygiene promoters,management groups, health workers, committees & volunteers, nutrition volunteers, Livelihood committees and groups where appropriate and tailored training will be provided to contribute positively to the management of natural resources and peaceful coexistence.Environmental impact assessment will be carried out if interventions require to do so and will be decided with sector ministries

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CARE will take extra caution in analyzing the protection of at risk groups, includingchildren and girls. Efforts will include: no creation of new risks, address discrimination,analysis of the most vulnerable, safety and dignity in service delivery, orientation andfamiliarization on humanitarian principles and rights, and promoting empowerment.Furthermore, CARE and partners will exercise principles of protection mainstreaming:meaningful access through consultation and involvement of the communities about thebest way to deliver the services and considerations for vulnerable groups, accountabilitymechanisms and Field visits. CARE and partners will empower communities by training &capacity building & awareness raising about key protection concerns and risks, consultingthem in decision making process, identifying volunteers and establishing and trainingcommunity committees. Strengthening community protection mechanisms and improvingaccess to services through information dissemination on available services, goodselection criteria and identified action of the most vulnerable and referrals will be prioritized.Do no harm analysis will be carried for each sector and the activities will be screened notto enhance risks for all especially women, girls and children. Facilities such as latrinesand water points, income generating activities and trainings will be analyzed anddiscussed with at risk groups to ensure that the beneficiaries are protected and that theservices are easily accessible and safely usable by all. Protection risks faced by womenand girls, and in particular S/GBV risks, will be the specific focus of capacity building forstaff and partners to ensure that they are able to detect cases of abuse. Staff will also beoriented in order to make sure that programming does no harm and that they are able torespond safely and appropriately to protection cases when they are reported, using aconfidential and survivor centered approach to disclosures of violence. An internal mechanism for reporting complaints and ensuring that required measure are in place will be promoted throughout the implementation period.
