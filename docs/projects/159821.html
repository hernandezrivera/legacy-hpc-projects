Project Summary

Providing gender sensitive Emergency WASH services to drought affected IDP and Hosting communities in Somalia and Somaliland. ARC is proposing an emergency WASH project with DRR component interventions in the regions of Banadir, Lower Juba, Middle Shebelle, Sool, Sanaag, and Togdheer regions in South Central and Somaliland regions. In strategy of ARC for implementation of this project, steps are planned to mobilize and socialize communities, especially community leaders/religious leaders so that women will be provided with the opportunities of contribution to implementation of proposed WASH activities. Such opportunity of interaction of women in the development and decision making affairs will have an impact on further gender dynamics to improve their condition and impact on Gender­Based violence (GBV), particularly for girls and young women, the lack of access to gender segregated latrines leave them vulnerable to sexual violence, which frustrates efforts for them to lead a healthy and productive life. This is a major concern as well as one of human rights in IDP camps in Somalia/Somaliland. This means that many girls and young women live in constant fear as there are no toilet facilities for them, hence they are often forced to relieve themselves in relatively villages peripheries during the early hours of the morning or late at night, when the likelihood of sexual assault or violence is higher, hence one of many triggers for open defecation.
ARC will provide an effective gender-responsive programming as per ARC’s Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) standards and the Global WASH Cluster agreed gender policy in the Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) sector. ARC will take into account several gender sensitive points into WASH intervention but not limited to reduce walking distances to latrines and install solar lights to rebel sexually abusive groups and empower adolescent woman/girls by providing gender segregated toilets and information and fundamental life skills in hygiene promotion and safe sanitation.
ARC targets over (60,146) beneficiaries, comprising 6,668 IDPs and 26,566 drought affected agro-pastoral and host community across these regions, with breakdown of Female 15,625 (12,294 women + 3331 girls) and Male 11,287 (9,087 men + 2,200 boys) through WASH infrastructure and services. This emergency WASH project will be integrated with Health; Nutrition, Protection and Livelihood sectors to have greater impact on the lives of the affected communities. The linkage to health component will ensure a comprehensive approach to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with diseases resulting from deficient sanitation conditions, lack of access to safe water, and low level of good hygiene practices. Most importantly, ARC will focus on increasing access to safe water by improving the water infrastructure. This includes protecting shallow wells from contamination or pollution in Kismayo, Banadir, Afmadow, Togdheer, Sool and Sanaag regions through rehabilitation of strategic boreholes by installing hybrid solar water pumping system, construction of water storage facilities and water distribution networks/extensions to health facilities, construction of water catchments and zero coliform by providing water treatment tablets to target household and water quality monitoring and inspections.
ARC’s WASH technical designs adhere to SPHERE standards, mainstreaming of gender considerations, including IASC guidelines for GBV Interventions in Humanitarian settings, WASH, securing access to the elderly and those with special needs (disabled people) as per Handicap International Guidelines, and to the environmental considerations.

Project Activities

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2019), in drought affected regions, water scarcity and inadequate quality are leading causes of forming internal displacement (IDP) and conflict Somalia /Somaliland, where women and children must often travel over 30 minutes to reach drinking water points. The country continues to suffer from AWD/Cholera and risk factors for outbreaks persist in several hotspots, including IDP camps and hosting communities in urban and sub-urban settlements, particularly in Jubbaland, Banadir, Togdheer, Sool and Sanaag regions. Additionally, due to prolonged drought conditions pastoral communities put huge pressures on already precarious water points. The coverage for safe drinking water stands at 56% in Somalia (UNICEF 2018). Only 28% of households can access an improved water source in rural areas (UNPO 04/12/2018). Women and children often travel over 30 minutes to reach drinking water points (OCHA 03/01/2019). Scarcity and the inadequate quality of water in Somaliland is a leading cause of displacement and conflict (OCHA 03/01/2019). In addition, supplies and spare parts for water systems in most of these areas remain problematic. From hygiene and sanitation perspectives - unreliable accesses to a temporary and/or inadequate WASH facility remain huge problems of the humanitarian landscape in Somalia/Somaliland. Hand washing with soap is practiced by very few because of the limited availability of soap and lack of functional hand washing points. Inadequate access to sanitation infrastructure in the abovementioned regions remains a challenge and results in widespread open defecations. Activities proposed by ARC also include construction of communal latrines and institutional latrines with hand-washing facilities for schools and health facilities. ARC will also promote improved hygiene in the target communities through trainings, sensitization of volunteers and community members, and provision of hygiene kits to AWD patients and families with malnourished children. For sustainability and ownership, ARC will create, train and equip community mechanisms and committees to improve and maintain sanitation and solid waste management in the targeted IDP settlements to reduce the spread of diseases. ARC will use methods like "Super Mothers" and camp cleanliness competitions, which have proven effective to ensure community engagement and behavioral change. Activities include:• Protection/Rehabilitation of 6 strategic shallow wells (1 in Banaadir, 2 in Jubbland and 2 in Sanaag and with solar powered pump and water storage. • Construction and extension of 6 water kiosk for IDP camps (Lower Juba, Sool, Sanaag and Benadir). • Rehabilitation of 5 boreholes in (1 in Togdheer, 2 in Lower Juba, 1 in Sool, and 1 in Banadir.) • Excavation of 3 water dams (1 in Sanaag, 1 in Sool, 1 in Togdheer ) • Train water management committees • Purchase water quality test kits i.e pH, TDS, EC, OPS, Turbidity meter and Total Coliform/E. coli Indicator Bacteria • Water quality monitoring, sampling, testing, analyzing and treatment (Chlorination) • Emergency water voucher to 3,000 HH in Lower Juba and Sool during the driest month of the year • Distribute 14000 hygiene kits and hold hygiene promotion in IDP and Hosting Communities • Hold community capacity building training (PHAST/CHAST/handwashing/AWD) and engage in community hygiene volunteer incentives. • Construct 75 communal latrines – 25 in Sool, 15 in Banadir, 15 Middle Shabelle, 10 in Sanaag, and 10 in Togdheer regions• 22 institutional latrines - 11 for schools and 11 for health facilities in Sool, Sanaag, and Togdheer• Engage in sanitation social marketing approach to build local capacity for Sool, Sanaag, and Togdheer districts• Engage in community led total sanitation trainings to ensure ODF zones

Mitigation Measures

ARC will prevent risks of exclusion based on displacement by ensuring that internally displaced persons benefit from the interventions just like the host communities. The project will target women, men, girls and boys in both IDP camps and host communities so that equity and equality is maintained at all levels. In actualizing this, there are risks that ARC will need to mitigate so that service provision to the most deserving communities is not hindered. All distributions will be done in safe, convenient places considering all aspects of protection and security of the beneficiaries as per the principle of "Do No Harm" ARC will also put in place community consultation and feedback mechanism Kuja Kuja to collect real time community satisfaction feedback (www.kujakuja.com). Please provide further explanation

Long distance to the nearest water source is a protection risk for most of the IDPs in Kismayo, Afmadow, Lasanod, Banaadir, Dhobley and Middle Shebelle (Balcad and Warsheikh), putting women and adult girls who normally fetch water under threat of rape and other sexual violence. In order to mitigate this risk, ARC will ensure water points are located at central locations that are easily accessible to all and less than 500 m to the targeted beneficiaries. Secondly, communal latrines in IDPs set up are currently not gender-separated, lack lighting and are not always lockable, which will increase likelihood of GBV cases occurring at the communal latrines. In order to reduce this risk, ARC proposes to construct gender separated latrines where possible, ensuring latrines are gender separate with lockable doors, lightening at night by providing solar lamps, and installation of disability features. The other protection risk that need to be addressed by the project is gender discrimination in family chores, which puts women and girls at risk. This risk can be avoided by ensuring men and boys take part in fetching water from long distance or getting water from a closer source. The risk mentioned above can be addressed by adequate community WASH facilities. It is also essential to ensure equal representation of both genders and that the voices of women are heard and considered in project planning and implementation. ARC's WASH program will ensure reduction of overcrowding at the water points, reduction of queuing time to the acceptable level (30 min. maximum per SPHERE standards), and that locations are accessible, convenient and don't pose a threat to the beneficiaries. ARC will also put in place community consultation and feedback mechanism Kuja Kuja to collect real-time community satisfaction feedback (www.kujakuja.com). All distributions will be done in safe, convenient places while considering all aspects of protection and security of the beneficiaries as per the principle of "Do No Harm." In Somalia/Somaliland, the ARC will conduct focus group discussions, representing different age and gender groups, as well as minorities and persons with disability to participate project consultation, design and beneficiaries selection processes. ARC will have a platform to submit all complaints, if violations are unaddressed. ARC will ensure that the project is monitored and evaluated reliably via effective project monitoring and control mechanism. ARC will register all contact details of the beneficiaries, WMCs, WASH Committee, local authorities, targeted institutions, vendors, hygiene promoters and any other stakeholders involved in the project implementation. The contact details will be accessible to all stakeholders for easy tracking of progress and beneficiary perception on their satisfaction on their entitlements as well as checking the progress of the project.
