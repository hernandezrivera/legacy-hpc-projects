Project Summary

Live saving: Emergency Health Services (EHS) in Fangak South and Panyijiar shall be responsible for providing emergency and primary health care services delivery to children under five and pregnant and lactating women and for all patients outside of coverage by other implementing health services agencies. It shall also deals with transportation of patients between the upgraded PHCCs that run comprehensive medical and surgical services or the hospitals and Public health facilities in the Counties of Fangak South and Panyijiar in Southern Unity State. TOCAA is working in partnership with MOH and the health cluster with the goal of providing constant health services to all areas by physicians and paramedics that undertakes comprehensive and integrated interventions that shall be generally performed only by Emergency clinician and physicians in all of the abandoned Health facilities which are recommended by the SMOH with approval from the national MOH to TOCAA after consultation with the previously existing few health agencies present in the same geographical areas.
TOCAA Emergency health Service program shall be an opportunity where the local paramedics apply their knowledge and skills in community based environments. In knowledge the shortage of physician and competences health care workers in the community. Paramedics, clinical officers have been upgraded as skill birth attendances to Clinical Associate 2 years training Task shifting program on reproductive health to save lives of new born babies women and girls during pregnancies or when giving birth. These medical cadres shall work in a collaborative referral pathway system with other big health facilities run by INGOs agencies in the Counties, aiming on delivering quality primary health care services to a remote/ rural and hard to reach community. The Long and Brier model shall be successfully achieve great access to primary health care services for the residents (Host and IDPs) of these two Counties. Consultation with residents and local authorities has highlighted the extreme gap on the basic health need. The community leaders and authorities also noted that their health status has not been known from HIV/AIDS and other diseases that can be screen at early stage of infection, due to absent counseling and testing services for HI/AIDS, Syphilis, etc. Also they expressed their grievance on the along waited times to have access to these basic health care services. Currently the data collection and reporting has shown low cover and discrepancies due to lack of systematic reporting channels which is a general challenge in the Country. This project shall strengthens and improve basic primary health care services delivery by making those children and women who has been cut off fundamental basic primary health care services , such as immunization for all childhood immunizable diseases including Tetanus toxoid for women at child bearing ages. And also cut off treatment of child hood illnesses and common major killers the communicable and none communicable diseases, children who missed opportunity of getting access to vaccination should be vaccinated, also we shall introduce introducing Reproductive Health i.e Basic Emergency Maternal Obstetric Care (BEMOC) including HIV/AIDS testing service and ART treatment and care services, Engage Community on awareness for services up take, Improve supplies delivery to the site and local staffs capacity building for Health care and Nutrition workers to reduce the high burden of major killer diseases, Such as Immunizable childhood illness, Tuberculosis, Prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone disease outbreaks. Needs

Save lives and alleviate suffering through safe access to basic primary health care services and resources with dignity .The protracted absent of basic health services such as routine immunization and basic emergency maternal obstetric Care for the last 5years during the civil war or crisis of 2013 to date, these services has been unavailable for a large majority of the population in Fangak South and Panyijiar Counties, with more than 56% of the population unable to access health care services. An unstable political environment, protracted conflict and resulting displacement, and continued economic deterioration and inflation of the local currency all create a disabling environment for well-functioning health system and impact health seeking behavior. A shortage of health workers, a national health information system with poor in reporting, and a reduction of health implementing partners on the ground since the July 2016 crisis has exacerbated the situation. Women and girls are disproportionately impacted by the protracted crisis due to an increase in sexual and gender based violence (SGBV). South Sudan repeatedly experiences multiple and simultaneous infectious disease outbreaks year after year, most recently the ongoing endemic cholera outbreak and Tuberculosis and kala azar endemic in Unity and Jonglei States the longest endemic and epidemic in the country's history. Challenges on health and medical supplies procurement and transportation to the sites are another challenge that leads to stock outs of medicines and medical supplies. As a result of these aforementioned factors, it is imperative that lifesaving health care services delivery is provided through both static and mobile clinics in areas highly populated with vulnerable individuals as a prioritized intervention. In collaboration with the health authority in Southern Unity State and Fangak States TOCAA has obtained authorization from the State Ministries of Health to fill in the gap by operationalize nonfunctioning health facilities which cannot be reach other Health implementing partners in the same areas. Five Health facilities (2 PHCCs, 3 PHCUs in Panyijiar County including the well-constructed permanent County Hospital to be run as a PHCC High level. In Fangak South TOCAA optained 5 Health Facilities which has not been functioning for all these 5 years of conflict. All 10 static clinics aimed to provide lifesaving and referral services as per the Ministry of Health’s Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services in Primary Health Care, and basic emergency obstetric and newborn care (BeMONC) which includes antenatal and postnatal care, PMTCT, and family planning, HIV/AIDS, STI services. TOCAA will establish Cold chain hubs in each County for mass and routine vaccinations through the expanded program on immunization (EPI), as well as health and hygiene education and promotion (both door to door, at clinics and through community based events). TOCAA will also establish comprehensive HIV/AIDS and TB testing and treatment services in the given Health Facilities by the State health authorities and the community leaders. To address and mitigate the high endemicity of the childhood immunizabe diseases such as measles and TB the affected the most vulnerable populations. The conflict has left men and women and girls and boys vulnerable to stress and trauma and the women girls become prone to sexual and gender based violence. Fangak County is amongst the acute emergency phases such as IPC5 in Jan to Jun 2018, TOCAA will continue to mainstream basic health, reproductive health, including mental health and psycho social support services (MHPSS) into ongoing PHC services by training the health workers in all components of the BPHNS. TOCAA has a capacity in emergency preparedness and response , and able to participate and conduct Initial Rapid Need Assessment, and and do the (Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) and post emergency situations.
