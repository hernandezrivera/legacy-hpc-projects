Project Summary

South Sudan context keeps changing especially with drivers that take on different forms as new ones come in. What is constant however is the increasing level of vulnerability caused by the compound of complex challenges i.e. conflict, insecurity, subnational violence, cattle raiding and child abduction, unprecedented flash floods, failing economy and impact of COVID-19 on the economy and livelihoods of the citizens.
The formation of Revitalized Transitional Government of National Unity (RTGoNU) somehow brought hope in the early 2020 which was quickly thwarted by the outbreak of COVID-19, increasing level of subnational violence and the unprecedented floods that affected most parts of the country and wiped out livelihoods among many.
The Protection concerns of the people in need have increased. All counties across the country are categorized under by IPC as either severe, extreme or catastrophe with the exception of just 5 which are still under stress.
HDC proposed protection project has activities under 3 Sub-Clusters, General Protection, GBV and HLP to respond these specific needs in 18 counties in Central Equatoria, Jonglei and Upper Nile States to 28,830 targeted persons in need.
General Protection will cover in Central Equatoria all the 6 Counties Juba, Yei, Lainya, Morobo, Terekeka and Kajo Keji. Juba will be through static response while others are mobile response.
In Jonglei is Duk, Pibor, Bor South and Twic East through static response. Due to good number of partners in Jonglei there will be no mobile response, however HDC in case of serious emergencies and when called upon can scale up to respond through mobile team in other areas within Jonglei.
Upper Nile include Malakal and Maban through static and Fashoda, Melut and Longochuk through mobile response.
The project budget for this HRP 2021 is estimated at 500,000 USD with 10% on Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) where markets are functional
As mentioned the response will be both static and mobile. HDC in the past years has seen itself engaged in more mobile responses in locations with close proximity to its field offices.
HDC is currently present in 7 out of the 10 States and 2 of the 3 Administrative Areas – Pibor and Ruweng.
We are now also an official mobile responder in Mobile Protection Working Group under Protection Cluster.
Due to the increasing spread of COVID-19, all activities implemented will take into consideration strictly following COVID-19 preventive measures as well as observing government restrictions under the lock down.
The required COVID-19 protective gear and materials will be made available to the staff and people in need in order to prevent the spread of COVID-19 while ensuring those in need are supported.
Equally important is that HDC will employ use of data collection using technology. Kobocollect app will be used to upload assessment tools and others on to mobile phones.
Other activities will still require use of normal pen and paper like FGDs or taking notes during counselling.

1 - Needs

South Sudan HNO provides substantial information on the humanitarian needs of those affected in South Sudan. However this information is further complemented by other sources of information including Reach’s Humanitarian Situation Monitoring, HDC own assessments and contextual knowledge of the situation as an indigenous humanitarian organization to convey the overall needs of the locations targeted by HDC for its proposed protection programming. The needs in Central Equatoria and Jonglei have been driven by conflict and insecurity which are further exacerbated by escalated subnational violence, increasing number and geographical coverage of unprecedented floods.COVID-19 has had a significant impact in sharply worsening the living conditions and pounding on the struggling economy. Many have lost their jobs especially those running small-scale businesses like restaurants as the consumer buying power diminished. In Central Equatoria there has been a significant increase of individuals not feeling safe in Kajo Keji as Juba continues to remain unsafe for most individuals due to insecurity. Other counties of Yei, Morobo, Lainya and Terekeka are equally insecure with floods affecting Kajo Keji, Juba and Yei. As a result, there has been displacement with its protection risks and many are unable to access food and water as well as markets. (Reach Humanitarian Situation Monitoring Sept 2020) In Jonglei the key major drivers of needs are subnational violence that has intensely increased in early part of 2020 running through most part of the dry season. The unprecedented floods uprooted almost the entire population of Jonglei with many displaced to Central Equatoria in areas like Mangalla and with Juba.Jonglei and Greater Pibor Administrative Area, Humanitarian Update (as of 8 December 2020) reports that it is likely that subnational violence and floods will again ocur in these areas of Jonglei and Pibor in 2021 displacing many more and pushing many further to poverty lines The above-mentioned drivers of needs result in serious protection concerns of neglect of older persons and persons with disabilities. Due to increasing vulnerability among the IDPs, host community, returnees and others, older persons are neglected as people scramble for the few resources both at household and community level. As reported in South Sudan HNO 2021, these drivers including COVID-19 are escalating the risk of GBV including Conflict-Related Sexual Violence (CRSV) and sexual exploitation and abuse. The condition created has also reduced access to sexual and reproductive health services for women and girls. Measures adopted to curb the spread of COVID-19 have also contributed to a rise in GBV incidents. Reports of abuse by intimate partners especially on women including children who are seen doing manual jobs in search of survival. Forced and child marriages have increased.In 2021, some 2 million people are estimated to be affected by GBV. The incapacity of the government to support those in need and address such challenges lays the entire burden on humanitarian partners. Though the government does its best to support access to persons of need by partners, such efforts and others are sometimes hampered or not sufficient especially with increasing insecurity in most areas. HDC will be particularly responding to these needs through static and limited mobile response in the 3 Counties; Juba in Central Equatoria, Duk and Pibor in Jonglei States.

2 - Project activities

General Protection-PROTECTION MONITORING AND ASSESSMENT: Protection Monitors in areas of static presence will do Protection monitoring that will identify protection risks through focus group discussions, key informant interviews, HH interviews or reported at the existing protection desks. Incidences reported by volunteers and staff will be followed up with FGDs and/or KIIs. HDC will participate in joint multisector mobile response assessments to identify protection needs in areas of priority.The reports will be shared with relevant protection sub-clusters and will also inform our protection programming. -INDIVIDUAL PROTECTION ASSISTANCECommunity volunteers or HDC staff that sit at the protection desk will register cases of vulnerability that are then assessment by HDC staff to ascertain the nature of vulnerability. Cases are identified during protection monitoring or referred to HDC by other partners.Based on the assessment, they are recommended for various kinds of support; cash assistance, non-food items, referrals (legal, PSS, etc) -PROTECTION AWARENESS RAISING AND PREVENTION MESSAGINGAwareness raising sessions will be informed by the protection monitoring incident reports and conducted through use of IEC materials, radios and loudspeakers. They will include topics on HLP, SGBV and general protection.The awareness sessions due to COVID-19 restrictions will be conducted with limited numbers but in high frequency to ensure the targets are reached. GBV -LEGAL AIDHDC will respond to only the legal needs of SGBV survivors or victims. This legal assistance will mainly be through cases received through referrals or self-reporting at the protection desk. The support will include legal counselling and advice, support with opening up a case if they choose to and subsequent representation in the courts of law by qualified legal officer. -SURVIVOR-CENTERED PSS AND CASE MANAGEMENTHDC staff will conduct regular monitoring visits, counselling and immediate referral to ensure access to medical services, legal counselling, safe spaces, etc. The survivor will receive life-skills training and/or cash assistance for small business start-up if recommended.HDC staff, community volunteers, community leaders and other key focal persons will receive training on PSS and case management -AWARENESS RAISINGThese sessions conducted among the affected community will have special focus on the importance of preventing and immediately reporting incidents. The community will also be sensitized on common and identified GBV risk factors in the community, mitigation measures and how survivors can confidentially access support and services -HLPHousing, Land and Property activities take on similar pattern of legal services provision through MLA.MLA include 3 periods; (i) awareness raising on HLP rights, procedures, etc, (ii) case intake (HLP assessment) (iii) provision of legal advice and subsequent legal assistance based on the case details.Other activities; support to/training of community-based dispute resolution committee CASH AND VOUCHER ASSISTANCEWill be used as a modality where in-kind is not appropriate and provided unconditional and also conditionally. The cash will be provided through contracted financial service providers and amount provided will be determined by the Cash Working Group Minimum Expenditure Basket for South Sudan. HDC will use the UNHCR provided SOP to carryout cash assistance.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Only a small percentage of the project will be through CVA modality. Though markets are opening, South Sudan remains volatile and annual rainy seasons make over 60% of South Sudan roads impassable as reported through Log Cluster updates.A mix of modalities of both in-kind and CVA is to be used. CVA is also still new and most of the partners and FSPs are still getting acquainted with the procedures.Only areas where markets are functional will cash assistance be appropriate like Juba.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The Joint Market Monitoring Initiative led by REACH reports show more areas are covered and more organizations are joining in the exercise across South Sudan even in the recent flood and subnational violence hit areas of Akobo and Ulang.In the year 2020 there has been a strong lead from the Cash Working Group coordinator to connect humanitarians with Financial Service Providers, many are now seen in regular CWG meeting. This is promoting use of CVA even in remote or hard to reach areas where markets are functional Through experience, we have seen persons of concern sell items to buy what they need most. Provision of cash will provide that choice coupled with dignity. It is also important that CVA is used in areas where markets are functional like Juba to promote the local struggling economy.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Community outreach volunteers will do routine household visits to reach out to the most vulnerable in order to ensure their views and feedback are also incorporated into the project designFGDs and KIIs will be conducted with these special groups to also ensure their views impact on the project design and implementation Feedback will be provided to them through home visits to persons with disabilities and older persons as they are unable to access most of the locations.

HDC will conduct project introduction meeting with local authorities, community leaders and other existing structures that are relevant in the communityInformation on the project will continue to be disseminated during awareness raising activities and training sessionsCommunity Outreach volunteers recruited from the community will be an important part of passing information. They will receive continuous orientation and have regular meetings with the staff on the progress of the project to timely address challenges

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project is kept flexible for issues or feedback from the community that might dictate changes in the implementation or design of the project.Feedback/complaints/challenges from the community will be channeled through to community leaders during meetings and partners during coordination meetings or through monthly reports to the donor.Mid year project revisions will be used to revise the project proposal/design in order to reflect the changes in the project.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Risk mitigation measures1. Risks of women, girls and boys without and with disabilities exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistanceTraining of staff on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse including its serious consequences for any staff involved. All staff required to sign code of conduct when signing their contracts. The staff will also receive training on case management and psychosocial support services to ensure that such cases are handled professionally. The beneficiaries will also be provided with hotline or focal person to report confidential cases.Protection monitoring activities will seek to root out such issues through interviews with affected persons including persons with disabilities that are most prone to cases of abuse. Appropriate measures will be taken against perpetrators including discontinuation of staff members involved.Continuous protection monitoring through community volunteers and staff to identify protection risks and recommend mitigation measures.Community volunteers, leaders and all will be sensitized on the need to protect their most vulnerable against SEA and that assistance provided by humanitarians is free of charge and no staff should ask for sexual favours or the likes.Conduct safety audits before distributions of any nature to identify safe locations and means/measures for distributionsInform community of available confidential reporting channels including GBV hotlines 2.
