4 - Needs

The most vulnerable children in SYRIA,200 CHILD AND GIRL(2022/2023) particularly returnees children, continued to be at risk due to the interruption of essential services like education, child protection and social protection. The COVID-19 pandemic had a particularly marked effect on school enrolment, attendance and retention, making it harder for vulnerable children in north western Syria, especially returnees and host , to continue their education. More than 10000school-aged IDPs children are still out-of-school and do not have any access to education opportunities. Moreover, the effects of prolonged social distancing and confinement measures resulted in increased unemployment and fewer livelihood opportunities for vulnerable families, which will make it more difficult for them to meet their basic needs and could likely to lead to increased negative coping strategies, such as child labor and early marriage. There is also an increased risk of gender-based violence and violence against children, with higher levels of stress within households and many women and children potentially confined at home with their abusers for long periods of time. In Particular orphans suffer double trauma, missing one or both parents on the one hand, and the psychological and social consequences related to being IDPs in a city that have many cultural differences and multiple barriers including traditions. During the preparation of action, (the needs from cluster in turkey sources)
