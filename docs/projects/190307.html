Project Summary

Woredas in Zone 5 of Afar Region were heavily affected by the conflict that expanded its coverage from North Wollo and pushed along the way to Dessie and Kombolcha to South Wollo in Amhara Region from October to November 2021. The conflict expanded further and affected areas in North Shoa Zone and woredas of Zone 5 in Afar that are along the border with Amhara Region . During the conflict, woredas in Zone 5 were hosting IDPs from Amhara Region until they became war zones which further pushed the IDPs downward until Debre Berhan. Devastations that the conflict caused in Zone 5 including Hadele’ela Woreda were immense. The woredas in the zone are now free from the conflict, however, the community is bearing the consequences of it till to date. The internally displaced persons (IDPs) face critical needs that include education and psychosocial support, livelihoods, food security, health and nutrition, and emergency WASH needs. Organizations such as Friendship Support Association (FSA), APDA, Islamic Relief, OSD, and AISDA are addressing the livelihoods, food security, health and nutrition, and emergency WASH needs of the displaced communities, but nothing has been done to provide education and psychosocial support. To address this critical need to provide emergency and non-formal education as well as psychosocial support for the school-aged children (7-17) who lost their parents due to the war. From October to December 2021, most students have been displaced to neighbouring woredas along with their parents, although there is no access to education. Following the Inter-agency Network for Education in Emergencies (INEE) Minimum Standards on Education in Emergencies (EiE), Chronic Crisis and Early Reconstruction, FSA will work to address these gaps by engaging in the highlighted activities for school-aged children (7-17).
Hadele’ela Woreda of Zone 5 in Afar Region is one of the most affected woredas that has experienced a severe humanitarian crisis and facing all types of insecurities: education, food, health, sheter, WASH, etc due to the northern conflict. The conflict led to widespread crises against the civilian population and large-scale displacements. There were mass killings, group rapings, abuses, tortures and family disbanding due to the conflict. There were incidences where a single woman lost her hasband, sons, father and other close relatives. This led the community to heavy trauma and desperate conditions at large.
According to the joint assessment led by FSA and Hadele’ela Woreda Administration, an estimated 362 children have been left without their parents and are in need of emergency education. In addition, 65 people were killed and 165 were wounded particularly in the target woreda due to the current conflict that took place. The assessment showed high need for learning centres, learning materials and school facilities to resume education in the next academic year. According to the Education Sector Multi-Agency Assessment Report, Hadele’ela is one of the woredas that was severely affected by the conflict. Damages to the education facilities were very huge. For example, learning classes of Undaledaba Primary School were totally damaged. Moreover, equipment such as photocopy, sound system, Plasma TV and TV with receivers were taken by TPLF forces while windows and doors of the classes were broken.
It is obvious that school is a best place to take children to help them interact with their peers and rehabilitate from traumas caused during such crises. However, school infrastructure were totally damaged and resources were looted. There is huge need for rehabilitation of school structure, latrine, library and furnishing with the equipment required to accommodate orphan children. These orphan children lost adult care, there is no support provided to them neither from the humanitarian sector nor from the local administration due to scarcity of resources.

1. - Projects activities

Major activities will include:• Rehabilitation of 7 learning classrooms, 1 library and 1 latrine. The latrine will have 4 toilets: 2 for boys on one side of the building and 2 for the girls on the other side. Construction of 2 blocks school and 1 latrine• Provision of stationery materials for students (Exercise books, pens, pencils, rubber, mathematical box) 362 students will receive the supports: each receiving a dozen of exercise books, pens, pencils, 3 rubber, 1 mathematical box and 1 school bag. • Purchase and distribute supplementary books for students of different grade levels. 300 supplementary books will be purchased and kept at the library to promote children’s reading habbit and improve their skills of using reference books. Text books will be provided by the woreda education office.• Purchase of school supplies and equipment This includes 112 combined desks (16 per class); 7 blackboards (1 per class); 17 tables (10 for library and 7 for classrooms – 1 per class); 47 chairs (40 for library and 7 for classrooms – 1 per class); 2 book shelves for library• Purchase of recreational materials to ensure children’s access to recreational activities (in-door and out-door games). This includes indoor play materials such as games, puzzles, drawing supports, etc and outdoor play materials such as footballs, handballs, basketballs, nets, mattresses, wistles, etc• First aid box with training to teachers to school to administer emergency medical care to school children and teachers such as bandages, alcohol, plasters, scissors, gloves, etc• Provision of reusable sanitary pad and underwear for 60 adolescent girls and each girl will receive 4 underwear and 4 reusable sanitary pads for the project period (i.e. 1 of each type per quarter, however, it will be good to give at once and they can use them based on their convenience)• Provision of Hygiene & sanitation materials for 362 students. 1 Body soap will be provided to each student every month for 10 months and 1 laundry soap will be provided to each student every 2 months for 10 months. • Train teachers and staffs on teaching methodologies trauma healing, gender issues etc. 7 teachers, 1 school director and 3 project staffs will receive the training for 6 days. • Work with the woreda education office for allocation of teachers and their salaries; provision of text books and conduct back to school campaigns. • Lobby woreda education office and woreda administration to cover running costs of the schools. This includes costs of chalks, pens for teachers, stationery materials, dusters, maintenance, utilities, salary of cleaners and security guards, etc.• Train on peace and reconciliation for 100 participants in 2 sessions on revitalizing customary forgiveness and confilict resolution mechanisms, on the value of peace and peaceful co-existence by outsourcing experts in the field.• Train most vulnerable victims of conflict on trauma healing for 400 vulnerable people in 8 seccions (50 participants/session for 2 days each) and provide psychosocial supports through counseling by referring to religious fathers and community elders.• Mobilize community for active participation in assessment, planning, implementation and evaluation of the education program• Mobilize community resources to support implementation of the education program and back to school campaigns• Child protection coordination meeting and monitoring every month with represntatives from key sectors such as woreda administration, education office, local police, local judiciary, community and beneficiary representatives (10 persons)• Facilitate Project handover and inauguration program (50 participants from woreda administration, education office, community leaders/representatives, beneficiary representatives and FSA staffs.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Beneficiary representatives will be involved from the beginning: in identification of beneficiary needs, planning, beneficiary selection and listing and follow up for proper utilization of the allocated resources. First, beneficiaries are requested to list down all the needs they have in a focused group discussions. Then, they are requested to put the listed needs in priorities. Based on the ranked priorities, FSA will go for the top need(s) based on the availability of financial resources. There is also a mechanism for beneficiary complaint handling mechanisms that allows them to present their complaints to the committee in charge of selection and distribution scholastic and other materials for the children. They can also report directly to the organization’s focal person (community facilitator) in the field. Those complaints will be analyzed and corrective measures will be taken jointly with local authorities. Complaints can be presented to the attention of responsible bodies through one on one conversation (Dagu). When required by the vulnerability of the community members, FSA communicates with the people on a home basis.
