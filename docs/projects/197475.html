Project Summary

The main objective of the project is to contribute to improving access to education by supporting two schools in Aleppo Governorate in Atarib sub-district.
Atarib sub-district is considered one of the area most in need and classified as No. 5 with severity including 179462 PIN.
The project will target two schools
- Banat Alatereb primary school, includes 33 teachers, and 603 students.
- Maret Alaterb Alsharkiye primary school, includes 19 teachers, and 467 students.
The intervention will include the following activities:
A. Rehabilitation: through repair and maintenance of classrooms such as doors, windows, desks, wall paintings, sewage system, etc.
B. Support: provision of teaching and learning materials and furniture (chairs, desks, whiteboards, cabinets, tables, fuel for heating) as well as providing students with development toolkits in early childhood.
C. Provide: incentives for 58 teachers and educational staff of up to $150 per month for 9 consecutive months.
D. Awareness sessions: about Cholera and personal-clearness preventive measures.
This project will support 1122 beneficiaries affected by the Syrian crisis (27 men, 25 women, 250 boys, and 820 girls including 4 PWDs).
These activities are directly linked to the Education Sector Objectives of the Humanitarian Response Plan which promote and increase access to quality education for boys and girls of all ages between the ages of 6 and 11 who should benefit from primary and secondary education. This support will improve the educational environment for obtaining and benefiting from educational services.
The project will achieve the outcome: Increasing efforts for education services by facilitating access to two schools in Atareb district. This outcome is in line with the EDU objectives 1,2,3 “Increased safe and equitable availability and access to in-person and home-based formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth”, “Enhanced quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth” and “Education systems and communities are better able to support a timely, coordinated and evidence-based education response”.
All the people in the targeted villages and markets will benefit directly/indirectly from the project outcome.
The protection cluster recommendations will be taken into consideration, wherein all the population in the targeted locations will benefit from MASRRAT protection services in Aleppo.
This proposal has not been funded yet.

3 - Needs

Based on HNO 2023 and according to UNICEF, the education system has been devastated by the conflict. Over 7,000 schools have been damaged or destroyed, limiting the ability of children to access accredited educational services, and leaving over two million children out of school and another 1.6 million children at risk of dropping out. As well based on HNO 2023, Atareb area is considered a high-severity area. MASRRAT team conducted assessment in Atareb to figure out the needs and the results show existing of:- a Lack of learning materials 16% in the schools , the roads to school is not safe 10%, a limited number of teachers 3%, challenges regarding transportation 6%, Limited capacity to enroll new children in school 1%, Poor quality of teaching 3%, Lack of sanitation, adequate WASH facilities in school 1%, Children and teachers are psychologically distressed 3%, Early Marriage 3%, Lack of access for disabled students 2%, Lack of certification and recognition of curricula.- Regarding the KIIs, the percentage of IDP children/youths enrolled in formal education in 2022-2023 school year the results were as follows: 5 – 11 years 80%, 12 – 14 years 67%, and 15 – 17 years 58 % of the total children.- Regarding the KIIs, there are a huge need to support the schools with rehabilitation and fuel of heating especially before the winter season.- Most the interviews with school managers reported about the need to support the schools with equipment.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

This intervention aims to present the most important protection risks and possible mitigation measures for education programs in Syria. Referring to the cluster protection risk analysis matrix, the pillars can be built from:1- Violence, intimidation, fear, etc. when students, teachers and/or other education personnel travel, corporal punishment or bullying in schools.2- Students are not adequately protected.3- Children are excessively exposed to risks or harm from teachers, school staff, organization staff or related entities.4- sexual Exploitation and Abuse by relief workers or associated persons use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should serve. Accordingly, MASRRAT will take these risks into account in order to overcome their potential and mitigate them. MASRRAT will provide awareness sessions for the stockholders regarding potential risks and conduct awareness sessions regarding child protection, sexual abuse, bullying, and sexual exploitation.MASRRAT team will be trained on the protection risk and how to mitigate the related risks.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

MASRRAT will recognize the risks faced by beneficiaries, and the difficulties they face in reporting harm they experience by the organization will not tolerate our staff, partners or associates to engage in or support any type of sexual exploitation and abuse or child abuse. The principle of ‘best interests’ will guide our actions in response to concerns and breaches of this policy. MASRRAT understands that, in order for the PSEA/Child Safeguarding policy to be well understood and effectively implemented, it is essential that there are high levels of awareness regarding the policy and that staff and others are clear, confident and competent in putting the policy into practice. Disseminate and promote the policy widely. Copies of the policy will be made available and accessible to all stakeholders, both in hard copy and electronically in order to ensure that the policy commitments, principles, and practices are publicized and that everyone is clear about MASRRAT position in relation to PSEA and safeguarding children. MASRRAT mobile teams, will encourage the beneficiaries to claim their rights and will follow up the issues with the project management in Turkey. Furthermore, the affected population will have access to the intervention services equally as appropriate with the need and without any discrimination. People with disabilities, women, and elders have been accessed during the needs assessment process and involved in the discussions in the community of intervention.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption:- Access remains to target locations and targeted schools.- security situation is stable in targeted locations.- The relationship between the displaced and the host community is good and stable.- The school condition is good- The schools are providing educational services to IDPs and LCs. Risk and mitigation:MASRRAT team will take into consideration all precautions according to the following Potential risks: 1- Interference in schools’ selection processes,Mitigation: at the beginning of the project, the team will conduct the community mobilization process and will explain the intervention and mechanism of selection of the schools according to the needs.2- High prices of materials and changes in exchange rates, Mitigation: freeze the prices of the contract with the vendors.3- lack of some materials and tools. Mitigation: Import from other regions inside Syria or from Turkey4- Deterioration of the security situation in the project’s areas. Mitigation: Postpone the implementation process until the stabilization of the security situation.5- Sexual exploitation and abuse to the beneficiaries/staff:Mitigation Measure: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, train staff on the code of conduct and code of conduct in all staff contracts Address any SEA complaints.6- Transition of Cholera infection to a community member, if any.Mitigation Measure(s): MASRRAT will follow up on the recommendation and measures of WHO and clusters on dealing with it.
