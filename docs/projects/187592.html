Project Summary

Bonyan Organization designed its 2021 ERL intervention based on the analysis of MSNA data in NW Syrian, need severity for ERL assistance, and key messages of HNO 2022, as well as, in line with SOs of HRP and ERL Cluster. The project aims to strengthen access to livelihoods, improve access to basic services and infrastructure, and support efforts of local economy-recovery in addition to support social cohesion for the affected-population, for 93,000 INDVs (40,920 M/ 52,080 F - 75% are children and youth) in Aleppo and Idleb.
The project activities fall into two main categories as follows:
** Support the maintenance & improve equitable access to basic services targeting 65,000 INDVs through:
• rehabilitation of basic electricity networks by installation of 100 solar system and 10 km of electrical network,
• promote / support the use of renewable energy by providing 1,000 solar system units,
• materials / equipment support to ensure the provision of public services for 12,500 INDVs, and
• rehabilitation of 10 Schools, 20 km of roads, 10 km of sewage networks, and 2 water stations for 42,000 INDVs.
** Increase access to Livelihood and job opportunities targeting 28,000 INDVs through:
• Financial support, including grants, seed funds, and subsidized loans to businesses with high potential for job retention and creation for 100 social or business entrepreneurship initiative and 5 support grants
• Provide vocational and skills training opportunities for 1,000 trainees
• Create long and short-term work opportunities for 450 INDVs
• Rehabilitation of 20 basic productive infrastructures for 20,000 INDVs
• Unemployment Youth capacity building by provision of soft skills training and enrolling 25 youths in apprenticeships.
BO will select the target communities based on scoring and prioritizing system in coordination with ERL Cluster, then will engage the selected community in all phases of the project. Also, BO will ensure that the support will be provided to the most vulnerable people through transparent process based on clear vulnerability criteria (HLP veriﬁcation will be included). Principles of Do No Harm and AAP as well as humanitarian standards are incorporated in the project design and will be ensured during the project timeline.
for CVA:BO will coordinate with all relevant stakeholders in the project area including the coordination clusters, and local councils. The coordination efforts will focus on filling the gaps in need as well as on prevention of any possible duplication. this component will help the youth from both IDPs, and HCs to be active and provide good example of the society that increase the acceptance of all community segments for each other and help in reduce tension and increase the overall social cohesion
for CfW: BO will work under “Cash for Work Minimum Recommended Actions Guidance for NWS developed by CWG and ERL partners” and will work in a close consultation with the communities regarding the wages rate, and other consideration to ensure no harm made to the labor market and to ensure that the intervention is meeting the need of the communities
MEAL team of BO will conduct ongoing monitoring of project activities to verify achievement of indicators and to correct any deviation.
The PwD will be involved in all activities as direct beneficiaries in CfW or rehabilitation of infrastructures … etc. Workers with disabilities will be chosen so that they have a mild / moderate degree of disability, so that they can complete tasks that do not require much muscular effort. For example, these people can conduct periodic cleaning campaigns, wiring works for the lighting systems, data collection survey, or sensitization sessions for the community members. On the other hand, PwDs will benefit directly from the improved services including sewage and drainage systems, solar lighting, and rehabilitation of roads.

4 - Needs

HNO2022 indicates that poor economic conditions, physical destruction of infrastructure, and large-scale displacement have led to competition over scarce livelihood opportunities and overburdening available basic/social services. Electricity assistance is considered a priority for 16,4% in NWS and is considered a major problem highlighted by the key informant interview assessmentDeterioration of the HH’s ability to meet basic needs is observed across all groups, 73% of IDPs, 73% of residents, 74% of returnees. 27% of the PiN are PwD, of which 75% are without access to medical social and economic support. The assessment showed that PwDs are vulnerable to exclusion from the humanitarian response58% in NWS had a job in the last 3 months, 90% of them are the head of HH. the top need for 71% of HHs remain food and 56% livelihoods. This evidence is even more disquieting if we consider that 38% of HHs more than one member is employed. Furthermore, in NWS, 50% of respondents noted that they cannot afford essential items, pointing to the instability and constant fluctuation of prices. An additional cost of 15% would imply that HHs would theoretically remove essential items from their consumption. inflation has increased more than the income, in 2019 the average income/expenses was 95K/96K SYP, in 2020 was 147K/177K SYP (20% gap), and for 2021 was 301K/449K SYP (49% gap) the sector foresees this gap to continue increasing in the coming years

5 - Implementation of activities

Bonyan Organization will coordinate with relevant stakeholders to conduct MSNA to collect most recent data about ERL needs of IDPs, HCs and RTRNs (with keeping in mind the specific needs of different age and gender groups), in addition to collect any other necessary dataBO will assess the market system’s functionality and capacity to absorb increased demand for goods and services, and potential impact on markets to apply the "DO NO HARM" aspectsThen, BO will select beneficiaries following transparent processes based on clear vulnerability/ eligibility criteria to ensure provision of support to the most vulnerable. Along with the implementation, MEAL team will carry out ongoing M&E activities to verify project indicators. 11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Targeted locations are secure, safe and accessible during implementing the project.- In asset support activities, beneficiaries will not sell assets and they will use it to improve their livelihoods. Risk and Mitigation:R1: Escalation of the security situation.Likelihood: M/Impact: HMitigation:1. Ensure there is a process established that allows to consult the communities on how to proceed with the proposed response in a deteriorating security environment2. Targeted locations are included in de-escalation agreements. In case of unexpected military actions, possible solution is to conduct activities in another locations. R2: Exposure to the risk of theft or kidnapping after receiving cash or asset assistance.Likelihood: L / Impact: HMitigation:1- Choose key locations to distribute cash / asset that are safe for all beneficiaries, including women and people with disabilities. 2- Not to publish the information and dates of receipt in the community to people other than the beneficiaries. R3: Selling asset after receiving it by the beneficiaries and not using it to generate income in small enterprises.Likelihood: L / Impact: HMitigation:1- Evaluate the beneficiary's situation and ensure his ability to work with the provided equipment.2- Signing contracts with the beneficiaries in the presence of a committee from the local community / local authority to ensure that the asset will not be sold and will be used to generate income only.
