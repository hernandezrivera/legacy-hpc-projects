Project Summary

The 2025 nutrition intervention will scale up and accelerate life-saving programs, enhance service quality, and leverage partnerships for maternal, infant, and young child nutrition in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) States. UNICEF will scale up preventive nutrition programmes and ensure the provision of life saving services (inpatient and outpatient care) that are backed by evidence and robust nutrition information systems.
The programme will prioritize life-saving activities, integrating preventive and curative services in priority locations identified by the 2025 Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (HNRP). Special emphasis will target hard-to-reach areas where nutrition is classified as serious or critical, covering 42 prioritized LGAs across the BAY States as identified by the integrated severity index and Northeast Nutrition Sector boundary setting.
The response will prioritize households with persons with disabilities, acknowledging their heightened vulnerability and barriers to accessing health, nutrition, and WASH services. These individuals often face food insecurity and multiple deprivations due to exclusion. Seasonal food security and nutrition variations will also be addressed, particularly during the lean season and rainy season, which often bring increased cases of diarrhea, malaria, and measles.
Lessons from the 2024 program will shape the 2025 Humanitarian Program Cycle (HPC) Project, ensuring alignment with evolving nutrition needs in Northeast Nigeria.
The 2025 nutrition response in BAY States will follow a context-specific approach based on:
Priority 1 – HNRP LGAs: These areas face worsening acute malnutrition between 2023 and 2024. GAM prevalence is classified as "Very High" in Borno (15.7%), "High" in Yobe (11.9%), and "Medium" in Adamawa (6.6%). Among pregnant and breastfeeding women (PBW), malnutrition rates are alarming with 25.3% in Borno, 15.1% in Adamawa, and 32.0% in Yobe. High-impact preventive nutrition interventions will be scaled up, focusing on hard-to-reach areas classified as serious or critical, covering 42 prioritized LGAs as determined by the integrated severity index and the boundary setting of the Northeast Nutrition Sector.
Priority 2 – Government-Led and Development Partners: In areas with IPC classifications of 2 or below, minimal conflict, and functioning government infrastructure, UNICEF will adopt a Development and Humanitarian Nexus approach. This strategy will use government institutions to deliver integrated services addressing WASH, health, and nutrition challenges. Key issues like poor nutrition knowledge, inadequate care practices, women’s workloads, early marriage, and low literacy will be supported. The focus will include nutrition-sensitive interventions and government investment to promote sustainable development.
Extremely Hard-to-Reach Areas: In locations with IPC phases 3 and above, geographic isolation, service disruptions, or limited partner presence, UNICEF will implement a Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) through local partners. These efforts will focus on delivering life-saving nutrition interventions where services and commodities are minimal.

1 - Needs

Based on the North-East Nigeria Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance conducted in August 2024, the global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates in Borno have seen an increase from 10.2% in 2023 to 15.7% in 2024, while Yobe had also experienced an increase from 8.0% in 2023 to 11.9% in 2024. In Adamawa, the GAM rates have increased from 4.0% to 6.6% in 2024. These high prevalences of GAM signify an extremely stressed population grappling with food insecurity, limited water and sanitation access, and suboptimal health conditions as the primary drivers of acute malnutrition. In 2025, it is estimated that over 2.5 million children under five and over 309,000 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) will suffer from acute malnutrition, . To address this, there is a need to significantly strengthen and scale up preventive nutrition services, such as micronutrient supplementation for women and children, the promotion of maternal, infant, and young children feeding practices as well as the provision of life saving services such as the management of severe wasting.

2 - Activities

Proposed Activities to Address Nutritional Challenges: To effectively meet the nutritional needs of children under five and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) in the BAY States, UNICEF proposes a comprehensive set of activities aligned with specific strategic objectives:1. Strategic Objective 1: Strengthen Preventative Nutrition Services• Distribution of Micronutrient Supplements including Vitamin A, SQ-LNS for point-of-use fortification among children aged 6 - 23 months and MMS for pregnant women.• Skilled Maternal, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) counselling for PLW.2. Strategic Objective 2: Improve Access to Quality Curative Nutrition Services• Procurement of nutrition supplies/commodities (RUTF, F75, F100, weighing scales, and medications).• Treatment of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) for children aged 0 - 59 months through Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and stabilization centers (SC).3. Strategic Objective 3: Strengthen Nutrition Coordination and Strengthen Situation Monitoring• Conducting nutrition surveys and joint assessments in collaboration with other sectors such as Food Security, WASH, and Health.• Implementing Cash and voucher Assistance (CVA) for nutrition outcomes.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project will work very closely with the protection section in UNICEF Maiduguri Field Office in assessing, identifying, preventing and responding to protection risks faced by programme beneficiaries. Although no PSEA case was observed or reported in 2024, the 2025 project will ensure active involvement of the protection section to ensure appropriate actions on PSEA. On GBV, the project will increase gender equality among children aged 6–59 months affected by malnutrition. The project will contribute to this through gender mainstreaming and sex disaggregation of data based on gender. Sensitization and awareness of gender-based violence during community engagement and capacity building of service providers will be conducted.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In this project, UNICEF will rely on the activities of trained OTP staff and the monitoring of the government IP staff to ensure quality service delivery is accessible to the targeted communities. Health workers will be engaged through a consultative process by UNICEF third-party facilitators who will identify the needs of women and children (boys and girls) and people with disabilities, all of which were factored into the project design. UNICEF third-party facilitators will monitor the mentoring of health workers and community structures with partners at the LGA level and mechanisms such as complaint boxes and hotlines will be set up for receiving feedback from the target population.
