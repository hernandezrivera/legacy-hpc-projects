Project Summary

The present project aims at fostering a safe and protective environment through the provision of both specialized and community-based services and strengthening an internal and external multi-sectorial referral system. The proposed intervention relies on a twin-track approach, implemented by HI for the past two years, including:
- Accessible multi-purposes safe spaces operated by HI staff and volunteers where community members will access group PSS/Protection sessions through an AGD approach. These activities will aim to reduce social barriers faced by marginalized groups and to enhance existing positive community initiatives. HI currently runs 10 static points in camps 1W, 4, 5, 9, 17, 21, 22, 27, Kutupalong and Nayapara Registered Camps. While maintenance activities will target existing community safe spaces, an additional 5 accessible community safe spaces will be established through maintenance and accessibility of existing facilities in camps 8E, 8W, 14, 15 and 26, through coordination with SMS, CIC and community consultations.
- Multi-sectorial mobile units composed of Social Workers, Inclusive Sport, Psychologists and Protection Officers will identify and provide tailored individual assistance to the most vulnerable individuals and their caregivers who might be excluded from community spaces due to mobility issues, distance or social barriers-including factors such as age, gender, disability.
Aiming at fostering a protective community environment, HI will disseminate key information on rights and major protection risks, while also relying on the findings from disability and gender-sensitive protection monitoring. These findings will inform the humanitarian response, including the working groups which HI is actively part of (such as the MHPSS WG, the PWG TT, the Age and Disability WG). Individual assistance will allow HI to detect and refer at-risk individuals, including women and girls with disabilities. Trained Community Volunteers will conduct awareness sessions and community mobilization on Protection and Inclusion, including the dissemination of radio sketches.
Through its structured PSS intervention relying on the first three layers of the MHPSS IASC guidelines, HI provides both individual counseling and group sessions. Based on individual participative care plans, HI focuses on empowering affected individuals, including persons with disabilities and their caregivers, to cope with emotional reactions including anxiety, negative feelings- including those related to complex caregiving roles- social isolation and stigma. Group sessions, life-skills, inclusive recreational activities facilitated by community Para-Counsellors and Inclusive Sport Officers, will help participants to develop common strategies to respond to stress and violence.
HI also proposes to strengthen partners’ capacities on Disability Inclusive Protection Programming, including on safe identification of persons with disabilities, informed consent, the strengths-based communication approach and support to caregivers. Through action planning for inclusion mainstreaming and provision of recommendations, HI will ensure the application of the AGD approach.
Participation and empowerment of communities will be ensured through supporting the development of self-protection capacities, including through the training of Community Volunteers. HI will also continue to provide support to humanitarian actors in consulting populations at risk of exclusion, to encourage their full and equal participation. HI will continue building the capacity of SHED, who has been HI local partner for 4 years.
HI is part of the PERU in several camps, therefore participating in the development of an effective early action system to any sudden-onset disaster. HI also relies on multi-sectoral mobile teams in the camps and host communities- therefore ensuring that at-risk individuals (especially persons with disabilities) benefit from a timely, adequate and need-based assistance

Needs

In 2017, 721,641 Rohingya refugees fleeing violence and persecutions in Myanmar have sought protection in Cox’s Bazar district, triggering the largest and fastest refugee influx into Bangladesh. Two years later, around one million refugees lives in extremely precarious conditions in 34 camps in Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazilas. Psychosocial distress resulting from persecutions and forced displacements is exacerbated by dire living conditions. Inadequate food, lack of employment, poor shelter and WASH facilities are identified by both men and women to be among the main causes of distress (HI MHPSS Field Assessment, released in May 2019). In parallel, while a significant portion of refugees are living with host communities, the existing burden of unemployment, market prices, limited environmental resources and public services have been exacerbated by the resulting increase in population. As a result, 47% of host community household report being unhappy with the presence of refugees in their communities. While needs of host communities remain unaddressed, 39% of households report the presence of children showing symptoms of distress (MSNA March 2019). Risks of exploitation, trafficking, kidnapping, forced/child marriage are predominant across both refugee and host populations. 61% of host community households report not being aware of community-level protection mechanisms (MSNA- March 2019). In parallel, 6% of host community households report being concerned by child marriage, along with 5% reporting child labor (JMSNA, Oct 2019). Vulnerability factors for persons with disabilities to violence, abuse and neglect are exacerbated due to social, communication and environmental barriers. Perceptions about their capacities to report violence and defend themselves, along with displacement-related family stress and consecutive loss of community protection mechanisms expose them to further risks. Where households have not yet rebuilt their support systems, some families resort to tying up or locking their relatives, fearing they experience community violence. Through joint FGDs conducted by HI and UNFPA-Mukti in Sept 2019 with women and girls with physical disabilities and their caregivers (Camp 9), the lack of access to basic, gender and age specific services and information were identified as the main protection risks faced. In Jadimura camp, 78% of persons with disabilities report facing a lot of difficulty or having no access to community spaces (HI, Jadimura camp Assessment, January 2019). While 28% of host community households report the presence of at least one individual with a disability or chronic illness, of these individuals, only 40% mention receiving services to meet their specific needs (MSNA March 2019/ yet without use of the WGQS to measure disability). Persons with life-long disabling conditions and their caregivers are particularly subject to dependency, decreased income, anxiety. In Jadimura camp, 0% of working age persons with disabilities and their household members reported participating in any economic activity (HI- Jan 2019) while, 44% of adult men and 4% of adult women living in Jadimura camp reportedly engaged in an economic activity (MSNA July 2018).While disability-disaggregated data is rarely mainstreamed through the humanitarian response, persons with disabilities remain absent from community consultations and decision-making, further impeding understanding of their needs and tailored interventions. In Jadimura camp, 0% of persons with disabilities respondents report participation in decision making committees (HI, Jan 2019). Such exclusion worsens the long-term impact of the crisis and their access to services essential to their protection and recovery. Information is similarly rarely conveyed in an inclusive way, limiting persons with sensorial and intellectual impairments’ access to assistance and personal safety, especially women and girls, who face subordinate position in society and greater stigma.
