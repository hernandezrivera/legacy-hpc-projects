Project Summary

UNOPS proposes a project to support the WASH sector through an integrated approach focused on rehabilitation and operational support to WASH facilities for public schools and healthcare facilities. The project will target some of the locations with the highest needs (severity 3/4/5 level) in Aleppo and Homs. The proposed interventions have been identified based on the UNOPS technical needs assessment of WASH facilities in several schools and PHCs in Aleppo, and the ongoing projects in Homs and Hama.
Within the WASH sector, UNOPS has extensive experience in the region. For example, in Jordan, UNOPS has been implementing a project to rehabilitate and upgrade about 250 km of water pipelines to improve access to quality water services in the host community of Syrian refugees. Moreover, UNOPS is currently undertaking a project in coordination with the Ministry of Environment (MoE) in Lebanon to improve solid waste management in selected serviced zones, including recycling and composting at the municipal level, and reduce Unintentional Persistent Organic Pollutants (UPOPs) and harmful emissions from open burning of solid waste. In Iraq, UNOPS has implemented the restoration of the water supply system (drilling of new wells, installation of steel tanks for water distribution, provision of solar energy systems and extended pipeline to transmit/distribute water) in rural areas of Sinjar Governorate.
Additionally, UNOPS has implemented infrastructure and procurement projects, aimed to enhance the quality of services and conditions in public schools and health facilities in Syria. UNOPS is implementing infrastructure projects focusing on rehabilitation works, supply and works of solar photovoltaic (PV) hybrid systems and provision of a medical waste management system to the Kidney Building, one of the Homs Grand Hospital buildings, and provision of renewable energy solutions in 10 PHCs and public schools in Hama.
Building on its vast experience in infrastructure, maintenance and procurement projects in Syria, the region and globally, UNOPS’ proposed plan will contribute to the WASH Sector Objective 1: Save lives and reduce Protection risks among displaced population and host communities by enhancing gender-friendly and disability-adopted WASH facilities in schools and health facilities. Given the above, UNOPS proposes to contribute to the following activities in 2024-2025. Activity 1.6 Improve WASH facilities and services within schools and child-friendly spaces, in close coordination with the Education and Protection sectors. Activity 1.7. Improve WASH facilities and services in health care facilities used by IDPs and host population, in close coordination with the Health sector.
Protection risks have been identified and mitigation measures have been developed. The main risk would be related to potential exposure to violence and abuse to the beneficiaries, particularly for women, students (girls and boys) and vulnerable populations during the implementation, when the UN staff and contractors commence the work on-site. The mitigation action would be to conduct PSEAH awareness training to the project team/contractors, and design the work plan and schedule to avoid or reduce contacts with the beneficiary. UNOPS will also put grievance mechanisms in place, including hotline, complain box in the project sites, and take remedial actions. Additionally, there is a risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/after installation) by armed groups and risk of the stolen materials to be used in conflict activities. To mitigate this and in order to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated facilities, Memorandum of Understandings (MoUs) will be agreed and signed with local authorities/councils. Moreover, regular field monitoring, consultations with communities and reporting systems will be in place to mitigate all protection risks.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In order to mitigate PSEAH risks, UNOPS plans to conduct gender-responsive needs assessments with female and male beneficiaries, including marginalized groups in order to identify their specific needs as well as perceived SEAH risks and reflect these in the project design works and implementation of rehabilitation. During the implementation of works, UNOPS will take measures to protect the security and safety of the beneficiaries, with particular attention to SEAH risk management through robust PSEAH training programmes to the project team and the selected contractors and strict adherence to UNOPS’ environmental/social safeguards measures that include PSEAH safeguards. Given the focus of the project on marginalized populations including women, children and PWD, it is crucial to include PSEAH measures in the project. PSEAH is particularly important in projects with high degrees of direct contact between humanitarian actors (including contractors implementing on behalf of the UN) and local community members (beneficiaries) as in the case of the proposed project. According to the Project Risk Analysis, the identified risks are as follows: 1) Fraudulent activities occur causing UNOPS operations to be suspended. 2) Delays in design works (if original drawings of the building structure are not available/provided to UNOPS). 3) Delays in receiving the necessary permits/approval from the relevant authorities. 4) Lack of awareness of contractors on health and safety measures at the project site, which may trigger incidents on site. 5) Deteriorating security situation and kinetic environment that experience frequent incidents which might hinder access to project sites. UNOPS takes mitigation actions against these risks, which are 1) Implementing a robust internal control framework with oversight mechanisms to mitigate the risk of fraudulent activities. Regular monitoring, quality assurance activities, reporting systems in place. Vendor due diligence and performance monitoring in place. 2) Coordination with the relevant authorities for their continuous support to provide the required technical information. 3) Active stakeholder management through Project Board for agreement/endorsement. 4) Mandatory HSSE training and inclusion of HSSE requirements in the tendering documents, and the selected contractors will be guided by the UNOPS team. Any incidents on site will be treated as per UNOPS HSSE procedures. 5) Close monitoring of the situation and coordination with UNDSS and the relevant stakeholders, and operational readiness and position staff to support alternate activities, as required in accordance with needs with Business Continuity Plans.
