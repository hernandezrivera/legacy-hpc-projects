Project Summary

This project aim to improve Water, Sanitation and Hygiene services in affected communities and to support cluster/sector coordination in affected region. Concerning the improvement of WASH services, the project plan to support:
- Training of NGO and Government on Rehabilitation protocole of water point
- Rehabilitate/transform at least 30 boreholes with handpump to Solar powered system based on joint diagnosis conducted by Government and NGOs
- Construct water point (40 small water supply system connected to a water source (catchment, borehole) ;
- concerning sanitation, focus will be put on supporting community approach for the construction of household latrines .
- The construction of WASH facilities will include innovative approaches and new technologies on sanitation and water provision appropriate to insular area
-Provision of WASH items (Including MHM for adolescent girls and women) to households, schools and health center
At least 150 000 people (IDP, Host community and Returnees) from NW, SW, Littoral (Moungo and Wouri) and West region (Menoua, Noun and Bamboutos) in priority are expected to directly benefit of the project
for Coordination, a support will be made on :
- Training of cluster/sector member on coordination and WASH program issues (Assessment, latrine construction, AAP, Gender, ..)
- Support two positions (Cluster coordinator and IMO)

1 - Besoins / Needs

The displacement of population due to insecurity, the insufficient investment of the Cameroonian Government in basic social services and the insufficient response provided by humanitarian actors in the North-West and South-West regions continue to exacerbate the water and sanitation needs of the affected population including in the neighboring regions (Littoral and West regions). The last MSNA conducted reveals that 55 per cent of assessed villages collect water from an unimproved water point. In some division of the North-West (Menchum and Ngo-Ketunjia) and South-West region (Ndian and Manyu), at least 75 per cent of assessed localities collect water from an unimproved water point. The three main challenges relative to water are the insufficient number of improved water points, the long distance covered to and from water point(due to insufficient number of safe drinking water points) and the lack of containers to collect and store water. In relation to sanitation, although open defecation is low (2.4 per cent of assessed villages), utilization of pit latrines without slab is predominant (78.8 per cent) among assessed villages. This represents a high risk of contamination of surface and groundwater used by the population in the concerned localities. The Littoral and West regions remain the regions hosting most of the displaced population from the South-West and North-West regions. The main needs are related to access to improved water points (35 per cent of assessed villages hosting IDPs still do not have access) and basic sanitation services. 52 per cent defecate in open holes and 45 per cent also use open holes without slabs. Thus, the difficulties reported by the affected population are unhygienic latrines (because of crowding of users) and the insufficient number of latrines. It should noted also that the region have been hit by a cholera outbreak. on 15 december 2022 a total of 15018 cases and 298 deaths have been reported with 40% of cases and 31% of death from South West. The littoral was the most affected with 49% of cases and 53% of deaths reported national wide. The main cause of this situation was the poor access to safe drinking water, inadequate sanitation practices and poor respect of good hygiene practices among population. Thus a need as per the strategy to invest in infrastructure to adress those root cause and contribute to behavior change among affected communities.As noted during MSNA, access to improved water point is a source of GBV in affected communities. The project will also implement intervention taking it into account. Cross sectorial issues such as AAP, gender and protection will need to be strengthen along the interventions. The WASH cluster is still activated with about 53 member and observers. Majority of them are new with a need to build their capacity on WASH response and coordination thematic.
