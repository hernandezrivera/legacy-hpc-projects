Project Summary

The overarching objective is to scale up SRH integrated services in newly accessible Local Government Areas and Rebuilding and strengthening health systems Capacity, to provide sustainable SRH Services and Medical Care to Survivors of Sexual Violence in Line with the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for Reproductive Health Services and the Nigeria Health Policy.
Objectives:
• Increase access to and utilization of family planning commodities including long acting reversible contraceptives for women of reproductive age;
• Increase access to and utilization of other SRH services such as antenatal and postnatal care, skilled delivery services including emergency obstetric care, post abortion care, clinical management of rape, HIV and Syndromic STI screening and management
• Improve capacity of health facilities to provide quality maternal and newborn care services such as antenatal care, emergency obstetric care, postnatal and post abortion care services.
• Improve referral networks to emergency obstetric care and survivors of sexual violence including rape.
• Strengthen referral systems for the provision of basic and comprehensive emergency obstetric care.
• Strengthened community health systems in supporting early recovery and promoting peace building
• Expand access to and utilization of quality SRH services including clinical management of rape and HIV/STIs.

Needs

The 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview identified a total of 5,216,907 people in dire need of life saving essential health services. These include 1, 304,227 women of reproductive age and 1, 043, 382 sexually active men. A total of 156,510 women are currently pregnant and the same number is expected to get pregnant within a period of one year; this will result in 208,677 live births with 23,477 facing complications and requiring emergency obstetric care services. A total of 31,302 of the pregnancies will end in abortion and would require post abortion care (PAC) services. Furthermore, 26,085 women of reproductive age are at risk of sexual violence and would require clinical management of rape services. There is an estimated 3,926,353 young people including adolescents in need of sexual and reproductive health services. According to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2019, less than one primary health care (PHC) facility per 10,000 inhabitants is functional. 70% of 751 health facilities in Borno State, 55% of 1,120 health facilities in Adamawa State, and 31% of 593 health facilities in Yobe states are partially or not-functioning, especially in remote/rural areas. There are insufficient numbers of skilled and appropriately trained health care staff; health workers are also reluctant to work in remote and newly liberated local government areas (LGAs). Poor referral services further limits access to life-saving emergency maternal and newborn healthcare services. Most of the IDP camps are located in urban areas and all clients are expected to pay-out-of pocket in order to access healthcare. As a result, pregnant women would not have access to antenatal care, delivery care, emergency obstetric care, and postnatal care. The project will be implemented in 44 of the 65 local government areas of Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. The project would primarily target women of reproductive age, sexually active men and young adolescents. The main strategic approach will be the minimum initial service package for reproductive health in emergencies (MISP) services. The key components include prevention and managing consequences of sexual violence, prevention and treatment of HIV and other sexually transmitted infections, preventing maternal and newborn death and illness, and strengthening of the health systems capacity for comprehensive sexual and reproductive health care. The Project activities would include: training of health workers on provision of MISP services; procurement and distribution of life saving RH kits; procurement of essential maternal health medicines and equipment; training of health workers on provision of quality family planning, antenatal care, obstetric care, postnatal including emergency care to mothers and newborn; deployment of health workers on medical outreaches in remote/hard-to-reach communities and where existing health facilities are not functioning; establish and strengthen referral networks for maternal and newborn emergencies; provision of adolescent sexual reproductive health services through safe spaces; and mobilization of communities for uptake of SRH services.
