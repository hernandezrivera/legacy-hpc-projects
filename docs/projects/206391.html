Project Summary

South Sudan faces a critical humanitarian crisis, with conflict, floods, and natural disasters displacing thousands of people. These displaced individuals are in urgent need of shelter and non-food items (NFIs) to secure their basic needs and improve their living conditions. Upper Nile, Unity, and Jonglei are the most severely affected regions.
IOM will continue to provide comprehensive and timely response to the 238,639 conflict and flood-affected displaced and returnee populations from the SNFI Cluster prioritized locations. This response will include:
Maintaining a mobile team for emergency response: IOM will continue to maintain a dedicated team of over 25 staff members for Shelter and NFI mobile and static response in the country. This team will ensure that they can be swiftly deployed to any location within 72 hours in the event of an emergency, particularly in situations where no other partners are available to respond to S-NFI needs.
Conducting needs assessments: IOM will conduct comprehensive needs assessments to understand the specific needs and vulnerabilities of each displaced group. The need assessment will ensure that assistance is tailored to the specific needs of each community and that potential negative impacts of the response are mitigated.
Distributing shelter and NFIs: IOM will provide a range of shelter and NFI assistance, including in-kind items such as plastic sheeting, ropes, cooking pots, kangas, solar lamps, and blankets. IOM will also provide cash and voucher assistance to enable households to purchase essential items.
Providing durable shelter solutions: In 2024, IOM seeks to implement innovative and sustainable shelter solutions that are culturally appropriate and adapted to the local climate. This will include the use of local materials and techniques, as well as promoting disaster risk reduction (DRR) measures.
Improving conditions in camps and camp-like settings: IOM will work to enhance the living conditions of displaced people residing in camps and camp-like settings. This includes shelter maintenance and shelter repair.
Coordinating with partners: IOM will continue to collaborate closely with other humanitarian organizations to ensure a coordinated and effective response to the crisis. The collaboration will include sharing information, coordinating distributions, and advocating for joint integrated multisectoral responses.
Support to Sout Sudan returnees: IOM will continue to provide shelter, in kind and cash support to returnees affected by the Sudan conflict. This includes constructing transitional shelters for returnees and providing them with in kind support.
Ensuring robust monitoring and evaluation: IOM will ensure robust monitoring and evaluation of its ongoing programs to improve program quality and accountability to affected people. This monitoring will include rapid, post, and continuous remote monitoring using the latest tools. IOM will continue to shares lessons learned and challenges with partners and updates its Activity Info dashboard.
Integrating HLP principles: IOM will continue to integrating HLP principles into its programming, conducting thorough risk assessments, and advocating for joint integrated multisectoral responses. Recognizing the sensitive nature of S-NFI activities, IOM will undertake gender-sensitive, protection-sensitive, and conflict-sensitive risk assessments and analyses prior to any activities to ensure adherence to the Do No Harm principles.
IOM's comprehensive approach to addressing shelter and NFI needs in South Sudan is guided by a commitment to providing timely, equitable, and effective assistance to the country's displaced and returnee populations. Through its strong presence, expertise, and partnerships, IOM will continue to play a crucial role in improving the lives of thousands of people affected by conflict and displacement.

1. Needs

South Sudan is facing a humanitarian crisis with 8.5 million people requiring assistance in 2024. Following are the need analysis for some of IOM’s targeted areasUpper Nile: Upper Nile State bore the brunt of sub-national violence in South Sudan in 2023. The clashes in Fashoda and the Tonga conflict displaced thousands of people, forcing them to seek refuge in Protection of Civilians (PoC) sites and near UNMISS bases. The Malakal PoC, the only one in the country, witnessed a tragic outbreak of violence in June 2023, claiming the lives of over 20 individuals.The prioritization data reveals that 32% of the total targeted people caseload resides in Upper Nile State, making it the second highest among prioritized states in terms of people in need. According to a recent IPC 2023-24 classification, approximately 56.1% of the population in Upper Nile State is facing IPC Phase 3 or above. Renk currently hosts over 50,000 returnees, while IOM has assisted over 120,000 returnees with onward transportation to their final destinations. In 2023, IOM conducted assessments and distributions in Malakal, Renk, Ulang, and Nasir Jonglei: Nearly all of Jonglei’s counties fall under Priority 1 or Priority 2 locations. Jonglei accounts for 19% of the total caseload in Priority 1 locations. IOM conducted SNFI assessments in Fangak, Canal Pigi and Pibor in 2023 and provided assistance in areas such as Fangak, Canal Pigi, and Pibor. Unity: Unity State is one of the most severely affected regions in South Sudan due to natural disasters, particularly floods. This has resulted in the displacement of thousands of people, who are currently residing in makeshift camps like Rotriak and Sites A to E. Unity State hosts the one of the largest camps for IDPs in the country. Furthermore, according to prioritization data, 39% of the total IDP caseload is concentrated in Unity State, making it the highest among prioritized locations. There are critical needs for NFIs in Rubkona county, including Rotriak, Leer, Mankein, and other key areas. While IOM completed SNFI distributions in Rotriak, Bentiu, Mankein, and other locations in 2023, hundreds of families still require SNFI assistance While only 4% of the total caseload in Priority Locations is in Abyei Administrative Area, the situation there remains dire. IOM responded to the recent violence by providing cash, shelter, and NFIs to over 12,000 households, however there are still many families waiting for assistance.Warrap and Lakes: SNFI needs are particularly acute in Cuei-bet and Twic in Lake State. Cuei-bet boasts the highest proportion of host communities among all priority locations requiring SNFI support. Additionally, Warrap State stands out as another high-priority location, receiving a significant influx of Sudanese returnees.Returnees: Over 370,000 individuals have returned to South Sudan, with 90% of them destined for Upper Nile, Unity, Jonglei, and other Equatorian regions. These returnees were permitted to bring with them 23 kilograms of their belongings, necessitating SNFI assistance for every household. While they may not fall under certain prioritized locations, IOM consultations with them have revealed an extremely high need for support, far exceeding the number planned under the HRP. Therefore, providing NFI assistance and HLP support is of paramount importance.In the face of limited resources, many individuals have adopted negative coping mechanisms, relying on low-quality and quantity of food, less resource to cover education, health, shelter, and WASH needs and borrowing from others. This situation leaves them highly dependent on humanitarian aid. Without continued assistance, the situation is likely to worsen. IOM's project aims to enhance the resilience of these communities by providing in-kind and cash support, enabling them to sustain themselves for a period and gradually transition from emergency relief to recovery and rehabilitation.

2. Project activities

Mobile Response: As the Shelter and NFI Cluster lead, IOM will maintain a robust mobile team based in Juba and other key locations to ensure a rapid and effective response to any emergency across the country within 72 hours of the alert. a)Needs Assessment: IOM will conduct comprehensive needs assessments to understand the specific needs and vulnerabilities of each group, ensuring that they are addressed equitably and that any potential negative impacts of the response are mitigated. IOM will coordinate with SNFI Cluster SFPs, local authorities and partners on the ground to obtain clear information. b) Verification and distribution: IOM will request the release of items from the S-NFI Core Pipeline once needs and risk analysis reports are shared and approved by the S-NFI Cluster. The specific items included in the in-kind S-NFI responses will depend on the needs assessment report, but common items typically include plastic sheeting, ropes, cooking pots, kangas, solar lamps, and blankets. c)Innovate and Sustainable Shelter Solutions: IOM seeks to implement a comprehensive shelter assistance program aimed at providing durable shelter solutions that are culturally appropriate. Bricks, bamboo, and thatched roofing are the preferred materials among the community, chosen for their adaptability to the local climate as well as to reduce dependency on Core Pipeline and promote local markets. Considering the community’s preference for vernacular materials, IOM could also support in promoting the production of Compressed Stabilized Earth Blocks (CSEBs), bamboo plantations, and training communities on developing sleeping mats using local materials. Sustainable construction practices including indigenous techniques and vernacular materials will be used to reduce the project's ecological footprint. Disaster risk reduction (DRR) will be an integral part of the shelter design and construction process. d) Shelter repair and maintainance : IOM will continue to enhance shelter conditions by shelter repair and maintainance in camps and camp-like settings, such as Sites A to E in Bentiu, and provide support to partners in Unity State, particularly in the Bentiu IDP camp, to address any gaps. e) Cash for SNFI: In areas with functional markets, IOM will support households with sectoral cash to meet their shelter and NFI needs while addressing the shelter needs of the households. Each household will be supported with a total of USD 78 to meet the shelter and NFI needs.Support to Sout Sudan returnees: IOM will continue to provide shelter, in kind and cash support to returnees affected by the Sudan conflict. This includes constructing transitional shelters for returnees and providing them with in kind support. f)MPCA: For multipurpose cash Assistance to meet the separate needs of the households, IOM will support households with USD 112 per household in line with the recommended value per household by the cash working group JMMI for MSSMEB. g) Monitoring and Evaluation: IOM will ensure robust monitoring of its ongoing program to improve program quality and accountability to affected people. The monitoring includes rapid, post, and continuous remote monitoring using the latest tools. IOM will continue to share lessons learned and challenges with partners and update the Activity Info dashboard on a regular basis.h) HLP support: IOM will remain committed to integrating HLP principles into its programming by conducting HLP awareness sessions, convening meetings with local communities, and proactively identifying HLP risks and challenges prior to any response. IOM will leverage the SNFI Cluster's due diligence tools to effectively assess and monitor HLP-related issues. IOM will engage with SNFI Cluster and protection partners for HLP support, coordinate with SFPs, attend Cluster meetings and share information. IOM has dedicated HLP team to support HLP activities that has trained Shelter team on HLP.
