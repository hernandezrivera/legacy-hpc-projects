Project Summary

The humanitarian situation remains dire as the additional strains related to the COVID-19 pandemic and the economic downturn in Syria continue to impact the 1.2 million people living in northwest Syria. Nearly a decade of conflict marked by multiple displacements, economic shocks, military operations, and violence is continuously eroding the resilience of the population. An estimated 1.1 million people rely on humanitarian aid to meet their basic needs such as shelter, food, water, and health.
This plan aims to provide complementary services and immediate needs for the most vulnerable newly displaced groups, supporting their immediate needs and enhancing their preparedness and resilience, and the intended budget is mentioned to cover 2022 and 2023 as well as the proposed activities.
The provision of streamlined humanitarian lifesaving, multi-sectoral assistance and strengthening the basic infrastructure support in existing IDP sites will be achieved through the following activities:
1- Support Site Care and Maintenace activities and/or protection mainstreaming for 60 camps including:
- Construct more life-saving critical infrastructure such as fire breaks.
- Camp operational and regular maintenance activities.
- help in the establishment of an in-camp IM system including registration.
- Establishing feedback/complaint (AAP) mechanism.
- Holding FGD sessions.
- Timely reporting displacement movements.
- Identifying the in-camp referral system for protection mainstreaming services.
- provide tools and materials required for maintaining the basic facilities/infrastructure.
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
2- Obtain Housing, Land and Property agreements, demarcation, and provide site management support.
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
3- Within the tents provision activities, WATAN team also will contribute to “improving the camp management quality in and accountability of IDP sites” by training camp inhabitants to establish services committees and maintain the daily needs in IDP sites.
- Establish CM committees.
- Help development for committees' members' selection.
- Training the CM members on camp management.
- Help in development for each committee's member ToR.
- Select and train members of IDP committees to maintain camp infrastructure (WASH, lighting, fence, replacement of broken parts, welding, painting, reinforcement gravelling of communal areas and roads.
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
Based on a technical assessment proving the availability of essential services (such as markets, public services, infrastructures…), also, taking into consideration HLP rights, WATAN will be operating in vulnerable communities following the Protection, vulnerability, and prioritizing the most in need Reference for Northwest Syria guidance and the AAP framework.
Also, in line with humanitarian principles, this plan's impact is supporting the reconstruction targets for the affected communities to avoid raising violence within the crises hosting communities that produce more armed conflicts and fewer protection conditions.

4 - Needs

Based on the primal HNO figures and the weekly and monthly updates from CCCM Cluster - XB, WATAN conducted a survey to better understand the needs and status of IDP households. Implemented in 60 camps, the field survey included questions related to how IDPs cope with winter conditions, and imminent threats to vulnerable categories, such as women, children, and the elderly. Women accounted for 20% of the survey sample. Since the conduct of the baseline survey. Approximately 40% of IDPs are currently residing in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates (23% and 17% of the total IDP population, respectively). , of whom 51% are estimated to be children and 25%, women. Idleb governorate has the highest burden of displacement, with IDPs representing almost 53% of the total population. In the context of the COVID-19 outbreak, the dire conditions in Northwest Syria have deteriorated, exacerbating the reliance on humanitarian assistance and diminishing positive coping strategies. At the same time, the preparations for the approaching winter season are expected to put a further strain on people’s finances, especially in light of rising costs.The prioritization of locations is: - Concentration of displacement (IDPs and returnees) - Presence of new displacements (IDPs and returnees) - Absence of humanitarian actors (cross-checked with updated 4Ws) - Accessed constraints to household items such as NFIs and Shelter materials.

5 - Implementation of activities

1- Community Selection: • As mentioned, the targeted areas will be communities with a 4/5 severity scale. • Conduct needs assessment and identify in-need locations in different IDP camps in both Aleppo and Idlib governorates. • Conduct pre- and post-assessments using mobile data collection applications. • Coordinate response with different SNFI cluster members. • Obtain Housing, Land and Property agreements in accordance with the HOUSING, LAND AND PROPERTY RIGHTS IN SHELTER DUE DILIGENCE GUIDELINES cluster reference. 2- Community Engagement: Procedures and policies to involve targeted communities in action were built to build partnerships with IDPs women and men of all ages and backgrounds by promoting meaningful participation through structured dialogue. 3- Beneficiary and Criteria Selection: Eligibility and vulnerability indicators will be used in a specific scoring system. Eligibility Group: IDPs - Households who lost their shelter and do not have suitable living conditions. Beneficiaries from the Eligible group will be selected based on the ranking of the vulnerability index: A- living in the IDP temporary settlement site. B- Household no source of income or no other means of financial support. C- Female and/or a single person head the household. D- Household headed by an elderly, minor or person with a disability. E- Household hosting orphaned, unaccompanied children, or homeless relatives due to the conflict. 6- Household member/s with disabilities.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: 1. Continued operations of PCs and informal sites will create negative pull and push factors for IDPs. 2. Needs assessments will raise unrealistic expectations among IDPs 3. COVID-19 in NWS spreads among the population without adequate health infrastructure 4. Management structures could provide legitimacy to actors who are not representative of the IDP communities, increasing inter-community tension. Mitigation:1. Maintain CCCM guidance on camp expansion and establishment ensuring that camps are a last resort. 2. Ensure assessments are brief 3. COVID-19 mitigation measures are applied during the project period). 4. Ensure management structure is representative of the community with selection criteria. 5. Provide gender-balanced awareness-raising with IDPs and field workers; ensure IPs work with humanitarian principles and apply the AAP frameworks. Risk 2: IDPs are evicted by local actors or security forces. IDPs living in PCs and self-settled sites are evicted by landowners, local actors and security forces without prior notification. Mitigation: 1. Establish communication between camp management and local actors. 2. Develop guidance materials to support IPs communication with local authorities. 3. Maintain referral and incidents mechanism. 4. Ensure camp closure and exit planning involve the affected community and local authorities. 5. Develop guidance for exit plans and camp closure. 6. Ensure IDPs are voluntarily leaving, and other shelter options are identified.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The planned activities ensure that its staff mainstream protection principles in all components of the project, including all beneficiaries, have access to services prioritizing their safety and dignity, ensuring "Non-discrimination" and "Do No Harm" in the delivery of assistance. Also, seeking the meaningful engagement of all the stakeholders to ensure that feedback mechanisms are in place to address any potential abuse or conflict during the project cycle. The project will ensure reaching the most vulnerable people and provide services for them or refer them through case management. The proposed intervention will further promote protection mainstreaming and the safety of newly arrived IDPs where the latrines will have the appropriate lighting to mitigate the risks and be gender-segregated. Also, where it's needed, the construction and maintenance of a system of solar-powered lights to illuminate the locations and paths to facilities in the camp will be provided. Feedback will be continuously in line with standardized camp management protocol and monitoring and evaluation procedures. Beneficiary feedback will continue to help with improving protection mainstreaming. Moreover, beneficiaries will be sensitized to their right to equitable and safe assistance and where and how to avoid sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA).


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:1- BAH and BAS borders will continue to remain open.2- HLP will be taken into consideration before delivering fieldwork or any assistance.3- Good collaboration with the IDPs and host communities.4- Access will be conducive to delivering assistance.5- COVID_19 Measures are in place and the vaccination for the humanitarian workers and the vulnerable people are active. Risks:1- Borders closure can affect money transferring or any Cross-Border and local activities.2- Staff mobility and access restrictions to the affected population.3- The risks of GBV can be compounded by overcrowding and lack of privacy.4- The outbreak of COVID 19 pandemic, especially in the coming winter season. Risk Mitigation:1- Coordinate with Shelter/NFI Cluster to avoid any delay or lack of approvals.2- Coordination with local councils for distribution in their quality as partners.3- WATAN will utilize HLP methodologies to investigate the ownership of land and discuss with owners and available site management.4- Signature of memorandums of understanding with local councils to gain confidence and to ensure the success of the project.5- Ensure Well organized distribution to avoid overcrowding with COVID-19 mitigation measures.6- Announcing project activities and criteria for selecting beneficiaries in distribution areas.
