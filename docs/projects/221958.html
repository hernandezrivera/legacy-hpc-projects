Project Summary

NRC’s shelter projects for 2025 aimed to continue to support the people affected by conflict, natural disaster and displacement in emergency context and static as pathways to long-term support and durable solutions for the IDPs, returnees and host community to return, resettle and reintegrate with host community. The interventions are designed to address the emergency shelter / NFIs needs through rapid response mechanism (RRT) and emergency response, transitional / emergency response to Sudan crisis affected populations, and contribute to improve people’s protection and durable solutions through NRC static shelter response. The proposed shelter interventions are to provide access to basic shelter services in underserved and hard to reach locations affected by disaster, disability inclusion and mitigating GVB and durable access to shelter among 188,889 IDPs, Returnees and host communities in South Sudan. NRC’s Shelter interventions will be integrated and implemented in close coordination with NRC’s other core competencies of WASH, Education, Livelihood and Food security, ICLA and protection programming and teams, applying an integrated approach to uplift people’s lives and contributing to South Sudan 2025 HNRP and Shelter Cluster objectives.
The project aims to provide Survival Kits /Emergency shelter/Non-Food items both in-kind and in cash modalities to the most vulnerable persons, newly displaced/host communities, and returnees. The project will focus on the lifesaving shelter/NFIs needs, as identified through assessment to protect the affected displaced people from harsh weather conditions and protection risks targeting all states of South Sudan. Moreover, the project will provide static as long-term and durable shelter solutions to the IDPs, returnees and host communities who wanted to return, resettle, or reintegrate with communities. The first line intervention will be implemented through mobile response as well as using its static teams based in Renk, Kajo Keji, Juba, Koch, Mayom, Leer, Bor, Duk, Twic East, Akobo, Aweil, Wau and Alek. However, the major focus areas will be in Jonglei, Unity, Upper Nile , Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Warrap states. Cash programming will also be considered in areas where markets are fully or semi- functional and shelter/NFI items are available and accessible by the community. When blanket targeting is not applicable, the intervention will target mainly lactating and pregnant women, households with malnourished children, and households with members who have certain health complications. Besides unaccompanied children, unaccompanied elderly and persons with no community linkages will also be targeted. The project will promote timely delivery of assistance through deployment of rapid response teams in line with humanitarian priorities for 2025 HNRP. NRC’s mobile response teams will be ready for deployment within 8 days to conduct assessments and within 14-21 days for full- edged delivery of NFIs and emergency shelters and other activities per location.
NRC’s responses including ERRM / RRT, emergency and static are well coordinated with Shelter Cluster at national and state levels, shelter partners working on the ground and local authorities to avoid duplication and double assistance of the services and utilize the resources effectively. NRC’s ERRM / RRT response will only be initiated following the allocation and approval shelter cluster. Moreover NRC will work with health and Nutrition partners as consortium to implement the shelter projects together with NRC’s other core competencies to implement the comprehensive services to achieve the holistic results .NRC is keep mapping and collaborate with partners working in NRC’s proposed locations to avoid duplications of shelter services.

1. Needs

The South Sudan Humanitarian Need and Response Plan ,2025 guidance and sectoral analysis revealed that; total of 4.2 million people will struggle to meet their basic shelter and NFIs needs due to continues destruction of houses and loss of essential goods from ongoing flood , conflict and displacement .The conditions are worsened by ongoing Sudan Crisis’s displacement, flood and conflict mainly in Upper Nile , Jonglie, , Unity , Northern Bahr el Ghazal , Part of Western Bahr el Ghazel and Western Equatoria states.. The cluster prioritized and targeted 1.37 million people in 35 priority 1 and priority 2 counties in different contexts; including collective sites and other spontaneous sites, flood and conflict affected areas and relatively stable locations where people are returning to their former habitual residence. Out of the shelter cluster target; NRC will be supporting 150,000 individuals across the country including NRC’s first line emergency response through Emergency Rapid Response Mechanism and static as long-term shelter solutions. The targeted caseloads lack access to essential lifesaving NFI items and are often unable to support themselves through communal connections or coping mechanisms through other locally available resources. The limited and depleting natural resources such as timber and grass for shelter construction result competition and may generate conflict and localized violence and lead to multiple displacement. The markets also lack essential items or have become non-functional, making it difficult for the community to access the required items for a safe and dignified life. Lack and Limited access to shelter may cause health and protection risks and poses a negative impact on the economic situation. The lack of privacy and protection risks, particularly for women and girls who usually go to the bushes to collect the increasingly scarce construction materials, exposes them to GBV. As a result of these compounding factors, the affected people have limited coping strategies. Since the conflict erupted in Khartoum, Sudan on April 15, 2023, South Sudan has seen the arrival of 871,388 people as of November 19, 2024. Most of these individuals have crossed through various entry points along the border, with over 698,704 entering via the border crossing in Renk County, Upper Nile state. The influx of individuals and families continues with an average of about 1,600 people arriving daily (dtm.iom.int/reports/Sudan). Unfortunately, conditions for returnees are deteriorating, and it is expected that a total of 228,877 individual (148,877 returnees and 80,000 refugees) are expected to transit through Renk, with an average of 627 arrivals per day in 2025. On 20th October 2024, SS Health Ministry declared cholera outbreak in Renk that has since spread to other regions. Compounding this issues, the capacity of Government authorities and local communities; there is urgent need for humanitarian assistance to protect the lives of those affected by this crisis in Renk and other regions. Women and children are particularly vulnerable, often arriving malnourished and in need of immediate life-saving assistance. It’s also anticipated that; Transition Center ( TC1) and TC2 will be overcrowded , congested and required additional shelters and NFIs assistance to support the new arrivals in 2025 in Renk. People’s S/NFIs needs are primarily linked to conflict and natural disasters induced displacement, but at times also to food insecurity, economic deterioration and disease outbreaks. Thus, timely provision of shelter and NFIs support to the crisis affected population will be able to live a dignified life.

2. Project activities

• The project will target 188,889 individuals for emergency , transitional , long tern shelters and NFIs including those who will be supported by cash depending on the market functionality.• The project provides access to lifesaving NFIs (in-kind )for newly displaced people in a secondary displacement. It will target 155,889 individuals for provision of NFIs.• The project provides cash / voucher support to rental solutions as first line shelter response where feasible. • Shelter upgrades / construction (In-kind or cash) for returnees /protracted IDPs/Host Community and people in secondary displacement. NRC will use the sector specific cash assistance in locations where markets are accessible and functioning locally. However, NRC will use mixed approach of in-kind supplies for the locations where there are not any functioning markets locally. In kind supplies will be received from CPL partners subjected to the availability and urgency of the response. Cash assistance for shelter upgrade will only be provided to the most vulnerable households who may not be able to upgrade / build their shelters. NRC will use vulnerability criteria including women/female headed HH, Child headed HH, person with disability, pregnant / lactating women at HH, vulnerable elders, more girls and women at HH and Households’ age dependency ratio with scoring system to select most vulnerable HHs for cash program.• Construction of communal shelters for longer term transitional centres and settlement sites specifically in Renk. NRC will construct 80 Communal shelters. The shelter will be semi-permanent long-lasting shelters can be accommodated by 28 individuals / 4-5 households at a time as per the sphere standard. However, all these 80 semi-permanent transitional shelters will be expected to occupy / utilize by 3,000 HHs / 18,000 Individuals who will be transitioning through Renk transitional centres. All semi-permanent shelters and infrastructures, which at the end of the project, can be used for other purposes by the host communities or those who will have chosen to integrate with the host community as part of durable solutions.• Construction / rehabilitation of shelter through owner driven approach. • Mainstream Housing, Land and Properties (HLP) and Collaborative Disputes Resolution (CDR) approaches throughout S/NFI response.• Mainstream GBV throughout all stages of S/NFI response.• Training communities/stakeholders on Accountability to affected population (AAP). • Establishment of Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM).• Carry out Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) in two phases; Rapid Monitoring while distribution is carried out to receive immediate
