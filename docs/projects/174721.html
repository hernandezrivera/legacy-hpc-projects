1 - Needs

Northeast Nigeria has been severely affected by insurgency led by non-state armed groups since 2009 and has been declared “state of emergency” since 2013. As clashes between Nigerian security forces and armed opposition groups (AOGs) persist into its tenth year in 2019, over 2 million individuals remain displaced across six states. Between December 2017 and August 2019, the number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) in the Northeast increased by 15.6%, after a gradual decrease in 2017, an indication of a highly volatile security context. In the Northeast, Borno State is the worst affected by the conflict with over 1.48 million IDPs recorded in July 2019 . Among those displaced, 73% indicated that food was their principal unmet need. While households in CRS’ food assistance programming have shown improvement in their individual food security, WFP recent Emergency Food Security Assessment shows a worsening of the prevalence of food security in CRS’ targeted intervention areas as follows: Kaga (84.1%), Gubio (73.0%), Magumeri (62.0%), and Maiduguri (52.5%) respectively. The assessment conducted in April 2019 identified 28.9% of households across BAY States as food insecure and 3.1 % of these households severely food insecure. These figures represent an increase of 2.4% in the prevalence of food insecurity compared to the previous year (February 2018) . According to the report, 14.5% of HHs in Gubio and 10.3% of HHs in Magumeri are severely food insecure with a prevalence of poor food consumption of 25.9% for Kaga, 25% for Magumeri and 7.6% for Gubio LGAs. The latest FEWSNET Nigeria Food Security Outlook projects the following outcomes until January 2020 for areas where CRS proposes or is currently providing assistance: IPC 3 for Gubio, Magumeri, and Kaga; and IPC 2 for Jere/MMC (with pockets of IPC 3). The dire situation is confirmed by the June 2020 Cadre Harmonisé analysis which indicates that, the main food security, nutrition and livelihood indicators are still at alarming levels and over 4 million people were projected to be severely food insecure during the lean season (June-August 2019) in the three northeastern states.

2 - Activities

The most vulnerable individuals in program LGAs (approx. 111,400 individuals) will receive monthly full food rations in 2021 (from March to December). In addition, 3,000 vulnerable households (approx. 18,000 individuals) with access to farmland will receive monthly full rations from April 2021 to September 2021 (six distributions) while also receiving additional farming inputs to ensure HHs self-sufficiency from October 2021 to February 2022.CRS has no finalized voucher value or associated costs. CRS will continue with covering 70% of the MEB in all locations as per the 2017 harmonization guidelines, and requires that 30% of the voucher assistance be spent on “nutrient rich foods”. The nutrient rich foods are determined in conjunction with our Nutrition technical team. The current costing analysis of fuel indicates that 2000 NGN per household is spent in most LGAs and the transport subsidy projected will not exceed 700 NGN. This will be based on assessment, geographical needs and the on the ground context as well as coordination with other actors for harmonization. CRS will support 3,000 rural HHs with farmland access from its current caseload to restore agricultural activities. Beneficiaries will receive seed and tool e-vouchers valued at $45 before the planting season, as well as agricultural training. To improve the food and nutrition outcomes of the program, CRS community mobilizers will conduct nutrition screening through mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) measurements of all children under five to identify cases of malnutrition in all targeted LGAs during house-to-house visits. CRS will cover transport fees ($0.86 - $2) for the referral of approximatively 150 children identified with SAM. CRS adopted costs associated with specific referral pathways per actor per locations, based on the distance’s families are required to travel. CRS has had considerable success with Tom Brown Supplemental Feeding under the scale up in FFP V. Approximatively 3,000 children identified with MAM will receive a maximum of 8 weeks of supplementary feeding. CRS will work with community mobilizers and “lead mothers” to prepare the Tom Brown feeding supplement by providing them with locally procured millet, guinea corn, yellow corn, soybeans, and ground nut, as well as a small cash payment to cover purchase of cooking fuel, milling, and transportation to and from the local mill on a weekly basis. Community mobilizers will carry out follow up visits on all SAM and MAM cases and will provide training in Infant and Young Children Feeding (IYCF) during weekly group sessions. While much of the food aid in the northeast is delivered to IDPs in camps, CRS will continue to deliver food assistance to IDPs living in host communities. CRS’ targeting strategy minimizes potential conflict among groups by focusing on vulnerability, allowing assistance to reach IDPs and returnees, as well as host families who will be identified during the registration process with the aim of alleviating the additional burden that host families face. Based on lessons learned, CRS uses a weighted-criteria scoring system to assess a “vulnerability score” based on both reported and observable criteria that compound food insecurity. CRS considers criteria such as: elderly, child or single-headed households; shelter; short term residence in LGA (indicating IDP status); presence of household members with disabilities; status of ‘Host’ to IDPs; length of displacement; household size and composition and other relevant factors.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CRS’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) team conducts ongoing, regular on-site monitoring surveys (OSM) during disbursement periods and post- distribution monitoring (PDM) surveys following monthly disbursements. These monitoring activities inform on beneficiary satisfaction with different aspects of the process, how beneficiaries use e-vouchers and why, who within the household decides how to spend e-vouchers, and other quantitative and qualitative data that guide program decisions and future program design. OSM and PDM surveys have been conducted using a set of pre- defined questionnaires at market and at the household level respectively. While using purposive sampling methodology, beneficiaries were randomly selected and surveyed on several sub-indicators including satisfaction with the quality of food purchased, number of vendors, and availability of stock in the vendors’ shops. Additionally, CRS offers feedback desks at registrations and verification activities. There is a toll free hotline as well to collect feedback, including anonymous feedback.
