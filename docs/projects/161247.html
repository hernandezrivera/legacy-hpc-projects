Project Summary

This project appeal is for a total budget of $325,000 beginning 1st January 2020 to 31st December 2020 in support of providing safe water to enhance inclusive healthy living and peaceful coexistence between the host communities, IDPs, returnees and the neighboring communities in Terekeka County (Gwor, Nyori and Tindillo Payams) and Juba County (Lirya and Lokiliri Payams).
The water situation is very appalling in the project targeted areas and according to the IPC September – December 2019 report; 12,000 people are in emergency, 86,000 people are in crises and 99,000 people are stressed in Terekeka County while 100,000 people are in crises in Juba County. Vulnerable target populations are unable to access safe water and hygienic facilities. This eventually forces women and young girls to walk long distances in search of water leaving behind the elderly people and children and their vital socio-economic activities. The water and sanitation challenges facing the rural host communities, IDPs and returnees are; lack of water facilities in the areas Gwor, Tindillo, Nyori, Liriya and Lokiliri Payams and needs drilling of 12 new boreholes and rehabilitation of 30 dilapidated non functional boreholes.
The concrete expected results of this intervention are; increased access to safe water, increased community resilience and awareness on water born and poor sanitation related diseases. The project implementation approach includes, training of 42 water source and sanitation committees targeting 252 water source and sanitation committee members (WSSC), 5 payam based pump mechanics (CBPM) and school water health clubs of which 60% of the water committee representation are women. It also incorporates an inclusive rights-based approach (claim-making capabilities of target groups) to safe water and good hygienic practices.

1 - Needs

According to the September – December 2019 IPC report, 12,000 people are in emergency, 86,000 people are in crises and 99,000 people are stressed in Terekeka County while 100,000 people are in crises in Juba County of which 24% of the affected population are women, 54% are children and 17% persons with disabilities. Lack of enough potable water results into increased water born diseases as women end up fetching unsafe water. It also increased GBV in the process of women and young girls moving long distances in search of water. Inter-community conflicts increases especially during the dry season as some communities refuse other communities and their cattle from accessing water points in their villages. This project is looking at providing potable water through drilling of 12 new boreholes and rehabilitation of 30 existing dilapidated non-functional boreholes in 26 villages in support of 2,336 households in Gwor, Nyori and Tindillo payams of Terekeka County and 4 villages covering 372 households in Liriya and Lokiliri payams of Juba County in Central Equatoria State. This project will greatly improve the worrying current water and sanitation situation for 16,248 people in those targeted vulnerable communities. Gwor, Nyori, and Tindillo are generally dry and arid. During the dry season, both people and animals struggle to access water. This often results in competition over water and grazing land which often results in conflict and thereafter affect inter-communal relations. In order to build peoples resilience and mitigate the prevailing water situation in the target communities, there is need to drill new boreholes and rehabilitate the non-functional boreholes in support of saving life and livelihoods of the people.Summarily, in critical water scarcity periods, women and girls are forced to spend most of their time in the few existing functional water sources. Conflicts are exacerbated amongst women, men, girls and boys in water sources during dry seasons. Older men are left home to take care of children for hours while women go a long distance to fetch water and hence losing vital productive time that is meant for agricultural, education and other socio-economic activities. The elderly and the chronically sick people cannot walk for long distances to access water and this affects their livelihood and health in general. On the other hand, people with disabilities are much affected when it comes to movement and access to water, sanitation and hygiene facilities. This has a negative impact on their life and also a total violation of their rights.

2 - Activities

1. Provision of timely and inclusive access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable populations in emergency and crises situations through drilling of 12 new boreholes and rehabilitation of 30 non functional boreholes.2. Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among 16,248 vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases.3. Build community resilience and strengthening livelihoods through hygiene education and training, improving animal health, vegetable production and communication of climate information services to the communities at the grassroots level. 4. Mobilization, formation and training of 42 water source and sanitation committees with a total of 252 water committee members and 5 payam water pump mechanics in support of promotion of water hygiene, maintenance and repairs of the boreholes

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, the rights of every beneficiary will be legally recognised. CAD is non discriminatory in regards to service provision. CAD is non-denominational, non-political and non-sectarian national relief and development organization formed and legally registered on January 15th, 2008, to address the humanitarian situation in South Sudan. CAD have a holistic, community driven approach to programming, ensuring that interventions are needs-driven and implemented in an inclusive integrated manner and in accordance with humanitarian principles.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, the project is designed to benefit populations in emergency and crises and this means displacement-affected communities, host communities and returnees will have access/ benefit from the project activities. For instance, this project will benefit 131 households (786 people) affected by floods in Terekeka. The flood victims have settled within the host communities and they will benefit from this project.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

CAD is working in collaboration with the County authorities (currently State Ministry of Physical Infrastructure, Directorate of Rural Water and Sanitation). The water assessment in 30 vulnerable villages was conducted in collaboration with the Directorate of Rural Water and Sanitation. The local leadership (chiefs and administrators) are part and parcel of this project. This strong community-local government partnership couple with the training of water source and sanitation committees will enhance the project sustainability in the target locations.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project is aligned with national objectives. The project has been designed to achieve the national objective of increasing access to improved water supply to 70 percent in urban areas and 65 percent in rural areas by 2020. The project design involved the beneficiaries and local authorities so that it benefits all peoples in emergency and crises situations regardless of social backgrounds.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the protection environment is sufficiently safe/ stable. CAD is able to reach all the villages of Terekeka and Juba Counties without any challenges in regards to the assessment and accessing the project beneficiaries

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the project design includes sustainability/handover. There will be water source and sanitation management committee members that will ensure day to day running of the project. The water committees will be selected based on commitment and trained. The involvement of the chiefs and local authorities are of paramount importance for the sustainability of the project.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

CAD have also trained several water source and sanitation committees over the years. Such training experiences helps in addressing several community challenges such as access to humanitarian assistance, community feedback mechanism, GBV, inclusion of women and people with disabilities in project activities, upholding data in a safe and ethical manner among others
