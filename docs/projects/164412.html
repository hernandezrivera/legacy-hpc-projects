Project Summary

UNHCR will provide WASH services in locations receiving returnees, through the repair and rehabilitation of water supply networks and support to sewage and solid waste management systems. Sector Objective 1: Support to water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria. The project will assist 291,184 individual /58,237 Households Returnees, IDPs and host communities.
Due consideration will be given to ensure that WASH facilities are equally accessible to both returnees and affected host communities. Through provided support to target populations the project will reduce the number of persons exposed to the risks of displacement mainstream and reduce risks of GBV and PSEA. The network of 3,030 outreach volunteers will ensure greater outreach to people in remote and hard-to-reach areas, for community engagement and consultations on project implementation to prevent SGBV and PSEA. Various participatory methods including FGDs and community/household visits will be used to consult with Syrian IDPs, returnees and host community. UNHCR works on raising awareness among UNHCR and Partner staff on PSEA, through basic PSEA trainings organized by UNHCR periodically ( 82 partner staff trained in 2019).
Partners will be strongly encouraged to provide input to the 4W regularly and in timely manner.

4 - Needs

According to draft HNO findings the main WASH needs across Syria remain the access to sufficient quantities of safe water, the damaged sewage networks and the poor management of solid waste. WASH systems have suffered from damages deriving from years of functioning at high capacity, limited or no maintenance and poor water resources management and continue operating at a fraction of their original design capacity, with many others non-operational. Up to 42% of the population is relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources, at least 80% of sewage is discharged untreated while at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional, 26% of garbage is inappropriately disposed within communities. Such factors pose significant public health risks and have harmful cascade effects on communities in terms of nutritional status, inequality and poverty.Assessments indicate an increase in water share received from the water distribution networks, from 52 % in 2016 to 74% in 2019, while water chlorination level remains insufficient to ensure the safety of the water in many areas. Limitations in regular operations, maintenance and repair have had a significant impact on the systems efficiency and water supply regularity and many households have no choice but to turn to costly, alternative water sources to meet their minimum water supply needs. Also, sewage treatment plants and networks require extensive repair and maintenance or are no longer operational due to damage or the non-availability of critical supplies and management support. Due to the lack of comprehensive garbage disposal strategies, lack of functioning garbage trucks or potential presence of unexploded ordnance contaminated rubble additional efforts are needed in specific communities to strengthen and upgrade the quality and regularity of solid waste management.Sub districts/communities are selected based on Governorate/municipality inputs on needs, areas of major returns,HNO severity scale.

5 - Implementation of activities

Through this project, UNHCR will continue to enforce the efforts to restore and keep the WASH infrastructure at the minimum level of operation. The program will reinforce and upscale the sustainable, recovery-oriented WASH solutions through support to those returning to their original homes and areas. In general, our WASH program will support the Shelter program in the communities of intervention. While the supply of potable water and water networks and infrastructures in safe areas, formal and informal urban settlements, and buildings are overloaded and cannot meet the needs of returnees and local communities, the situation is further exacerbated by significant damage to potable water systems in locations receiving returnees and also hosting IDPs. UNHCR’s interventions will therefore consider provision of essential WASH services in these locations by contributing to the rehabilitation of the existing water networks and waste water system, with provision of power supply to improve the water supply and sanitation systems, in coordination with the local authorities and the WASH Sector. Also the solid waste management will be part of UNHCR program, with the distribution of solid waste metal bins in selected communities. The implementation modalities of the WASH interventions will follow the endorsed WASH Sector guidelines. The following main activities will form the core of UNHCR’s WASH program: 1. Repair and rehabilitation of water systems through the rehabilitation of existing water networks, with the provision of power supply to improve the water and sanitation systems. 2. Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems, through the replacement of broken or damaged pipes that connect residential buildings, schools or clinics to the main sewage network, repair damaged manholes or increase the number of them, and supply and distribute solid waste bins in the same areas of water and sewage system projects.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: There is a risk of unequal distribution and/or allocation of WASH services, including water supply and sewage projects, which might inadvertently empower or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors. Mitigation: This can be mitigated establishing strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time, organizing joint planning culture and agree on minimum standard, including community and authorities in targeting, ensuring sensitive information is handled with care.UNHCR and its partners will conduct regular monitoring visits to ensure WASH program is implemented as planned, taking corrective actions when any deviation is observed. Risk: There is a risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation and construction materials, such as generators, pipes, pumps, etc. during the implementation of WASH projects, such as rehabilitation of the water supply network, sewage network and equipment of WASH pumps. Mitigation: To mitigate this risk, UNHCR and it is partners will engage with the local authorities and the community to ensure local ownership of the project and promote cost recovery systems for longer term maintenance and ownership to infrastructure to promote community ownership. As well as operation and maintenance program with MOLAE will complement the activities to ensure water and sanitation systems are functional after completion of the projects. Risk: There is also a possibility of explosion and human casualty and asset destruction due to mishandling of WASH materials, such as chlorine and gasoline. Mitigation: UNHCR and its partners will ensure that project staff will comply with the specifications outlined in the supplies on delivery, storage and distribution modalities. Also, UNHCR will ensure that suitable storage facilities are in place to ensure safety of staff, beneficiaries and assets during the storage phas.e

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Access restrictions to geographical areas due to active fighting and/or shifting of frontlines and interference of different armed groups would prevent the humanitarian actors to provide timely emergency relief to the affected population. Mitigating measure: UNHCR WASH program must take into account safety, security and access to services in the implementation location and must include an understanding of the local context and dynamics, so as to avoid or prevent any potential issue and constraints. There are open communication lines between UNHCR and its partners and the contextual changes and security threats are closely monitored and analysed. Incidents of interference are reported at the inter-agency level to ensure collective action and messaging (to armed opposition groups). Risk: WASH implementation risks include the potential for aid diversion, corruption and empowerment, which leads to reduced assistance to the persons of concern to UNHCR and a decline in donor funding.Mitigating measure: SOPs are jointly developed between UNHCR and its partners for the project selection and selection of vulnerable districts to be targeted. This risk will be addressed by UNHCR through intervention design, management and monitoring system. Assumption: The potential risk of underfunding will likely impact the scope of UNHCR WASH response, decrease the capacity to properly assist returnees, with a consequence of assistance principally targeted at maintaining life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian activities for those most vulnerable. The reduced scope of the response will preclude from undertaking activities intended to provide more durable WASH solutions and lead to a reduction in the number of water, sanitation and solid waste systems supported.
