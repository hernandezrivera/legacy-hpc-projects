Project Summary

"The project aims to strengthen disaster resilience among vulnerable host communities in Cox’s Bazar district through effective multi-hazard risk management. The project will target host and local communities (100%) in Cox’s Bazar. The Project will work in close collaboration with ISCG Sectors, Local Government, and other relevant stakeholders to support ISCG's overall disaster risk management strategy through the following project activities:
(i) capacity development of local government and humanitarian stakeholders to manage disasters and extreme weather events (target 300).
(ii) improving localized weather forecasting, landslide early warning, and rainfall monitoring systems (target 18,500).
(iii) supporting inclusive DRM planning, disaster preparedness, and anticipatory actions in Rohingya hosting unions (target 35,792).
(iv) developing a comprehensive landslide risk profile and a landslide risk management strategy for Cox’s Bazar district (target 300).
(v) facilitate and support multi-hazards/DRR analysis, plans, and products for local government, ISCG sectors, and humanitarian actors (target 200).
(vi) capacity building (including the provision of DRM equipment and gears) of local first response agencies/professionals (frontline volunteers, CPP, FSCD, Ansar/VDP, etc.) for inclusive disaster preparedness and response (target 200).
(vii) training of government and humanitarian on the application of nature-based solutions in disaster risk reduction and mitigation (target 100).
(viii) implementation of community-based disaster risk reduction interventions adopting nature-based solutions in host communities (target 11,500).
(ix) supporting DRR awareness raising (IEC materials/ drills/ simulations, etc.) in host communities (target 14,500).
The project targets the most vulnerable host communities (81,392; see activity-wise targets above), especially women (50.43%), children (11.02%), elderly (8.24%), persons with disabilities (1.43%), LGBT groups, and ethnic communities.
The project will improve localized weather forecasting, disaster risk assessment, and landslide early warning and dissemination system. The project will train first response agencies/volunteers on DRM aspects and will provide DRM equipment and gear to support response and early action in Rohingya camps. The project, in collaboration with the local government and DMCs, will support DRM planning, preparedness, and early action in the Rohingya hosting unions. The disaster risk reduction schemes adopting nature-based solutions will be implemented in coordination with the FSS sector, EETWG, and local government in the host communities.
The Project will develop strategic alliances and strengthen partnerships with Agencies implementing disaster risk management initiatives in the host communities, ensuring synergies, optimum use of resources, and effective programming.
Gender issues will be effectively mainstreamed across all disaster risk management (DRM) interventions and approaches. For instance, the project will prepare a gender action plan and will ensure that activities are tailored to different gender needs. The project will ensure the active participation of women, persons with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups in the DRM interventions. The project will support gender-sensitive DRM training for the government and humanitarian. The project will also train women volunteers/ representatives. The project will ensure that local DRM plans include gender and disability-inclusive elements, such as the needs, risks, vulnerabilities, and capacities of women, girls, the elderly, and persons with disabilities. All assessment, monitoring, and reporting systems will include sex, age, and disability disaggregated data. The project targets 50.43% of women as beneficiaries and prioritizes women and female-headed households for direct support."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Cox’s Bazar is one of the most disaster-prone districts in Bangladesh and is highly exposed to natural hazards and extreme weather impacts, including cyclones, landslides, flash floods, torrential rains, storm surges, and extreme temperatures. Cox’s Bazar also remains amongst the poorest districts in Bangladesh and has long struggled with food security, livelihood opportunities, insufficient WASH facilities, weak infrastructures, poor health and education indicators. Cox’s Bazar is now home to around one million Rohingya refugees since 2017. The Rohingya crisis has significantly increased the size of the population at risk, as well as added pressures on the environment, which undermine resilience. The location, climate, topography, and human interference makes the refugee camps and hosting areas more vulnerable to natural hazard and extreme weather impacts. Between January to September 2021, natural hazards, and extreme weather events such as windstorms, heavy rains, slope failure, landslide, and flooding have impacted 206,820 individuals (44,190 households), displaced 29,292 individuals, and killed 35 individuals in the camps (SMSD incident report, 18 Sept21). Even in the absence of a major disaster, this climatic context has a considerable impact on the refugee and host community response operation in Cox’s Bazar. Landslides remain one of the most substantial damaging and recurrent hazards in Cox’s Bazar, typically due to excessive and prolonged rainfall in a short period, high population density, unplanned land use, and rampant hill cutting. The Rohingya refugee crisis has further exacerbated the pre-existing landslide susceptibility due to the removal of vegetation and hill cutting for refugee shelters, other facilities, and new roads. The women, girls, children, elderly, persons with disabilities, and other minorities were particularly affected by these natural hazards and extreme weather events in host communities. Natural hazards and extreme weather events have particularly pushed women and girls into more vulnerable and marginalized positions in Cox’s Bazar. In Cox's Bazar, female local and host communities have a lower socio-economic status. This leads to unequal access to opportunities and risks, making them more vulnerable to natural hazards and extreme weather events. Despite the high vulnerability to disasters, women increasingly play a critical role in disaster risk management. Therefore, empowering women is the key to strengthening disaster resilience among host communities in Cox's Bazar. The vulnerable communities will be identified following consultation with the local government, FSS sector, and humanitarian stakeholders, with priority areas identified and verified against known hazard and risk maps. For the selection of participants from the communities, representation of both genders, youth, the elderly, people with disabilities, and other minorities will be actively sought. The major challenges for comprehensive disaster risk management in the district include: the lack of capacity of government and humanitarian to plan, manage and respond to disasters and extreme weather events; lack of effective weather forecasting, landslide early warning, and dissemination system to trigger early action; lack of capacity of first response agencies to manage search, rescue and response operations in hilly terrain; inadequate nature-based solution initiative for DRR, especially for landslide disaster; limited DRR awareness among excluded groups; limited gender-sensitive and disability-inclusive DRR interventions; lack of capacity for mainstreaming DRR into development planning and interventions. The project will closely work with ISCG sectors, local government, and other relevant stakeholders to address these challenges and needs."

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

"The project was designed and will be implemented in alignment with the ISCG’s rationalisation principles, particularly focusing on principles 1, 3, and 4. The Project will work in close collaboration with ISCG Sectors, government, disaster management committees, and other relevant stakeholders to support ISCG's overall disaster risk management strategy. The project will consult with ISCG sectors/working groups and the government before engaging in an activity. The project will develop the capacity of implementing partners to meet basic requirements. The project will contribute to and improve inter-sectoral camp-based coordination when implementing disaster risk management activities at the host communities. The project will contribute to improving DRM-related service delivery for host communities. The project will build relationships with the local government (particularly with the UNO/DC office) for effective project delivery. The project will contribute to ensuring stability in the refugee operation and will improve relations with refugees, the Government, and other partners working in the camps."

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

"The project will mainstream protection issues across the activities. The project targets the most vulnerable host communities and will contribute to enhancing safety and dignity, and promoting the rights of host communities, encouraging inclusiveness without contributing to discrimination, particularly those arising from disasters and extreme weather impacts. The project will follow do-no-harm and non-discrimination principles. The project will set up appropriate mechanisms to ensure the active participation and inclusion of affected populations throughout the project cycle and will place measures to address concerns and complaints. The project will support the participation and empowerment of individuals and communities and will contribute to realizing their rights. The project will provide PSEA training to staff and partners to ensure PSEA issues. "

3.2 - Gender

Gender issues will be effectively mainstreamed across all disaster risk management (DRM) interventions and approaches. For instance, the project will prepare a gender action plan and will ensure that activities are tailored to different gender needs. The project will ensure the active participation of women, persons with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups in the DRM interventions. The project will support gender-sensitive DRM training for the government and humanitarian. The project will also train women volunteers/ representatives. The project will ensure that local DRM plans include gender and disability-inclusive elements, such as the needs, risks, vulnerabilities, and capacities of women, girls, the elderly, and persons with disabilities. All assessment, monitoring, and reporting systems will include sex, age, and disability disaggregated data. The project targets 50.43% of women as beneficiaries and prioritizes women and female-headed households for direct support.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The project will contribute to age inclusion and child safeguarding throughout the project. Despite the high vulnerability to disasters, the elderly and children increasingly play a critical role in disaster risk management. So, the project targets the elderly (8.24%) and children (11.02%) group to reduce their disaster risks and build their DRR capacities. The project will prepare a gender and inclusion plan and will ensure that project activity is tailored to different age group needs. The project will ensure the active participation of the elderly, children, and youth in the DRM interventions.

3.4 - Diversity

The project considers individuals and communities from a range of different social and ethnic backgrounds and of different genders and sexual orientations and will ensure that no one is left behind. The project will ensure that project assistance and strategies are tailored and appropriate to the diverse characteristics, vulnerabilities, and capacities of diverse groups. The project particularly mainstream disability inclusion. The project will ensure the active participation of persons with disabilities and other diverse groups and will address their needs, risks, and capacities throughout the project. All assessment, monitoring, and reporting systems will include sex, age, and disability disaggregated data.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"The project mainstreams the ‘accountability to affected populations (AAP)’ principle into the project cycle. The project will follow the IASC and ISCG AAP guidelines and tools. The project will consider and take action to promote ‘information, communication, and transparency;’ to ensure ‘participation, engagement, and inclusion;’ to place ‘complaint and feedback response mechanism;’ and to improve ‘learning and adaptation.’ The project will set up appropriate mechanisms to ensure the active participation and inclusion of affected populations throughout the project cycle and will place measures to address concerns and complaints. The project will train staff and partners to ensure that core humanitarian standard are respected in the project implementation. The project will engage communities through different meetings, consultations, and awareness-raising activities. The project, in collaboration with CwCWG, will implement the information, education, and communication materials on DRM.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project solely focuses on strengthening disaster risk management, disaster risk reduction, and community resilience for host communities. The project will support ISCG's overall disaster risk management strategy. The project will closely involve and assist ISCG in advancing DRR/DRM within the realm of the Rohingya crisis response and will facilitate coherent DRR efforts among the stakeholders working in the host communities. The project also substantially focuses on environmental issues and climate change mitigation measures by adopting nature-based solutions.
