Project Summary

This project attempts to improve average food consumption among 16 food-insecure communities of Ekondo-Titi and Mbonge Sub-Divisions.
The use of mud-adapted trucks for transportation of food will increase the cost per beneficiary significantly, compared to the more accessible areas, but this will be mitigated by community efforts. In the Upper Balue area, communities have already expressed to Reach Out their willingness to transport food from the last accessible point (Berenge). The extreme food insecurity of the average beneficiary also justifies the extra cost.
This project, therefore, aims to provide emergency food assistance to improve food consumption, dietary diversity and reduce use of coping strategies for vulnerable populations of Ndian Division through 8 monthly food distributions to 13,500 Persons in Need of Ndian and Meme Division.
Activities:
1) Access mediation
Introductory meetings will take place at community level to ensure acceptance for Reach Out’s activities and respect for neutrality and safety during distribution.
2) Creation of Food Distribution Committees (FDCs)
Village assemblies will be called to present the project and create Food Distribution Committees. This committee is a voluntary unit that supports targeting and distribution.
3) Creation of Monitoring Committees (MCs)
Monitoring Committees receive and review complaints, carry out validation of beneficiary lists and community monitoring of fraud and food losses. Gender and ethnic balance between the FDC and MC will be requested.
4) Targeting
• Geographical targeting – ration allocation per site based on objective data (remoteness, level of displacement, population data).
• Food Distribution Committees collect beneficiary data.
• Validation is carried out by Reach Out Monitoring Committee by visiting a sample of prospective beneficiaries and checking consistency.
• Validated data is typed into a scoring database that gives out the priority list.
• FDC validates final list.
I) Voucher distribution (5,100 beneficiaries)
1) Market Assessments
2) Selection of partner suppliers and contracting
3) Design and printing of vouchers
4) Orientation of Food Distribution Committees and vendors
5) Distribution of vouchers
6) Redemption and price monitoring
7) Verification
8) Reimbursement
Voucher value will be 7,000 francs per family member.
II) In-Kind Distribution (13,500 beneficiaries)
1) Market Assessments
2) Call for tenders
3) Review Panel and selection of service providers
4) Purchase of commodities
5) Quality control
6) Bagging
7) Transportation to Ekondo-Titi
8) Warehousing
9) Secondary transportation to Distribution sites.
Each person will receive 7.35 KG of rice, 4.5 KG of beans, 0.735 KG of oil and .105 KG of salt, which represents 70% of minimum monthly consumption.
5) Food Security Scanning
Scanning is conducted at baseline and every 3 months. It will include beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries and discontinued beneficiaries. It will be designed and conducted in collaboration with the Food Security Cluster.
6) Referral of vulnerable beneficiaries to Protection Services by trained staff.
7) Livelihoods support. 800 vulnerable beneficiary families (representing at least 4,000 persons) will be trained and supported with farming tools and improved cassava cuttings. This will contribute to improved food security levels after the intervention.
8) Gender Assessments
02 gender assessment sessions will be carried per distribution site. Separated by gender and in a conclusive plenary, the differences between women and men, youths, and the elderly are identified by community members in order to make specific resolutions so the project can support all of them in a fair and equitable manner. (eg. Priority lines, targeting, closing and opening times, the use of alternates)
Note about management of beneficiary data:
Consolidated beneficiaries data will only be kept in a separate hard-drive without access to internet.

Besoins / Needs

Before 2018, malnutrition was so rare in the South-West that GAM data was not even tracked. In Reach Out´s recent Measles Vaccination campaign (December 2020), out of 2,969 children 0-5 years screened, 209 cases of GAM were identified. 156 were in Bafaka and Bisoro alone, out of 760. Malnutrition is connected to food security. Serve as an example the background note of one of the cases identified by Reach Out´s Community Health Workers in Ekondo-Titi in October 2019. “These four [malnourished] children are from the same household. They are being taken care of by their grandmother who is unable to farm. These children can go several days without food. “ Before the crisis, Ekondo-Titi was already isolated. The main avenue to access the Sub-Division is Mbonge Road, a dirt road that becomes mud during the rainy season. It takes more than 3 hours to move from Kumba to Ekondo-Titi (55 KM) during the rainy season. To go from Ekondo-Titi central to any other rural community it can take as much as a 24 hour trip using bikes and walking on foot. Communities have extremely muddy roads and hills deteriorated and destroyed by landslides, as well as swamps and rivers that need to be crossed. Nalende, for example, requires a-4 hour walk for a healthy man coming from Kitta to be accessed. Kitta itself takes about an hour and a half by car or motorbike from Ekondo-Titi, even though it´s just 7.5 KM away. More than 40% of Ekondo-Titi Sub-Division are swamps and creeks not fit for agricultural exploitation. The major crop cultivated is palm, as a crash crop to sell oil outside of the Sub-Division. Moreover, climate change has made season changes unpredictable, and the general weather drier and hotter. Some crops and vegetables have become extinct or do not yield as much as before. Before the crisis, an average farmer would make 14,704 francs a month (25$,– Data from Ekondo-Titi Council Development Plan), and complement with fishing or recollecting forest fruits. Now the situation is even worse, most farmers have devolved into farming exclusively for self-consumption. The three major clans of Ekondo-Titi, the Balondo, Barombi, and Balue all display male-dominated cultures. Reach Out has identified 45 cases of GBV in Ekondo-Titi in the year 2019, mostly physical violence. About 40% of the female adult population is illiterate, as a result of the prioritization of male education in the past. Women marry early and have an average of 5 children. They access land, but they do not usually have legal ownership, limiting their access to credit and capital. Women don´t farm the more lucrative cash crops (cocoa and palm) but rather focus on subsistence agriculture and kitchen gardening, and use unprofessional methods with insufficient inputs. If they grow and sell commercial crops, these are the less profitable okra and cassava. It has been observed that females of all ages are hosting displaced children from family members and neighbours, adding to their responsibilities. These inequalities and specificities call for a tailored project that considers women´s extreme vulnerability and specific needs.Moreover, youths are suffering of massive unemployment and wide-spread teenage pregnancy, and need support in order to be included in community power structures. Most elderly practice subsistence agriculture with no income. Many aged persons of Ndian are illiterate, particularly women. Project communication will take this into consideration. Despite these extreme conditions, WFP’s 2020 strategy did not include Ndian Division in its target areas, and the 2020 Strategic Plan of the FSC only targets 6,715 beneficiaries in Ndian. Right now, Ndian stands a strong chance to remain untouched while food security levels keep deteriorating, three years after the beginning of the war.
