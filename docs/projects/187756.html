Project Summary

This project will respond to the needs of 314,271 individuals (Host:199,986, IDPs: 112,874, Returnees:1,411, Male: 155,391, Female: 158,880, PWD: 86,796) for 23 months through 3 interventions as follows: The first intervention will target 11 informal settlements in Dana, Idleb, Janudiyeh, and Maaret Masrin sub-districts. Work will be done to connect these camps to the nearby water and sewage networks and then to support them with operating costs to pump the safe water from the network towards these camps daily. At the same time, safe water trucking will be delivered into these camps daily, desludging septic tanks for three months, pending the completion of the network connection operations.
Meanwhile, this intervention will provide chlorinated water to 76,850 individuals in 62 informal IDP settlements in Idleb and Aleppo. In addition, more polyethylene tanks will be installed to accommodate the increase in the amount of water that has been recommended by the WASH cluster. The water will be delivered to communal water tanks. Additionally, light maintenance activities for water storage tanks and handwashing points will be conducted to ensure functioning and accommodating of the water for one day at least, that, has been recommended by the WASH cluster as a protective measure to confront the outbreak of the Corona pandemic. IhsanRD will test the water (at the source and communal tanks). In addition, CVA and consumable hygiene kits will be distributed in all targeted locations. In terms of sanitation services, Ihsan will construct new Latrines, complete with water tanks, septic tanks, and hand washing basins in the informal IDP settlements. In addition to connecting a few camps with nearby sanitation system. IhsanRD will collect solid waste from 69 camps and remove the waste to landfills. IhsanRD will distribute waste containers to prevent littering.
Important hygiene promotion activities including COVID-19 awareness materials will be disseminated, accompanied by the distribution of soap. This component contributes to the mobilization of the community as the hygiene promoters will come from the community with taking into consideration all preventive measures.
The second intervention will provide sustainable WASH infrastructures in Araee, Kafruhin, Hamama - Kafr Debbin, Sarmin, Teftnaz communities that have been considered as high-priority locations through the rehabilitation of 5 water stations and 3 sewage systems to serve 165987 individuals This intervention seeks mainly to provide sustainable and affordable power sources through installing hybrid solar energy and/or connecting the water stations to the electricity grid. In addition to carrying out the other rehabilitation works required to ensure the proper functioning of the water systems.
This intervention will cover the operations and maintenance costs for 3 months for the new rehabilitated water stations and 9 months for Al Bab, Bazagha, Qabasin and Sandi communities. As the efforts are made to establish/re-activate a tariff collection and accounting system, and to sustain the water system in these communities in coordination with Services Units.
The rehabilitation of sewage systems in Teftnaz, Kafruhin, and Hamama - Kafr Debbin communities. The works will include installing concrete pipes, excavation; backfilling; cleaning; unblocking sewage pipes and manholes, the extension of sewage networks, and repairing the concrete in existing drainage and manholes 30392 individuals.
The third intervention will target the people with disabilities living in the four camps targeted in the first intervention of this project. The activities will include addressing all the physical obstacles that impede their access to water, washing points, or toilets, considering the condition of everyone according to the disability s/he suffers, in addition to conducting hygiene awareness sessions and providing personal hygiene materials and NFIs with different modalities according to PWD needs.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2022 WASH needs of affected populations will continue to be high across 2022.In IDP sites access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management, and/or hygiene supplies will remain a challenge for more than 2 million people in IDP sites, highly dependent on continued humanitarian assistance. Up to 96% of interviewed residents of IDP sites received some WASH humanitarian assistance, were not fully satisfied with it, due to insufficient quantity or quality of assistance. Water trucking services are the only source of water for 73% of IDP site residents and only 24% HHs are connected to (simplified) sewer networks. Despite the sector assistance, 24% of IDPs in sites couldn’t access one or multiple hygiene items, 23% faced barriers to effective handwashing. Also, 35% of HHs reported issues with toilet functionality or wastewater disposal. In Communities the WASH infrastructure requires significant support and substantial investments where up to 47% of the population is relying on often unsafe alternatives to piped water, to meet or complement their water needs. Also, the investment in sanitation infrastructure is critical, as at least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional. PWD and the elderly face more constraints and various protection risks in accessing WASH services. As per HNO2022 The final severity score of WASH for the target locations is ranked 4 out of 5.

5 - Implementation of activities

Work will be done to implement the three interventions of the project simultaneously. After making the project announcement and signing MoUs with the local councils, and after consulting with stakeholders and updating the BOQs according to the gap that exists, taking into account the needs of the most vulnerable people and people with disabilities.The necessary purchases and contracting with service and material providers will be carried out in accordance with our approved standard procurement procedures.In the first intervention, work will be done to connect 11 camps to the nearby water and/or sewage network. At the same time, work will be done to provide materials and integrated services in these camps. On the other hand, work will be done to continue providing 75 camps with all WASH services within the project period. In the 2nd intervention, work will be done to rehabilitate 5 water systems and support them with operating expenses for 3 months, including fuel, chlorine, oil, and electricity, in addition to supporting 17 stations in Al-Bab with operating costs for 9 months as well. Work will also be done to rehabilitate the sewage systems in 3 cities. The 3rd intervention will provide WASH services and materials for PWDs in the camps, through working close to PWDs and their companions to determine the optimal services that are commensurate with the disability situation for each person, and activities will be implemented accordingly.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA is at the core of IRD responses. All IRD staff will be complied with it. Effective systems are in place for preventing and responding to SEA acts by integrating PSEA in IRD policies and making sure that all personnel read and sign the code of conduct , and that all contracts with suppliers/contractors include clauses on PSEA and CSG. IRD also provides awareness training to all staff in the organization on PSEA and CSG principles.Also, IRD will ensure to provide awareness-raising sessions on GBV, PSEA, and CSG to consider all prevention measures in COVID-19. Moreover, the referral pathway will be carried out when needed.Implementation will be conducted through integrated WASH and Protection mobile teams during all the projects that apply for protection and humanitarian principles for all activities. Since all the project staff are well qualified and trained to deliver such activities, the quality of services under PSEA will be assessed will be guaranteed.This project is built taking fully into account: Do No Harm, Age, Gender, Diversity mainstreaming (AGDM), and commitment to safe and equal access to assistance. The project team will set the necessary participatory mechanisms for different groups in the targeted locations, including men, women, boys, and girls, in addition to the PWDs, elderly people who are the affected people in the COVID -19 and decide which stakeholders need to be involved in the different implementation stages.
