Project Summary

The proposed activities will be implemented over a period of 12 months by DPI in collaboration with SMOH and the project is aim to directly cover 530,000 Boys, Girls, Women and Men including IDPs’ Returnees, Vulnerable Host communities in East Jebel and South Sudanese Refugees around AL Radom localities in addition by Mid 2023 (18 Months) , other stakeholders and the community CBOs and Health Workers . To achieve timely delivery of results and activities under this project, DPI is including the following as key steps in its implementation plan. Inception meeting of this project will be conducted at field sites at state levels. This will give better understanding of the project for all stakeholders and staffs. Recruitment plan and process will be initiated for which roles and responsibilities of the position will be defined. As health workers are also seconded by MOH, DPI will liaise with SMOH to obtain staffs from MOH with necessary skills and competencies. Food items will be received from WFP and UNICEF at state level. As the project entails procurement of medical and non-medical items, procurement process will be initiated based on the plan and pipeline prediction. In addition, necessary service contracts and tenders will be launched as necessary. Local NGOs will largely manage and support the community based activities with direct technical and operational support from DPI. They ensure that nutrition education, outreach activities and IYCF activities, referrals and commodity distributions are well handled at the community level. This project will be overall led by the country director and program director. The support, management and functional lines at field level are maintained by area coordinators and medical coordinators. Procurement and logistical support will be developed and delivered on time. DPI will provide due focus on availing essential drugs and supplies for the nutrition services. They manage the day to day activities under this grant with further delegation, sharing and direction of activities by technical and operational staffs at the field level. DPI functions in coordination with key actors to improve impact, avoid wastage, reduce duplication, and promote sustainability of project activities. These are MOH, Humanitarian Partners, Local NGOs, and the community at large Health Workers (CHW) . MOH, as the ultimate health actor for the country, avails strategies, standards, technical guidance, Deployment of staffs, participates in assessment and joint monitoring. It also supports provision and clearance of selected medical supplies Humanitarian Aid Commission will support in issuing clearance of travel arrangement, supporting the recruitment process, permitting assessments and responses, and evaluating the projects. In an effort to mitigate the environmental impact associated with this programming (rehabilitation of clinics, waste from RUTF packaging), DPI will support collection all RUTF waste packaging from beneficiaries. One Nutrition Coordinator provides additional oversight, technical support, and supervision of all aspects of DPI’s nutrition programs in Darfur, as well as liaising with core other stakeholders. Medical waste management will be ensured in all clinics and rehabilitation will include maintenance. All rehabilitation will take into consideration the environmental friendly materials. The project will include formulation and establishment of Community Health Workers, Nutrition Health Care Taker (NCT) maternal Workers.

1 - Needs

Al-Radom: The health and nutrition systems in Al-Radom remain weak and inadequately prepared to provide basic lifesaving health services and respond to outbreaks. In Al-Radom locality, it is reported that 28% and 26% of the health facilities are not functional. Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) coverage in health facilities is only provided in 15% in Al-Radom locality. Only 1 of the clinics in the locality are providing Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses (IMCI) services. Al-Radom, the coverage of measles vaccination is only 55.9% while only 28.3% of children are fully vaccinated in Al-Radom locality, there are challenges in ensuring that all signal functions of BEmONC are provided. In both clinics the only laboratory service provided is for rapid tests. Only 54% of the cases are managed according to the protocol despite training and supportive supervisions, which signals the need for strengthening support and follow up to ensure adherence to protocols. H&N facility delivery rate is also low (11.5%) which requires an increased effort on identification and referral of pregnant mothers to give birth at facility level. Detection and referral of pregnant mothers is weak because of low coverage of community outreach activities as a result of low number of CHWs and mother groups. According to the SMOH, in Al-Radom some 900 children suffer from malnutrition annually (Global Acute Malnutrition) out of which over ,1,200 children , lactating pregnant women and elder aged persons suffer from (SAM) (H&N Sector, meeting of SD June 2021. EJM (Sharig Jebel Mara): While the unilateral ceasefires have enabled a general improvement in the security situation across EJM, humanitarian needs continue. Humanitarian partners have identified an estimated 5.8 million people in need of humanitarian assistance across SHN (SHN) in 2021 have increased by one million people due to influx of diseases outbreaks, economic difficulties, food insecurity and malnutrition. Despite improved harvests in 2020.Together, these have contributed to significant increases in prices for staple foods, including domestically produced sorghum and millet by approximately 10-20 percent since November. Prices for wheat flour and bread more than doubled on some markets in December. These changes in the macro-economic of the country are likely to result in further increases in staple food prices and reduce poor households’ ability to access food from markets. as a result of increased inflation rate in the locality. In 2020 1,230 IDPs in East Jebel Mara locality need humanitarian assistance. Children represent 60 per cent of people displaced in the locality showing a level of vulnerability in the camps. In all areas hosting the internally displaced populations, the humanitarian needs in H&N protection,. All sites under this proposed project host large numbers of IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities living in these camps under protracted displacement. Eleven out of the eighteen states have a malnutrition prevalence of above 15%, which is above the critical threshold as per the WHO standards. The GAM and SAM rates for East Jebel Mara locality are 15.9% and 3.5% (MICS 2014), above the critical thresholds. In January 2020 DPI also conducted a SMART survey, which showed a GAM and SAM rate of 16% and 4.2% in EJM Camp. . Exclusive feeding was reported to be 42.7%.Exclusive breastfeeding rates remain low at 50.4% in SD., complementary foods were introduced in timely fashion for 60.9% of children 6-8 months of age The 13 targeted surrounding villages of Suuni and Abuhuraira administrative units (Suuni , keeba, Tabla, Diniara , Kadroa, Rokona, Nabak filfil , Kirro, Doo, Daalo , Dimbiyala , Biyara , Algardud and Jawa ) with estimated number of 380 HH per village approximately. All these villages (clusters travel a distance of at least 3-4 hours to Diribat town by Donkey Cart (the main EJM town) for H&N services. Some cases of death while on the way.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The influence of the local communities, IDPs, Returnees and Vulnerable Host Communities, is significant in ensuring sustainability of the project outcomes. To this end, the project will closely work with these community groups in addressing their specific and communal needs. Besides, one of the aims of the project is strengthening the capacity of the community to recreation of sustainable health and Nutrition aspects and through changing the community behaviors, provision of full packages of medical and supplies of health and nutrition supplies, advocacy and trainings will be disseminated. The community health workers, composed of various committees at grassroots level, when functioning well, sustainably address the root causes of Environmental vulnerability. The project will focus on recreation / rehabilitation and productive aspects including recreation and rehabilitation of environmental results to later support their standards of living. In an effort to mitigate the environmental impact associated with this programming (rehabilitation of clinics, waste from RUTF packaging), DPI will support collection all RUTF waste packaging from beneficiaries. Waste management training will be conducted particularly to address the effects of the waste towards causing and infection of diseases within the communities.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Actions incorporated in this project are all safe and proven to be applied in humanitarian setting. DPI will also continue to analyze the local challenges and context so as not result in unwanted negative effects of its operation on social, cultural, political and economic capacities of the affected population. DPI has a standard drug and supply management tools to monitor the safety and relevance of medical supplies for this project. Health and Nutrition education based on standards and evidence base CMAM and IYCF support will also be conducted. DPI strives to protect breast feeding and apply complementary feeding practices using the locally available foods. By sustaining this, DPI will support the coping capacity of the communities and prevent untoward negative consequences. Consultations with community leaders and constant communication with leaders and beneficiaries will be regularly done. This ensures that concerns are addressed and that all needs are being met. Especially during defaulter tracing, but also throughout all service provision, DPI continuously asks beneficiaries why they do or do not decide to seek treatment, what the barriers were, and what can be done to ensure beneficiaries access services. DPI considers the principles of "Do No Harm" in all of its projects, and is careful to ensure that projects respond to direct needs identified in the community and avoid unnecessary secondary effects that could potentially have unseen consequences. DPI currently uses community feedback mechanisms that include use of suggestion boxes at each clinic, use of feedback logbook, patient exit interviews and monthly meeting with community leaders. Feedback are reviewed by a centralized committee at each site where the response is organized delivered.
