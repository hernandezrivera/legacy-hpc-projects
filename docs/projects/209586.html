Project Summary

The project aims to reduce morbidity and mortality by providing integrated life-saving interventions to the most vulnerable and in need children and families in Mafa and Konduga, while increasing their protection and building their resilience in a sustainable manner, while reducing challenges to humanitarian action with localization. The program is designed around three specific purposes:
• Purpose 1.1: Provide lifesaving multisectoral assistance to IDPs inside camps through Cash and Vouchers for food with supplementary nutrition, protection, and WASH interventions through community-based approaches throughout the year.
• Purpose 1.2: Provide a foundation for resilience for IDP HHs in communities through livelihood interventions in a protected, safe, and secure environment with nutrition, protection and WASH services all year round with food assistance during the lean season.
• Pupose 1.3: Provide a forum for coordination among INGOs in the North East BAY states, and strengthening the engagement and capacities of Nigerian Local and National NGOs in Adamawa.
To achieve these proposed purposes, SC will deliver activities in eight sectors: Food Assistance, Nutrition, MPCA, ERMS, WASH, Protection, Humanitarian Coordination, Information Management, and Assessments (HCIMA), and Humanitarian Policy, Studies, Analysis, or Applications (HPSAA). SC will implement the project’s activities in compliance with Sphere, Child Protection Minimum Standards (CPMS), Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS), Minimum Economic Recovery Standards (MERS) and other humanitarian standards. Likewise, SC will coordinate this intervention with the humanitarian system (cluster, working groups, coordination forums), local authorities, humanitarian and development partners and ensure collaboration and information exchange to improve the quality and effectiveness of the humanitarian assistance provided.

1 - Needs

To meet the immediate food needs of conflict/disaster affected vulnerable HHs, SC will continue supporting the current caseload of beneficiary HHs while assessing new arrival IDPs (enrolled first into MPCA and then transitioned to food assistance) in camp to receive unconditional food assistance based on the approved food minimum expenditure basket (fMEB) set by the FSSWG. SC will provide unconditional food assistance using a mixed modality of electronic commodity vouchers restricted to the purchase of sector approved fMEB commodities and electronic value vouchers to cash-out over the counter at the same local agents to cover the costs associated with food preparation. With the FSSWG approved transfer value of NGN 5,870 (USD 8.7) per capita , HHs will receive a transfer value ranging from NGN 19,610 (USD 29.05) to NGN 48,960 (USD 72.53) depending on their HHs size. Through this support, HHs will be able to shop for pre-determined food items at local vendors, including beans, maize, milk, tomato paste, sorghum, millet, rice, pasta, groundnuts, sugar, salt, Maggi, vegetable oil, palm oil, eggs, and onions, as well as to cash out up to 2,000 naira at the same local vendors to cover the costs of cooking fuel and food preparation (i.e. millet grinding service cost). Electronic commodity and value vouchers will be transferred on NFC-enabled cards with multiple wallets (one wallet with a QR code to procure food items and another wallet also with a QR code to cash-out). This distribution mechanism is fully in place and has been rolled-out in previous BHA awards. SC has an already established offline e-voucher system that enables HHs in locations without network coverage to access food items and cash from selected vendors. The system allows the beneficiaries to shop at safe times and locations of their choice and use their voucher once or on multiple occasions. Targeted HHs are already familiar with the modality and distribution mechanism as part of previous BHA award and SC can monitor remote redemption/purchase activities. SC will be responsible for cash transfers to project participants, selection and enrolment in to food assistance program, while EYN with SC support will conduct beneficiary assessment, and registration, monitor program activities (including shopping at vendors’ shops), undertake data collection for monthly and quarterly PDMs as well as monthly market monitoring. EYN will be responsible for direct implementation of food assistance activities including day-to-day interaction with beneficiaries, sensitization and mobilization for information sharing, and handling complaints, with oversight and support by SC staff. EYN staff and field assistants will supervise monthly shopping at vendors’ shops and hold monthly meetings with community committees in respective communities.

2 - Activities

To address the gravest threats to life, health, and safety of vulnerable children and families in Mafa and Konduga, Save the Children (SC), Green Concern for Development (Greencode) and Ekklesiyar Yan’uwa a Nigeria (EYN) propose to provide lifesaving food assistance, multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA), critical nutrition services, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), and protection services to the most affected and in need Households (HHs). Moreover, to help people move out of crisis, efforts will be made to improve resilience through improved and diversified livelihoods. The program will build its intervention on the lessons learned and best practices of the current BHA FY21 by proposing an integrated and multisectoral approach to address the most urgent humanitarian needs of affected children and families while improving their resilience and avoiding humanitarian aid reliance. To this extent, SC and its partners through this project will provide food assistance during the lean season to avoid the use of severe negative coping mechanisms for HHs living within communities. Other services will be provided throughout the year to enable IDPs live in a safe, secure and protected environment. Only IDPs HHs living in camps will receive food assistance throughout the year. The program will concentrate most of the activities during the lean season while having a light touch during the off-lean. Likewise, SC will provide unconditional food assistance to vulnerable 22,615 HHs (142,505 individuals) in IPC phase 3 and above in 22 communities in Mafa and Konduga (communities supported by the earlier award) during the off-lean season in IDP camp and expanding outside in the 22 targeted communities hosting IDPs during the lean season. Newly displaced HHs arriving in IDP sites will be supported with multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) to meet their immediate basic needs. HHs in host communities already receiving food assistance in the previous BHA award and to transition off in October 2023 will be supported with a one-off cash transfer to cover 100% of the fMEB to limit the adoption of negative coping strategies. A total of 17,157 HHs (108,237 Individuals) will be targeted under this activity. To support HHs transitioning off food assistance and enable them to generate a monthly income to meet their food needs and sustain their livelihoods, SC will provide conditional cash grants to 2,000 HHs to start a new livelihood or restore their livelihoods. HHs will be trained in managing and improving their income-generating activity (IGA) as a condition to receive cash transfers to start up or expand their IGA.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

SC and its partner will provide impartial access to services by implementing transparent processes and procedures for selecting beneficiaries and providing support based on need and predetermined vulnerability criteria. The communities will receive adequate sensitization on relevant services, including contributing to the most culturally acceptable feedback mechanism and criteria for selecting beneficiaries. SC will be intentional in selecting suitable sites and times for field activities, including meetings, focus group discussions, and key informant interviews with program participants, community leaders, and key stakeholders. Priority will be given to sites near program communities to ensure all people are able to return home before dark to minimize risks. SC will train partner staff, including vendors, enumerators, and field assistants (volunteers), on child safeguarding, accountability, gender equality, disability inclusion, and SC’s Code of Conduct. SC and partners will mainstream protection across all activities, ensuring that older caregivers, child-headed HHs, persons with disabilities, and female-headed HHs are referred to the food assistance program as vulnerable HHs with minors.• Prioritize Safety and Dignity and Avoid Causing Harm: SC and its partner will continue working with already established community structures, including existing community committees, while building local capacities to be intentional in identifying and selecting the most vulnerable IDP HHs in the communities. SC will also target returnees and host community HHs who are vulnerable to increase community acceptance and participation while avoiding community tension. SC recognizes the polygamous nature of most of the supported HHs. Each wife and their dependents with will be considered as specific HHs to ensure appropriate access to cash and vouchers across HH members while reducing risks of intra-family tensions. In turn, husbands heading polygamous HHs will not be considered as cash and voucher recipients to avoid duplications. SC will continue sensitizing beneficiaries on safety and security measures to consider before/during/after cash and voucher redemption and train participating vendors on safeguarding and do no harm approaches, to ensure that children and other vulnerable groups, especially girls, women, and elderly people are safe, and not exposed to harm when redeeming their cash and voucher. Shopping will continue to be staggered (transfers are made to a few communities at a time) to avoid overcrowding vendor shops while program staff and partner staff (field assistants) will monitor shopping activity.• Meaningful Access: Through sensitization meetings and radio messaging, SC will reiterate messages to beneficiaries, partners, community structures, and other community stakeholders emphasizing that food assistance is inclusive, free, and impartial, that the right to aid is unconditional, and that no one should ask for any kind of favor in return for aid, particularly sexual and/or monetary favors. SC will ensure vendors continue to prioritize locations within implementing communities for easy access to participants. Across distribution sites, participating vendors will be mandated to provide shaded areas, sanitation facilities, drinking water, safe spaces for PLW, and other essentials for beneficiaries waiting to be served at their outlets. SC will ensure that beneficiaries with additional vulnerabilities are prioritized for services at vendor stores.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SC will continue to promote transparency and accountability in all its operations and ensure that beneficiaries, community members, and other community stakeholders have access to a gender-responsive and inclusive system of feedback and reporting mechanisms (FRM). The feedback mechanisms will be established together with and for program participants, including children, adults (women and men), persons living with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups, and take timely corrective actions to address safety, dignity, and access issues raised by users and/or to redefine their assistance. SC will always ensure confidentiality to protect beneficiaries. SC will utilize the preferred feedback mechanisms selected by the community; these include a toll-free mobile phone number, a help desk, and community feedback meetings. Within the shopping areas, SC has established an FRM help desk that is managed by staff with the support of two field assistants (male and female) to ensure beneficiaries can provide feedback on any challenges they face relating to food assistance and other components of the program and that they are addressed efficiently. Community feedback meetings to engage with community structures and beneficiaries on the impact of program activities on the community and ways of improving the support provided will be held quarterly. At every given opportunity, training, and meeting, SC will provide short presentations on FRM, gender equality, and inclusion to reiterate their importance. In addition, two weeks after distributions, SC will conduct post-distribution monitoring (PDM) to obtain feedback from beneficiaries about their CVA experience.
