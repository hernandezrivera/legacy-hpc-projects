Project Overview
Project Title: Climate-Smart Agriculture Initiative for Food Security and Community Resilience
Location: Bamenda, North West Region, Cameroon
Target Population: Smallholder farmers, internally displaced persons (IDPs), women, and youth
Duration: 24 months
Implementing Organization: Hunger Relief Sheroes (HURES)
Funding Required: $250,000 USD
Project Rationale
In Bamenda, Cameroon, climate change is exacerbating food insecurity, reducing crop yields, and increasing the vulnerability of communities, especially women, youth, and IDPs who rely on agriculture for their livelihoods. Erratic rainfall, soil degradation, and extreme weather events pose significant threats to the region’s food systems, disproportionately impacting smallholder farmers (https://reports.unocha.org/en/country/cameroon/card/3PCil3TdaA/). This project seeks to introduce climate-smart agricultural practices that will enhance crop productivity, strengthen resilience to climate impacts, and improve the overall food security and economic well-being of these vulnerable groups.
Project Goals and Objectives
Goal: To build resilience among smallholder farmers, women, youth, and IDPs in Bamenda by promoting sustainable and climate-smart agriculture practices to ensure food security and reduce climate vulnerability.
Objectives:
1. To train 500 smallholder farmers, including women and youth, in climate-smart agricultural practices.
2. To establish five demonstration farms that showcase sustainable farming techniques and serve as learning hubs for the community.
3. To improve soil health and water management practices to enhance agricultural productivity and resilience.
4. To empower women and youth through knowledge transfer and involvement in decision-making processes related to climate-smart agriculture.
5. To strengthen community awareness and advocacy for climate adaptation and environmental sustainability.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The “Climate-Smart Agriculture for Resilience and Food Security in Bamenda, Cameroon” project aims to address the following critical needs:1.Food Insecurity and MalnutritionFood insecurity is a major problem in the Bamenda region as a result of falling agricultural output brought on by land degradation and climate change. Particularly for vulnerable groups like women, children, and displaced people, conflict has further disturbed agricultural methods, restricting food availability and exacerbating malnutrition. The project's main goal is to solve these problems by boosting crop yields using sustainable methods.2. Climate VulnerabilityBamenda farmers are particularly dependent on rain-fed farming, which leaves them open to floods and unpredictable weather patterns. Degradation of the soil, limited water availability, and variable planting seasons are all results of climate change. As part of the project, climate-smart practices like agroforestry, effective irrigation systems, and drought-tolerant crops will be implemented to increase resilience.2.Economic Instability for FarmersDecreased productivity and problems with market access result in lower incomes for agricultural communities. Opportunities for livelihood diversification are necessary for women and young people, who are disproportionately impacted by poverty and unemployment. In order to increase household financial security, the initiative will offer training in income-generating activities (such as value addition and market links). 3.Capacity Gaps in Sustainable FarmingFarmers do not have the resources, knowledge, or abilities needed to implement sustainable farming methods. To close this knowledge gap, the initiative will provide farmers with climate-smart agriculture training, resources, and extension services.4.Environmental DegradationLand and environmental deterioration is being accelerated by exploitation of resources, deforestation, and unsustainable farming methods. The project will encourage agroforestry, ecosystem restoration, and soil conservation methods to reduce these effects.6. Social and Gender InequitiesYoung people and women, including those who have been displaced, frequently do not have access to resources, land, or positions of decision-making in agriculture. Their ability to participate in sustainable development is restricted by this marginalization. In order to empower these groups and guarantee equitable benefits, the initiative will integrate gender-sensitive measures. Through targeted activities, the project will address these needs, contributing to improved food security, climate resilience, and socio-economic stability for the Bamenda region.
