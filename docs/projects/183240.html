Project Summary

This project aims to support vulnerable communities maintain school accessibility for children, and seeks to identify clusters of children, both Lebanese and migrants, who are out of school due to economic reasons. The project will provide support to families in the form of Cash for Education and food assistance. Families will be selected via needs assessments, designed to identify out of school children, and uncover socioeconomic causes for children’s school absence. This assistance is aimed at lowering economic pressures on individual households, thereby enabling children to attend school. The cash modality cannot guarantee a return to school result, however by selecting families with socioeconomic vulnerabilities, and in coordination with other assistance modalities to ensure complementarity not duplication, the cash modality will alleviate vulnerabilities. The targeting and selection of beneficiaries maximizes the prospect of positive impact on school attendance of child beneficiaries. The project also seeks to promote home-learning, particularly given the remote-learning conditions imposed by COVID-19 restrictions. Both home-learning and access to school will be supported by provision of appropriate educational supplies to selected families and schools, with efforts also applied to formulating and providing catch-up learning to children who have suffered prolonged periods outside of school, while also helping to overcome enrolment barriers due to invalid documentation, particularly among migrant children. IOM will oversee implementation of the project which will be done directly by local educational NGOs, civil society organizations (CSOs) and community-based initiatives, demonstrating the required expertise, and with support from IOM. Selection of implementing partners is yet to be determined and will de done via consultation with sector partners.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

As part of its implementation strategy, IOM will focus on promoting resilience and sustainability as well as ensuring IOM’s programming are safe, dignified, and accessible to all. This will be achieved by ensuring women’s and girls’ voices and migrant-led initiatives are promoted during all phases of the response; enhance community-led initiatives by engagement of outreach volunteers to expand awareness of, and referrals to available services; train partners and outreach volunteers on protection, GBV core concepts, PFA, humanitarian principles, risk identification and mitigation measures, data protection, PSEA, AAP and code of conduct; support evidence-based response and learning between partners using data collected through direct assistance.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

IOM’s approach toward meeting AAP standards, will be guided by a range of frameworks and tools developed by IOM HQ, and designed to see key principals put into working practice. These frameworks cover a range of themes and issues, from operating in crisis contexts, gender equality, mainstreaming protection, and humanitarian principles. IOM’s AAP approach, will see a critical attention paid to upholding several core operational principles, including do no harm, non-discrimination, zero tolerance for sexual abuse and exploitation, data protection, principled humanitarian action, and protection.
