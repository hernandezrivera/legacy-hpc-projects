Project Summary

MEHAD (Ex: UOSSM FRANCE) is aiming to sustain the provision of multi level of health services throughout the 30 HFs that have been established in the 4 governorates of NES. To secure the functionality of all these HFs during 2023, to enhance the access to health services for all civilians in NES.
These 30 HFs are providing Primary , Secondary and Tertiary Health Services to around 256200 patients as unique beneficiaries (monthly basis). The health services are being integrated with Protection (PSS Psychosocial Support and Case management) and Nutrition services. The facilities are including:
10PHCCs-5BEMONCs-5Mobile Clinics-8Dental Clinics-4Dialysis Units-1CEMONC-2Thalassemia Units-2Blood Banks-2Central Medical Labs-1Radiology Unit-2Medical Training centers.
Covering the three main governorates in NES (ِAl-Hasakeh , Ar-Raqqa , and Deir-Ez-Zor) in addition to Al-Shahbaa area in Aleppo Governorate.MEHAD is running these30HFs since2018,covering the salaries of 660 medical and non medical staff members and the monthly running cost of the centers.All these HFs are being managed by a field coordination team in NES (50 Staff members) with direct supervision by Mid. and Senior Management team in Erbil in KRI (25 staff members).MEHAD has dedicated a quality assurance team consist of field staff from the technical medical advisory department and field staff from the MEAL department, to monitor the application of the Essential Health Services Packages under primary, Secondary, and Tertiary levels. These two team members are being supervised, trained, coached by the mirrored departments in Erbil office.Also a contingency outbreak team members in each HF consisted of ( General Practitioner GP + IPC worker + Admin office) to secure full compatibility and application of the MEHAD Cholera and COVID 19 (and any other outbreaks that may arise in the future) PRP which aligns with the WHO protocols and standards.One of these HFs in Deir-Ez-Zor Governorate is planned to be upgraded to become a Cholera Treatment Unit CTU,in addition it is important to highlight that the locations of these 30 HFs are not scattered into30different communities, some multiple HFs are stationed in the same building.
The Essential Primary Health Care services are being provided at fixed sites and extended out-reach services through mobile clinics functioning inside and outside IDPs camp;
On the other side:
Our Primary Health Care centers (PHCs) are offering preventing and curative consultations related to communicable and non-communicable disease, infection and prevention control, pediatric consultation management of common childhood communicable diseases (IMCI), reproductive health and maternity service. in addition to dental consultations.NUTRITION services:Case management and distribution of supplements; care of non-breastfed and malnourished child; CMAM according to protocol (IYCF)
Community health programme services are provided at the community level through community health worker teams who conduct households visit.The CHW services are aligned with the WHO protocols which include: Reproductive Health,Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses Infant, Young Child Feeding, CD and NCD.RH services includ:ANC-PNC care, GBV, Family planning, Maternity rooms, Mother and child care, Neonatal unit, CEmONC.On the tertiary health care level:MEHAD goal is to improve access to quality Tertiary health care services including:Hemodialysis services,Thalassemia management,Blood transfusion and its derivatives services,Radiology services.Medical Training: More than 2000 people have been trained on:Emergency Management for Injured in Wartime Course,Cardiopulmonary resuscitation Course,Emergency Echography Course,Ventilator Training Course,Emergency obstetrics and gynecology, first aid, ATLS,BLS,ACLS.
Finally:MEHAD has supported the NESHWG in finalizing EHSP for all levels.The plan in 2023 is to enroll the packages and monitor the application in coordina with NES HWG.

3 - Needs

This project is considered as sustainability for the functionality of MEHAD (Ex: UF) 30 HFs in Northeast of Syria, which have been established since 2018. The Syrian war is one of the most brutal crises worldwide and the most complicated. The Syrian Humanitarian crisis is one of the unique crises where the needs are increasing year by year and the funds is reducing. The tearing war has affected almost all the population in Syria. Causing a massive need in all humanitarian sectors. Eleven years of crisis have heavily disrupted the health system in Syria, where tens of attacks against HFs have been reported, up to 70 percent of health workers have left the country, and many others have been injured or killed. 64 percent (13.2 M) of the population lives below minimum standards for healthcare services. Forty-two administrative districts – home to 14.5 million people – are below emergency standards for hospital beds. Health personnel shortages are particularly acute. Distance, overcrowding, and long waiting times are the top barriers reported by patients to access health services. Accessibility and transport cost also impede access to services for persons with disability. The impact of these limitations was felt especially keenly once the COVID-19 struck NES on 17April2020 and Cholera in September2022. Lack of preparedness in the northeast has affected public health in general but has had a particularly devastating impact on prenatal care, child health care, and chronic diseases.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project activities are being implemented to achieve better enhanced access to health services to all vulnerable civilians in north east Syria.MEHAD is planning to sustain the implementation of all the following activities in all the 30 Health Facilities:- Provide salaries for the medical and nonmedical staff members in each health facility.- Provide running costs to cover all expenditures needed to secure the functionality of each health facility (renting of building + utilities + PPE kits + IPC materials + IEC materials for the community health + rehabilitation of the building + maintenance of medial and non medical equipment + car rental for staff movement and for patients referrals).- Procure needed medications and medical consumables for all the Health Facilities, and store them in the three central warehouses managed by MEHAD Supply chain team. Provide refreshing and updated technical medical and non medical training to all medical and non medical staff members, by the technical medical advisory team in the coordination office in NES and the management office in Erbil in KRI in Iraq. - Conduct quality assurance and monitoring monthly check, with field visits and controls by the technical medical advisory team and the MEAL team. - Document all the consultations and services provided by the health facilities to all patients through the data entry and data management utilizing the new developed EMR (Electronic Medical Report) system under the HIS managed by IM.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

MEHAD believes that having good gender equality and safeguard mechanisms is fundamental to ensure its projects' social sustainability. Therefore, MEHAD has taken many actions to develop these mechanisms at different levels: HR: Code of conduct: All teams should sign a code of conduct before starting their job. This code of conduct consists of the principles and rules that employees must respect and follow while working with MEHAD In fact, gender principles and safeguards have an essential part of this code of conduct. Recruitment: the equality of gender principles has been taken into account in all recruitment processes. Developing the job description by avoiding including the words that may be repelling qualified female applicants such as nonessential skills and qualifications and masculine terms like "assertive" or "aggressive. Interview: by including a female in the recruitment committee.Balance in the number of employees hired: MEHAD seeks to ensure a balance in terms of the percentage of female employees. According to the latest Human Resources Report of the MEHAD approximately 50% of all MEHAD employees are females.Health services: To ensure that the women patients are examined in a comfortable environment by offering enough female medical staff, and conduct refreshing and updated GBV basics and Gender Sensitivity training. To ensure the child safeguarding (especially in the HFs of pediatric health services) all medical and non medical staff members are trained.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

MEHAD HR and MEAL departments are responsible of applying the adopted PSEA Code of conduct and builds the related capacity of all staff, a dedicated compliant mechanism related to PSEA is being applied, monitored and followed up. Training: MEHAD, under all projects, has conducted GBV training for all CHW and RH teams; moreover, MEHAD started to provide PSEA training to all medical and non medical staff. Health services: To ensure that the women patients are examined in a comfortable environment by offering enough female medical staff. MEHAD provides GBV services at different levels from the community level by raising awareness regarding this issue, identifying and refer such cases at the PHC level and managing CMR cases at some BEMOC and CEMOC facilitiesBuilding the capacity of health-related staff, through providing them with the standardized trainings according to WHO and UNFPA resources. UOSSM France manages two well equipped training centers in the NES, Derick training center and Ar-Raqqa training center. Where all MEHAD medical and non medical staff are frequently oriented, refreshed and trained on all PSEA related topics. MEAHD has zero tolerance against all SEA suspected cases , and is following a strict procedures under the PSEA CoC through applying compliance procedures and investigations supervised by HR team and the Senior Management Team.
