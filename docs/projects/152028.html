Project Summary

Since the beginning of 2015, the North-East of Nigeria has witnessed an increase in violence perpetrated by the Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG), causing a major humanitarian crisis. The intensification of attacks has resulted in prolonged insecurity, exacerbating the plight of vulnerable civilians and triggering waves of forced displacement as well as violations of human rights resulting into forms of Gender-Based Violence (GBV). More 1.9 million individuals have been displaced due to the conflict and displacement continues to be a significant factor in 2018, with the highest number of displacements and returns taking place in Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe States (IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Round 23). Under this project, activities will focus on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and stand-alone protection interventions through MHPSS teams and resource centers/safe spaces, capacity building for humanitarian actors and GBV risk mitigation. . IOM’s MHPSS Mobile Teams and safe spaces will implement GBV prevention and response services in camp and camp-like settings. The proposed project aims to strengthen the existing protection and MHPSS response in the North-East, through the following areas of intervention:
i. In response to cases of GBV in camp and camp-like settings, IOM’s MHPSS Mobile Teams will provide the following array of services: survivors of GBV will be provided with Psychological First Aid (PFA) (Level 1 and 3 of IASC MHPSS Intervention Pyramid) to attend to acute needs of the individual and support long-term interventions by linking them to specialized services (legal, medical, mental health); targeted survivors of GBV will be provided with structured psychosocial counselling, either as a stand-alone service or to complement other specialized services (Level 3 of IASC MHPSS Intervention Pyramid); Survivors of GBV will be referred to specialized services for case management and dedicated follow-up as appropriate (Level 4 of IASC MHPSS Intervention Pyramid); Families of GBV survivors will be provided structured psychosocial counselling and family support sessions (Level 3 of IASC MHPSS Intervention Pyramid). IOM will ensure that safety, confidentiality, respect and non-discrimination principles are adhered to, to protect the rights and dignity of survivors and promote resilience. IOM will also maintain and ensure a survivor-centered approach through making sure that the survivor’s informed consent is obtained before any action is taken.. Referral of survivors in need of other specialized services will be conducted as to benefit from comparative advantages across the agencies. Services part of the standard and discrete service package will be delivered by the support of IOM MHPSS safe spaces. The IOM MHPSS Safe Spaces act as resource centres to the community and support the carrying out of IOM’s MHPSS activities, recreational activities, livelihood activities, lay counselling and focus group discussions.
ii. To mitigate the risk of GBV in camp and camp-like settings IOM Mobile Teams will conduct sensitization and awareness campaigns in the targeted camp and camp-like settings (Level 1 and 2 of IASC MHPSS Intervention Pyramid). Campaigns will be tailored to the contextual needs of the community, and take in to account age and gender categories as well as socio-cultural, ethnic and religious aspects. This will help increase coverage and reach across population subgroups. Moreover, campaigns will be designed in participation with the community with equal representation regardless of gender, sex and identified vulnerabilities.
iii. To build the capacities of humanitarian actors in North East Nigeria by mainstreaming protection principals in programming and GBV risk mitigation. . This will be carried out with the overall objective to improve the health, security, privacy and dignity of conflict-affected populations in displacement settings and in areas of return. With the objective and the centrality of protection in mind, IOM will seek to target humanitarian actors who deliver services in both displacement sites and areas of return.

Needs

The crisis in North-East Nigeria remains one of the most severe in the world. In the three-worst‐affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe, the insurgency has displaced 1.9 million people. Over 80 per cent of IDPs are in Borno State, the epicentre of the crisis, and over 60 per cent are living among host communities while other IDPs are living in formal and informal camps, collective settlement centres, and transitional centres and in host communities (IOM DTM Round 23). The insurgency has led to a meltdown of socioeconomic and development structures, the destruction of health facilities, lack of security, dysfunctional legal and justice systems amidst increasing safety and security concerns. Termed a protection crisis, human rights violations including GBV occurring during movements within IDP camps and other settlements are widespread. Insecurity, triggered by the insurgency and military operations against NSAG, has increased the number of displaced persons with an estimated 130,000 new arrivals in various locations since the end of October 2017. These movements present major humanitarian challenges to the already stretched resources in arrival locations. The ensuing insecurity continues to compromise safety, propagate a lack of access to essential services for the communities and hinder the capacity for humanitarians to provide much-needed life-saving support; hence the difficulty in humanitarian aid delivery. The insurgency exacerbates challenges related to the protection of women and girls. According to the HNO, at least 4,000 women and girls abducted/ kidnapped since 2013. Whilst in captivity, survivors reported being subjected to rape and forced marriage amongst other types of GBV These actions indirectly lead to reduced access to education in general and bare even worse implications for girls, already subject to minimal participation in the region. Women and girls endure negative impacts on reproductive, psychosocial as well as their general health. This group is exposed to an increased risk of sexually transmitted diseases, unsafe abortions and reproductive health challenges. Human as well as economic and social rights of women and girls are frequently violated, and there is an increased pressure on women to act as heads of households amidst high levels of poverty for these individuals in particular.An estimated 3.0 million people need protection from GBV across the conflict affected states; of these 1.1 million are IDPs, 1.0 million are returnees. According to the 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview, 920,000 care givers (of whom 80% are women) in the three most affected States are in vulnerable conditions. Widows, women headed households, single caregivers with several children, and foster parents are considered particularly vulnerable. The conflict affected population is facing additional stressors created by the displacement itself, such as family separation, difficulties and sometimes denial of access to services, limited opportunities to livelihood, breakdown of family and community support mechanisms, potential stigmatization, secondary and repeated displacement. These factors can contribute to increasing risks of exposure to GBV. Exposure to GBV risks also includes exposure to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Boys and men are at risk of forced recruitments by armed actors and at risk of arbitrary detention and exposure to different forms of violence, yet the life-saving services are not readily available in some areas. Increased access to empowerment and livelihood will mitigate the occurrence of survival sex. Thus, need for provision of continuous psychosocial support, engagement of women and girls in livelihood activities, will encourage women participation in decision making, and ensure we mitigate all risks that might predispose women and girls to GBV.

Activities

Activity 1: Deploy MHPSS teams who will provide community-based psychosocial support, GBV sensitization activities, support GBV survivors (including new arrivals) through counseling and psychological first aid and facilitate referral to specialized services including access to justice, safety and security mechanisms. Activity2: Develop and update specific GBV, MHPSS and Counter trafficking referral pathways and ensure appropriate referrals for vulnerable personsActivity 3: Safely engage vulnerable persons including survivors of GBV, trafficking in persons, mental health and extremely vulnerable care givers and non-vulnerable persons (ensure do no harm principle) in livelihood activities.Activity 4: Engage women, men, girls and boys in psychosocial activities through MHPSS mobile teams and safe space/ resource centerActivity 5: Provision of material support and facilitating referrals to relevant sectors Activity 6: Assessment and identification of community-based complaint mechanisms for enhanced community participation, engagement, and awareness on reporting on SEA, GBV, and relevant camp concerns. Activity 7 Conduct periodic observational safety audit to identify GBV risk and analyze findings to establish priorities for immediate site improvement for CCCM, WASH, PSS and Shelter in the camps. Provide support to CCCM/Shelter sector particularly to site planning, attend CCCM meetings and share protection related CCCM needs to be addressed.Activity 8: Conduct awareness raising sessions on Gender Based Violence, availability of services, SEA and trafficking in persons with the focus to increase male’s participation especially for new arrivals at reception centers. Activity 9: Develop messages and print IEC materials with culturally acceptable messages for information dissemination Activity 10: Provision of technical guidance to CCCM, NFI/shelter, site planning, WASH in integrating GBV risk mitigation. Activity 11: Conduct focus group discussions with women, men, girls and boys to ensure participation in identifying concerns affected them and findings will be shared with other service providers for response.
