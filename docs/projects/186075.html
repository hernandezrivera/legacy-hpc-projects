Project Summary

The armed conflict that started in 2016 actually affects the two English regions of Cameroon. In the south west region, the crises and CP issues are most prominent in Manyu, Meme, Fako and in Ndian divisions respectively. While some of these divisions experience very regular confrontations between parties to the conflict thereby generating CP needs, some division act to receive an influx of IDPs resulting again to different CP Issues. Priority response
Analysis of capacity, we will work with the existing capacities
1. Traditional structures (Cultural/development associations, traditional councils)
2. Church groups (Sunday school)
3. Njangi groups and other social groups.
4. Youth groups
Their original role is not to ensure child protection but they can be identified and strengthened to provide a protective environment for children and also mainstream child protection in their activities, this ensures sustainability in that, these structures will continue to function after the implementation of the project. Other structures available in these communities include government structures/services like MINAS, MINROFF etc.
How to mobilize them in the protection response?
Conduct a participatory community mapping to identify existing structures, prevailing needs. The mapping enables organizations to identify who is where, what they do and what capacities they have and need. These will include; Institutional Capacity, Local and state institution
State institutions: social workers deployed by MINAS and MINPROFF. However, these state organizations may have a challenge intervening in communities given their relationship with the government. In such a case, members of the communities like teachers, can be identified and trained to respond to the needs of children.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Generally, children affected by crises in Cameroon are particularly exposed to the risk of family separation, violence, abuse, neglect, school dropout, forced labor, and juvenile delinquency because of idleness and can easily undergo an indoctrination by the armed groups. Vulnerable families adopt harmful practices like early marriage, and other forms of exploitation as survival mechanisms to overcome their distress. Moreover, they often live in a precarious socio-economics conditions resulting in a low protected environment and a higher exposition to various risks, accentuated by the COVID-19 pandemic which bursts in Cameroon in March 2020. It comes out from interventions of the UNHCR and partners that, 157 (18,6%) in the North-West and SouthWest regions and 29 (3,4%) in urban areas, especially in Douala and in Yaounde. The highest number of case are in « other forms of violences » category (46,5%). Data collected in the context of the development of the childhood protection strategy for 2020-2023 showed that information on various forms of violences on children are not always reported by clearly disaggregating each type of violence. It comes out that a certain number of cases has been classified in this general category for lack of sufficient details to classify them in more specific categories. Regarding more specific categories, it comes out that children are primarily affected due to negligence (19,4%), followed by sexual abuse (12,5%), physical abuse (10%), emotional/psychological abuse (8%) and exploitation (3,6%). Note that amongst identified children who were confronted to protection incidents, girls (53,9%) are more affected than boys (46,1%). This reality is particularly higher in the case of sexual abuse, which concern 74,5% of girls and 25,5% of boys. Moreover, it appears that non-accompanied and separated children (orphan also) as well as out of school children with health problems or living with a handicap, are most affected by these incidents. In the North-west and South-west regions, the perpetrators of acts of abuse, neglect, exploitation, and/or of violence against children are primarily actors in the conflict (66,4%), followed by civilians (19%) the remaining percentage relating to unknown perpetrators (14%) or the wish of the children and their families not to share information in this regard (0,6%). However, it comes out that acts of physical abuse, emotional/psychological abuse and negligence are sometimes committed by the parents or guardians of children. Sexual abuse by teenagers and young men out-of-school, drinking drugs and having small trades (for example moto-taxi drivers) as well as members of the family like parents or uncles (especially for early and forced marriage cases) and neighbors. Besides, incidents of exploitation often have as perpetrators family members and owners of small trades (restaurants, egg sale, donuts, etcHence the need for community sensitization in awareness raising and building on existing community structures and education personnel as well as school children on better ways to prevent and or respond to child protection concerns.
