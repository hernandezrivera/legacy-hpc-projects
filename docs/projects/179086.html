Project Summary

High-quality Early Childhood Development (ECD) programs provide the foundation for children’s future academic success, health, prosperity, and wellbeing. A large body of scientific evidence emphasizes the need for increased investment in early childhood, particularly for children experiencing conflict, crisis, and displacement; these children are extremely vulnerable to the long-lasting effects that prolonged stress has on the developing brain.
Scientific evidence shows that the type of experiences a child has in their first five years of life plays a critical role in later development, as 85 - 90% of brain development occurs during this period, so providing developmental assessments and intervention services for young children experiencing significant adversity before they exhibit problems in their behavior or development increases their chances for more positive life outcomes and builds a foundation for the later achievement of higher-level skills. The effective programs for vulnerable, young children generate a substantial return on investment through significant reductions in the later costs of special education, grade retention, welfare assistance, and incarceration. the toxic stress changes the architecture of the developing brain leading to changes in learning language, social-emotional and cognitive skills as well as behavior in how the child develops adaptive strategies to future adversity.
Therefore, the NGO will work to target both children from 3-5 years old, as well as the caregivers, through several interventions designed to develop early childhood through crises, as in the context of the Syrian war, all Syrian children are considered negatively affected by the crisis and must intervene to help them mitigate the toxic effects of this on their development and future, Whereas Early Childhood Development is an investment in the future, and a preventive intervention. Some cities and camps that are not covered under the AS project will be targeted, with the aim of reaching the largest possible number of children and caregivers, as learning and play spaces will be established to receive children and caregivers and integrated sessions will be provided within program models called low and high contact, with a focus on play and also providing multimedia content that simulates the child's behavior and development in the psychological, social and cognitive areas. The project will take place in the three governorates: Al-Hasakah, Al-Raqqa and Deir Ezzor.
Al-Hasakeh: Al-Hasakeh city (the severity is 4) - Nawroz Camp (the severity is 4) - Tal-Kochar city (the severity is 3)
Ar-Raqqa: Ar-Raqqa City (the severity is 4)

Great reliance will be placed on partnerships to strengthen the capacities of local NGOs in the ECD sector, as there are clear weaknesses and great needs in this sector in NES.

In addition to that 20 ECD centers will be provided with a number of school furniture in order to contribute to increasing children's access to these centers and increasing the quality of the program.

4 - Needs

NES children's lives are marked by conflict and chaos. Few children have opportunities to learn and play; many are neglected; some have been exposed to extreme violence and trauma that will have a long-lasting impact on their health and future. They are at risk of “toxic stress,” a biological response to prolonged, severe adversity that disrupts a child’s brain development. Children are resilient - if we can reach them early, we can reduce this damage and have a positive influence on their lives. Research shows that nurturing care and learning can reverse the effects of toxic stress, and skills developed in early childhood last a lifetime. Beyond emergent literacy and math, these interventions will help children develop social and emotional skills necessary to succeed and cope with challenges. For instance, they will learn how to understand and manage their feelings, follow simple rules and empathize with others.Children are not the only ones who need guidance. We will also be working with parents, teachers, and caregivers to more effectively engage and support refugee children as they learn and grow.Early childhood education can help reverse the harmful impacts of early stress and trauma while restoring the possibility and hope for a brighter future.Research shows that play vital for children’s psychological, emotional, and cognitive health, and development, and. also hones the resilience they need to overcome adversity and build their futures. Early adverse experiences negatively affect the development of brain architecture, which provides the foundation for all future learning, behaviour, and health. By providing play-based learning to children in crisis we can mitigate the effects of displacement and trauma.

5 - Implementation of activities

The NGO will delivery ECD interventions in learning and play spaces through a programmatic model called low contact for both children (under 6 years old) and caregivers, which is a set of integrated sessions and activities that promote early childhood development in the four sectors: physical, social-emotional, Cognitive and language, so that these spaces are equipped with all that is necessary for these activities, especially games and are safe for the child, and a programmatic model called high contact will be presented to caregivers through home visits in order to further qualify caregivers to learn techniques for dealing with the child at this age to contribute to his development in right manner and transform the toxic and negative effects of the crisis into positive effects on the development of the child. There will be also an integrated training intervention for facilitators as well, to equip them with all the necessary tools and techniques of ECD.All of these will be supported by a robust monitoring, evaluation and learning system through a set of tools designed to monitor all activities and collect data, thus contributing to increased programming quality. Where there will be tools for registration and attendance, measuring the satisfaction of the beneficiaries, assessing the safety of spaces in terms of physical and programmatic, and assessing the performance and knowledge of the facilitators.The project will also build the capacities of local organizations in the early childhood development sector through several training workshops dedicated to this, as well as through partnerships with others.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Protection Risk: Learning facilities attacked by community members or from outside. Mitigation: To reduce the risk of military attack learning facilities will use deconfliction mechanism administered by UN-OCHA, which informs various military forces of either static humanitarian locations or humanitarian movements in Syria to ensure, to the best extent possible, learning facilities have contingency safety plans and safety protocols in place. Awareness raising and information on program will be shared to engage the community.Contingency safety plans and safety protocols should be regularly practiced by students,teachers and education personnel and parents and caregivers should be aware of plans.2- Protection Risk: ECD personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect children.Mitigation: Learning facilities have enforced code of conduct that covers the conduct of education personnel, enabling learning spaces to be a protective environment. The child safeguarding policy must be applied to all project personnel and training related to this policy conducted. Spot checks, monitoring visits, relevant training, feedback mechanisms for children administered.3- Protection Risk: Insufficient water and sanitation facilities result in an unwillingness or inability to use WASH facilities or a mixing of sexes and ages.Mitigation: Sufficient water and sanitation facilities should be provided and maintained. WASH facilities should be sex and age segregated and accessible to people with disabilities. Soap and water should be available, and facilities kept clean.4- Protection Risk: COVID outbreak and impact Mitigation: Distribution of PPE, IPC supplies, sterilization of centers, referrals

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment of women and girls’ clients by staff and volunteers. Mitigation: IEC materials for beneficiaries explaining reporting channels for beneficiaries to report PSEA and ASH. NGO adheres to a strong internal code of conduct related to prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and safeguarding beneficiaries, especially children, as well as a safe environment for reporting that allows for anonymity. Each year, all NGO staff attend a full day NGO Way Code of Conduct Training that includes discussions on NGO’s commitments to beneficiaries and NGO’s zero tolerance for sexual misconduct. NGO will work with partners utilizing their existing beneficiary mechanisms to establish common standards for beneficiary feedback and ensure protection from sexual exploitation and abuse and accountability to beneficiaries and other stakeholders. This will include capacity building activities with partners to reach these standards, as necessary. The NGO is a member of PSEA networks inside Syria and provides regular updates on the number of staff receiving PSEA training and number of complaints received through feedback and complaint channels.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Access and Security situation: Where the security situation is not completely stable, as there are always possibilities of engaging between the SDF and Syrian regime forces, as well as the Turkish army's intervention in many areas of northeastern Syria, and there are many ISIS sleeper cells that are active from time to time. The NGO's Access and Security Unit monitors all developments in the context and takes the necessary security measures such as suspending activities, evacuating or continuing under new conditions and conditions, as NGO regularly reviews and updates contingency plans for its operations inside Syria to mitigate against changes in context. This has recently been updated for NES based including multiple potential scenarios regarding changes in territorial control, continuation or interruption of cross-border access and access to locations of implementation. Risk: Community acceptance and approval of local authorities: Some interventions that do not receive community acceptance or approval from local authorities, depending on their nature and the extent to which they respond to the needs or adapt them to the cultural and social considerations of the community. At design stage, stakeholders will be involved and informed of the activities. Risk: Coordination and integration: Coordination must be made with all stakeholders to ensure that needs are met and no duplication is minimized. NGO ECD program is integrated into other program sectors. NGO joined EWG in March 2021, relationships with other NES education actors. Risk: Lack of Accountability: ECD program established a system for accountability that takes into account the nature of the program in terms of dealing with young children and caregivers.
