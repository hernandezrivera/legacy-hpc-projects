Project Summary

RI has been implementing Cash For Education (CFE) program in Bekaa since 2016 through the provision of conditional cash assistance to parents/caregivers of children in schools. The project supports retention of students in public schools through provision of homework support groups in order to enhance their educational capacities and performance in school. RI is looking to expand its successful and effective CFE program outside Bekaa, with the aim to increase access to education through a focused intervention across multiple target locations, supporting children and caregivers of vulnerable Lebanese and migrant communities in North and Bekaa regions of Lebanon. RI aims to improve education through outreach, retention, support, Psychosocial Support-Social Emotional Learning (PSS-SEL) activities and cash support to encourage participation and ongoing learning, and limit the risk of drop out and uptake of negative coping mechanisms.
Due to the continually worsening economic situation in Lebanon, Lebanese and migrant households (HHs) across the country have been pushed into severe economic difficulty. With limited financial capabilities, HHs are potentially forced to drop education as a necessary and life-saving aspect of their children’s lives due to the need to save money and/or resort to negative coping mechanisms. This can result in removing their children from education to save on expenses and/or place children in the labour market to contribute to the HHs financial needs.
RI will conduct a needs assessment in Bekaa and North regions to identify Lebanese and migrant Out Of Education Children (OOEC), including dropouts from the impact of the socio-economic crisis. RI will recruit two Education Community Supervisors and 40 Education Outreach Volunteers across Akkar, Bekaa, and North. In alignment with the outreach activity, RI will ensure awareness raising and referral on OOEC to basic services and to bring them back to education.
RI will support 1,350 students (1,242 Lebanese and 108 migrants) directly affected by the economic crisis and students’ influx from private to public schools in Bekaa by providing relevant and inclusive materials/equipment (procurement of needed stationary, books, light equipment etc.) adhering to national and international standards on Education in Emergencies (EiE).
Aligned to the education cluster strategic objectives, RI will address the educational needs of 500 Lebanese children and 100 migrant children aged 12 and below in North with CFE assistance for $22 per child per month during 2021-2022 scholastic year (October 2021 – May 2022). Cash assistance will be provided in US dollars through Bank of Beirut Finance (BoB Finance,) which is RI's current modality and service provider for its current CFE project in Bekaa.
Building on its expertise, RI’s education team will increase access to education, promote learning, and also provide essential mental health support through the provision of PSS-SEL to children and parents/caregivers alike. The proposed intervention will contribute to educational support for vulnerable Lebanese and migrant communities, and help decrease drop out and negative coping mechanisms amidst the economic crisis, reaching 600 children and their parents/caregivers and 1,350 children inside public schools. RI currently supports five education centers in the target areas of the proposed project and as such will draw on extensive experience and pre-existing relationships to reach all target areas.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

- Conduct needs assessment to identify OOE Lebanese and migrant children - including dropouts, targeting 10,000 in North, Bekaa and Akkar through a network of 40 education outreach volunteers (OVs).- Provide awareness sessions to 10,000 Lebanese and migrant parents and children on education and refer 5,000 OOE children to basic services, including education through 40 OVs and two Education Community Supervisors.- Provide critical CFE support to 250 Lebanese HHs (500 children) and 50 migrant HHs (100 children) of the most vulnerable families to enable retention in education in North and Akkar. The assistance will be provided in USD cash through RI's financial service provider Bank of Beirut Finance (ATMs are not an option as bank notes smaller than 100 USD cannot be found).- Two Social Workers will provide basic and digital literacy support to 300 parents/caregiver and support 600 children and 300 parents/caregivers with PSS-SEL, violence, bullying, online safety, and SGBV in North.- Supporting 120 vulnerable Lebanese and migrant HHs with emergency access to low cost solutions at home in North.- Support 1,350 Lebanese and migrant children and youth with age appropriate learning materials (internet recharge cards, power banks, printing materials etc.) in Bekaa.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

RI will abide by key humanitarian principles to maintain integrated protection, gender and social inclusion and ensure prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). With engagement of communities at design phase, and monitoring of implementation, RI will provide services to all, regardless of age, gender, and nationality. Essential gender and protection activities across the project will protect all vulnerable, eligible groups, such as sharing key messages on protection principles by posters, flyers, pamphlets and field staff; RI’s hotline for feedback and reporting will be visible in all target areas to report any incidents/suspected cases of violation and abuse; and feedback mechanisms will be available in languages, formats and media appropriate and accessible to all groups. Through capacity building trainings, RI and partner staff will be trained on and accountable to uphold PSEA policies and practices. In addition, RI has signed the inter-agency SOPS on PSEA.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

RI will adhere to the Interagency Standing Committee (IASC) five commitments: 1) Leadership/Governance through RI global Humanitarian Accountability guidelines that guarantee Feedback and Complaints Response mechanism (FCRM), enforce protection measures for staff and beneficiaries, and ensure a gender balance; 2) Transparency and Information Sharing through awareness sessions about types of services provided and available mechanisms for feedback in appropriate languages and accessible media to beneficiaries; 3) Participation of Beneficiaries at all levels of project cycle from different age groups, nationalities and gender; 4) Design, Monitoring and Evaluation by tracking performance Indicators, conducting data reflection meetings and evaluations including a requirement to involve affected communities; 5) Feedback and Complaints collected from all beneficiaries to ensure queries are responded on time, meeting concerns of affected populations in need of humanitarian assistance.
