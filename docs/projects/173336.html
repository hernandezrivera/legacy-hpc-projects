Project Summary

The project will ensure the provision of essential primary healthcare services to 367,558 Rohingyas (about 52% female and 55% are children) of 11 different camps (1E, 2W, 7, 8E, 9, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16 and 22) through 12 BRAC Health Facilities (5 Primary Healthcare Centres and 7 Health Posts). In 2021, BRAC has planned to establish a new primary healthcare centre in the camp 2W to fill the gap as per rationalization by ISCG Health Sector and GoB. Alongside to extend health service delivery as per recommendation from health sector one health post will be upgraded to Primary healthcare centre (PHC) in the camp 11. In addition, the structure on another ongoing PHC in the camp 8E, will be upgraded for sustainability, better work environment, infection prevention and control (IPC) and durability during monsoon and cyclone season and other potential disasters. All the health facilities will ensure desegregated facilities for women, adolescents and persons with disabilities. The project will provide essential primary healthcare services according to the essential services packages (ESP) with special focus on sexual reproductive health (SRH), maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health, non-communicable diseases and mental health psychosocial services in addition to the regular and routine services such as outpatient medical consultation, immunizations, referral services, information management, waste management and IPC, laboratory services and medicine dispense. In addition, the 5 PHCs will provide round the clock SRH services, which will contribute to increased utilization of SRH services and facilities-based deliveries. Furthermore, there will be fully equipped 24/7 ambulance services to facilitate round the clock emergency referrals. All the service providers will be trained and equipped with IPC measures.
The project will also focus to improve the health seeking behavior of rohingya community through engaging Rohingya male and adolescents (boys and girls). This project will introduce a new cadre of 60 male community health volunteers (CHVs) who will conduct community and household level awareness raising and group awareness session for men and boys, to sensitize them about the importance of SRHR, as well as health care and COVID-19 prevention. Alongside, 20 female CHVs will reach adolescent girls for the same activities.

1 - Identified Needs

In 2017, the Rohingya minority fled Myanmar seeking refuge and found shelter in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh resulting in the current presence of over 860,000 Rohingya (including 39,048 previously registered refugees). This displaced population currently lives in overcrowded shelters across 34 camps with limited access to basic services like food, water, sanitation, protection, and health care. The Rohingya community as a whole is a vulnerable group, with women, children, elderly, and people with disabilities being more vulnerable and at risk of harm. The majority of the Rohingya population are women and children (52% are women and girls; 55% children under 18). The Rohingya refugees have limited access to healthcare services, especially women and children, as their movement is restricted by males and superstition is very much prominent in this community. As a result, women sometimes face difficulties in accessing essential healthcare services, especially during pregnancy and delivery where it is estimated that 2.4 percent of the Rohingya population are pregnant and lactating. In a Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) during July-August 2020 it was found that, 12% of households have pregnant women and among them only 51% of pregnant women were enrolled in an antenatal care program. As per JRP 2020-midterm review, only 53.2% deliveries are being conducted in the health facilities. As such, a large percentage of pregnant women are without adequate ANC care and deliver at home, increasing their risk for maternal mortality and morbidity. In addition, critical gaps exits in family planning services. Long acting reversible contraceptives are not widely available, with IUD devices offered in only 43%of facilities. In addition, services for gender based violence are not yet offered at all facilities; less than 50% of primary healthcare centres offer comprehensive GBV medical services. Coverage of Pentavalent 3 is only 67% among children under 1 year in the Rohingya community, whereas the target is >95% and coverage of measles-2 vaccination is only 25%. According to the Early Warning, Alert and Response System the major infectious diseases with the highest proportional morbidity reported in the Rohingya refugee camps are: acute respiratory infection (18.6%), acute watery diarrhea (4.6%), suspected varicella (4%), unexplained fever (3.9%) and other diarrhea (2.6%). Furthermore, the J-MSNA found that 42% of households reported that they had at least one person with an illness serious enough to require medical treatment.IOM (2018) report on mental health and psychosocial wellbeing in the Rohingya camps found that 74% adults reported feeling always sad, 64% always tense and 48% always nervous. Among children, 50% are always sad; 50% always tense and 58% always nervous. Hypertension is highest among non-communicable diseases among the Rohingya, followed by COPD and DM.The host community of Ukhiya and Teknaf upazilas of Cox’s Bazar, face their own challenges and as one of the most lagging districts in the country, has been further impacted by the refugee influx. Bangladesh Demographic Health Survey (2017) reports: health seeking behaviours such as ANC, PNC and institutional delivery rates are lower than the national average. The use of modern family planning methods in Cox’s Bazar is 45% and unmet need for family planning is 18%, compared to the national rate of 52% and 12%, respectively; 91% mothers receive one ANC and 36% mothers receive four or more ANCs compared to the national average of 82% and 47% respectively in Chottogram Division. With the onset of COVID-19, the already weakened healthcare system is more at risk of widespread infection transmission in the camps and host community and women, children, transgender persons and persons with disabilities will face more barriers in accessing health services particularly sexual, reproductive and maternal health services.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Provision of essential primary health care services: In the year 2021 BRAC will keep operation of its existing 11 Health facilities in the camp (3 Primary Healthcare Centre and 8 health Posts). These facilities will provide primary health care services according to the essential primary health care services package (ESP) with special focus on sexual & reproductive health, maternal; neonatal; child health; and adolescent health, non-communicable diseases and mental health and psychosocial services in addition to the regular and routine services such as outpatient consultations, inpatient care (normal delivery, observation of diarrhoeal patients and minor injuries), EPI, nutrition counselling to pregnant women and mother of under 5 children, family planning, referral services for complicated cases, information management, waste management and infection control, tuberculosis and malaria control screening and treatment.2. Upgrade and establishment of health facilities: In 2021, BRAC has planned to establish a new primary healthcare centre in the camp 2W to fill the gap as per rationalization by ISCG Health Sector and GoB. Alongside to extend health service delivery as per recommendation from health sector one health post will be upgraded to Primary healthcare centre (PHC) in the camp 11. In addition, the structure on another ongoing PHC in the camp 8E, will be upgraded for sustainability, better work environment, infection prevention and control (IPC) and durability during monsoon and cyclone season and other potential disasters.3. Capacity Building: Ensure the continuous capacity building of the health team on different subjects with special focus on SRH, child care, adolescent SRH and newborn care, NCDs, MHPSS including but not limited to workshops, training, capacity building activities, OJT, mentorship programs and providing technical assistance to ensure high quality of care in the facilities and community.4. Community outreach activities: The project will also address to improve the health seeking behavior of rohingya community through focusing Rohingya male and adolescents (boys and girls). This project will introduce a new cadre of 60 male community health volunteers who will conduct community and household level awareness raising and group awareness session for men and boys, to sensitize them about the importance and needs of SRHR, as well as health care and COVID-19 prevention. Alongside 20 female Community health Volunteers will reach adolescent girls for the same activities. 5. Disaster preparedness and response (Including Monsoon Preparedness): To mitigate the risk of outbreaks and the potential disruption of health services during the potential disasters BRAC has already upgraded the health facilities to be more disaster ready; has mobile medical teams and trained them on first aid, mass casualties, and psychological first aid; also BRAC will ensure the appropriate requisition of drugs and medications, and will also create oral rehydration therapy corners in every health facility in case of a potential AWD outbreak.6. Community feedback and improvement of health services: The project will take community feedback through exit interview and community consultation and addres the recommendations to improve the health servcie delivery from each health facilities (PHC and HP)7. Reporting: The project will collect daily and monthly service data disaggregating age, sex and disabilities and report to BRAC MIS, Health sector and DHIS2
