Project Summary

The project aims to provide Shelter support and integrated WASH services leading to dignified and safer living conditions for the most vulnerable IDPs in conﬂict-affected areas of Afrin, Salquin and Dana district, reaching a total of 8832 conflict-affected individuals. Through the provision of dignified shelter and its companion infrastructure, improvement of the site to reach a better-dignified living conditions.
Other integrated services will be provided in the same sit, which will be submitted for different clusters.
The activities are designed based on the need assessment in each location based on community consultation and discussed with the individuals and key stockholders. IHR adopted the integrated approach in project design.
The activities are as follows:
1. Installation of better-dignified shelters with the site's infrastructure. This activity will help 200 HHs in Bawabat Afrin camp. This type of shelter will secure adequate space and more dignity and privacy. This activity will ensure midterm sustainability for protection from weather factors and floods
The design of the dignified shelter units and infrastructure were selected according to the SNFI guides 2022 and by consultation with WASH and CCCM clusters and with active NGOs in the same locations to ensure the project is integrated and IDPs resilience increased.
2. Providing essential infrastructure rehabilitation for 9 IDP camps in Afrin, Salqin and Dana districts (roads, drainage system) 8.832 individuals will be targeted.
3. Installation of solar lighting poles in camps to enhance safety and improve the living conditions in the same location
IHR will work closely with SNFI cluster and other I/NGO which provide other services in the same locations to achieve the highest integrations in delivering services.

3 - Needs

According to the shelter cluster, CCCM cluster, and HNO reports, and to have a full overview and better understanding of the needs of all the population groups in targeted locations, a rapid need assessment was conducted by IHR staff. The results of these assessments can be summarized by: 1-- Because of the harsh winter season and based on the need to increase access to the camps, it is recommended to improve the roads out/in the IDP camps by laying layers of gravel and damping it well to make a solid surface resist the weather’s conditions 2- According to the nature and muddy land of the camps sites and as a result of heavy rain where floods form and wash out the tents, it is highly recommended to establish a drainage system inside the camps to protect the tents and roads from flood and keep the access active in all situations. 3- Since the existing tents are directly established on the farmland, it is recommended to elevate the ground of the tents to secure good insulation and good protection from the rain. 3- The existing type of shelter units are not suitable for a dignified life, lacking and poor for the minimum requirements for living conditions, no privacy, and no protection from weather conditions or from outside actors; all inhabitants reported the acute need to improve this type of shelter to match the minimum living standards. 4- in the absence of lighting in the IDP camps, a lighting system is recommended to provide protection for women

4 - Implementation of activities

The main planned activities to be implemented in this project will be as follows:• First rehabilitation of 7 km of roads inside 9 IDP camps • Second, establishing drainage systems in 9 IDP camps.• Third: installation of 200 shelter units to better-dignified shelters made from Carbon Reinforced Organic Polymer (CROP) blocks element for walls, (CROP) roof elements for the roof, PVC for the windows and internal doors, iron for the external door, ceramic for the toilet and washbasin, marble for kitchen sink.• Fourth installing lighting systems in 9 IDP camps. IHR, through coordination with the shelter cluster, CCCM cluster, and local authority, will implement this project in the proposed targeted locations; the implementation of the activities will be through local vendors and suppliers, the workers will be from the targeted locations, this will give the project advantage to contribute to the income of the IDPs in the camps. The IHR team will share the project information with the IDPs before starting through the project staff and project IDs as a type of community engagement. The technical engineers in the field will work closely with the suppliers to ensure good quality of the implementation,M&E team will conduct regular monitoring of all activities to ensure all is going well, and weekly reports from the program and M&E will be sent to the main office in Turkey, the senior project manager in HQ will follow the implementation.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Under the current context, the most vulnerable categories, such as women-headed HH, PwD, may be excluded from accessing the service due to socio-cultural barriers. Likelihood: medium, Impact: High Mitigation Measures. a)IHR increases visibility for self-referral to ensure the inclusion of the most vulnerable individuals. b) IHR applies a scoring system to all selected beneficiaries in order to categorize them according to their vulnerabilities. Protection Risk: The high target of the project may lead in some cases to reduce the quality of the service. Likelihood: low, Impact: medium. Mitigation Measures: a) Prioritization of safety and dignity and avoiding causing harm: in IDP sites/camps, the program and MEAL teams constantly monitor the quality of the assistance delivered, ensuring that only quality activities are provided. b) IHR has a strong CRM in place that is regularly advertised to local communities, currently, due to Covid-19 prevention measures, beneficiaries, and non-beneficiaries can register any feedback/complaints via IHR’s WhatsApp number. Protection Risk: The blanket approach of the program limits the possibility of providing tailored service to persons in Need. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: MediumMitigation Measures: IHR coordinate with protection actors to enhance referral pathways and ensure providing the service based on the needs of beneficiaries.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The IHR's Code of Conduct and Associated policies are consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Risks related to this project have been identified in a separate multi-sectoral strategy with appropriate mitigation measures identified. The latter includes, but is not limited to, the appointment and training of PSEA focal points in all project sites and offices and trained with supported from the Syria PSEA network. It also includes the use of the PSEA networks developed checklists for the observation of operations to identify, prevent and mitigate potential SEA and SEA risk factors in ongoing field activities.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks- Further escalation of the conﬂict, or deterioration in the security situation, in NW as a result of any of the following: -Escalation in the ongoing conflict between SDF and Government of Turkey or GoS, extending beyond the current areas of operation. -IS resurgence with extension of attacks and activities to Afrin and surrounding areas. -Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conﬂict, jeopardising access to affected project areas. -Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations. -COVID-19 pandemic escalates, new lockdowns restrict access. Assumptions- This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the program accordingly. There will be no signiﬁcant shifts in control over key locations in Afrin district - This project will be able to access beneﬁciaries, assess their speciﬁc needs and implement the program accordingly - Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and goods are available locally/regionally. Mitigating Measures- Strengthen operational resilience. In the event of increased insecurity in an area, shift operations to nearest unaffected base of operations. -Flexibly shifting modalities as required depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and availability of key goods. -COVID-19 SOPs, activity level SOPs. -Build local capacity and staff
