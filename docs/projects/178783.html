Project Summary

Through the proposed intervention, SIF will contribute to achieving objectives of the 2021 Protection Sector’s Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Syria through providing psychosocial support (PSS) to children, including the identification of cases facing protection risks, their access to specialized services and case-management. The project will also include awareness raising on a high range of protection concerns such as GBV, general protection, child protection (CP) and risk education (RE) toward explosive hazards. The proposed intervention will be part of an integrated programme which will consist in providing both CP and Education assistance in centres and public schools given that both sectors’ needs are huge and intertwined in the targeted localities.
The target intervention will focus on the age-group from 12-18, SIF will tailor awareness sessions aiming to meet their development needs specifically. Youth and adolescents will be involve in all project phases.
Children with disabilities will be also involved in the activities that fit to their disabilities. specialized staff will be appointed for inclusion.
The project will be implemented in Dara’a, Rural Damacus, Hama, Homs, Al- Hassakeh, Dier- Ez-zor and Aleppo Governorate. The districts and sub districts in these governorate are selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and SIF field assessments. SIF has ongoing child protection and education programmes in the target locations with 10 schools in Dara’a governorate, 4 schools and 2 centres in Hama governorate, 4 schools, 3 centres in Homs governorate and 2 centres in Aleppo Governorate. This project will aim to scale up these interventions.
SIF will consider and ensure gender-and-age aspects across all sub-sectors of focus – CP, GBV, and general protection issues. Cultural preferences and sensitivities will be considered through consultations with the staff – the majority of whom will be from the local community – and the affected populations. Specific needs of girls and boys, people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups will be considered in the design of the intervention, and will be continuously evaluated throughout the implementation.
Participatory approach will be adopted in all phases of the project cycle including design, planning, implementation and the monitoring phase. The MEAL team will set up a complaints and feedback mechanism. The project will be monitored and evaluated programs to incorporate inputs from stakeholders. Covid prevention measures will be applied during the implementation of the programme. PPE kits will be provided to staff, facilitators and beneficiaries along with maintaining social distancing. Where possible online sessions will be conducted in case of wider spread of the virus.

4 - Needs

Syria is enduring a protracted humanitarian crisis as a result of ten years of ongoing conflict and insecurity, displacements and damaged infrastructure coupled with the deterioration of the economic situation and the spread of the Covid 19 Pandemic. From the beginning of the crisis Dara’a, Hama, Homs and Aleppo governorates witnessed significant air strikes in addition to heavy artillery shelling targeting residential areas and vital facilities such as hospitals or schools, resulting in mass displacements and destruction. Children of all ages face violence and protection concerns. Girls and boys have been exposed to protection risks, ranging from lack of access to documentation, psychosocial distress, early marriage and gender-based violence (GBV) to physical disability, injury or death. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview Syria 2021, the Protection sector has reported a total of 13.1 million people in need with 6.7 million females, 6 million children and 3.2 million people with disabilities who are facing protection crisis in Syria.During SIF’s field assessment, severe issues of GBV were reported, though cases remain under-reported and difficult to identify due to the absence of case management services and societal constraints. High prevalence of child labour is preventing school attendance. Psychosocial distress caused by children’s exposure to violence in their daily lives, community, schools and homes, along with repeated displacements, loss of or separation from family along with divisions in their community, and lack of basic social services is profoundly affecting children’s well-being and development of girls and boys. Unexploded ordinance remains a serious problem till date.

5 - Implementation of activities

Child protection (CP) assistance will be directly implemented by SIF in all the targeted areas of intervention and will be complementary to SIF’s WASH and Education programme. Assistance will be provided through three different intervention modalities, depending on the context and the needs identified. (1) Public schools: child protection activities will be implemented during the weekends and holidays in public schools, in coordination with SARC, DoE and MoE. (2) Integrated service centers: child protection interventions will be implemented in centers established by SIF, 5 days per week in the absence of sufficient and suitable public facilities in these targeted areas. In these centers, children will be provided with both non-formal education and community based child protection services. (3) Outreach Volunteers will be deployed in order to reach the population of surrounding villages.Community facilitators (50% women, 50% men) will be trained by SIF in risk education, child protection minimum standards and psychosocial support. SIF will ensure participation of adolescence and youth in all phase of the project. SIF will ensure daily monitoring on the project progress to ensure that standards of project implementation and fund resources are being effectively implemented. Participatory approach will be undertaken in all stage including project design, implementation and monitoring phase. Discussions will be gender-disaggregated and conducted by women facilitators when necessary. Pre and post-tests will be conducted to evaluate the impact of the awareness and training sessions, while the project implementation will be monitored according to the work plan and program indicators.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Perceptions that assistance is delivered in a discriminatory or non-equitable manner leading to tensions. Likelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation: (1) selection of localities based on the Protection sector severity scale analysis, (2) community consultations to ensure risk of discrimination is minimized and processes are transparent and informed (3) coordination with other service providers (4) prioritization of services to groups considered particularly vulnerable, keeping the selection of beneficiary transparent (5) monitoring and feedback during and after service provision develop schedules Risk 2: Community reluctance towards protection services, stigmatization and violence against victims of GBV Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: (1) gender-balanced staffing; men and boys participating in awareness sessions on GBV (2) case management in coordination with other services; data protection policy ensuring confidentiality; child-friendly room ensuring confidentiality and privacy during case management and counselling (3) careful assessment of risk before taking any decision always respecting the principles of do no harm and the best interests of the child (5) identify solutions that are seen as culturally acceptable to the family or community and that uphold the rights of children.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Not getting approvals from national security to enter the area. Likelihood: Low. Impact: high. Mitigation: SIF will target easy-to-reach areas and establish a constant dialogue and follow-up with the authorities. Risk 2: Risk of increased prices due to heavy inflation. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: (1) SIF has already accounted for the increased inflation rate in the budget, (2) in case of continuous inflation SIF will adjust the BoQs accordingly. Risk 3: The security situation deteriorates and effects the implementation of the activities. Likelihood: low. Impact: HighMitigation: (1) regular monitoring of the security situation by SIF Logistics and Security Manager (2) ensure updated contingency plans are in place at field and coordination level (3) ensure the preparedness of SIF team through regular briefings and regular updatesRisk 5: Risk of Spread of SAR Covid 19 virusLikelihood: High Impact HighMitigation: (1) SIF will train staff on Covid preventions measures. (2) Children sensitized on the Covid Situation and briefed on prevention measures, (3) maintain social distance, (4) provision and access to PPE kit. (5) Adopting alternative methodology to in case of wider spread of the virus like conducting online session. Risk 6: Risk of fraud and misuse of resourcesLikelihood: MediumImpact: HighMitigation: (1) Ensure that community based complain mechanism are at place to report any fraud. (2) Close monitoring by MEAL department (3) Random spot checks (4) Ensure procurement procedures are transparent and in life with SIF and donor requirement (5) Ensure SIF’s Anti-Fraud policy is strictly followed to detect, prevent and address fraud.
