Project Summary

The project overall objective is to improve food security and livelihood diversification of vulnerable communities and Host communities in Khartoum State – Oumbada locality and White Nile State- AjJableen locality.
The economic downturn, outbreak of disease and COVID-19 has had huge impact to livelihood of vulnerable community. Most vulnerable families with low incomes, whose income depends on daily work were hugely affected by the Covid-19 first wave lockdown measures in Khartoum. Currently, most of these households are still experiencing food shortages coupled with the deteriorating economy. .
The targeted communities in the two states have limited livelihood skills and opportunities, unemployment especially, youth. There is also instability and the poor rainfall season has on own food production accompanied by deterioration of irrigation canals. These shocks have hugely impacted on the livelihoods of targeted communities thus increasing their vulnerability.
The project will target 2500 households –(1249 Male and 1251 Female ) total 12,500 individuals, that is (500 families in Khartoum and 2000 in White Nile. The project targeted participants shall be vulnerable individuals from the host communities, internally displaced persons facing severe food insecurity due to limited accessibility and availability of adequate, sufficient, and nutritious food. Small holder farmers facing major constraints in farming because of high costs of production, post-harvest losses, access to farmlands, markets, and information shall be targeted by the project.
The project intervention includes agricultural support (Seeds and Tools), Livestock Support (Treatments, Kits), Formation and Training of Savings and Internal Lending Communities (SILC groups)Formation and training of savings and loan groups and supporting them with income generation kits and vocations training support for women. Project monitoring shall include field visits, partners update, and progress report. An internal mid-term review will also form part of the monitoring system, and this will also provide an opportunity for the team and communities to reflect and adapt the adaptive monitoring system.
The team will capture unanticipated as well as planned changes and this will be planned methodically (each quarter) by the team and involve community members, government officials and other key stakeholders. These will be captured through case studies and stories of change by using narrative photos and videos

1 - Needs

Sudan HNO 2023 stated that after one year of political and military unrest in Sudan, there are record-high levels of humanitarian needs throughout Sudan, and it is uncertain how the three-year-old transition to a new Sudan will go. Compared to the previous year, there are now around 2 million more people who are food insecure. A third of the population, or 15.8 million people, are predicted by humanitarian partners to require aid by 2023. The number is the most since 2011. Around 8.7 million of the persons in need of assistance are children under the age of 18. Sudan FSL/ HRP 2023 key finding that, acute food insecurity in Sudan is escalating rapidly because of sharp rises in the cost of food and other commodities, low harvest, and ongoing conflict crises.
