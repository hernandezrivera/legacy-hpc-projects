Project Summary

Through the proposed project, UNFPA will invest in empowering youth, especially young girls aiming to enhance their resilience, by building their personal and social assets that can promote their positive development and enable them to reach their full potential. The Project will also contribute to the wellbeing of the young people’s families and communities, enabling them to have access to livelihood opportunities and social participation by building the capacity of youth to take the lead and the responsibility to identify and prioritize their needs as well as the community needs. Specifically, the Project will focus on supporting youth female headed households for business startups and provision of seed funding to support them in leading entrepreneurship projects.
Two youth friendly spaces will be established in Rural Damascus and Hama. A set of activities will be provided at the center aiming to promote young people’s wellbeing, leadership, participation and economic empowerment. The project will be implemented in Rural Damascus and Hama, the communities selected based on the 2022 severity scale and on the fact that they are priority return destinations and accessible to UNFPA IPs.
The project will follow a gender transformative and gender equality approach, as per existing IASC guidelines. More so, the interventions in this proposal are in line with the “Compact for Young People in Humanitarian Action” launched at the World Humanitarian Summit (2016). UNFPA promotes efforts to put young people at the center of humanitarian action and does so through key actions as follows:
1) On services: Promoting and increasing age- and gender-responsive and inclusive programmes that contribute to the protection, health and development of young women, young men, girls and boys within humanitarian settings
2) On participation: supporting systematic inclusion, engagement and partnership with youth, in all phases of humanitarian action, through sharing information and involvement in decision-making processes.
3) On capacities: Recognizing and strengthening young people’s capacities and capabilities to be effective humanitarian actors by their active participation in the community and livelihood opportunities.
4) On resources: increasing resources intended to address the needs and priorities of adolescents and youth affected by humanitarian crises and identify ways to more accurately track and report on the resources allocated to young people in humanitarian contexts.
UNFPA is committed to invest in youth and emphasizes the rights of adolescents and the need to increase their potential to contribute to positive social transformation. UNFPA is mandated to address the needs of young people that are considered life saving for their future well-being, and work on projects that will contribute to empower young people and prevent them from participating in harmful practices that affect their decisions, health and future.

3 - Needs

In 2023 the estimated number of people in need of humanitarian assistance is reached 15.3 million of which the majority are women, children, adolescents and youth (aged 10 - 24). The protracted crises continue to negatively impact the socio economic conditions in Syria. According to ESCWA, it is estimated that more than 90 per cent of the population lives below the poverty line. Around 50 percent of Syrians have no jobs, which affects their overall wellbeing and social engagement. In the absence of decent job and livelihood opportunities, more and more people living in Syria are resorting to harmful coping mechanisms including gender based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse and early and forced marriage. The crisis has led to a significant change in the role of women, including female youth, in society and many of them became the only bread winners in their families. Limited participation in community life and decision processes from females and young people is observed across the country. Between 50 and 55 per cent of the respondents declared that the one taking decisions on behalf of the community are “senior males” . A decade of the crisis has placed a mental strain on Syrians with increasing reports of stress and mental health concerns, particularly among youth. In a conflict and protracted crisis setting like Syria, youth can play a special role in building social cohesion in their communities.

4 - Implementation of activities

In cooperation with its partners and related UN agencies, UNFPA is planning to effectively link economic empowerment activities to the opportunities in the local market in Rural Damascus and Hama through the requirements to strengthen a suitable environment that is needed to enable young people to start up their own business. UNFPA is working on expanding the coverage of support to youth related services, while ensuring gender mainstreaming and gender equality and to effectively increase the resilience of young people by providing them with access to opportunities to exercise leadership and participate in recovery, humanitarian action and in sustaining peace. The proposed interventions are aligned and integrated with UNFPA SRH and GBV programmes and will include: establishment of two youth friendly spaces in Rural Damascus and Hama; SRH/GBV educational sessions; building capacity on life skills, community needs assessment techniques and prioritization and; supporting young girls’ social participation. The Project will also and, include the provision of financial support to 20 youth led initiatives addressing protection issues; build the capacity of 100 young people on business startups and provision of seed funding to support 60 entrepreneurship projects; establishment of career counseling clinics and provision of psychosocial support and recreational sports competitions and musical events.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will follow the UN Sustainability Framework of Do No Harm and Do Good approach. The project emphasizes a gender-responsive approach in humanitarian setting. Agreement will be made with the implementing partners (IPs), that the project will have to be initiated ensuring that young people will be selected in a transparent process with the involvement of the concerned authorities from the initiation of the project until its evaluation. It will also be agreed, that in case of any signs of escalation, both UNFPA and the IPs will be consulting with the local communities on how to proceed further with a context relevant response. Based on the results of the consultation, the project activities will be shifted to meet the new requirements. The project will be implemented in close involvement and participation of the local communities to 1) assess the needs, 2) undertake prioritization and 3) ensure equal access to those in needs. Finally, the IP will have to share on a regular basis the achievements of the activities and report on them.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNFPA enhances the prevention and mitigation of protection risks through mainstreaming the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) into all activities, where UNFPA’s partners integrated PSEA policy into the code of conduct for the facilities staff. Risks: - The deteriorating economic situation inside Syria has led to more human rights abuses, including SEA. - Ongoing conflict enables a context where SEA cases are inadequately addressed. Mitigation: - UNFPA organizes specific trainings on PSEA for staff and IPs - UNFPA conducts PSEA assessments of all its IPs and supports them in developing and validating a PSEA policy. - UNFPA has established a hotline to report any SEA related incidents. - UNFPA has a zero tolerance policy with regards to SEA. - UNFPA ensures that implementing partners apply the following: 1. Embed PSEA into policies, contracts, and code of conduct; in the health facilities UNFPA ensures that all HFs staff are aware and signed the codes of conduct. 2. Embed PSEA into MEAL toolkits; UNFPA is committed to integrating the PSEA and protection mainstreaming components into the design of all monitoring tools.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The major risks might be encountered during the project implementation: • Delay in obtaining official approvals to start the activities in the selected two locations. UNFPA will discuss with the donor the possibility of changing the locations.• The high cost of transportation might hinder youth’s access to the youth friendly space. UNFPA will discuss with the selected IP the potential for giving transportation allowance for beneficiaries. • Financial Risk due to the exchange rates fluctuations might affect achievement of targets. Close monitoring, guidance and consultation will be done with the IP to ensure the optimal use of the project financial resources
