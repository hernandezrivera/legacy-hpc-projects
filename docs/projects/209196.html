Project Summary

Following the multiple crises that affected Sudan since February 2003, and in response to the need for accurate information on displacement and the identification of beneficiaries for the delivery of humanitarian aid, the International Organization for Migration (IOM) began its Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) program in 2004 to support the Government of Sudan (GoS) and its humanitarian partners in establishing an accurate data collection system and providing data on displacement in the areas most affected by the conflict and natural disasters. DTM is a system to track and monitor displacement and population mobility. It is designed to regularly and systematically capture, process and disseminate information to provide a better understanding of the movements and evolving needs of displaced populations, whether on site or en route. Data collection strategies and tools are based on DTM global methodology but have been adapted to reflect the context and population movements specific to Sudan. Since the start of the current conflict that erupted in April 2023, between Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), there has been an increase in the destruction of key infrastructure in affected areas, significant displacements and increase in morbidity and mortality, and disruption of humanitarian activities across Sudan. Despite the notable limitations to DTM's logistical operations and the connectivity and telecommunication challenges, DTM has been able to maintain key data collection operations, releasing information products in as little as 24 hours after the start of the initial clashes on 15 April 2023. DTM is operational in 180 of Sudan’s 189 localities across all 18 states (90% of the country) and will continue to provide detailed updates on displacement in Sudan which currently has the largest displacement caseload in the world.
The methodology and modalities for the data collection in 2024 will be flexible to the needs of the context but can include: (1) Mobility Tracking data collection exercise to gather vulnerable population's data baselines; (2) Early Warning Flash Alerts to update the humanitarian community of recent or potential incidents of displacement; (3) Emergency Event Tracking to provide the humanitarian community with datasets relating to sudden incidents of displacement; (4) Weekly Displacement Snapshots to update the humanitarian community with timely information on displacement context post 15 April 2023; (5) Monthly Displacement Overviews to provide the humanitarian community with comprehensive indicators on displaced caseloads. (6) Situation Assessments to provide information on displaced caseloads in non-emergency context, where possible. (7) Integrated Location Assessment to provide the humanitarian community with information on access to services at major sites of displacement. (8) Return Index to be provide critical information on living conditions in IDP locations which support returns of displaced communities, where feasible. DTM will also continue to provide the humanitarian community with routine information on rapid incidents that affect displacement, population mobility, shelter typology, return intentions, and priority needs as well as population baseline estimates and bespoke information products. Given the pressing emergency context, DTM is best placed to provide essential and evidence-based information and forms a core component of IOM’s ongoing response to the unfolding humanitarian crisis.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IOM's DTM activities are conducted with strict adherence to IOM's data protection principles and international best practices – see data protection manual and principles https://publications.iom.int/system/files/pdf/iomdataprotection_web.pdf. The exercises draw on IOM's global expertise in conducting similar activities to ensure that information is non-identifiable, stored securely and only accessible, by request, by approved partners. Furthermore, DTM is committed to collecting information on age and sex breakdown and vulnerabilities of target populations per location of assessment, which provides a foundation and insight to the demographic and specific gender nuance for protection focused programming. IOM will also use male and female enumerators from target locations possessing experience in collecting information and language skills to support data collection exercises for persons with specific needs and make key informants, IDPs and returnees more comfortable to provide information. IOM supports the Information Management Working Group (IMWG) to collect data for all clusters including but not limited to WASH, ES/NFI, Health, and Protection to support collection on information for specific needs and use data for cluster and inter-cluster response. DTM teams are also trained on PSEA and protection to ensure appropriate data collection including identifying needs and gaps in service provision and supporting referrals.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The basis of DTM work is interviews with key informants and conversations with displaced populations. During these conversations and interviews, informants are offered the chance to describe their experiences, needs and priorities in their own words. The insights provided by affected populations are the primary data used by DTM to inform partners and ensure that humanitarian work is accountable to the voices and needs of displaced populations. In addition, enumerators share their contact information to enable informants to give feedback on DTM processes. Enumerators are trained in referral pathways so that, if an informant needs assistance, they are positioned and trained to support their needs through the appropriate channels. For information product users, DTM includes contact information on all products released, encouraging product users to contact DTM with feedback and suggestions. As of November 2023, DTM introduced a user feedback survey on the DTM Sudan website to gather feedback on how subscribers use DTM data, their perceptions of DTM information products and suggestions for improvement.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

DTM data relies on key informant interviews and conversations with displaced individuals to better understand their needs and priorities. This information is then analyzed at the population level and presented through DTM products for humanitarian organizations to use to ensure that the work that they are doing is meeting the needs of, and accountable to the populations they serve. As such, DTM is an inter-agency accountability mechanism for the humanitarian response, in addition to IOM's internal accountability to affected populations (AAP) mechanisms and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) standards. IOM has a zero-tolerance policy on SEA, which is in line with the UN policy, and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA, as well as guidelines from IOM's Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV in crises. IOM staff will receive refresher training on how to report SEA and ethical and appropriate ways to handle a GBV disclosure and make safe referrals through existing referral pathways. These guidelines will also be included and highlighted to enumerators to ensure compliance.
