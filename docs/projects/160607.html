Project Summary

The Project aims at reducing the exposure to and ensure timely protection from violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation for at-risk IDP, returnees and host community children and their families through enhanced access to child protection services in Lakes, Jonglei, Eastern and Central Equatoria States, South Sudan.
The project will provide life-saving child protection prevention and response services through provision of Comprehensive case management services and referrals for children with critical protection concerns (750 boys and 750 girls); Psychosocial Support (PSS) activities (4000 boys, 4000 girls, 800 men and 1600 women); Training of staff, volunteers and Government officials on CP (168 men and 168 women); Child protection awareness raising and outreach (5500 men, 6500 women,7000 boys, 8400 girls) and Strengthening of community-based child protection mechanisms to prevent and respond to child protection concerns (132 men and 132 women. In total, the project will target 40,040 most vulnerable individuals (13,150 girls, 11,750 boys, 8,470 women and 6,670 men). The project will be implemented Plan’s existing field locations of Eastern Equatoria (Kapoeta East); Lakes (Rumbek Centre and Rumbek East); Central Equatoria (Yei) and in Jonglei (Pibor). The project will target IDPs, returnees as well as vulnerable host communities. Plan International will coordinate closely with the Child Protection Sub-Cluster and UNICEF in implementing this project. Further, in the different field locations, Plan will work closely with other child protection partners (Save the Children, DRDA, TDH, IPCS, GREDO) to avoid overlapping and duplication of efforts. For instance, will work in different payams or bomas from other CP actors in the target locations. Plan will also coordinate with CP partners through referrals of CP cases.

1 - Needs

According to the HNO (2020) over 2.5M people(IDPs and refugees are estimated to be in dire need of humanitarian protection and assistance of which about 2M are children. Despite the revitalized peace process, UN reports indicate that all parties to the conflict have repeatedly violated international humanitarian law and perpetrated serious human rights abuses, including gang rape, abductions, sexual slavery of women and girls, and recruitment of children, both girls and boys. At least 2,700 verified incidents of grave violations against children affecting over 81,000 children have been reported (UNICEF). UNICEF also estimates that about 19,000 children are currently recruited into armed forces/groups. The ongoing fighting and the resulting displacement continue to separate families, with an estimated 12,000 children in need of family tracing and reunification. Gaps in psychosocial support structures for children and caregivers persist due weakened families’ ability to cope with protracted crisis and sudden shocks as well as breakdown local protective mechanisms. There are an unmet needs regarding the social and emotional wellbeing of children and caregivers with approximately 1 million children being in psychosocial distress (HNO). About 17,125 children, of which 9,076 are girls and 8,049 are boys, are still in need of family tracing and reunification (CPIMS). Gender Based Violence (GBV) is widespread. Although the full extent of gender based violence (GBV) is unknown, there is a large amount of evidence showing that GBV is an ongoing risk for women and girls throughout the country. According to UNICEF, nearly 25% of documented incidents of conflict-related sexual violence affect children. Landmines and unexploded ordnance pose constant threats to the civilian population. In Pibor, inter- communal violence has exposed the Pibor communities to multiple vulnerabilities with limited access to basic services. Age-set fighting is common place. Girls face the risk of child marriage and early booking as cultural practice and adolescent boys and male youths continue being at risk of recruitment into the armed groups and forces to protect their communities. 75% of FGDs (Plan, March 2018) held reported the existence of children engaged in paid work. Community members have limited knowledge and capacity to provide a protective environment for children. Pibor is experiences climatic shocks (flooding) during rainy season (June- Dec) causing population displacement and migration. Implementation and monitoring of activities is very challenging during the rainy season. PLAN has been implementing CM and the current case load (Dec 2018 till September 2019) of children in need of CM services is 175 (84G, 91B) with 2 cases on UASC. In Yei, PLAN conducted a multi-sectorial needs assessment in Nov. 2018 and Feb. 2019 covering 9 IDP centres located in Yei Town focusing CPiE, SGBV prevention and response for children, EiE, and FSL. The main CP issues were presence of UASC (at least 30); CAFAAG - many children among the IDP population had been forced to integrate with armed groups; threat of SGBV - including physical violence, sexual violence, and CEFM - was the form of violence that concerned adolescent girls the most. The findings also showed that children were involved in paid work in Yei town with more girls doing the work compared to boys. In the assessment, 92% of girls reported that they had experienced distress compared to 67% of boys. In Lakes State has been hosting over 164,384 IDPs from Jonglei State since the December 2013 conflict (HRP 2018). Majority of the IDPs are in Mingkaman (Awerial County) whereas the rest are scattered in Rumbek. In Rumbek intermittent clashes between the pastoral communities remain rampant due to cattle raiding. According to a recent assessment conducted by PLAN (Sept. 2019) in both RE and RC, there are high numbers of school dropout amongst boys and girls (for boys due to revenge killings, CEFM for girls)

2 - Activities

1. Identification and documentation of UASC children and provision of FTR services (250 boys and 250 girls) 2. Provision of case management services (including cash based interventions) and referrals, to boys and girls with critical protection concerns (UASC, child marriage, CAFAAG, sexual violence) (500 boys and 500 girls)3. Provision focused and non-focused PSS activities for children in schools and communities (4000 boys and 4000 girls)4. Training of caseworkers and CP staff on Child Protection case management and child protection5. Training of caregivers on positive parenting skills and PFA; establish peer to peer support groups for the caregivers. The groups will be tasked to conduct home visits to reduce child maltreatment and abuse (800 men and 1600 women)6. Establish or strengthen Community based child protection committees and train them on child protection ( 132 men and 132 women)7. Establish peer to peer/ adolescent clubs in the communities 8. Establish or strengthen child rights clubs in schools and in CFSs 9. Conduct child protection awareness raisings or campaigns on prevention and response to child protection risks and on referral mechanisms (5500 men, 6500 women, 7000 boys and 8400 girls)10. Capacity building of government officers on CP (80 men and 80 women)11. Training of non-child protection staff on CP (18 men and 18 women)12. Mapping of the existing child protection systems including service providers and updating the referral pathways13. Conduct child protection risks assessments in target locations to identify child protection risks and concerns, regular monitoring visits and provide reports that will assist in informing child protection response and advocacy

Yes, the project targets displacement affected communities hence they will benefit from the project. The view of the displaced persons have been reflected in the needs assessments.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the project will work closely with the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare and other relevant Government agencies. The project will also work with community based child protection Networks (CBCPNs) and local authorities. PLAN will also recruit volunteers and staff from the local community in the target locations. PLAN will train government officials, CBCPNs and volunteers on child protection.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, such as the Child Protection Sub-cluster objectives, Case management objectives and and PSS working group objectives

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

No. There are inter-communal clashes due to cattle raiding in certian locations such as Rumbek and Pibor, Conflict between IO and Government in pockets of rural Yei; Climatic shocks are common occurence in the target locations such as Pibor (flooding); Possibility of epidemic diseases such as Ebola in Yei; Bad road conditions and limited accessibility in most rural areas.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

PLAN will prioritize local communities for local positions to ensure community ownership and sustainability of the project. Further, PLAN will work with CBCPNs in target locations. The CBCPN structure members are selected from different community representation; women, youth, teachers, religious leaders and local authorities and thus ensure the ‘buy in’ and acceptance by the community.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Plan International will conduct project kick off meeting with representatives of the community (women, men, boys and girls). During this meeting information on the assistance to be provided by Plan will be shared as well as the nature of the target beneficiaries. Plan will also work through CBCPNs who are indviduals from different community representation including minority groups, IDPs etc Disaggregated Focused Group Discussions with women and girls will be held inorder to ensure that their needs are heard and taken into account. Women and girls will be encouraged to take lead in certain activities such as awareness sessions. PLAN will also make an effort to employ women in certain roles who will serve as role models for girls. PLAN will orient staff and volunteers on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, expected behaviours from PLAN towards beneficiaries and the staff and volunteers will sign the Code of Conduct. Any staff or volunteers found or reported as exploiting beneficiaries will receive a displinary action. 8.17 - PLAN will conduct a risk assessment using an internal risk assessment template which outlines possible risks in implementing the activity and mitigation measures. This will be done jointly with the beneficiaries. A ‘Do no Harm’ approach will be adopted in the implementation of activities in order to ensure the safety and dignity of beneficiaries. The CFS will be in located in central place which is accesible to children. The meetings and CP awareness sessions will be held in a location that is accessible to the community. Plan will engage with the local authorities when conducting activities to ensure that they are alert in case of any eventualities.
