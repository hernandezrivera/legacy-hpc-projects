Project Summary

The project aims at saving lives of the most vulnerable households to meet immediate food and basic needs with dignity through multi-purpose cash-based support. The multi-purpose cash-based support will be combined with local markets support, distribution of productive assets (fishing gear, seeds and tools) and training of producer and market groups. These activities are expected lower the use of irrevocable and damaging coping strategies and improve household food security and livelihoods while reducing the aid-dependency and increasing the overall resilience to shocks. The programme will use a conflict sensitive approach, do no harm principles and will mainstream protection issues, with special focus on GBV, and climate change adaptation

1 - Needs

Continued insecurity has resulted in displacement of farmers in Greater Equatoria, Northern Jonglei, and Upper Nile; resulting in a large area of agricultural land left uncultivated. Some areas of Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria and Jonglei state have experienced continued large-scale fighting in 2018 and populations have been forced to move on several occasions often due to shifts in political control or armed clashes. Although the prevalence of the national and local conflicts reduced in 2019 due to the revitalized peace agreement, yet ‘’6.35M (54% of the population) people are still facing severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3+) and are in need of urgent actionsupport. … aHence, all counties in Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Jonglei (except Pibor), Upper Nile (except Maban), Unity and Counties of Gogrial East, Gogrial West, Twic, Warrap, Budi, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South, Lafon, Eastern Equatoria State, Awerial and Yirol West, Lakes are classified as IPC AMN Phase 4. (IPC - 11 September 2019 issue)’’. According to the August 2019 IPC analysis, the proportion of the population experiencing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes during the May-August lean season slightly declined from 59 percent in 2018 to 54 percent in 2019. An estimated 6.35 million people faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes at the July-August peak of the lean season in the presence of humanitarian assistance. Food security is expected to seasonally improve in several areas with the availability of the main harvest through January, but Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes or worse will likely remain prevalent. A risk of Famine (IPC Phase 5) will continue to persist in areas already classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse. Parts of country have been served from worse food insecurity crisis due to humanitarian food assistances. The number of beneficiaries reached with humanitarian food assistance has declined since May. (FEWSNET September 2019). The relative stability led to an increase in the number of returnees, putting pressure on the scarce resources of the host communities. Reduced household production of crops and livestock coupled with climatic shocks (flood/erratic rainfalls), pests and diseases, slow recovery from prolonged asset depletion, and poor macro-economic conditions have led families to primarily to accessing food primarily from markets, humanitarian assistance, by changing livelihoods strategies, and or gathering wild foods. In the targeted areas, as found by DCA's market and needs assessments as well as post-distribution monitoring conducted on regular basis, most households buy more than 75% of the consumed food at the market, but conflict, poor infrastructure, hyperinflation, and economic crisis have raised food prices and left many households unable to buy sufficient food. The central markets in Upper Nile, Jonglei, and Eastern Equatoria States function to some extent and have the capacity to supply the key commodities, mainly food items. Cereals, pulses, sugar, salt, fish and sometimes meat and milk are the most common commodities sold in the markets in Fashoda, Ulang, Nasir, Maiwut, and Nimule. This information has been collected by both DCA and its implementing partners on a regular basis throughout 2019. While markets in the targeted locations are functioning, (DCA market and needs assessments, market surveys and PDMs), the need to support traders to ensure a continuous supply of goods at a reasonable price has been identifiedcontinues to persist. High inflation, poor infrastructure, low stock and capacity to restock, and the lack of access to financial services are some of the challenges faced by traders. Strengthening local markets and market actor groups is aimed at restoring the supply chain, increasing trade options and improving the availability and access to quality of food and other basic commodities at the local markets serving the target population.

2 - Activities

DCA will target 7,970 most vulnerable households (47,820 individuals) with multipurpose, conditional and unconditional and unrestricted cash assistance to allow people to buy food and key commodities/services according to their household needs and preferences. Vulnerable households who are labour constrained will be targeted for unconditional and unrestricted cash whilst vulnerable households endorsed with labour will be targeted with cash for work activities. DCA chooses a cash-based intervention because money is easier to move, therefore responding with cash where markets are functioning is faster and more efficient. At the same time, it gives people choice and dignity. Through the provision of production inputs, such as crop and vegetable seeds and tools, at least 5,900 households (35,400 individuals) will be targeted. The cereal crop seeds and some tools will be procured locally while vegetable seeds that are often not available in the target areas will be procured in Juba or Sudan / Ethiopia depending on the trading routes. 6,315 households (37,890 individuals) will receive fishing kits (nets, twines, hooks) to enable the beneficiaries to address immediate food security needs and reduce their vulnerability. 150 fishing groups of 30 members each will receive basic training on preservation, utilization and nutrition, as well as equipment repair, marketing and value addition. The subsequent processing of the catch will target women as they are often not able to participate in the actual fishing due to cultural restraints. 60 farmer groups of 1800 members each will receive training in improved agronomic practices. The project will repair key community assets through cash for work modality where able-bodied young men and women will be selected to repair community assets which will be identified by the community through consultation and participants of cash for work will be paid per task. Assets have in the past included vital infrastructures such as bridges, feeder roads, and dikes. Aside from the injection of cash into local markets through cash transfer programming, the project will provide critical support to 12 markets and 180 groups (petty, retail and whole sellers) so that markets recover and grow and will be more resilient to shocks in the future. Interventions will be based on ongoing assessments of markets and activities will include training and provision of equipment to store, transport and preserve food products; cash grants for transport to markets; small initiatives for income generation; improved storage facilities for traders; small cash grants for traders; provision of canoes/boats to business collectives. At the same time, support to local markets and traders to ensure availability of and access of goods in community markets. In all the counties DCA will strengthen the community protection committees to be able to identify protection concerns, map referral pathways and establish a basic early warning system. The identified protection gaps will be addressed through cash for work initiatives. Communities will receive small grants to use to address the identified protection gaps/issues. DCA will establish and strengthen cash-based emergency response mechanism to support crisis/shock affected populations with life-saving support. The ERM will only be triggered when agreed indicators have been met. DCA intend to respond with cash within 2 weeks of triggering the ERM.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Consultation with the targeted communities, including separate discussions with men and women and with different age groups enabled DCA and its partners analyses the specific protection needs of different population groups. Gender and protection are integral parts of all assessments, monitoring efforts and community consultations, which enables DCA and its partners to identify and respond to any problems and trends, including gender-based violence. In addition, the integrated complaints handling mechanism allows beneficiaries to voice concerns, which can then be addressed by DCA and partners. Communities will be informed of the complaints handling mechanisms at the beginning of each meeting or project activities. Protection is mainstreamed throughout the project, including key issues such as careful selection and sensitization of financial service providers, consideration of distances beneficiaries has to travel to markets and distribution sites, security around cash distributions, gender-specific protection needs, and monitoring of beneficiaries' protection concerns. DCA and partners will ensure that all cash for work projects have a component that women will be able to provide labour. A quota will be reserved fr women on the cash for work activities.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

DCA and its partners aim to address inequalities through inclusion and participation through careful consideration of protection risks and existing inequalities and in consultation with targeted populations, specifically with vulnerable and marginalized groups. As an integral part of the project planning and design phase, potential barriers will be identified and mitigation measures, such as additional support to and prioritisation of certain groups (i.e people with disability, elderly) will be considered and guided by the beneficiary selection criteria. In addition, the targeted communities will be sensitized regarding the importance of inclusiveness and the targeting rational. Through the establishment and close collaboration with community-led cash committees, DCA and its partners will monitor access and inclusiveness on an ongoing basis. Early identification of changes in the ability of beneficiaries to access assistance is addressed and mitigated. An example of a mitigation measure is the provision of transport to physically challenged beneficiaries to allow them to access distribution sites and markets. The choice of distribution site also takes into consideration the distance beneficiaries have to travel.
