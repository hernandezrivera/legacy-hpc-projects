Project Summary

This project aims to increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities in areas with high severity of needs under strategic objective 3.
Hence, Muzun's primary objectives are to support maintenance and improve equitable access to basic services and increase access to livelihood and job opportunities.
This will be achieved through the implementation of ERL activities in Idlib Governorate.
1. Rehabilitation of 11 km of roads, including scraping, smoothing, graveling, and liquid asphalting (MCO), as well as reparation of 3 km of sewage lines to enhance access to livelihood opportunities by establishing a connection for the displaced to markets through the restoration of main roads linking shelter camps to markets in Idlib. This activities aims to benefit 31839 individuals (7,277 women, 5,966 men, 8,760 boys, 9,836 girls) and 6006 households of displaced people residing within the camp, where 800 beneficiaries are presently undergoing vocational training within the camps and 400 beneficiaries are scheduled to receive small grants (under donor SCHF SA1) of five camps in Dana and Maaret Tamsrin sub-district. Local residents will have the ability to visit their relatives within the camps. All individuals in the targeted villages and markets will directly or indirectly benefit from the project outcomes.
The targeted camps are Atta Alkhair camp, Affad camp, Haifa Carmel camp, and Sadaqa Tash camp, Kelly (Al Rayhan) camp, and Talmenes(Ard Aljameh) camp. This road will connect the IDPs in these camps with the markets in Kelly, Kafr Jales, Deir Hassan - Darhashan, Mashhad Ruhin, and other communities.
2. Providing 22 days of vocational training for 300 individuals from displaced and host communities in four key professions identified in the recent needs assessment by Muzun in targeted locations. 300 IDPs and host communities (150 females and 150 males, including 25 PWDs) will receive training in five different vocational and income-generating skills at two IDP sites and two communities in Idleb (50% IDPS and 50% HC. where Muzun will select those beneficiaries of previous skilled ones, who don't have any income generation currently.
The training will cover:
a. 75 Mobile maintenance
b. 75 Barbering
c. 75 Hairdressing
d. 75 Knitting wool
3. Provision of support grants for 150 females, and 150 males, including 25 PWDs of the beneficiaries who received vocational training in Kelly (Al Rayhan), and Talmenes(Ard Aljameh) camps and Kafr Takharim and Kelly villages.
camps in Idlib governorate.
4. Empowering 300 IDPs and HC with Small Business Grants in Idleb Governorate
Muzun is dedicated to empowering 500 individuals, comprising women, men, and PwDs, with small business grants. The breakdown of these grants is as follows:
75 small grants for mobile maintenance
75 small grants for barbering
75 small grants for hairdressing
75 small grants for knitting wool
These activities include providing life skills and vocational training, facilitating community mobilization for self-help livelihood projects, and connecting trained women and youth groups to livelihood start-up and small grants programs. Coordination with ERL cluster interventions outside the camps will also be a part of this effort. Furthermore, the project will support community peacebuilding and life skills recreational initiatives.
Protection mainstreaming :
Muzun will continue to prioritize gender mainstreaming in all project activities, ensuring gender balance in staffing, selected beneficiaries for training, gender data collection, gender community engagement (empowerment), and gender inclusion.
Muzun will establish and strengthen quality referral mechanisms to ensure that individuals with protection concerns receive appropriate and timely support, as well as conduct a risk assessment to identify potential protection protection risks.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The implementation plan includes:Reparation of Roads:1. Conduct community mobilization in the targeted camps as well as signing the MoUs with local authorities. 2. Conduct a re-assessment of the selected roads and publish the advertisement about the work period.3. Contract with the suppliers to start reparation of the roads.4. Conduct light repairs on sewage lines with the selected roads.Vocational training and a small grant:1. Community participation in project and activities implementation2-Advertising the vocational training and the type of selected trainings as per the need assessment.3- Registering the applicants and verification process of their information4- Selecting the 300 trainees (male and female and PwDs) based on the advertised selection criteria5- Conducting vocational training for 22 days.6- Supporting the 300 trainees with small and business grants to start up their job opportunities, in addition to strengthening the social cohesion between the host communities and IDPs through joint projects like the saloon and barber shops in and out the campsace and development in the target areas. PSEA:Muzun will provide training to staff and beneficiaries on PSEA principles, establish mechanisms for reporting and responding to protection concerns, and ensure that all activities are designed and implemented in a way that minimizes risks to beneficiaries, especially women and children.PWDs inclusion:Muzun will conduct a thorough assessment of the specific needs and challenges faced by PWDs in the targeted communities. This assessment will involve consultation with PWDs themselves, as well as relevant stakeholders.Muzun will also offer customized materials for PWDs during training to meet their specific needs. In terms of road repair, Muzun will build small ramps or inclines next to the roads close to the residences of PWDs. To ensure the meaningful participation of PWDs, Muzun will adopt a participatory approach that involves PWDs in the decision-making. This includes forming a PWDs committee, and providing training opportunities for persons with disabilities, and facilitating access to information and resources. In addition, Muzun will regularly consult with PWDs to identify and address any barriers or challenges they face in participating in project activities. AAP and Complaints Mechanisms Muzun is committed to establishing an effective feedback and complaints mechanism that empowers affected IDPs to assess and comment on humanitarian action's performance. This mechanism covers sensitive matters like sexual exploitation and abuse, fraud, corruption, and discrimination. Muzun provide multiple avenues for IDPs to raise their concerns and suggestions, including complaint boxes, email addresses, Facebook and social media pages and a phone number/WhatsApp.Muzun prioritizes the confidentiality and protection of personal information for individuals making complaints, guided by the "Do No Harm" principle.
