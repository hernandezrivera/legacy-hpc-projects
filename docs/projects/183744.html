Project Summary

The project aims at supporting impoverished vulnerable families, to meet their basic needs through multipurpose cash assistance. 500 families will be targeted based on socioeconomic and vulnerability criteria. The targeted localities include Khan Yunis (Bani Suheila, Khan Yunis, Khan Yunis Camp, Qizan an Najjar and Al Mawasi localities) and the Middle Area (Deir Al Balah, Al Bureij Camp, Al Maghazi Camp, An Nuseirat Camp, Zawayda, Deir Al Balah, Deir al Balah Camp and Wadi as Salqa localities). The 500
families will be those with serious poverty, food insecurity, and protection concerns.
The project includes to provide the target families with multipurpose cash assistance for five months with a value of 1046 NIS per family per month. The targeted families will be provided with nutrition counselling sessions to enhance their dietary practices Nutrition awareness material will be coordinated with Ministry of Health (MoH), health and nutrition cluster/nutrition working group. At another level, the project also will build the capacity of 2 vulnerable communities in the Gaza Strip to undertake innovative community-led collective actions/initiatives to address community-based humanitarian needs, foster preparedness for emergencies and reduce potential risks and threats.
The project contributes to FSS objective #2: “Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs”, and FSS indicator: “# of individuals benefiting from unconditional cash support”, and FSS activity: “Providing support through unrestricted cash modalities”.
The project will contribute to the HRP strategic objective #3 “The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued."

1 - Needs

In Gaza Strip, recurrent shocks including frequent hostilities, continuous blockade and Covid 19 pandemic exacerbate the humanitarian situation in the Gaza Strip which undermine significantly people access to their basic needs. DCA/NCA and MA’AN have been working the most vulnerable communities in the Gaza Strip supporting their capacities to access their needs and to manage for overcoming the ongoing shocks. Recent intervention has been implemented in Khan Yunis and the Middle Area addressing the GBV and WASH vulnerabilities. The intervention included to conduct a survey to identify the socioeconomic vulnerabilities of the targeted communities, in May 2021. The assessment addresses the poverty situation, food security, access to basic needs, and the protection concerns in place. 131 families were surveyed including 24.4% female-headed households, 14.5% elderly headed households, 4.5% people with disabilities, and 2.8% pregnant/lactating women. Among the assessed population, 96.9% are abject poor and other 3.1% of the families were found as poor. Furthermore, 34.4% of the families have reported no access to any source of income and they are totally dependent on aid. The assessment revealed that 99.2% of the families rely on negative coping strategies to handle the poor access to food needs which included reducing the number of meals, buying food on credit, reducing consumed food quantities and purchasing low quality market leftovers. 96.9% of the families reported accumulated debts, where almost 96.4% of the debts were on food, shelter, and Nonfood items. In addition, the bad socioeconomic situation and food insecurity have negative reflections on the social relations of the targeted families and undermine the psychological wellbeing of women. The assessment has revealed that the most affected groups are women (especially lactating, neonates and pregnant), children especially those under 5 years and older people. Women and older people sometimes reduce the no. of meals per day to make food available for other family members.

2 - Activities

The project will target 500 vulnerable families from the Gaza Strip. The project targets Khan Yunis (Bani Suheila, Khan Yunis, Khan Yunis Camp, Qizan an Najjar and Al Mawasi localities) and the Middle Area (Deir Al Balah, Al Bureij Camp, Al Maghazi Camp, An Nuseirat Camp, Zawayda, Deir Al Balah, Deir al Balah Camp and Wadi as Salqa localities). The target families will be from DCA/NCA and MAAN pool of beneficiaries who have been targeted through the humanitarian interventions intervention. Beneficiaries will be selected based on socioeconomic criteria. Food insecurity and poverty will be the essential criteria for targeting. Female headed HH’s, families including people with disabilities, children under five years, pregnant and lactating women will be prioritized. The intervention will be in coordination with Ministry of Social Development (MoSD). Further coordination with MoSD for beneficiary selection will be ensured DCA/NCA and MAAN will follow the unified vulnerability assessment tool (UVAT) for beneficiary selection. The proposed project will include the following activities:1. Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance: 500 HHs will benefit from multi-purpose cash assistance over five months (one payment per month). The transfer value had considered the updated standards set by the CWG which is equal to 1’046 NIS (around 330 USD) per household per month. Locations of the cash distribution and times of distribution will be determined upon consultation with the beneficiaries, in particular women to ensure safety and accessibility . The targeted families will benefit from nutritional awareness sessions to help them enhancing their nutritional status.2. The project will include a complementary component promote the capacity of 2 vulnerable communities to undertake collective actions towards reducing the potential risks and threats of the most pressing hazards. In the past four years, DCA/NCA and MAAN have conducted capacity building activities to strengthen the community-based emergency response mechanisms including the partner CBOs, community-based committees and groups of volunteers where women were very well represented. However, DCA/NCA and MA’AN have recognized that the targeted vulnerable communities still lack the capacity to lead humanitarian response and deal with the potential hazards and threats in proactive manner. In June 2019, DCA/NCA has conducted training/co-design workshop with its partners including MA’AN Development Center on a new approach for community-led response called “Survivor and Community Led Response (SCLR). This approach is based on supporting the vulnerable communities to plan for and implement actions that aim to reduce the negative impact and mitigate risks of future hazards as well as to address current humanitarian needs at the community level. This project will address protection threats through promotion of diversified and participatory community responses addressing the needs of women, men, girls and boys. The community-based structures will be empowered to address communities’ humanitarian needs but also to contribute strengthening the long-term resilience. Participation of different community groups including women and youth will be ensured in the composition of the community groups. The intervention has been coordinated with Ministry of Social Development (MoSD). Further coordination with MoSD for beneficiary selection will be ensured. Special attention will be made by a well-trained female staff to receive GBV related complaints, address female needs, monitor and report on GBV incidents, where detected survivors will be referred to specialized services through referral pathways of the protection cluster/CPWG. The project fit with the cluster response plan & complement other clusters’ actions.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

MA’AN and DCA/NCA will pay a specific attention to ensure engaging beneficiaries in addressing the expected risks and challenges and empowering them to participate in the monitoring and evaluation process of the project. This project was built based on consultations with the project key stakeholders and beneficiaries and have considered the past experiences, emerging needs, and expectations of the beneficiaries in the design of the project activities. The complaints and feedback mechanism will be introduced to the project beneficiaries at the beginning of the project to ensure their understanding for the mechanism and encourage them to share feedback. All project beneficiaries will have the right to raise concerns; share thoughts and opinions; and submit complaints to MA’AN using different means including writing, phone, SMS, or direct verbal communication with the responsible staff. The received complaints and feedback will be documented and analyzed by MA’AN’s M&E and program staff to feed into the decision-making process and inform possible corrections during implementation but also the design of other projects. The complaints and feedback will support for increasing the efficiency of the adopted work system and procedures and will allow for adjusting the work mechanisms and approaches in a way that ensures a better response to the needs of the vulnerable groups. A variety of complaint channels that the beneficiaries can access such as hotlines, web based complaint mechanism, emails, and through the field staff will be adopted. Specific complaint mechanisms are in place to address sensitive complaint in a confidential matter. MA’AN will conduct (FGDs) during the project implementation to collect feedback from the project beneficiaries and measure their satisfaction. The collected information will be used to inform decision making by searching for possible areas of improvement and revisiting the implementation approaches and tools.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

This project contributes to addressing the multisectoral basic needs of vulnerable families in the Gaza Strip and enhancing emergency preparedness capacities for vulnerable communities through proposing a set of integrated activities and adopting approaches that respond to the identified needs while maintaining the dignity, safety, and security of the beneficiaries. The project adopts gender-sensitive and culturally responsive approach to implement the project activities which will highly contribute to addressing the social and cultural risks that might undermine the participation of the beneficiaries, especially women in the project activities. Other risks such as safety and security risks were also identified and considered, and the mitigation measures will be further developed to ensure that these risks are addressed during implementation. The MPCA modality will allow beneficiaries to address their needs as they deem relevant to their priorities. Time and locations of cash distribution will be identified based on consultation with target group, women and people with disabilities in particular. Cash distribution facilities (banks) will be ensured to be easy to access for people with disabilities. In case women and/or people with disabilities will not be able to receive cash (i.e. due to accessibility issue is relation to people with disabilities, women do not have control over the use of cash/can be subjected to violence due to receiving cash), the project will consider other modalities to address the mentioned groups’ needs (i.e. in kind distributions). The monitoring process will include PDM, regular field visits and random calls to check if beneficiaries are satisfied with the services provided to them and that they are treated with dignity and respect. The community led response component will build the capacity of vulnerable communities to take lead in emergency response and to undertake collective actions to mitigate the threats and risks of potential hazards. This will contribute to enhancing the overall safety and security of the targeted communities and will enhance their protection in emergency and non-emergency contexts. The training and capacity building activities will be implemented at CBOs that are located in safe places and are well-known to the beneficiaries. MA’AN and DCA/NCA will also keep close monitoring for the security situation in the Gaza Strip and will advise the donor and partners in case any emergency indicators arise. MA’AN and DCA/NCA will also adjust timeframe and activities in case any emergency happens to avoid security and safety risks.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This project aims at improving the resilience, alleviating the suffering and enhancing the preparedness mechanisms of the most vulnerable people in the Gaza Strip, realizing the principles of impartiality, independence, and equality in the targeting process and in the delivery of assistance to people. DCA/NCA and MAAN adopt Anti-discrimination policy that criminalizes all aspects of discrimination and guides the implementation of interventions in light of the humanitarian principles. An assessment will be conducted to select the project beneficiaries based on clear selection criteria related to poverty and food insecurity. The assessment will be conducted by trained field workers, who have the required skills and expertise to collect precise data, under the supervision of the M&E team at MA’AN. The collected data will be analyzed and scored following an accurate statistical methodology and using Excel to ensure the accuracy of selection. The final selection results will be also verified and approved by MA’AN’s management and DCA/NCA. All selected beneficiaries will be treated equally, impartially, and transparently, with particular attention to gender issues, considering the extra vulnerability inflicted on women in the society. In addition, further technical criteria will be applied to select the beneficiaries of community initiatives. The selection criteria will be communicated with the target groups from the beginning of the project through orientation sessions, brochures, and feedback sessions, as relevant, to ensure accountability. The project applies human rights standards and gender-segregated data at all levels of intervention. DCA/NCA and MAAN apply a needs and rights-based approach to their work within humanitarian response and community mobilization investments. The intervention will improve the access of the targeted communities to equitable and accountable humanitarian assistance in accordance to safety and dignity.. The intervention addresses the right of target people to food, where, everyone is entitled to food that is sufficient, safe, acceptable, accessible and affordable. The MPCA-will be adopted through the intervention as a dignified approach, where it addresses the needs of the different vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly and people with disabilities as relevant to their priorities. The following actions will be considered to ensure providing equitable access to the basic services for men, women, boys and girls:1. Ensuring significant involvement of women through the participation in the community engagement committee.2. Having safe procedures to guide the handling of gender-sensitive complaints through the complaint’s mechanism3. Orienting the project team on gender policy and GBV incidences.4. Ensuring that the developed baseline tools address the perceptions and concerns of women. 5. Ensuring that the baseline information contained gender segregated data DCA/NCA is a Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) certified agency (www.hqai.org) and works continuously to improve the quality and effectiveness of the assistance it provides to the affected population. PSEA mainstreaming will be part of this project.
