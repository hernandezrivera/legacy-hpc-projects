Project Summary

ACTED will provide unconditional cash transfer to an estimated 1,362 households (approximately 7,492 individuals) following a sudden shock, delivered immediately following displacement (caused by conflict, climate-related shock, or other types of natural or man-made disasters). The estimated unit value would be of 150 USD per MPCA round out of camp (with three rounds per household) and 100 USD per MPCA round (with three rounds) in camp. It is estimated that approximately 20% of the beneficiaries will be likely in camps (but not receiving food assistance), while 80% will be likely found out of camp.
The process followed to trigger a cash intervention will be as follows:
- Step 1: ACTED team deploys into a new area of displacement or underserved protracted displacement and conducts a rapid needs assessment including a rapid market assessment to assess the feasibility of a cash-based intervention (i.e. access and functionality); The RNA also includes a cash risk assessment.
- Step 2: Based on the results, ACTED consults and coordinates with the FSL cluster, per the CWG guidance, to target the most vulnerable households, who are not covered with food assistance.
- Step 3: ACTED carries out a rapid Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) within ten days to two weeks after each distribution.
ACTED will coordinate with the local authorities, humanitarian actors and clusters to discuss the best approach to adopt to respond to the needs and coordinate the action to bring all capacities together, ensure a timely response and avoid duplication.
Considering the extreme vulnerability of the beneficiaries, ACTED will ensure that the distribution is organized in a timely manner after the shock. The distribution will be organized during the day in a safe and central location to avoid the beneficiaries carrying money with them on long distance. The distribution will be disaggregated between women and men as needed. Women and men staff will be available and each beneficiary will receive information concerning the CRM to be informed about their rights as well as the purpose of the cash distribution. ACTED will thoroughly monitor potential risks it can create for staff and beneficiaries.

3 - Needs

While large-scale hostilities have subsided following the March 2020 Idleb ceasefire agreement, localized hostilities have continued throughout 2022, including artillery shelling, airstrikes and land mines, with devastating impact on civilians and humanitarian activities. Households across the country face a gap between incomes and expenditures on essential needs, and this has worsened in 2022: at the end of the third quarter of 2022, the national average price of a WFP standard reference food basket reached a new record at SYP 329,526, up 40 percent since the onset of the crisis in Ukraine and fourfold since two years. Affected populations cope with the purchasing of lower quality items, increasingly borrowing money, cutting down on non-food expenses, and skipping meals. The lack of ability to meet basic needs is evident despite 94% of households with an employed head of household. Most often, households cite the combined impact of high prices, lack of income as the main reasons for their inability to meet basic needs. Eight per cent of households are completely unable to meet, while 77% are insufficiently able to meet basic needs. According to REACH, in Greater Idleb, 94% of assessed communities reported insufficient income of households, citing rent, fuel, water trucking, food and health services as unaffordable.

4 - Implementation of activities

ACTED will provide unconditional cash transfer to around 681 households following a sudden shock, The estimated unit value would be of 150 USD per MPCA round out of camp (with three rounds per household) and 100 USD per MPCA round (with three rounds) in camp. ACTED will conduct a rapid assessment in the affected area to identify the extent of the needs (either in the area of origin or areas of potential displacement). ACTED will coordinate with the local authorities, humanitarian actors and clusters to discuss the best approach to adopt to respond to the needs and coordinate the action to bring all capacities together, ensure a timely response and avoid duplication. The distribution will be organized during the day in a safe and central location to avoid the beneficiaries carrying money with them on long distance. The distribution will be disaggregated between women and men as needed. Women and men staff will be available and each beneficiary will receive information (both orally and leaflets) concerning the CRM to be informed about their rights as well as the purpose of the cash distribution. ACTED will thoroughly monitor potential risks it can create for staff and beneficiaries. The M&E will oversee the monitoring process and provide timely and relevant feedback through systematic on-site monitoring during the multipurpose cash distributions, and shall raise awareness on the CRM. M&E will carry out a PDM within ten days to two weeks after each distribution.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups and minorities needs during project design/implementation Likelihood: Low – Impact: High Mitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community sensitization, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and community meetings prior to implementation. ACTED addresses where possible gender and protection concerns. In areas out of the scope of programming or expertise, ACTED relies on referral procedures to specialized agencies. Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse Likelihood: Low – Impact: High Mitigation measure: all ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to PSEA and GBV. As part of ongoing efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as that of the contractors, and to relay any issue, including SEA or GBV.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All ACTED staff receive induction and regular refresher trainings on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration and respect ACTED Code of Conduct. Information on the CRM will be communicated to camp manager and community leaders and as much as possible directly to camp residents. Information will also be displayed on the channels available to raise complaints. The presence of women staff, in the programme and monitoring teams, will be guaranteed particularly during monitoring and evaluation activities conducted at household-level. ACTED staff, programme and monitors, are trained on protection and gender mainstreaming and on how to refer SEA incidents and/or suspicion internally. All SEA incidents are investigated by ACTED SEA Committee and communicated to the PSEA Network, as part of ACTED responsibility as member of the network.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Conflict escalation: movements are suspended in specific areas; relocation of staff to other offices to continue activities • Community acceptance: monitored daily by liaison assistants, regular meetings with local community leaders and Key Informant network in order to prevent any tensions; regular dialogue ensured with all relevant authorities, with support from various coordination mechanisms at area level. • Recruitment related risks: process entails 2- week advertisement. Longlist prepared by HR office in the area. Selection process is completed after interview records are received and reference check completed by HR office in the area and cohesion dynamics: • CRM inside Syria: stakeholders can safely provide feedback and report any project issues or concerns.
