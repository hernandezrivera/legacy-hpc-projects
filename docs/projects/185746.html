Project Summary

The project aims to continue strengthening and improving a wide range of Site Management components including: enhancing the capacity of site management actors through targeted modular training, maintaining and improving site management facilities, ensuring continuous access to services, promoting community participation, promoting adherence to Site Management standards and practices, enhancing site development including improvement of settlements’ infrastructure, site planning that address vulnerability to natural hazards and natural resource management.
In partnership with the RRRC, CiCs, IOM and Site Management partners, UNHCR will work towards strengthening coordination structures, site management and development at the camp level. UNHCR will continue supporting the Government in strengthening and promoting community representation structures and civilian administration of the camps, while its partnership with MoDMR and RRRC will remain key to UNHCR’s engagement in the camps.
UNHCR expects the gradual transition of Site Management functions to the Government to proceed in 2022, and accordingly through the Capacity Sharing Initiative will continue to prioritize training of the RRRC, CiC and partner staff to enhance understanding and application of Site Management standards. UNHCR’s response will focus on engaging more closely with refugees to ensure interventions respond better to their needs; capacity building and support to the Government; enhancing decision-making structures based on inclusive refugee representation structures and strengthening linkages between refugee preferences and available assistance; and promote the sustainability of interventions in collaboration with partners while continually searching for comprehensive solutions.
UNHCR will aim to build on the lessons learned from the COVID-19 pandemic by strengthening remote Site Management through refugee volunteer networks and remote management tools. In order to improve participation and ownership of critical infrastructure and other community assets, UNHCR will continue working with refugees and the Site Management and Site Development Sector to implement community-led projects.
Community and protection assessments have shown that refugees, specifically women and girls continue to face various protection risks and challenges such as poor lighting at night and increasing threats targeting female volunteers have also added to the sense of insecurity in the camps. UNHCR will strengthen initiatives to reduce tensions and promote peaceful co-existence between the refugee and host communities and will design programs based on community consultations and feedback received from refugees and host communities including through the rollout of the Common Feedback Mechanism.
As Bangladesh is particularly vulnerable to climate change, UNHCR will continue to enhance the resilience of both refugees and host communities against seasonal natural disasters such as monsoon and cyclones as well as man-made risks such as fire hazards, and to strengthen preparedness and ensure timely activation and deployment of personnel, partners and volunteers, as well continue supporting them with adequate gears equipment and relevant DRM trainings, drills and simulations, while continuing the prepositioning of essential relief items ahead of disasters.
UNHCR will work to ensure implementation of the Climate Action Plan to further the greening of the camps and plans to invest in bamboo plantations to mitigate the impact of excessive bamboo use for shelter and infrastructure construction. An LPG assessment conducted by UNHCR’s partner found 80 percent reduction in deforestation as a direct impact of the sustainable use of LPG. At the same time, UNHCR will improve community safety through continuous functioning of solar streetlights, mini-grids, and other camp infrastructure.

1 - Identified Needs

Strengthening the management of the camps remains a priority, including enabling all coordination actors to have adequate knowledge and tools to ensure the effective delivery of services in line with agreed standards. Over the years, Site Management functions established in partnership with the RRRC, UNHCR and Site Management partners continued to be improved. UNHCR’s focus is to consolidate these functions to the extent possible to enhance refugee participation and representation at camp level as well as strengthening the RRRC capacity both in Cox’s Bazar and at the camp level. The Covid-19 pandemic adversely affected the overall response in 2021 due to limited access and there is need to continue; reinforcing RRRC, CiCs and site management capacities; strengthen camp level coordination; ensuring all sectors understand roles, responsibilities and participate in decision making; increase and strengthen representative refugee community structures (with gender parity); ensure strong Disaster Risk Management (DRM) for effective emergency response. Increased government role in site management functions will require the RRRC to ensure harmonized exercise of authority and consistent practices by CiCs across all camps. Through the Capacity Sharing Initiative, UNHCR will continue to work with IOM to provide direct technical support and training to CiC personnel and partners. UNHCR is supporting CiC staff in all camps under an agreement with the Government and will make concerted efforts to ensure checks and balances in the governance model in the camps. UNHCR and its partners will continue to design and provide information products that reinforce the coordination system in camps which enable humanitarian community to make informed decisions both for regular and emergency programming. The interventions shall be designed to; Ensure refugees including women, men, boys, girls, older person, person with specific needs have access to services; Conduct regular service monitoring; Ensure protection mainstreaming; Care and maintenance and small-scale camp improvements linked to DRM; community participation and representation; Capacity building through CSI; Emergency Preparedness and Response; Information management and enhanced application of Camp Coordination and Site Management standards. Bangladesh is a country prone to natural disasters, including cyclones, storm surges, heavy rains, flooding, landslides and fires. With a monsoon season of four months of continuous heavy rain and two cyclone seasons in April/May and October/November, the camps are exposed to recurring emergencies. In collaboration with CiCs and partners, including Site Management agencies, UNHCR will continue to invest in the capacity of Safety Unit Volunteers (SUVs) and Site Management Volunteers (SMS volunteers) through trainings and simulations. These refugees will continue to be the first responders to natural/ man-made disasters and other emergencies, and as a community-based response mechanism they will play a key role in saving lives. UNHCR will maintain remote management tools connecting UNHCR, CiCs and SMS partners with volunteers to coordinate the response and will continue to support Emergency Response Teams in UNHCR-managed camps linking the response to the HCC and the ISCG’s EPR Working Group, RRRC, the Army and other local authorities, and enhance the work of Camp DMCs chaired by the CiC, UNHCR and Sector. Efforts to enhance the resilience of refugees and members of host communities to respond to natural disasters and other emergencies will continue. UNHCR continues to seek technical solutions linked to the natural environment, ensuring it is safe and supports good living conditions. Protecting the environment through rehabilitation, reforestation, disaster risk reduction, and habitat improvement with respect to biodiversity conservation will continue, and households will have ongoing access to renewable energy and lighting.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1- Facilitating camp coordination mechanisms with Camp in Charge (CiCs), Site Management and Support (SMS) and other stakeholders for smooth operations of the camp and enhance access of services to the refugees.Activity 2- Continuous implementation of the Capacity Sharing Initiative through multi-sector trainings for CiC staff to capacitate and strengthen RRRC in Site Management functions.Activity 3- Building on existing community structures, enhance community participation in the camps with age, gender and diversity lens, including promoting camp and block representation committees in liaison with RRRC, respective camp in charge, community-based protection, site management partners and relevant stakeholders.Activity 4- Enhance complain response and feedback mechanism (CRFM) in camps for refugees to concerns and complains through the Common Feedback Mechanism Platform with camp management agencies ensuring they address or refer the raised issues and close the feedback loop within a diligent time frame.Activity 5- Ensuring information management activities in camps including but are not limited to; needs assessments, camp profiling, services mapping and monitoring, facility mapping, contact directories, training to relevant stakeholders, risk and vulnerability analysis, monsoon incident reporting, response tracking and application of camp management standards. Activity 6- Site improvements and camp care and maintenance works to improve access and address infrastructure damages.Activity 7- Site Development works including DRR activities; repair and maintenance, re-excavation of drainages, canals; access improvement through repairs of roads, pathway, bridges, stairs and constructions, slope stabilisation to reduce vulnerability to natural hazards.Activity 8- Emergency preparedness to enhance resilience and reduce the effects of the monsoon seasons and cyclone shall continue by capacitating and mobilizing SUVs and CPP volunteers in camps and host communities. Further; ensuring multi-hazard camp plans to mitigate extreme weather effects and other related emergencies that emerge in camps including fire-preparedness amongst others. Activity 9- Community based environmental and biodiversity awareness across the camps to address environmental impact caused by the large camp settlement.Activity 10- Strengthen the supply chain of planting materials required for soil stabilization and environmental restoration in collaboration with forest department.Activity 11- Rehabilitation of water ways to abate water pollution. Conservation activities shall be furthered to include; “Wildlife human contact” with rescue and relief programmes.Activity 12- Installation and Maintenance of camp solar street lights.
