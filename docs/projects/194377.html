Project Summary

Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) report (November 2022) indicates that 7.76million South Sudanese (62.7%) of the population will face severe food insecurity (IPC 3+). In Fangak, 178,000 (over 90%) of the people will face severe food insecurity with about 20,000 of these people facing catastrophic food insecurity. In Koch County, 75,000 (over 75%) while in Canal/Pigi 85,000 people (80%) of the people will face IPC 3+ conditions. The main causes of food insecurity are floods and conflicts which have destroyed farms, livelihoods and resulted in displaced populations. Agricultural productivity has been persistently low and coping capacity of these populations has been eroded. At the Macro-economic level, depreciation of the local currency and effects of War in Ukraine have made food more expensive and scarcer. Three consecutive years of flooding have been experienced in these counties. Crops were destroyed before harvesting and in storage. Livestock died through drowning and starvation as grazing grounds were flooded. Coupled with flooding, are intensified sub-national violence arising from political instability, cattle raids and revenge killings. Violence causes instability limiting access to land for cultivation but also results in deaths of productive members of the HH. Coping strategies of affected households have been exhausted and household assets eroded. Without timely humanitarian intervention, this situation could worsen and plunge populations into famine situation.
The goal of this intervention is to support the vulnerable populations in these three counties with livelihood kits and training so as to help these vulnerable communities to restore and sustain their livelihoods. This intervention will be guided by the three cluster objectives (CO). The first CO is to provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations. The second CO is to enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock. The third CO is to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. It is expected that through this intervention, target populations will be able to supplement food provided through general food distribution which had proved to be irregular and therefore unreliable but also increases dependency to external support systems. The intervention will also contribute towards reduction in flood related displacements in the target counties. This will be achieved through disaster risk reduction strategies that will build the community capacity on early warning signs and mitigation measures including construction of dykes to control flow of water. The project will also ensure minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.
A total of 338,000 beneficiaries (56,333 HH) in Koch, Fangak and Canal/Pigi facing IPC 3+ food insecurity will be targeted for this intervention, comprising of 60% women, elderly, youth, disabled and other vulnerable groups will be targeted for crop and vegetable kits. Out of this number, a total of 8,450HHs will be targeted for training on improved agronomic methods, post-harvest handling and utilization. Similarly, 6,500HHs will be targeted with fishing kits, including training on appropriate harvesting methods and fish preservation. This intervention will also target 1,500HHs with Food for Asset intervention to help them build community structures to mitigate effects of flooding. To help improve sustainable access to quality seeds, the project will work with 500HHs to produce high quality seeds and avail them to other farmers for subsequent use.
The intervention will run from January to December of 2023

1 - Needs

It is estimated that 7.76 million people will need humanitarian assistance in 2023 (IPC November 2022). Factors driving this vulnerability include floods, sub-national violence and effects of COVID-19. These shocks have complicated an already fragile agricultural system which has been compromised by limited access to quality seeds and tools as well as a weak agricultural extension system. FAO assessments show that about 65 107 hectares of land planted with cereal crops have been damaged with an estimated loss of 37,624 tons of grain in the flood-affected areas. In most of the affected locations, the floods arrived when the crops were either in late maturity stage or at the time of harvesting (matured crops). Loss of cereals during the post-harvest period is high due to the prevailing wet conditions and poor storage facilities. An estimated 795,558 livestock have perished due to flooding this year in eight states. This includes cattle, goats and sheep, and an unknown number of poultries and donkeys. Coupled with flooding, are intensified sub-national violence arising from political instability, cattle raids and revenge killings. Violence causes instability limiting access to land for cultivation but also results in deaths of productive members of the HH. Indirect effects of COVID 19 and war in Ukraine on supply chains as well as protracted macro-economic crisis have continued to limit food supplies and continued to push up food prices. Coping strategy of affected households have been exhausted and household assets eroded. The average price of most of household items has increased compared to the five-year average. To the contrary, purchasing power of livestock dependent households deteriorated in the flood affected areas.Fangak County – It is estimated that 305,000 people from Jonglei state have been affected by flooding and need livelihood support. The Inter-agency assessment reports estimated that 52,827 and 41,325 people in New Fangak and Old Fangak respectively were affected by the flooding. The IPC (November 2022) indicates that 178,000 people, equivalent to 90% of the entire county population falls under IPC 3+, with some pockets of IPC 5. Markets have similarly been disrupted by the flooding. Tonga market in Upper Nile State is always a major route of entry of cereals and other foods into Fangak. However, fighting between different factions has affected the trading routes and restricted food imports from Sudan. Physical access has also been limited. Communities who would ordinarily rely on wild foods cannot access these foods as flooding water has restricted movement.Koch County – The County was also ravaged by floods for the greater part of the year 2022. An IRNA report from September 2022 floods have continued to affect the county leading to population displacement and destruction of crops. Koch has also been affected by recent conflicts, especially in the early part of 2022. The main driver of conflict is the tensions between government and SPLA-OI. Conflicts limit investment into long term undertakings like farming is communities have no confidence in harvesting their crops. This has an overall impact on food security situation. At least 75,000 people (over 75% of county population) will face IPC 3+ food insecurity. Canal/Pigi County is prone to perennial flooding and intercommunal conflicts. It is estimated that 85,000 people an equivalent of about 80% of the population will be in IPC 3+ food insecurity and in need humanitarian and livelihoods support.Shocks described above have had disproportionate impact on the most vulnerable segments of the community, particularly women, disabled & elderly. Shocks have forced the community to adopt negative coping strategies including collecting wild foods and skipping meals. Women are exposed to sexual abuse. Elderly and disabled have reduced mobility and collecting wild foods is a challenge. Skipping meals only worsens their already weak nutritional status

2 - Project activities

Project activities will be structured around the three cluster objectives.Objective one: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3). Under this objective, the main activity will be construction of community assets through conditional food assistance. Intended activities will include construction of dykes around farms to prevent perennial flooding, excavation of drainage channels, construction/rehabilitation of access roads, de-silting water pans to collect water during the rainy season, excavation of shallow wells. FFA will help communities establish communal assets, mitigate effects of flooding on food production and enhance preparedness and response to flooding which has become a yearly occurrence in the target counties.Cluster Objective two: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock. Under this objective, main activity will Emergency food production focusing on main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fishing, vegetable & crop kits. Dry season activities will include Emergency food & livelihood support through provision of fishing kits/ vegetable kits; and at least three variety of fast maturing vegetable seeds. Dry season farming will focus on water efficient farming methods to produce sufficient and nutritious vegetables for consumption and possibly sell for some cash. This is to ensure increased availability of food even during the dry season where availability of vegetables becomes a challenge and therefore complicates the food insecurity and malnutrition situation. Main season activities will concentrate on crop production specifically maize, sorghum and legumes. Fisheries activities will cut across the main season and dry season as there are sufficient fishing grounds for both seasons.Cluster Objective three: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. The main activity will be Training and capacity building on both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production targeting, farmers through field schools and on demonstration plots as well as the agropastoralists. All activities will be implemented so as to establish a nexus between Humanitarian response, Peace building (conflict sensitivity) and Resilience programming. A strong emphasis will be laid on accountability od affected population to ensure the do-no-harm principle is observed. There will be a strong coordination at county level with other partners to reduce duplication and ensure efficiency and wider coverage. The project will emphasis integration with other sectors to achieve greater impact in meeting household needs. For instance, the project will support establishment of kitchen garden within Nutrition centers and target mothers and caregivers of children enrolled in nutrition program with training and input support. This is to ensure that those children discharged from nutrition program do not relapse but have sufficient and nutritious food at home. The project will also incorporate WASH messaging at home, especially with reference to preparation and utilization of food, human waste disposal and provision of WASH items including water containers and purification tablets, cooking utensils and food preservation containers from other partners. To improve access to milk and household income, the project will provide small ruminants to caregivers of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) children within the Nutrition centres. This will be implemented through the mother-to-mother strategy and Care groups for both distributions and training. The project will carry out regular monitoring with participation of Boma management committees, and will develop and share project reports on monthly, quarterly and annual basis.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The most vulnerable members of community will be included in every aspect of project implementation. The project implementation committee (PIC) with representation from vulnerable groups including women, girls, youth, PLWDs and elderly will be a key mechanism to disseminate information to the beneficiaries, but also provide feedback to the project. Women and girls will be consulted to ensure that activities planned are in line with the socio-cultural needs of women and avoid over-burdening them through this intervention. For instance, distribution of inputs as well as training will take into consideration feedback from women to be in line with other roles assigned to women like fetching water, firewood on daily basis and cooking at home. Based on feedback from previous project, the project will target to include 60% of beneficiaries as women and girls. This is so because women bear the burden of ensuring that the Household is fed. Providing women with an opportunity to produce food will ensure that Households food security situation is improved. Persons living with disabilities (PLWDs) will also be consulted to ensure their concerns are considered and accorded special consideration. For instance, during distributions, PLWDs, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), and elderly will be accorded an opportunity to be served first. The PIC will be consulted to give feedback on training needs and concerns of vulnerable people. Training schedule and timing will consider the needs of such groups based on the feedback received. Similarly, training will also take into consideration needs of the illiterate population. Most of the project beneficiaries are actually illiterate. The project will employ most of its staff from the local community who can be able to communicate and conduct training in the local language. Training will also involve mostly demonstrations and practical sessions which emphasize more on learning via doing activities than theory. Any Information Education and Communication (IEC) material developed will be in pictorial form to make knowledge transfer learner centred and easy. IEC Materials developed will also be translated into the local language to make reading much more accessible. Use of local the language will be an effective way to transmit messages but also facilitate feedback from the targeted beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community engagement will be ensured at two levels; with community leaders and directly with the beneficiaries. Project objectives will be discussed and agreed upon by community leaders at County, Payam and Boma levels. Number of beneficiaries, targeting of beneficiaries and geographical location will be discussed at project inception. Feedback provided from these meetings will be used to adjust subsequent plans to ensure project is aligned to the context and needs of the beneficiaries. Secondly, there will be a project Implementation committee (PIC) at every Payam. This PIC will be involved in beneficiary identification, selection and registration. The PIC will include representatives from the vulnerable groups including women, disabled, youth and elderly. The PIC will also be involved in project monitoring and evaluation. Feedback provided by PIC at every stage of project implementation will be used to improve on project delivery. Right from inception, beneficiaries will be engaged at various levels. At project level, the project directly engages Lead Farmers on voluntary basis. Lead Farmers are representatives of the community. They will be the contact between the project and beneficiaries. Communication on project objectives and expectations will be done primarily through the lead farmers. Lead farmers will also provide feedback on project implementation. The Monitoring and Evaluation team working with the protection team will institute a complaint registration and resolution mechanism. Complaints will be registered at every stage of project implementation and solutions found as quickly as possible or referred to other levels for resolution. During beneficiary registration and inputs distribution, beneficiaries who feel unfairly left out will be free to register their complain and have their grievance addressed either by the project team or referred to higher management for resolution. Same mechanism will be instituted during training and any other project implementation stage. There will also be structured means to collect data from beneficiaries. There will be a post-distribution monitoring (PDM) conducted after every distribution. The feedback provided from these PDM will be adopted to improve on service delivery. The project will also conduct post-planting and post-harvest surveys. Similarly, feedback provided from these surveys will be used to improve programming including future projects.WRSS will also establish a complaint response and feedback mechanism (CRFM). This mechanism will allow the beneficiaries to give feedback on relevance and effectiveness of program delivery. The CRFM as contained in the WRSS guideline will provide an array of avenues through which complaints and feedback will be provided. Considering that the majority of project beneficiaries are not able to read and write, WRSS has locally recruited staffs with training on protection. These staffs have the confidence of the local community. Beneficiaries will be allowed to speak directly to these staffs who will record their complaint and forward it to project or management team for action. However, WRSS will also provide CRFM boxes strategically placed within Health and Nutrition facilities. Literate community members will be sensitized on the use of these boxes. They will provide anonymous feedback on the project which will be analyzed and formal feedback provided either through community leaders or during community meetings and other public events. Similarly, where telephone network is available, WRSS will have a delegated telephone number where beneficiaries can call directly to report complaints or provide feedback.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

World Relief South Sudan (WRSS) has in place a PSEA policy. All staffs are trained on PSEA and are required to sign on it as a proof of having gone through and understood the policy. During project implementation, all staffs are not only expected to adhere to PSEA policy but also to sensitize beneficiaries on this policy. The protection team within WRSS is charged with responsibility to ensure continuous sensitization about PSEA. Sensitization will be provided at all stages of project implementation and any public gathering. Such public events will include registration of beneficiaries, during distribution of inputs and during training. The protection officer will be provided time to explain to beneficiaries concerning PSEA as well as the role of community in ensuring PSEA. The Project Implementation Committee (PIC) will be involved in identification and selection of distribution and training sites. The PIC will also be trained on PSEA and instructed to disseminate similar information informally during communal interactions and formally during organised events such as trainings and distributions. Distribution and training venues will be located in central places for ease of accessibility to all persons, but also in safe locations. This is to ensure that women and girls are not exposed to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse as a result of their involvement in any project activity, including training or inputs distribution. Distributions will also have to be conducted and completed on time to allow women and girls time to reach home before dark. WRSS will also provide a feedback mechanism. All beneficiaries will be sensitized on the availability of this feedback mechanism for their use. Confidentiality on any information provided through the feedback mechanism will be ensured to build the confidence of the community to use this feedback mechanism. Complains registered will include Sexual Exploitation and Abuse or any attempt of such. These complains will be handled with utmost respect and confidentiality and will be handled firmly. Inputs will be provided in small quantities to ensure that beneficiaries will not have another financial burden to transport the inputs. Such financial burdens can expose these women and girls to sexual Exploitation and abuse.Any reported or suspected cases on SEA will be investigated by a committee of minimum five persons including both men and women and lead by WRSS gender focal point. These investigations will be handled with utmost confidentiality and respect for cultural norms of both the suspect and victim. Upon completion of the investigation, a report will be developed and shared with the senior management for decision making. Depending on the outcome of the investigation, firm action will be taken against the offender including dismissal from duty and sharing findings of such an investigation with other partners for any future reference. Where outcome of the investigation turns negative, the outcome will be communicated to the suspected staff and any negative effects arising from the investigation will be appropriately ameliorated.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback received from beneficiaries will be pivotal in designing and or re-designing project interventions. Given that WRSS has been operational in these project locations for a long time, WRSS has had a chance to engage the beneficiaries at various stages and at different times. In the past, WRSS has been able to conduct post distribution monitoring, Post-planting and post-planting surveys. The project has also been able to conduct Knowledge, Attitude and Practice Surveys. The baselines being used to develop this plan are from these past surveys. WRSS will continue to collect feedback from beneficiaries through such engagements and use the feedback to redesign project objectives and activities to reflect any changes in context and needs of the target beneficiaries. Beneficiaries will be engaged during mobilization and sensitization. Project objectives and key activities will be discussed. Feedback provided from these sensitization meetings will be used to tailor project interventions to meet the needs of the beneficiaries. The Project Implementation Committee (PIC) will also be very instrumental in providing feedback on the project. Selection criteria and targeting of beneficiaries will be discussed with the PIC. Feedback from PIC will be used to adjust the selection criteria and targeting. PIC will also be consulted to ensure training and distribution locations are easily accessible and are safe for all beneficiaries. The PIC will also be involved in monitoring project progress. During these monitoring sessions, views from the PIC as well as views of the direct beneficiaries will be used to adjust subsequent schedule and type of activities so as to address the needs expressed by the beneficiaries. For instance, if certain trainings were not well understood, they could be offered again. Feedback on timing, quantity and type of inputs will be received through PDM. This feedback will be used to adjust subsequent distributions. Based on the feedback, the project will adjust on duration of time spend in training or distribution centers. Frequency of training will also be adjusted based on feedback received from beneficiaries.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Beneficiaries could be exposed to sexual abuse and exploitation during registration and distribution of inputs. To mitigate this risk, beneficiaries will be consulted on location and timing of registration and distribution of inputs. The training and distribution locations will be located in central areas that can be easily accessible. Distributions and training will also be conducted timely so as to allow beneficiaries time to walk back home before dark. The inputs will also be provided in quantities that beneficiaries can easily transport with having to require money or other man-power to transport which can easily risk women and girls to sexual abuse and exploitation. Owing to the low literacy levels and cultural taboos that emphasize male dominance, women and girls tend to take a back sit. Boys are accorded more chances of accessing education which makes literacy level within women and girls even higher. This exposes women and girls to more exploitations as they may not be aware of their rights and entitlements from project interventions. Women and girls and elderly people may not even be aware of the available complaint mechanisms especially where such mechanisms involve reading materials that may be written in English or other unfamiliar languages. This project will therefore ensure that people in vulnerable groups are specifically targeted and accorded an opportunity to have their views heard and included in the project's decision-making process. Available complaints registration and resolution mechanisms will be communicated verbally and explained in the local language. The project will also ensure that common resources like demonstration sites are equitably accessible. Before demonstration farms are established, there will be consensus on location and management so that all beneficiaries have unhindered access to the demonstration site when they want to use it for training and other uses. Demo farms will also be sited in locations that have no dispute on land. The tenure of the Demo farm has to be ensured for the whole length of the project and even after the project life to ensure that beneficiaries can still continue to learn from it.Target locations have highly patriarchal gender relations. Sexual exploitation and abuse can be perpetuated sometimes unconsciously. It is generally acceptable to marry underage girls or for girls to be married even against their preference and wishes. WRSS has a PSEA policy which guides both staff and partners to ensure no sexual misconducts are perpetuated against girls and women. WRSS PSEA policy will be trained to all staffs and signed on it to guide interactions with beneficiaries. All partners will also be sensitized on WRSS PSEA policy. Local government authorities always attempt to influence registration of beneficiaries to suit their own needs. Whereas local leaders are much more familiar with context and targeted beneficiaries, when such a process is left entirely left to the local authority it is prone to abuse. Selected beneficiaries will be relatives of the local authority staffs recruiting them, while the most vulnerable are left out. When communities feel disfranchised by their leaders, conflicts are bound to arise. To mitigate this, the PIC will be in charge of the selection process based on a targeting vulnerable beneficiaries. This project will work with protection partners to develop referral pathway which will be translated in the local language and communicated to beneficiaries. Working with protection partners, beneficiaries will be sensitized on any areas suspected to have remnants of war, including unexploded ordinances.WRSS runs PHCC services which can handle clinical management of rape or refer such cases for further management. WRSS will also able to work with protection partners to establish referral pathways. Through these referral pathways, children, women at risk and GBV survivors will be able to access information on prevention and management of GBV.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The three target locations have been ravaged by floods for three consecutive years. With most of the locations in these three counties still flooded in the first quarter of 2022, likelihood of flooding again is very high. WRSS will work with communities to institute disaster risk mitigation measures across the target locations. The project will build the capacity of the community to institute early warning signs that will trigger response before the flooding occurs. Through these early warning signs, the community can respond with appropriate measures. This project will encourage farmers to respond through early planting so as to harvest before flooding sets in. Flooding sets in mostly around Late September or in October. With early planting and using early maturing crops, it is possible to plant and harvest before flooding sets in. Another mitigation measure that the project will work through with the community on flood control will be on berms and dykes. These activities will be conducted through conditional food transfer where possible. The community will be capacity built to identify and prioritize areas prone to flooding. The community will then be facilitated to establish dykes or berms that will be used to control the flooding water. This will help to reduce flood related displacement in the target locations.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

WRSS has an integrated approach in service delivery. This includes provision of protection and Health services. WRSS provides primary health care services in both Koch County in Unity State and Fangak County in Jonglei state. In these two counties, WRSS runs both PHCU and PHCC services. WRSS intends to expand same health services in Canal/Pigi due to unmet needs in this county. WRSS also ensures protection to children, women at risk and GBV survivors. WRSS will also able to work with protection partners to provide minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV. 1) These services will include; counseling-empathetic listening, inquiring about needs and concerns, validating, and ensuring safety of the victims.2) Treatment of injuries associated with GBV3) STI screening and treatments4) Rapid HIV testing and counseling services and referral to care and treatment as needed5) Post exposure prophylaxis (PEP) within 72 hours for sexual exposure and to be taken for 28 days6) Emergency contraception within 120 hours7) Referral and linkage to other services:-child protection, legal counsel, police, psychosocial support, shelter) . Through these referral pathways, children, women at risk and GBV survivors will be able to access information on prevention and management of GBV. WRSS is also able to handle clinical management of rape within the PHCC and where such cases would be beyond WRSS capacity, WRSS is able to refer such cases to other facilities with better capacity.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The project targets to improve food availability through increased crop production. Farmers will be provided with inputs and training for them to restore their farming capacity and engage in farming activities. This will address two of the main challenges hindering enhanced productivity within the target locations; access to inputs and appropriate farming knowledge. Each farmer will receive inputs support comprising of Sorghum and Maize seeds as well as tools. This will be sufficient to establish at least one fedan of cereal crops (approximately 1 acre). Inputs provided will also be supported with training on improved farming methods. A combination of inputs and training will enhance the production capacity of the farmers. An acre of cereals well cultivated and tendered to is enough to feed a household of six members and provide surplus for sale or stored for future use. The Project intends to reach out to at least 50% of the population in each of the three counties with input support and training and increase household food security by at least three more months. Target beneficiaries will mainly be in IPC 4 and IPC 5 populations in each of the counties. When productivity is increased in 2022, food stored will take the targeted households through the lean season of 2023 and thereby reducing the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).This intervention will aim at reducing prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children, Pregnant and lactating women. WRSS has organized intervention activities through integration centered around nutrition services. WRSS will organize care givers of malnourished children as well as PLWs through care groups which are groups of 12-15 families with malnourished children as well as PLWs. These Care groups will meet regularly to share experiences on managing malnutrition at community level facilitated by care group supervisors, promoters and volunteers. The Care group supervisors, promoters and volunteers will continue to impart knowledge to these beneficiaries through trainings and mentorship. The same Care groups will be targeted for Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) Interventions. They will be provided with inputs and training so as to be able to produce their own food and have sufficient supply at home to prevent relapse of malnourished children as well as PLWs after discharge from the Nutrition program. Similarly, WRSS Nutrition programme working with the FSL program will incorporate kitchen gardens at every Nutrition center to be used for training care givers of malnourished children. Part of the training will involve use of improved agronomic practices as well as cooking demonstrations to impart beneficiaries with knowledge on preparation and utilization of different food staffs including vegetables. These beneficiaries will therefore be able to produce and utilize high nutrient vegetables at home from the inputs and training offered and thus be able to reduce prevalence of global acute malnutrition.
