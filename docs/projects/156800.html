Project Summary


The proposed Action aims to reach the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities in the crisis-affected areas. Target locations have been proposed according to severity of humanitarian needs and number of population in need. They may be altered depending on the evolution of the context on the ground, access and people's movement.
The Action aims to create an access to multi-purpose cash grants to improve food security and coping strategy.
The Action is informed by rapid assessments, observation visits, Oxfam's ongoing work, consultations with Syrian and international NGOs, and reliable secondary data, including the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2018. The action is designed to meet specific life-saving needs around emergency food security (EFS).
Oxfam plans to provide multipurpose cash grants to the most vulnerable HHs in the targeted crsis-affected locations. This plan forms the second phase of piloting cash by Oxfam in Syria. In July 2018 Oxfam completed a pilot cash distribution to families living in Herjelleh IDP site in Rural Damascus. Oxfam will perform thorough market assessments in order to make sure that cash modality is appropriate and to avoid any increase in the inflation in the local markets, in addition Oxfam will monitor price fluctuation in the local markets. Oxfam will perform thorough vulnerability analysis in order to agree on the vulnerability characteristics and select the most vulnerable HHs accordingly.
Oxfam will transfer cash through local licensed Financial Service Providers FSPs after running all the required vetting and checking processes.

Needs

The unabated violence and dire humanitarian situation continues as the Syrian conflict enters its eight year. More than 13.5 million people within Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance. The response in Syria to date has largely been based on in kind assistance, which at times has been hindered by insecurity and access to move goods to areas near to active conflict lines, hard-to-reach areas and besieged locations. Globally, over the past decade, cash-based approaches have become an increasingly common form of humanitarian assistance. Cash-based approaches, which include cash transfers and voucher programs, have only been implemented on a limited scale in Syria. While most assistance to date has been delivered in-kind, there is widespread interest in expanding the use of cash based approaches. The cash modality has not only the potential to offer choice, dignity and value for people affected by crises, but as well the potential decrease in cost of in-kind aid cost and expenses. Gender dynamics has been changing as many men have been forced to flee from the conflict for several reasons, which affected and reduced men presence in many families. Thus, women had to assume both the role of caretaker and breadwinner, making them significant participants in the economy, society and family life. However, due to differential gender roles and discriminatory legal provisions, households with a female main income provider have less access to services, resources and justice. Women are often found to be the head of the household carrying the financial burden; one in every three households in Syria are now women-headed. Some key informative Oxfam volunteers(M&F) in the field across Syria highlighted that some NFI items such as sanitary pads were likely de-prioritized and exchanged with cash by both women and men to be able to fulfill other needs such as food, clothing, education, transportation, etc. This would compromise meeting women and adolescent’s girl’s specific needs and increase the protection risks. Cash is culturally relevant and context specific, where the following two options are potentials: 1- Risks: Cash assistance could potentially increase the risk of domestic violence related to household decision making and expenditures in addition to increase the divorce rate, which could leave a woman destitute2- Positives: it can improve women’s decision making and control over household resources. Their increased ability to contribute to household food security and control an amount of money and make decisions about its use can lead to improved self-esteem especially among most vulnerable women. Feasibility studies carried out in Northern, Central & Southern Syria demonstrate the preferences of affected people to receive cash assistance over in-kind. In WFP’s study (Dec 2017), up to 70% of respondents reported preferring cash assistance with only 3% preferring vouchers. Recent research with Syrian refugees in Jordan complements the feasibility studies conducted inside Syria. The research shows unconditional cash is ‘unequivocally’ better value for money than vouchers. Cash provided recipients with better purchasing power, enabling them to bargain with vendors for more food for the same amount of money, making savings of up to 18%. Moreover, as the study further identifies, as food security levels improved, and with the ability to shop more frequently, cash recipients oriented to purchasing better quality, fresher items. Prior to distribution a needs assessment will collect information on families’ spending priorities in operation. Observational evidence of functioning markets in all targeted areas, indicates cash programming is a feasible response option in the targeted communities. Price monitoring ahead of distribution will ensure the value of the assistance is in line with market conditions.

Implementation of activities

In July 2018 Oxfam completed a pilot cash distribution to families living in Herjelleh IDP site in Rural Damascus. There are two key learnings to highlight from the Herjelleh cash pilot: the cash distribution was implemented directly through the financial service provider without a Syrian NGO partner. Verification of the selected beneficiary list was not possible through this approach. Oxfam is proposing to work with LNGOs and/or local licensed consultancy service provider, who will be able to collect quality data from community through their locally based approach, and maintain oversight of the project throughout implementation. Secondly, the pilot proved that working with private sector financial service providers is feasible. One shortfall of this approach is the dependency on approved national ID cards. Oxfam is exploring other options for cash delivery in the targeted locations. Oxfam’s cash programming will be fully in line with FSA sector and Cash Working Group standards and disciplines. This project is designed to support vulnerable women and men. The methods of implementation and rationale for such methodology will address the latter mentioned potential risks as follows; 1- Consultation process with community’s women to understand further family dynamics to identify the preferred recipient of cash assistance (husband or wife); 2- Community advocacy to denounce sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) and sensitization around SGBV; 3- Monitoring women’s and girls’ safety throughout the project and providing a strong and safe referral and feedback mechanism to access SGBV prevention and response services as well as measure beneficiary’s satisfaction. A gender responsive cash transfer programming checklist practical tool was designed to support Oxfam EFSVL teams work towards reaching CTP standards for gender in emergencies while opening avenues for future programme integration and gender specific initiatives. Oxfam will conduct several activities: - Needs Assessment with focus on specific needs of women and men, girls and boys. - Vulnerability assessment that would reflect power dynamic.- Beneficiary selection with consideration to vulnerability criteria mentioned in analysis for women and men as direct beneficiaries and their families as indirect beneficiaries.- Gender sensitive Market assessment (quality and quantity of products) that would look into accessibility of women and men to the products / services - Conduct regular price and availability monitoring to ensure the intervention is doing no harm to the local markets and community- Cash distribution; with consideration for women and female headed household as target beneficiary. - DDM will be carried out to ensure beneficiaries are satisfied with the process of the distribution- Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM)

Protection Risk Analysis

- Assessment is not done properly using right instrument and methodologies: in order to mitigate against this risk, Inductions will be conducted for consultancy research service provider’s staff to do the assessment according to Oxfam’s relevant standards. Oxfam will prepare detailed analytical framework with clear indicators and will share relevant references with the potential providers that will be asked to prepare their proposals accordingly. Furthermore, the team will be using gender checklist to ensure proper analysis. - The implementation may not be continued and will be stopped for an extended period due to high security concerns, like airstrikes, influence by non-state actors, etc: In case of any security risk, work will be suspended and security measures be adopted to resume the work where possible.beneficiaries cannot receive their due payment from the FSP as 1) they do not have a valid Syrian ID card and because 2) in addition, without a valid ID card, they might be arrested at a checkpoint through which they need to pass to reach the FSP: Oxfam will transport the participants' due payments from the FSP office to the targeted community. The beneficiaries will be asked to submit alternative available legal documents (at least two documents). Distribution plans should address equal and equitable distribution for women and men.- Money is stolen during the transportation: Only small amount of money will be transported. Oxfam will use 2000 value bank notes to minimize the size of the money transported. The total value will be carried by two persons (one from MEAL, one from EFSVL). A car from the Directorate of Social Affairs and Labour will escort Oxfam's vehicle. Directorate's car will include only civilian officials, no weapons will be carried in the Directorate's car.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:- Compromised ability to achieve project objectives due to lack of approvals to work in government and opposition controlled areas - Escalation of conflict resulting in insecurity near project locations which may limit Oxfam’s abilities to achieve project objectives- Difficulty in accessing communities for data collection to assess their actual needs and/or measure impact - Rapid increase of beneficiaries in targeted locations- Increased tension between IDPs, returnees and host communities- Risk of compromised impartiality and independence- Occurrence of gender-based violence - Oxfam is able to transfer funds to Syria through the international banking system due to the indirect consequences of sanctions on Syria. Assumptions:- Security situation in target areas allow movement ability of Oxfam staff & volunteers to operate. - Oxfam is supported by relevant authorities to continue with the unconditional cash distribution programme- Cultural / religious biases to women’s participation in the training are minimised - Intra-household tension and conflict on project benefits are minimised. Mitigating Measures:- Training will be conducted for staff to do the assessment in systematic way and independent verification will be conducted to mitigate the risk of any exclusion/inclusion errors.- involve communities in organise joint planning and avoid duplication- Remote monitoring will be adopted- Assisting people to claim their rights, access available remedies and recover from the effects of abuse. Raise awareness and apply Oxfam safeguarding three policies to prevent and response to abusive and violence behaviours. Utilize referral pathway in coordination with protection sector and Oxfam gender team. - Apply Oxfam gender minimum standards. Harmonise and standardise gender friendly assets among the sector members and indicate quantity and instruction for use. Project gender sensitive analysis and community effective participation expected to provide clearer understanding on systemic influencing factors including power dynamics, shifting gender roles and responsibilities and intra-community cohesion. This would adequately support process of adopting activities to the deferential needs.- In case of any security risk, work will be suspended and security measures be adopted to resume the work where possible.
