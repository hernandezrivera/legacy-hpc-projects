Project Summary

This project is a roll over from 2019 HRP HPSE19-SHL-150755-1. The project overall objective is to ensure that most vulnerable households exposed to impacts of natural disasters (harsh weather and flooding) are supported to meet their basic needs and enhance their coping capacity by providing a timely, efficient, and effective response with emergency shelter support and NFIs (Shelter/NFI Cluster Objective 2). Providing essential shelter support meets basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation (Strategic Objective 2) and strengthens their capacity to cope with the protracted crisis (Strategic Objective 3).
As part of its emergency response for the 2018/2019 winter season, NRC will support already identified 480 most vulnerable households exposed to harsh winter climate (flooding, storms) by providing winter emergency repair & winter NFI support. Winter emergency repairs include basic sealing off for roof, external door and walls, and windows to ensure that affected households are protected from harsh weather (modality: conditional cash). Winter NFIs include sealing off-kit, bedding-kit, and winter clothing sets for boys and girls of climate-affected households (modality: in-kind/voucher/cash).

1 - Needs

77.500 HHs in Gaza are unprotected from harsh winter weather & Flooding (HNO2019), especially those with poor roofing (asbestos/corrugated sheet) which makes themmore vulnerable to potential flood-related displacement. NRC’s assessment “Mapping of Humanitarian Services at Community Level in the Gaza Strip” (Apr 2019) confirmed that people living in high risk food areas are displaced every winter for one or more weeks. Most affected from natural hazards are families in acute poverty with exhausted coping capacity especially in food-prone areas. Selling basic assets tomeet other needs is used as common coping strategy. NRC’s recent assessments alreadyidentified a most vulnerable caseload in need of winter emergency support: based on datafrom MoSD (2018-2019), 593 hardship-HHs are in need for emergency sheltersupport. In Oct 2018, NRC conducted HH vulnerability assessments of 41 out of593 HHs in 3 vulnerable areas in Middle Area (Deir Al Balah refugee camp), Gaza(Al-Shati refugee camp), North Gaza (Jabalia refugee camp). 17% are exposed to floodrisks. 61% live in shelters with poor roofing. More than 50% are not protected againstharsh weather due to major gaps in core shelter items (56% without sealed windows, 56%without functioning external door, 66% without sealed roof; 56% with cracks and holes inexternal wall). 68% lack essential winter-NFIs (bedding set). Based on data from MoE(2018), 12.000 children registered in MoSD’s hardship-database are in need of winterclothing and shoes especially those who live/go to school in food affected areas. NRCalso conducted KIIs with & vulnerability assessments of 10 GMR-affected male/femalehouseholds in Gaza & Khan Younis Governorates (Sep/Oct 2018). 10% are exposed toflood risks. 50% live in shelters with poor roofing. 80% are not protected against harshweather due to major gaps in core shelter items (80% without sealed windows, 70%without functioning external door, 80% without sealed roof; 70% with cracks and holes inexternal wall). 70% lack essential winter-NFIs (bedding sets). According to NRC’s FGDsand vulnerability assessment (Sep 2018), renters – due to their inability to pay rent – havebecome a vulnerable group either at risk of eviction or already evicted. The latter arevisible in public areas with temporary shelters (tents) if they cannot resort to anotheralternative shelter solution, while others move to already overcrowded hosting conditionsor to unsafe shelters (garages, storage-units etc.). The assessment of 10 renter-familieswith low shelter vulnerability found that 63% lack essential winter-NFIs (bedding set)mainly due to high/severe socioeconomic vulnerability. NRC’s IDP assessment (2018)found that out of remaining 4,450 (now 3,000) IDP-families who are still waiting for apermanent housing solution, 12% live in host families & 10% in tents/caravans/make-shiftshelters in inadequate & often unsafe conditions unprotected from harsh winter weather.NRC identified 102 families as 2014-IDPs living in host families & lackingemergency shelter support. Based on above assessments and available data, NRCidentified the most vulnerable cases out of those in need of winter emergency supportbased on a rapid household vulnerability assessment. Out of the identified 480households living in acute poverty, 170 are social hardship-HH registered with MoSD, 110are Great March of Return-injured, and 100 are 2014-conflict IDPs living in hostcommunities. In addition to 100 households lives in rented housing unit in need to winterNFIs (bedding and clothing). In the above mentioned recent NRC assessments (Sep/Oct2018) males and females were consulted & specific needs and concerns documented.Women and girls & those with limited physical mobility are most affected as they areculturally expected to spend more time at home being more time exposed to climateeffects. Boys and girls studying at home are also affected by the exposure to cold/humid climate inside.

2 - Activities

As part of its emergency response for the upcoming winter season, NRC will respond to harsh winter climate (flooding, storms) out of the already identified caseload by providing:1) Winter emergency shelter support to ensure that affected households have core items essential for climate protection such as a sealed roof, external door and windows (modality: conditional cash). 2) Winter-NFIs such as sealing off-kit, winterization-kit, household kit which includes – based on need – bedding/lighting/clothing sets (modality: in-kind/voucher/cash) As part of its emergency response for the 2018/2019 winter season, NRC will support already identified 480 most vulnerable households exposed to harsh winter climate (flooding, storms) by providing winter emergency repair & winter NFIsupport. Winter emergency repairs include basic sealing off for roof, external door and walls, and windows to ensure that affected households are protected from harsh weather (modality: conditional cash). Winter NFIs include sealing off-kit, bedding-kit, and winter clothing sets for boys and girls of climate-affected households (modality: inkind/voucher/cash). NRC engages male and female beneficiaries of all ages in designing the optimal response to each households’ specific needs and protection concerns and supports them in prioritizing their needs considering budget limitations. Male and female beneficiaries are also involved in identifying the most suitable modality of implementationbased on preference and special needs of each case.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In order to prioritize safety and dignity of vulnerable families and communities over other considerations, NRC will conduct protection risk assessments (Do no Harm), adopt a conflict and context sensitive approach in dealing with beneficiaries, ensure informed beneficiary consent and acting on instructions of beneficiaries, securing confidentiality and data protection. In addition, NRC will use additional protection technical expertise to supplement its interventions by coordinating and referring cases to specialized protection actors.NRC’s Protection Policy, Gender Policy, Code of Conduct and Child Safeguarding Policy and guidance notes and tools including Core Competency Quality Assessment tools and the Protection Resource Pack all contribute to reinforcing and providing guidance on how to action the protection policy. NRC’s Code of Conduct holds staff, partners and associates (e.g. contractors) accountable, while projecting a common set of standards to the beneficiaries being served. PSEA prevention and response training is part of the induction for all staff. At the community level, NRC will support multi-sectoral, sustainable, and integrated programming with all NRC’s Core Competencies and with other humanitarian actors, which will allow people to preserve or increase their resources and opportunities, thus making them more resilient, and more likely to find durable solutions. Shelter, WASH, ICLA and UDOC will ensure community engagement on different levels of implementation and echoing community feedback. NRC will support OCHA in the assessment and dissemination of information in order to ensure coordinated prevention and response to emergency. NRC will actively and regularly participate in the Shelter Cluster, Emergency and NFIs Technical Working Group and collaborate with other Clusters and actors on awareness raising, sensitisation and information dissemination activities to ensure that people have meaningful access to information and feedback mechanisms. NRC Advocacy unit will seek to maintain the humanitarian situation of Gaza on the international agenda in an effort to shape priorities and address the impact of the blockade through an evidence-based approach with the consent of the individuals involved, while upholding the do no harm principle.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

NRC programs are designed to ensure that the most vulnerable Palestinians in hard-to-reach areas have access to basic rights and humanitarian assistance, to reduce their vulnerability and build resilience. The community engagement at the design and implementation stage of the project will minimize the risk of impartial access to aid, as NRC will define the vulnerable groups in accordance and coordination with all local stakeholders and sectorial groups (youth, elderly, people with special needs and women representatives) regardless of age, social status and gender.
