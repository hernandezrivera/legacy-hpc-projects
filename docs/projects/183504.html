Project Summary

This proposed project aims to improve access of 34,000 persons, comprised of 9,588 men, 9,418 women, 7,718 boys, 7,276 girls, 1,147 senior citizens and 952 persons with disability, to WASH services in underdeveloped communities which lack WASH services and have been affected by the May 2021 escalations, specifically in eastern Buraij area, Deir Al Balah Governorate, and south of Gaza, Gaza Governorate. The project comes as a direct response to the humanitarian needs of those two regions as the escalations hasn’t only damaged the water and wastewater infrastructure there, but also added further shocks to those areas which require not only the rehabilitating of the escalation-damaged networks, but also expand the works to accommodate to the actual needs of those communities; according to the joint needs assessment conducted with the CMWU, 31,000 and 1,500 people do not have basic access to safe wastewater disposal services at southern Gaza and Al Buraij respectively, knowing that some of those areas has been suffering from the lack of reliable wastewater disposal services for over 15 years. Moreover, around 3,000 persons have no access to safe potable water supply due to the deterioration and damages sustained by the domestic water networks, knowing that the water networks at Al Buraij area are still made of steel and need to be replaces with UPVC pipes. Additionally, 2,000 persons suffer every year from floods caused by improper storm water collection and disposal/injection infrastructure.
The project will not only rehabilitate damaged WASH infrastructure in the aforementioned areas, but will provide safe access to reliable and efficient WASH services to 34,000 persons in the aforementioned regions. The interventions planned for the aforementioned areas will provide:
1- Supplying and installing 1,400 and 4,700-meter, PVC wastewater communal carrier lines in Al Buraij and Gaza respectively with diameters of 160, 200, 250 and 300 mm. the works will include the vacuuming of existing septic tanks/cesspits and filling it with sand to avoid the formation of sinkholes which would damage people and property in addition to installing manholes and household connections for around 1600 households.
2- Supplying and installing UPVC potable water networks at Al Buraij serving around 550 households while replacing the 25-year-old damaged steel carrier lines in addition to installing new household connections under the direct supervision of the CMWU.
3- Establishing a storm water collection and evacuation infrastructure to protect 2,000 persons from winter floods. The implementation will include gutters, manholes and 350-m long PVC carrier line.
Furthermore, the interventions in those areas will also include WASH awareness campaigns to:
i- Introduce the project and its importance/impact on the target areas.
ii- Provide awareness regarding water conservation and sound hygiene practices (including best practices and alerting to negative practices such as flushing diapers and napkins down the toilet).
iii- Increase awareness towards COVID-19 related hygiene practices in line with SOP’s published internationally via the WHO and adopted by governmental and UNRWA institutions.
iv- Distribution of hygiene kits designed to accommodate the needs of all family members based on gender, age, and ability/disability conditions. The kits will also include COVID-19 protective kits, including face masks, latex gloves and 70% rubbing alcohol per WHO and MOH guidelines.
Once completed, the expected impact of the planed interventions will include:
1- Replacing and rehabilitating WASH infrastructure damaged during the May 2021 military escalations.
2- Reducing aquifer/groundwater contamination as a result to damaged wastewater infrastructure and the use of septic tanks/cesspits.
3- Promoting safe hygiene practices, water conservation and containing the spread of COVID-19 among 1600 households located at Eastern Al Buraij and Southern Gaza.

1 - Needs

The May 2021 escalations have added more layers of vulnerability on Gaza Strip’s most marginalized communities, especially when it comes to the WASH sector. Based on the Rapid Damage Needs Assessment (RDNA) conducted by the World Bank Group in partnership with the European Union and the United Nations, Municipal Services in the Gaza Strip suffered damages estimated between 25 to 30 million USD, of which the WASH sector suffered 5-10 million USD alone. Gaza city alone incurred 47% of the overall damages due to the direct and indirect targeting of WASH infrastructure, including pumping stations and underground infrastructure. Furthermore, and based on the Gaza WASH Sector Damage Assessment conducted by the WASH Cluster partners, damages inflicted by water and wastewater networks in the Gaza city jeopardize the lives of 342,000 and 285,444 people respectively. Those damages of course add an additional burden to Gaza’s protracted WASH sector crises; nearly a quarter of Gaza residents live in areas without adequate sanitary sewage infrastructure, while existing infrastructure is in a state of disrepair as a result of shortage of the electricity supply, restrictions on the entry of the required materials including the spare parts, and limited infrastructural operation and maintenance. Despite the fact that Deir Al Balah governorate did not suffer during the escalations as Gaza, there is no deny that the small eastern region of Al Buraij refugee camp also had its share of damages, with around 3,000 persons with no safe access to WASH services, including water, wastewater and storm water services. To cope with the current situation, CMWU maintenance crews refuged makeshift solutions, while many communities still in need for decisive solutions to unreliable wastewater disposal solutions, especially in the areas of Tuffah (Daraj), Zaitoun and Sheikh Ijlin at Gaza city and Abu Meddain, Sinjab and Al Masri at Al Buraij, which suffer from the use of sporadic cesspits which are usually associated with frequent flooding and sudden collapses which make the people’s lives prone to multiple threats. According to rapid assessment conducted in partnership with Gaza Municipality, nearly 45,000 vulnerable individuals (12690 men, 12510 women, 9990 boys and 9810 girls), including 3060 people with disability, are negatively affected by the lack of access to sanitation services. Similarly, Al Buraij municipality, through the CMWU, indicated that 1,500 persons, comprised of 1500 persons (415 women, 423 men, 321 girls and 341 boys) have no access to reliable wastewater disposal services and rely exclusively on cesspits. Children are more exposed to water-borne diseases as they spend most of their time playing in open areas and streets in these localities. Additionally, the residents living without proper access to sanitation have been suffering from bad odours and vector outbreaks and have been forced to spend substantial amounts of money on cesspit vacuuming. According to the Environment Quality Authority in Gaza, on August 19th 2020, around 63 per cent of Gaza’s beaches were contaminated due to the discharge of 117 million litres of raw or partially treated sewage into the Mediterranean. Human Appeal has conducted a field and rapid assessment in Sheikh Ijlin, Tal El Hawa, Zaitoun and Tuffah areas in close cooperation with Gaza Municipality, Ministry of Local Government and CMWU in 2020 and updated the analysis to match the current post escalation situation. It was shockingly found that more than %30 of residents of Gaza governorate are living without adequate access to sustainable sanitation services. Some families in Tuffah and Zaitoun reported having absolutely no access to any sanitation services in the past 15 years and they only rely on septic tanks. According to Municipality of Gaza, septic tanks in these localities are oversaturated and somewhat damaged due to the escalation, which cause frequent flooding in open areas. Same concerns apply to Al Buraij Abu Meddain, As Sa’ayda, Sinjab, and Al Masri who also reported similar issues.Areas with sandy soil like Sheikh Ijlin pose a grief danger to the aquifer due the rapid wastewater percolation to the aquifer. Surveys indicate that 60% of the community rely on cesspits and septic tanks that keep frequently flooding into the streets. On the other hand, the residents of the community cannot afford the constant septic tank vacuuming cost due to the overall sharply deteriorating socio-economic situation in the Gaza Strip. Due to rapid urbanization in this community, cesspits and septic tanks are no longer sufficient to cope with the population growth in the community. Significant consideration should be given to the bombardments and its impact on infrastructure and cesspits, as the impact of military ordinance created seismic waves that affected the integrity of the soil structure in many regions which ultimately lead to the collapse of cesspits and exposing the target areas to diseases.With deteriorated potable water networks and the presence of septic tanks, people at Al Buraij are exposed to the danger of wastewater infiltration to their water supply, meaning that they will bathe, wash and potentially drink from water contaminated with a myriad of water borne diseases that would cause severe illnesses especially to children; the least of which is blue baby syndrome due to inorganic nitrogen compounds contamination, and the risks can go all the way to the contraction of enteric viruses, pathogenic bacteria such as meningococcal meningitis, and wide array of lethal eukaryotic pathogens.Lastly, the exposure to floods at eastern Al Buraij, especially Abu Ataya area, has exposed 2,000 persons to a myriad of risks such as mobility restriction and diseases; surface runoff water is usually laden with pollutants and can infiltrate water networks and affect the population in the target area along with restricting their mobility to and from the target area.

2 - Activities

The salient project interventions include: 1. The installation of Water supply, wastewater disposal and rainwater/floodwater drainage networks Output 1.1: 32,500 (31,000 in Gaza and 1,500 in Al Buraij), comprised of 9002 women, 9165 men, 6955 girls and 7378 boys in addition to 1008 elderly peoples and 910 PWDs, have improved access to safe wastewater disposal services. Output 1.2: 3000 persons (831 women, 846 men, 642 girls and 681 boys) have enhanced access to safe potable water. Output 1.3: 2000 persons (554 women, 564 men, 428 girls and 454 boys) are protected against floods during extremeweather conditions. Sub-activities: 1.1 -Human Appeal and local partners’ technical staff will visit the target areas to conduct an in depth needs assessment / analysis of the target areas’ infrastructure, have consultations with the communities and other relevant key stakeholders and do feasibility assessments. An in-depth market survey will be conducted to assess availability and prices of the material required for various project interventions. Based on the findings of the needs’ assessment and feasibility reports, Bills of Quantities (BoQs) will be prepared for each individual intervention. 1.2 – HA will follow standard procurement procedures as per its policy and guidelines and the donor compliances to procure all the material required for the proposed interventions. It will include complete tender/bid dossier, appropriate method of collecting/inviting bids, bid evaluation, shortlisted supplier negotiations and award of contract. 1.3- The installation of wastewater disposal networks, potable water UPVC network and floodwater drainage lines will be technical facilitated, supported, supervised and monitored by HA local partners and CMWU. HA will provide all the technical support from initial assessments, feasibility surveys and procurements to finalization of the installation work. After the completion of the installation and at the time of handing over, all the drawings, layouts, maps and requirements for O&M will be shared with the CMWU, Gaza Municipality and Al Buraij Municipality. 2. Hygiene and COVID-19 Awareness sessions Output 2.1: 6,000 persons (1,662 women, 1,692 men, 1,284 girls and 1,362 boys) are fully aware of water conservation and safe hygiene practices especially precautions for and prevention to the COVID-19 pandemic. Sub-activities: 2.1 The public health promotion team will prepare the material required (HA global communication department will provide technical support to ensure the messages are appropriate and re-enforcing) to raise the awareness regarding safe hygiene practices and COVID-19 precaution and prevention measures, and water conservation. The awareness raising campaign will ensure women, men, girls and boys of different ages and different levels of education and persons with disabilities are reached out in line with GAM programming actions. They will be educated on prodential use of water, isolating the solid wate from waste water (toilet paper, pads, etc...) in order not to block the network. 2.2 Hygiene kit will be designed (items selected) as per WASH cluster guidelines, COVID-19 standard precautionary measures, and specific needs of senior citizens, infants, women, men, girls and boys. The hygiene kit will mainly cater for the needs of a 6-member family for a month and will include the following: * Hand sanitizer with alcohol solution of at least 70% alcohol• Soap bars, • Adult and baby shampoo• Antiseptic Liquid• Bleach • Disposable face masks• Disposable gloves • Tissues and wet wipes • Skin washing sponge/Shower loofah• Disinfection solution for cleaning shelter/house/toilets similar to Dettol (750 ml)• Detergent• Dish washing sponge• Toilet paper 2.3 Satisfaction surveys will be conducted at different stages of the project cycle to assess the expected impact and benefit to the target population, their feedback, suggestions, participation, and ownership of the interventions 3. Closing and Reporting: Sub-activity: 3.1 HA will ensure timely quality narrative and financial reports are shared with UNOCHA Opt as the contract. All monitoring reports and satisfaction surveys will also be shared with UNOCHA and where required with other key relevant stakeholders. There will be proper handing and taking over ensuring the sustainability of the project interventions beyond the life of the project.

3 - Indicators

Key indicators to assess progress and impact of the proposed project include: 1-# of people have access to improved WASH services affected due to the Israeli restrictions, attacks and blockade: Number of beneficiaries: 3000 persons (831 women, 846 men, 642 girls and 681 boys) Justification: The target areas are located to the East of Al Buraij, near the Israeli borders and thus marginalized and vulnerable. The wastewater disposal, portable water and flood drainage systems are damaged by Israel attacks. Sources of Verification: Photographs, Videos, Surveys, Awareness sessions attendance sheets 2- # of people have access to improved water sources for drinking and domestic purposes: Number of beneficiaries: 3000 persons (831 women, 846 men, 642 girls and 681 boys) Justification: 3000 persons are in need for access to safe and sufficient quantity of potable water for domestic purposes. The water supply lines have got rusty and leaked at several points and need for replacement. The people utilizing water received through these lines are falling victim to water related (borne, based and washed) diseases as wastewater infiltrates into the water supply lines. Sources of Verification: Photographs, Videos, Surveys, awareness sessions attendance sheets, payment requests, progress reports. 3- # of people have access to improved functional and safe sanitation facilities: Number of beneficiaries: 2,820 persons (415 women, 423 men, 321 girls and 341 boys) Justification: people in the target area have no access to safe and reliable wastewater disposal systems. Instead, they rely on cesspits that pose a threat to underground aquifer, road infrastructure and immovable public and private property, risk of people falling into the cesspits and lose lives and overall public health risks. Sources of Verification: Photographs, Videos, Surveys, Awareness sessions attendance sheets, payment requests, progress reports. 4- # of people affected by flooding events are protected: Number of Beneficiaries: 2000 persons (554 women, 564 men, 428 girls and 454 boys) Justification: Abu Ataya area's topography makes it a catchment area for precipitation events which almost exclusively occur in winter season. The accumulating rainwater/floodwater frequently causes considerable threat to the surrounding communities in terms of mobility, accessibility and protection. Sources of Verification: Photographs, Videos, Surveys, Awareness sessions attendance sheets, payment requests, progress reports. 5- # of people benefiting from improved access to sanitation, solid waste, and hygiene services. Number of beneficiaries: 6000 persons (1662 women, 1692 men, 1284 girls and 1362 boys) Justification: There is lack of awareness among the target communities regarding the safe hygiene practices, they do not have basic hygiene items and not following COVID-19 SoPs and also do not have the financial capacity to purchase masks, gloves and sanitizers.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

1- The project aims mainly to evacuate and terminate cesspits before becoming a life-threatening risk, as cesspits tend to collapse and form sinkholes that may inflict harm on people and property.2- The excavation works to implement water and wastewater networks will incorporate installing railing and/or warning tapes to mark the worksite and prevent people from accidently falling. It should be noted that all worksites must include insurance to cover any potential harm inflicted on people.3- Human Appeal - UK upholds CHS principles highly and indicate that the do-no-harm aspect is fully applied and followed through conducting a thorough risk analysis and mitigation plan. 4- the project staff and awareness team are trained on safeguarding policies to avoid any possible harms on the beneficeiries.5- The involvement of beneficeries will allow them express their needs explicitly and introduce them to the compaint mechanism with focus on confidentiality and protection.
