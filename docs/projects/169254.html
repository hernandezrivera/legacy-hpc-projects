Project Summary

In support to the WASH sector and all of its’ objectives under the Beirut Port Explosion Flash Appeal, ACTED is proposing continuity and expansion of the emergency and medium-term WASH intervention across the most affected locations with pre-existing vulnerabilities namely Bourj Hammoud, Basta and Karantina as well as support to the BMLWE. This programme designed in line with the COVID guidelines aims to support national coordination mechanisms of the sector and Lebanese institutions and initiatives on the ground with the area-based and punctual assistance complementing other (Shelter, Food Security, Protection) interventions to estimated 17,200 individuals (with possible double-counting) via:
a) Provision of assets/equipment to the BMLWE and potentially operational support
b) Water supplementation/distribution to the 230 residential/institutional buildings HHs disconnected from the network for estimated 10,200 individuals (4,794 women and 5,406 men)
c) Reconnecting 150 HHs to the water, wastewater and plumbing system/sanitation network and/or provision of the cash assistance (estimated as 750 direct beneficiaries: 353 women and 397 men)
d) Distributions of WASH items to the 7,900 community with specific needs (3,000 elderly, 900 babies) of which 5050 estimated women and 2850 men.
As co-lead of the Urban WASH Task force, a member of the Explosion Response Task Force and WASH Core Group and ACTED advocated to ensure that there was coordination between assessments and implementation between the WASH and shelter sectors. As a partner of EBML since 2013, ACTED responded to immediate requests from EBML to support in rehabilitation of infrastructure and assets, and will implement works in coordination with the sector and UNICEF and ICRC who conducted the initial assessment of infrastructure damage. ACTED has been closely coordinating with all sectors in response to the Beirut explosion and has been on the ground responding to critical needs since August 5th. To date, ACTED has distributed 1064 core relief items (including sealing off kits, dignity and baby kits) and removing over 1232 tons of debris from the streets and HHs of Beirut.
ACTED’s response will ensure access to water and sanitation facilities in residential and public buildings impacted. ACTED will rehabilitate buildings where safe water for drinking and cooking is not sufficient due to damages of water tanks, plumbing systems and network connections. ACTED is closely coordinating with LebRelief to identify and refer households in need of water trucking and supporting LebRelief on the ground in their response to ensure delivery to buildings considered structurally safe (in line with UN-HABITAT guidelines). To ensure access to hygiene items, and in n close coordination with UNICEF and the sector, ACTED will be continue to distribute additional hygiene, baby and elderly kits in the areas assigned in sector coordination to ensure a rapid and coordinated response.
ACTED’s historical presence and long term programming in the target areas made it one of the first and key responders to provide immediate relief to the Beirut Port Explosion. ACTED has been conducting emergency programming in the Greater Beirut area since 2013 and has developed extensive relationships with key local authorities and other critical stakeholders to access the most vulnerable communities in this complex urban environment, providing assistance to a total of 14,137 households (88,922 individuals) in 14 neighbourhoods of Beirut.

1 - Needs

The Beirut Port explosions occurred at a time when Lebanon was already coping with a multifaceted crisis of economic contraction, increasing poverty rates and rising prices of basic commodities. Since October 2019, Lebanon has faced dramatic political and financial challenges which have culminated in an unprecedented economic recession, further compounding existing vulnerabilities among the country’s population – Lebanese and refugees alike. The explosions further compounded extreme vulnerability in many neighbourhoods across Greater Beirut, including several with poor, migrant workers and refugee communities - including Karm el Zeytoun, Bourj Hammoud, Basta and Karantina. Many of these communities have limited resources to reconstruct damaged shelters, access basic services and buy essential commodities. According to key informant interviews (KIIs) KI facilitated with the Municipality of Beirut in ACTED’s Rapid Needs Assessment (https://reliefweb.int/sites/reliefweb.int/files/resources/Lebanon_Beirut%20Blast_Rapid%20Needs%20Assessment%20Report_August%202020_final.pdf), it was estimated by the municipality that 11,160 buildings were damaged. Estimates by the municipality in terms of extent of damage were as follows: In Bourj Hammoud, 70-90% of the houses / dwellings are reportedly destroyed or partly destroyed. 50-90% of the neighbourhood population currently lives in a partially damaged house / dwelling, refusing to leave. This figure increases up to 80% in Karantina, which is located nearer to the blast site, with a large share of those damaged buildings not inhabitable., and 20% of the neighbourhood population currently living in a partially damaged building. According to preliminary assessments by ACTED, 35% of Basta el Tahta, an already extremely underdeveloped area of Beirut is damaged. Damages in residential and public buildings also impacted service networks and delivery, ex. safe water for drinking and cooking is not sufficient in many neighbourhoods where the water networks were damaged due the damages of water tanks and plumbing systems. Preliminarily results indicate that from 20% to 25% a have been disconnected from the main water network (Flash appeal and preliminarily results of the LRC assessments). ACTED’s Rapid assessment menionted above also points out that in Karantina as well as in Nabaa, populations currently rely on water trucking and purchase. In Nabaa, less than 25% of households have water containers with lids, while this share increases to 25-50% in Karantina, and more than 75% in Bourj Hammoud. Caritas and Farah Social Institution are the only two actors reported as providing water assistance in a 1 km ray of the explosion location. BML Water Establishment as a main service provider suffers from significant financial deficit due to high water demands and is not able to cope with the unprecedented strain that the tragedy has put on the service provision not only in water in sanitation but also electricity, waste management, health and other basic services.

2 - Activities

In support of all of the WASH Sector 2020 Flash Appeal Objectives, ACTED will provide critical water and sanitation assistance to the individuals critically affected by the Port explosions in the marginalised urban neighbourhoods of Beirut – namely Bourj Hammoud, Karantina, Basta al Tahta. This will be done via: a) Support Beirut and Mount Lebanon Water Establishment (BMLWE) in rehabilitation of WASH infrastructure and maintain of service 1. Provision of assets/equipment to the BMLWE and potentially operational support1.1. As per the initial needs assessment from the WE this support can include provision of materials/equipment for the rehabilitation of Bourj Hammoud Warehouse. ACTED will support EBML to reconnect residents to EBML services where required, and will continue close coordination with EBML to address urgent needs as they arise in coordination with sector partners.b) Whenever needed, or a gap identified in the replacement of tanks or the connection of the building to waster pblic network, ACTED will be able to respond as part of the shelter response. ACTED will coordinate with actors who have completed technical assessments in affected areas to ensure a coordinated response for the residents who have been impacted by the explosion, ensuring the minimum number of assessments before action. c) Promote and support good hygiene practices 3. Distribute WASH items to the 600 community with specific needs (elderly, babies, women)3.1 ACTED in coordination with UNICEF, UNFPA and other lead actors will continue distribution of respectively 600 hygiene kits, 300 baby kits, 200 elderly kits and 1,000 dignity kits (also included in the Protection appeal) supported through the whole programme (including shelter rehabs, PFA, MHPSS and individual counselling). It shall be noted that the estimated numbers might have change depending on the sector decision and assignment to the specific zone. The final numbers under this activity are also contingent of sector decision on the modality of the assistance – ex. switching to the cash assistance unless not feasible to absorb by local businesses.
