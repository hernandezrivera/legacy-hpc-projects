Project Summary

The aim of this intervention is: conflict-affected communities in BAY state are sustainably protected from, and treated for, the main causes gender-based violence (GBV) against adolescents’ girls and vulnerable women Protection needs, while being protected and provided with the legal assistance they need. To achieve this, the proposed project will be implemented by a consortium led by the International Rescue Committee (IRC), in partnership with the Danish Refugee Council (DRC) and Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC). The consortium will leverage the expertise of each partner to deliver a multi-sectoral, area-based response, providing integrated services across the following sectors: Health, WASH, Nutrition, Food Assistance, Multi-purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA), Nutrition, Economic Recovery and Market Systems (ERMS), Protection and Shelter and Settlements. The consortium aims to meet the most immediate and life-saving needs of IDPs, host communities and returnees in MMC and Jere, which will translate into reduced mortality and improved living conditions. The consortium will also address the ongoing, protracted needs, and supporting early recovery through capacity-building, the strengthening of existing health systems and community-based structures, and increasing the ownership of life-saving, life-sustaining, and life-building services by local authorities, service providers, and local communities to ensure sustainability. In order to build the resilience of individuals and communities, the unique humanitarian and development challenges in MMC and Jere LGAs (presence of service delivery actors juxtaposed against an ongoing flux of IDPs in and out of camps and host communities throughout the city) must be addressed simultaneously by increasing access to quality, sustainable basic services, building sustainable livelihoods, and strengthening local systems and structures.

1 - Needs

Since 2009, AOGs have terrorized communities in Northeast Nigeria, and brought essential services to a near-standstill. People face grave protection threats, markets are regularly targeted, and people are unable to practice agriculture safely outside of LGA capitals, resulting in limited ability to earn income, severe food insecurity and malnutrition. Health infrastructure, staff, and medical supplies are limited, and large-scale displacements of IDPs into neighboring states and urban areas place additional stress on host communities with limited services. The threat of camp closures, following the launch by the Borno State Government of the IDP return process in August 2020, has already led to premature and coerced return of IDPs – an infringement on the Kampala Convention on voluntary returns in safety and dignity. As a result, the most vulnerable populations are exposed to increases protection risks. In Northeast Nigeria, due to pre-existing patriarchal social structures and practices, women and girls are more vulnerable than their male counterparts to violence and abuse. They face increased exposure to Gender-based Violence (GBV) while performing daily tasks, such as traveling to water sources, collecting firewood, and using communal latrines. This in turn makes them more vulnerable to Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) risks, including sexually transmitted infections (STIs), HIV, and unwanted and unsafe pregnancies. Children also face serious protection concerns, including physical abuse, child neglect and child labor. Indeed, while women account for 20% of those affected by the crisis in Northeast Nigeria, 58% are children in need of protection services. . land across the Northeast is contaminated with the presence of explosive ordinances, impacting freedom of movement, socio-economic recovery, durable solutions to displacement, and safe access to affected populations by humanitarian personnel.
