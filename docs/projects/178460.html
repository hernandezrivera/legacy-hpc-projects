Project Summary

In line with SO1 “Enhance access to market-based livelihood opportunities and production” DRC will support vulnerable conflict-affected populations toward self-sufficiency. DRC will aim to achieve the below outcome: “To improve the adaptive capacities and socio-economic well-being of people, civic actors and communities”
Through:
- Increasing households’ income, assets, and consumption of needed goods and services.
DRC believes that households’ income, assets, and consumption of needed goods (including food) and services will increase if people participate in functioning markets which will be restored if people have appropriate skills for income generation and employment; and if people have safe and equal access to capital, markets, and employment; and if business norms enable people’s equitable economic participation.
- Creating more cohesive, inclusive, and equitable communities in Syria.
In order for people to achieve cohesive, inclusive, and equitable communities, marginalized groups must be able to exercise social and economic influence equal to those of other dominant groups and participate in decision-making. A total of 1622 beneficiaries will be targeted through DRC intervention in Daraa, Homs, Hama, and Rural Damascus.
DRC will deliver Early Recovery assistance to persons of concern (PoCs) targeting conflict-affected youth and adults, men and women, including people with disabilities, to develop skills and increase income-generating opportunities with an aim to strengthen their access to livelihoods through microgrants for business development (start-up or scale-up), business development skills training, vocational training, distribution of toolkits in addition to the rehabilitation of community infrastructure restore or improve access to spaces in support of livelihoods. PoCs with protection-related concerns and vulnerabilities will be particularly targeted, and internal and external referrals will be made to ensure that protection services are provided in parallel based on identified needs. Initial needs assessments will be conducted jointly with MoE, MoSAL, and SARC field staff, when applicable. These assessments will allow DRC to select specific locations for its interventions where multi-sectoral assistance will respond to specific priority needs identified on the ground. In addition, at the beginning of 2021, a labor market assessment will be conducted to be informed on the type of training/internship services to provide, as well as the type of businesses to priorities within the business-start up support.

4 - Needs

Identifying the needs was based on initial data collected from different resources including the HNO 2021, but more specifically through Labour Market Assessment and PDM conducted by the DRC team in the targeted locations.According to the HNO 2021, In 2021, the ERL sector estimates 11.6 million people in need of early recovery and livelihood support. 51 percent are estimated to be women, 45 percent children, and 26 percent persons living with disabilities (PLWD). At the end of 2020, unemployment is estimated at 50 percent of the working-age population, and over 60 percent of youth. Overall poverty is estimated close to 90 percent, with an increase of 3-4 percent points compared to 2019 (emergence of the “working poor” category). Extreme poverty is estimated between 55 and 65 percent. People need assistance in re-establishing their livelihoods and regular sources of income. Livelihood opportunities are scarce in Syria and many households depend primarily on unsustainable and negative coping strategies to generate income (sale of property and assets, borrowing, remittances from families and friends, etc.). The food security situation in Syria has steadily worsened during the COVID 19 Pandemic and the enforcement of country-wide lockdowns that will hinder access to persons of concern and in-person services. DRC will support market-based and basic needs interventions to be able to contribute to people of concern resilience by providing vocational skills, supporting business start-up or business scale-up, rehabilitation of livelihoods community infrastructure, and distribution of multipurpose cash.

5 - Implementation of activities

DRC will implement the following activities: Foster access to regular employment: through internships to provide an opportunity to qualified human resource to under-go the supervised training for the period of two to four months and to explore their interest and develop their professional skills and competencies to engage in the local job market. Provide vocational and skills training opportunities: through conducting Vocational Training Courses targeting IDPs, host communities and returnees through and providing toolkits. In preparation for skills training, the field teams will engage with local stakeholders, working in the community to map the local labor market demands and available skills, and to identify the gaps and needs for vocational training. Support to entrepreneurship: through business development skill (BDS) training, PoCs who successfully pass the BDS training will receive start-up grants. Rehabilitate local economic infrastructure: through community, projects to support the rehabilitation of damaged productive infrastructures. This is critical for delivering essential services relating to agriculture/livestock/fishing to ensure the communities can fully recover their livelihoods activities. DRC has strong monitoring systems in place. The Economic Recovery team will conduct different monitoring exercises to measure the progress and the impact of the activities, from PDMs to HHs surveys and FGDs tools will be used to ensure the participation of the people of concern and capture lesson learned and best practices

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Below are some risks identified along with related mitigation measures:- Security situation deteriorates, and humanitarian support (whether directly DRC or contracted vendors) cannot reach the areas of intervention. Likelihood: High, Impact: High. Mitigation: Assess areas situation before intervention and constant communication with donors to have certain levels of flexibility linked to the selected areas of intervention in case it requires shifting due to the lack of access- Selection of beneficiaries and lack of community ownership can create community tensions. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Mitigation: Clear communication plan to be transparent with the people of concern, as well as the mechanism of complaints in place. Close coordination with local authorities, communities, and SARC.- Increased risk of inter-community tension due to inequality of services provided. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Mitigation: Conduct conflict analysis before the implementation of activities and involve communities at all the stages of the intervention

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

COVID 19 pandemic will affect every aspect of the lives of men, women, boys, and girls. Increased prevalence of violence in the home or family, including intimate partner violence (IPV), the loss of employment, restricted access to essential services, and restrictions on freedom of movement can place significant added stress on a family unit and result in the use of negative coping mechanisms. Mitigation: support family members to ensure that COVID-19 prevention and control measures do not exacerbate harmful norms, provide them with psychosocial support, prepare to respond to possible surges in gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse; and ensure that vulnerable populations are included in national surveillance, preparedness, and response plans and activities. DRC at the country level is regularly verifying that critical elements of prevention and response plans to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) are in place and prepare for contingencies where they are significantly impacted by the pandemic through training (including refresher) on Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and PSEA policies; transparent response to SEA complaints; GBV mainstreaming in operational spaces/activities; Feedback and Complaints Reporting Mechanism.
