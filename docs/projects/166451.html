Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) plans to establish a health facility in camp 21 with up to 60 beds to provide isolation and treatment for patients with COVID-19 infection. The facility will serve both the Rohingya refugees and the host communities. The objective is to provide quality care for moderate and severe cases, and to contribute to the reduction of mortality from the disease. This intervention will also reduce transmission rates in the community by isolating sick patients. In order to have maximum impact on health outcomes, the isolation and treatment center will be run in parallel to community approach that includes the continuity of essential non-COVID-19 services, acknowledging that challenges including increased demand for consultations, isolation requirements will be increased. Additionally, SC's facility-focused health programme will be complemented by a network of community health workers who will promote key health messages, identify and refer cases. Given that COVID-19 is a fast spreading respiratory infection, SC will strengthen the services of health posts and primary healthcare center including medical screening services to identify any acute or chronic medical conditions including reproductive health and basic MHPSS conditions. SC is working with WHO to develop community-based approaches for the management of severe cases at household level. SC’s approach is to have Community Health Workers linked to Mobile Medical Units, comprised of Medical Assistants, who are licensed to prescribe, and who can initiate antibiotics, analgesia and palliative care drugs as required. SC ensures active engagement and participation of women, men, youth and children (boys and girls). SC acknowledges within the targeted beneficiaries the different needs of women/girls and men/boys, analyzes these needs within the needs assessment and integrates these needs into the planned activities and outcomes. SC ensures that the principles of protection, gender equality and inclusion and accountability to affected populations are central and cross-cutting in the proposed actions and implementation.

Needs

Bangladesh currently hosts almost 859,808 Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar (UNHCR Population Figures, March 2020). The refugees live in densely populated areas with poor access to water and sanitation, with low quality shelter and limited access to health services (ACAPS, 19 March 2020). As such, they are vulnerable to a serious outbreak. Save the Children anticipates that COVID-19 will reach the refugee camps in Cox’s Bazar and it will have a significant impact. As the coronavirus is highly contagious and the refugee camps are densely populated, it is likely that the Rohingya to be infected easily and become panicked. The capacity to meet the existing health needs of this population are already limited. There is no system in place to screen for COVID-19, and there are no intensive care units or isolation centers for the nearly 1 million refugees, half of whom are children. An outbreak of COVID-19 within this population threatens to severely disrupt an already fragile situation. Given the existing poor health status of the population, with high levels of pre-existing conditions such as hypertension and heart disease, it is likely that the mortality rate will be significantly higher than seen in other settings, with estimates from John Hopkins University putting total numbers of deaths between 1,600 and 2,100 and up to 1,931 new cases daily requiring hospital admission. The number of indirect deaths as a result of COVID-19 may be higher, as people will struggle to access general health services such as routine vaccination and antenatal care and also care for minor injuries, thus the true excess avoidable mortality may not be clear until much later. The acute respiratory tract infections (ARI) already count for a significant burden of disease in the Rohingya camps, with 174,128 reported cases of ARIs, since January 2020 (WHO, March 2020). The WHO/ISCG estimates that there is a need for at least 1,900 isolation beds to service the camps and the surrounding host community in immediate vicinity. Several agencies have volunteered to support the establishment of additional isolation facilities, including Save the Children. Existing inpatient facilities in the camps are already operating close to full bed occupancy, and all scenarios (low, moderate and high) indicate that they will be overwhelmed. Under current conditions, if existing beds are used solely for COVID-19 cases, taking into account the admissions and discharges, 340 bed capacity will be exceeded within 2-4.5 months after the first introduction of the virus, dependent on the transmission rate. Even with additional planned bed capacity, producing a total of 630 beds across the camps the capacity will be exceeded by 3-10 days on average (Johns Hopkins, March 2020). Therefore, a massive amount of additional hospitalization capacity and human resources are needed to respond to the COVID-19 outbreak.

Activities or outputs

Objective 1 (links to SO2). Activity 1. Establish 60-bed Severe Acute Respiratory Infection Isolation and Treatment Centre (SARI ITC) in camp 21 with protection and gender considerations.Activity 2. Procure personal protective equipment (PPE), pharmaceuticals, medical supplies, equipment and non-medical supplies.Objective 2 (links to SO2 and SO3).Activity 3. Create provision of community-based healthcare (for early detection and treatment of COVID-19 cases) and home-based healthcare (for patients with mild and moderate symptoms of COVID-19 at the pick of outbreak); Establish 16 Mobile Medical Units (MMUs) will be under Community Case Management (CCM) in eight camps - 4, 15, 17, 18, 20, 21, 25 and 27.Activity 4. Strengthen community outreach activities for identification of suspected COVID-19 cases, referrals to the appropriate health facilities, and promotion of COVID-19 preventive measures with hygiene kits and key health messages.Activity 5. Strengthen integration of Health into WASH and Nutrition services.Activity 6. Roll out context specific (COVID-19 Crisis) guidance, training and mentoring to the health service providers, community health workers and volunteers.Activity 7. Ensure community engagement aligned with CwC and Risk Management strategy and guidelines.Objective 4 (links to SO1).Activity 8. Strengthen triage and screen of the cases in all 8 health posts and 1 primary healthcare center (PHCC) to separate and identify suspected COVID-19 cases for appropriate referral and further management with physical distancing measures and gender considerations.Activity 9. Ensure accountability to the beneficiaries through utilization of the complaints, feedback and response mechanisms, and the Child Safeguarding Policy and PSEA.Activity 10. Support referral mechanisms for the patients to access to secondary healthcare facilities.Activity 11. Regularly participate in meetings of Health Sector, Strategic Advisory Group and sub-group/working groups and share project information and lessons learned as well as contribute to development of strategies as relevant.Activity 12. Regularly feed COVID-19 information into Health Sector information database.
