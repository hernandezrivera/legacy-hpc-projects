Project Summary


The project will ensure that 6,059 (3,978 girls and 2,081 boys) children, adolescents and youths (3-17 years old) will have access to education programming and attend schools in Abiemnom and Pariang counties, Ruweng Administrative Area. Ten (10) temporary learning spaces will be established in line with Inter-INEE's & government of south Sudan Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and 10 temporary learning spaces or schools rehabilitated. About 3,021 (1,362 girls and 1,659 boys) will have access to new gender sensitive WASH facilities in like sex-segregated pit latrine and rehabilitated boreholes and protected wells line with INEE and sphere standards (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls).
Addition in the project, about 6,059 (3,978 girls and 2,081 boys) children and youth (6-17 years) will benefit from recreational material supplies and children (3-5 years) benefited from Early Childhood and Development (ECD) kits including being provided with teaching and learning materials. About 1,829 pupils (890 girls and 939 boys) with a third being persons with disability (450, 250 girls and 200 boys) youth and adolescents (14-17 years) will also benefit from life-skills and basic vocational training.
The adult direct beneficiaries who are teachers’ and PTA/SMS members will be trained on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-cantered methodologies, targeting 210 teachers (140 males and 80 females) and they will also be trained on PSS, CP and referral mechanisms. Other adult beneficiaries are community education committee members and local education officials who will be trained in school management, Psychosocial Support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction in Education (DRR) targeting 210 people (140 males and 80 females). Key adult beneficiaries will be orientated/trained on PSEA and PSEA guidelines inform of books will be distributed to the school stakeholders to ensure safety of the learners. PSEA reporting mechanism will be made known to the community at all costs to ensure that the beneficiary are protected which is also a form of empowerment of the local community members. The education community mobilisers will be working hand-in-hand with the school stakeholders to identified malnourished children giving special focus to refugees and returnees children so that they are referred to nutrition/health facilities for care. In addition to this, one of the senior teachers in all benefiting schools will be trained on basic response to malnutrition including identification of children, school & community response to malnutrition, referral methods, school kitchen gardening, among others to help fight malnutrition.
Active mobilization campaigns to encourage back-to-learning to increase and retain school enrollment will be organized per school term where Parents, teachers, children, the general community members, leaders, representative of partners, local leaders, among other key stakeholders will be massively engaged to achieve the set project target/goal. In addition to this, education community mobilisers will be trained and equipped with skills on how to monitor teacher attendance and other school developmental activities including ensuring that the planned project activities are implemented. As part of accountability to the affected/benefiting community, Integrated Humanitarian Aid will use the education community mobilisers to be a bridge between the community and partners including government where they will be invited in monthly project meetings to discuss the progress of project. Furthermore, the education community mobilisers will help in need assessment to ensure the need of the vulnerable people especially people with disability are met in the implementation of planned activities. Added to the above, peace clubs will be instituted in the schools promoting protection aimed to have peaceful generation.
Integrated Humanitarian Aid has active presence in these project sites funded by UNICEF & Save the Children.

1. Needs

Humanitarian needs overview for South Sudan issued in January 2022, in the wake of persistent fragility and economic crisis, an estimated 3.4 million (51 per cent girls) school-aged children have lack of or inadequate access to education services in 2021. This is a slight increase from the 3.1 million girls and boys in need in 2020. In addition, there are 98,500 school-aged refugee children, of whom 18,000 children are out of school. The negative impact of COVID-19 and subsequent school closures have resulted in an increased number of children in need of education support. Education in the country has been severely impacted by successive years of conflict, economic instability, displacement and widespread flooding. Only 6 out of every 10 school-aged child, less than 45 per cent of those attending are girls. The current trend in girls’ enrolment in schools in South Sudan is alarming. Girls are more likely to be excluded from receiving education as parents prefer to send boys to school. This increases the risk of girls ending up in an early marriage. In 2019, about 17 per cent of girls were enrolled in pre-primary, 58 per cent in primary and only 25 per cent of girls received a secondary level education. In an education need assessment by IHA team in Abiemnom for Save the Children and UNICEF, our team established that 62% of the villages in Abiemnom hosting a population of more than 55,000 people do not have a functional school. In the same assessment, it was established that the situation in Pariang is even worse where 81% of the villages don’t have a functional school out of a population of 128,000 people. In the last Education need assessment by International organization of Migration (IOM), it was established that for those children attending school, 64% have to walk for more than 60 minutes to reach their place of education, 22% have to walk up to 60 minutes, 12% walk up to 30 minutes, and only 2% walk for less than 15 minutes. This is a likely indication that many of the villages don’t have functional school just as per the assessment done by our team in September 2023. The report estimates that yearly an estimated 1,000 school children drop of school and of these 65% are males while 35% are females. The report by IOM reflects that the construction standards of educational buildings in Ruweng Administrative Area were found to be extremely poor. 55% of the functioning schools are constructed out of local materials such as grass, mud and/or thatch, or other materials. A significant number of the buildings are found to be in need of maintenance 32% of educational facilities are actually classes held in open spaces, mainly under trees for shade. Only 13% of the schools are in permanent structures. Meanwhile, in a need assessment conducted in Abyei Administrative Area before submitting this proposal, IHA staff established that the continued dispute over this region between South and South Sudan has completely cut the region from receiving government services. The disputed region of Abyei lies on the border between Sudan and South Sudan. It is claimed by two communities: nomadic Misseriya (from the north) and pastoral Ngok Dinka (from the south). The above dispute coupled with the ongoing land dispute between Twic Dinka from Warrap and Ngok Dinka communities has increased on humanitarian need of the people. Humanitarian Aid relief trust fund estimates that 240,000 people are in dire need of humanitarian Aid in Abyei. The last clash between the Twic and Ngok dinka has recorded in November 2023 were people were killed at Abyei border which is under the care of UNISFA.The existing few schools in Abyei like Mulmul primary school are over congested because there are few schools in Abyei Region.This project is designed to respond to the above needs to better education performance in Abiemnom and Pariang and Abyei where IHA has active presence and most particularly also to encourage girl child education in benefiting counties.
