Project Summary

This project aims to urgently respond to the current emerging needs among acutely vulnerable farmers, breeders, GMR-affected and female-headed households in order to enhance their access to food, other essential basic goods, and restore livelihood capacities through urgent cash assistance. Through this project, Mercy Corps will target 508 vulnerable households (2,896 individuals) from Khan Younis and Rafah. The two governorates were selected as both areas have vulnerable groups that match the intervention criteria. Khan Younis is the second-highest percentage (55%) with people with accumulated Loan and Debt (SefSec, 2018), as well as farmers in Khan Younis and Rafah severely suffer from agricultural challenges including, high cost of production, and water crises in terms of scarcity, salinity, accessibility and availability of underground water wells (Annex 1). Additionally, 44% of the official GMR injured people list are from Khan Younis and Rafah. The selection also considered the logistical issue when targeting Khan Younis and Rafah, as targeting two nearby governorates reduces some of the operating costs and makes the project more cost-effective.
The project will target 88 female-headed households with limited economic opportunities, 200 crises-affected farmers impacted by the economic deterioration and at risk of losing their livelihood, 50 venerable farmers severely affected by power and irrigation water crises, 20 small poultry breeders who’s their livelihood in urgent need for rehabilitation and has incurred losses resulting from high-cost alternative energy and 150 vulnerable GMR-affected households. Mercy Corps will identify, assess eligibility, and distribute cash assistance to each household in monthly installments to eligible vulnerable farmers to rehabilitate open land, or rehabilitate water ponds and have durable photovoltaic solutions. Breeders will receive cash to rehabilitate their farm and will be provided by durable photovoltaic solutions. Women and Female-Headed households will receive two-months Cash-for-Work opportunities. Vulnerable GMR-affected households will receive an urgent unconditional and unrestricted multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA).
This urgent cash support will allow vulnerable farmers, poultry breeders to have reliable, and timely financial support to addresses their urgent needs, restore their livelihoods, build resilience and improve agricultural production cycles; women, female-headed and GMR-affected households will have improved access to food and other essential basic goods for themselves and their dependents.
Through well-defined vulnerability criteria, Mercy Corps, in partnership with two local NGOs [Al Foukhary Association for Country Development (in Khan Younis) and Yaboos Charity Society (Rafah)], will identify, assess and verify eligibility to select vulnerable households to receive designed assistance, monitor activities, and ensure full coordination and engagement with the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA), Disability Working Group (DWG), Cash Working Group and all relevant stakeholders.

1 - Needs

In the wake of a collapsing economy in Gaza, Palestinian living standards have continued to decline, with aid flows no longer providing the impetus for growth (World Bank). For 12 years, a comprehensive land and sea blockade imposed by Israel has further deteriorated the humanitarian and socio-economic situation. As poverty deepens, vital services are underfunded, inaccessible, or non-existent; the poor quality of essential services do not meet the individual’s most basic needs and are inadequate to respond to the heightened vulnerabilities presented by the current crisis. All socio-economic indicators and humanitarian conditions continue to decline as more than half of the population is living in poverty and without a job (WFP). Unemployment in Q2 of 2019 was 47% (World Bank), and almost 69%of youth and two-thirds of women are unemployed with more inequality of opportunities, including limited access to productive resources and assets and employment (ILO). Salaries of about 62,000 employees were cut by PA since May 2017, while another 22,000 de-facto government employees receive only part of their salaries on an irregular basis (OCHA). Poverty rates in the Gaza Strip have significantly increased from 39% in 2011 to 53% by the end of 2017 (PCBS) and according to SefSec 2018, 68.5% of people in the Gaza Strip are food insecure. The prolonged daily power blackouts and the high price of fuel to operate water pumps, wells, and electric generators reduce the quantity and frequency of the water supply to farmers who depend on wells to irrigate their crops, limiting agricultural production. Additionally, the imposition of a ban on the exit of agricultural produce from Gaza has adversely affected farmers by reducing revenues In September 2019, Mercy Corps conducted an assessment with different vulnerable groups to identify and confirm their needs. The assessment confirmed the needs identified in 2018 and revealing that the farmers are in need of cash assistance; alternative and durable power solutions, rehabilitation of livelihood infrastructure; material inputs; and income opportunities (emphasized by women households to meet their basic needs). The results of the assessment confirmed the challenges mentioned above and needs concluded in previous assessments (2017). The current challenges have forced vulnerable households to adopt negative coping strategies such as: farmers cultivating less land or using less quantity of material inputs to reduce incurred costs especially related to irrigation water, fuel, and herbicides; families selling food and other vital household non-food items received through humanitarian aid programs in order to purchase more relevant or cheaper goods to meet household needs; and purchasing lower quality food and goods.In April 2019, PCDCR published a study on the multi-sectoral needs of those injured in the GMR. Households reported that the assistance provided did not meet 50% of their needs. The identified needs were: food assistance; health care; job opportunities or income sources; NFIs; and shelter adaptation.In conclusion, the needs for farmers and women have not significantly changed from what revealed in HRP 201 (needs are mentioned above, for more details see Annex 1 and 2) The newly emerged needs are around GMR-affected Households: Already vulnerable families with an injured person from GMR, especially those who developed a permanent or temporary disability. Based on the needs assessments, Mercy Corps used the “Gender Analysis Framework” tool to help identify the gender-specific information needed to design and deliver smart, targeted project activities that meet the needs of both men and women.Please see Annex 1: Mercy Corps’ “Needs Assessment - FSS Gaza” Link: https://bit.ly/30U6HXOPlease see Annex 2 PCDCR - English- Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment For GMR Injured https://bit.ly/30RDNHL

2 - Activities

Project activities are designed to address the needs of the most vulnerable community groups based on well-tested approaches, ensure protection to beneficiaries, and do-no-harm to the targeted communities. To maximize the impact, the activities are designed to provide reliable, and timely support that addresses the urgent needs of venerable men and FHH to recover their food security. MC uses standardized social and technical vulnerability selection criteria consistent with humanitarian principles and practices. The selection criteria for farmers/breeders will include small farmers/breeders who are: dependent on their livelihood as their sole source of income, HH dependents (PWD, children <5, chronic diseases, .etc.), own 2-8 dunums/ farm (breeders) and currently in use, incurred losses to their livelihood because of the economic decline, lost/close to losing their production due to increases in operating cost, Not GMR-affected HH, currently affected by the irrigation water and power crises, and has not received similar assistance over the past three months.Built on proven methods using community mobilization and local ownership, MC and local partner NGOs will use an open application process, a strict scoring system, and verification through social and technical visits by experienced staff. Final lists of selected beneficiaries will be cross-checked with Food Security Sector partner’s database, MoA, NGOs, and INGOs providing similar assistance, including ICRC, PUI, ACF and Oxfam. Act01: Urgent cash assistance to venerable crises-affected small farmers for repair and restore their agricultural livelihood assets through open land rehabilitation, provision of seeds and seedlings, rehabilitation of irrigation networks and other essential inputs to restore their production capacity. MC will identify and target most affected and vulnerable (200) farmers: KY (123) and Rafah (77). Act02: Urgent cash assistance to vulnerable small farmers severely affected power and irrigation water crises through the restoring their farmlands water storages by rehabilitation/expanding their water ponds, the provision of durable solar energy solutions (PV) to access irrigation water regularly, and resorting their production capacity. MC will identify and target (50) most affected and vulnerable farmers from KY (31) and Rafah (19).Act03: Urgent cash assistance to vulnerable small poultry breeders through farms rehabilitation, and provision of durable solar energy solutions (PV) to overcome the high cost of energy resulting from long hours of power outage, and for lighting and ventilation during daylight and at night. MC will identify and target (20) most affected and vulnerable breeders from KY (12) and Rafah (8). Act04: Assistance to vulnerable women and FHHs and other vulnerable community members from KY and Rafah. The selection criteria will include FHHs who i) have no or limited employability and limited access to food and other basic needs. ii) Have a large number of dependents. ii) Currently not receiving any similar assistance. (88) Unskilled women and FHH: (44) from KY and (44) from Rafah, will work on producing pastries and clothing. The activity will be hosted by a CBO, ensuring easy access to the beneficiaries. Each beneficiary will receive a 2- month CfW opportunity. Processed food and clothes will be distributed to 1,500 vulnerable families and 3,000 vulnerable children respectively. Activity 05 (New): Distribution of Urgent Cash Assistance to Vulnerable GMR-Affected HHs from KY and Rafah. HHs with a member injured (permeant or temporary disability) in the GMR, on official injury lists, with no source of income, do not receive similar assistance, and unable to meet food and other essential needs, will be selected to receive an urgent Unconditional and unrestricted multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) giving HHs freedom of choice to prioritize their own needs. 150 GMR-affected HHs will be targeted: (92) from KY and (58) from Rafah.

The project team from MC and local partner NGOs will oversee the day to day implementation of the project and produce regular reports. All project activities will be closely coordinated with Food Security Sector coordinator in Gaza. Regular progress reports showing the project activities progress and deliverables will be prepared and shared with all relevant stakeholders and UNOCHA.Mercy Corps promotes a participatory approach to project cycle management. Community participation in the decision making and design of activities is ensured through the Community Committees (CCs). Their engagement ensures a fair and transparent selection of beneficiaries. 8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Mercy Corps maintains a “Community Accountability Reporting Mechanism - CARM” system, that creates accessible, and secure reporting mechanisms to all beneficiaries; including a special private channel for girls and women to report unlawful situations or inappropriate conduct. In the locations where program activities will be conducted, posters will be displayed describing the CARM and providing a Mercy Corps phone number and email address for beneficiaries (or any other interested party) to contact program staff with concerns, complaints or questions. Correspondence to the CARM email address is received by all of the following individuals: Mission Director, Director of Programs, Operations and Human Resources Manager in order to ensure senior management is aware of all issues and that the issues are followed up on and appropriately resolved. Once the complaint received, the committee will review the complaint and refer it back to the concerned team member for follow-up and corrective action. Each category of feedback will have identified response time to the complainer.Beneficiaries will be given the opportunity to convey their feedback about their specific needs during the technical and vulnerability assessments. The program team will interview the farmers, women, and GMR-affected families to collect their thoughts and input in regard to the delivered service in order to respond to their exact needs, which will be considered during the implementation of the cash assistance.Local partner NGOs are also required to adhere to Mercy Corps’ CARM policy when implementing program activities and will be trained on the CARM in order to facilitate receiving and referring feedback to Mercy Corps. Mercy Corps’ comprehensive monitoring systems allow program staff to have real-time access to information on the ground. This allows program managers to quickly adapt programming as necessary and maintain our strict standards and highest quality standards.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project will ensure the safety and dignity of beneficiaries through provision using standardized social and technical vulnerability selection criteria consistent with humanitarian principles and practices and through gender-appropriate activities. There are protection risks associated with cash programming in Gaza including (with associated likelihood): the exclusion of women or individuals with special needs in the process of awarding the cash (e.g. due to secondary social status and restrictions on movement) (medium); and increase in domestic violence and/or intra-household power imbalances (i.e. who controls and spends the cash) (medium) The project will start with a beneficiary orientation that informs male and female household members about the potential risks especially for female-headed households and GMR-affected households; the orientation will include an introduction to Mercy Corps Community Accountability and Reporting (CARM) system; on a case-by-case basis, the conducting of households visits for orientation; use of female staff members for PDM to make female household members more comfortable; training of Mercy Corps and local partner NGO staff on detection of potential protection risks; and in-person visits if households are not able to collect cash. The nature of the project (Cash) helps the beneficiaries to meet their basic needs and recover their food security while upholding their dignity and giving freedom of choice among their needs. Mercy Corps will ensure timely and efficient cash transfer installments that promote the dignity, flexibility, and freedom of choice to households

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

All of the project activities will use an open process application to ensure equal and impartial access to all households in the targeted areas. At the project start, a beneficiary orientation that informs male and female household members about the potential risks especially for female-headed households and GMR-affected households; an orientation that includes an introduction to Mercy Corps Community Accountability and Reporting (CARM) system; on a case-by-case basis, the conducting of households visits for orientation; use of female staff members for PDM to make. female household members more comfortable; training of Mercy Corps and local partner NGO staff on detection of potential protection risks; and in-person visits if households are not able to collect cash. People with disabilities are often marginalized in Gazan society preventing them from traveling and attending outside activities or potentially accessing Banks. Mercy Corps and local partner NGO staff will ask about who has any issue with provided service and will respond accordingly and appropriately maintaining equal access to all vulnerable groups. If disabled heads of households are not the ones accessing cash staff will probe to determine the reason. To ensure the inclusion of People with Disabilities (PWD), Mercy Corps will target the GMR –affected families that have at least one family member with temporary or permanent disability as a result of the GMR as well as, will include PWD family members as part of the vulnerability selection criteria. During and post-implementation, the program team will interview the farmers, women, and GMR-affected families to collect their thoughts and input in regard to the delivered service in order to respond to their exact needs, which will be considered during the implementation of the cash assistance.
