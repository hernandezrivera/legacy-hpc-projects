Project Summary

PUI seeks to improve access to free Nutrition services in Monguno LGA through emergency nutrition activities focusing on Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN), prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition, including severe and moderate acute malnutrition and supplemental nutrition assistance. The proposed intervention will contribute to reducing morbidity and mortality caused by acute malnutrition. The activity will support treatment and prevention services against acute malnutrition. Treatment services will include in-patient (SC) and out-patient therapeutic programs (OTP) to manage cases of under five years old children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) with and without medical complications who are admitted at the Stabilisation Centre and OTPs respectively. PUI will provide supplemental nutrition assistance to treat children suffering from Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM), preventing their status from deteriorating into SAM. PUI will scale-up its pilot Tom Brown model of Supplemental Feeding through a voucher and cash modality. Children suffering from MAM but who cannot enroll in the Tom Brown program will be provided essential Micro-nutrient and referred to partners implementing Supplemental Feeding Program (SFP). Prevention effort will focus on promoting best practices of Maternal, Infant, Young Child Nutrition Practices in Emergency (MIYCN-E) to foster social and behavioral change (SBC). Awareness raising activities will be conducted both at the supported health facilities and within the communities through a network of Community Nutrition Mobilizers (CNMs), Mother Care Groups (MCGs) and father-to-father support groups targeting. The project will target 15,390 individuals (Female 10,797, Male 4,471)

1 - Needs

Findings of the PUI Smart Survey conducted in July-August 2022 indicate that Monguno’s nutritional status is already critical. According to October 2022 Cadre Harmonise and IPC Acute Malnutrition reports, without urgent action, 4.4 million people will face hunger and more than 2 million children under-five will face acute malnutrition at the peak of the 2023 lean season (from June to August) in the BAY states. This figure includes 696,000 children (338,949 in Borno) who are likely to be severely malnourished, a condition associated with increased mortality among children.According to PUI SMART survey findings in Monguno, 27.7% of infants are receiving complementary feeding by their mothers / caregivers after 6 months, or at the right time, meeting the age-appropriate nutritional needs of infants, implying than more than 70% are not. Although, having high percentage of exclusively breastfed children is commendable, it still translated to an alarming prevalence of SAM recorded; perhaps due to the low complementary feeding pattern in the two areas The alarming prevalence of SAM outcomes in Monguno is further buttressed by the fact that only 21.8% of those in the age category of (6-23) months, consumed from at least five out of nine defined food groups during the previous day. Therefore, they met their minimum dietary divers it, however about 70% and above of the children were found not to have met the minimum requirement for dietary diversity.Overall, there is the need for all mothers/caregivers to adhere strictly to the norm of initiating breastfeeding within the first 1 hour of birth for maximum benefit both to the child, his immediate family and community at large. Although, this study did not sought to find out why complementary feeding was not effectively initiated in Monguno; it could be attributed to the lean period usually associated with non-availability of sufficient food coupled with the present deteriorating economic situation in the Nigeria. Therefore there is a need to improve MIYCN advocacy and enlightenment campaigns with urgent humanitarian intervention in Monguno LGA. In 2022, PUI supported 45 Community nutrition Mobilizers and 120 mother care groups (10 mothers per group) in t5 supported health facilities’ catchment population areas. Mother care groups, community nutrition mobilizers, community health workers, community reproductive health workers are part community outreach and sensitization of child and mother health care practices with special focus on MIYCN-E practices for <2 years children, pregnant and lactating women. In 2022, 64,025 individuals received messages from behavioral change interventions to improve MIYCN-E practices. PUI targeted 7,821 children on receiving micronutrient supplementation as against 5808 children who received MNP supplementation across the five PUI supported locations. Lower achievement was the result of irregular supply chain from UNICEF and sector.

2 - Activities

Training of health care staff on the prevention and management of acute malnutrition: PUI will conduct regular assessments of the knowledge and skills gaps of staff members through supportive supervision. Any gaps identified will be addressed through the planning and implementation of new or refresher training, with a focus on Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) and the treatment of malnourished children aged 6-59 months in the TSFP, OTP and SC. Support and rehabilitate the sites for managing acute malnutrition: In Monguno, PUI will continue to support three acute malnutrition treatment sites, including two Outpatient Therapeutic Programmes (OTPs) and one Stabilization Centre (SC). The two OTPs are located in Monguno Maternal and Child Health (MCH) and Gana Ali PHCC, while the SC is situated in the MCH. Screening of children 6-59 months using MUAC tape for malnutrition: PUI trained Community Nutrition Mobilizers (CNM) will conduct scheduled door-to-door MUAC screenings on all children 6-59 months within their assigned coverage area within the community in PUI operational areas on a bi-weekly basis. Provide treatment of acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM): PUI will continue to provide treatment of acute malnutrition, SAM and MAM, to children under 59 months old, following the current Nigeria Nutrition Protocol, supporting 2 Outpatient therapeutic Programs (OTPs), one Stabilization Centre (SC) and Tom Brown activities.Children under 5 suffering from SAM without medical complications will be referred to and admitted in the two OTPs PUI will continue to support, while children suffering from SAM with medical complications will be referred to the SC. Each child discharged from the SC will be referred to the nearest OTP. The SC bed capacity will be increased from 25 to 35 during the peak season to meet the increasing needs. Tom Brown’s community-based model approach for the treatment of MAM children was launched in Monguno in late January 2021. Following this pilot approach, mothers / caregivers of children identified as suffering from MAM are gathered into groups where they are taught by Lead Mothers how to prepare a nutrient rich supplemental food with local ingredients over a 12 to 13 weeks program. The first 3 weeks are usually dedicated to the children aged 06-59 months’ active screening, with the identification, and selection, of those suffering from MAM. They are then gathered in groups of 15 children each, with their mothers/caregivers, forming the first batch of beneficiaries. Every two weeks, they then gather in the Lead Mothers house to prepare with local ingredients the Tom Brown food, including millet, sorghum, groundnut and soybeans, PUI procures the local ingredients, and pays for the cost of processing, which involves sun-drying of the cereals, de-husking, roasting and milling. Each mother is given a ration (1.5 kg per child per week) to last for 2 weeks, with three meals a day. After 2 weeks, they gather again to process another feed, for a total of 8 weeks. The children are graduated at the end of these 8 weeks and another batch of beneficiaries is formed. Along with this cooking recipe, the use of clean water and hygiene practices are part of the education mothers receive. They are then encouraged to cascade the knowledge they have acquired to other community members.Establishment or strengthening of MCGs: Mother Care groups (MCGs) are community-based groups composed of pregnant and lactating women and mothers / caregivers of children aged 0-24 months, who are resident in the communities, and headed by one Lead Mother. These women are selected through CNMs in active collaboration with community elders and stakeholders in liaison with the Bulamas in each Bulama administrative area. The Lead Mother is selected based on some criteria by the group members themselves.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

All PUI’s interventions are designed and implemented respecting PUI’s four pillars of Safe and Dignified Programming: promote the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries, ensure equitable and meaningful access, and support the accountability to, participation and empowerment of the communities PUI works with. Safe programming is mainstreamed throughout all sectors and activities. All PUI staff have been trained and will receive refreshment on Safe programming principles, and safeguarding policies. PUI has effective protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) networks and systems in place beside a robust child protection policy. Both policies are explained, read, accepted and signed by all the PUI staff, vendors, suppliers and service providers.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The routine monitoring of activities and feedback and complaints informs the proposed nutrition program programing. As a core requirement of the program, PUI will also actively seek input from the program participants during its implementation to adapt where necessary and to improve policy and practices. Communities whom we are working with will be informed about PUI’s complaints and a feedback mechanism through which complaints can be reported to PUI’s MEAL department. PUI MEAL department has accessible and well-understood mechanisms for suggestions and complaints in all supported nutrition centers in collaboration with the Protection Coordinator and Human Resource department to ensure that the complaints mechanisms are in line with PUI policies and the Protection Cluster recommendations. In coordination with the protection team, PUI MEAL team regularly conducts presentations and briefings during monthly community committee meetings on main trends, issues, and mitigation measures to ensure community engagement and understanding of the feedback mechanism. PUI MEAL department will guarantee reception, follow-up and processing of all the complaints received, through established feedback and complaints channels.
