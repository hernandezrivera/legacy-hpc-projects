Project Summary

This project seeks to implement life-saving activities in line with improving access to clean water and sanitation facilities among refugee and host communities in refugee’s relocation areas in Maradi and Tillabery region. In addition to improving access to facilities the project will promote good hygiene practices as a means to prevent diseases especially during this rainy season. The response team will seek to strengthen community mobilization of 17314 people of both refugees and host communities for 2472 households (HH). By the end of the project from refugees and host communities:
 2472 HH Will access safe water through the installation of mechanized systems of water supply
 500 HH Will access hygienic sanitation facilities.
 500 HH Will receive hygiene kits that include (Sandals, perfume, flashlight, soaps, nail cut).
 2472 Will benefit from good hygiene practice promotion activities
 Community mobilization activities will enhance social cohesion among refugees and host communities
Further to that, management committees and volunteers will be trained to ensure proper functioning of the infrastructures and continue hygiene promotion activities.
By the end of this project, refugees will have received essential WASH NFIs that meet their basic needs, have access to sustainable safe water source as well as sanitation facilities enabling them to live with dignity within host communities with minimized risk of conflict and misunderstanding that may emerge due to sharing of limited resources. The burden and stress on the host population, with their already limited resources will be alleviated, and conditions created for them to continue welcoming those fleeing persecution.

Besoins

Over the last couple of months, the north-western Nigerian states of Katsina, Sokoto and Zamfara have witnessed a spike in violence and criminality as well as mass human rights violations by non-state armed groups. Twenty-six villages, according to the DREC, have welcomed new refugees. This violence has triggered massive population displacements within the states and into Niger Republic. It is estimated that over 60,000 people are internally displaced in these states and that over 35,000 people have crossed the border into Niger. Since the beginning of the current crisis, the Government of Niger has led consultations with UNHCR, UN agencies and humanitarian actors in order to coordinate and provide assistance to refugees and vulnerable host communities. Joint assessments were conducted on 9 and 10 June, with the government's technical services, UN agencies and several INGOs, including World Vision, the Red Cross, IRC and ACTED. According to the directorate of Civil Status, Migration and Refugees (DREC) - Ministry of Interior, there were 35,000 refugees as of July 25th (UNHCR). In the Tillaberi region 138,000 people are displaced including 100.000 IDPs, 38.000 refugees from Mali and Burkina. In order to save their lives, Nigerians abandoned their homes and in the process abandoned their livelihoods, overnight finding themselves in the most desperate of situations. The most urgent needs as established by UNHCR are: food, water, shelter, protection and non-food items. World Vision has been approached by UNHCR for specific support and urgent support to the WASH needs (water supply, construction of sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion). As of the end of August 2019, close to 40,000 people in Gudan Roumji and Madarounfa, including refugees and host communities, had no access to decent sanitation or clean water. According to our own needs assessment, confirmed by other assessments conducted jointly by HCR and humanitarian actors in Maradi, most of these people are living in unsafe environments (open bush) without proper or adequate sanitation and hygiene facilities. They are constantly exposed to infections and the risk contamination.Despite efforts committed by different humanitarian actors there remains huge needs.World Vision will expand its operations to provide clean water, basic latrines and promote good hygiene practices in emergencies in Maradi and Tillaberi regions.The need for clean water and sanitation / hygiene services is very critical. Thus access to the latrines is already very limited for the host population while the refugees who arrived in the same communities must start a new life in a new site, which means that everything is missing: water, sanitation, hygiene, etc.
