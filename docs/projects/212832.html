Project Summary

In 2024, a staggering 16.7 million people in Syria, comprising 8.4 million females and 8.3 million males, require humanitarian assistance, witnessing a rise from the 15.3 million recorded in 2023. This dire situation is exacerbated by the aftermath of a series of earthquakes in February 2023, causing significant displacement, loss of lives, and severe damage to already fragile infrastructures. HNO for 2024 reveals a critical scenario where 5.5 million people, including over 2 million residing in last resort sites, are displaced. The earthquakes resulted in nearly 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 injuries in Syria, further straining a population already grappling with prolonged hostilities and economic crises. Families lost breadwinners, adding to the vulnerability of millions unable to meet basic needs.
Through the proposed intervention, SIF will contribute to achieving objectives of the sector through 1) capacity building activity for peaceful coexistence and social cohesion-GP20- through the provision of organizational trainings on PSEA; Diversity, Equity and Inclusion 2) Direct Risk Education (RE) session delivered by public service providers 3) Training of humanitarians/community focal points and public service providers in Explosive Ordnance Risk Education EORE 4) Providing children with school-based psychosocial support programs in non-formal settings under the education sector, serving as a complementary approach to NFE activities.
Locations for intervention were strategically chosen based on needs severity, accessibility, and SIF experience, aligning with a comprehensive, multisectoral and multiyear approach. The aim is to promote sustainability, community self-reliance, and resilience.
The intervention will primarily focus on the age group of 12-18, tailoring awareness sessions to address their specific developmental needs. Youth and adolescents will actively participate in all project phases, with special consideration for children with disabilities through tailored activities and dedicated staff. Targeted governorates include Aleppo, As-Sweida, Dara, Hama, Homs, Idleb, Lattakia, Quneitra, and Rural Damascus. The intervention will prioritize gender and age aspects across sub-sectors, including Child Protection (CP), Gender-Based Violence (GBV), and general protection issues. Cultural preferences, sensitivities, and the unique needs of vulnerable groups will be continuously evaluated throughout implementation. The project will adhere to safeguarding and protection principles, including the Do No Harm approach, SPHERE standards, and humanitarian principles. SIF's commitment to accountability to the affected population will be realized through strengthened child-friendly community-based complaint mechanisms and improved participatory assessments. Close coordination with various stakeholders, including MoE, MoSAL, Protection sector, INGOs, contractors, SARC, Clusters, local committees, and community leaders, will ensure an effective response, bridging gaps in available services and enhancing access for those in need. Child Protection assistance will complement Education, WASH, Shelter, and Early Recovery and Livelihood programs. SIF emphasizes youth participation, ensuring effective resource utilization, adherence to workplans, and gender-disaggregated discussions. Pre and post-tests will be conducted to evaluate the impact of awareness and training sessions, employing a participatory approach across all stages. In 2023, SIF targeted 222,077 individuals through protection interventions. In 2025, SIF plans to expand its operations to address rising humanitarian needs in new areas. Committed to identifying and responding to urgent needs in communities facing significant challenges, this expansion aligns with SIF's core mission of providing timely and impactful assistance to vulnerable populations, ensuring no one is left without support.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

SIF will submit project and implementation approvals to the relevant authorities. Selection of locations and PiN will be conducted in collaboration with sector, donors, SARC and municipalities. Maintaining transparency and inclusivity, SIF engages continuously with affected populations through participatory approaches, ensuring structured dialogues with diverse groups to address protection concerns. Regular feedback mechanisms, community consultations, and grievance redressal processes are integral to SIF's accountability framework, incorporating community needs into project planning and implementation, fostering ownership and empowerment among beneficiaries.The risk of sexual exploitation is addressed by implementing comprehensive measures. These include integrating PSEA indicators into the monitoring framework, conducting rigorous vetting during recruitment with a focus on application and reference stages, ensuring the signing of a code of conduct that strictly prohibits SEA for all individuals associated with SIF, including staff, volunteers, and contractors. Additionally, contractors sign the SIF Code of Conduct at the time of the purchase order. SIF employees undergo PSEA training, and each targeted governorate has a designated PSEA focal point. Annual awareness sessions for community members cover the definition of SEA and reporting/whistleblowing channels. Community-based complaint mechanisms are accessible in all response locations, and SIF has developed a structured process, steps, and timeframe for the investigation of SEA allegations. SIF faces various risks, each with a likelihood, impact, and mitigation strategy. Measures include dialogue and follow-up to mitigate delays in local authority approvals, donor coordination and BoQ adjustments to manage inflation-driven price hikes, monitoring and preparedness to address security deterioration risks, financial controls and beneficiary verification to mitigate fraud and aid diversion risks, prevention measures and testing to address COVID-19 and cholera spread risks, and monitoring and planning to mitigate fuel shortage risks.The Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) activities will be conducted in coordination with the Ministry of Education (MoE). This collaboration aims to equip school staff with the necessary materials and skills to conduct EORE sessions effectively. Moreover, it will ensure that children and adolescents are provided with the awareness needed to recognize and avoid the risks of explosive ordnance, as well as how to respond to such dangers appropriately.This initiative is particularly crucial in locations like Dara, which are heavily affected by mine risks. By focusing on this aspect of the intervention, SIF aims to mitigate the potential threats posed by explosive remnants of war and promote safety within communities, especially among vulnerable groups such as children and adolescents
