Project Summary

This proposal is in line with the objectives and the framework of the OPTI country 2021 HRP strategy under objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL and objective 3: The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. Furthermore, this project is aligned to the WASH cluster HRP objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank and objective 3: Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank, to reduce threats and cope with emergencies and shocks.
The project’s overall objective is to contribute to strengthening the capacity of essential WASH services for the vulnerable population threatened by the ongoing Covid-19 crisis in the Gaza Strip, while the specific objective of the project is: To provide an appropriate, integrated WASH response for vulnerable communities and households affected by the ongoing Covid-19 crisis including women, children, girls and boys in fragile and critically under-served communities. The objectives are in line with the needs identified in the humanitarian need overview (HNO) and Oxfam’s recent assessments.

1 - Needs

WASH vulnerabilities in the oPt have been driven by the longstanding Israeli occupation and blockade, the internal Palestinian divide, and, most recently the COVID-19 outbreak, along with the fiscal and socioeconomic consequences of these factors. In the Gaza Strip the ability of the authorities and service providers to upgrade, maintain and operate at full capacity more than 500 water and sanitation facilities has been undermined by major funding gaps impeding the purchase of required inputs. The COVID -19 outbreak and its associated lockdowns, compounded by the growing PA budget deficit and suspension of salaries for government employees, led to the erosion of WASH coping mechanism by vulnerable households, alongside the adoption of negative practices, such as reducing water consumption and consuming water from unsafe sources. According to HNO 2021, 20,200 poor families in the Gaza strip rely on water collection in bottles and buckets from public filling points. This exacerbates their exposure to waterborne diseases and the risk of a waterborne epidemics affecting the wider population. Additionally, there are major shortages of adequate sanitation facilities at the HH level. Coping mechanisms common in vulnerable areas in Gaza include the sharing of latrines by several HHs and the use of shelter spaces other than bathrooms for handwashing or bathing. Such practices generate privacy and protection concerns, particularly for women and girls. There are longstanding challenges and critical shortages in the health system with chronically low stocks of essential drugs, contamination of drinking water and poor electricity coverage. At least 140 health care units in Gaza suffer from insufficient WASH facilities (toilets, drinking and handwashing stations) to meet the Ministry of Health (MoH) standards. This resulted in the overcrowding of existing facilities and the undermining of hygiene practices, raise especial concern within the context of the ongoing pandemic. Based on the HNO 2021, Oxfam’s Covid-19 Response Plan, WASH cluster Vulnerability Map conducted in Aug 2020, Oxfam’s gender analysis (May 2020), coordination efforts with WASH cluster members including Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU), and Palestinian Water Authority (PWA), and Oxfam’s rapid needs assessment carried out in September 2020 (conducted remotely through group mobile conversations complying to WHO’s and the MoH’s COVID-19 mitigation measures, interventions at community, HHs and Health Care Facilities (HCFs) were identified as per below: 1- Community level: CMWU and Oxfam agreed to prioritize two critical water wells that serve vulnerable communities in Gaza strip with water consumption less than 50 l/c/d in the middle area (Alshurta, Jafarawi and Abu Mogheseb, Hayi Al Wadi and Juhor al Dik). Accordingly, PEF carried out a technical field assessment in September 2020 to identify the specific needs of each well by CMWU, Oxfam and PEF to the T52 and the Eastern water wells in the middle area. In addition, 4 remote FGDs (2 women and 2 Men) were conducted in the targeted communities with 23 participants (8 women and 15 men), participants confirmed their need to rehabilitee the prioritized two water wells to ensure adequate access to water which would improve care responsibilities and house chores for women and children as they bear the brunt of securing water in their HHs. In addition to the development of an operational and management plan to ensure the operational sustainability of the public WASH facilities in Gaza strip. 2- HHs level: a coordination discussion took place between Oxfam and CMWU, PWA, AAH, GVC, and Save the Children in Sep 2020. Households with outdated sanitation facilities in Aslan community in Bait Lahia, Al Shejaiya and El-Zaytoun in Gaza were identified to be targeted under this intervention by Oxfam. Accordingly, Oxfam and MAAN conducted a rapid assessment on 2nd Sep.2020 where 6 remote FGDs (3 women and 3 Men) were carried out in the targeted communities with 32 participants (12 women and 20 men) and 1 KII conducted with Municipality representative. The assessment revealed that 100 HHs in Aslan are not connected to sewage networks, therefore rely on unsafe sewage disposal systems including cesspits and septic tanks. The assessment highlighted the need to improve access to adequate water and sanitation services at HH level for 250 families in the targeted communities. The assessment also found that the majority of population have poor hygiene practices especially in terms of water handling which increases the risk of COVID-19 infection. Moreover, the updated Oxfam’s assessment during May 2020 indicated that women are mostly affected by the lack of access to safe water and sanitation services, reporting high concerns about their children’s hygiene, health and safety. WASH needs were prioritized to improve adequate wate and sanitation services including proper sewage water disposal through installing of sewage water connections. 3- Health care facilities level: In September 2020, Oxfam and MA’AN agreed with the MoH in close coordination with WASH cluster members and GVC to conduct in-depth assessments for three health care centres located in vulnerable communities with insufficient and inadequate WASH facilities. The assessment illustrated the need to intervene in the three identified Health care centres, Sabha Al Aharzeen and AL Sourani located in Al Shujaia and Horria Health Care Centre in Al Tofah locality by upgrading the water network and replacement of old deteriorated water and sanitation items, replacement of the old existing desalination unit, construction of a water well to ensure sustainable water flow for the unit and installation of a PV Solar system to operate the desalination plant and the well, in addition to carry out community engagement activities to promote women’s participation and representation to influence the attitude of predominantly male participants.

2 - Activities

This project will respond to needs of targeted male & female beneficiaries in the targeted areas including children, elderly and people with disabilities as the rehabilitation and maintenance work that will implemented for the two selected wells will enhance the sustainability of the wells operations which will improve the access of targeted people to sufficient quantity of domestic water for cooking, personal and domestic hygiene and will contribute to alleviating the burden on women and children. While the rehabilitation and upgrading of WASH facilities at HHs and HCFs will contribute to alleviating the burden on women and children and mitigate the transmission of COVID-19 outbreak. The complementary hygiene promotion and public awareness campaigns will enhance gender and age responsiveness by enhancing women’s knowledge and awareness related to COVID-19 specifically. The main humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration especially the do-no-harm principle. This is particularly relevant in the Gaza context, where social and political instability and conflict are evident in many areas with conservative protection norms. Explicit plans to reduce risks, and to competently mitigate and respond to any incident, including safeguarding training (i.e. on sexual harassment, discrimination and exploitation, and child protection) will be elaborated by Oxfam and monitored, adjusted, refreshed if the context changes. Oxfam will be responsible for the implementation of the action in coordination with PEF, MA’AN and relevant stakeholders (PWA, CMWU and WASH cluster) to ensure access to systems, procedures and standards to achieve effective, high quality delivery. Oxfam’s particular added value will be in its ability to bring together multiple stakeholders, local authorities, end users through its active participation in the WASH cluster and engagement with related stakeholders, ensure action delivery in accordance to the agreed upon project action plan and budget in compliance to the donor programmatic, visibility, reporting, financial and procurement guidelines. In addition, Oxfam will provide advice and support on gender and protection sensitive project implementation and gender mainstreaming, support PEF and MA’AN in designing and implementing the gender and hygiene promotion plan. PEF and MA'AN will lead the implementation of the community mobilization actions, the beneficiaries’ selection process, the engineering technical assessment and announce the tenders in local newspapers in compliance with the donor, Oxfam and their organization’s procurement regulations and guidelines.MA'AN and PEF work closely with vulnerable communities in diverse locations including farmers, women, children and youth. They have extensive experience in WASH and access to sustainable and safe water, sanitation services in the Gaza strip. MA'AN and PEF were fully engaged in the project’s design, needs assessment and community consultations. The two partners will implement the project in coordination with Oxfam with joint responsibility for M&E activities and stakeholder engagement. Noting that PEF will lead the implementation of output 1.1 in the southern governorates while MA’AN will lead the implementation of outputs 2.1 and 2.3 In Gaza North and Gaza governorates. Based on Oxfam’s Common global Approach to MEAL and Social Accountability (CAMSA) the project will integrate a comprehensive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) framework. Monitoring of project actions will be conducted at two levels: Leadership Monitoring: Oxfam’s MEAL staff will lead the design of the project intervention logical framework, theory of change, MEAL planning, MEAL actions tracking, project internal and external evaluations, and learning. Technical Monitoring: Oxfam WASH technical team and partners will ensure the quality of implementation in line with technical standards, action plan and budget as well as baseline and end-line data collection. Oxfam and partners will conduct direct implementation of the project actions and day to day follow up by their field teams and experts. The MEAL framework will promote gender sensitive collaborative and effective, results-focused and accountable MEAL and social accountability practices throughout project implementation. Learning and feedback will also be key practices that will be an integral part of the MEAL framework and will be documented by Oxfam in close coordination with implementing partners. In case of emergency, Oxfam’s emergency MEAL minimum standards as well as minimum gender mainstreaming standards will be applied. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection principles are considered in the project as part of the humanitarian principles governing Oxfam’s humanitarian interventions. Oxfam premised its project design on the ‘do no harm approach’ ensuring that project activities or any resulting consequences do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to the essential WASH needs which would have put them at risk, had it not been available, and by conducting ongoing monitoring and collecting beneficiaries' feedback to be aware of any potential harm and work on prevention measures. The action responds to Oxfam’s and PWA’s needs analysis where women highlighted the need to be engaged in community committees and municipality activities due to their severe lack of representation in public life. Community mobilization sessions on gender, water and sanitation will be implemented encouraging participation of men and women community leaders and mentors. These sessions will also serve as an awareness raising tool to sensitize households and community to accept women participation. This will contribute in mitigating any risk/ harm that women could face as a result of this newfound participation in public life. Gender will be mainstreamed, ensuring that the needs of men and women, girls and boys are addressed in line with Oxfam’s gender in emergencies minimum standards and gender mainstreaming strategies. This action’s response is grounded on the fact that the lives and dignity of all women and men beneficiaries including people with disabilities, youth, children and elderly, both direct and indirect, must be protected. Key humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration especially the do-no-harm principle, particularly important in Gaza’s context, where social and political instability and conflict are evident in many areas with conservative gender norms. Local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement and ongoing consultation with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the project, thus enhancing community engagement and reducing any potential protection risks or exclusion at community level. Building the capacities of targeted communities to improve their self-protection mechanisms will be ensured through conducting orientation sessions to inform and consult with targeted communities about services that will be provided as well as on safety measures and access modalities. It is worth mentioning that preserving dignity is essential to maintain self-esteem and confidence, important to cope in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence will be positively reflected and will enhance the roles of each member of the household and will result in the overall improved wellbeing of the family, especially children as care receivers.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

How does the project enable equal and impartial access to assistance and services and the targeting of vulnerable groups and people with specific needs?As part of the main principles of protection mainstreaming in all of Oxfam’s humanitarian work, special attention is paid to achieve equal and impartial access to aid. This will be done throughout the different stages of the project; the selection of targeted communities is premised on concrete and updated needs assessments confirming the vulnerability and the need of the intervention. Similarly, selecting the beneficiaries follows a clear process with selection criteria and presents equal opportunity for all eligible to benefit. Gender and age are also taken into consideration meaning that women, men, youth, elderly people, people with disabilities and children will be able to benefit from the intervention as fit for their needs without any discrimination based on their gender, age or status. Using a combination of community outreach approach, using virtual platforms for information sharing and actual work in the field will also work to address any obstacles related to the spread of COVID 19 that could hinder access to aid. Having a feedback mechanism available and accessible for all the targeted community will facilitate knowing people’s concerns and comments that could highlight obstacles of access whether physical or related to the processes of implementing the intervention and would inform response to ensure equality and impartiality.
