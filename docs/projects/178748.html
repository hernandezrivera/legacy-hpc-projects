Project Summary

NES population is facing an extreme density of explosive hazards contamination: Explosive Remains of War (ERW) and Improvised Explosive Devices (IED). The population is confronted not only with the damage of a 10 year-conflict affecting their living conditions and their long-term prospects, but also with the impossible projection in a "post-war" period as the threat of explosive hazards of all types forbids the return and the rehabilitation of cities, villages, social and economic infrastructures.
Humanitarian response is also heavily affected by that, especially making it more difficult to access some areas or start rehabilitation projects.
Halte aux Mines Antipersonnel (HAMAP-Humanitaire or HAMAP) project would aim at supporting the local population, internally displaced people and humanitarian organizations by providing two Risk Education/Non-technical survey (NTS) teams, and two clearance teams.
In two years of HAMAP’s presence in NES, we came to strengthen the capacities of two local Syrian organizations, RMCO and WIFAQ (Qadar), by training, mentoring and monitoring:
Two teams for non-technical surveys and risk awareness and ten deminers/searchers, two deputy team leaders and two team leaders; divided into two clearance teams.
Four medics divided in two teams, each one supporting a clearance team.
Successive military and sanitary crisis in NES (military and then sanitary), have made our team face major challenges such as the loss of our joint base with RMCO in Ain Issa. However, thanks to the will and the unceasing efforts of our local and expatriate teams; we were able to maintain activity, even reduced, throughout the duration of our projects. HAMAP-Humanitaire has proved to be an enduring, adaptive and resilient organization in the most difficult times in NES in the past years. It has also an impressive achievement records, acknowledged and hailed by the local authorities, as well as the NES Forum and many local and international NGOs.
The current project started in September 2020 to set up the new base in Raqqah and start operational activities. HAMAP-Humanitaire reorganized its operations and chose to open its local office in Raqqah and a transit base has been installed in Hassake.
Under the responsibility of the NES project coordinator, two deputy field coordinators and three EOD demining experts, respectively in charge of operations/instructions and team leaders, accompany local employees in their training and organize refresher trainings when necessary. The objective of HAMAP-Humanitaire is the strengthening of local capacities and skill transfer. As such, each expatriate function is doubled by a local assistant, who will benefit throughout the project from daily mentoring by the expatriate experts and coordinators. The activities carried out so far have mainly focused on the governorate of Raqqa, but also episodically in the governorate of Aleppo, Al-Hassake and the governorate of Deir EzZor.
This project will make two mine risk education teams available to populations and aid organizations, as well as a non-technical survey team to mark suspected hazardous areas, and finally two demining teams to clear marked areas and make it possible to re-appropriate the sites.
The project directly tackles two of the three strategic objectives to "save lives" and "enhance protection" by preventing explosive hazards ordnance-related risks. Furthermore, Mine Action is one of the main areas of response priorities within the protection cluster and focuses on at least two aspects of the project:
-Expansion of existing humanitarian mine action capacities and improve the quality of services.
- Delivery of tailored risk education messaging

4 - Needs

People are at daily risk of an explosive device accident throughout NES. Towns and rural areas have been exposed to contamination by explosive devices, whether through fighting or voluntary entrapment by armed groups. Raqqah and its outskirts are heavily polluted with explosive devices, due to the former presence of Daesh. With the liberation of the city, people who fled the conflict seek to return to their homes, but access roads and abandoned houses can now be trapped and pose a danger.It is absolutely necessary to sensitize the population to the risks of these devices to contribute to the safe return of the displaced persons.Following the liberation of the city of Raqqah and the takeover of the governorate by Kurdish forces, a large amount of pyrotechnic pollution, added to old ISIS stockpiles, still contaminate the city and its surroundings. Rehabilitation activities in the city are slowed down by the risk of construction accidents linked to explosive devices of all types. The decontamination and demining of buildings and homes destroyed is a prerogative of any rehabilitation activity with a view to the return of the population and a revival of activities.It is necessary to provide an investigation and clearance capacity in the governorate of Ar-Raqqah to contribute to rehabilitation. The damage caused by the 10 years of conflict is considerable and beyond the danger linked to the presence of mines, ERW and IED, other humanitarian aid activities are necessary to tend towards decent living conditions for the populations and displaced persons. It is necessary to provide a demining interventn capacity so that emergency humanitarian aid can operate safely and without delay for the benefit of the population.

5 - Implementation of activities

GO: Carry out mine, ERW and IED clearance operations in the city of Raqqah and its periphery, in the governorate of Ar-Raqqah and in the governorate of Al-Hasake (mostly urban areas, and sometimes rural areas). Outcome 1 Opening in Raqqah a life base and a work base including offices, equipment storage room and secure parking. Preservation of a drop-off point in Hasake for teams when traveling. Outcome 2 HAMAP conducts non-technical surveys in Raqqah and Deir Ez Zor governorates for clearance activities. These NTS sessions allow data to be collected from the population and potential beneficiaries of suspected dangerous areas and allow the conduct of risk education awareness sessions. Outcome 3 HAMAP carries out demining operations in the city of Raqqah, its periphery, and the governorates of Ar-Raqqah and Al-Hasake thanks to the 2 NTS teams and the 2 OPS teams, respectively composed of a team leader, a deputy and 4 deminers. Outcome 4 HAMAP-Humanitaire conducts risk awareness workshops for the benefit of the population in the city of Raqqah, its periphery, and the governorates of Ar-Raqqah and Al-Hasake. Specific Objective 2 Offer support to NES humanitarian actors, local and international, facing significant presence of pyrotechnic pollution. Outcome1 – The actors of the humanitarian emergency response in NES, local and international, forward requests, via the coordination networks (NES Forum), for places that require control / depollution in order to be able to safely operate their own assistance activities. humanitarian. Outcome2 Upon request, HAMAP can provide specific training for the benefit of NGOs beneficiaries and staff related to prevention and risk education.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Vulnerable groups might not have the same access: HAMAP is offering support to humanitarian actors implementing projects in non-cleared areas with EORE activities for both their beneficiaries and staff as well as NTS / clearance activities upon request. Familiarization may cause the civilian populace to become overconfident or complacent to the threat of explosive hazards: Coordinate with local authorities to explain importance of RE/clearance and to ensure access to vulnerable groups, ensure adapted and tailored EORE sessions with the expert team. Disruption of access for Protection actors to vulnerable persons may result in higher risk to them if this disruption is viewed as completion of activity, or is not communicated to the people: Disruptions in activities must be explained and communicated to the population to make them aware of lack of services in relation to risks. Clearance of contaminated areas may open up territory and land disputes: establish links and communication with HLP cluster and humanitarian and development organizations dealing with land issues.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Armed Conflict:If active fighting should resume; the daily security monitoring will allow to anticipate and foresee an evacuation/sheltering of every team. Full fuel tank vehicle with driver 24/24 7/7.Anytime shifting of the daily travel routes, curfew at 8 PM and no going out in the evening. 2 armed guards flank the operations, and everyone is equipped with bulletproof vests.Kidnapping:All activities are operated in teams. No isolated staff, each team has a designated driver who can perform an extraction at any moment. Demining operations are overseen by armed guards and living places and workplaces are protected 24/24/ and 7/7. Traveling by foot and alone is forbidden. Health protocol (caution concerning water and infectious diseases):Drinking water tank for a daily use, cook in the Raqqah base prepare the meals once a day for all team returning from an operation. Kit with basic medicines and first aid supplies, under control of the health support team, clinics and hospitals are identified in case of need of care and for serious cases a medical evacuation procedure is set up. The Raqqah workplace base as well as the living places allows quarantine if needed. Sensibilization workshop about safety measures will be given for every team and the material needed (gel / water and soap / masks etc.) will be provided. Health support teams can establish monitoring process / testing et oversighting of the staff. Propensity to natural disasters : Drinking water tank in reserve, heating, and air conditioning. Moreover, every building is in town and in areas registered as not liable to flooding.COVID-19: HAMAP established a response in NES (safe distance, PPE, lunchtime division etc..)
