Project Summary

In 2025, HEKS/EPER will implement “Improving WASH in Healthcare Facilities” project to enhance the capacity of WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) in healthcare facilities. This initiative will focus on human right, gender-responsive, inclusive services in the government healthcare facilities of Ukhiya, Ramu, and Eidgaon Upazilas under Cox’s Bazar district.
WASH in Health is one of the most critical areas of operation that should reflect the exact needs of the people. Uninterrupted access to safe water, inclusive sanitation, hygiene (WASH), proper medical waste management and environment cleaning in healthcare facilities is a key to ensuring quality care and achieving Universal Health Coverage (UHC). HEKS/EPER will also address adequate and equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene in healthcare facilities, and continuously advocates those issues. All activities will ensure the do-no-harm principle by prioritizing the safety and dignity of women and girls. HEKS/EPER will continue its sensitization regarding safety measures before and during natural disasters, AWD/dengue outbreaks, IPC/COVID issues, etc.
To improve the WASH in the targeted healthcare facilities, operations and maintenance and key stakeholders' engagement, community feedback, and response mechanism will be addressed or aligned with other activists, during the project implementation period. HEKS/EPER will continue to operate and maintain the WASH facilities in the HCF (Healthcare facility), and targeted schools of the host community to increase knowledge and practices on IPC, MHM, and handwashing. To improve the overall WASH situation and increase the quality of healthcare services and ensure a safe healthcare environment, this project aims to enhance infection prevention and control as well as the hygiene situation in health facilities. Through this project HEKS/EPER partiaally and fully covered 47442 individuals in Ukhiya Upazila.
The major activities of the project are
• Capacity Building: The project focuses on enhancing the skill and capabilities of healthcare service providers through training, orientation, demonstration and workshop for sustainable improvements by adhering WASH FIT standards.
• Provision of WASH & IPC Materials: To improve the health and safety of both service providers and recipients, the project supplies WASH and IPC materials to the targeted healthcare facilities.
• WASH Facilities Construction: To meet project targets and standards, WASH infrastructures, including medical waste management systems, are constructed in the targeted healthcare facilities.
• Health Education Sessions: The project initiates school sessions in targeted girls’ education institutions to build knowledge and practices on personal hygiene, including menstrual hygiene management.
• Awareness Raising: Various initiatives, such as day celebrations, development of IEC (Information, Education, and Communication) and BCC (Behavior Change Communication) materials, and campaigns, are undertaken to enhance knowledge and practices among health service providers and recipients.
• Community Engagement: The project mobilizes healthcare facility management committees to prepare annual facility improvement plans, conduct monitoring, and support facility improvements in WASH.
• GEDSI Initiatives: Materials and infrastructure support are provided to ensure inclusion in all healthcare facilities regarding WASH.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Concern over the management medical waste, water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) has become one of the major issues in Cox's Bazar district due to the ongoing spread of infectious diseases among health workers, sanitation workers, waste handlers, and patients. Healthcare facilities still lack the capacity in healthcare facilities to overcome the poor state of WASH and medical waste management, which is considered essential to encourage health and WASH users to re-evaluate and follow the standard rules and practices of quality and safe healthcare. The problem of medical waste disposal and other toxic hazardous waste is growing rapidly throughout the world because of rapid urbanization, population growth, and climate change. Medical waste or clinical waste that poses serious threats to environmental health requires specialized treatment and management before its final disposal. Simply disposing of it in dustbins, drains, and canals or finally dumping it on the outskirts of the community poses a serious public health hazard. It was observed that the capacity of healthcare facilities during the spread of novel corona virus.Cox’s Bazar is one of the vulnerable districts of Bangladesh regarding the WASH in Health and IPC, because it is a tourist area and a huge number of Rohingya FDMN are living in this area. After the secondary level of discussions with the health care authority and ground-level health care providers, both parties agreed and conducted a joint assessment at Ukhiya & Ramu, to see the field-level challenges, gaps/limitations, and way forward strategy. Which represents the other Upazila of Cox's Bazar. The information collected through KoBo tools, observation, KII, and JMP SAS v12.6 was used to make all the quantitative analyses and other associated supports in this assessment. Some significant sources indicate that this could increase the risk of COVID-19 and other transmittable diseases in the host community due to the lack of waste management, inappropriate medical waste segregation, lack of safe water, sanitation facilities, hand washing facilities, and lack of adequate IPC materials. Only 3% of healthcare facilities have access to the waste management and treatment process. Only 21% of facilities have a reliable source of drinking water for staff, patients, and visitors. 68% of the facilities do not have adequate numbers of usable latrines for the staff, patients, and visitors. 97% of infectious waste is not stored in protected areas before treatment. 55% of the facilities have hand washing stations within their 5m periphery areas. (Source: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/health-care-waste)

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This integrated project encompasses Health, WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene), and Protection components, targeting both host communities to provide enhanced services. The project aims to improve healthcare facilities by constructing, repairing, and maintaining structures that are inclusive of all genders (male, female, LGBTQ+), ages, and persons with disabilities.Ensuring access to water, sanitation, and hygiene facilities is essential for good health for everyone. Article 28 of the Convention guarantees equal access to clean water services for persons with disabilities and ensures they receive appropriate and affordable services, devices, and assistance for disability-related needs. Often, persons with disabilities face limited access to WASH services, especially in healthcare facilities during normal and emergency situations. The project promotes hygiene and menstrual hygiene knowledge and practices through health education sessions at girls’ schools, awareness-raising activities, and the distribution of menstrual kits. These efforts aim to build capacity and improve hygiene practices among the community.The project integrates protection principles to ensure that all interventions are under “do no harm” that’s mean safe, dignified, and inclusive. To maintain high standards of transparency, accountability, and quality, the project implements complaints, feedback, and response mechanism at all stages. This ensures that the voices of the community are heard and addressed effectively.The project mobilizes healthcare facility management committees to prepare annual facility improvement plans, conduct monitoring, and support facility improvements in WASH. This engagement ensures that the community is actively involved in the improvement process.By integrating these components and focusing on protection mainstreaming, the GEDSI approach, and inclusion, the project aims to create a more equitable and accessible healthcare environment for all beneficiaries.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Through the project activities, healthcare facilities will improve that services are accessible and inclusive, particularly for children, minorities, males, females, LGBTQ+ individuals, persons with disabilities, and elderly people. The Clinic Management Committee (CMC) will play a leading role in fostering community participation. HEKS/EPER will organize training for service providers and duty bearers, ensuring that gender and safeguarding issues are integrated into project activities and discussed as agenda items in training sessions and CMC meetings.This comprehensive approach aims to create a safe, inclusive, and supportive environment for all beneficiaries, focusing on the needs of children, persons with disabilities, and the elderly.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Under the AAP, IASC, and HEKS/EPER framework, all activities will follow three main phases:- Taking Account: The project team will engage beneficiaries at the project’s outset to update baseline data, share all relevant project information, and involve them in the decision-making process.- Giving Account: HEKS/EPER will conduct a series of consultations to share information with communities about the progress of project activities, particularly during multisectoral community outreach sessions and counseling.- Being Held to Account: HEKS/EPER will organize formal and informal meetings at key project milestones (start, annual reviews, and end-line evaluations) to provide communities with opportunities to assess the project and offer positive and negative feedback on its performance.

This project is committed to preventing project staff, and beneficiaries form of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and promptly reports any suspicions. 2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, this project effectively addresses climate change adaptation and mitigation in healthcare facilities, the project will focus on several key strategies. - The project will provide orientation to the healthcare facility management committee on climate change adaptation and mitigation practices.- This project designs and implements disaster-resilient infrastructures that utilize climate-resilient and environment-friendly materials. - The project will ensure the distribution of climate resilient essential WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) and IPC (Infection Prevention and Control) materials.- The project aims to enhance the resilience of healthcare facilities, ensuring they can continue to provide critical services during and after disasters, while also contributing to long-term sustainability and preparedness.
