1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

WFP’s assistance to conflict- and displacement-affected communities in northern Mozambique in the context of a deteriorating humanitarian crisis has a three-pronged approach: 1) Provision of life-saving food assistance through General Food Distributions (GFD) and Food Assistance for Assets (FFA) or Cash for Work (CFW) using different transfer modalities that include in-kind, vouchers and cash-based modalities, 2) Provision of live-sustaining livelihood support for mid and long term food security and 3) Facilitating the provision of complementary assistance for other agencies and organizations. WFP targets 850,000 people in the provinces of Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa, including both IDPs and host communities. Activities will be adjusted according to the needs and conditions of the supported target groups: Recently displaced, or on the move and ‘hard-to-reach' beneficiaries: As people seek safety from violence they need immediate life-saving assistance. WFP also includes those who find themselves in hard-to-reach areas into this category. Vulnerable IDPs and host communities: Vulnerable IDPs in temporary centers and staying with host communities that have gone through the vulnerability-based targeting process and are registered but have not been allocated land to resettle and have no access to livelihood options. Host communities, resettled and returned populations: Resettled populations or those living with host communities who have gained access to livelihoods as well as host communities living in proximity will be assisted to alleviate pressure on shared resources and contribute to social cohesion. In 2022, WFP may also have to respond to voluntary returns should security conditions on areas of return be conducive.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

WFP priority activities are categorized per level of access constraints as outlined below: High access constraints (Central Cabo Delgado)In areas with access constraints, WFP plans to use the Joint Response Plan (JRP) set up by WFP, UNICEF and IOM as a flexible system for delivery of life-saving food assistance. The JRP will require logistics support by WFP and will be delivered through a combination of air, sea and road logistics. Where needed, and in support of opening up access for other humanitarian actors, WFP may also distribute Immediate Response Rations (IRRs) in hard-to-reach areas outside of the JRP. No access constraints / moderate access constraints (Nampula, Niassa and southern/western Cabo Delgado) • Recently displaced will receive IRRs that cover 100% of the daily requirements for 7 days for those recently displaced until they have been verified and registered. • Vulnerable IDP and host communities: For both vulnerable displaced IDPs and vulnerable host families, WFP will provide unconditional food assistance through a combination of in-kind food, commodity vouchers, value voucher or cash modalities considering local contexts and market assessment findings. Following a vulnerability based targeting exercise, WFP will aim to provide Family Food Rations (FFRs) of 100% of daily kilocalorie needs for those most in need for 850,000 the first 3 months of the year (Jan-March 2022), and reduced to 700,000 people from May to June. From July to December 2022, the number of vulnerable IDP and host community beneficiaries is expected to further decrease to 635,000 people as a result of investments in livelihoods by WFP and other actors. • Host communities, resettled and returned populations: To continue supporting IDPs and host communities to rebuild their livelihoods, WFP will scale-up interventions aiming at improving food security by creating productive assets that will ensure long-term benefits to both displaced and host communities for a total of 95,000 people. This will focus on four main outcomes: (1) Natural resources conservation and management (2) Support to the restoration of livelihood activities, including agricultural, pastoral, fisheries (3) Improve access to markets, social services, and infrastructures (4) Capacity development, including skills training to create, manage and maintain assets IDPs in resettlement sites, host communities and (potential) returnees are expected to increasingly gain access to livelihoods and have reduced vulnerability levels. These beneficiaries will receive rations of 50% of the standard ration. Food assistance will be provided through a combination of in-kind food, commodity vouchers, value voucher or cash modalities considering local contexts and market assessment findings. Food For Assets (FFA) and Cash for Work (CFW) activities which will be used to improve the situation of IDPs in resettlement sites and/or areas of return. Complementary activities: 1) A Minimum Expenditure Basket is being calculated to ensure a shared understanding of basic needs and inform program design.2) Conflict Analysis: as the basis for conflict-sensitive programming 3) Enhance coordination and overall FSC response through capacity building of partners and information sharing and support to Cash working group.4) Financial Inclusion training package to IDPs and vulnerable Host communities assisted

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

WFP is committed to ensuring the centrality of protection throughout the humanitarian assistance. WFP takes calculated measures to ensure a conflict sensitive approach so that actions do not negatively affect humanitarian access in the immediate nor long term, considering the rapidly changing dynamics in the evolving conflict. WFP works with humanitarian actors through local and national level coordination groups such as the protection cluster and community engagement/ AAP working group to share and apply feedback from the affected population. Findings from surveys and other community-based assessments are analyzed and reflected in operational planning, in parallel with reports from the CFM as relevant and possible in the dynamic context.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

WFP is committed to ensuring zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse, ensured through codes of conduct, online and face-to-face training, and several reporting mechanisms (see above). As the lead agency for Linha Verde 1458, WFP works with the PSEA network to ensure awareness of the service and to determine constructive approaches to handle SEA cases in a timely manner internally and with the Government as relevant. WFP is committed to action on SEA reports within 24 hours, prioritizing the safety and well-being of the survivor, working in collaboration with WFP’s ethics office and OIGI.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Understanding the significant access barriers for disabled people to access timely information and attend distributions, WFP works with implementation partners and local authorities to ensure clarity on vulnerability criteria, seeking targeted assistance to directly identify and support people with disabilities to access their entitlements in a safe and fair manner. In addition, WFP works through the protection cluster and in collaboration with the disability working group to identify access barriers and identify measures to address these effectively.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

All program activities will be screened for environmental and social risks during design, at a stage when the implementation details (i.e. location, targeted beneficiaries, inputs, transfer modalities, outputs, and timeline) are being defined, but before the design is finalized. From 2020, The WFP Environmental and Social Safeguards are the set of principles, standards, and tools to avoid or manage the risk of causing environmental or social harm to people, communities, or the natural environment. The WFP Environmental and Social Standards will be used serve as a benchmark for the risk screening and risk management. All the asset creation activities planned as part of the FFA will be analyzed using WFP’s Environmental and Social Risks assessments tools. Risk screening not only helps identify environmental and social risks: each activity will be classified by the level of the risk: low, medium, or high. Any program activity that entails some risks (medium or high) will come with risk management measures that aim to avoid or reduce the risks or mitigate or offset the expected adverse impacts.
