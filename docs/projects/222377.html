Project Summary

O MOZ2050 é um plano estratégico e holístico, que visa promover a paz sustentável e a coesão social em Moçambique, com foco na recuperação e reintegração das comunidades afetadas pelo conflito armado, incluindo deslocados internos, ex-combatentes, e jovens vulneráveis, em particular as crianças associadas a forças e grupos armados (CAAFAG). Através de uma abordagem sistémica, multissectorial e dinâmica, o projecto busca transformar as adversidades em oportunidades para o fortalecimento da identidade nacional e o desenvolvimento inclusivo, em consonância com os Objetivos de Desenvolvimento Sustentável (ODS).
Objectivo Principal: Promover a recuperação psicossocial e o desenvolvimento sustentável das comunidades afetadas pelo conflito, utilizando uma estratégia abrangente de capacitação, apoio à inclusão social e criação de oportunidades económicas. Este plano será implementado através de mais de 200 actividades, desenvolvidas de forma gradual e consequente, com impacto a curto, médio e longo prazo.
Público-Alvo: O projecto é direcionado a populações deslocadas e retornadas, especialmente mulheres e crianças que enfrentam múltiplas vulnerabilidades e necessidades específicas de apoio psicossocial. Jovens em risco, incluindo ex-combatentes e CAAFAG, que serão integrados em healing homes, com o objectivo de diminuir vulnerabilidades e promover a reconstrução do tecido social. Comunidades locais e actores sociais, que terão actividades de prática de mediação de conflitos e resolução de tensões, com o foco na construção de uma paz duradoura e sustentável.
O MOZ2050 propõe uma estratégia inovadora e sistémica de construção da paz, que integra as lições aprendidas de programas realizados noutras regiões pós-conflito, como o Afeganistão, Paquistão, Somália, Quénia e Ruanda. A colaboração de múltiplos actores – incluindo organizações da sociedade civil, instituições do Estado, líderes comunitários, e parceiros internacionais – será fundamental para a implementação e sucesso do projecto. Além disso, o projecto visa não apenas responder às necessidades imediatas das populações, mas também contribuir para uma identidade nacional coesa, respeitando e reconhecendo a cultura africana e moçambicana em particular. O MOZ2050 está alinhado com 13 dos 17 ODS previstos para 2030, e prepara o caminho para os objectivos que serão definidos para 2050.
Principais Componentes e Actividades:
Apoio Psicossocial e Psicoterapêutico: programas para crianças e adultos que sofreram traumas devido à violência, com foco na desradicalização e reintegração psicossocial.
Capacitação Profissional e Educativa: para promover a inclusão social e económica dos deslocados e ex-combatentes, com cursos de formação e actividades vocacionais.
Mediação Comunitária e Resolução de Conflitos: fortalecendo as capacidades das comunidades para gerirem tensões e promoverem a convivência pacífica.
Empoderamento das Mulheres e Igualdade de Género: combatendo a violência de género e promovendo a participação activa das mulheres no processo de reconstrução social.
A implementação do MOZ2050 será conduzida por uma equipa transdisciplinar, envolvendo mais de 200 actividades que serão realizadas de forma gradual, com atenção às necessidades imediatas e ao impacto a longo prazo. A mudança de atitude e comportamento será um dos principais pilares, visando não apenas transformações individuais, mas também uma mudança estrutural que permita à sociedade moçambicana reconstruir-se de forma inclusiva e sustentável.
Com base em lições passadas e experiências internacionais, o MOZ2050 reflecte uma abordagem abrangente que inclui programas de desradicalização socioculturais, psicológicos, religiosos, educativos e vocacionais. Este modelo é interligado entre 10 pilares essenciais, e visa promover uma paz duradoura e reconciliação entre as comunidades afectadas. Aposta numa visão geracional, com a participação activa das comunidades e na construção de uma paz sistémica, que garante a sustentabilidade.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The MOZ2050 initiative presents a holistic, multi-sectoral strategy aimed at fostering long-term peace, resilience, and sustainable development in Mozambique. Designed with a systemic and transdisciplinary approach, the project encompasses over 200 carefully coordinated activities targeting displaced persons, returnees, and vulnerable children. It integrates best practices from global peacebuilding efforts (e.g., Afghanistan, Rwanda, Somalia) and applies them to the Mozambican context, prioritizing national identity, cultural respect, and intergenerational transformation. 2ResCom Module (Resiliência e Reintegração Comunitária - Resilience and Comunity Reintegration):This module focuses on rebuilding the resilience of displaced populations and returnees, ensuring their reintegration into society. Key activities include the provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services at levels 2 and 3, including individual counseling, group therapy, and community-based psychosocial initiatives. It also emphasizes protection monitoring, with mechanisms for identifying risks and providing critical referrals to essential services such as healthcare, legal aid, and shelter. Local capacity-building plays a pivotal role, training community leaders, humanitarian actors, and government officials in protection advocacy and conflict-sensitive interventions. In total, 2ResCom supports over 17,000 individuals across multiple districts, ensuring community cohesion through educational campaigns on explosive ordnance risk and child protection. CAAFAG Module (Crianças com Autonomia, Alento, Força, Alegria e Gratidão - Children with Autonomy, Encouragement, Strength, Joy, and Gratitude):Dedicated to the rehabilitation of children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG), this module addresses the complex needs of over 41,000 children and caregivers. Services include trauma-informed counseling, alternative care for unaccompanied children, vocational training, and educational support. The module collaborates with national networks to develop culturally relevant resources and guidelines for intervention, ensuring a context-sensitive approach. It also facilitates training for and promotes awareness about child rights, fostering reintegration through community dialogues and structured recreational activities. Special emphasis is placed on long-term behavioral change, reinforcing positive identity development and psychological healing. MOZ2050's innovative nature lies in its interconnected framework, where both modules operate independently yet synergistically. For example, data gathered from protection monitoring in 2ResCom informs targeted interventions in CAAFAG, while community cohesion efforts bolster the reintegration of children. This dynamic model not only addresses immediate protection needs but also cultivates a culture of peace and resilience at the grassroots level. The project actively promotes 13 of the 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), including Quality Education, Gender Equality, Peace, Justice, and Strong Institutions. By fostering collaboration among national and international stakeholders—government agencies, civil society organizations (CSOs), religious leaders, and humanitarian partners—MOZ2050 enhances local ownership and sustainability. The project's multilevel impact, from individual healing to community transformation, underscores its commitment to systemic change, positioning Mozambique as a global example of post-conflict resilience and innovation. In essence, MOZ2050's comprehensive approach addresses the complex interplay between trauma, reintegration, and sustainable development. By combining psychological support with socio-economic empowerment and cultural respect, it provides a blueprint for lasting peace and prosperity, ensuring that the most vulnerable populations are not only protected but empowered to shape a more resilient future.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project consists of two integrated but independent modules: 2ResCom and CAAFAG, focusing on mitigating radicalization, trauma, and enhancing community resilience in conflict-affected areas in Mozambique. These modules build upon global best practices, tailored to the specific needs of vulnerable populations. Module 1: 2ResCom (Resilience through Communication and Reconciliation)This module strengthens community resilience and reconciliation through communication strategies and psychosocial support. Priority activities will target internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities in conflict-affected districts. Activities include: - Risk Assessment and Trauma ScreeningInitial assessments will screen for radicalization and trauma, ensuring interventions are tailored to individual needs. - Micro-Peace Agents ProgramThe program will train Micro-Peace Agents—social activists who will promote peacebuilding, non-violence, and social cohesion within communities. They will play a vital role in raising awareness about the risks of radicalization and fostering community resilience. - Psychosocial Support and Mental Health ServicesActivities will provide psychological first aid, individual counseling, and group therapy (SMAPS) for both children and adults at levels 2 and 3. - Community Awareness and SensitizationCommunity-based activities will focus on peacebuilding, social cohesion, and counteracting radicalization, leveraging local cultural values and closing the generation gap. - Strengthening Local CapacitiesLocal authorities, community leaders, and stakeholders will receive training in conflict resolution, peacebuilding, and protection services. Module 2: CAAFAG (Children Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups)This module targets children who have been associated with armed groups, focusing on their reintegration and psychosocial support. Key activities will include: - Risk Assessment and ScreeningEach child will undergo screening for trauma and radicalization, allowing for personalized intervention. - Psychosocial Support and ReintegrationThis component will provide psychological care and reintegration programs, focusing on both individual and group activities to help children heal and reintegrate into their communities. - Family Reunification and Alternative CareEfforts will be made to reunite children with their families or, where this is not possible, provide safe and supportive alternative care arrangements. - Vocational Orientation and Educational SupportVocational training and educational support will be provided as part of each child’s individualized development plan, helping them achieve their life goals and integrate better into society. - Community-Based ProtectionThis activity will work with local communities to establish protection mechanisms and ensure children are safeguarded from further harm. - Capacity Building for Local NetworksA national network will be established to build capacity for managing cases of CAAFAG, including the development of guidelines and materials to support service providers in child protection and reintegration. Both modules will be implemented across Ancube, Chiure, Ibo, Macomia, Mocimboa Da Praia, Mueda, Palma, and Quissanga. These districts have been identified based on the severity of displacement, radicalization risks, and the number of children affected by armed conflict.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Identification of Potential RisksIn conflict zones, risks can arise from various sources, making conflict-sensitive approaches essential. Key strategies to identify risks include: Conflict Sensitivity Assessment: Conducting an assessment to understand how interventions might inadvertently escalate tensions or favor one side. This is crucial for ensuring the project doesn’t deepen divisions in the community. Stakeholder Engagement: Involving local stakeholders, such as community leaders and conflict resolution practitioners, to gain insights into the risks associated with power imbalances, societal divisions, or extremist ideologies. Risk Mapping: Creating and continuously updating risk maps based on real-time security information and local conflict dynamics, ensuring that interventions are tailored to the security context. Mitigating HarmOnce risks are identified, steps will be taken to minimize harm and ensure the project does not cause unintended consequences: Psychosocial Support: Providing trauma-informed care to address the psychological needs of children associated with armed groups (CAAFAG) and other vulnerable individuals. This ensures that interventions avoid retraumatization. Safety Protocols: Implementing safety protocols for staff and beneficiaries, including evacuation procedures, confidentiality agreements, and secure transportation, as well as regular security briefings. Do No Harm Framework: Applying the Do No Harm framework ensures that interventions do not exacerbate conflict or cause harm. The framework guides project activities to avoid deepening societal divisions or creating new conflict lines. Inoculation Against Fake News and MisinformationMisinformation and fake news are significant risks in conflict zones. To mitigate these: Media Literacy Campaigns: Running community-level campaigns to equip people with the skills to identify credible information, especially through social media and local news outlets. Youth Micro-Activists: Training young people as micro-activists who will lead efforts to combat misinformation in their communities. They will raise awareness, share accurate information, and build resilience against extremist narratives. Social Media Monitoring: Establishing a system to track the spread of fake news and extremist content, allowing for rapid responses and countermeasures. Security Risks in Conflict ZonesSecurity risks in conflict zones require a tailored approach: Risk-Based Programming: Adjusting interventions based on the security situation. Activities in high-risk areas may be modified to ensure the safety of beneficiaries and staff, such as conducting certain programs in more secure locations. Flexible Programming: Ensuring that activities can be adapted or delayed based on evolving security dynamics. A contingency plan will be developed for emergency evacuation. Continuous Monitoring and FeedbackContinuous monitoring will allow the project to adapt and avoid causing harm: Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E): Regular tracking of project activities to ensure they are appropriate for the context. Feedback from beneficiaries and local authorities will guide adjustments to interventions. Adaptive Programming: Based on real-time monitoring and feedback, activities will be adjusted to ensure they remain relevant and do not inadvertently cause harm.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The proposed project encourages meaningful access to assistance and active participation from marginalized groups, particularly women and elderly persons, by creating pathways for them to lead and engage in community-driven initiatives. Through a mentorship model, the project aims to empower these groups, enabling them to take ownership of their development and contribute to the community without the need for financial aid. Mentorship for EmpowermentAt the core of this project is the idea of mentorship rather than direct financial support. By focusing on mentorship, the project encourages participants—especially women and elderly individuals—to develop skills and capacity for leading initiatives in their communities. Mentorship will provide guidance on organizing, managing, and sustaining community-driven programs, including cultural activities, educational initiatives, and social projects. This process aims to help participants grow their leadership potential and create lasting impacts within their local environments. Cultural Activities Led by Elderly Community MembersElderly individuals will play a central role in the cultural activities that the project supports. As the custodians of cultural knowledge, they will be mentored to lead educational and artistic programs for children and youth. These activities could include traditional crafts, storytelling, music, dance, and games. By involving the elderly in leadership roles, the project not only helps preserve cultural heritage but also strengthens their position in the community, giving them a sense of purpose and recognition. These cultural programs foster intergenerational exchanges, as children and youth benefit from learning cultural traditions while elderly individuals gain the opportunity to impart their knowledge. This promotes social cohesion by bridging generational gaps and creating mutual respect between the youth and elderly. Creating Leadership Opportunities for Women and Elderly IndividualsThe project emphasizes the importance of providing leadership roles for women and elderly individuals in community development. These groups will be encouraged to take on leadership positions in various initiatives, including peacebuilding efforts, educational projects, and community health campaigns. Through mentorship, women and elderly participants will be trained to take ownership of their local initiatives, allowing them to develop confidence and gain recognition as community leaders. The project will also support the creation of women-led initiatives, where they can become active participants in local governance, conflict resolution, and peacebuilding efforts. These initiatives will promote the leadership capabilities of women, enabling them to have a stronger influence in their communities. Sustainable Community-Led InitiativesMentorship will not only help participants develop leadership and organizational skills but will also teach them how to make their initiatives sustainable. By focusing on creating self-sustaining, community-led projects, the mentorship will emphasize the importance of resource mobilization, long-term planning, and collaboration within the community. Women, elderly individuals, and youth will be equipped with the skills to identify local resources, organize events, and maintain their activities independently, ensuring the sustainability of these efforts even after the project concludes. Focus on Non-Monetary SupportThe project will avoid providing direct financial assistance to avoid complications associated with cash transfers, particularly in environments where financial aid could be misused. Instead, the mentorship approach ensures that individuals are empowered to create their own projects and initiatives, supported by skills training, leadership development, and guidance on sustainability. This model fosters self-reliance and community ownership, making it more likely that the initiatives will continue to thrive through time.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will adopt an inclusive approach to identifying and assisting people with disabilities (PWD), ensuring that they can fully participate in the project’s activities. To achieve this, we will take the following steps: Identification of People with DisabilitiesWe will identify PWD through collaboration with local organizations, community leaders, and health professionals who have knowledge of the local populations. Additionally, disability assessments will be conducted during community outreach efforts. Special attention will be given to reaching vulnerable and marginalized groups, ensuring that no one is left behind. Our efforts will also focus on raising awareness about disabilities within the community to reduce stigma and encourage PWD to come forward. Removing Barriers to Accessing AssistancePhysical Accessibility: Activities and facilities will be designed to accommodate individuals with physical disabilities, ensuring that venues are wheelchair-accessible, with appropriate ramps, seating, and other necessary features. Accessible transport will be provided for those living in remote areas, ensuring mobility is not a barrier.Communication Accessibility: For individuals with hearing impairments, sign language interpreters will be provided, and written materials will be made available in accessible formats such as braille or audio. These formats will ensure that all participants have access to the same information and can participate fully in activities.Tailored Services: The project will provide specialized support, including psychological, educational, and vocational training services tailored to the needs of PWD. These services will be adapted to the specific requirements of individuals with different types of disabilities, such as cognitive, sensory, or physical impairments, enabling them to benefit fully from the project’s offerings.Inclusive Participation: PWD will be encouraged to participate in the design, implementation, and evaluation of the project. This will include gathering input through focus groups and community consultations, ensuring that their voices are heard and their needs are prioritized in decision-making.Mentorship and Support Networks: We will implement a mentorship program where PWD can receive guidance and support in developing their own initiatives. This will foster a sense of leadership and community integration. Additionally, we will work with local disability networks to create support systems that ensure ongoing access to resources and services.Monitoring and EvaluationWe will continuously assess the accessibility and effectiveness of our interventions through feedback from PWD and community members. This will include regular surveys, focus groups, and consultations to monitor the participation of PWD and identify areas for improvement. The project will adjust its activities as needed to ensure that PWD continue to have access to all aspects of the project, and that no barriers remain.ConclusionThe project will take a comprehensive approach to ensuring that PWD have full access to the services and opportunities it offers. By focusing on physical and communication accessibility, as well as ensuring tailored services, we will create an inclusive environment where PWD are not only recipients of support but active participants and leaders within their communities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed project does not focus directly on environmental issues; however, it integrates environmental considerations within the context of its activities and planning, particularly through a lens of civic responsibility and disaster preparedness. Environmental ConsiderationsWhile environmental assessments are not a primary objective of the project, it is important that the activities are carried out in a manner that acknowledges the broader environmental context. Specifically, the project will ensure: Sustainable Resource Use: Materials provided during the training and awareness sessions will be selected with sustainability in mind. We will prioritize the use of environmentally-friendly materials, minimizing waste wherever possible. This includes printed materials and other resources that participants will receive.Civic Responsibility and Environmental Awareness: The training programs, particularly those related to psychological first aid and community mobilization, will incorporate elements of environmental awareness. This will encourage participants to be mindful of their surroundings and the broader impacts of climate change and other environmental factors that may affect their communities.Crisis Management and Adaptation to Climate Change: One of the key areas where environmental considerations come into play is in crisis management. The psychological first aid (PFA) training that will be provided to community leaders, volunteers, and other stakeholders will include elements that enable them to address mental health issues arising from environmental disasters, such as those related to climate change (e.g., floods, droughts, displacement). This approach ensures that communities are better prepared to cope with the mental health consequences of environmental crises.In addition, the project will train individuals on the psychological aspects of climate-induced displacement, ensuring that displaced populations, particularly in vulnerable areas, receive appropriate support in line with the principles of disaster management.Planning and Implementation of Activities: All activities will be planned with an awareness of environmental issues, ensuring that events and initiatives, such as community gatherings, awareness campaigns, and group discussions, minimize their environmental impact. For example, we will encourage the use of digital platforms where possible, reducing the need for printed materials and contributing to environmental sustainability.Mitigation of Environmental Impact: While the primary focus of the project is on social and psychological support, we will also encourage community members to implement small-scale environmental mitigation measures during project activities. For instance, awareness of waste management practices, recycling, and proper disposal of materials will be promoted in community training sessions.Crisis and Environmental Impact:In the event of an environmental crisis, the materials and skills developed through this project will be invaluable. Participants will be trained to provide psychological support to individuals who are impacted by climate-related disasters, such as flooding or drought. This capacity-building will enable communities to better cope with the mental and emotional challenges that often accompany environmental emergencies. By preparing local leaders and activists with the necessary skills to assist in mental health support during climate-related crises, the project indirectly supports resilience-building efforts. While environmental assessments are not directly part of the project's objectives, the mental health component will ensure that communities are better equipped to handle the mental and emotional toll of environmental challenges.
