Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Somalia remains critical due to ongoing impacts of drought, displacement and conflict. As per the FSNAU-FEWSNET 2018 Post Gu findings, 4.6 million people are still in need of urgent assistance, including an estimated 2.5 million children. While food security is overall improving, the nutrition situation remains critical with more than 954,000 children under-5 expected to be malnourished over the coming twelve months, including 173,000 severely malnourished. Despite the minimal improvements in overall prevalence of acute malnutrition over the 2018 Gu season, pockets of high levels of malnutrition persist and disproportionately affecting IDPs and rural communities.
The post Gu 2018 FSNAU findings underscore once again the multi-causal nature of malnutrition, requiring an integrated approach to avert a worsening situation. UNICEF will continue to focus on delivering an integrated response, ensuring prioritization of the most vulnerable populations, including IDPs and marginalised groups in hotspots and hard-to-reach areas.
In light of the persistent high rates of severe acute malnutrition (SAM), there is need to continue delivery of lifesaving nutrition interventions targeting in priority IDP camps and hard to reach communities. Mogadishu remains a priority since it is hosting larger of IDPs as well as Galgaduud and South Mudug regions where many pastoral communities lost their livelihoods and are vulnerable.

Needs

Somalia is characterized by a complex political environment, extreme poverty, food insecurity, conflict and instability. As per the FSNAU-FEWSNET 2018 Post Gu findings, 4.6 million people are still in need of urgent assistance, including an estimated 2.5 million children. While food security is overall improving, the nutrition situation remains critical with more than 954,000 children under-5 expected to be malnourished over the coming twelve months, including 173,000 severely malnourished and in need of life saving and livelihoods interventions. Amidst the political and socioeconomic challenges, climatic shocks, armed conflicts and poor infrastructural development have exuberated the humanitarian needs and resulted in acute food shortage.The continuous needs assessments with stakeholders in Banadir,Galgaduud,Mudug,Lower Shabelle during the ongoing nutrition specific and sensitive interventions by CISP show that basic health and nutrition services are urgently needed among the vulnerable conflict affected populations including urban and rural IDPs and host communities.The major gaps and needs identified were insufficient capacity of health workers to manage malnutrition chronic lack of or insufficient nutrition supplements drugs and consumables to address specific nutrition needs Limited knowledge and poor IYCF practice among caregivers. Poor health seeking behavior; Recurring conflict incidences and resultant displacement of families and destruction of means of livelihoods, food insecurity and Successive droughts and lack of access to safe water for communities in these regions. These identified and inferred risk factors are associated causes among others, the largely unchanging high levels of malnutrition and morbidities. Despite concerted humanitarian efforts, huge needs still persist among the population with varying levels among the host communities, refugees, displaced persons and returnees. This is due to the complex emergency situation in Somalia.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The community resilience approach will be overlaid with basic service provision of key health and nutrition, basic community-based public health services for prevention, promotion and referral and will be done by trained community based workers. The community based strategy will be channeled through service committees such as community development committees (CDCs). Community consultation and action planning will support the delivery of community based public health and nutrition. Linked with local governance structure, we will work with district and regional health authorities on delivering of prevention and promotion services through training and supportive supervision for stewarding community nutrition and public health interventions.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The risk involved lack of gender equity and social inclusion: This will be mitigated CISP will prevent exclusion and inequality by ensuring equal participation of all people, including individuals at risk of exclusion and ensure they reach their full potential, both in terms of its economy and its level of governance.Social Inclusion will be promoted through: • When hiring incentive staff equal opportunities will be given to all irrespective of their social background, gender and disability• Equal representation in the Community Health Committees (CHC) and Community Development Committees (CDC0.• Training Health staff on do no harmThe proposed project will equally target women, boys and girls, particularly those from vulnerable families affected by ill health and drought whose vulnerabilities are exacerbated by the worsening drought, water scarcity and displacement into IDP settlements where children live in extremely precarious conditions with only limited community support. This project will support the MOH to identify the staff with optimum qualifications and experiences taking into account the different capacities and needs of men and women. As well as assigning equal opportunities, tasks and responsibilities to both men and women. Community based workers selection and trainings will also consider gender sensitivity including both men and women. All this will ensure gender issues and equity is addressed. -CISP will prioritize safety &dignity, and avoid causing and any unintended negative effects of its intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks.CISP will ensure people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The proposed project will equally target women, boys and girls, particularly those from vulnerable families affected by ill health and drought whose vulnerabilities are exacerbated by the worsening drought, water scarcity and displacement into IDP settlements where children live in extremely precarious conditions with only limited community support. This project will support the MOH to identify the staff with optimum qualifications and experiences taking into account the different capacities and needs of men and women. As well as assigning equal opportunities, tasks and responsibilities to both men and women. Community based workers selection and trainings will also consider gender sensitivity including both men and women. All this will ensure gender issues and equity is addressed. CISP will endeavor to leverage on her technical prowess in Protection programming to mainstream protection intervention in the project. This will be achieved through sensitization of the health workers on protection including assessment and management of GBV cases. Beneficiaries will also be sensitized on self-assessment mechanisms and services available.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Armed conflict between pro-government forces and militant groups continues to trigger displacements and the number of IDPs increases each year. This also hampers free flow of humanitarian aid since some town in Somalia keep on changing hands between the government and AMISOM forces on one side and Militant groups on the other.Clan based conflicts are still existing in Somalia which also contributes to displacements in the country and creates vulnerability each pasing year.This can only be mitigated through proper reconciliation among warring clans in Somalia to foster peace and co-existence.
