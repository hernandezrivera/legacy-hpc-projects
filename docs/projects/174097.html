Project Summary

SC proposes a one-year integrated CP programming with Nutrition in Borno state. High nutrition and protection needs, heightened by C-19, requires SC to continue supporting vulnerable communities in Jere and Konduga LGAs, while expanding to Mafa LGA, where high needs and service delivery gaps require immediate intervention. Due to recent increases in protection needs and risks faced by girls and boys, SC plans to: (i) reinforce existing CP Committees (CPCs) in Jere and Konduga, and establish new ones in Mafa. (ii) SC will continue to provide PSS to improve wellbeing of vulnerable girls and boys. SC will establish 2 new and support 6 existing CFSs, where structured, age- and gender-specific recreational and social activities will be held. To mitigate spread of C-19, SC also adopted a house-to-house approach to provide PSS. SC Plans to reach 10,000 Children in 2021 with psychosocial support activities including to reinforce the PSS activities with HEART approach which is an evidenced based Programme to improve the wellbeing of children affected by the conflict through healing and education through Art. SC plans to reach 1500 Children with case management service provision including those at risk of GBV, survivors of SGBV, CAFAAG etc.

1 - Needs

SC is proposing to continue implementation in Konduga and Jere LGAs, while expanding to Mafa LGA. Due to the high number of IDPs, returnees and host community members in need, these three LGAs are among those prioritized by the Nutrition and Protection Clusters. The areas of Central Borno – to which the targeted LGAs belong – are notably classified as having the highest levels of acute malnutrition in the country. Specifically, Jere hosts the highest number of people in need across BAY (581,689). Priority sectors of intervention and target groups: In line with the 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), SC proposes to address the most pressing life-saving nutrition and protection needs in the above mentioned LGAs in Borno.Protection: A recent UNHCR vulnerability screening across 11 Borno LGAs revealed high rates of CP concerns: CEFM (44%), child-led HHs (21%), UASC (16%), adolescent parents (11%) and orphans (8%). The Protection Sector highlights significant gaps in protection service delivery, with only 10% of the response funded as of Q3 2020. Only 14% of individuals with identified protection needs are provided with assistance in the BAY states. The psychosocial needs of children and adolescent are widely overlooked, with only 36% and 14% of those in need of psychosocial support (PSS) and mental health (MH) services receiving support. Unsupported, they may suffer from Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder, depression and other MH challenges.12 Further, only 28% of reported SGBV survivors receive case management (CM) and specialized services across BAY. In Borno, SGBV survivors lack access to PSS, CM and legal assistance in 80% of all wards. Moreover, funding requirements have increased by 14.1% to account for new needs resulting from C-19.28 The closure of schools due to C-19 mitigation measures has affected 400,000 IDP children attending learning activities in camps and host communities. Activities in child friendly spaces (CFSs) have also been suspended, leaving PSS services to focus only on high risk cases. UASC are one of the most vulnerable groups. The CP Information Management System (CPIMS) database shows that 204 UASC (101G, 103B) were registered between April 2019 and November 2020. Without access to care options or CP services, UASC increasingly fall victims to exploitation. Children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAGs) also face unique challenges. Although estimates vary due to underreporting and data gaps, a total of 1,947 cases of recruitment and use of children by armed groups were recorded in Borno in 2018. According to escapees, CAAFAGs are subject to forced marriage, religious conversion, labor, physical and psychological abuses, including SGBV.2 A growing number of girls and boys are forced to join these groups to escape poverty. CAAFAGs suffer from stigma and rejection, which prevents their reintegration into communities, further putting them at risk of abuse. SGBV survivors are also extremely vulnerable. SGBV is pervasive in NE Nigeria, with 99% of reported cases being perpetrated against women and girls.
