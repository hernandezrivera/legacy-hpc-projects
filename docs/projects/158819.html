Project Summary

The intervention contributes to support livelihoods of youth, graduates, women-headed
households and other food insecure households in the Gaza Strip through cash-for-work
and income-generating activities for 240 direct beneficiaries in CFW, and CFI. As well as
improving ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including
environmental threats, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and
shocks are pursued through IGA support for 50 women directly benefitting from
intervention and resources to support their agricultural resilience. Contributing to
2018-2020 HRP Strategic Objective 3 and objective 2, and Cash based support as
a cluster activity for households to challenge limited access to food and income
opportunities and provide their basic food needs.

1 - Needs

The recurrent outbreaks of violent conflicts and the ongoing blockade continue to pose a serious threat to life, liberty and security of all Gaza population. Armed conflict, military incursions and the resulting damage and destruction of facilities and property disrupted productive sector.The Great March of Return has added to this instability, where violence and clashes have become to take place weekly, causing high number of casualties including 172 people were permanently disabled (OCHA, 2019). In addition to the political context, the situation is further complicated with the high unemployment among Gaza youth (18-29) which is critical as it reached 69% (PCBS, 2019);Over 68 %of households in the Gaza Strip are severely or moderately food insecure which in the oPt is primarily caused by limited economic access to food whereby in the Gaza Strip the economy is “in free fall (OCHA, 2018). Of the approximately 244,000 people recorded as “employed” in Gaza, about 62,000 are public employees on the PA’s payroll whose salaries have been cut since March 2017.Food insecurity has been further jeopardized by the shortfall in funding to UNRWA and decreased support from the Palestinian Ministry of Social Development to the most vulnerable, leading to higher levels of household debt (OCHA, 2018).In addition to low labor demand, women also continue to face challenges to join the labor force as the female participation rate in Gaza is 25.5%, with an unemployment rate of 74.5% (Gisha, 2019).Altogether women are far more vulnerable given the humanitarian conditions across Gaza, where unequal access to resources and assets triggers their food insecurity (UNCT,2016), this is evidenced by the fact that the percentage of poverty among individuals in households headed by women in Gaza Strip was 54% (PCBS,2019). Women vulnerability in participating in the labour market is not solely due to the political and humanitarian situation but also due to the socio-cultural context, where women continue to be viewed as the primary caregivers while men are the breadwinners.Thus despite their high educational attainment, women unemployment as shown is high and when they do enter the labour market they are subject to discrimination, as employers prefer to hire male applicants rather than female (UN Economic and Social Commission for Western Asia, 2019) and women earn less than men (PCBS,2019).Consequently women are forced to depend on male relatives which may lead women to be trapped in material or domestic abuse. During ongoing SYFS work in cash for work, cash for internship and income generating interventions the reoccurring recommendation from the youth and women beneficiaries, and unskilled and low skilled workers is to provide similar interventions to meet the increasing basic needs of impoverished and otherwise vulnerable and marginalized families, especially in times of current USAID cuts of funding for such projects and UNRWA cuts in services and employment. During SYFS work the communities in different parts of the Gaza Strip asked SYFS to act as intermediary towards the donors to fund interventions like the one presented below that will serve 150 workers with cash for work intervention and 150 youth graduates (22-29 years old) with cash for internship, as well as 100 women-headed and other food insecure households with income generating activities.At least 50% of targeted beneficiaries are women with special consideration given to women-headed households.Potential activities will include sewing and other activities acceptable by women.Potential activities includes sewing, carpentry (cash for work), graduates in IT and social sciences (cash for internship), sewing, home garden, small green houses, breeding rabbits, sheep, cows etc. and other activities, especially those acceptable by women (IGA).All sectors have been identified based on needs identified through SYFS current existing records in internal database of beneficiaries in need of such support

2 - Activities

The potential beneficiaries will be identified based on SYFS recent projects beneficiary waiting lists (internal database), hardship cases nominated by the MOSD, UNRWA, and beneficiaries identified during the fieldwork of SYFS community engagement and additionally coordinated with this entities, as well as with CPWG in relevant cases. Following the identification of potential beneficiaries, verification and home visits will be conducted to determine eligibility for the project assistance and specific protection requirements. Then, beneficiaries will be ranked and the most vulnerable will be selected. The selection criteria will include: (A)Household food insecurity (B)Household characteristics (number of individuals in households, PWD in the family, elderly persons in the family, pregnant and lactating women, single women, women-headed households, large families with children, other health conditions); (C)Economic situation (no income/low income per household member, vulnerable according to UNRWA and MOSD database); (D)Beneficiaries who have not received similar assistance/unemployed for 6 months or longer; (E) readiness to devote full time to the allocated job/internship/IGA. In addition, youth graduates will have to graduate within the past two years and be unemployed ever since to be considered for the selection. The project benefits from multi-year planning to expand the outreach to the most vulnerable beneficiaries in the subsequent years. Activity 1: Identification and verification of beneficiaries and private sector actors for CfW, CFI (including matching) and IGA. A participato approach will involve the representatives of the communities, both women and men. Through the field staff, the target communities will be involved in sharing the vulnerability criteria for selecting the beneficiaries of the action. Women and youth representatives will be part of the consultation in order to identify special needs from these groups of beneficiaries. In this stage, project staff will also assess special needs related to disabilities among the beneficiaries and will discuss with the communities the best way to adapt the project activities in order to address these issues. Output 1: 315 beneficiaries and their households (at least 50%women; in addition also 50%youth 22-29 years old) selected and matched with employers. Activity 2: Cash for work for 3 months for unskilled and low skilled workers (potential sectors include sewing, carpentry-sectors identified based on needs identified through SYFS current existing records in internal database of beneficiaries in need of such support). Output 2: 120 workers and their households (at least 50%women) regain access to livelihoods. Activity 3: Cash for internship for 3months for youth graduates 22-29 years old (potential sectors include graduates in IT and social sciences-sectors have been identified based on needs identified through SYFS current existing records in the internal database of beneficiaries in need of such support). Output 3: 120 youth graduates and their households (22-29 years old, 50%women) gain professional experience and access to livelihoods. Activity 4: Provide intensive capacity building in IGA followed by beneficiaries’ applications with specific ideas and budgets (modules including basic life skills, basics of marketing and financial skills for IGA). Output 4: 75 beneficiaries (at least 50%women) participated in the intensive capacity building. Activity 5: Select and support (financially and with technical expertise) IGA. Output 5: 52 beneficiaries and their households (at least 50%women) selected and supported (potential sectors include sewing, home garden (small greenhouses) (an area of 500m2 and water tank of 1,000 liters capacity) and/or breeding sheep (3sheep+270kilos of fodder), especially those acceptable by women, sectors have been identified based on needs identified through SYFS current records in internal database of beneficiaries in need of such support).

Potential adoption of indicators such as CSI under the guidance of the FSS, upon the availability of resources and consolidation of good practices. SYFS uses an integrated, systematic approach to monitoring and evaluation: identifying, capturing, evaluating, managing and sharing all program knowledge through periodic tracking of outputs and outcomes and dissemination of regular reports. In the starting phase of the project the baseline data will be collected against which progress will be tracked, as well as specify data sources and collection methods and schedules, explain limitations and quality protocols, and describe evaluation methods. Indicators will be quantitative measures that can be tracked and standardized. The project staff will also employ qualitative methods as appropriate in order to capture the impact of the project. Sex and age disaggregated data collection includes review and analysis of project documents, structured and semi structured surveys, focus groups, interviews with beneficiaries and relevant stakeholders (women, men, girls, boys), site visits and observation. Ongoing feedback from beneficiaries is regularly reviewed during weekly staff meetings to address any arising issues, including protection, to assure the accountability to the affected and targeted population. Following the baseline, activity/output data are collected monthly, entered into monitoring database, internally assessed against relevant benchmarks and compiled into reports used to inform project management and donor. At the end of the project external evaluation is conducted to explore the extent to which results are achieved and the underlying reasons. It assesses the achievements of the project, as well as implementation process and management. It is also used to identify best practices and lessons learned for future relevant programming. Findings from such evaluation are shared with donor and relevant stakeholders.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

SYFS will utilize regular field visits for feedbacks through out the field monitors, where a direct access for beneficiaries will be communicated throughout the intervention. Analysis and lessons learned coming from the feedback system in place will be taken seriously to be guidance notes through implementation of the intervention, to ensure easy access to information, SYFS will use an electronic approach for feedback and the information coming from feedback will be categorized by the following cases; 1. Request for information; in case the feedback is more about requesting information from SYFS, the field monitor will visit the field with solution, and more information to be handled individually with the household2. Giving information; in case, the household is providing negative information about the placement locations, or feedback tackling protection of target group a further coordination and assessment will be carried out with the placement location, and ensure the feedback is resolved, or a better placement conditions is provided by beneficiaries 3. Protection feedbacks; upon level of protection feedback including abuse and harassment a restrictive measure will be taken in account in terms of dealing with the situation with knowledge and involvement of SYFS senior management.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention addresses a specific criterion for enrollment, where at the first stage of the project a general recommendation from MoSD will be considered for proposed list who are going to be visited, to support the potential of their engagement, the list with the supporting documents will be updated to FSS again. Women, who will be targeted in the IGA will have to comply to family size of 5 persons or above, and to be heading the households. The intervention of IGA will also crosscheck the potential of installing an IGA that can provide the family with livelihood and can generate income. All protection principles in humanitarian actions are adhered to by ensuring safe environment for the beneficiaries which includes that the placement/ work places that the beneficiaries will be involved with are safe and secure, filed visits will also be conducted to ensure that the beneficiaries are safe and there is no discrimination in the work place. In case of an escalation, activities will be suspended and re-scheduled until it is safe to resume work. Women are positively encouraged through the participation of IGA training and the provision of conditional cash-support. The intervention is a focused target groups with a pool comprising total of 315 households, and ranking the beneficiaries for support will be computed, and confidential specifically for the purpose of ranking, with data protection measures will be taken into the account throughout the process, where the intervention activities will equally target women a specific focus will be given to women who is a bread winner and with chronic/disabled members of the family. During placement period of CFW and CFI, all beneficiaries will be insured in a local insurance company, to make sure that they are well-treated if some of work-related accidents commences that includes households benefitting from IGA. Special support and efforts will be made to link CFW, and CFI young people both female and male in the work places that are more conducive to disabilities, where attention of specialized staff is going to be paid to the abilities and the demands of the placement location. In the case, of recommended household with severe disability that prevents him/her from working the household will be referred to other intervention services including IGA.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The intervention will make sure that equal and impartial access to aid through compliance process in place including a feedback mechanism will be utilized through google form. All targeted households will benefit from the project equally in terms of setting off a clear process of household qualification criteria addressed by MoSD and will be utilized by viability and vulnerability criteria, that includes being affected by the harsh economic situation, with or without skills, and the household has income below the minimum wage or no income at all. Data of beneficiaries will focus more on the most affected populations who are able to work regardless of their abilities and inabilities while places of placement and coordination will be based to utilize conducive working environments. For IGA beneficiaries, the main focus is to enable women and women with disabilities to be more resilient to surrounding challenges including financial, that depends on the husband's inability to work, and with family at large size who is vulnerable food unsecured, All data will be shared in advance with FSS to ensure that the target group is not duplicated with any intervention from any sort or benefiting from government salaries and other funding sources, applying the same criteria from the MoSD. SYFS will be committed through out implementation of the intervention to IHL, applying the selection criteria for households where the selection criteria will be communicated with the stakeholders throughout intervention, as well as a link/portal for compliance will be in place where targeted households will be trained to use it in order to submit their feedbacks throughout intervention.
