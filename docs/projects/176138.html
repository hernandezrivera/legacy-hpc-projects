Project Summary

This project will target 14,750 individuals in CES (Kajo-Keji, Yei) and Lakes (Rumbek East, Rumbek Centre,) to provide GBV support and services.
Ideally, this will include the establishment and maintenance of Women and Girl Friendly Spaces (WGFS) as well as the distribution of WGFS kits. Titi Foundation will support case management for GBV survivors, awareness raising, establishment and strengthening of referral pathways, provide Mental Health and psychosocial support (individual and group), Legal education, safety and security education, as well as livelihoods training relating to fuel sources. Additionally, Titi Foundation will engage in GBV monitoring, assessment and information sharing in each of the locations targeted in this project.
This project will help both the targeted GBV Survivors and other highly exposed vulnerable members of the communities at risk of being exposed to gender based violence (GBV) in regaining and maintaining their dignity in the society through Sustainable livelihood programs like Fuel Energy saving stoves (FESS) and briquettes.
PSEA and Gender mainstreaming continues to be the fundamental approaches that have been well integrated within the program with each beneficiaries engaged accordingly in all programs aspect that strengthens gender-sensitive and inclusive mechanisms, Titi Foundation will seek to ensure that all of its activities under this project are accessible and accommodating for all community members through an integrated Community awareness campaigns on GBV,EMAP, SASA models and Legal Aid, safe spaces for GBV survivors, PVC, Stakeholders forum, GBV support groups, Survivor network and linkages.
In conclusion, TITI Foundation GBV minimum package will be structured and SOP developed to define the critical basic support for the rescue and repatriation of the survivors. Furthermore, to ensure that other vulnerable groups, such as people with disabilities, are included in the distribution of key items, Titi Foundation will work closely with Local Monitoring Groups, as well as community leaders and civil society groups, to ensure that these community members are identified prior to implementation and the necessary accommodations are made.

1 - Needs

GBV has caused extreme harm to the population restricting survivors from co existing within the community due to the Humanitarian needs in South Sudan which are mainly driven by the impacts of years of conflict and exacerbated by the impact of climate change. Spikes in sub-national violence during 2020 resulted in more than 2,000 civilians killed between January and October and people displaced. COVID-19 has had a devastating and multi-faceted socioeconomic impact on people, including severe economic contractions, spikes in prices of basic commodities, loss of livelihoods particularly in urban areas, increasing protection risks, and disrupted access to basic services.Armed conflict, sub-national organized violence, COVID-19 restrictions and flooding have exacerbated the already extreme levels of GBV. Over 6,000 GBV incidents were recorded between January and September 2020. Nearly all, or 97 per cent of the reported GBV incident survivors, were female. A fifth of survivors were less than 18 years old. Intimate partners accounted for more than half of the alleged perpetrators. Displaced women and girls continue to bear the brunt of GBV. A third of people displaced live in communities where women and girls avoid certain areas due to fear for their safety, while basic GBV risk mitigation measures around sanitation facilities remain extremely rare outside large IDP camps.53 IDP women also reported feeling unsafe due to congestion in the camps, given that it leads to a lack of privacy which contributes to increased risk, and actual occurrence of, sexual violence.54 Sexual violence, including rape and sexual slavery, continue to be documented. According to UNMISS reports, Yei in Central Equatoria remains a major hotspot of conflict related sexual violence, followed by Unity State. Brutal rapes including gang-rapes, have been among the violations committed by armed forces and groups during cattle raids in Warrap and Lakes state.According to the IRNA conducted in Kajo-Keji, 1st to 5th of February 2021; states that the cases of rape and sexual assaults are likely to increase with the rampant existence of armed cattle keepers, different warring groups like the NAS and IO in the area. Already in July 2020, 7 rape cases were reported in Jalimo Boma and in January 2021, 1 rape case was reported in Bori inflicted by armed cattle keepers, while 10 rape cases were reported in kerwa, ajio and korijo alonside 5 cases of physical assaults and child marriage registered between October 2020 and January 2021.TITI foundation will strengthen its response plan as per the HNO 2020 to 2021 needs analysis on the plight of the South Sudanese women and girls who continue to face extreme levels of gender-based violence (GBV), much of which goes underreported, with limited availability of lifesaving GBV response services to survivors. Ninety-seven per cent of the reported GBV incident survivors were female. Left without access to the protective environment of school by the pandemic, the risk of children being neglected, abused or exploited increases significantly. The COVID-19 pandemic poses enormous risks to children, women, girls, families and communities including mental health and psychosocial, distress, intimate partner violence, family separations, violence against children, physical and emotional maltreatment, and risk of exploitation including child labour among others. Since COVID-19 was declared in South Sudan in April 2020, approximately 1,500 teenage girls in the Equatorias have either been married off or impregnated since April 2020

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Titi Foundation has a comprehensive PSEA policy that all staff are trained on, including those located outside of Juba. Additionally, whenever services or projects are implemented, communities are provided with information on the PSEA policy, awareness on what PSEA is, and the process for them to report any incidents. Beneficiaries are encouraged to come forward with any concerns or information they have to any member of Titi Foundation, and are also provided with information on referral pathways for each community.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

All of Titi Foundations Local Monitoring Groups are inclusive of different population groups, to ensure that the staff are receiving feedback from even the most vulnerable members of a community. The establishment of the complaint desk with a focal person allows for people who are not able to utilize the suggestions box to also provide feedback. The Local Monitoring Groups also consult with the broader community, and are able to advocate on behalf of their most vulnerable members. Titi Foundation staff also consult with communities to identify vulnerable members to ensure that they have access to the program’s activities and services.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

In each community, a suggestion box and complaints desk is set up with a relevant focal person. The location of both is determined in agreement with community members and leaders, to ensure that they are accessible and that they are provided in a location that beneficiaries feel comfortable with. At the beginning of each project or activity, beneficiaries are provided with information on the purpose and location of both the suggestions box and complaint desk.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Titi Foundation has established Local Monitoring Groups in each of the locations that it works in, to ensure that in addition to M&E mechanisms, community members are able to provide regular feedback on project assistance. Titi Foundation values and takes seriously all of the feedback it receives through these mechanisms, and uses it to inform the design of projects and activities where possible. This may include changing the location of activities to increase accessibility, assisting in the identification of referral services needed, or other mitigating measures to reduce risk or harm to beneficiaries, while also ensuring all projects are inclusive and accessible. Any complaints made against Titi Foundation’s staff or programming will also initiate an internal review and investigation, in alignment with the organizations policies and code of conduct.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Low risks • Risk of explosive hazard contamination in project target area Risks are low in the areas selected for this project. • Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources Titi Foundation maintains strong ties to community leaders and members in all of the locations that it operates in, reducing the risks of tensions around the distribution of services. The establishment of Local Monitoring Group, which are inclusive committees constituted of community members, also reduces the likelihood of this occurring. • Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries Titi Foundation has digitized its data collection procedures for M&E protocols to ensure both data privacy and quality. All staff are trained on ethical protocols for data management. • Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms The locations of the suggestion box and the desk for the focal point to receive complaints was decided in conjunction with communities, reducing the likelihood of this risk. • Risks that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA – Titi Foundation has a comprehensive PSEA policy which all staff are trained on in all locations. The organization’s management works closely with field-based staff to ensure that they have the resources and support needed to address any issues that may arise in this area. Communities are also provided information at the beginning of each project to ensure that they are familiar with the mechanisms within Titi Foundation that are available to support them if they raise any concerns or complaints. • Risks of women, girls and boys without and with disabilities exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance – Titi Foundation meets with community members before implementation to outline the plan and discuss any concerns that community members may have, including risks they may encounter when accessing services. For community members that may encounter barriers or difficulties in accessing humanitarian assistance, Titi Foundation works with the households and community members to identify safer alternatives, i.e. a household member can collect items on behalf of a person with disabilities, or Titi Foundation’s staff will deliver to their household while accompanied and monitored by community members. • Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.) Establishment of, and communication about, referral and protection services are communicated with each of Titi Foundation’s projects and services, to ensure that all community members have the information they need to access assistance. This is provided in all locations that the organization is working in. • Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services Titi Foundation monitors the security context and any threats or risks posed to its beneficiaries in all of its locations. Services will be postponed when risks of violence to beneficiaries and staff are too high. In cases where alternative accommodations can be made, Titi Foundation will work to establish a secondary plan with community members.

Titi Foundation has projects in FSL, S-NFI, WASH, Protection (GBV and Child Protection), and Health. As a result, the organization is well-suited for cross-sectoral services for vulnerable populations. The locations proposed for this project host IDPs, returnees, refugees and other vulnerable groups, and as a result they are all able to benefit from the organization’s services and projects. A cross-section of the population, including women, children, older persons, and persons with disabilities, are actively identified prior to project implementation, and are supported with any accommodations they may need.
