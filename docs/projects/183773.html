Project Summary

ADRA Sudan will Provide protection services in B.N, W.N, WD, CD , Kassala, Gadarief and Khartoum, due to the conflict in Sudan Women and girls are further exposed to protection risks due to needs arising from the humanitarian situation and even more with rapid inflation and soaring prices of basic needs. Parents send their children to work, exposing them to unsafe migration, trafficking, and sexual exploitation. Female IDPs face high protection risks and harassment and are occasionally killed or raped. Women and girls especially face protection risks associated with water, sanitation and hygiene practices. Women and girls remain the primary collectors of water and sometimes must travel more than two kilometers each way for water collection—over four times the Sphere standard of 500 meters. Women also report openly defecating, which exposes them to the elements and gender- based violence. The impact of SGBV is exacerbated and prolonged due to a lack of services and reporting mechanisms. Despite the new government of Sudan making positive strides towards equality with four females holding important cabinet positions, there remains a strong patriarchal culture, especially in areas that have experienced years of conflict.
ADRA’s assessment has shown SGBV is still especially prevalent. Over 78% of respondents affirmed that SGBV is present in their communities, meanwhile almost all respondents (89%) believed that they did not know of any agency or body providing protection services to SGBV survivors and only 61% would report GBV incidences. Women and girls have limited options to access latrines, either going to a distant neighbor or using open field. Further consultations with UNHCR, UNFPA, and HAC indicate that protection services are few while protection needs are expected to be large given increased access to newly freed areas.
ADRA will coordinate and integrate activities within the organization as well as with other agencies. The protection component requires collaboration with other agencies and continuing the efforts made by ADRA to develop a robust and functional referral system and solid case management processes.
ADRA will update the service directory through a service mapping exercise. A people from community groups will be trained on identification of cases and protection issues within their communities and trained on the referral system in the camp. The same groups will identify prevention measures needed to decrease the current protection risks and identify persons at risk eligible for IPA. To fortify services to at risk cases, this action will engage in psychosocial counselling to trauma and GBV survivors as well as case management. Case management will ensure that survivors identified through the community structures receive appropriate services, ADRA acknowledges that the needs are high across both communities and continues its efforts to ensure social cohesion and peaceful coexistence

1 - Needs

ADRA conduct the baseline assessment in Sep .2021 and our finding that women, girls and children they are most affected. The assessment show that most of the sexual abuse happens due to lack of services nearby houses which need women and girls to go far distances to fetch water sources particularly during the dry season, or collection of firewood, in addition to the lack of latrines in houses premises which force women to go to the field for 0pen defecation. Also existing of traders from outside of the communities resulted in different types of abuses due to unbalanced of powers and the need for money which resulted in child labors and sexual abuse. Children's lives and their well-being is threatened by conflict, floods, disease outbreaks, poverty, displacement, and unemployment, causing high levels of life-altering traumatic injuries and stress. Children continue to be at risk of abuse. According to the monitoring and reporting mechanism of grave child rights violations, at least one form of grave child rights violation was recorded against 183 children (35 per cent of whom are girls) in 2021. Sexual violence, killing and maiming were the most common forms of violations, with all survivors of sexual violence being female. According to the (FCPU) over 12,000 children (30 per cent of whom are girls) witnessed or experienced domestic violence in 2021, with the highest number in South Darfur, Blue Nile and Khartoum. Due to COVID-19 and conflict, during the first half of 2021, the number of children requiring family tracing and reunification services reached 65,000 (40 per cent female). According to a recent child protection assessment conducted across 16 states, child protection risks were already on the rise in the first half of 2021 compared with the same period in 2019, with a reported 24 per cent increase in child marriage (girls more affected than boys) and a 35 per cent increase in child labour; boys more affected than girls. (HUMANITARIAN NEEDS OVERVIEW SUDAN). In Blue Nile ADRA’s assessment showed that there are major protection gaps, as 94.1 % mentioned that there are no types of child protection support. Only 5.4% reported to have received support. 44.4% mentioned that they get support from CBOs 27.8%, from NGOs 16.7%, from INGOs and 11.1% get support from government. However from the consulted households; 45% think that women and children are subject to abuse, violence and denial of human rights.West Darfur continues to experience violence related to inter-communal conflicts triggered by competition over land and natural resources, resulting in displacement and criminality. Poverty and conflicts have impeded child protection and access to education in West Darfur, and COVID-19 has – as described in section B1 – made everything worse. The main causes for school dropout in conflict-affected areas include lack of access due to inadequate infrastructure, lack of security and too high costs for poor parents. When families lose their livelihoods, they become unable to pay school fees, resulting in children dropping out of school. Qualitative data collected by ADRA in May 2021, showed the lack of protection services in the many of the localities, especially Elgenina and Kerenik. The assessment had also highlighted the severe lack of trust between many of the communities and the duty bearers, and the lack of protection training and information that duty bearers and service providers have on protection. There is a big absence of robust and secure referral mechanisms in the state, and due to the conflict many of the partners face issues in conducting regular service mapping and assessment.

2 - Activities/Outputs

people in BNS, WNS Khartoum and West Darfur Central Darfur, Kassala and Elgadarif have improved protection environment. - Establishment of a service mapping and referral system. ADRA proposes to conduct 4 service mapping exercise in the target locations and there by develop the referral mechanism and referral pathway which will be followed by dissemination of information within the community to promote help seeking behavior and enhance a multi sectoral response to protection cases in general and GBV cases in particular. Printing and posting of 400 pictorial referral pathways in public areas of the target locations will be employed to raise awareness on the available services in the target locations - Establishment of CBPN. To promote community participation and ensure sustainability of protection projects; ADRA proposes to establish and support 20 CBPNs, 5 CBPN per target location , which will be primarily tasked to raise community awareness on protection issues; identify and referral of protection cases to service providers and conduct protection monitoring or GBV safety audits in their respective locations. CBPNs will be supported with 8 trainings,2 trainings in each target locations, on topics such as basic concepts of protection: child protection and GBV concepts, PFA. To enable them carryout their responsibilities in kind incentive such as umbrella; bags, t-shirts will be provided which in turn can also serve as IEC/BCC materials- Capacity building (service providers).Based on the findings of the service mapping and prior lessons learnt, ADRA plan to strengthen the capacity of 200 duty bearers in preventing, mitigating and responding protection risks in the target locations. Capacity building activities include training of health staff on clinical management of rape; referral pathway; GBV case management and GBV and Sudan Law.- Raising communities awareness through advocacy interventions on protection issues.ADRA supported CBPN members will conduct 200 awareness raising sessions, each CBPN conducting 10 sessions in their respective community, on child abuse and exploitation, CP/GBV referral pathways, harmful traditional practices, and sexual violence in the proposed target locations. ADRA will also engage community members through tea talks and public (market, schools, health centers and water points) by printing and disseminating IEC/BCC materials. Moreover, through commemoration of 16 days activism, African child day and international women’s day ADRA plans to raise the awareness of communities on protection issues in general and violence against women and children in particular.- Support community based protection networks- cash assistant. - Support community based protection networks- cash assistance.-

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

We ensure protection mainstreaming through all phases of project from the designing phase up to the implementation by applying the protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity. - Prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm: prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. - Meaningful Access: arrange for people’s access to assistance and services -without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services.- Participation and empowerment: support the development of self-protection, capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including - not exclusively - the rights to services.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

ADRA Sudan will ensure coordination with the WASH Sector at both the federal and state levels. ADRA WASH Technical Specialist represents ADRA Sudan in sector meetings at the national meetings, while the Project Manager/WASH Coordinator attends the WASH Sector meetings at the field level. All project activities and targeting were developed based on the gaps identified by the sector through sector reports and meetings. Prior to the project conception, ADRA conducted a joint meeting with OCHA, HAC (Humanitarian Aid Commission of Sudan), SPLA-N, WES (Rural Water Authority & Drinking Water Authority), and UNICEF at the field level, whereby the activities and villages where identified. During implementation, ADRA will ensure regular reporting to the WASH Sector. ADRA will coordinate with different national and international partners in the area, such as World Vision International (WVI), International Organization for Migration (IOM), and ADD (Addition for Disasters Assistance and Development). As WASH is closely tied to Health & Nutrition, ADRA will collaborate with International Medical Corps (IMC) and RI Blue Nile to engage community nutrition/health volunteers and Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) groups in assisting in both the design and the dissemination of hygiene information. Health stickers and posters will also be presented to PLW when they visit the health facility, as they are one of the high-risk groups. In this time of the second COVID 19 wave , ADRA will take in consideration all Covid 19 measurement getting feed back from the affected people IDP and returnees in the project targeted areas. also while collecting the SEA and abuse complain which will be preserved as top secret. because it harm the dignity of the beneficiary if unfolded by Adra staff or by health worker.
