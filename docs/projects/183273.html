Project Summary

Access to Justice for Migrant Domestic Workers addresses the needs of migrants as identified in the 2021-2022 Emergency Response Plan for Lebanon, with a focus on migrant domestic workers, most of whom are women. The intervention will advance the ERP strategic objective of enhancing timely, unhindered, and equitable access to protection assistance for migrants. This will be achieved through the provision of direct legal aid for migrant domestic workers, including information, assistance and representation. In addition, and strategic litigation will be employed to challenge the overall legal framework that facilitates the oppression and exploitation of migrant domestic workers. Through this double-faceted approach, the intervention will meet the ERP goal of delivering emergency assistance to the most vulnerable populations while supporting the transition to sustainable approaches and linking to non-humanitarian frameworks.

The living and working conditions of migrant domestic workers in Lebanon are shaped to a large degree by the sponsorship system known as Kafala, which ties the legal residency of a migrant to their employer, giving the employer undue control over the migrant’s life. Kafala excludes migrant workers from the protection of the Lebanese labour law, and fails to meet international human rights and labour standards. The ERP identifies some of the abuses migrant workers face under Kafala as including violence, exploitation, overwork, restricted movement and non-payment of wages. The grave conditions of migrant domestic workers have been worsened by recent events in Lebanon, with assessments indicating that half of all migrants today are unemployed.

The protection of vulnerable migrants has never been more imperative amid ongoing current crises. The present action will empower migrant domestic workers to seek their rights using available legal and protection mechanisms, by employing a two-pronged approach. First, the project will provide direct legal aid, including information, counselling, mediation, assistance and representation, to resolve issues such as unpaid wages, violence or abuse, and the provision or replacement of civil documentation. LAW will employ its existing networks within migrant domestic workers groups, official affiliations and coordination mechanisms with local and international stakeholders, including IOM, Al Makan Shelter, Concern Worldwide, and ARM organization, and related Embassies to implement an organized outreach strategy in order to reach the widest vulnerable migrant groups. Legal aid will attempt to secure adequate remedy for violations suffered. Informational legal awareness posts through social media will be used to empower a large audience of migrant domestic workers to recognize their rights under Lebanese law and according to universal human rights standards.
Second, the project will seek to precipitate sustainable change in the protection status of migrant domestic workers through strategic litigation and awareness campaigns. LAW will utilize its expertise in creative lawyering and strategic litigation to mount a strategic litigation case on behalf of a migrant domestic worker, complementing the first case filed in 2020 on behalf of an Ethiopian migrant domestic worker. Strategic litigation will be used to set and strengthen precedents for the criminal prosecution of abusive sponsors and recruiters. LAW will use social media platforms to generate public interest in the case, creating public pressure for delivering an effective outcome, and contributing to the ongoing transformation in attitudes and behaviours in relation to the exploitation of migrant domestic workers. The litigation will be complemented by a coordinated strategy targeting key stakeholders and decision-makers including civil society actors, legal actors, and representatives of migrant country-of-origin embassies.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Outcome 1: MDWs subject to human rights violations receive effective and meaningful access to justice. 1.1: Outreach to MDWs to reach persons in need of legal services.1.2: Legal information sessions provided to MDWs on their rights and access to justice with the distribution of legal booklets in multiple languages. Target: 500 persons (34 sessions).1.3: In select cases, LAW will provide direct legal assistance and representation to clients, which can involve filing civil documentation and/or requesting specific court orders. Target: Assistance: 125 cases; Representation: 20 cases.1.4: Online legal awareness campaign for MDWs using tailored content and boosting strategies to maximize reach to vulnerable individuals. Target: 15,000 persons reached. Outcome 2: Protection for MDWs is strengthened through strategic litigation and advocacy.2.1: File one strategic litigation case on behalf of a MDW to effect change in judicial precedents and attitudes, complementing the case LAW filed on behalf of an Ethiopian MDW in 2020. The case will be publicized in different media outlets and social media.2.2: Organize one event for civil society, legal actors and state representatives to discuss impact of strategic litigation and strategies to strengthen protection of MDWs and their inclusion in the labor law. 2.3: Conduct four closed-door meetings with representatives of MDW country-of-origin embassies to coordinate joint efforts to support MDWs.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Protection measures are in place to reduce the risk of SEA and minimize the likelihood of its occurrence. LAW starts by assessing risks and vulnerabilities of migrant domestic workers on an individual basis upon initial engagement. Based on individual risk assessments, LAW can put in place a plan to mitigate the risks such as referrals to psychosocial organizations and health actors, support from GBV partner actors to find shelter and economic support, and discussions around enhancing family and community support networks. Survivors can choose to seek protection orders and/or prosecutions with skilled and gender-sensitive lawyers. Moreover, LAW staff including lawyers and legal information officers are trained on PSEA. Complaint mechanisms for SEA are well established and swift actions are taken in response.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

LAW will work closely with affected populations and relevant stakeholders to ensure their utmost involvement in project planning and implementation. LAW will engage individuals and communities sensitively with a survivor-oriented approach, including an awareness of migrant domestic worker's vulnerabilities. LAW will also focus on designing its interventions in response to needs assessments which provide thorough understanding of the legal needs of migrant domestic workers. In addition, LAW will provide effective legal aid capable of engaging the legal system to resolve the issues identified by affected people. LAW will then focus on understanding indirect impacts, including how the legal aid provided translates into enhanced security, dignity, and a sense of justice for migrant domestic workers. LAW's previous experiences have equipped it to deliver results and reach the most marginalized migrant domestic worker groups to assist them in achieving their rights using the best tools.
