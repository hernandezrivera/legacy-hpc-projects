Project Summary

Relief International’s (RI) proposed intervention will provide health, nutrition, and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services to refugees, vulnerable host communities, and returnees, with focus on widows, orphans and poor families the East Darfur localities of Abu Jabra, El Ferdous, and Al Matariq. The goal of the project is to reduce morbidity and mortality among vulnerable and conflict-affected communities. The project has three core objectives: (1) Improved access to quality primary health services; (2) Improved access to quality preventative and curative nutrition services; and (3) Improved access to and utilization of quality WASH services. During the initial HRP Planning Exercise, RI identified Health and Nutrition only as this is the current scope of its East Darfur programming. However, to properly address the interconnected nature of health, nutrition, and WASH, RI is now proposing to add this third sector to better serve health and nutrition needs of vulnerable refugee and host communities through reduced public health risks from improper access to safe water and basic sanitation, and practice of good hygiene practices.

Needs

Based on UNHCR’s Nov 2018 East Darfur Population Dashboard, there are 14,574 refugees in Abu Jabra; 8,752 in El Ferdous; and 7,903 in Al Matariq with numbers continuing to rise throughout the State making the already strained resources shared between refugees and host communities unable to meet current needs for primary health care, nutrition services, and safe and accessible WASH facilities. RI’s June 2017 Multi-Sector Emergency Needs Assessment in El-Ferdous locality revealed substantial gaps in the health sector, inconsistent stock of key medical supplies, inconsistent staff presence, & poor infrastructure. RI also observed that the SMoH struggles to provide essential health services, as the state is new and still working to establish its social service delivery system. Many of these health facilities face challenges in establishing solid supply chains of tracer drugs and essential medical commodities without ruptures. Chronic food insecurity and acute malnutrition continue to afflict communities as the price of staple foods rise, local currency devaluation persists, and food transport limitations continue. From Jan to Oct 2018, a total of CU5 567 SAM cases and 1,284 MAM cases were admitted into RI-supported nutrition centers. Poor feeding practices and lack of nutritionally-diverse food sources contribute to malnutrition (IYCF KAP Survey in El-Ferdous locality, RI). Highlighting the link between health, nutrition, and WASH, East Darfur has recorded over 504 suspected cases of AWD, mostly among refugees. Poor WASH conditions lead to an increase in worm infections and diarrheal diseases. The 2018 HNO in Sudan notes 4.4 million people in need of WASH services nationally with a vulnerability index ranging from 4-5. The demand for water continues to grow by a few percent yearly and, as rainfall fluctuates between wet and dry periods, and gradually declines, water availability is a problem for people, livestock, and crops. Water was scarce for host communities even prior to refugees settling. Land constraints have led to high latrine usage ratios across refugee areas with many latrines in disrepair and in need of replacement. In consultation with the state’s WASH Cluster lead, RI identified WASH critical coverage gaps in the three proposed localities. RI proposes adding this third sector in order to address the needs identified and provide more sustainable programming in line with the Government's humanitarian-development nexus approach through integration of WASH infrastructure and behavior change/social norm shifts within the facilities and communities served with health and nutrition interventions.

Activities

RI proposes an integrated suite of activities in Abu Jabra, El Ferdous, and Al Matariq based on needs and existing coverage. RI assumes 47% male & 53% female participation. Health: RI will support one clinic in each locality to provide primary health & RH services to 56,949 people. Quality care will be ensured through training seconded MOH health staff (23/locality), CHWs (3/locality), and VTMs (26/locality). RI will also support clinic rehabilitation; provision of supplies & pharmaceuticals; emergency referral services; & disease surveillance, reporting, & response. Nutrition: RI's preventive approach covers: practical BCC, CMAM, & IYCF counselling with positive deviance to tackle social norms; 1,500 kitchen gardens for food access; & cooking demonstrations for feeding & care practices. BCC will integrate WASH and health. Screening, referral, follow-ups, & BCC will be done by trained CNVs (16/locality). Complicated SAM cases will be referred to El Dain Hospital. WASH: RI will rehabilitate 10 village handpumps plus WUC training, water tank construction (1/locality) with chlorine tab treatment, safe drinking water infrastructure at the health/nutrition facilities (1/locality), & water quality monitoring support per locality. Toilet facilities plus solid waste incinerators/pits will be provided to the same facilities, and 50 IHHLs/locality will be constructed. CLTS will be used for hygiene promotion & sanitation demand coupled with distribution of 300 hygiene kits/locality.

Impact on environment/natural resources

According to Sudan's 2016 National Adaptation Plan (NAP), East Darfur ranges from a semi-arid climate in the north to high rainfall savannah in the south. Climate predictions see decreasing annual rainfall and increasing variability. In line with the NAP, RI will ensure water facilities are monitored for both quality and depth to better understand groundwater resources and drilling of new water wells.Medical waste will be managed under WHO guidelines to minimize any solid waste impacts. The kitchen gardens would have minimal potential impacts. RI will ensure that the type of seeds are suitable to the local area and culture, and will not promote chemical inputs or irrigation efforts beyond hand watering/micro-irrigation.
