Project Summary

As part of its response to the Beirut Explosion, WVL will support in immediate shelter support to affected communities. Shelter interventions will include damaged household repairs, including water tanks and pipes when needed, in this initial phase of the response.
WVL's engineers will assess the most urgent needs in damaged apartments. WV will use several modalities under this outcome including direct provision of service to the most vulnerable households by hiring skilled labor, provision of cash/value vouchers to support families to purchase required items and conduct their own repairs and cash for work to skilled laborers to complete medium scale repairs. WVL will also ensure that affected households have access to water and sanitation services through the rehabilitation of damaged plumbing services inside households.
WVL will aim to reach 1,366 households (5,464 individuals) in in Achrafieh, Gemmayze, Mar Mkhayel, Rmeil, Geitawi, Bourj Hammoud, Sin El Fil, Mazraa, Karantina, Basta, Khandaa al Ghamee and Bachoura. Coordination on exact locations will be ensured through sector coordination.

1 - Needs

The blasts damaged numerous buildings in the Greater Beirut area. Numerous residences have also been damaged or destroyed. Families have been forced to seek temporary shelter at schools, families/ acquaintances, and other administrative buildings. The Lebanon Red Cross, as part of a rapid needs assessment with the Governor of Beirut and the municipality of Beirut have identified Gemmayzeh, Mar Mikhael, Ashrafiyeh and Qarantina as priority districts. Complete destruction of buildings and infrastructure was reported within a 2 km range from the port. Buildings up to 8 km from the explosion site reported extensive structural damage, while properties 20 km from the blast reported minor damage. As per the initial assessment conducted by WVL, 80% of surveyed individuals had broken windows, while 10% had ceilings or walls falling and 6% of the houses were completely destroyed. Most of those surveyed (78% of them) were still residing in their damaged home. Those who were displaced are staying with friends and relatives or have accepted offers of free accommodation from fellow citizens. Local communities may not be able to sustain this support for an indefinite period however. In fact many of displaced people have been seen to go back to their damaged apartments, despite them not being repaired yet. As accommodation alternatives had to be found quickly it is unlikely that COVID-19 distancing measures were observed. The socio-economic impact of the financial crisis that surfaced in October 2019 already dragged vulnerable households into extreme poverty. A representative survey of households with children registered in ongoing WV programs found that nearly half of their parents lost their work and their means of earning income. 83% of them reported to be accumulating debt , meaning family coping mechanisms that would be drawn on in response to a disaster have already been diminished or completely depleted. According to UNHCR , there are 916,156 registered refugees in Lebanon of whom about 300 000 refugees settled in Greater Beirut , but estimates suggest a share of refugees in the city of up to 20%. With 1 in 4 people in Lebanon being refugees; the stress on infrastructure and public services was already great prior to the explosion. The explosion caused more people to lose their jobs or livelihoods (formal and non-formal) pushing them further into poverty. The increase in prices and reduction in the purchasing power of the people will also make rehabilitation works very difficult, especially that most suppliers are pricing their products at the USD rate.

2 - Activities

Conduct rehabilitation directly for major rehabilitationProvide cash for shelter to people for minor rehabilitation With 300,000 people having lost their homes, providing support for immediate shelter needs is a key priority. Shelter interventions will include damaged household repairs, including water tanks and pipes when needed, in this initial phase of the response. WV will have engineers visit different areas and conduct a rapid assessment of the most urgent needs in damaged apartments including water or shelter provision. Based on this assessment households will be provided with essential life-saving water and shelter solutions. Repairs will include door and windows repairs/ replacements, repair of damaged walls and ceilings, weather proofing and other basic repairs. As part of the rehabilitation works conducted in damaged apartments, WV's engineers will evaluate the need for water and sanitation rehabilitation. Based on this assessment, households will be provided with essential lifesaving water and shelter solutions. Repairs can include toilets, handwashing, pipes, etc. WV will use several modalities under this outcome including direct provision of service to the most vulnerable households by hiring skilled labor, provision of cash/value vouchers to support families to purchase required items and conduct their own repairs and cash for work to skilled laborers to complete medium scale repairs. This multi-modality approach allows World Vision to meet the needs of individuals affected, using the most appropriate approach based on their vulnerabilities and unique needs. unit costs for rehabilitation of minor and major damages will be aligned to the sector guidance ($1,500 for minor repairs, $4,500 for rehabilitation) and locations will be coordinated at the sector level. The intervention that WV will be following while doing rehabilitation will take into consideration the environmental intervention, meaning that the team will be promoting the usage of the water saving and energy saving methodologies while ensuring energy conservation measures whenever applicable (e.g. LED lighting, window insulation). In addition to the above, WV will liaise with the social stability working group to make the needed recommendation for an environmentally friendly interventions of proper waste disposal.
