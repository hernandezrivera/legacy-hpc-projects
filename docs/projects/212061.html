Project Summary

Needs.
• In Quneitra, the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has surged twofold in the past five years, escalating from 2.7 per cent in 2019 to 5.1 per cent in 2023 across surpassing acceptable to stress levels (SMART survey-2023). Presently, an estimated 3,647 children under the age of five in Quneitra are stunted. Furthermore, the dietary patterns of children aged 6–23 months have deteriorated, with only 1 in 10 consuming a Minimum Acceptable Diet in 2023. Additionally, 1 in 4 children consume a Minimum Dietary Diversity, correlating with stunting.
• One in five teenage girls (15-19 years old) either has children, is pregnant, or lactating. Teenage mothers exhibit a higher likelihood of having children aged 6–59 months suffering from wasting, with a higher prevalence of wasting themselves. Wasting levels among women and girls in Quneitra reads 6.6% (SMART,2023). Alarming statistics reveal that one in four women aged 15–49 years suffers from anemia, with an even higher prevalence observed among teenage girls, where 1 in 2 girls is anemic. Additionally, 1 in 10 women is wasted, contributing to increased risks during pregnancy.
Intervention.
• Support screening of children agreed 0-59 months and PLWs.
• Identify and refer children aged 0-59 months and PLWs malnourished cases.
• Provide adolescent girls with micronutrient supplements that contain adequate iron.
• Provide PLWs with Multiple micronutrients supplements (MMS) including iron folate for 6 months.
• Support skilled individual IYCF counselling to benefit PLWs.
• Support Cash Transfer for nutrition targeting children aged 6-23 months and PLWs at risk of malnutrition.
Implementation of activities.
• Nutrition interventions will be provided through outreach community teams and in close coordination with DOH in Quneitra and the Nutrition sector to refer and follow up on the identified malnourished cases among children and PLWs. Cash transfer for Nutrition will be transferred through one of the financial service providers in the country to households that have PLWS or children at risk of malnutrition. The list of targeted beneficiaries will be selected based on technical criteria as developed and shared by the Nutrition sector.
• The above package of the interventions will be conducted in three sub-districts that administratively belong to Quneitra governorate Fiq, Al-Khashniyyeh and Quneitra.

Protection Risk Analysis
Children are particularly vulnerable to all forms of undernutrition in times of instability and crisis, as they are dependent on others, and are often physically fragile. The first 1000 days of life are critical for child development (physical, mental, and cognitive), and it is important to make sure that children’s growth is not compromised during times of stress. In addition, nutritional habits, food taboos, and discriminatory access to food within the home can differently affect women, men, girls, and boys, imbalances that can worsen in times of crisis. Risk prevention measures should be integrated into nutrition-related activities, emphasizing the safety of the affected population as a sub-objective. Discussions pertaining to protection, including psychosocial support and gender-based violence (GBV), should be incorporated into mother-to-mother nutrition initiatives. Recognizing the prioritization of the most vulnerable populations, namely children and pregnant/breastfeeding women, nutrition initiatives will collaborate with the protection sector. This collaboration aims to establish referral pathways between nutrition programs and GBV/child protection services. Nutrition workers will undergo training on straightforward methods for identifying GBV and child protection cases, enabling them to refer individuals to the required services
