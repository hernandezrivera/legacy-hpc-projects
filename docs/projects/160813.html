Project Summary

The project will focus on the recovery of the crisis-affected populations through the provision of inputs for improved agricultural production as well as income generation activities for the development of sustainable livelihood opportunities. The project will foster the resilience of Affected Communities by developing social cohesion by developing social networks and resilience of the displaced and their integration with the host communities by developing social protection programs. The rebuilding and establishment community assets, as well as basic service infrastructure, will build the capacity of communities to cope with crisis; infrastructure development includes, health facilities and WASH infrastructure as well as retraining and strengthening existing systems of governance and capacity for relevant Ministries, local authorities and State actors to respond to the needs of conflict-affected populations. Outreach will prioritize the needs for different age-groups and sex will be prioritized and the protection of beneficiaries will be ensured during the implementation of activities.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Northeast Nigeria has endured for over ten years has eroded and destroyed public infrastructure and access to basic services remains inadequate, especially due to the continued influx of internally displaced that places additional pressure on existing services. Currently, 5.6 million people face increased hardship (compromising of 1.8 million girls, 1.5 million boys, 1.2 million women, and 1.1 million men) across the BAY states (HRP, 2019). The number of displaced continues to increase; according to the IOM DTM Round 28 (August 2019) reports that 2,018,513 individuals remain displaced compared to 1, 980,036 reported in Round 27 (June 2019). Host communities struggle to cope with hosting a majority of IDPs, 59% reside with the host community and 41% in camps (IOM DTM Round 28, August 2019) especially as the region faced a relatively low poverty record prior to the conflict at 76% in 2010. The cost of the damage attributed to the conflict has been significant $6.9 billion in Borno and $1.2 billion in Yobe state (RBPA, world bank, 2016). 4.6 million people are being targeted for early recovery and livelihood assistance to develop their self-reliance and improve equitable access to basic services and employment opportunities. Farmer-herder conflict across the Middle-Belt region and the Boko Haram conflict in northeast Nigeria have severely affected food production systems. The areas most severely affected by food insecurity include Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states in northeast Nigeria, where over 2.7 million people are projected to be food insecure (IPC Phases 3-5) in the June-August period (Cadre Harmonise 2019). Women and child-headed households are further excluded with limited formal education, limited engagement in the formal economy and value addition activities and they also face cultural systems of patriarchy that restrict their roles and responsibilities - ability to influence key decision making processes in the household. Host communities face vulnerabilities being in a situation of poverty prior to conflict and facing poor access to resources for their food production (especially as some areas of Borno planting is limited to a 1-2km periphery area) and markets are disrupted and the conflict causes inflation (resulting from restricted cross-border trade, movement curfews and attacks by NASAG). As a result girl, youth, women and the elderly are prioritized under ACF's strategy

2 - Activities

Activity 01: Fostering Resilience of Affected Communities through Restoration and Strengthening of Productive Assets, and Supporting Income Generating Activities.OUTPUT 1.1 : Community assets recovered, restored, and protected livelihoods through resilience programmingOUTPUT 1.2: Beneficiaries trained to improve the productivity of household, including extension, entrepreneurship, capacity building, agricultural best practices, value addition and processing, and technology transferThe output 1.1 will be given priority most vulnerable affected households with female-headed households and families have SAM beneficiaries undernutrition program. The activities include Micro-irrigation, rehabilitation of irrigation facilities, environmental/soil & water conservation at the farm level and Adaptation to climate change and environment-friendly agriculture. The output 2.2 will be focused on Capacity building and knowledge transfer, Formation of local saving and lending associations (SLAs) and strengthening existing societies and the Linkage of beneficiaries to access micro-credit. Activity 02: Improvement of sustainable WASH services to affected male, female, boys and girlsOutput 2.1: Water facilities rehabilitated or constructed within affected communities for man, women, boys and girls.Output 2.2: WASH facilities in Health facilities/schools improved for man, women, boys and girlsThis activity will be implemented in communities with IDPs, schools and health facilities with close coordination of RUWASA, Sate ministry of water resources, Ministry of Health, Ministry education and BOSEPA/YOSEPA to improve the WASH services for man, women, girls, and boys. The activity include water user management formation and training with 50% of female members, Rehabilitation/construction of water scheme including water quality testing, school hygiene club formation and training with 50% of female members, training of health workers with focus on 50% of female, rehabilitation/construction of gender-sensitive WASH facilities in schools and health centers and solid waste management. WASH facilities will be located after consideration of male and female beneficiaries to ensure protection.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure accountability to affected populations, Action Against hunger set up mechanisms to create awareness to beneficiaries. When the communities/camps were identified for support, the accountability team held community discussions to ensure communities/beneficiaries understand the intended purpose of the project, rights beneficiaries as well as complaints/feedback mechanism put in place by the project. The team places banners showing access to the feedback mechanism in strategic locations in those camps or communities. The Accountability team also holds bi-monthly feedbacks days in camps/communities where the projects been implemented. Such feedback days are used as an avenue to continuously sensitize the targeted project beneficiaries on their right and availability of the feedback mechanism. Toll-free lines are clearly written on beneficiaries’ smart cards used for disbursement so that they can easily have access to the line when they have complaints or feedback regarding the projects. At all intervention sites, banners of complaints/feedback mechanisms are compulsorily displayed as well as prices of supported food items. Complaints/Feedback Channels is mechanisms or channels for receiving complaints/feedbacks in the projects include the toll-free line, complaint boots, help desk, community meetings and focus group discussions as well as staff field visit and post-distribution monitoring exercise. Complaint booths and help desk will develop to receive the beneficiaries face to face in the office and communities.
