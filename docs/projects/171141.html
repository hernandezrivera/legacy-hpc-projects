Problem statement
According to the recent FSNAU report, Shebelle river floods have resulted in displacement in Lower Shebelle region particularly villages under Marka, Afgoye and Walanweyn. SAREDO assessment revealed that an estimated number of 6,850 households (41,100 populations) have been affected by the floods in Afgoye. Almost 1/3 of affected households, which is at least 2,232 HHs (13,392 populations) have been forced to displace.
Furthermore, 2500 Households (15000 populations) have been displaced in Km50 township, among them conflict IDPs, who lost their livestock and homes during armed conflicts as their livestock had been rustled.
Besides Marka and Afgoye districts, About 4000 HHs (24000 populations) have been affected by floods in Wanlaweyn districts.
The floods have had damaging effect not only the communities but also the infrastructures. Shallow wells, schools, and feeder roads have been damaged in the flooding with subsequent displacement of the communities. Thus, when the flood waters drain, the effected families are anticipated to return to their villages; however they face several challenges with regard to damaged agriculture infrastructure such as silted secondary canals and damages of canals road crossings/culverts. These challenges are regarded as one of the main aggravating factor for flood affected populations since there are no other sources the water may be streamed to, given that canals which would swallow the rain waters too are silted.
Additionally, damages of feeder roads resulted in challenges for transporting agricultural products to markets. This is directly related to post harvest losses as well as losses of profits as perishable commodities may not be exported to markets during rainy seasons.
The rational of this project is that agriculture is the only way to mitigate food insecurity, supporting agriculture sector is a crucial way to address the continuing food security and livelihood crisis in Somalia.
The project will assist communities to get access to income generating opportunities through the rehabilitation of canals through cash for work in addition to creating agricultural labor opportunities for the affected farmers. This will improve cash flows in the local markets resulting in economic and food security improvement.
Moreover, the flood affected IDPs need to be assisted with agriculture inputs and rehabilitating irrigation canals, otherwise they may not return back to their villages and remain in the squalid IDP camps they currently live. This will worsen the situation of the internally displaced communities in lower Shebelle region, leading to overcrowding in the cities as well as social and security challenges to the country. To void such challenges, this project aims to contribute to finding durable solutions for the flood affected IDPs by assisting them to restart their preferred income generating activities(farming).

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The justification for the CTP aspect of this project is that the target flood affected small holder famer households lost their assets through complex protracted climate change and conflict related crisis, with this regard, they may not be able to resume farming without additional support. With the provision of cash assistance for 3 months (one cropping season) coupled with trainings on good agriculture practices, they will be able to produce food for their immediate livelihoods needs while they would be able to market the surplus to purchase agriculture inputs, meet their other basic needs, and sustain farming after the project phaseout. Without cash assistance, the target households may not be able to wait the cropping season harvest and they would be once again affected by food security and livelihoods crisis. The cash assistance will also prevent target households from consuming or selling the distributed agriculture inputs to survive.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Because of the improving security situation in Lower Shabelle region, access to markets increase to thrive as new roads open. This increases target households access to affordable commodities during and after project response. Generally there is also increasing demand for agriculture commodities in both Lower Shabelle region and neighboring regions, hence with SARED support to the target flood affected households resumption of food production, markets prices will be stabilized, and in return the supported target families will have access to market where they are able to sell their commodities, improve their income, meet their basic needs and contribute to more sustainable food security and livelihoods at both community and national levels.
