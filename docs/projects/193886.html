Project Summary

DanChurchAid/Norwegian Church Aid (DCA/NCA) and its partner, the Ecumenical Accompaniment Programme in Palestine and Israel (EAPPI) propose a project that aims i) to offer protective presence to vulnerable communities in Palestine living under the occupation, ii) to monitor and report on human rights violations, iii) to support Palestinian and Israeli peace groups, and iv) to advocate for policy change to end the military occupation of Palestine. The Protective Presence that The Ecumenical Accompaniment Programme in Palestine and Israel (EAPPI) is providing contributes to creating a safer environment for women and men to address their own needs, to solve their own conflicts and to protect vulnerable individuals (women, men, and children) and communities. The project´s aim is to ensure a continuation of the Ecumenical Accompaniers (EAs) presence in the most vulnerable areas of the West Bank to deter violence, document violations of international laws and regulations, and gather information for international advocacy work to raise awareness amongst the international community and duty bearers with regards to the current situation in Palestine and the violations of IHL and IHRL, pursuing the vision of a just peace with freedom, equal rights, dignity, and security based on international law.

1 - Needs

DCA/NCA through its implementing partner EAPPI provide protective presence for vulnerable communities (women, men, children, and elderly) living in areas close to settlements mainly area C, Hebron H2 and the seam zone. EAPPI’s data shed light on the coercive environment across the oPt, especially in Area C and H2 that leaves Palestinians with high levels of vulnerability facing violence and harassment from Israeli settlers and military. In addition to violations and restrictions that are occurring to Palestinians by the Israeli security forces, the situation has been escalating in the recent years due to the absence of international protective presence during Covid-19. As HR and IHL violations are only increasing and as access constraints are still ongoing, people are harassed and violently attacked in Area C and H2. The need in providing protection to the Palestinians living in the West Bank (WB) is urgent and pressing. Additionally, the expansion of settlements and the increase in settlers’ attacks; means that families depending on agricultural and shepherding income face additional threats to their livelihood. Worthy to note that, women are deeply affected by the occupation, and checkpoints, house demolitions, and settler violence. Women are the ones who run their households without regular or sufficient water and are usually the ones at home during settler attacks and, thus, are the ones who must shield their children. Additionally, the traditional role of women in communities at risk places them in a position to deal with the trauma, emotional toll and the psycho-social impact of systemic settler violence on children. In the last 4 years there has been an increase in human rights abuses. In 2019 only, EAPPI documented 993 violations which is the highest number of incidents documented since 2016. These incidents were as follows: 26% were displacement incidents, 30% were Access/attacks incidents and 44% were violence incidents. In 2020 before the start of the COVID 19 pandemic, between the period 01-01-2020 until 03-03-2020 EAPPI reported 288 (people affected female adults 13,215, male adults 33,738, male children 11,201, female children 11,083) compared to 128 incidents in the same period in 2019. Since the start of the pandemic and the absence of international protective presence 2020 and 2021 witnessed an increase in violations. In 2020 only, the oPt saw an increase in demolitions and confiscation of property by 1/3 compared to 2019. Likewise, local community members and NGOs reported an increase in settler harassment and violence. 2020 and 2021 has had a higher number of displaced people with 1,009 (244Girls, 240 Women, 245 Men, 280 boys) in 2020 and 962 (246 Girls, 297 Boys, 208 Women,297 Men) in 2020 and 958 till September 2021 while in 2019, 907 were displaced. In the West Bank, in 2021, 84 (5 Women, 17 Boys, 62 Men) Palestinians were killed and more than 1686 injured, including 1139 by live ammunition. The need therefore has increased as vulnerable communities are rendered even more vulnerable in the current climate. Since the start of 2022, EAPPI has documented between January 2022 till June 2022, 412 incidents 221 of them where violence, 96 where access to livelihood, 95 displacement and 18 others, the majority of these incidents were carried out in Hebron with affected 11,703 in total (4452 M, 4057 W, 1759 B, 1435 G). The increase in number of incidents shows the importance of the Protective Presence provided by the EAPPI teams on the ground especially in the areas were Palestinian civilians and Israeli settlers meet such as H2 area in Hebron and in area C friction points where direct confrontation between Palestinian civilians and Israeli settlers might take place.

2 - Activities

Recruitment and training: Recruiting and training of female and male EAs in home countries to upgrade the protective presence and thus monitoring and documentation in Area C, and Hebron (H2). During their training all EA’s read the code of conduct and carry out an online training with regards to the code of conduct with an emphasize on what constitutes sexual exploitation and abuse. They are informed of the rules, how to report misconduct and know the consequences. In addition, DCA/NCA and EAPPI are part of the PSEA network in the oPt and will ensure that a plan is put in place on how the partners will raise awareness about PSEA among their targeted communities this will include developing a joint DCA/NCA and EAPPI awareness raising materials including messages about PSEA and the available complaints mechanisms and their entry points. In addition, DCA/NCA will carry out through its GBV local partner, a GBV detection and referral to EAPPI field team who is a non-GBV actor so that EAPPI can provide this information to the EAs as part of the training package. Protective presence: Providing protective presence operating from five placements, covering seven Governorates in the West Bank. (Depending on the needs on the ground, as areas such as H2 requires constant presence by EAs whereas other locations are visited on a weekly, bi-weekly or a monthly matter. This depends on the needs of the community and the vulnerability of that community in that time.) - Joining farmers in different seasons (ploughing, planting, and harvesting) when they need to access their lands in some of the hot spots: for example land near the settlement fence, or when coordination with the Israeli authorities is needed. – Joining herders; EAs accompany herders during shepherding, especially in places near outpost where settlers are trying to take over land. In addition, EAPPI responds to sleepover requests by the local communities and provides protective presence overnight after getting the security clearance from the Security Department. Walking with girls and boys to school to provide protective presence on a regular basis for protection against settler violence in the old city of Hebron (H2) and other communities in Area C in addition, children with disability (boys and girls) are also provided protective presence while attending the school and provided assistance by the EAs on the ground. - Presence at non-violent demonstrations and monitoring the situation (after getting security clearance from the Security Department). - Responding to emergency needs following alerts from local contacts and partner organizations – Furthermore, female EAs conduct woman-to-woman conversations between female EAs and female beneficiaries. The EAs have at their disposal a female interpreter that can ease communication. In these conversations the female EAs follows up on the needs of the women in the each of the communities and how they can support and offer protection and if they have certain concerns, if there are any issues that they are facing that can be helped by protective presence or any incidents that they need to be aware of and document. For People with disabilities (PwD), EAs in every group will meet with the head of the community to see if any PwD reside in the community and meet with them to see if there is any assistance in terms of protective presence that they can support with. Reporting and database: Updating the EAPPI database and referring incidents; EAPPI Incident Database is the tool used by EAPPI to refer incidents taking place on the ground to Diplomats, UN bodies, INGOs, NGOs and other actors on the ground. The incident report submitted by the EAs in the field, which is the first step in the process, include the following information: Date, Community Name, Location, Checkpoint Type (closures / barriers), Checkpoint Name (if applicable), Perpetrator, Other organizations present, School name (if applicable), Victims, Witness, Consent (To report, To use picture), Follow up by the Authorities (if applicable), Has or will complaint be filed?, Any type of Disability?, Description, The needs of the Community (Legal, Psychosocial, Protective, Shelter, other), Violation and the Human Rights Affected. The second step is done by the Field Officer at the Jerusalem Office, Incidents submitted are reviewed and referred to other organizations (email Generated from the Database). The selection of who receives what is based on 1- The request from the organization itself on which incident they are interested in. 2- The organization’s field of work. 3- The location of the incident. Note: EAPPI reports on; child rights violations and access to education incidents, legal aid needed, settler violence, access to land and livelihood, road closures, need for shelter, settlement expansion, PSS, arrests, and incidents at checkpoints. Based on the needs of the beneficiaries the incident will be referred. As an example, if they needs is legal aid the incident will be referred to legal aid and the field officer will follow up on the matter. If the need is protection, EAPPI will refer it to its project partners as well as the protection cluster. UNOCHA is copied on most of the incidents because they work on documentation and the UNOHCHR is copied on incidents where there is a need for referral. Advocacy: Advocacy efforts by EA’s when they go back to their home countries - Receiving international delegations and taking them for a field visit - Advocacy trips on a capitol level and to UN human rights council.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

DCA/NCA will support partners to increase the capability of the complaints and feedback mechanism to inform decision-making, enhance accountability and transparency. This will be ensured through engaging beneficiaries in addressing the expected risks and challenges and empowering them to participate in the monitoring and evaluation process of the project. DCA/NCA and EAPPI will engage the community through consultation sessions to ensure the complaint channels are relevant and accessible to the target groups. A variety of complaint channels that the beneficiaries can access such as hotlines, web-based complaint mechanism, emails, and through the field staff. Specific complaint mechanisms are in place to address sensitive complaint in a confidential matter. In addition, there are various ways in which the local beneficiaries can submit complaints to the office. For instance, the beneficiaries can present their complaint directly to the EAs who will forward it to the office. Another channel that they can use is the EAPPI driver or the local contacts who are also the focal point for community-related matters. Another option is to call the field officer or the Jerusalem office or submit complaints via e-mail to the office. In addition, DCA/NCA holds regular field visits and meets with beneficiaries to receive feedback from them and the beneficiaries are able to file a complaint through DCA/NCA complaint mechanism DCA/NCA have been also supporting its partners in increasing the capability of the complaints and feedback mechanism to inform decision-making, enhance accountability and transparency. The feedback will be used for; planning future activities, monitoring the success and for improving the activities for the future.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

DCA/CA and EAPPI apply a Do No Harm (DNH) approach that is integrated in the project at all levels. EAPPI is trained and aware of the approach which is part of the human rights-based approach adopted by EAPPI. Risk analysis and management is covered by EAPPI’s Security Department, Field Officer and Local Program Coordinator. This ensures that all actions that are taken by EAPPI staff and accompaniers follow the security procedures and guidelines. The security protocols are regularly updated with the aim of minimizing the risks and creating a safe working environment. Informed consent: All EAs make sure that the rights-holders that they interact with in the field give consent to the use of photos, quotes, and other information for reporting purposes. When evaluation practices include children, consent is obtained by parents. Furthermore, EAPPI will not operate in any area without the community and its decision makers' consent. Management on data confidentiality and sensitivity: All staff members are obliged to sign a clause on Respect of Confidentiality and Data Protection. EAs have to sign the Terms of Use before starting to use the Hub and the Incident database. All manuals that the teams receive are marked with Confidentiality sign and the disposal of hard copies is taken care for by the Jerusalem Office. Furthermore, there is a limit of access to different kinds of users, e.g: the EAs have limited access compared to the LPC. For trainings, EAs get an initial training in their home countries about the history of the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, an update on the current situation in the region, training on HR and IHL, security briefings, language course, cultural sensitivity, an introduction to non-violent methods and accompaniment etc. All EA’s receive the code of conduct, are informed by it and sign it before entering the countries. Additionally, all EA’s participate in an online training for the code of conduct which they are required to do. In Jerusalem, the EAs receive a 10- day training on team building, Humanitarian Law, Stress Management, Communication, Local Security Briefing, Incident reporting and Data Management, Child Protection, GBV detection and referral and briefings on activities particular to each placement. Additionally, to apply the do not harm and gender sensitivity all groups of EAs have female and male representation. The new teams spend five days in their placements with the old teams who introduce them to the activities on the ground. With regards to monitoring, the monitoring process will include regular field visits by EAPPI’s vist officer and field visits to check if beneficiaries are satisfied with DCA/NCA is a Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) certified agency. PSEA mainstreaming will be part of this project in the following ways: •DCA/NCA implements a code of conduct that is signed by all its staff that reflects among others the values and standards regarding PSEA. DCA will ensure that the cooperation agreement with implementing partners consists of an article that requires its partner to have an internal staff code of conduct in place that reflects as a minimum the values and standards of partners regarding PSEA. •DCA/NCA will ensure that the target groups and communities have an accessible, a confidential and a safe complaint handling mechanisms during the implementation of the project, where the sensitive complaints are dealt with and acted upon with confidentiality (either by using DCA’/NCA’s already functioning complaints mechanism: me.complaints@dca.dkand/ or using the partner complaints mechanism channels - EAPPI) •DCA/NCA staff will monitor closely the practices of partners to reinforce the PSEA and ensure that the targeted communities are aware of PSEA and the available functional complaints mechanism. •DCA/NCA’s and EAPPI’s PSEA local focal points are an active member in the oPt-HCT-PSEA Network. DCA/NCA will continuously update its partners with relevant and needed PSEA information.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

DCA/NCA and partners adopt Anti-discrimination policy that criminalizes all aspects of discrimination and guides the implementation of interventions considering the humanitarian principles. All beneficiaries will be treated equally, impartially, and transparently, with attention to gender issues, considering the extra vulnerability inflicted on women in the society, and to people with disability. A principled humanitarian response will be implemented. EAPPI is guided by the notion of principled impartiality, meaning that it is not taking sides in the Israeli-Palestinian conflict. It supports groups from both communities working for peace and justice. Also, within the Palestinian communities, it does not engage in political action or preferential treatment of particular segments of the population. EAs are committed to hearing from as many perspectives as possible and having an open and honest conversation with the people living in the region. EAs value the work of deepening interfaith relations and are thus bridging the gaps among various communities. As a faith-based programme EAPPI stands in solidarity with churches and the minority of indigenous Palestinian Christians in the Holy Land. Additionally, EAPPI is committed to gender and age mainstreaming that takes place through guiding and strategy documents, e.g. plans, checklists, etc. at organizational and programmatic level. Specifically, the EAPPI Strategic Plan 2017- 2021 includes a sub chapter addressing the mainstreaming of gender: “Gender equality, justice and mainstreaming”. Organizational documents and tools, e.g., work plans and M&E tools are used specifically to disaggregate data. Gender data and needs are collected through female translators and higher involvement of female EAs, this is carried out in the woman to woman conversations that are held. Safeguarding mechanisms and policies are also used to support gender sensitivity, e.g code of conduct, sexual harassment and security policies. The disaggregation of data, safeguarding and gender sensitivity are included as part of EAs training in Jerusalem. An assessment that addresses needs of different age groups has been conducted through external consultations completed in 2016(overall program, including gender) and 2020(protective presence in education). With regards to people with disabilities, WCC-EAPPI operates within the overall vision of WCC, which as a fellowship of churches is committed to a vision of inclusion of people with disabilities. Operationally, people with disabilities have been included as local contact persons and have taken part in the design and strategy of the work. Disabled persons also have fed into the 2016 baseline study. The program prioritizes support and protection of children/people with disability at checkpoints and when attempting to access education. In addition, as EAs are present in the communities they are in direct contact with people of disabilities and offer their support in protective or to document any incidents that occurred and if there is a need for a referral. The program has informally adopted many aspects of the IASC guidelines in regard to information management and protection. Worthy to note that even in situations when EAPPI alone cannot provide assistance to vulnerable communities, the referral system to partner organization makes it possible to signal the need of particular individuals or communities to other actors that have the knowledge or expertise in a specific area.
