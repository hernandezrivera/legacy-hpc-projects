Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims at improving the food security of vulnerable Syrian households, in both the short and the medium-long term.
In the short-term, ACTED will reduce the food security of conflict-affected communities through the provision of rapid and flexible food assistance. This will include the following 3 modalities: 1) RTERS for exceptional cases in which beneficiaries do not have access to cooking facilities; 2) Food rations for households living in areas where markets are inaccessible, or food is unavailable; 3) Food vouchers for families with access to functioning markets.
In the medium and long-term, ACTED food security assistance will be underpinned by the rationale of giving vulnerable households the resources and skills to obtain a self-sufficient food production. In order to achieve this, ACTED will provide kitchen garden kits and training to vulnerable households. Overall, this project will benefit approximately 47,580 individuals.

Needs

According to the HNO 2019, an estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. Moreover, an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households have outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people adopt negative coping strategies. With an 83 - 89 percent people living below poverty lines, and with cumulative GDP loss of $226 billion between 2011 – 2016 out of which $16 billion in losses and damages attributable to agriculture sector alone, the relationship between poverty and food security is becoming inseparable. A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 80 percent of an unskilled laborer’s monthly salary and 50-80 percent of a public service employee’s monthly salary. Finally, unprecedented livestock asset losses estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation affecting livelihoods leading to irreversible coping strategies. (HNO 2019). Additionally, availability of food in Syria has also been hugely impacted by the crisis as well as the drought. The CFSAM 2018 reports that at 1.2 million metric tonnes, wheat production in 2017/2018 winter season is the lowest it has been in 30 years, also lower than the previous year at 1.8 million metric tonnes and only 30 percent of the pre-crisis average of 4.1 million metric tonnes. In addition, barley production at 390,000 metric tonnes is the lowest since 2008. Large areas of rainfed cereals failed due to an extended dry period early in the planting season, and irrigated cereal yields were reduced because of unseasonably late heavy rains and high temperatures (HNO, 2019). In 2019, food insecurity remains high in Northwest Syria. In Idleb governorate, the food security severity ranking qualifies as major in 4 out of the 5 districts (Idleb, Harim, Jisr Ash-Shugur, Ariha), and moderate in the remaining one (Al Ma’ra). Out of the 2,379,650 individuals living in Idleb governorate in August 2018, 638,377 were considered to be food insecure, and 472,913 were considered to be at risk of becoming so. 40% of the population is represented by internally displaced persons (IDPs),(HNO PIN Severity 2019).

Implementation of activities

The proposed intervention aims at improving the food security and agricultural livelihood Syrian vulnerable households through a multi-layered approach: Emergency food security assistance to address the immediate food security needs of vulnerable households through the most appropriate modalitiesACTED will address the acute needs of food insecure households, including recently displaced ones, vulnerable host communities or returnees’ households, households who did not receive any or enough food assistance in the previous month, and with a low income. The specific modality (in-kind, voucher or cash) will be decided based on results of market assessments. This will include:• Distribution of one-off Ready to Eat Food Ratios (RTERs) to cover the needs of a 6-members’ households for one month (for households who do not have access to cooking facilities)• Distribution of in-kind food rations for three continuous months (for households who do not have access to market)• Distribution of food commodity vouchers for three months, where beneficiaries have access to markets. Support to small-scale food production for vulnerable households ACTED will provide support to vulnerable households for whom farming is not a full-time profession, but who own a small piece of land suitable for cultivating vegetables. The assistance provided will aim at increasing their food security while encouraging them to start a self-sufficient food production. This will be achieved through:• Distribution of in-kind home gardens kits (one per each agricultural season) to vulnerable households who have access to agricultural land or a backyard garden of a minimum of 20 square meters, have access to water for irrigation and who have a low income. Each kit will contain seeds, seedlings and gardening tools. Beneficiaries will also take part in training on kitchen gardening techniques, climate-smart agriculture, planting, nutrition practices.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Perception of discrimination when an activity targets a specific group of people, resulting in the exclusion of otherLikelihood: Medium – Impact: High Mitigation measures: All selection criteria are explained to the local authorities and local communities during sensitisation session. ACTED staff take time to respond to any question or concern about the selection criteria and selection process throughout the project implementation. ACTED clearly communicates on its humanitarian values and principle of neutrality and will ensure that targeted communities have access to the Complaints and Response Mechanisms run by ACTED inside Syria (notably through Whatsapp and on-site feedback to ACTED staff Finally, ACTED developed a strong acceptance throughout its previous intervention in Northeast Syria and works closely with local authorities and obtains all necessary authorizations needed to conduct the activities. Risk: Low participation of women in the projectLikelihood: Medium – Impact: HighMitigation measures: The proposed intervention has been designed and will be carried out in accordance with ACTED gender strategy. All along the project implementation, ACTED team will ensure the dignity and safety of women is at all-time respected and ACTED will strongly encourage the inclusion of women in the activities. First, women will be particularly encouraged to participate in small-scale food production activities. Experience from a similar intervention currently being carried out within another project has showed that while most of beneficiaries registered for the distribution of kitchen garden are men, women in their households are then the ones working in the garden, planting and cultivating the seeds. For all activities, the inclusion of vulnerability criteria will enable to target female-headed households where they wish to participate in this project and benefit from an activity which can be done from their home, particularly when their status of single mothers does not allow them to work in other types of income-generating opportunities. Risk: Lack of meaning full access for the most vulnerable groups Likelihood: Medium – Impact: HighMitigation measures: The special needs of women and girls, elderly and people living with disabilities will be taken into account throughout the implementation of the activities, and in particular during the phase of beneficiary selection, conducted by Programme Team and ACTED AMEU. ACTED will ensure that distribution and training sites are safe, easily accessible. During distribution, separate lines will be arranged for women. In addition, the particular necessities of pregnant and lactating women, female head of household, and elderly or disabled women will be taken into account, and should they be unable to reach the distribution sites, they will be able to obtain the kits by proxy. Risk: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuseLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measures: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as well as the work of the contractors, and to relay any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issue. ACTED has a strong PSEA policy which stresses the importance of prevention but also has a mechanism in place for reporting and investigation.
