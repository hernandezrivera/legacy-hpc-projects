Project Summary

In response to the growing humanitarian crisis in Lakes and Eastern Equatoria States, AVSI Foundation is proposing the following interventions to expand lifesaving GBV, protection and child protection services to vulnerable populations in Lakes and Eastern Equatoria States. The complexity of the operating environment requires multifaceted approaches that are tailored to each location including:
1. Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in Eastern Equatoria State and Lakes State. This will be done through Psychosocial support and access to documentation
2. AVSI will Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach areas in Kapoeta South and Kapoeta North Counties of Eastern Equatoria State then Cueibet and Rumbek East Counties of Lakes States this will be done through peace and development agencies existing on the ground.
3. Prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience which will be done through integrated approach developed to harmonize key messaging that is also informed by the findings from the protection monitoring analysis
4. Implement protection assessment and monitoring to inform protection and overall humanitarian response. This will be done to Strengthen coordination and information sharing among humanitarian actors.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains critical with an estimated 8.5 million people of whom 53% are children in need of humanitarian assistance, including protection from violence, abuse, exploitation, and violations of their human rights (HNO, 2022). The 2022 Humanitarian Need Overview projects 5.6 million women, girls, men, and boys in the country as facing protection risks and other violations with children amounting to 58%. Vulnerable people such as women, children, elderly, youth, and persons with disabilities are among the most at-risk groups in the affected communities. People who are less privilege to access essential services are vulnerable to various forms of violence, including child abuses, SGBV, human rights violations, displacement and erosion of coping mechanisms and social cohesion. According to the 2022 HNO, grave violations against children’s rights remain a critical concern in South Sudan of which this situation has been exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic, protracted displacement, and limited access to basic services which continues to significantly affect the physical, mental, and social well-being of children, adolescents, and caregiver. Boys and girls continue to endure multiple protection risks and rights violations, including family separation, widespread recruitment, and use of children by armed forces and groups, psychosocial distress, GBV and exploitation. Violence against children and adolescents, including grave violations, remains a major concern in communities in South Sudan. The Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting on grave child rights violations verified 196 grave violations including the recruitment/use of 129 children (five girls) in 2021, compared to 62 children (one girl) in 2020, presenting a surge in recruitment despite the signing of a Comprehensive Action Plan by the Government to put an end to the six grave child right violations. In addition, according to the GBVIMS, of the 6,237 GBV incidents reported between January and September 2021, 28 per cent of the cases are child survivors under the age of 18. It is worth noting that the percentage of child survivors increased from 21 per cent in 2020 to 28 per cent in 2021. While progress has been made in the provision of psychosocial support to children and their caregivers, the experience of any humanitarian crisis increases the vulnerability of children and exerts a heavy toll on their mental health and psychosocial well-being. This situation has been worsened by the limited availability of mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services and the low level of core child protection services, including family tracing and reunification, case management, and reintegration services for children affected by armed forces and groups, leaving many children untreated and unable to access the needed services. The State of World’s Children Report (2021) On My Mind: Promoting, protecting, and caring for children’s mental health highlights a high burden of mental health needs and estimates that 13 per cent of adolescents aged 10-19 live with a diagnosed mental disorder. Similarly, the 2021 FSNMS+ qualitative findings indicate the presence of mental health and psychosocial concerns within communities and identify an urgent need for MHPSS and life-skills education.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concerning protection, AVSI Foundation will work collaboratively with other actors (UN agencies, NGOS, FBOs and CBOs) involved in child protection issues to safeguard the delivery of protection services in the schools and communities. AVSI will be accountable to the beneficiaries by enabling them to participate in the project at the planning, implementation, and evaluation phases. Moreover ,appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints will be set up.On regularly basis, AVSI conducts community engagement meetings such asawareness campaigns, innovative youth events that allows openparticipation/discussion over the quality of progress of the activity conducted.AVSI embrace a last-mile approach through fi eld-based personal and daily visitsto the fi eld and conducts impact analysis and post monitoring reviews, whichare analysed distinguishing relevant categories of people with the objective ofproviding detailed information about the targeting of the intervention andbenefi ciaries barriers to access services. Subsequent projects are designed toaddress such barrier and put in place a system of monitoring that puts specialemphasis on monitoring the targeting of those groups of benefi ciaries found toface particular barriers.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The capillary and continuous presence that AVSI has in the communities targeted by the intervention (AVSI has more than 80% of its staff, including senior positions outside Juba), and the extensive use of staff recruited locally among people recognized by the communities, ensures that such fruitful cooperation with project beneficiaries continues throughout the implementation of the project. All project’s beneficiaries (female and male IDPs and returnees’ children, CWDs and host communities’ children) will be considered as capable and active resources rather than as passive recipients who do not have a say in the project. Thanks to a very localized approach with AVSI staff regularly present and usually coming from the same community of project locations, accountability is pursued also directly through its staff that can act as bridge between top management and beneficiaries. Furthermore, considering the purposely field-oriented management team (officers and also project managers are field based), beneficiaries and affected population at large as constant possibility to interact and fi le complain to staff at different level. Yearly, AVSI conducts surveys which results are discussed through open meetings with the community, the county and payam officials and traditional leaders (Boma and sub Boma Chiefs). During these events, AVSI explains to the community its plans for the incoming years as a means to an informed discussion among community members and receive solid suggestions. AVSI extension officers, AAP committees before selecting a person/group/community as beneficiaries, establish a case specific dialog where AVSIs expectations are compared with the one from the beneficiaries in order to evaluate possible challenges/intervention failure chance. Beside the surveys, AVSI’s beneficiaries have access to information during participatory meetings which provided platform toaddress issues linked to power-relations whereby some strata of the population culturally marginalized can fi nd the opportunity to express themselves, include the view of beneficiaries in programming and allow intervention in case of issues reported thus increase transparency and accountability through awareness of beneficiaries on protection-related issues as well as project-design.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

AVSI Foundation will place protection and gender issues at the centre of theintervention by engaging directly with the IDPs, returnees and hostcommunities’ children and CWDs so that their needs, priorities, rights areaddressed within the scope of the project. AVSI Foundation will always createconducive and interactive environments that encourage them to express theirneeds, rights and priorities. Apart from the active involvement of the rightsholders in the project, AVSI Foundation will ensure that the duty bearers(Government, PTAs/SMCS, institutions and non-state actors) play an effectiverole to promote the rights of the CWDs and girls. Activities will be developedwith the active participation of women and parents in all stages of theimplementation process, to ensure that girls’ priority needs are taken intoconsideration and that WaSH infrastructures meet the right gender standards(IASC, 2015, Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions inHumanitarian Action: Reducing risk, promoting resilience and aiding recovery).Moreover, prevention of GBV (Sexual Exploitation) is pursued by displaying SEAcode of conducts at service access points (schools, health facilitates,distribution points and community). On the other hand, safety audits areconducted, and multiple locations selected to avoid far travel of benefi ciariesand reducing risks. Community members, staff and stakeholder’s capacity willbe strengthened through specifi c PSEA trainings.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

AVSI has in place a tested system of yearly multi-sector surveys that throughthe employment of more than 400 questions (including question on FSL,nutrition, GBV, hazards, protection, disabilities, education, gender inclusion, etc.),a large number of interviews (more than 400 HHs) capable of providing detailedreference at the subcounty (payam) level, an analysis centred on specifi cgroups (single mothers, families with members with disabilities, poor farmers,etc) and the participatory discussion of such fi ndings with the staff andrepresentatives of the communities ensures not only that projects are designedon the base of the specifi c needs of the different groups of the population, butalso that such knowledge is interiorized by AVSI staff and becomes the base ofan informed conversation and a common understanding with localcommunities, ensuring feedback from the most vulnerable within thecommunity. In the last 12 months AVSI has produced 4 of such surveys which,along with other research aimed at monitoring the results of AVSI’s projects, areaccessible on the “Assessment” section of the Humanitarian Response’swebsite. The questionnaire used for the Multi-Sector Survey can be accessed atthis webpage https://ee.humanitarianresponse.info/x/#Yztl. Beside thesesurveys, AVSI has a tested system of detailed post distribution Monitoringwhich is used to gather precise feedbacks over the targeting, timing,transparency and overall implementation of activities oriented at thecommunity level. During each fi eld visit, AVSI personnel is required to fi llapposite forms that include a section for open-ended feedbacks andcomplaints. Finally, the numerous permanent bases that AVSI has across thetargeted communities are equipped with mail boxes, suggestion boxes; focalpoint protection help desks for unanimous and isolated reporting of abuses orcomplaints.
