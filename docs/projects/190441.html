Project Summary

Approximately 18 million people need humanitarian assistance during the first half of 2022 due to multiple and concurrent shocks to food security in Ethiopia including back to back droughts, desert locust upsurge (worst in 25 years), the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID‑19) pandemic, and its socio-economic impacts, abnormally high food prices, and protracted conflict and insecurity. Due to the 2021 short rainy season, much of the arid and semi-arid zones of Ethiopia received less than 70 percent of average rainfall with a few areas experiencing one of the driest seasons on record. These poor rains, combined with the effects of the previous two below‑average rainy seasons (October–December 2020 and April–June 2021) have driven poor rangeland and water availability for pastoralists, with remote sensing data showing significant negative Normalized Difference Vegetation Index anomalies (measurement of vegetative greenness) and many livestock watering holes at “alert” or“near dry” stages. Food prices have risen sharply due to the combined effects of pre‑existing macroeconomic challenges that have been recently exacerbated by the economic impacts of the conflict in Tigray. Even though low in presence currently, the food security impacts of the desert locusts in the past two years have been significant in worst‑affected areas on food security. In Tigray, families are exhausting all remaining means to access food, with three quarters of the population reported to be using extreme coping strategies to survive. The level of food insecurity is expected to worsen in the coming months as remaining food stocks from the last harvest, which was half of the normal year production, get depleted and humanitarian assistance is not delivered at scale and on time due to lack of road access into Tigray to bring in supplies.
For the larger part, the poor rangeland and water availability for pastoralists, has resulted in deteriorating animal body conditions, increased disease outbreaks, poor livestock market value, livestock deaths, reduced milk yields, ultimately resulting in increased households’ food insecurity, and loss of assets. This effect on and decline in milk and meat production due to starvation and drought-induced diseases directly impacts nutrition, especially in children below five years of age and the elderly. This state of affairs exacerbates an already fragile food security situation across much of the dryland zones of the country. Moreover, due to the drought in the remaining parts of the country, FAO has noted that water rationing for both human and animals is already in place.
In line with FAO’s Rapid response and mitigation plan to avert a humanitarian catastrophe due to drought and conflict in the horn of Africa, FAO is proposing a humanitarian programme that will provide animal feed, treat and vaccinate animals against infectious diseases, and parasites, support food production, forage development, facilitate slaughter destocking and give cash to the most affected pastoral and agro-pastoral communities. The proposed interventions are meant to safeguard their livelihoods The specific objective is to improve food security and protect the assets of pastoralist, agro pastoralist and conflict affected communities in Ethiopia in the year 2022. The objectives will be attained through i) Providing feed for 900 000 core breeding and milking animals belonging to 450 000 households ii) Treating 4 million animals against infectious diseases and parasites iii) providing crop seeds and farm tools and training for 515,000 households, iv) Facilitating the destocking of 30 000 animals belonging to 30 000 households and distribution of meat to 60000 households and iv) Providing 65 000 families with cash to meet other household basic needs. The FAO proposed interventions in 2022 will support a total of 1,320,305 households (6,601,525 persons) selected from Oromia, Somali, SNNP, Afar, Amhara and Tigray regions.

1. - Projects activities

i. Enhance access to livestock feed for core breeding and milking animals FAO will provide 60 kgs of Multi-Nutrient Blocks (MNB) per animal for 90 days. For the livestock feed, FAO focused on MNB because it is composed of different nutrients, which makes the feed better quality than hay. The intervention will facilitate production of milk for children. Saving core breeding animals to some extend will safeguard the livelihoods of pastoralists and accelerate the restocking of animals. According to the LEGS guidelines the feed rations are 3.5 kgs total mixed ration (TMR) or 0.5kgs of Multi-Nutrient Blocks (MNB) per animal per day. The beneficiaries will also recieve an unconditional cash transfer making this intervention a cash plus. ii. Enhance access to animal health services improved and capacity of stakeholders to identify the most appropriate responses enhanced. This intervention intends to improve the capacity of woreda veterinary clinics to provide basic veterinary service (treatment and vaccination) to drought affected households. This would be through procurement and supply of veterinary inputs (drugs) and equipment for the veterinary clinics. In addition, the project will train Community Animal Health Workers and 1800 animal health technicians from the regions and support vaccination of 4,000,000 animals. iii. Destocking + protein supplementation to drought affected HHs This activity will benefit 30000 pastoralist HH from Somali, Oromia and Afar Regions. Around 30000 cattle with poor body condition (1 from each household) will be slaughtered and owners of the slaughtered animals would be partially compensated for the value of their animals. The meat from the slaughter destocking will be provided to poor households as source of animal protein supplement. Around 60 000 poor households will be provided with 2kgs of meat every week for 12 weeks in each cycle. iv. Provision of crop seeds and agricultural tools/implements i FAO will procure and distribute crop seed to 515000 of the most affected small holder farming households v. Cash transfer/Assistance (Non conditional) It is planned that 65 000 households will directly benefit from the project. Priority will be given to the poorest households facing acute food insecurity as a result of the drought in the region and other shocks. In addition, the project will prioritize female-headed households, families with malnourished children under five years out of these households. FAO will use Kobo for beneficiary registration and Excel to manage the master beneficiary list. All the necessary efforts will ensure that the distribution sites will be safe and accessible to vulnerable communities. The beneficiary selection criteria is clear on contextual and household level vulnerabilities of targeted groups to mitigate tensions and disputes during the targeting process.Beneficiaries of the cash support will also be supported with animal feed and animal health.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

Crisis-affected people are consulted at the project design stage to ascertain the livelihood challenges and priority needs emerging on the ground from the drought and conflict activities. They are also involved in the rapid food security and cash market assessment exercises. The selected interventions are in line with the top priorities identified by the affected communities, with the unconditional cash transfer activity included to cater to the non-pastoralist, non-agricultural or non-food needs of the households as identified by the affected population..FAO organizes inception workshops prior to programme implementation in all the target regions where the representatives of the crisis-affected population along with key resource persons, decision makers are invited to participate. During the workshops, FAO and its implementing partners explain the beneficiary selection criteria and facilitate the identification of beneficiary selection committees. Awareness is also created at the village level where farmers were informed of the veterinary drugs that will be used to treat their animals. FAO and the implementing partners also disseminate information about the programme deliverables, service dates and quantity/amount of entitlements to each of the selected households using materials such as posters, and village notice boards as well as fliers and brochures. The information materials are developed in the local languages. The affected population (through selection committees) are involved in the selection of beneficiaries, although FAO is responsible for the final verification and confirmation of potential beneficiaries. FAO and the implementing partners ensure that this process is participatory and community-based and that the views of women, girls and marginalized groups are adequately representedFAO also uses regional government extension and advisory services through the bureaus of agriculture and livestock to ensure that the awareness and input needs of the targeted populations were adequately met. In collaboration with respective government offices and implementing partners, FAO also sensitized beneficiary farmers on relevant guidelines, input technical specifications, handling, and the feeding regime for stock feed. The Government, where possible, works closely with an established network of Community Animal Health Workers who provided basic animal husbandry services and advice to livestock keepers. Crisis affected persons are also consulted during the post-distribution monitoring assessment.FAO establishes a complaints and feedback mechanism anchored within the community, FAO field offices and among implementing partners. A complaints committee is established within target communities and unanimously appointed by community members. Within this committee representatives of women, youth and elderly pastoralist-headed households selected from the crisis affected population. Beneficiaries are required to report their grievances to the village complaints committee. In the instance that a resolution cannot be reached, the grievance would be addressed by the implementing partner and eventually at the FAO field offices.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

As one of the operational activities, a call me back facility will be established, where beneficiaries will be able to request FAO officers to call them back if they want to register a complaint, give feedback on a service or receive support. Instead of setting up parallel structures, efforts will be made to integrate with existing grievance redress mechanisms, for example, the Productive Safety Net Programme where there are already focal persons appointed by the community to register and forward feedback to the implementing partners is a safe and confidential way. The call back facility will be complimentary to the already established mechanisms and will be managed by FAO staff in the affected regions. Through a participatory monitoring and evaluation approach, beneficiaries will be given the opportunity to participate in the identification of indicators and to evaluate the programme based on those indicators while checking that their feedback is well assimilated at all levels
