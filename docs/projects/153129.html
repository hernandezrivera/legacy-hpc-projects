Basic Info

Project Name

Provision of Primary and Accelerated Learning education program to conflict affected and out of school children and youth in Rokon, Lainya, Nimule and Magwi counties of Central and Eastern Equatoria State.

Start Date

01/01/2019

End Date

31/12/2019

Project Summary

the aims of this project include to increase access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education (formal and non-formal) for children and youth affected by emergencies, to improve the quality of education to ensure continuity of relevant education services for children and youth in emergencies and to strengthen the response capacity of communities and education actors to mitigate the impact of emergencies on children and youth. The project will target young people among both returnees and host community members in Eastern and Central Equatoria.

Needs

"South Sudan remains a protracted and complex crisis fueled by several intersecting and mutually reinforcing factors.1/3 South Sudanese have experienced internal or external displacement reaching 4 million in 2017 and many among these have experienced multiple episodes of displacement Of the 4 million displaced, over half (2.4 million) are under the age of 18, and 2.5 million are seeking refuge in neighboring countries. Conflict, displacement, drought and a deteriorating economy have generated a food crisis. In Feb 2018, the UN declared that 5.1 million people (almost half the population) were severely food insecure. Gender inequalities play a significant role in the crisis. Entrenched socio-cultural norms and practices put women and girls at greater risk of food insecurity than men and boys .52% of girls are married before their 18th birthday.The UN estimates that almost 2 million women and girls throughout South Sudan are at risk of GBV in 2018. South Sudan ranked 169th out of 188 countries on UN HDI 2015 & UN GDI 2015-Global gender Development Index. Currently, 51% of the population is under the age of 18, and two-thirds under the age of 30 In 2018 alone, 1.7 million South Sudanese and 158,173 refugee children (3-18 years old) will need support to access education. The education crisis risks fueling new conflicts as more than 19,000 children are already recruited into armed groups. Many teachers, particularly in rural areas, have been displaced due to the conflict, causing a shortage of qualified teachers (this exacerbates school closures and creates a lack of faith in quality of the schooling system). In some instances, community volunteers have been recruited to fulfill the role of teachers but with little pay incentives and often the community view them as poorly trained and not having the skills required to provide a quality education. A number of schools destroyed or closed due to the ongoing conflict This intervention will support education of especially girls through community mobilization campaigns like the go-back to schools to support education, especially girls’ and protection in schools centres from attacks and settlements (focus on community leaders and key gatekeepers). The project will also support peer sessions and life skills training (SRHR, GE, GBV, resiliency, peace-building, conflict resolution) with especially adolescent girls in schools. The project will also provide school supplies to schools, teachers and students with the view to maintaining girls’ child in the school. Construct/rehabilitate/refurbish gender-responsive WASH facilities and latrines in the schools. Provide training to teachers in order to support girls’ school enrollment rates, and to prevent and reduce violence against girls and boys in schools (gender-responsive pedagogy teacher training and child protection). Provide training of teachers on quality education delivery including PSS, code of conduct, security training, risk reduction, resilience, peace-building, and conflict sensitivity. Support the provision of literacy and life-skills courses to teachers, particularly female teachers. Rehabilitate classrooms and provide furniture, where needed or where have been destroyed. The program will also Support alternative learning programs, including accelerated learning and community girls’ schools and temporary learning spaces, for OOSC, over-age and young mothers. Support school clubs as venues for children to play, share ideas, and voice their opinions. Strengthen school governance (SMCs, PTAs, children’s clubs, girls’ clubs)

Activities

1. Establish and rehabilitate temporary learning spaces and classrooms2. Establish and rehabilitate gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion3. Provide school-aged children with fortified school meals/take home ration4. Provide emergency-affected children with quality education and recreational material5. Provide young children with ECD kits6. Equip teachers with EiE skills to provide an improved teaching and learning experience for emergency-affected children.7. Undertake training for Educators, community members and authorities on psycho social support8. Equip SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management and governance

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Information provision is the essence of Education. information will be provided through formal instructional learning, through the provision of learning materials and literature and there are many opportunities for beneficiaries to cross learn from one another in both the formal and informal school setting. Key awareness messages and sessions will be shared with beneficiaries in schools as well as in the communities. This will help them to understand the importance of education and ensuring conducive learning environment.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The Education curriculum developed by the Ministry of Education and Social Welfare is tailored for age groups and academic level. Overall it is geared towards gender segregated context specific analysis. This is meant to address challenges that hindered girls access to education.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

The project shall take into consideration integration of projection in running education activities. This has to be included in teachers professional development, planning and classroom management aspect .

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The consultation community leaders, location authorities and children on education and protection challenges and needs. Lot of activities endeavor to be implemented are basically to addressed the needs of targeted communities and age group as well.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Community feedback mechanism shall be considered in schools targeted to enable schools and education stakeholders including boys and girls share their concerns that can lead to creation of conducive environment for girls and boys in schools. FGD and suggestion boxes are the ways among other that could be considered in getting the views and complains.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The targeted communities and schools will be engaged to promote effective participation in the management of schools as well in addressing issues of girls, boys and teachers in schools. They will also be engaged in conducting awareness on the importance of education for children, especially girls. The community shall also be conducted in reviewing the progress through quarterly reflection meetings.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Plan International conducts annual needs assessment to establish the education gaps among different age groups including adolescents and over age children. rapid needs assessments are also conducted to establish the education needs of children affected by new shocks/ displacements/ epidemics and among new returnees.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

the project intends to identify the barriers that affect especially girls and young women from accessing education, and then work with the targeted beneficiaries to develop their capacity to reduce those barriers, for example lack of school fees, learning materials etc, negative attitude to education, fear of SGBV

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

the projects addresses some protection threats and inequalities through the provision of school meals to enable all children to attend school without the risk of hunger, providing MHM for girls to ensure they dont miss school unnecessarily. school based protection clubs also provide safety information to young people to be able to identify potential abuse as well as the available reporting and/ referral pathways for speedy resolution.
