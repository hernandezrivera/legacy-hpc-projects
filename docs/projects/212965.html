Project Summary

Through this project, Silk Road aims to address people's needs by offering seasonal supplemental non-food items (NFIs) and shelter for affected beneficiaries (Host, returnees, and IDPs), regardless of whether they were tenants or not. The actual needs of the targeted individuals will be determined and confirmed by our field team, and Silk Road will supply information and data about current vulnerabilities pertaining to damage level, negative coping strategies, and needs for NFIs. While the other side of the project, which is focused on NFIs, will address the housing and most pressing material needs of populations affected by conflict, particularly IDPs are protected from seasonally and adverse weather as well as their core and essential non-food item needs, the Shelter side of the project will assist in sustainably repairing or rehabilitating housing for owners, tenants, returnees, and host families. The project's first phase aims to target 600 shelter units by offering housing rehabilitation services to IDPs and returnees, as well as affected beneficiaries who may be tenants or owners, based on the actual needs of the targeted population, which will be determined and confirmed by our field team after identifying communities that actually require shelter assistance. Silk Road will also provide information and data about current vulnerabilities related to shelter, negative coping mechanisms, and damage levels.At least 20% of female- and child-headed households will be the focus of this project's side (child-headed households refer to the loss of supporting adults, while female-headed households indicate the absence of a male supporter; both scenarios are categorized as vulnerable). In addition, the provision of shelter rehabilitation services can strengthen physical protection, enhance the shelter and living conditions for the targeted population, and satisfy one of the fundamental survival needs of women, girls, boys, and men.
The project's second phase attempts to take care of the needs of NFIs that are seasonal, enhance the living circumstances of vulnerable IDPs, and provide housing for families that find it difficult to survive the harsh winters. Silk Road approach in the implementation of this part depends on accuracy and efficiency where we will conduct the vulnerability assessment for all registered /potential beneficiaries (Lists come from secondary data we have, local councils, other like active actors on the ground) to identify the most vulnerable in need of seasonal NFIs support as follows:
1- Provision of winter NFIs and winter clothing for 1870 families. Silk Road will allocate $40 per person for purchasing winter non-food items that include, but are not limited to:
Mattress / Sleeping mats, Blanket, Carpet, Plastic Sheet, Boots, Gloves, Hat, Jacket, Jumper / Wool Sweater, Socks, Trousers, Scarf and Thermal Underwear. This means that not all families will receive the same amount (Silk Road will consider the cluster and Cash working group guidelines).
The vouchers will be loaded with 50% of the total amount allocated to the family at the end of November while the rest amount will be loaded after 20-25 days as redemption will be opened for targeted beneficiaries for 20 days after the loading each time. The purpose of this system is to enable the monitoring team to verify that the amounts are being utilized according to what is planned for purchasing winter items.
The proposed activities will ensure that they do not create additional risks for the affected population but will provide the best solutions to the suffering population in the targeted areas, also Silk Road will ensure that there is no health impact in this S-NFI approach on the targeted population beside we will adhere to internationally recognized standards and best practices, ensuring that services are provided in line with the Do No Harm framework, and humanitarian.
