Project Summary

The project is targeting 25,477 individuals of 4,246 households including vulnerable children, women, and men from IDPs and host communities in Nasir County (Upper Nile State) and Pigi’ County (Jonglei State) who are living with chronic emergency WASH needs and impending cholera outbreaks. The planned WASH activities will as well support cholera prevention and management interventions by creating synergies through preventing WASH related diseases aimed at reducing morbidity and mortality rates through provision of clean, safe and adequate water, dignified sanitation services, Cholera kits as well as hygiene promotion messaging among the affected population in Nasir and Pigi Counties. This project targets IDPs, returnees and host communities.
The project activities and targets will include; refresher training of 50 hygiene promoters (30 females and 20 males) and 18 hygiene campaigns across the two Counties and benefiting 3,000 men, 3,400 women, 10,590 boys and 8,487 girls through participatory hygiene promotion, training of 30 water management committee members benefiting 150 people (100 females and 50 males), distribution of WASH NFIs benefiting 4,246hh or 25,477 people (3,000 men, 3,400 women, 10,590 boys, and 8,487 girls) and distribution of menstrual hygiene products benefiting 3,566 girls and women (30% of female population). The project will further promote sanitation through construction or installation of 45 emergency latrine structures with 4 stances each (amounting to 180 stances) aimed at promoting proper human excreta disposal in public places at Boma level. The project intends to benefit 25,477 people, majority of whom are women and children.
This project comes at a time when Nasir and Pigi counties have received high influx of IDPs due to displacements resulting from the recurring conflicts in other parts of Jonglei State and mostly from Akobo, Nyirol and others as returnees from other Counties hosting them as IDPs. The situation in the IDP sites has negatively impacted on the already limited basic WASH services that are shared with the host communities in the target areas. Therefore, this project will address the needs of the most vulnerable women, men, boys and girls affected by the conflict and lacking sufficient basic WASH services.
Among others, the deliverable for the project will include:
• Vulnerable and affected populations have access to safe and sufficient water (based on Sphere standards), provided through the active involvement of local communities.
• Vulnerable and affected populations have knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases.
• Vulnerable and affected populations enabled to access adequate, safe, secure, and dignified and gender segregated sanitation services (based on Sphere standards)
• Availability of trained hygiene promoters, volunteers and local community on good WASH practices.

Needs

In the recent past, South Sudan has experienced political turbulence leading to insecurity and inter-communal conflicts. This has resulted in loss of lives, destruction of property and population displacements. It has also increased levels of humanitarian catastrophes particularly in Jonglei and Upper Nile states with Nasir, Ulang (Upper Nile) and Pigi (Jonglei) falling among the most affected counties. Jonglei State is among the three states where people suffered heavily from indiscriminate violence against civilians since the December 2013 and July 2016 national conflict that disrupted peace and induced major humanitarian consequences in South Sudan. On October 19, 2017, U.S. Chargé d’Affaires, a.i., Michael K. Morrow declared a disaster in South Sudan for FY 2018 due to ongoing violent conflict, population displacement, restricted humanitarian access, and disruption of trade, markets, and cultivation activities, all of which have significantly exacerbated food insecurity, scarcity of WASH facilities and humanitarian needs (South Sudan - Crisis fact sheet #6, fiscal year (fy) 2018 april 9, 2018. The recurrent intercommunal fights in counties neighboring Nasir in between January and August 2018 displaced an estimated 54,576 people (2019 HNO_People in Need Cluster Update_WASH VO3 new OCHA numbers). The displaced populace is living in Nasir County (Upper Nile). While in Pigi County, estimates of 12,527 individuals are living in displacement across the payams of Atar-3, Dhiel, Kuarwai, and Kolonyang. This population displacement is reported to have occurred due to the SPLA-IG and Murle attacks on civilians in Jonglei State (Walgak in Akobo West and Bung in Akobo Central) where homes were looted and burnt, property destroyed, people killed, children abducted and cases of sexual violence were reported. The majority of people displaced were women, children and the elderly, including persons with special needs. The humanitarian needs of the affected IDPs have continued to escalate each day (According to the WFP Situation Report #22620th August 2018) on South Sudan Crisis. 67,273 most vulnerable people; 54,746 in Nasir and 12,527 in Canal/Pigi Counties of Jonglei and Upper Nile States (South Sudan) are in urgent need of WASH services. Malnutrition and food insecurity make communities more prone to water-related diseases, especially children under 5 yrs. In 2017, cholera epidemic spread to new areas that were previously not affected by the outbreaks (Per UNICEF In-depth report on ‘Epidemiological study of cholera hotspots in South Sudan, April 2018), placing even more pressure on already vulnerable communities. In the IDP hosting areas (Nasir and Canal/Pigi), most of the existing water infrastructures are destroyed and unavailable in some areas, making the situation even more dare. In addition, leaving both IDPs and their host with no other option than drinking unsafe water

Activities

1) Training 12 WASH staff on protection and GBV guidelines2) Conduct 14 (8 Nasir & 6 Pigi) community consultative meetings3) Train 30 Water management Committees of 150 members (100 females and 50 males) to support the maintenance and fencing of 25 water sources4) Conduct training of 50 hygiene promoters (30 females & 20 males) with equal and inclusive participation to conduct house-to-house hygiene promotion and education5) Train 30 girls and 20 boys on hygiene promotion6) Conduct social mobilization of 25,477 individuals (13590 male & 11,887 female) for hygiene education and provision of cholera kits..7) Training 32 health workers (18 in Nasir & 14 in Pigi) in health facilities on WASH minimum standards (0.5ml/g chlorine safe drinking water, 0.05% chlorine hand washing, safe sanitation and waste management)8) Conduct routine joint WASH epidemiological investigations on cholera, AWD, typhoid, EVD and HEV related occurrence9) Equip 18 community social structures (Schools, churches, mosques, court halls, food distribution points etc) 10 in Nasir & 8 in Pigi with WASH minimum package (Safe drinking water, hand washing facilities with soap and safe sanitation)10) Conduct (Through female staff) 16 focused group discussions with women and girls on WASH related GBV (8 in Nasir & 6 in Pigi)11) Provide 3566 women and girls (2140 in Nasir & 1426 in Pigi) with menstrual age with MHM kits

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

This project was design in consultation with community. The beneficiaries will be briefed through project inception meetings before project kickoff.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

WASH is a cross-cutting issue and in this case, messages are context specific and CAO encourages promotion of women as equal partners through policy strengthening, rights education, family values education and counseling. Therefore, this project will target 50% male headed households (Men and boys) and 50% female headed households (Women and girls).

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project highlights all needs of the affected population and therefore the activities spelt out will address the links between protection and GBV risks. CAO recognizes the community engagement and therefore will ensure participation of community members in activities at community level hence championing community protection mechanisms. This will be done through training of community based existing structures such as schools, churches, boreholes on the protection principles.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Group discussion and meeting were used as method of gathering information on the design of the project. CAO will ensure meaningful and safe women and girls’ participation during assessments to ascertain that its interventions are context appropriate. Speaking to local people or colleagues with in-depth knowledge on women and girl’s problems is an important criterion to understand sensitive and politicized issues regarding women and girls’ decision-making power in regards to risks associated with WASH.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The confidential complain mechanism will be establish and all the issues that may arise will be addressed without discrimination. At every population catchment point during hygiene promotion campaigns, CAO will position a complain desk and box in a visible area and place a caseworker from its protection unit to receive complains. Complains received will then go through the case management process in a bid to generate appropriate solutions and feedback.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

CAO will work closely with local leaders and authorities to create community awareness on the targeting and selection criteria. It will also engage some host community and refugee members during the identification of project beneficiaries in a bid to build a strong relationship between refugee or IDPs and their host community members. The monitoring team will give the priority to the community who are also the beneficiaries.All age group and gender will be considered during the monitoring.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

CAO will employ many approaches to promote beneficiary engagement and are diverse ranging from informally seeking out beneficiary views for needs assessments, to far more formal participation in project reviews. Many other methods exist, of course, such as open house policies for all beneficiaries to attend any meeting of the organization; community meetings and social events where the beneficiaries can comment on their safety while engaging on the project. More complex approaches such as social safety on beneficiary shall be will also be used.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The dignity of the beneficiary will be measured to ensure no discrimination. Treating beneficiaries and their host members with respect and dignity is essential to excellent humanitarian response; yet, unlike other elements of responses, respect and dignity are difficult to define, let alone measure. In an effort to promote beneficiaries being treated with respect and dignity, CAO has a code of conduct that allows staff to treat beneficiaries with respect and dignity.
