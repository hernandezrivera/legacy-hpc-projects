Project Summary

Blumont will continue to implement food security inventions supporting over 215,000 Syrians living in camps and in host communities, including provide daily bread to all camp and informal settlement populations, supplementary food vouchers to provide fresh foods to WFP standard food basket recipients, and early recovery activities including agricultural support for smallholder farmers and households.

4 - Needs

In December 2021, the nationwide average price of the WFP food basket reached SYP220,112 ($88) representing the highest recorded level since 2013. The highest month over month increase was reported in Al-Hasakeh, where the price reached SYP198,044 ($79). According to WFP, almost half of all HHsreported inadequate food consumption in December, a 10% increase from the year before. As the SYP continues to depreciate, rising food costs are straining HH purchasing power. This forces families to adopt negative coping strategies such as buying less expensive food, eating fewer meals per day, prioritizing child food consumption needs, and engaging in child labor and early marriage, while restricting adult food consumption. In 2021, NES experienced some of the lowest levels of rainfall the region has seen, particularly in high-crop producing areas of Al-Hasakeh. Wheat production was estimated at around 1.05 million tons, representing a 63% decline compared to 2020 and about 25% of the pre-crisis average . Wheat production for 2021 is thought to be one of the lowest levels recorded in Syria over the past 50 years. The shortfall of wheat production is a result of lack of rainfall during the 2020/21 agricultural season, in addition to several heatwaves, as well as the high price of fuel and high-quality agricultural inputs. Exacerbating these concerns is the GOS’ continued suspension of flour (200–300 MT daily) to Al-Hasakeh, which is likely to get worse given last year’s wheat harvest.

5 - Implementation of activities

1) Agricultural Support: Smallholder farmer agricultural inputs (seeds, fertilizer, harvesting bags) and pre and post harvesting training. 2) Homestead Vegetable Gardens: Provision of agricultural training and HVG kits that include locally procured, in-kind inputs (seeds and toolkit), and a drip irrigation system. 3) Bakery Support: Local and/or regionally procured wheat flour and yeast to local bakeries. 4) Bread Distribution: Bakeries will produce bread for sale at subsidized DOC prices to an estimated 80,000 local host community population at pre-identified distribution points. Blumont will buy bread from the bakeries and distribute it free of charge to approximately 135,000 people in four formal camps (Al-Hol, Mahmoudly, Areesha and Newroz), five informal camps (Washokani, Al-Tuwayhinah, Tel al-Samen, Abu Khashab and Serkaniye), and 68 CCs in Al-Hasakah and Ar-Raqqa. 5) Supplementary Food Vouchers: To supplement standard WFP food baskets, supplementary food vouchers will be provided to food insecure HHs for at least eight months to purchase fresh foods.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Blumont will continue to work with local communities, LAs, and INGOs in target locations to mitigate protection risks to beneficiaries and any unintended consequences of program activities. Blumont’s field staff will consult with LAs and community leaders (including sharing beneficiary criteria) for assistance in communicating program activities, beneficiary selection criteria, and conflict resolution. Blumont’s M&E team will also help ensure distributions do not create inter-community conflict and do not take place in areas of armed conflict. For bread distributions, Blumont will mitigate intra-community conflicts by making bread accessible to all participants. Supplemental food vouchers will also target food-insecure host community members. Vulnerable female, child, PwD, and elderly-headed HHs will receive selection priority, and bread distribution points will be located near underserved and hard-to-reach areas to ensure equitable coverage and reduce protection risks for vulnerable HHs. Camp distributions will be organized to ensure that lines for women, children, PwDs, and elderly-headed HHs are separate. The agricultural activities involve provision of in-kind inputs specifically to reduce protection risks or mobility challenges for such HHs and to promote livelihoods for women and other vulnerable groups. Trainings for HVG and smallholder farmers will be gender-segregated as needed, and typically Blumont’s female staff will lead or support assessments for FHHs.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Blumont understands and accepts the responsibility to help ensure SEA does not take place through the proposed project. Blumont has a dedicated PSEA strategy which covers the following elements: recruitment policy; induction and training; performance management; whistleblowing; discipline and grievance/complaints; partnership agreements; policy implementation, monitoring and review; and promotes an organizational culture concerned about these issues coupled with top-down leadership from its Board of Directors and Executive Management Team. Blumont also sensitizes vendors on its Code of Conduct and its intolerance for gender discrimination and sexual harassment or abuse.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Blumont has identified the following risks, assumptions and mitigating measures: 1) COVID-19: The pandemic continues into its third year and is still a threat to all staff and program beneficiaries, especially those still unvaccinated. Blumont will continue to monitor developments in relation to COVID-19 through advice being published by WHO and the U.S. Government. Blumont staff wear gloves and masks, use hand sanitizer frequently and practice physical distancing by maintaining two-meter distance between themselves and camp residents. 2) Civil unrest: Blumont continuously monitors for tensions within the camps to address with relevant stakeholders, monitors national news, media and social media, and maintains low profile and avoid areas of demonstration or protest. 3) Physical Security & Crime: Program staff or objectives may be impacted through physical violence and theft of project materials. Blumont’s security team continuously monitors the on-the-ground context and Blumont Global Safety & Security Policy and Security SOPs are in place.
