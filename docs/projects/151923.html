Project Summary

Under the Rapid Response Mechanism for WASH the Project will support the distribution of food assistance by WFP; more than 5000 families will be given food rations. We will establish the distribution sites, handle all the in-kind food from off loading to distribution and verification of beneficiaries. The total estimated number of people getting food asstiance will be 35,395. Total of 365,000 will be targeted under Under the WASH emergency response for affected population by ongoing insurgency between Nigerian army and non government armed group, disease outbreak and any other natural disasters. Improved access to water supply will be provided through water trucking, quick repairs and rehabilitation of water points, construction of water points , training of WASH committees and water quality monitoring. The access to sanitation will be provided through construction of emergency latrines, provision of solid waste management kits & pits and desludging of existing latrines. emergency hygiene promotion will conducted during the hygiene kits distribution, house to house visit and through training volunteers. Also, cholera task force will be trained and prepared for any cholera outbreaks together with project team members.

Needs

Nine years of conflict between the Nigerian military and non-state armed groups in northeast Nigeria has taken a heavy toll on the population. Approximately 1,926,784 individuals (364,635 households) remain displaced in North-East Nigeria and the ongoing conflict is the primary reason (94%) for displacement. Borno state, is the most affected and hosts the highest number of IDPs (1,441,635) and in Yobe State there are 137,588 IDPs (IOM DTM Round 24, 2018). As per the IOM DTM Round 24, August 2018, 54% percent of IDPs needing assistance are female, 27% are children under five, 81% are children plus women. The host communities support 91% of the IDPs, placing burdens on households and existing infrastructure that are already stressed. According to the Nigeria Center for Disease Control (October 2018) a total of 39,540 suspected cases of cholera, including 804 deaths with CFR: (2.05%) had been reported around 20 states, including Yobe (1,905) and Borno (5,465) states. Within 60% of the displacement sites surveyed during the IOM DTM in August 2018, the average amount of water per person per day was 10 /15 liters and in 14 % of sites IDPs had access to an average 5/10 liters per person per day.. Due to overcrowding in camps and host communities, enormous pressure is placed on water sources and sanitation facilities resulting in negative coping strategies such as Open Defecation and use of unhygienic water sources which has resulted in cholera outbreaks.
