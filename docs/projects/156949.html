Project Summary

The objective of the project is considered part of humanitarian life-sustaining assistance, by supporting income generating activities including vocational training to 180 households who are part of 2.5 million individuals are thought to be at risk of food insecurity, through providing access to regular employment through the work opportunities (cash-for-work) for 150 slaughterhouses workers, providing vocational and skills training for the slaughterhouses workers, and rehabilitating 30 chicken slaughterhouses for improving local poultry value chain around Aleppo and Idleb governorates; to improve the overall food security and livelihoods situation of rural and peri-urban Syrian agro-pastoralists, in collaboration with local governance institutions, so that they can reduce risks, adapt to the context, and recover in a sustainable way from the impact of the conflict. The Action includes enhanced access to inputs, and better market functionality in collaboration with local governance institutions (where possible and appropriate).
It is vital to restore and create livelihood opportunities for households to reduce use of negative coping mechanism in order to meet their daily needs. These objectives aim to restore and strengthen productive assets and create livelihood opportunities for the targeted populations, thus contributing to the overall food production and subsequently to food availability in Syria.
This project objective is aligned with the FSL sector objectives (2&3) and relates to strengthening communities’ productive capacity. Community support will aim to restore and rehabilitate the chicken slaughterhouses as key community structures, train casual workers to maintain the chicken slaughterhouses as part of community assets. In total, this objective will target directly 180 households (30 slaughterhouses owners and 150 casual workers’ households), which will indirectly benefit the whole catchment population in the target locations from these activities.
The project will directly target 30 slaughterhouses in NW Syria where the market is facing shortage of chicken meat availability and high prices.
In addition, improvements to household income for the targeted poultry farmers is expected to support local traders by increasing the purchasing power of targeted households for essential goods as well as increasing the demand for livelihoods inputs in the future. Temporary labour opportunities will involve the completion of rehabilitation work in the SH and market. It is anticipated that with the reinvigoration of poultry farmers’ livelihoods there will be continued demand for high hygiene quality of the chicken meat in the area, beyond the project. In addition, supporting those slaughterhouses owners as small/medium businesses and market actors to re-start or expand their activities will contribute to business development, generate income and enable them to sustain their operations, contributing to the functioning of the local market. Capacity building and training of local stakeholders in improved and chicken meat production best practices as well as the involvement of local councils and extension workers throughout project activities will ensure that knowledge and capacity gained will remain within the community with a view to building sustainability at household and community level.

Needs

Eight years into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity which in turn resulted in limited physical and financial access to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous depletion of livelihoods assets of the most vulnerable populations. In light of the severe impact of the crisis, unemployment has risen to 55 percent. A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 80 percent of an unskilled laborer’s monthly salary and 50-80 percent of a public service employee’s monthly salary thus demonstrating the existence of “working poor” in Syria. In some areas, females’ salaries working as agricultural labor are lower than males’ earnings (CFSAM). Child labor and early dropouts of school continues to be a major coping strategy of farmers and displaced households. Lack of employment, resulting in low purchasing power, is the main economic challenge faced by the majority of households. IDPs reported working multiple jobs and resorting to child labor as commonly employed coping strategies to meet their needs. Women have become the bread winners for many families where men are not present. Given the vast extent of the destruction in both urban and rural areas, major investments will be required to rebuild livelihoods in the coming years (CFSAM). As per the HNO 2019, livestock production is further constrained due to reduction of livestock number as compared to last comprehensive livestock census in 2010. According to the Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reform, poultry numbers fell by 55 percent during the first three years of the crisis. The main challenges continue to be high fodder prices and insufficient coverage of veterinary services.PRE-crisis: Chicken meat has been developed following the decision of the Syria authority to developed national production capacity to achieve self-sufficiency; meanwhile opening agro food sector to private investment allowed the establishment of large poultry meat production companies. Production of chicken transferred from open tradition activities with low productivities and quality to modern activities with high productivity and become very important for the national economy. Slaughterhouse normally slaughter quantities that required in the market, but sometime they keep extra chicken in freezing stores when price were low. There was a big difference between producer prices and trader and consumer prices. This clearly appears in the market chain analysis since a big marginal profit was allocated to slaughterhouses holders and retailers while producer get limited profit and some time they lose. The barging about the price was usually not completed at the day of selling, because HH preferred not to fix the price until they check the market next day. In Syria were two kind of slaughterhouse: manual slaughterhouse and mechanical slaughterhouse. The manual slaughterhouse now in a very low hygiene as the result of missing the necessary law and license for meat condition production as well as very high price of the mechanical slaughterhouse. SR has already rehabilitated and supported 16 slaughterhouses in Idleb and Aleppo in 2018, still the market needs more due to the highly increased demand. Because of the hostilities, largescale displacement of people within Syria continues; and as part of local agreements, which largely occurred in areas that were previously classified by the UN as either besieged or hard-to-reach; resulting in 1,470,523 IDPs in the targeted districts classified to be in major and severe conditions of food insecurity, including those living in last resort sites and collective centers, in addition to overburdened host communities.

Implementation of activities

A1.1.1 Community mobilization to raise awareness on the project and planned interventions and generate local ownership and buy-in: Local stakeholders, e.g. local councils and representatives from the agricultural directorate, will be informed of the planned interventions, objectives and timeframes, as well as involved in the identification of local veterinarians and extension workers to participate in implementation.A1.1.2 Rapid need assessment in target areas to finalize inputs, market condition, SH input vendors: Specific packages of assistance to be provided to targeted households in relation to SH including identification of suitable local markets will be determined with local stakeholders.A1.1.3 Finalization of beneficiary targeting criteria with local councils and beneficiary selection: Syria Relief will work closely with local councils to facilitate the selection process of beneficiaries. This will be through an application process, where the criteria for selection and activities will be disseminated at the community level (through meetings and outreach) to encourage eligible households to apply. The Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) team of Syria Relief will then verify applicants to determine eligibility and to finalize the selection of target beneficiaries. It is anticipated that applications will be scored based on the above-mentioned targeting criteria and prioritized according to socio-economic vulnerability.A1.1.4 Procurement and distribution of inputs (with involvement of beneficiaries) including post-distribution monitoring following each distribution cycle: Veterinarians on the Syria Relief team, or hired locally, will provide quality assurance at the point of chicken enter point in the SH. This will previous vaccination history, antibiotics and a general health check of the animal prior to purchase. All information will be provide from the veterinarian of the chicken farm support from the same project. A MoU will be sign between SH owners and chicken farms outlining the terms and details of an understanding, including each parties' requirements and responsibilities. A1.1.5 Training for selected SH owners in relevant practices (e.g. SH care and management): Targeted beneficiaries will be trained in chicken meat practices, including disease identification and response, as well as skills related to income generation from their SH. Brochures and visual materials will also be provided to beneficiaries. Syria Relief has considerable experience implementing in partnership with FAO and therefore all materials are in line with agreed standards of the Agriculture Working Group and LTWGA1.1.6 Monitoring, on-going technical support and end line assessment: Following the initial training, veterinarians in the Syria Relief team and the trained extension workers will continue monitoring visits to all targeted households on a regular basis, e.g. monthly or by monthly. These visits offer an opportunity for beneficiary households to troubleshoot issues with their equipment, seek professional advice on any diseases or health conditions and ensure that SH conditions are regularly monitored. Based on experience, Syria Relief will establish a hotline with local veterinarians for beneficiaries to call if they have issues. An end line assessment will also be conducted with beneficiaries at the end of the Action to allow comparison to the baseline data collected and to determine the effectiveness of the project in achieving its outcomes. The MEAL team of Syria Relief through household interviews, key informant interviews and/or FGDs will conduct these activities.
