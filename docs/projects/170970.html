Project Summary

Schools in Bay and Bakool serve as a unique entry point for other lifesaving services, including access to health, safe drinking water, food, AWD/cholera prevention, and protection mechanisms reaching one of the most vulnerable groups – children. Safe learning environments and access to education are critical to ensuring that children displaced by or affected by conflict, natural disasters and pandemic such as COVID-19 are better able to cope, survive and recover from the negative impact of the crisis.
This project targets 3156 learners (1397 girls, 1759 boys) 316 of whom are disabled children in Baidoa, Dinsoor, Waajid and Hudur aged 6-18 years who are enrolled in primary schools from an estimated 3 million school-aged children who are out‐of‐school. Enrolment rates are lowest for the most vulnerable children such as girls, rural and internally displaced children (IDP) and particularly children with disabilities. READO estimates that 150,000 children (45% girls, 55% boys) out of the 1.4 million crisis affected children in Somalia will need education assistance during 2021 in Bay and Bakool regions. This project alongside other partners interventions are hoped to address these needs in Bay and Bakool.
Protection Return Monitoring Network (PRMN) estimates 893,000 people displaced in Somalia between January and August 2020. Of these, an estimated 357,200 (40%) are school-aged children. UNHCR Somalia also reported caseload of 11,118 refugees and asylum seekers and 46,034 refugees, returnees school-aged children needing education support. READO hopes to reach 5,550 of these IDP, returnee and refugee children in Bay and Bakool.
COVID-19 school closure had affected 1.1M8 children enrolled in the schools across the country from March to August 2020. Districts with the highest displaced people in need including Baidoa with 15 % of nomads, 24 % of IDPs and 34 % of rural children enrolled in school. This project will address the challenges of Covid-19 in education as well as the SG Report on Children and Armed Conflict 2020 which cited that in 2019, 1,442 children (53 girls) were recruited and/or used mainly by non-state armed groups, and that 227 girls reported sexual violence. These children will be mobilized to return to schools to save their lives by providing them with safe and protective learning environment, food, water and EiE supplies including infrastructure rehabilitation to increase the Gross Enrolment Ratio (GER) of 30% in Bay and Bakool.
GBV AoR assessment on the effects of COVID-19 reported a 34% physical violence, 20% sexual abuse and harassment and intimate partner violence. 81% of the respondents stated that they know ten or more girls cut or undergone FGM. On the other hand, a Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting (CTFMR) verified 1,184 grave violations, registering the recruitment of 546 children (30 girls), the killing and maiming of 469 children (107 girls) and 29 attacks on schools during the reporting period of January and June 2020. As a response to this, READO has planned sessions of trainings targeting teachers, CECs and caregivers on areas of psychosocial support, DRR and referrals for specialized GBV case management in coordination with child protection AoR in Baidoa, Xudur, Waajid and Dinsoor.
Education Cluster Children’s Voices survey conducted with 1,290 school children in May 2019 showed that 36% of children do not have hand washing facilities, 15% do not have gender segregated latrines, 67% do not have a girl friendly space at school and 46% of girls do not receive sanitary materials. These children are further disadvantaged as many of them are disabled (EMIS 2018/2019 shows 189 enrolled with disability out of the 21,600 school aged children living with disabilities). READO will rehabilitate 20 disability friendly classrooms affected by floods and conflict in Bay and Bakool and construct 12 VIP latrines and 12 hand washing facilities to encourage enrolment and retention of learners at school.

Mitigation Measures

Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance will be mitigated by ensuring all actors sign a code of conduct and adhere to PSEA policy which staff and contractors must sign against. Awareness sessions and trainings will incorporate aspects of prevention of GBV and PSEA.Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms will be mitigated by ensuring the communities have the toll free call in number displayed at all project sites and where necessary drop boxes for anonymous complaints will be provided.Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources will be mitigated through elaborate coordination mechanisms with all stakeholders including government and civil societies.Moreso, Education as a weapon provides learners with skills and knowledge to be productive and contribute to the general growth of the country's GDP in the long term.

Please provide further explanation

It is to be noted that education provided perhaps the most durable solution to all EiE. With more and more people getting education in Somalia, self reliance and growth of GDP will soon be a reality. These interventions are meant to provide the children of Somalia with the necessary weapons to fight poverty, unemployment, diseases and hunger and enable them to become dependable future citizens.This project is designed to be implemented over a 12 months’ time frame with benefits gained from involvement extending beyond the duration of this intervention especially in terms of improved education and child protection. Through the design of the activities, the project gives beneficiaries the ability to access education in the short-term through incentives and contractual agreements to supply education needs while also providing direct support and sensitization about children’s rights and protection. READO has designed the construction and rehabilitation works to conform to the INNE, SPHERE and WASH standards to guarantee durability.By working with civil society leaders, district committees and other key members of local and regional administration and linking them together for maintenance and monitoring of the works will help reduce tensions between the groups, improve cohesion and aid with coordinated maintenance and responsibility of the works in the long term to ensure sustainability of the legacy of this work and community ownership and smooth handing over. By linking in with other works being carried out by READO in a programmatic fashion, complementarities between the projects have been developed; benefits accrued will experience a multiplier effect as various sectors and groups will be supported in a comprehensive manner thus further increasing the sustainability and long-term outputs the projects achieve.
