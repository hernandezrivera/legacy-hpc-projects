Project Summary

This WASH and protection intervention by CARE international is designed to deliver a comprehensive WASH and protection package to refugees and asylum seekers in Gadaref and East Darfur refugee camps. The overall objective of this project is “Decreased WASH related morbidity and mortality and protection risks through provision of improved and sustained access to water supply, sanitation, hygiene promotion, and protection services for Ethiopian and south Sudanese Refugees in Gadaref and East Darfur camps and surrounding host communities”. This objective is developed in line with the revised humanitarian response plan of 2024, cluster priorities and strategies in supporting resilience and self-reliance through rehabilitation of facilities, operation and maintenance and capacity building of communities. The total number of individuals targeted by this project is 100,000 Refugees and asylum seekers settled the targeted location and host community members settled around the target refugee camps. The total target doesn't include duplication in targeting beneficiaries in WASH and protection. However, the number of targeted beneficiaries will not exceed 100,000 per sector. The project implementation will focus on gender equity and equality in all activities including hygiene sessions, training, and latrines rehabilitation and beneficiaries’ selection. The project will be focus on women and children as most vulnerable community members particularly in terms of mother and child health. Women will be consulted by the project team to identify their priorities and be part of decision making within their communities to encourage more equitable solutions. CARE will integrate an inclusive approach, with a strong focus on gender and social habits of the beneficiaries, on addressing the needs of people with disabilities and mitigating against GBV risks.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Along with the 15th of April conflict, the long-standing conflict and displacement in Sudan have made children, girls, men, and women are vulnerable to various forms of abuse and exploitation. In their daily life, women and girls face a significant risk of abuse. Women and girls travelling to far places for various reasons, such as to collect water and firewood, and even to distant marketplaces, face different kinds of abuse and men are not able to move freely as they will be exposed to killing and kidnapping by the armed people. Domestic violence is also common. For CARE and its partners, understanding how men and women are affected differently by conflict, and designing the right programs and projects that address gender issues is a priority, with the aim of preventing and responding to GBV risks. protection and safety of the targeted community will be taken in to consideration throughout the project implementation period. Priority will be given to the protection and security of the targeted groups who walk for more than 3-5km to collect water. Coordination with protection and WASH clusters will continue through-out the project period to ensure safety, protection and security of all beneficiaries who involved in receiving CARE support. protection and safety of the targeted community will be taken in to consideration throughout the project implementation period. Priority will be given for setting up the water tanks or distribution points at strategic locations so that it reduces the travel time from the water source as per the SPHERE standards (within 500 meters). Prior to the installation of water tanks or rehabilitation of water systems, women, men, children and people with special needs will be consulted for the selection of water schemes sites in order to ensure access, safety and security. The project will ensure implementing safe programming approaches by taking in to consideration DO NOT Harm principles and approach by making sure that our interventions do not create and/or increase risks to affected people’s safety and security. CARE will endeavor to continue improve the protection of targeted beneficiaries by addressing issues related to systematic conflict-related violence. Sexual Violence will focus on raising men’s awareness about issues of systematic violence. This will focus heavily upon reducing communities risk from sexual violence, specially women’s. Attention will also be placed upon raising awareness among beneficiaries of the available humanitarian services and how to access assistance and securing access to humanitarian assistance for newly affected people

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

A comprehensive and multi-entry Feedback and Accountability Mechanism (FAM) portfolio, including phone lines, complaint boxes, and in-person reporting mechanisms is in place across all project sites for participants, partners, and staff to report any cases or suspected cases. Stakeholders will provide direct feedback to CARE staff, community volunteers, or via suggestion boxes or phone calls in the project locations. Complaints received via FAM channels will be documented in an “compliant issues log” maintained by MEAL staff. With an ongoing rolling basis, complaints will be monitored and prioritized based on protection and risk issues, with the highest risks addressed immediately by the program team’s senior management. AS CARE has Zero tolerance policy for Sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse, the MEAL team will review complaints received through the FAM; sensitive cases will be referred to the Country Director, such as sexual harassment, exploitation, GBV. All CARE staff and related personnel receive training on PSHEA and are required to sign the PSHEA policy and protocols which are well socialized. In this project’s budget, PSHEA and GBV risk mitigation training is a planned activity for staff, partners, nutrition and health care service providers, and outreach workers. Central to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) are the responsible use of power and CARE’s FAM will be used to monitor and act in response to any identified risks of fraud/corruption, exclusion of beneficiary sub-groups, or diversion of resources during project implementation. CARE will work to ensure that all standards of confidentiality and protection of participants are followed. Complaints are logged and followed up by CARE’s Monitoring Officers, and subsequent appropriate and necessary adjustments to activities are made as part of the response.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Through its Accountability framework, CARE ensures participation of the affected population and relevant stakeholders in CARE's programmatic approach. The people whose lives are affected by our work, including women, children and other vulnerable groups have a right not only to voice their opinions and raise complaints, but they also have a right to be involved in shaping our interventions and in determining the channels we use to communicate with them. CARE defines accountability as explaining, being held responsible for, and hearing the perspectives of others about how well we are meeting our commitments and then actively making changes and improvements based on what we have learned and heard. Therefore, the community members, inclusive of women and all relevant stakeholders are regularly consulted during project design and implementation, ensuring that CARE remains responsive to the needs of the different groups. CARE will consult with communities, with a focus on women and youth groups. Women and girls will be consulted through feedback and accountability mechanisms (FAM) to ensure their specific needs and challenges are considered, particularly in relation to their access, safety and dignity.
