Project Summary

This project will provide support to vulnerable small holder farming households in 16 districts across Matebeleland North, Matebeleland South, Midlands and Masivingo provinces – (Agro Regions iii, iv and va), to increase the production of drought tolerant crops including bio fortified varieties and improve grazing land based on the principals of regenerative agro-ecology using low external organic inputs. The project will be split into four components;
i) Standard input pack provision for crop production
ii) Lead farmer demonstration approach for agro-ecological crop production.
iii) Pasture and grazing land improvement. Establishing demonstration areas with training on communal grazing management.
iv) In addition, awareness campaigns to promote gender action learning and the consumption of bio-fortified crop varieties and a healthy diet.

1 - Activities

The key activities of this project include:Component 1. Crop Production for agroecological regions iii and ivThe procurement and distribution of bio-fortified seed varieties such as NUA 45 sugar beans, cowpea, sorghum, millet as well as other legume crops. Component 2: Agroecology• The procurement and distribution of organic rich fertilizers• Establishing 0.5 Ha ‘lead farmer’ demonstration plots to show case principals of agro-ecology.• Cascade training to livestock farmers through ‘look and learn’ training activities. Component 3: Improved pasture• The procurement and distribution of improved pasture/forage seeds such as Buffle Grass, Rye grass, Eragoris superb (lovegrass) for average 5 Ha. per farmer. Component 4. Gender Action and Learning and Nutrition Awareness• Training of peer facilitators• Training of farm households• Training of households on bio-fortified food preparation and consumption• Training of households on healthy diets

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

This project was designed in consultation with the local communities. FAO has a long history of community engagement in these areas from implementing a wide variety of projects. In addition, different vulnerability assessments (such as the ZIMVAC and Crop, Livestock Assessments, IPC) have been carried out in these areas which involve consultations with local communities and other key stakeholders from government ministries and non-government organisations. The results and recommendations of these assessments have been used to formulate the project Outcomes, Outputs and Activities. Local authorities and traditional leaders will be fully involved in beneficiary selection and monitoring of project activities. Further consultations will be held with women and youth during project implementation especially the planning and monitoring processes to ensure gender equality. Further more, FAO will ensure a project oversight committee will be established in each district. The Oversight committee will have representatives from different stkaholders, including farmer representatives. FAO will establish a grievance reporting mechanism to cater and answer to any grievance case brought by the local communities.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Through the promotion of agro-ecological principals this project will actually protect and improve the environment. This will be done through; • Reduced Soil and water contamination from agro-chemicals – due to use of organic fertilizers and Integrated Pest Management (IPM) – push and pull • Reduced risk of soil erosion–due to soil fertility management from increased organic matter, permant soil cover (use of mulch and cover crops) and minimum soil disturbance. • Reduced risk of flooding through permanent soil cover, reduced overgrazing and construction of water conservation structures such as contour ridges• Increased bio-diversity through the introduction of grass species and multipurpose legume crops The parameters noted above will be monitored through the demonstration plots and will be part of the M&E system
