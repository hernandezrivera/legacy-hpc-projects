Project Summary

Every year in South Sudan, heavy rains cause widespread flooding which devastate lives and livelihoods of the nearby communities living alongside the River Nile’s banks. In 2020, seasonal floods reached unprecedented levels, affecting over 400,000 people in Jonglei and more than 125,000 people in the Greater Pibor Administrative Area. The floods caused damage to infrastructure, housing agriculture and livestock.
The situation intensified in Jonglei in July 2020, when the dyke built to control the flow of the River Nile collapsed at multiple locations, exacerbating the rising flood levels in Bor Town and parts of Greater Bor. As a result, despite being at the height of the dry season, parts of Greater Bor remain flooded. Displaced households are unable to return and rebuild their home. Furthermore, significant sections of the main road that serves as the gateway from Bor to the rest of Jonglei are submerged by water, cutting off the main route to supply goods. Consequently, humanitarian access has been severely restricted and humanitarian support cannot reach the local populations in Bor. Access to this region is absolutely critical for WFP and all Logistic Cluster partners, who utilize the dry season to preposition supplies prior to the heavy rains, before access is further limited. Therefore, WFP and all humanitarian actors operating in Jonglei State require the flooded area to be drained, to allow for the rehabilitation of the dyke and road, to ensure communities can resettle and humanitarian actors, include WFP, can access the region, prior to the rainy season. Additionally, it is essential that this area is drained and restrictions lifted, so commercial access is possible. Providing commercial access will contribute to critical commercial and market growth in this region.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is one of the most logistically challenging countries requiring a coordinated logistics response to ensure timely and efficient delivery of relief items. Only 192km of the country's estimated 17,000km of roads are paved, which is equivalent to two percent. Furthermore, two-thirds of these roads become impassable during the rainy season, cutting people off from markets and basic services and delaying the delivery of humanitarian assistance, due to increased reliance on costly air transport. There is extremely restricted access to Jonglei as a result of intense rainfall, which has submerged sections of the main road that serves as the gateway from Bor to the rest of Jonglei, cutting off the main route to supply goods. Consequently, humanitarian access has been severely restricted and humanitarian support cannot reach the local populations in Bor. Access to this region is absolutely critical for humanitarian actors including WFP who utilise the dry season to preposition supplies prior to the heavy rains before access is further limited. Draining this area to allow for the rehabilitation of the dyke and road, will ensure communities can resettle, and humanitarian actors can access the region, prior to the rainy season. In addition to improving access for humanitarian actors, draining this area and rehabilitating the dyke and road will allow for improved commercial access, which ultimately contributes to sustainable commercial and market growth across the region.

2 - Project activities

As a response to this flooding emergency, WFP South Sudan Logistics propose a three-fold project. Firstly, the project will rehabilitate the 120 damaged dykes points along the Baidit-Jalle-Maibor route. Secondly, the project will drain the flood water from the main Bor-Baidi-Jalle-Maibor Road. Finally, the project will rehabilitate the Bor-Baidit-Jalle-Maibor section of road, which will subsequently open access to six counties in Jonglei State, in need of food assistance. This three-fold project will allow WFP to gain access to the region and preposition food requirements for Jonglei State, using the most cost-efficient delivery modality. If WFP and other humanitarian responders do not gain access to this region the only modality that WFP will be able to use is airdrops, which would be at the cost of $100 million.

3 - Project indicators

WFP will measure its performance by reporting to OCHA on the following indicator: Amount of kilometres of road rehabilitation completed.
