Project Summary

Project aims to provide live-saving and sustainable WASH assistance to disaster affected displaced (25%) and non- displaced populations(75%). The response targets 109,900 people in severe and extreme WASH needs in order to reduce malnutrition especially among children as result of acute water diarrhea. About 68% of AWD/cholera cases reported between June/May 2021 were <5 children from flood-prone districts whose population depends on unprotected water sources, these districts have also high GAM prevalence rates of >15 per cent. The project will continue to restore access to safe and equitable basic WASH services in targeted locations in a dignified manner. Lack of adequate WASH services to the population is a main cause of spread of Acute Diarrheal Diseases (ADD)/ cholera as well as conflict over water sources and related displaced in distress.
Access to safe water will be increased through rehabilitation of water sources including 8 boreholes, 10 shallow wells and 15 pipeline extension to reduce distance to water point hence increase protection of women and girls. All water systems will be equipped with distribution infrastructure and hybrid energization systems. Taps stand will be located within a radius of at least 500m to reduce distance and enhance security of women and girls. Project will construct 2 major improved water dams in locations that lack other options. Well heads will be raised at a minimum 0.5m to reduce risk flooding and contamination. Water committees comprising men and women from all community groups including the minority and the marginalized will be trained on management, O&M. Local technicians will be trained and provided with O&M tools to undertake repairs whereas consumers will pay for the technician’s services. For major water supply in urban areas, Private Public Parternship (PPP) management system will be adopted. Where possible NRC will partner with local private investors to establish and manage major water supply systems in a sustainable manner. Project will construct 400 latrines for families reintegrated through the durable solutions. NRC undertakes sanitary surveys periodically to identify contamination risks for timely mitigation. All beneficiaries of latrines will be provided with kettle to promote handwashing and sensitized to use soap for handwashing. Design of infrastructure will take into consideration the needs of special groups in the community including the disabled, old, children to ensure they have equitable access to the services. Life-saving WASH interventions will be implemented in case of sudden disaster to reduce the related mortality and morbidity especially among children. This will be through temporary provision of safe water to 6000 HHs through direct delivery or restricted cash depending on context and market functionality, provision of hygiene kits through voucher (3000 HH), construction of 550 simple design emergency latrines, latrine emptying (450 latrines) and safe fecal sludge disposal and hygiene promotion. Schools and health facilities will be provided with latrines (200 institutional latrines). Latrines will be raised above ground level to reduce the risk of flooding during rains. Women and girls will be involved in identification of sites for facilities to ensure their safety and security is taken into account. Latrines will be gender separated, provided with lockable doors and handwashing equipment. Solar street lights (35 posts) will be provided near water points and latrines to improve the security and safety of women and girls especially in crowded IDP settlements. The beneficiaries will be provided with adequate information on NRC’s complaints, response and feedback mechanisms.

1. - Projects activities

8 boreholes will be rehabilitated equipped with solar hybrid pumping system and appropriately designed distribution infrastructure. • 5 new shallow wells will be constructed and protected with raised well head for protection from contamination and floods. Depending on population size and yield, they will be equipped with solar pump where appropriate. • 5 Shallow wells will be rehabilitated (deepened to increase yield, protected with raised well head for contamination and floods protection). • 2 mega rainwater catchments will be rehabilitated. To improve quality of drinking water an infiltration well equipped with a solar pump will be constructed beside the rainwater catchment. • 15 pipeline extension will be done especially for IDPs in urban set ups to reduce distance to water point hence increase protection of women and girls • WES Committees will be trained for all water supply systems. Private Public Partnership (PPP) management systems will be established for major water supplies. Borehole Operators trained and provided with maintenance tools • 20 Schools & Health facilities will be provided with large scale water filters • Sanitary surveys will be undertaken as a standard procedure before rehabilitation of shallow ground water sources. Water sources will be chlorinated in 5 districts. • 200 Institutional latrines with handwashing facilities will be constructed for schools and health facilities. • 400 disludgeable Latrines with handwashing facilities will be constructed as part of the durable solutions programme. 550 Emergency latrines will be constructed for suddenly displaced people. • 35 Solar lighting posts will be installed near latrines and water collection points to enhance security especially for women and girls. • 6000 Hhs in sudden emergency provided with water through vouchers • 3000 HHs in sudden emergency provided with Hygiene kits through vouchers • 450 latrines will be disludged/ rehabilitated • 90 CHPs will be trained to promote good hygiene • 150 sanitation tool kits will be provided and clean up campaigns undertaken. Design of infrastructure may hinder certain population groups such as people with disability from accessing WASH services. Project will deliberately consult these population groups to ensure they contribute to the design of the infrastructure to they are equally able to access services. A portion of latrines will specifically be designed for people with disability including the elderly. They will be provided with a ramp, side rails and a toilet seat. Dimensions and design for tap stands will take into consideration the needs for people using walking aids including clutches, wheel chairs, walking sticks thus ensuring that such people are able to use the facilities without difficulties.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

There is an existing water supply market in all locations within Somalia whereby emergency water is provided to willing buyers through existing water suppliers who deliver water using trucks. The project will utilise the opportunity whereby community or household water vouchers will be provided to most vulnerable communities and or households to enable them access water from the existing local suppliers. Hygiene kits will be provided through cash or vouchers only where functional markets for such items exists and can sustain the demand created through provision of the cash. Past and present experience have shown the modality as a viable and sustainable solution for supporting households and small WASH businesses. Using cash promote dignity of beneficiaries in comparison to in-kind humanitarian assistance.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

NRC has gained experience in provision of WASH services through Cash and vouchers. In particular, cash and vouchers have been used for delivery of emergency safe water supply and for provision of hygiene kits. In locations where NRC is currently operating, information on market functionality has been collected and it informs the project design. Where there is inadequate information, CTP will be preceded by market assessment to inform the appropriate modality of response.
