Project Summary

The project will contribute to the HRP strategic objective 1, and the FSS cluster strategic objective 1. It will do so by providing Food Security support to small scale breeders and mix farming families living in Area C of West Bank who are particularly affected by the political situation since they are continuously under the risk of demolitions, displacement and increasing settlers' violence. Expansion of the settlements, restriction of movements and access to land and water undermine the livelihoods of these communities.
The project is based on a needs assessment conducted by MA’AN in July-August 2019 as part of the design of its Humanitarian Strategy in Area C, within which the present proposal is framed. the needs assessment includes updated statistics from the Palestinian Authority (PA) Ministry of Agriculture and from UN/OCHA data (OCHA Vulnerability Profile Project/VPP 2013), in addition to updates and additional information based on MA’AN’s direct engagement in the Food Security Sector. MAAN’s own findings from previous field assessments (e.g. those of previous projects funded by the HF/OCHA, AMENCA3) are also included. The need assessment identified the Breeders rely mainly on fodder purchase for the animal nutrition due to the limited access to grazing areas. Expenditure for the fodder is the largest cost of herder households and restricted access increases also the cost of other inputs, such as water, and veterinarian assistance. Losses of assets due to diseases and reduction of flock production due to the decrease of quality and quantity of input provided by the herders to their animals to cope with the current situation, are putting at risk of livelihood insecurity the breeders households.
In order to respond to the identified needs and to contribute to the HRP and FSS cluster objectives, MA’AN proposal aims at addressing the vulnerability of the Palestinian living in area C communities through a comprehensive humanitarian relief approach that includes livelihood/food security and protection-related humanitarian responses to increase communities’ resilience. The intervention has been structured in three interrelated components: One component will tackle the high rates of animal mortality and diseases, and increasing the awareness and the knowhow on preventive measures and providing livelihood related infrastructure to the most vulnerable families. A second component will provide small scale breeders and mix farming families living in area C with means to maximize the use of the land available, thus increasing the productivity of the land and decreasing the risk of its confiscation. A third component will endow the communities with response mechanisms to address and object to the Israeli military orders by increasing the awareness among communitarians and duty bearers and providing tools and means to put those response mechanism in practice.
The proposed project builds on current interventions being implemented by MA’AN and partners, and predominantly aims to alleviate poverty and food insecurity for the most marginalized farming households in Area C. While another proposed intervention predominantly focuses on farmers with damages from settler violence and occupation regime. This projects focuses on vulnerable Farming HHs with demonstrated protection and socio-economic vulnerabilities, with a particular focus on herders and livestock rearers.
The proposed governorates and interventions aim to complement ongoing interventions by MA’AN, and have been proposed based on consultations and recommendations by the MoA and its directorates and other actors to serve the needs of farming households in Area C.

1 - Needs

In July-August 2019 MA’AN Development Center conducted an overall needs assessment as part of the design of its Humanitarian Strategy in Area C, within which the present proposal is framed. the needs assessment includes updated statistics from the Palestinian Authority (PA) Ministry of Agriculture and from UN/OCHA data (OCHA Vulnerability Profile Project/VPP 2013), in addition to updates and additional information based on MA’AN’s direct engagement in the Food Security Sector. MAAN’s own findings from previous field assessments (e.g. those of previous projects funded by the HF/OCHA, AMENCA3) are also included. As stated in FSS HNO 2020, communities in Area C are faced with the limited access to land and water sources. Palestinians with land near settlements or behind the Wall in the West Bank are physically barred from their lands, also closed military zones prevent farmers from tending their land and herders’ animals from grazing which is highly difficult for those communities who depend on range lands and water sources for their livelihoods. People living in Area C, who are mainly herders and Bedouins relying on livestock farming as main source of food and income, are particularly affected by the political situation since they are continuously under the risk of demolitions, displacement and increasing settlers' violence. Expansion of the settlements, restriction of movements and access to land and water undermine the livelihoods of these communities ( Farmers in the Access Restricted Areas (ARA)- FSS HNO 2020). Breeders rely mainly on fodder purchase for the animal nutrition due to the limited access to grazing areas. Expenditure for the fodder is the largest cost of herder households and restricted access increases also the cost of other inputs, such as water, and veterinarian assistance. Losses of assets due to diseases and reduction of flock production due to the decrease of quality and quantity of input provided by the herders to their animals to cope with the current situation, are putting at risk of livelihood insecurity the breeders households. The needs of Vulnerable farming households in area C communities remain vast, and disproportionately addressed by aid actors. These restraints in sustenance of livelihoods and needs include:• Constrained productive capacities due to damages of vulnerable farmers Agricultural assets resulting from settler violence and other occupation regime; Subjection of lands nearby settlements to a regime of permits and stop work order and movement restrictions; Shortage of water for agricultural purposes and lack of networks that facilitate access to water in communities; Lack of farmers’ access to agricultural lands due to inadequate agricultural roads and/or presence of land in “no-go” zones; Substandard livestock shelters coupled with increased shrinkage of grazing lands, poor farm management, and insufficient feeds; Lack of access to resources enabling sustenance of livelihoods, mainly connection to electrical grids, which has proved needs for solar units to ensure proper livestock management and access to water from present cisterns and filling points. The activities being proposed, are aiming at improving food security of marginalized herders and families in Area C of the West Bank by securing and protecting their livelihoods. this goes along the lines of one of the main Socio-Economic and Food Security Survey (SEFSec) 2018 “ Farmers, herders and fishers are particularly vulnerable considering their direct exposure to the effects of the conflict and their dependence on small scale productive activities to meet their basic food and income needs”. Herding represents one of the main local sources of income for a relevant portion of poor and/or vulnerable Palestinian households living in Bedouin and/or rural areas, it contributes to enrich the diet of households in marginalised Bedouin and rural areas and it supports breeder families living in under high risks of abandonment and and

2 - Activities

The beneficiaries are marginalized families and herders living in the Area C of the West Bank, a special attention will be given to provide services to women-headed households, households with a high number of members or with a disabled member. Some of those household are also sedentary and semi-sedentary herders and Bedouins communities that live in the South areas of Hebron, Eastern Bethlehem and Qalaqiliya particularly affected by the Israeli security Wall as well as the encroaching presence of military and civilian settlements. The herders and Bedouins targeted by this intervention are mostly under the risk of eviction from their land or house demolition by the Israeli Occupation and they reside in highly isolated localities in Area C and in East Jerusalem. Criteria for choosing the marginalized families: • To be resident in the Area C of West Bank and belong to those communities who are under risk or affected by demolitions/evictions or any violations of the IHL in the West Bank.• To be small ruminant breeder, or having the breeding as main source of income for their family. To be considered a small ruminant breeder the criteria is the number of animal owned, households with a range between 30 and 160 head.• Families that lack of water source and has small piece of land either adjacent to the house or in the vicinity.• Families headed by women will be considered priority• Families who are able and willing to take part in the planning and implementation of the project including participation in the training• Large family size will be given priority• Families who have disable people will be given priorityTo rely on an income partially derives from the flock and who are considered vulnerable due to the large number of household's members (> 5 members). In addition, the criteria will include also vulnerability due to the loss of assets because of the outstanding debts or other shocks; or due to any natural or man-made disaster (stormy weather and demolitions/evictions, violations of IHL). 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The communities targeted by the project are very risky locations (surrounded by settlements or under ICA threat of demolition), therefore MA’AN , in alignment with the “Do No Harm” Principles for humanitarian assistance depends approaches to reduce risks to beneficiaries as a result of intervention. MA’AN adopts key principles that serve to reduce unintended negative impact of the project; these principles include: Effective feedback mechanism:, strong linkages with beneficiary communities, with a feedback mechanism that has allowed targeted communities to participate actively in the design of the project, and will further allow them to participate in the beneficiary selection process, and determining needs per each beneficiary. Feedback/complaints, and suggestions of targeted communities will be documented and taken into consideration. The project staff will further hold regular meetings with villages’ representatives and will update them about the progress and workplan of the project. Further, MA’AN will implement the project in a transparent manner where beneficiary list will be made public and reason for any person not being selected will be stated. The community will have a period to object to the list. MA’AN will ensure that the assistance is provided in times where control is limited (at night, during week-ends) in coordinated times with the community committee and beneficiary to assess the security situation of the community. MA’AN mobilizes/employs local coordinators for its projects in Area C to mitigate access risk in locations where security incidents are likely to occur. And when possible in alignment with its procurement policy and guidelines procures from local sources. MA’AN has been working for many years in the target , including for support to livelihoods, and have a good relationship with the communities, so they will be made aware of any problem with ICA or IDF in the communities in real time. Aligning with the “Do No Harm” Principle, the implementation team will ensure all planned rehabilitation work by the beneficiaries through and project is completed with project closeout. In instances where HHs are unable to effectively conduct the land rehabilitation, MA’ANs technical team will directly supervise the rehabilitation work for disadvantaged households (female headed households , persons with disabilities …etc).for each phase of the proposed intervention, MA’AN will conduct risks analysis aiming at identifying and mitigating possible risks associated with the action. 9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The intervention is designed to promote and help protect and the rights of people who have historically been marginalized or discriminated against, such as women, children, the elderly and PWDs practicing agriculture and herding in Jordan Valley and Bedouin Communities in area C. For target HHs, the selection criteria is designed to be transparent and ensure Equal and more importantly equitable access to aid. The criteria will focus on the socio economic conditions of the households as well as on the vulnerability of the land and rehabilitation needs and the gender make up of the household. As women are responsible for the household in Palestinian society, the number of females in the household will also be an important part of the application process. As women-headed households are disproportionately affected by poverty.The needs of both genders are addressed equally. The entire family, regardless of sex, age, and disabilities will equally benefit from the intervention. As a positive measure towards women, the project team will take all necessary measures to assure equal access of women farmers to benefit from this project. These measures will include but are not limited to ensuring women participation in project orientation session, and monitoring activities. All beneficiaries will be treated with dignity and respect.Considerations to the needs of the elderly and PWDs are taken and will ensure that special arrangements are made to rehabilitate or construct green houses to them. At the start of the project, the project will hold an orientation workshop in each location to explain the project and tentative time frame. In order to involve the affected population in the project implementation. Community Based Project Support Committees (CBPSCs) will be formulated to assist in the project implementation and to ensure accountability and transparency with beneficiaries as it is part of MA'ANs community integration and ownership approach to community development. The (CBPSCs) will consist of members of the targeted communities, ,Municipality representatives, representative from the Directorate of Agriculture -DoA and representatives from local CBOsand the project team. Women and people with disabilities will be encouraged to be part of the committee. The committee will assist in the beneficiary selection according to the set criteria and provide information to the project management as needed on any special cases or circumstances. The (CBPSCs) will supervise the public announcement of the project among the inhabitants of the targeted communities. Representatives of the communities will participate in the application process. Women of these communities will play an integral part in the selection of beneficiaries through participation in the application selection process. A comprehensive analysis of the candidates will be conducted to select final beneficiaries of the land rehabilitation components. (CBPSCs) and beneficiaries will be encouraged to provide feedback about the process of application, selecting beneficiaries by contacting the field coordinators and/or the project manager. All suggestions and complaints will be taken seriously and needed action will be taken accordingly. Furthermore, the (CBPSCs) will also be involved in monitoring the project through conducting regular visits with the project staff, monitoring materials delivery, and conveying feedback on progress of rehabilitation to the project team.
