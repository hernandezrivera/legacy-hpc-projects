Project Summary

The project interventions are intended to lead to increased food security and strengthen community resilience to conflict and climate shocks through enabling sustainable increases in agricultural and livestock productivity and adopting alternative livelihoods which will decrease competition over natural resource. The ultimate beneficiaries of the project are mainly the farmers and pastoralist, Internally Displaced Peoples (IDPs), returnees and poor residents in Blue Nile, North Darfur and Kassala states (around 124000 people).
Agricultural tools, drought resistant seeds and seedlings, vegetables and horticulture will be provided to increase household food production. Promotion of climate smart and resilient crops and livestock production systems, protection of livestock based livelihoods in pastoral and agro pastoral community through provision of emergency livestock assistance, livestock watering, restocking with goat and poultry; awareness raising on the important of healthy diet in addition to women training on food processing for malnutrition, establishment of women farms, these inputs will build the capacity of the targeted communities to achieve their own food security and improve their livelihoods by increasing their production and income to better able them to withstand future shocks, droughts and other natural disasters. The project will facilitate regeneration of ecosystem services through a range of activities that aims to increase access to water, pasture and forestry which in the past directly contributed to conflict between farmers and nomadic pastoralists. Training will be provided on the management of these resources and environmental conservation techniques. The project will support the formation and reinforcement of farmers’ groups and community based associations strengthen their institutional capacity and consolidate their links to government service providers and other actors; to facilitate delivery of other project activities. For example, the training and technical support will be provided through these groups. The associations will also be given the role of managing the productive infrastructure provided under the project. The Project will apply participatory approaches for prioritizing and planning community needs to ensure that community planning and service delivery is tailored to local needs. More over the project will arrange to raise community awareness on themes other than production of the value chain products, including gender, protection and environment. As with elsewhere in the three targeted states men and women have different and unequal access to agriculture and livestock production inputs and technology (women represent about 49% of the total population yet are responsible for more than 65% of agriculture and livestock livelihood activities); therefore the project will ensure that everyone in the affected community - women, girls, boys and men - have a voice in the development and implementation of the project and have access to opportunities that the project gives. Restocking with livelihoods kits, training on animal husbandry, vegetable growing and marketing will enable women and youth to better engage in income generating activities and production process. The project will be run in environment friendly pattern, saving natural resources and increase target population awareness about environment issues through discussion and orientation sessions.

Needs

Food security: According to the latest IPC results about 5.759 million people represents 13% of the population will be food insecure and expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) & emergency (IPC phase 4) level of food insecurity situation. Factors that trigger food insecurity & poor food consumption in Sudan including drought & natural factors such as dry spells & floods lead to decrease production of food & cash crops; FSL HNO 2019 identifies rising prices, compounded by limited access to livelihoods opportunities which have led to a severe decline in the purchasing power of the most vulnerable communities including returnees, refugees, IDPs, as well as vulnerable host communities and residents. Moreover, the ongoing economic situation, along with the recent lifting of fuel and wheat subsidies, and high rates of inflation resulting in an increase in food prices & costs of agricultural production. FEWS reports – December 018, indicates that 2018 Food prices are already 150-200 percent above average & are expected to increase to 200-250 percent above average during the 2019 lean season, reducing household food access. The report further mentions that, much of North Darfur, Blue Nile and Kassala will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3), population of greatest concern are IDPs and poor households in SPLM-N controlled areas. Between March & May, Critical level of acute malnutrition (GAM (WHZ)>15%) is likely in several areas including those identified as having a MUAC around 11 percent in November 2017 (among which are North Darfur & Blue Nile states). In Kassala state, the conducted S3M by UNICEF during 2013 has shown high malnutrition rates, a yearly burden of 5,700 MAM, over 1,000 SAM and over 10,000 stunted <5’s was projected. Institutions: The limited organizational, networking and advocacy capacity of CBOs & national NGOs is a major constraint to their playing a more decisive role in coordinating & effectively delivering humanitarian support to the affected populations. Women lack adequate representation & have low voice in decisions for their communities. Strengthening the capacity of these institutions in various areas will enable them to plan, prepare & effectively respond to future food insecurity and livelihoods hazards. It worth noting that, Practical Action has extensive experience through different projects in developing the organizational, institutional & technical capacities of civil society organization and government institutions. Environment: The degradation of natural resources has forced the majority of the rural population to overgraze range lands, expand cropping in marginal lands to compensate for low yields & cut trees for fire wood & charcoal making for sale as coping strategies. People have also become involved in conflict & competition over dwindling natural resource base. All these activities will continue to further degrade the environment unless efforts are exerted to support, conserve and restore the natural resources.

Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will be run in environment friendly pattern, saving natural resources and increase target population awareness about environment issues through discussion and orientation sessions. The project will support solar irrigation system which will reduce hazardous gases emission from burning fuel for water pumping in addition communities' women and men committees will be trained to establish forest nurseries utilizing waste water to start community forests around water supply points. On the other hand, the project will encourage farmers to adopt agro-ecological farming practices.
