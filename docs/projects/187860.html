Project Summary

The project aims to provide lifesaving essential integrated health, nutrition, and child protection services, covering the following communities in Idleb subdistrict:
Kreiz, Mastumeh, Nayrab, Bruma, Bshmaron, Tab Issa - Eastern and Western, Deir Al-Zaghab, Sijer - Bqesemtoh, Arshani, Northern Ora, Aqrabat, Ein Shib, Falyun, Qminas, Kafruhin, Martein, Idleb.
And the following communities in Ariha subdistrict:
Al-Assadiyah, Al Mokbelah, Ariha, Bab Ellah, Berjhab, Shinan, Kafr, Shalaya, Kafr Najd, Mseibin, Motaram, Maarbalit, Maarzaf, Maalali, Nahleh.
The project duration will be 24 months, covering the mentioned locations throughout 2022-2023, with CMAM, IYCF, TSFP, in addition to awareness messages about handwashing practices, food processing, toilet and sanitation, and COVID-19 signs, symptoms, means of infection, and IPC measures.
Total project targets include 59,859 U5 children, among them, there are 10,176 children with disability, in addition to 19,048 PLW’s, including 3,238 with a disability, as those groups are the most affected by the poor nutrition situation across Syria.
Projects activities, locations, targets, and budget are fully coordinated with the nutrition cluster, to ensure optimal planning, in addition, full coordination will be maintained during the implementation, to ensure the optimal coverage of the need, without any duplication or overlapping, and to receive the nutrition supplies from the nutrition cluster, according to the needs, and the availability of supplies.
Project activities will be implemented using two different modalities:
1. Orient Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) center: the center provides equitable access to high-quality, lifesaving, curative nutrition services through early identification, and treatment of acutely malnourished boys and girls under five years and pregnant and lactation women (PLW). Provision of management of acute malnutrition will be provided at the health facility, and in integration with infant and young child feeding services, micro-nutrient supplementation, and primary health care services. The center is also receiving all the referred cases that need either nutrition or primary health care services from Orient RRT teams or any other partner in the area.
2. Orient Rapid Response Teams (RRT): 10 RRT’s covering the whole of Idleb subdistrict, and part of Ariha subdistrict, Provided services include screening for malnutrition and referral, SAM and MAM treatment, one-to-one IYCF-E counseling, IYCF-E messages, micro-nutrient supplementation, screening for external visual signs of corporal punishment and suspected abuse and violence cases and referral, screening for danger health signs and referral to healthcare centers, in addition to WaSH messages on handwashing practices, food processing, and toilet and sanitation, and as a part of the COVID-19 response plan, dissemination of information on COVID-19 signs, symptoms, means of infection, and IPC measures.

4 - Needs

The poor nutrition situation is still a major challenge for public health in NWS, mainly affecting infants, U5, and PLW, causing different forms of malnutrition, including wasting, SAM, MAM, chronic malnutrition or stunting, and micronutrient deficiency especially iron-deficiency anemia.According to the SMART survey in NWS 2021, there is a clear deterioration in the nutrition situation in 2021 compared to 2019 in the same area, GAM by MUAC has increased from 0.9% in 2019, to 4.7% in 2021, while the GAM rate by WHZ has increased from 0.73% to 2.5%. 10.8% of WRA are malnourished, while this rate was higher among PLWs by 12.4%.Stunting prevalence was 24.5% in 2021 survey, with no significant change from 2019, and the prevalence of anemia among children 6 – 59 months was 46%, and 53.85% among WRA.The survey showed sub-optimal IYCF practices, with only 53% exclusive breastfed, the level of timely initiation of breastfeeding was only 19.1%. Children are not accessing adequate and quality diets, fail in terms of timing, repetition, and diversity, with only 20.73% of children having an acceptable MDD, 31.61% had an acceptable minimum meal frequency, and the minimum acceptable diet 10.8%.While the HRP 2022-23 data shows that targeted subdistrict have the highest severity score (5), the percentage of IDP’s in Idleb and Ariha subdistrict is 48% and 22% respectively, the Prevalence of exclusive breastfeeding for children 0-5 months is 54% in both subdistrict, and the MDD is 20%.

5 - Implementation of activities

Orient OTP and Orient RRT's are already up and running in the mentioned areas, the aim of this project is to ensure the continuity of the preventive and curative nutrition services in the implementation area, throughout this project, orient will maintain a high level of coordination with nutrition cluster team, and with all the nutrition partners in the area, to ensure the optimal coverage without duplication or overlapping, and to organize the referral process between the nutrition partners, all the project activities will be reported to the nutrition cluster on monthly basis, throughout the 4W mechanism.Monitoring will be conducted at every stage of the program through the collection and analysis of routine data on a continuous basis throughout the life of the program. Data collection tools are appropriate and tailored to the program's needs. The methodology for monitoring will include using different tools and steps, such as beneficiaries disaggregated information, facilities status, pictures, and other relevant data to measure the achievement, observation checklist in the facilities to ensure that the staff complies with all Orient policies and procedures.in addition, a feedback mechanism has been established to affirm that affected communities have the right to complain if Orient is not abiding by commitments made to them, this mechanism provides communication channels to the communities to voice their suggestion and provide their feedback about our interventions.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

-Violence against humanitarian Facilities and humanitarian services providers: Orient will use the Syria humanitarian notification system and report any attacks through MRM Mechanism, Provide preparedness training for the facilities and staff-care in case of incidents, and continue advocacy efforts by the UN agencies with all parties to the conflict to engage them on protection topics and respect of IHL. -Gender-Based Violence GBV: Enabling factors such as the presence of female staff, to increase access of women and girls to the services and offers a safe environment, all staff, especially those who are likely to come in contact with survivors, should be trained on providing survivor-centered first-line support, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. In addition to training on communicating with persons with diverse disabilities to enable women and girls with disabilities to be identified and receive support on an equal basis. Coordinators on the ground must work with GBV counterparts to identify safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases – in a manner that guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor. Ensuring the confidentiality of all beneficiaries – for exams, communication, and record-keeping.-Humanitarian Worker Well-Being: Establishing “duty of care” policies detailing clear implementation procedures, ensuring the resilience and well-being of contracted staff.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Orient is an active member in the WoS PSEA network, all the staff has been trained on PSEA on all health facilities, all the recruitment process includes SEA screening the criteria of selection and all the staff should sign Orient code of conduct and PSEA policy, in addition, information on PSEA policy and procedure, including the code of conduct and reporting channel details, will be displayed in all offices and at project sites. All the contracts and partnership agreements will include a standard clause requiring contractors, suppliers, consultants, and sub-partners to commit to a zero-tolerance policy on SEA and to take measures to prevent and respond to SEA. A systematic vetting procedure will be in place for job candidates (e.g. reference checks, police records, Google searches) in accordance with local laws regarding employment, privacy, and data protection, including checking for prior involvement in SEA or other safeguarding concerns. Mandatory training for all personnel on the organization’s SEA policy and procedures will be held every 6 months, including a definition of SEA (that is aligned with the UN's definition); 2) a prohibition of SEA, and 3) actions that personnel is required to take (i.e. prompt reporting of allegations and referral of survivors). Mechanism and procedures for personnel, beneficiaries, and communities, including children, to report SEA allegations that comply with core standards for reporting.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

-Risk: changing front lines, changes in the parties controlling the implementation areas, and significant population movements as they flee shifting conflict areas.Mitigating measures: Orient regularly monitors the local situation, and Context-specific Safety and Security Management Plans will be in place and regularly reviewed and updated. - Risk: risks due to the COVID-19pandemic, that may result in lack of access of children and their caregivers to the project sites or even the suspension of project activities.Mitigation measures: relevant program adaptations have been developed and are currently in place, in addition to strict IPC measurement. - Risk: Interruption of nutrition supplies line if the UNSCR was not renewed.Mitigating measures: Orient is supporting all the advocacy efforts about the importance of the UNSCR renewal and works closely with the nutrition cluster and other partners to develop a contingency plan to mitigate this risk.- Risk: cooperation and coordination with the local stakeholders.Mitigating measures: throughout our long experience in implementing health and nutrition projects in the targeted area, Orient has gotten good relationships with all the stakeholders in the area, including the health directorates, local councils, and local communities.
