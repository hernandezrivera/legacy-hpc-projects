Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) aims to ensure the safety and dignity of the refugees through delivering transitional shelter assistance (TSA) to 1,000 households (also 150 extremely vulnerable individuals (EVIs)) in Camp 11. Community sessions on sustaining safe shelter, and training for carpenters (for better understanding of construction tools and best practices of it), TSA II and orientation on child-led DRR activities (involving children in consultations, developing awareness materials with pictures and child-friendly language, engaging them in activity based learning and enabling them to act as passive first responders) will be organized as part of the sustainability of the project, and of creating community ownership. SC will monitor the distributed materials, by the community mobilizers, in the distributed areas to ensure that distributed materials are safe and not sold to the local markets. SC will engage women, older people, PwSN and youth in light activities, such as, cleaning, making fences, waste management, supervisory, survey and data collection, and other relevant skills identified, while men will be involved in masonry and carpentry. SC ensures that the principles of protection, gender equality and inclusion and accountability to affected populations are central and cross-cutting in the proposed actions and implementation. Through adopting accountability mechanisms (community meetings, hotlines, helpdesk, children’s consultation and post distribution monitoring) which are accessible to even older persons and people with disabilities to raise their complaints in a safe and dignified manner, SC collects information from the affected populations regarding their views on the services. SC will work to minimize the safety risks facing female workers when receiving cash for work payments. SC will pay special attention to include women and girls in community consultation and accountability processes to increase their level of involvement in decision-making affecting them. SC will create labor pools of women, who will support the improvements to their own shelters and neighboring households, and hire community mobilizers, volunteers and required technical persons to support activities from the host community. SC will select targeted beneficiaries following the criteria: poor HHs, deteriorating shelter materials; extremely vulnerable households, families with multiple dependents, PWD, PLW, elderly and chronically sick, and families headed by separated, divorced, or widowed women with no or minimum support.

1 - Identified Needs

Despite previous shelter interventions, the settlement area still needs significant shelter repair/upgradation. Issues with shelter remained a common concern for the majority of households, with almost one third of households reporting having bought shelter materials to make repairs. More than a quarter of households reported not being able to make improvements despite reporting issues, largely due to limited access to materials. All shelters are needed to be safe, repaired/upgraded with materials that are resistant to short term deterioration and can withstand during adverse weather, as 69% of households reported having faced issues with their shelter in the 6 months prior to data collection (J-MSNA, Refugees, 2020). Provision of support for EVIs during the repairs/upgradation of shelters is required. Assessing needs from different gender and age groups to incorporate those needs in the shelter intervention process is highly required. Treated bamboo for the longevity of the structures: To enhance the better performance of these shelters, treated bamboo is needed to enhance the duration of a robust structural approach. Shelter requires a thorough maintenance to sustain its quality for a certain period of time. Otherwise, the quality and strength of the shelter deteriorates. The communities are needed to be aware of the use of construction tools and materials, and the knowledge of maintaining safe shelter. As such, there is great need of rolling out training and orientations of safe shelter maintenance. The beneficiaries need to be aware about DRR, environment and sustainable living. Hence the beneficiaries also needs to have sessions for them to introduce and aware them about DRR, environmental issues and sustainable living. Due to the geographic difficulties, camps are always at risk of natural disasters, community awareness sessions or disaster preparedness are required to improve living conditions of the refugees with protection and safety.

2 - Activities or outputs

(a) Distribute TSA – II materials to the selected 1,000 beneficiaries in line with Sector selection criteria and in coordination with other actors operating in Camp 11; provide treated bamboo in the TSA-II materials (untreated bamboo will be exchanged for treated bamboo from IOM Bamboo Treatment Plant); take feedback from the community through FGD to identify their needs in shelter and implement them with rational effort (different gender and age group will be part of FGD); Location: Camp 11(b) Provision of training: DRR/shelter reinforcement (considerations for female HHs to be trained in an appropriate setting); Target Individuals: 1,000; Location: Camp 11(c) Supporting EVHHs with materials delivery and implementation; Target HHs 15% or 150; Location: Camp 11(d) Shelter activities involving women, older people, PwSN and other forms of gender diverse population; Target Individuals: 150; Location: Camp 11
