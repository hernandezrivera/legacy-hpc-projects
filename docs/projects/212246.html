Project Summary

Needs: In the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) Summary, it is stated that around 16.7M people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase from 15.3M in 2023.
Implementation of activities: ZOA will conduct a comprehensive market analysis before starting implementation. ZOA will determine the beneficiary selection criteria in collaboration with local stakeholders and community committees and coordinate the support to ensure there is no duplication. Examples of vulnerability criteria could include; female headed households, households including family members living with a disability or chronic illness, households with children under 5 or elderly members. The transfer value and frequency will be in line with the recommendations of the Cash Working Group, which is currently 1,050,000SYP per month for a minimum of three months for emergency response, and a minimum of 6-8 rounds for the regular response. The transfer value could potentially be increased later in the year based on inflation or exchange rate changes. Manager of Program Quality supervises MEAL officers in-country. ZOA will monitor its interventions based on the logical framework indicators & activities with sex, age & disability disaggregated. Field level monitoring will be key in quality assurance, tracking progress, improved accountability & for demonstrating impact.
Risks, assumptions and mitigation measures
1. Changes in exchange rate & increasing inflation: ZOA closely monitors market prices, and contractors are committed to their offers for a specific period to avoid big changes in pricing due to exchange rate changes or inflation.
2. Lack of fuel availability: ZOA has contracts with several transportation service providers, allowing us to conduct the necessary trips without major constraints.
3. Lack of skilled labor: ZOA already has contracts with experienced local skilled laborers.
4. Delayed approvals: ZOA is coordinating with relevant stakeholders to facilitate a smooth approval process.
5. Security risks affecting implementation in the field: ZOA continually assesses the situation in the field and coordinates wit INSO, UNDSS, local authorities and community leaders.
6. Fraud and corruption: ZOA has clear policies preventing and mitigating risks of fraud and corruption including segregation of duties, and regularly trains its staff on these topics. All financial transactions are documented, filed and monitored and regular spot-checks and audits are conducted. Communities and stakeholders are well informed about their entitlements using multiple channels and good reporting channels are in place.
7. Protection risks: Please refer to protection risk analysis and PSEA strategy under Project summary extension in the response plan.
Please note that the activities mentioned are for two years (2024-2025), but the targets and budget covers only 2024. Additionally, for 2025, ZOA aims to support an estimated 50,000 persons with a budget of 1,4M USD.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection Risk Analysis: The project will adopt an inclusive and non-discriminatory approach so that all groups can safely and equitably access assistance. Target populations are at the center of decision-making processes throughout the project, for example consulting on the eligibility for assistance, which are based on context specific needs, preferences and vulnerabilities. This project considers the safety and dignity of beneficiaries to avoid creating or exacerbating protection risks for individuals, households or communities. (i.e. safety of beneficiaries, social tensions, intra-household dynamics). Project related protection risks include potential abuse, exploitation or other unacceptable behaviors by contractors, suppliers or staff within the community during the implementation of the works or during distributions. Therefore, all contractors, suppliers and ZOA staff/ volunteers will sign ZOA’s Code of Conduct. Also, contractors are required to provide a safe and secure work environment for their workers. Multi-channel feedback mechanisms are in place and ZOA staff knows how to deal with different types of feedback, including referrals for psychological and protection services and support. All staff including volunteers are regularly trained on protection, PSEAH, ZOA’s CoC and child protection. PSEA Strategy: All ZOA staff, volunteers and contractors sign a Code of Conduct informing them of their obligations as well as consequences of involvement in sexual exploitation and abuse. Staff is regularly trained on PSEA and knows how to make safe referrals to relevant services for people identified to be in need of specialized protection services in coordination with the Protection Sector. The project will put in place and inform beneficiaries about several accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries, staff and partners, that include a variety of confidential channels, eg. hotlines, social media, complaints box.
