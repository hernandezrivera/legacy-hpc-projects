Project Summary

INTERSOS proposes to promote a protective environment for conflict-affected people, in particular children and women. Three main elements will be addressed: 1) Prevention by addressing the risks of sexual and gender-based violence and responding to the immediate needs of survivors of GBV, 2) prevention of child abuse, neglect and exploitation; and
3) identifying and strengthening existing positive community protection mechanism.
Outputs to prevent and respond to immediate GBV and other protection needs: Integrated GBV Case management, establishment of Women and Girls Friendly Spaces, share INTERSOS’ Hotline, provision of psychological first aid (PFA), referral and accompaniment to specialised services, individual follow up. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS): individual & group counselling, communitybased recreational activities, art therapy, basic skills acquisition and informal education. Targeted material assistance for identified protection needs: Dignity kits to vulnerable women and girls including PwSN of reproductive age, Mama kits for vulnerable pregnant and lactating women and girls, cash for support for transportation of wounded or sick survivors to health facilities and other specialized services. Outputs to prevent violence and abuse: Community capacity building through Male agents of change groups: Partipants will be trained on GBV principles & personal/ relational accountability. Sensitization & awareness raising on gender equality and GBV prevention/response. Prevention of gender based violence through regular safety audits and risk/vulnerability mapping in the target areas of intervention.
The overall objective of the project is to foster a community-based approach for the protection of vulnerable women and children by contributing to the reduction of gender-based violence (GBV) through promoting socio-cultural norms that respect gender equality, through holistic care of survivors by promoting their recovery and resilience. Also, the empowerment of community members and groups in identifying, referring and responding to protection concerns they face by addressing vulnerabilities related to Child protection. The project will be based on an integrated approach based on the promotion and respect of women and girls' rights. Based on "Behavior Change Communication" approach, the project aims at initiating key changes in behaviors and socio-cultural norms in order to sustainably reduce GBV. Through awareness-raising campaigns and the active involvement of community leaders, and men as agents of change, communities are called upon to adopt attitudes that are respectful and promote gender equality. In addition, the needs of GBV survivors shall be addressed through the provision of holistic support (medical services, legal services, psychosocial support and economic empowerment) in order to mitigate the consequences of these violations and the vulnerability of survivors. Women and girls’ friendly spaces are key avenues to providing confidential medium for disclosure of GBV without the fear of stigmatization, they also provide survivors and other women an opportunity to build support networks through peer to peer activities and build on their recovery and resilience through activities that foster social interactions. It is imperative to engage women in the identification of protection risks they and their children are prone to as well as the risks linked to GBV and provide support in finding coping mechanisms to deal with them.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Protection issues: In the NWSW, the violence between the Non- State Armed Groups (NSAGs) and the State Forces has resulted in several protection concerns such as summary killings and arbitrary arrests, destruction of properties, population movements, family separation, lack of a protective environment in schools, child labor, children recruited into armed groups, sexual and other forms of gender-based violence against women and girls, and many other concerns which are all interwoven and connected to the general state of heightened insecurity. Child protection: With school-aged children and adolescents forced to stay at home due to the ongoing conflict and subsequently the COVID-19 outbreak, resultant effects are an increase in early pregnancies, forced and early marriages, survival sex, child labor, forced recruitment of adolescent boys into non-state armed groups, child abuse neglect and exploitation, unaccompanied and separated minors, and dangerous and negative behaviors and coping mechanisms where children’s rights are constantly abused. Sexual and gender-based violence: Women and children are at particular risk of facing incidents of Sexual and Gender-Based violence as well as abuse, neglect, and exploitation. INTERSOS Protection Monitoring has recorded a significant increase in SGBV since the beginning of the crisis. Cases of SGBV that are often reported by the women and girls are mainly rape, sexual exploitation, forced and early marriagesphysical and psychological violence, and harmful cultural practices. These have had an impact on the health and economic situation of women and girls, increasing their vulnerability. Girls and women who are heads of households as a result of the recruitment or radicalization of their husbands and sons engage in transactional sex to support their families and survive or in exchange for the freedom of their husbands and children. Some of the pregnant girls perform unsafe abortions that endanger their health. Survivors require lifesaving GBV response services to deal with the consequences of GBV and recover and capacity building to ensure self-protection through community-based protection mechanisms.
