Project Summary

The proposed intervention is intended to contribute towards humanitarian support to food insecure population in the intervention regions of Somalia. In line with food security priorities, the objective of the project is to improve immediate food access for the target population as well as support their livelihood. The proposed project plan is to address food insecurity mainly through cash transfer programming and support project beneficiary households through livelihood inputs and tailored skill development trainings. Although fishing and agro-pastoral livelihood from the intervention areas are envisaged to benefit from the proposed project, the bulk of the support will go to the pastoral livelihood.
Even though the last GU rains was relatively good and above average in part of the country, Somalia was faced with recurrent drought over the recent years that resulted in significant loss of livelihood for the people to benefit from the good rains. According to September 2018 FSNAU and FEWSNET technical release, food security situation in most part of the country improved partly because of the average and above average 2018 GU rainfall but the pastoral population remain in acute food insecure situation because they lost their livestock to drought in the previous year, became destitute and are now living among internally displaced families in camps. The spatial distribution of the last GU rains and the fact that some regions did not receive rains at all for more than two years are also factors under consideration. Despite relatively better seasonal rains of the last Gu’, there are parts of Puntland which are affected by drought due to poor performance of the rains and the impact of the prolonged drought over the past two years (humanitarian coordination meeting, October 18, 2018).
Adeso has active intervention in the regions proposed under this project and reports and ground need assessment from these region details livelihood challenges faced by these communities and the proposed intervention are recommendations to address these community felt needs. For instance in Sanaag, over 90% of assessed families have completely lost their entire livestock holding or have less than the livestock threshold to support the household food needs and as a result high level of debts ($2,000 to $5,000), Pastoral communities mainly in and around Gebi valley, Sool plateau and coastal belts reported limited pasture and therefore there are significant internal migration to the neighboring regions. In the same region, the dollar against Somali shilling is not stable. Three months ago one US dollar was going for Sshs 28000 and now the rate is 33000 Sshs for 1USD. While livestock birth is reported to be approximately 10% of goats and 5% for camels and since the drought situation is severe, gestating livestock are likely not to support their young ones after birth. These phenomenon is same or similar for Sool, Nugaal, Mudug and through north west of Galgadud regions and generally northern somalia regions. This situation is also echoed by FSNAU September technical release finding where 1.5 million people including close to 300,000 children are reported to be in food security crisis, somalia wide.
The coastal communities of Lasqoray, Ely, Jariban and Hobyo districts have reported availability of fish both in variety and in numbers but raised concerns with limited skill, fishing related resources like fishing gear, preparation, appropriate storage and transportation facilities. Internal Adeso reports and assessment also confirmed improve fish and fish product uptake in major cities like Garowe, Galkacyo and Cadado.
Pastoral communities in east and south east of Cadado district and Haradhere district are facing unique challenge. Early this year the pastoral communities clashed with Al-Shabaab authority that control part of the region and as a result these communities were forced to migrate to Hobyo district. According to inter agency coordination assessment report more than 4262 HH (25,572people), formerly pastoral were displaced are now in IDPs or have settled and resumed pastoral life in Hobyo district. The assessment team have also identified community based child care centre where over 300 unaccompanied children from displaced families are kept. The parent of these children have raised concerns after al-Shabaab requested the communities in the region to surrender their boys’ age between 9-14 years to be enrolled to Islamic school and later recruited to join the group military. Communities in the region declined to the request and instead sent their children to various other district including this community child care centre.
In Bay, Bakool and Gedo the food security challenges may be different but the humanitarian situation is the similar to that of northern somalia regions discussed above.
In Lower Juba
The proposed project interventions will include cash based response through conditional cash transfers but also unconditional to cater for the disabled, elderly and other physically challenged members of the community. Other intervention components includes livelihood support through provision skill development training, procurement and distribution of various livelihood inputs and income generating activities to boost small scale traders.
The proposed intervention will therefore target vulnerable households within the target communities chiefly pastoral households but also includes the disabled, minorities, female headed households and orphaned children and have been worst affected by the drought situation in the target regions. Adeso will use Inclusive Community Based Targeting (ICBT) approach to identify the most affected households within the target communities.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Adeso has other ongoing interventions in the proposed regions and location and is fully aware of the risk associated to displaced population. Understanding the presence of displaced people within the community and or in separate camps is key to proper planning and designing the correct approach towards exclusion risk. In addressing exclusion of the vulnerable, Adeso adapted an Inclusive Community Based Targeting (ICBT) approach which takes into account the different needs of minorities including displaced persons and marginalized community members that also fit the beneficiary selection criteria. During the formation and community entry process Adeso will ensure members from the minority and displaced person will be part of the village committees that are task to facilitate project beneficiary selection. In addition, Adeso uses Protection Mainstreaming checklist as a technical guidance, developed Somalia cluster to support Somalia Food Security Cluster (FSC) members to mainstream interventions in Somalia which was developed after a series of intensive consultations with Somalia FSC members; where food assistance, nutritional and livelihood activities be implemented in non-discriminatory and impartial ways that promote the safety, dignity and integrity of the people receiving assistance.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

By design and action Adeso recognizes gender mainstreaming in all programming. From the project design, planning through implementation the needs and concerns of men, women, girls and boys are taken into consideration with particular interest on the roles they play in food security and nutrition at the household and community levels. The participation of women and men considering gender balance in representation will be taken into consideration during selection and formation of food security village committees. Adeso will also be keen to address gender and power dynamics that may affect participation and decision making of women and the elderly representatives in the village committees. The project will also take into consideration the age diversity considering the varied food and nutrition needs of infants, children, adolescent girls and boys, elderly, pregnant and lactating mothers, for instance in selecting cash transfer beneficiaries. The project will be keen to ensure active participation of women as they are the main care givers and any support given to women will ensure that there are high chances of the targeted households benefiting more if the primary beneficiary is a woman. We are aware of the universal acceptance that humanitarian assistance must meet the distinct needs of women, girls, boys and men to generate positive and sustainable outcomes. Consequently, our key staff in program design and implementation has been trained on gender marker through UNOCHA Somalia. The proposed project are subjected to gender analysis and the assessed needs as well as the expected outcomes advances gender equality.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The project will also take a Do No Harm, gender equality and participatory approach ensuring that interventions and interactions are context specific and involve the community in all aspects of the project, before, during and after implementation thereby reducing risk of conflict over resources. Using mobile money modality for the cash transfers will also reduce risk of beneficiaries to insecurity, gender based violence and transport costs as they will not be traveling to collect their money. Instead cash is transferred directly to beneficiary phones. The design of this project will ensure that the introduction of cash does not exacerbate community tensions and relationships between beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries of assistance and monitoring of risks leads to program adjustments as necessary. If risks are envisaged, ADESO will create indirect benefits such as improving household nutrition or identifying opportunities for direct participation in complementary activities or services (i.e. spaces for women and girls or training for heads of households). This project will be linked to the protection project and cluster to ensure that challenges associated with protection are identified and addressed immediately.
