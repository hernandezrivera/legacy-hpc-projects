Project Summary

The Emergency Food Security and Vulnerable Livelihood (EFSVL) Rohingya Response Programme focuses to provide continued supports to the women, men, boys and girls of Rohingya & Host Community (HC) people for the live savings interventions (e.g. COVID-19 livelihood supports such as Unconditional Cash Grant; Cloth Masks & cloth Sanitary Napkins production, complementary food supports to host community farmer’s market initiative; enhancing DRR resilience linkages to the food security; self-reliance and sustainable livelihood opportunity etc.) to improve access to adequate diversified food access and livelihood opportunity. In addition, EFSVL project will be directed on Homestead Garden & Pit Composting; rural infrastructure through Cash for Work (CFW) approach; entrepreneurship and small trade business through Income Generating Activities (IGAs) and Vocational Training (VT) for the HC people through a market-based livelihood opportunity. Considering current COVID-19 Pandemic, needs of food security & livelihood supports as well as Government policy stands for restriction on direct cash injection in the Rohingya camps, Oxfam GB prefers to do self-reliance activities for the Rohingya camps and COVID-19 livelihood activities (e.g. cloth masks and sanitary napkins production etc.) for the women, men boys and girls of Rohingya community. The proposed interventions will contribute to Rohingya and HC people for strengthen the relation and social cohesion aspect in camp and host community in Cox’s Bazar to live with good health, dignity and better safety.
With Oxfam’s global commitment to build capacity of the local organization through a partnership approach, Oxfam’s EFSVL sector will continue to work with local partners as part of its programme strategy. As part of the transition process, Oxfam EFSVL sector has developed the “Rohingya Response Planning Framework” for two years 2020 & 2021 which have been revised considering the COVID-19 Pandemic situation in Bangladesh as a guide for the Rohingya Response Crises in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh.

1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar District remains one of the poorest in Bangladesh, making it highly susceptible to environmental and economic shocks such as cyclones, monsoons, and COVID19/health pandemics. The pandemic and its subsequent containment measures impacted food security and livelihoods, exacerbating existing poverty in the host communities (ISCG 04/2020; WFP 04/2020; WFP 05/2020; BBS, 2011). Emergency Food Security: Access to fresh and nutritious food continues to be an obstacle for the refugee population residing in camps. Based on the findings of the WFP REVA April 2020, level of vulnerability remains high: 94 percent of Rohingya refugees are highly and moderately vulnerable and in continues need of humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs. Multiple factors continue to drive high vulnerability including limited livelihood opportunities, restrictions on movement to search for income opportunities, depleted savings and asset stripping. Increasingly, Rohingya refugees are adopting unsustainable coping mechanisms, which exacerbate vulnerability (REVA 2019). Dietary diversity remains low; and whilst basic food needs are being met by the WFP general food basket, access to other types of food (dairy, meat, fruits and vegetables) remains limited. Before COVID19, 94% of Rohingya refugees were considered highly or moderately vulnerable and in need of humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs (WFP, April 2020). The limited income generation activities available pre-COVID19 have been disrupted by movement restrictions, market closures, and reduced humanitarian-led income generating activities (e.g. participating in CFW programme). Due to the COVID19 outbreak both Rohingya refugees & host community households reported having had to adopt food-based coping mechanisms in the 7 days prior to data collection due to a lack of food respectively approximately 71% Rohingya refugees and 76% HC households. Proximately 93% of households reported diminished income/loss of income source as an impact of the COVID19 outbreak in the HC areas (J-MSNA, July-August 2020). Gender norms place the burden on women and girls for care work, including food preparation, but they are the last to eat in a family (Oxfam, 2018 Gender Analysis). The majority of refugees incur debt for food or health needs with an increasing trend from 35% in 2018, 45% in 2019 and 69% in 2020. A wide gender gap is seen for those who do have access to stipend or income: 27% of men have access to income and only 2% of women do - despite women having an interest to earn their own income (Oxfam, 2018 Gender Analysis). Cox's Bazar Rapid Gender Analysis also found that confinement, a rise in tensions and restrictions on services and access for humanitarian workers will increase levels of gender-based violence (GBV), child abuse and neglect and sexual exploitation and abuse.Similarly, in May 2019, Oxfam conducted a study on the efficiency of aid (re-sell of in - kind food aid). The preliminary findings of the study show that few beneficiaries after receiving vouchers do not retrieve them from the shops and instead, sell the vouchers to middlemen or vendors to access additional money to purchase other items. On the other hand, currently, host communities gain little benefit from the increased demand that came with the refugees. Most rhe food providers come from areas that are away from the camps. From several social cohesion studies, Oxfam has undertaken around the relationship between refugee population and host communities. It shows clearly that restoration of livelihoods is an important demand expressed by the host communities.

2 - Activities or outputs

A1. "Multiple Purpose with Value Voucher: Rohingya Refugees can have access to fulfil their needs in addition to FFV (ongoing system) and WFP Food Baskets. This Voucher Programme will include additional basic needs (e.g. Food Items; Non-Food-Items) for the household consumption and other purposes (e.g. shelter items, NFIs, wash, transport etc.) for the Rohingya Refugees.A2. Homestead Gardening & Pit Composting (HC & Rohingya Refugees): Under this homestead garden, pit & Vermi composting for the HC people to increase their diversified nutritious fresh vegetable at the household level. HC farmers may sell their productivity from their gardens to have more income opportunity. This approach will help with sustainability of the farmer’s trainings to build their skills and knowledge on homestead gardening and pit / vermi composting. A3. Income Generating Activities (IGAs): entrepreneurship business development interventions for the HC, to enhance their skills and competences to increase income opportunity/options by taking different small or trade business through income generating activities (IGAs). Oxfam provides skilled developed trainings and necessary input supports (e.g. cash grant; input supports) to the target beneficiaries. A4. Vocational Training (VT): Skilled developed entrepreneurship for the beneficiaries for self-employment in the HC area ensures market linkages are anticipated. This will be in close coordination and liaison with the government line department (e.g. Youth department; Livestock) to receive their technical guidelines and support. 5. Dried Fish Livelihoods Programming: Oxfam conducted a value chain assessment of dried fish (Shutki) in December 2018 and recommendations of that assessment, which were: (a) Increase production of shukti and strengthen link with markets (b) Improve access, availability and variety of market level priced dried fish in refugee settlements and host communities & (c) Increase capacity and quality Shutki wholesale tradeA6. Support existing small and medium producers through skills training and cash grant inputs in the HCA7. Establish existing small and medium shutki production cooperative (e.g. trainings; inputs support) for women in/near Rohingya settlementA8. Cash for Work (CFW): Improvement of rural development infrastructure through the CFW Schemes in collaboration with the local community and government bodies like Union Parishad-UP to improve community assets. These CFW Schemes include new road construction; repairing of school/mosques/any community center and its playground and other construction (e.g. Bamboo bridges; ring culverts).A9. Village Saving and Loan Association (VSLA): Oxfam in coordination with local partners will initiate this approach among the target beneficiaries under the livelihood project (e.g. small business/ entrepreneurship) to establish a group of people who save together and can take loans from those savings. A10. Literacy and Numeracy Functional Class: To increase knowledge on basic business management to ensure they can read and write of their book keeping record, daily transaction; monthly cash injection and business profit and losses. A11. Special Attention on Self-reliance activities for Rohingya Refugees (e.g. skilled based training such as food processing; homestead gardening; tailoring; handicraft etc.)A12. COVID19 emergency livelihood and self-reliance activities (e.g. masking cloth masks and sanitary napkin pads etc.) through increasing skills and capacity of the women and adolescent and other vulnerable groups in both Rohingya and host communities in close coordination with Livelihood Working Groups (LHWG) and other Government representatives (e.g. CiC; UNO; UP etc.).A13. Waste management control to be used as a manure for agricultural cultivation. A14. Strengthen Sustainable natural Resource ManagementA15. Contingency plan for the emergency operation (e.g. NFT; Complementary food packages, Unconditional cash supports etc.)
