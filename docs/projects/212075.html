Project Summary

Families and children in Syria are sinking deeper into poverty, facing immense challenges in making ends meet. Over 13 years of conflict have worsened the situation, marked by armed conflict, loss of human capital, economic downturn, water crisis, food insecurity, impacts of the Ukraine-Russia war, and health emergencies like Covid-19 and cholera. The aftermath of the February earthquake adds to the devastation
The humanitarian crisis continues to worsen, with an estimated 16.7 million people needing aid in 2024. Syria has plummeted 42 places on the Human Development Index, its economy regressing to mid-1990s levels, and child development indicators falling back over three decades. Poverty is rampant, with critical infrastructure on the brink of collapse. UNICEF's State Budget Brief 2023 highlights the decline in public expenditures, now less than one-quarter of pre-conflict levels. The 2023 budget is a mere USD 5.88 billion or USD 256 per capita, significantly lower than before. Foreign aid, especially in the health sector, surpasses government allocations, four times higher than those for water and sanitation. In urban and peri-urban slums of Syria, especially those headed by females or caring for orphaned children or internally displaced persons (IDPs), cash assistance is crucial for meeting basic needs. Seasonal peaks like winter and the start of the school year escalate financial strain. UNICEF survey underscores the socio-economic vulnerability of female-headed households, often relying on charity or remittances. To address this, UNICEF will implement targeted cash assistance and protection measures, aiming to improve well-being and resilience while enhancing stakeholder participation
The program outputs include 1) Targeted Cash Assistance: A Structured schedule and selection criteria for cash assistance to families affected by the earthquake, female-headed households with children, and families of children facing heightened health-related financial burdens, in NES/DEZ, Aleppo, Hama, Homs, Lattakia, AS Swidaa, AL Qunitera, and Rural Damascus. 2) Strengthened Community Empowerment: The response will utilize a categorical-geographical targeting strategy, focuses on impoverished urban and peri-urban areas, particularly those severely affected by the crisis, natural disasters and climate change impact, e,i droughts, floods, and fire accidents
Targeted Beneficiaries for 2024: A total of 235,000 individuals will be assisted. Of this group, 32,341 families extremely affected by earthquake in Hama, Lattakia and Aleppo will receive their second cash installment. Additionally, UNICEF will provide MPC assistance to 21,595 individuals/ 4,319HHs in Homs and Rural Damascus, each receiving three rounds of cash totaling $180 during the winter season spanning 2023-2024. The remaining 51,700 individuals / 10340 HHs will benefit from a humanitarian cash transfer program designed to meet their basic needs. These families will receive four Qs payments of $90 each, along with supplementary top-us for children’s additional expenses for each school-aged child and/or children with chronic illnesses
Efforts will be amplified to enhance stakeholder engagement in pinpointing the specific communities in need. This will involve empowering civil society organizations to effectively convey the eligibility criteria and selection procedures to the target audience
Coordination and Accountability The Prog aligns with MPC interventions in Syria. UNICEF coordinates with CWG for targeting and TV harmonization.Also, beneficiary accountability will be considered via redress, PDMS, and onsite monitoring
Risk Analysis and Mitigation: Cash assistance programs pose various risks, like social tensions, abuse by implementing partners, and protection risks during distribution. UNICEF's PSEA strategy combats sexual exploitation and abuse among aid workers, co-chairing the inter-agency network to enhance networking, identify risks, and preserve affected people's dignity

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Identification and registration: (Locations and communities) special considerations: Unicef and its partner will reach out to the initially targeted communities and identify the priorities through (i) situation analysis reports that can support the identification of the communities (ii) the use of the CSOs / sectors and HNO database (iii) a communication campaign with stakeholders and (iv) coordination with key informants. Also, UNICEF will use this program as a shock-responsive social protection platform, in addition to the other contracts with banks and financial services providers in affected areas. UNICEF will use HOPE “ Humanitarian cash Operations and Programme Ecosystem “ for a humanitarian cash transfer management information system, to collect beneficiary data and associate data with cash programs, manage payment lists and send them to Financial Service Providers (FSP), reconcile payments, triangulate payment verification information directly from beneficiaries and handle grievances and feedback, as well as reporting. HOPE is flexible and can use either UNICEF-collected beneficiary data using KoBo Collect, or data collected by other partners including UN agencies. Risks Assumption: The overall socio-economic situation in the country continues to deteriorate, increasing the needs of targeted populations. Risk #1: Delays in obtaining official approvals can delay implementation likelihood (Medium)Mitigation measures#1: (i) development of standardized agreements with the IPs across targeted governorates to ensure speedy review; (ii) establish good communication channels with MoSAL officials to ensure all challenges are shared and addressed promptly.Risk #2: A high level of inflation limits the effectiveness of the cash component. likelihood (High)Mitigation measures #2: (i) negotiate with the Central Bank a preferential exchange rate; (ii) adjust transfer values on inflation. Risk #3: Delay in the process of cash distribution to beneficiaries due to the limited absorption capacity of the FSPs likelihood (High)Mitigation measures #3: Expansion of the FSPs as feasible Risk #4: Difficulty in obtaining data of eligible families as per the targeting criteria Likelihood (high)Mitigation measures# 4: Conduct door-to-door visits by the field monitors of IPs in the targeted locations.Risk #5: Financial ceiling prevents scale up of HCT, Transfer of risks FSP through the reimbursement modality means that FSP imposes a ceiling of the maximum money it can advance at any given time. This creates ineffectiveness and inefficiencies in the management of HCT and delays payment to beneficiaries, Mitigation measures# 5 (i) Develop and implement solid risk management practices to assure that risks related to the advance modality can be managed, (ii) Authorize the advance modality for all Banks and FSP, (iii) Define and agree with FSP standard payment time for each list.
