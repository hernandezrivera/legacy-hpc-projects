Project Summary

This proposal is in line with the objectives and the framework of the OPTI country 2021 HRP strategy under objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL. Furthermore, this project is also aligned to the WASH cluster HRP objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation.
The overall objective is to contribute to strengthening the capacity of essential WASH services for the vulnerable population threatened by the ongoing Covid-19 crisis in the West Bank, while the specific objective of the project is to provide an appropriate, integrated WASH response for Deir al 'Asal al Fauqa vulnerable village located in area C of Dura community in the West Bank to mitigate the ongoing Covid-19 crisis targeting women, children, girls and boys in fragile and critically under-served communities. The objectives are in line with the needs identified in the humanitarian need overview (HNO) and Oxfam’s recent assessments.

1 - Needs

In the West Bank, the restrictive planning regime enforced by the Israeli authorities in Area C and East Jerusalem continued to prevent the connection of Palestinian communities to water and sewage networks, as well as the deployment of rain-harvesting cisterns and mobile latrines. Between January and September 2020, 33 WASH-related incidents were recorded due to the lack of building permits, which are nearly impossible to obtain from the Israeli authorities. Additionally, many communities which are connected to the water network are poorly supplied, due to the limited amounts of water available to Palestinians as a result of Israel’s unequitable exploitation of the shared-water resources. The COVID -19 outbreak and its associated lockdowns, compounded by the growing PA budget deficit and suspension of salaries for government employees, led to the erosion of WASH coping mechanism by vulnerable households, alongside the adoption of negative practices, such as reducing water consumption and consuming water from unsafe sources. Due to the aforementioned factors, it is estimated that nearly 158,950 Palestinians in the West Bank, reside in areas that are either not connected to piped water services or are very poorly supplied; consequently, they consume less than 35 L of water per day per capit a. While, nearly 396,000 households in the West Bank are not connected to any sewer system and discharge their sewage in adjacent streams and open areas, creating health and environmental hazards to downstream communities. This has undermined the hygiene standards of affected families, which are critical to prevent the transmission of COVID-19, as well as the living conditions of families depending on herding and agricultural livelihoods, primarily in Area C. In Sep 2020, a disaster has occurred in Deir al ‘Asal al Fauqa (population is 3,500 inhabitants) which is a vulnerable village in area C of Dura community when six young men died in a sewage absorption pit due to the deteriorated sanitation system in the village. Based on the needs highlighted above, Oxfam’s Covid-19 Response Plan, WASH cluster Vulnerability Map conducted in Aug 2020 and coordination efforts with the WASH cluster and Oxfam’s local partner ESDC, it was agreed to prioritize Deir al ‘Asal al Fauqa vulnerable village under this intervention. Accordingly, a rapid assessment was conducted by Oxfam and its local partner ESDC during the 1st week of October 2020 to identify the specific needs of the village, where, two FGDs and 2 KII were carried out in the targeted community with the participation of 20 persons (4 women and 16 men). The assessment revealed that more than 30% are classified below poverty line, 25% are not connected with the water network and the water price is 18 ILS/m3. The average water consumption is less than 30 L/c/d. People depend on cesspits due to the lack of a sewage network as the installation of sewerage networks is faced with great difficulties and restrictions by the occupation and the land topography. Moreover, participants confirmed that more that 90% of the current absorption pits are opened, detartrated and constructed randomly without considering any health or safety concerns. The assessment also found that the majority of population have poor hygiene and safety practices and lack COVID-19 awareness which increases the risk of COVID-19 infection. Moreover, the assessment indicated that women are mostly affected by the lack of access to safe water and sanitation services, reporting high concerns about their children’s hygiene, health and safety. WASH needs were prioritized to improve adequate water and sanitation services including proper sewage disposal and water network through sustainable, environmental, and low-cost solution addressing the current need in the targeted community.

2 - Activities

This project will respond to the needs of targeted male and female beneficiaries in the targeted area including children, elderly and people with disabilities as the construction of transmission pipelines and extension of water networks and HH connections that will be implemented will improve access of targeted beneficiaries to sufficient quantity of water needed for cooking, personal and domestic hygiene and will contribute to alleviating the burden on women & children. While the construction of the cesspits will contribute to alleviating the burden on women and children and mitigate the transmission of COVID-19 outbreak. The complementary hygiene promotion & public awareness campaigns will enhance gender & age responsiveness by enhancing women’s knowledge & awareness related to COVID-19 specifically. The main humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration especially the do-no-harm principle. This is particularly important in the area C context, where social and political instability and conflict are evident in many areas with conservative protection norms. Explicit plans to reduce risks, and to competently mitigate and respond to any incidents, including safeguarding training (i.e. on sexual harassment, discrimination and exploitation, and child protection) will be elaborated by Oxfam and monitored, adjusted, refreshed if the context changes. Oxfam will be responsible for the implementation of the action in coordination with ESDC and in collaboration with the relevant stakeholders to ensure access to systems, procedures and standards are in place to achieve effective, high quality delivery. Oxfam’s particular added value will be in its ability to bring together multiple stakeholders, local authorities, end users through it is active participation in the WASH cluster, SAG working group and engagement with related stakeholders, ensure delivering of the action in accordance to the agreed upon project action plan and budget in compliance to the donor programmatic, visibility, reporting, financial and procurement guidelines. In addition, Oxfam will provide advice and support on gender and protection sensitive project activities and gender mainstreaming, Oxfam will also support the ESDC in designing and implementing the gender and hygiene promotion plan. ESDC will lead the implementation of the community mobilization actions, the beneficiaries’ selection process, the engineering technical assessment and announce the tenders in the local newspapers in compliance with the donor, Oxfam and their organization’s procurement regulations and guidelines.ESDC work closely with vulnerable communities in diverse locations including farmers, women, children & youth. They have extensive experience in WASH and access to sustainable & safe water, sanitation services in the West Bank and Gaza. ESDC was fully engaged in the project’s design, needs assessment, & community consultations and will implement the project in coordination with Oxfam with joint responsibility for M&E activities & stakeholder engagement. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection principles are considered in the project as part of the humanitarian principles governing Oxfam’s humanitarian interventions. Oxfam premised its project design on the ‘do no harm approach’ ensuring that project activities or any resulting consequences do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to the essential WASH needs which would have put them at risk, had it not been available, and by conducting ongoing monitoring and collecting beneficiaries’ feedback to be aware of any potential harm and work on prevention measures. The action responds to Oxfam’s and PWA’s needs analysis where women highlighted the need to be engaged in community committees and municipality activities due to their severe lack of representation in public life. Community mobilization sessions on gender, water and sanitation will be implemented encouraging participation of men and women community leaders and mentors. These sessions will also serve as awareness raising tool to sensitize households and community to accept women participation. This will contribute to mitigate any risk/ harm that women could face as a result of this newfound participation in public life. Gender will be mainstreamed, ensuring that the needs of men and women, girls and boys are addressed in line with Oxfam’s gender in emergencies minimum standards and gender mainstreaming strategies. This action’s response is grounded on the fact that the lives and dignity of all women and men beneficiaries including people with disabilities, youth, children and elderly, both direct and indirect, must be protected. Key humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration especially the do-no-harm principle, particularly important in Gaza’s context, where social and political instability and conflict are evident in many areas with conservative gender norms. Local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement and ongoing consultation with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the project, thus enhancing community engagement and reducing any potential protection risks or exclusion at community level. Building the capacities of targeted communities to improve their self-protection mechanisms will be ensured through conducting orientation sessions to inform and consult with targeted communities about services that will be provided as well as on safety measures and access modalities. It is worth mentioning that preserving dignity is essential to maintain self-esteem and confidence, important to cope in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence will be positively reflected and will enhance the roles of each member of the household and will result in the overall improved wellbeing of the family, especially children as care receivers.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As part of the main principles of protection mainstreaming in all of Oxfam’s humanitarian work, special attention is paid to achieve equal and impartial access to aid. This will be done throughout the different stages of the project; the selection of targeted communities is premised on concrete and updated needs assessments confirming the vulnerability and the need of the intervention. Similarly, selecting the beneficiaries follows a clear process with selection criteria and presents equal opportunity for all eligible to benefit. Gender and age are also taken into consideration meaning that women, men, youth, elderly people, people with disabilities and children will be able to benefit from the intervention as fit for their needs without any discrimination based on their gender, age or status. Using a combination of community outreach approach, using virtual platforms for information sharing and actual work in the field will also work to address any obstacles related to the spread of COVID 19 that could hinder access to aid. Having a feedback mechanism available and accessible for all the targeted community will facilitate knowing people’s concerns and comments that could highlight obstacles of access whether physical or related to the processes of implementing the intervention and would inform response to ensure equality and impartiality.
