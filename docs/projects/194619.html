Project Summary

Emerging from decades of conflict which eroded physical infrastructure, social fabric and caused high level of vulnerabilities. South Sudan’s health system is still facing challenges to provide essential life-saving health services. Although the government is obliged to provide basic services this is limited with the current actual expenditure not in line with the increased budget allocation from 1.9 percent in 2020/2021 to 7.9 percent in 2021/2022 national budget allocations (HNO, 2022). The country still bears some of the worst health indicators as maternal mortality ratio is over 1,150 per 100,000 live births – one of the highest maternal mortality ratios in the world (MMEIG, 2017); WHO estimates that low birthweight contributes 5% of total deaths and about 40% of the under-five deaths occur during the first month of life reflects the high risks associated with delivery and immediate post delivery period. Malaria, diarrhea, and pneumonia are responsible for over 75% of deaths among under-fives- the burden for malaria is over 50% and a major contributor to the high burden of malnutrition among children in the country. According to HNO 2022, 62% of the households could not access health care in past the 3 months due to distance, cost, insecurity, disability, and ill equipped and staffed health facilities. This is compounded by the significant reduction in health pool fund by 24% negatively affecting health service delivery in at least 220 facilities across the country. In addition, SS has reported outbreaks of cholera, malaria, measles, with alerts issued for Ebola.
The national health situation corroborates Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) that entails Pibor/Boma. Existing health infrastructures including cold chain rooms are old and dilapidated. Poor geographical access cuts across people residing in settlements further than 5- 9km from functional health facilities and most of the Bomas are still not having Community health system. JAM endeavors to Provide essential health care assistance to 20,000 (10755 females and 9245 men); in terms of the age Sex, age, and disability disaggregated (SADDD) - 4142 (2041 boys and 2100 girls below the age of 5 years); 5270 children and adolescent (between 6 -17 years of age that includes 3153 girls and 2117 boys) will be reached. 10,588 adults and elderly above the age of 18years will be supported and this includes 15% PWD. In line with the Strategic Objective 1, 2 and 3 and anchored to health cluster sectoral objectives 1,2 and 3. Health education, promotion and risk communication during disease outbreaks will be supported; through existing capacities and services the target is to reach 14,000 individuals female and, that includes youths, adult (men and women) PWD and elderly. During Supplementary Immunization excises (SIA), that includes measles and other VPD (Vaccine Preventable Disease) campaigns, JAM will ensure these activities are conducted in area of response, including training of staff and transportation of the vaccine Antigens. There will be Surveillance and IDSR reporting and essential services will be providing through four (1) static Primary health care units (PHCU) and 2 mobile units. Through engagement of qualified staff, clinical Consultation services to 20,000 beneficiaries of the total population 121,034; this includes 21% (4142) of children ≤ 5 years (2041 boys and 2100 girls) will access curative services. The plan is to reach 2096 IDP and 1661 returnees within the project location. In Maternal child health, One PHCU Plus will be established with an aim of reaching out Pregnant and Lactating women (10%) of the target 20,000 that is 2000 individual; Trained mid-wife will be engaged who will provide both ANC and PNC care, support abrupt deliveries and also train MCHW. RH supplies will be access through UNFPA. In MHPSS and SGBV, staff will be trained on basic psychosocial first aid and use of existing referral pathway.

1 - Needs

The country’s health system and infrastructure are among the poorest in the world. Health facilities are poorly equipped and staffed. There is a severe shortage of trained health professionals in all categories. According to Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO, 2022), Only 49% per cent of the population can access health facilities in one hour’s time. While 66 per cent of the population has difficulty in accessing health services, women and girls specifically faced additional barriers. Inadequate health workers, limited supplies, long waiting hours, water, maternal health services and lack of follow up care were reported. Pibor County faces significant challenges on basic services, existing health infrastructures including cold chain rooms are old and dilapidated and the maintenance are partially done. The cold chain fridges have not been replaced in some facilities after destruction in (2019-2020 Intercommunal conflict) having an impact on access to routine vaccines. Due to vastness within this counties, Poor geographical access cuts across people residing in settlements further than 5- 9km from functional health facilities and the Bomas are still not having Community health system. Among the greatest challenges is the poor health status of the population experiences regular outbreaks of communicable diseases such as measles. Malaria is endemic across and its accounting for almost a quarter (24.7%) of all diagnosis reported by health facilities.The Infant mortality is high at 60 deaths /1000 live births, and children are susceptible to outbreaks of vaccine preventable disease (VPD) due to low vaccine coverage. In the years 2021 Expanded Program for Immunization (EPI) only 21% (2416) of the eligible children 9 – 11 months of the age received (2,416 children Penta 3 and 29% (3391 measles vaccine). Maternal and neonatal services are under- utilized, with Proportion of ANC mothers who made at least 4 antenatal visits during pregnancy is approximately 18% (2021 DHIS 2); No partners implementing HIV/AIDs and PMTCT, however CHD office provides treatment to clients who are enrolled in the program. The Proportion of births attended by skilled health worker is at 21% of. Through the constructed of 2 theatre wing, to support basic emergency maternal and neonatal care (BEMONCC) or other obstetric 4% was proportion of live births performed by caesarean section but still no proper maternal audit mechanism in the county.GBV and violence against women is widely spread because the culture normalize gender based violence and violence against women and girls. it was noted that many women can only bring children to the facilities when they have been permitted by their husbands.There is need to provide array of services to meet the basic package in most of the areas, these includes upgrading primary health care center with proper MISP that includes Obstetric and emergency services with clear referral system in Kaziongora Payam; laboratory investigation; mental health and psychosocial support, HIV and TB services within the locality. Provision of essential clinical package through mobile in order to reach out the underserved communities. Recruit qualify staff including trained mid-wife; and preposition of Medical commodities and supplies. The component, behavior change communication, and awareness creation to address conditions of public health concerns remains key. There is also need to scale-up stabilization Centre services especially in hugely populated Payams with higher rate of malnutrition like Kaziongora and Gumuruk. The need to respond to SGBV survivors clinical package and MPHSS, starting from basic psychosocial first aid and establishing community based structures and intervention.Community participation and involvement in designing, implementation, monitoring and feedback to address health related concern, including gender responsive services, is paramount.

There will be training and capacity building on vegetable production and food utilization to caretakers/caregivers of children . The project intends to integrate fish harvesting activities that can increase protein intakes and diet diversity. Provide cooking demonstration using locally produced crops and involving women and men Through health and nutrition education, behavior change communication it will sensitize the community to ensure that food production is consumed at the household level and thus assisting the Agriculture FSL sector in meeting the objectives. The project will also refer beneficiaries to access foods that are energy, protein or micronutrient-rich, including fortified complementary foods. It is expected that through the integrated effort there will be decrease proportion of populations facing IPC 4 and 5.
