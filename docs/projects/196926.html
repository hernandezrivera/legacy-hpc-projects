Project Summary

The proposed action, in line with identified priorities and taking advantage of the field experience of the health actors in the consortium, aims to target in-camp and out-of-camp high-need/neglected areas with a comprehensive package of primary health care services, specialized assistance for PWDs, health waste management, and community outreach. A coordinated and integrated approach will enhance efficiency and effectiveness in reaching vulnerable populations, ensuring continuity of service to key facilities already supported by the ongoing phase four consortium project. Additionally, looking at continuing with quality response through better awareness in the community, augmented accessibility, and the offer of a wider portfolio of inclusive PHC, HWM, referrals and protection services that ensure a holistic response to high-priority health needs. Specific Aim 1: Provision of live-saving and specialized health assistance to targeted populations in North East of Syria, with a focus on the most vulnerable groups Specific Aim 2: Improve sanitation and hygiene in target healthcare facilities by providing comprehensive and safe WASH monitoring and health waste management services to minimize environmental risks and promote a do no harm approach.

3 - Needs

Over 14.6 million people are in need across Syria in 2022, the highest number since the start of the conflict almost 12 years ago. 12.2 million people need lifesaving health services, including those with poor physical and mental health and with limited access to health services. July 2022 findings highlight the ongoing impact of Northeast Syria’s (NES) economic crisis on access to livelihoods, water, healthcare, and food. Economic insecurity affected people’s ability to meet basic needs and forced them to engage in negative coping strategies. Syria is affected by a severe and long-term drought, with poor vegetation conditions reflecting the persistence of drier-than-normal precipitation seasons. Water deficits have been exacerbated by unusually dry conditions during the rainy season (October–May) and by abnormally high temperatures . Water scarcity has affected crops and agricultural livelihoods, worsening access to food and contributing to driving up the price of food and commodities, as consequence increasing rate of malnutrition. Financial barriers, especially when coupled with breaks in supply chains for essential medicines, are barriers to health access. With ratios of functional health facilities to a population far below emergency standards, patients face transport costs and insecure travel routes to reach care services. There has been an alarming increase in the PwD, and a lack of specialized rehabilitation services for these vulnerable people.

4 - Implementation of activities

Activity 1.1: Comprehensive financial support to targeted HCFs for the provision of Life-saving and Primary Health Care assistance.The proposed action will include full support to As-Suwar PHCC, Shaddadi PHCC, Al Hol Camp Phase 4 and Washokani. Partners will ensure the provision of the following services: Reproductive Health care, Paediatric care and Internal, Level 1 Emergency health care, Community Health Outreach Activities, Life-assistance through ambulances for urgent delivery and maternal care, Referral and transport to hospitals for stabilized cases, EWARS for epidemiological surveillance and response, Integrated health waste management system and Laboratory. Activity 1.2: Provision of pharmaceutical and medical commodities. UPP will procure essential, lifesaving pharmaceuticals and medical commodities for the target PHCCs.- Activity 1.3: Capacity Building and on-the-job coaching. The NGO will continue the capacity-building efforts delivered during the previous programs to ensure the needed transferable skills and technical capacity is in place to continue services even with a gradual and guided exit strategy. A.1.4 Protection Mainstreaming to ensure humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, dignified and accessible manner.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Limited access to services, especially for vulnerable groups identified (women, children, persons with specific needs, PWD, foreign families). Mitigating measures: Carry out safety audits and community consultations for safe beneficiaries’ access to site locations, including appropriate gender separate areas, confidential spaces, lack of physical barriers; Anti discrimination measures include staff training and sensitization, coordination with PwG and advocacy on a local level; Staff recruitment/composition will be culturally and gender sensitive; Staff training in outreach and prevention will increase access to services for women and girls; Staff training and dedicated trained staff for referral to protection specialized staff for case management; Outreach activities and awareness campaigns free and available to all services, including outreach and consultation with most vulnerable groups. 2: Risk of Sexual Exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers in activities including in clinics, mobile units and in the community. Mitigating measures: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism; Train staff on code of conduct and PSEA mechanisms; Increase awareness among communities about PSEA and the complaints mechanism; Deploy Protection specialized staff to monitor protection standards including do-no-harm, ensuring gender representation amongst staff; Carry out safety audits for safe beneficiaries’ access to site locations, including appropriate gender separate areas.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The NGO and implementing partner staff are trained on Humanitarian Principles and all staff members have signed the CoC. Moreover, trainings on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) are conducted on a regular basis.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The assumption behind the Action is that context situation allows supported HFs and ambulances to be operative 24/7 to provide life-saving, PHC and emergency referrals to the war-affected population. Main risks are: - Further escalation of violence between SDF and GoT, and increased waves of population displacement (high risk); - ISIS resurgence and regained control over areas or infiltrates carrying out attacks (high risk); - Lack of access and difficulties to provide services and supplies for implementing partner; difficulty to monitor the activities in case of remote management (medium risk); - Cross-border INGOs evacuate expat staff and therefore are more limited in their capacity to respond while active hostilities are ongoing (high risk); - Presence of Covid-19 cases in NES, inducing loss of humanitarian access, loss of part of the health workforce, evacuation or quarantine of UPP and impl. partner's non-medical staff; shortages of medical supplies; shortages of pharmaceuticals; - Turkish forces expand “Peace Spring” operational theater eastward and westward to counter Kurdish presence in NES. Mitigation strategies include flexible health structures to be relocated according to conflict scenarios. Strengthening of all COVID-19 measures already in place with particular focus on IPC. maintaining access also unstable areas and negotiate access to accessible western territories with relevant authorities and military councils. Move to remote management modalities.
