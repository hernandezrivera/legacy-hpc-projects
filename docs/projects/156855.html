Project Summary

Non-communicable diseases remain among the most prevalent health issues in Syria. WHO estimates that approximately 12 percent of the population has diabetes and 20 percent have hypertension. Estimates for the prevalence of chronic respiratory diseases and cancers together is around 6% while reliable data on the prevalence of thyroid disease, thalassemia, and other less common NCDs is not available.
SAMS in collaboration with Idleb Health Directorate launched this unit in November 2018 with the goal of targeting and treating breast cancer and Lymphoma patients in Northern Western Syria. The Oncology unit provides services under the supervision of cancer treatment specialists ranging from chemotherapy and the provision of medical consulting services for cancer patients.
This project aims to provide cancer treatment to Syrian local and displaced Breast and Lymphoma cancer patients located in North West Syria. This will be achieved through the provision of cancer treatment, consultations and supplies to the Oncology Unit in addition to supporting the unit and its staff with operational cost and salaries.

Needs

Close to eight years into the crisis, the overall scale, and complexity of humanitarian needs of people in Syria remain staggering in terms of magnitude and severity with an estimated 13.2 million people who are in need of health assistance in Syria. Females make up 72 percent of the PiN. Vulnerable groups include 5.2 million women of reproductive age, 2.2 million children under 5 years old, and 2.3 million people aged over 50, (HNO 2019). Across Syria health-related needs continue to be driven and exacerbated by insufficient access to healthcare due to a combination of damage and destruction of health facilities, insufficient human resources, and imposed access constraints. According to the WHO Surveillance System of Attacks on Healthcare (SSA), 137 targeted attacks on health care took place in Syria during the first 10 months of 2018, resulting in a reported 97 deaths and 169 injuries, (World Health Organization. Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care. See ssa.who.int for the Public Dashboard). September 2018 Health Resource Availability Monitoring System (HeRAMS) data indicated that across Syria 46 percent of health facilities were either non-functional or partially functional, with 167 facilities completely destroyed, and a further 419 facilities partially destroyed and in need of rehabilitation. The national ratio of fully functional Primary Healthcare Centres (PHCs) of 2.28 per 50,000 populations is above Sphere Standards, but the distribution is highly unequal, with 101 of 270 surveyed sub-districts reporting having no fully functional PHC, and an additional 9 sub-districts not meeting minimum standards. Of these services, Oncology hospitals and centers remain as one of the biggest gaps in the Health services provided. As per the Health Cluster’s Humanitarian Needs Overview estimation for the year 2019, Noncommunicable diseases including cancer patients remain among the most prevalent health issues in Syria. This is even evidenced with the unavailability and gap when it comes to accurately estimate the amount of cancer cases in NWS where WHO estimation only covers that approximately the prevalence of chronic respiratory diseases and cancers together is around 6% while reliable data on the prevalence of thyroid disease, thalassemia and other less common NCDs is not available According to the recent study titles ‘Cancer Care at Times of Crisis and War: The Syrian Example’, most commonly diagnosed malignancy in Syria in general as well as in the northwest is Breast cancer in women and the second leading cause of cancer death among women. In accordance with the study results, multiple essential diagnostic and treatment options were found inadequate or unavailable. Early detection is the single most important factor in surviving breast cancer because the less advanced the disease, the easier and less costly it is to treat. As indicated by the study results, magnetic resonance imaging remains either unavailable or limited to certain regions and, thus, could not be used to supplement traditional diagnostic procedures in identifying locoregional extensions in younger patients. On the other hand, and according to the Oncology records, Lymphoma still the highest reported cancer case among male patients and the second highest for female patients of which the Unit is targeting an average 12 cases of Lymphoma on a monthly basis.

Implementation of activities

The implementation of the project activities will take place in Idleb Central Hospital in Idleb District and will include the following: 1-Mapping and identifying the availability of qualified staff (Oncologist) within the region to provide the oncology consultation/ treatment in the Oncology Unit in Idleb Central Hospital;2-Provision of medical consultations to the extent of all types of cancer types3-Procuring the medications for breast cancer and lymphoma patients4-Provision of treatment to breast cancer patients and lymphoma patients including Hodgkin and Non-Hodgkin lymphoma patients (Chemotherapy)5-Procuring of Mammogram Device and locating it at the Oncology unit in Idleb Central Hospital6- Perform Routine screening of breast cancer for women over 40 years through Mammography7- Capacity building of the service providers through specialized oncology training by SAMS technical committee from the USA8- Organizing Self-care and Staff Care activities

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Field staff isn’t aware of PSEAMitigation Strategy: Training sessions for field team will be conducted Risk: No PSEA and Child safeguarding PoliciesMitigation Strategy: PSEA policies will be prepared and implement and awareness sessions will be conducted Risk: Breach of the confidentiality toward cases in the case managementMitigation Strategy: Very limited staff will work on the cases, and the cases will be coded and saved in a secured and protected database, GBV protocol will be followed and in place, Turkey project team will conduct orientation and awareness sessions for the responsible employees for case management in Syria on the cases security and how to save them securely so we will not be causing any harm to our beneficiaries

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Attack by GoS and its allies/othersMitigation Strategy: Selecting fortified locations - Security assessment (site & building) - Evacuation SOP - Sourcing alternative locations, in case of need for relocation. Risk: Potential turnover of staff Mitigation Measures: Repeating the training regularly to ensure that all new staff delivering it. - Adopt appropriate salary-scale to retain the staff as possible. Risk: Stakeholders’ poor collaborationMitigation Strategy: Increase the participation of the Health Directorates and the health partners, (regular meeting with the local councils, regular updates about the project in the health cluster and other meetings, activate the accountability mechanism). - Advocate for the project outcomes and goal. Risk: Interruption of Supplies and medications procurement due to the closure of Syrian-Turkish borders Mitigation Strategy: Procuring medication and supplies in bulk quantities as appropriate.
