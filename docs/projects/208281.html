Project Summary

Summary: This is an education project, aimed at ensuring that vulnerable girls and boys and youth affected by conflicts and other humanitarian crises across the 3 states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe in Northeast Nigeria. The project will provide the vulnerable children with inclusive, innovative and quality learning opportunities in a protective and safe learning environment that promotes the well-being and resilience of the most vulnerable groups, including girls, boys, internally displaced persons (IDPs), children with disabilities, out-of-school children, caregivers, and education personnel. The project will target 9,200 (60% girls) in and out of schoolboys and girls with formal and no-formal education services across the 3 states, focusing on IDPs, returnees, host community children, CWD etc. The project will support provision of cost effective education services through enhancement of enrolment of out of school children, provision of essential teaching learning materials, improvement of teaching and learning through capacity building and support to teachers, provision of flexible to ABEP learning programs, piloting the TaRL programs and piloting the distance or online learning programs

1 - Needs

This project will respond to the educational needs of the most marginalized children deprived of their rights to a quality and inclusive education services. In Nigeria, access continues to be challenging, and the fate of many children remains unknown due to access concerns. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2023), almost 50% (though Borno peaks with 60%) of all the schools in the Borno-Adamawa-Yobe (BAY) states have either been partially destroyed or rendered non-functional by conflicts or lack of investment in rehabilitation. Access to education for girls and boys of school-age (3-17) has been obstructed by a range of complex challenges, including poor and unsafe school infrastructure, lack of proper water and sanitation facilities, insecurity, attacks on educational facilities, shortage of teachers, lack of premises adapted for children with disabilities and other protection concerns. These challenges have varying effects on different population subsets, for instance, girls are likely to face the risk of early marriage, while boys may force to recruitment by armed groups. The crisis has exacerbated the situation for children with disabilities, who constitute 15% of the population (8% of whom are girls), depriving them of essential, needs-based educational services. In fact, 56 percent of all public primary classrooms are deemed inadequate and nearly 1,400 schools have been destroyed since the onset of the crisis in the North-East. The 2022 and 2023 flooding and climate change affected 132 schools depriving 50,490 girls, 48,510 boys and 9,000 children (4,860 girls) with disabilities of access to educational facilities. The Education Sector identified 1.55 million children aged 3-17, affected by crises need critical educational assistance. The projected decrease in funding in 2024 can lead to huge education response gap and potentially exacerbate child protection issues such as child labor, child marriage, early pregnancy, GBV and recruitment into armed groups. Furthermore, in the BAY states, an increasing number of children persistently encounter gender-based violence, including instances of sexual exploitation and abuse, particularly when they are denied access to education services. An estimated 24 per cent of the GBV survivors who sought assistance from GBV service providers were children, adolescents, young girls and boys, ranging from 1- 18 years of age. The assessment further reported that only 60 per cent of teachers had some sort of qualification (JENA2021). The learning environment and lack of qualified and skilled teachers, many of whom are reluctant to return because of insecurity, has a profound impact on both the quality of learning and children’s prospects for the future.

2 - Activities

Based on the above needs analysis, and the mere fact that the conflict has led many children to be out of school or gotten displaced, the project will implement interventions that will return the learners back to school. Community outreach and mobilization activities on sensitizing on the importance of education for all children (boys and girls) in the targeted LGAs. This will include back to school drives and enrollment for both formal and non-formal learners using the different media. This will help the communities to have a good understanding of value of educating girls and hence provide them the opportunity to go to school and promote their learning. In order to address the infrastructure needs, the project will rehabilitate and construct 5 TLS in targeted and learning centers. Since there is WASH needs in most schools in BAY region, the project will support construction and rehabilitation of latrines 5 latrines. The project will also support with simple rehabilitation of drinking water points. Hygiene, sanitation and safe environmental awareness sessions will be conducted for teachers, SBMCs and child clubs to ensure proper sanitation and hygiene management. The project will also pprovide dignity kits to 300 girls in supported schools and learning centres. This will help girls to practice safe and better sanitary practice with confidence and will help girls to comfortably stay in school. The project will provide training of teachers in supported schools and learning centers on basic child centered teaching and learning approaches, including ABEP, TaRL, multi grade and multi age teaching system and cross cutting issues on child rights, child protection and teacher code of conduct. Training of both male and female teachers which is also gender sensitive will promote better teaching and learning practice in which girls and boys will get attention and support from their teachers.The project will support provision of basic teaching, learning and recreational materials for supported schools. This will help to facilitate the learning in school and the recreational facilities will help children to play and enjoy being in school. This has positive impact on their physical and mental development for learningAs part of efforts to support the teachers, the project will support provision of incentives for volunteer teachers in both formal and non-formal schools. The project will also support the training of teachers and SBMCs on mental health and psychosocial support to detect signs and cases of abuse or trauma among students and provide psycho-social support and on improved health, nutrition and hygiene and sanitation practices. Girls are the most affected in terms of abuse and this will benefit to have awareness and protect as well as provide sufficient support for victims through appropriate referral mechanisms.The project will also support reactivation and training of school child clubs in good practices of health, hygiene and nutrition practices. This will benefit children, especially girls to develop better hygiene and sanitation and nutrition practice which is important for healthier life.As part of continuous capacity building for the SBMCs, the project will provide refresher training for SBMC members from supported schools and learning centers on school improvement plans, school management and resource mobilization. Trained SBMCs will promote girl’s education in their respective communities.The project will support Ministry of Education, SUBEB, SAME at state and LGA level to carry out quarterly monitoring and supervision support to all supported schools.
