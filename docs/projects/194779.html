Project Summary

ALIMA aims to increase access to basic primary healthcare and nutrition services by supporting a full PHC package in the two IDP Camps and support case management for SAM with medical complications and improve quality of care at the Stabilization Center. The objective of the project is to contribute to the reduction of mortality and morbidity associate with maternal and childhood diseases among the Internally Displace People (IDPs) in El Fasher locality in North Darfur State.
This project will support the implementation of life-saving curative and preventive interventions and improvement of services provided through minor rehabilitation of health facilities, training of healthcare workers, curative consultations, MUAC screening, BE/CEmONC, management of severe and moderate acutely malnourished under-five children, provision of essential medical supplies and support to emergency referrals.
As North Darfur is facing an acute food insecurity crisis, and the burden of SAM cases are expected to rise drastically among children under five. In 2023, a caseload target of 1,320 SAM cases with medical complications needing stabilization is expected in El Fasher locality. ALIMA aims to expand the bed capacity of the SC from 36 up to 60 beds (depending on needs) during the peak season with a target of treating at least 720 cases annually and covering 55% of the locality’s caseload.
ALIMA will also ensure basic MHPSS services and/or referrals are available at all ALIMA supported health facilities. MHPSS activities will also be integrated into health promotion and IYCF to increase sensitization and strengthen resilience, especially among the highly vulnerable patients and caregivers at the SC.
The capacities of the of the SMoH Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) will be strengthened to deliver minimum healthcare services to population affected by disease outbreaks and population displacement due to flooding and armed conflict.

1 - Needs

North Darfur is facing a worsening humanitarian crisis with armed conflict, food insecurity, and economic crisis driving displacement, and IDPs and vulnerable host communities face numerous barriers to accessing healthcare including unavailability of services and/or health personnel, lack of medicines in health facilities, and high cost of services. According to the DTM Round 4, El Fasher locality hosts 483,075 internally displaced persons (IDPs) with 98% living in camp settings. Abu Shouk and Al Salam are two of the largest IDP Camps in El Fasher hosting 75,000 and 84,000 IDPs respectively and lack sufficient service coverage to meet the urgent health and nutrition needs of the population.In October 2022, ALIMA conducted an assessment of El Fasher town and found very few services available in Abu Shouk and Al Salam IDP Camps. Currently, one health facility is closed and others are offering only minimal outpatient services with limited hours and days of operation. The fee for service model suggests many vulnerable beneficiaries cannot afford to access care. Furthermore, there is no mechanism in place for emergency referrals from the two IDP camps to secondary care, and therefore there is an urgent need to increase access and strengthen referral pathways for lifesaving care.ALIMA’s assessment of the Stabilization Center of the Pediatric Hospital in El Fasher highlighted the need to improve quality of care and increase the bed capacity. With an expected caseload of 1,320 SAM with medical complications, the Stabilization Center capacity should be scaled up to at least 60 beds to improve access and coverage of at least 50% or more of the total caseload in El Fasher locality.ALIMA aims to increase access to basic primary healthcare and nutrition services by supporting a full PHC package in the two IDP Camps and support case management for SAM with medical complications and improve quality of care at the Stabilization Center.

Main objective: Contributing to the reduction of mortality and morbidity associated with maternal and child diseases in El Fasher, North Darfur. Objective 2: To improve access to quality Nutrition care for IDPs and host population in El Fasher Locality. Activities: • Rehabilitate and support the function of the PHCUs’ infrastructure; • Provide additional trained staff to support the MoH staff in the nutrition treatment facilities;• Provide essential medical supplies including medicine, consumable and equipment to all the nutrition supported facilities;• Support referral of SAM cases with complication to SC;• Through training, strengthen the capacity of community health volunteers (CHV) to conduct community nutrition activities;• Recruit and train women of child bearing age with focus on pregnant and lactating women (PLW) mother MUAC activities;• Through the CHVs, Conduct MUAC screening for children age 0-59 months in the health facilities and communities, and refer malnourished case to the supported health facilities;• Provide treatment for MAM and SAM cases with and without complication; • Provide essential trainings to all nutrition staff in the bid of improving quality of patient care. 6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To make the community aware of the services, community health workers (CHW) will be chosen from each community to conduct awareness raising activities about sexual and reproductive health and gender-based violence, health promotion, personal and environmental hygiene, and referrals from the community to the health facility. Considering the lack of sensitization and level of stigmatisation against survivors of GBV, this community-level intervention is critical to increase the number of survivors reaching life-saving health and psychological care services. This approach aims at building the confidence of communities to reduce stigma and to foster disclosure by survivors and encourage them to reach to available GBV services. Survivor-centered GBV case-management, such as clinical management of rape and MHPSS services will be offered at the ALIMA supported facilities to the most vulnerable patients. ALIMA has a leading role in the SGBV Referral Pathways Taskforce to strengthen community sensitization and support the coordination of referral mechanisms to link survivors with timely, appropriate medical and mental health care as well as protection services.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The projects will have a feedback and complaints handling mechanism that allows the local population to provide feedback. In addition, an e-mail address is made available and communicated to all project staff. These feedback and complaint mechanisms are inclusive. Feedback and complaints are stored in a secure database. In terms of Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse ALIMA is committed to prevent any sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries by humanitarian workers. ALIMA's staff recognizes the problem related to the imbalance of power in the humanitarian field and the potential exploitation that this could cause. Sexual exploitation and abuse are condemned by law and international standards and ALIMA condemns such behavior. They are absolutely contrary to the humanitarian principles and ethics to which ALIMA and its employees adhere. Measures are in place to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse: Any employee involved in a case of sexual exploitation or abuse will be subject to immediate termination and ALIMA may refer the matter to the national authorities or the authorities of the country of origin of the employee being prosecuted. Sexual activity with persons under the age of 18 is strictly prohibited, even in the event of differing local laws regarding age of consent or age of majority. Mistaken belief in a child's age will not be taken as a defense. Any exchange of goods or services, including assistance due to recipients, for sexual services or other sexual or exploitative behavior will not be tolerated. All ALIMA representatives and managers are responsible for creating and maintaining a safe environment that protects against sexual exploitation and abuse and are encouraged to pay attention to promoting the safety of women and girls and any other vulnerable population. ALIMA staff are urged to report any inappropriate behavior they may witness. Several complaint mechanisms have been defined. One of them is to send an alert to this newly created email address: alert@alima.ngo. All of ALIMA's employees are key elements in the enforcement of this policy. We are a humanitarian organization and therefore have a great responsibility, individually and collectively, to prevent abuses or violence from being committed in our name or through the positions that are assigned to us by our organization At the field level, the HR/Admin Manager is in charge of enforcing the policy, while at the country level the HR coordination, the Deputy Head of Mission as well as the Head of mission have the task to enforce the policy.
