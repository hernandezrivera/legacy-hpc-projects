Project Summary

The project objectives are considering the FSL Sector objectives and will provide immediate support to save lives and support vulnerable people to sustain their lives while building their reliance to withstand future stresses and shocks on the short and long term. This will have different strategic responses. The project interventions include addressing immediate lifesaving humanitarian needs as first top priority, through emergency agricultural assistance including crop, livestock and veterinary and fishery inputs and extension services targeting 2,350,000 people. This will include as well building the capacity of governmental institutions and sector partners to improve preparedness and response for new emergencies. The second priority is provide support for vulnerable people to restore and improve food and nutrition security and enhance self-reliance to cope with current and future shocks and stresses. Agricultural production will be improved through provision of climate-smart crop production, livestock and veterinary inputs and support to services. Similarly, small scale farmers will be targeted for reduced food losses and strengthened post-harvest techniques through improved food and crop storage and processing facilities. On and off farm livelihood assistance will be diversified and managed and coordinated to respond to food chain threats including animal and plant pests specially locust; In addition, communities will be involved in building, restoring and maintaining productive assets through cash and/or food for assets schemes. About 18,000 people will be targeted for cash transfer. Accountability to affected populations and environmental concerns will be mainstreamed into the proposed activities, also risk to GBV will be taken into consideration and application of the PSEA measures in will be applied in collaboration with sister UN agencies.

1 - Needs

Food insecurity remains alarmingly high in Sudan with increased and protracted displacement, economic decline and inflation, and high food price hikes exacerbated by the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic and the high inflation rates. The lockdown measures to prevent the spread of COVID-19 significantly decreased commodity movement, market function and cross-border trade, and compromised livelihoods and daily labor opportunities, reducing household purchasing power and food access of the vulnerable population.An estimated 7.1 million people face crisis or worse levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and above) and need urgent action to save lives. This represents a 22 percent increase from 5.8M compared to the same period last year (IPC analysis 2019). Around one million of the 7.1 million food insecure people are facing emergency levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4) and around 6 million people are facing crisis levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3). An additional 17 million people are estimated to be under stress (IPC Phase 2), and any additional shocks like floods, inflation and poverty can push them to severe levels of hunger.The Mid – season assessment 20/21report indicated that, the application of most agricultural inputs (fuel, seeds, fertilizers, herbicides, labour and agricultural machinery) to crop production, was generally lower compared to the previous year in 2019 and this trend will be further exacerbated for the 2020/2021 agricultural season. It was indicated in the FAO rapid flood impact assessment report that, the floods and waterlogging of soils due to intense rainfall, heavily damaged crop and pasture lands across wide areas in the assessed states; flash floods also caused major losses of agricultural inputs, tools and pumps, together with destruction of agriculture and livestock service facilities. Limited movement to access farms/markets was also recorded, affecting the food security and livelihoods of thousands of already vulnerable communities across the country. The report indicated the total number of farmers and pastoral households affected by the floods and heavy rains in the traditional rain fed agriculture, irrigated system, pastoral and fishery systems amounts to 597,689 farmer and pastoralist households in the 80 assessed localities across 15 States. Moreover, the wet environment, which was created by the flood, constituted favourable conditions for spread of weeds, plant pests and livestock diseases, posing additional risks to the agriculture and livestock sectors, expected to negatively affect the upcoming 2020/21 main harvest season. The above stated situation intensified the food insecurity, malnutrition and livelihoods impoverishment of already vulnerable populations and further exposed them to falling into more severe food insecurity phases of IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and Phase 5 (Famine) if no robust external Donors’ support is provided.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

This project has a medium impact on the environment since it focuses on agriculture, livestock activities in 13 states out of the 18 states in Sudan. The project by its nature has some negative implications on the natural resources particularly soil due to agricultural and livestock activities. Soil erosion related to deforestation and inappropriate agricultural practices is a key challenge to sustainable natural resources use in the targeted areas. The project will promote practices that favour soil protection and regeneration and don’t harm the local ecosystems to favour their progressive recovery. It will promote the cultivation of multipurpose legume crops that enrich and protect the soils, and strongly contribute to improving the nutrition of humans and their livestock. It will ensure that wastes from livestock vaccination and treatment are disposed in a proper way. Awareness rising in better natural resource management will be part of training. The project will engage in a number of mitigation activities that include tree plantation, training of households in effective and durable caretaking of the planted seedlings, and on agro‐forestry practices and use of LPG and fuel‐efficient stoves for domestic uses.The project has an environmental impact in general and in Darfur and Eastern Sudan in particular, are experiencing more frequent droughts, floods and storms causing damage to the environment and people's livelihoods. Poorly managed livestock is much more implicated in GHG emissions and its intensity varies between regions causing climate change phenomena. The project will contribute effectively to the efforts that can build resilience and prepare for and adapt to climate change through some mitigation measures that are explained below: Increasing livestock productivity through supplementary feeding interventions will help in reducing the number of non-productive animals which bear full responsibility in increasing emissions; Reducing the prevalence of diseases and parasites through the animal health component would eventually reduce emissions intensity as healthier animals are more productive and thus produce lower emissions per unit of output; Adoption of climate smart agriculture techniques by targeted trained farmers will result in incorporation of manure into soils which can reduce emissions while maintaining farm productivity; Pasture rehabilitation and tree plantation activities will store more carbon into the soils; Training the target groups on sound disposal of carcasses would reduce environmental pollution due to flooding.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The Organization’s overarching goal is to improve the food security and nutrition of communities affected by shocks and disasters, while enhancing families’ self-reliance and resilience to threats and crises in line with national priorities and FAO’s corporate Strategic objectives. The project will consider the protection of people depending on their needs and requirements, the project will provide suitable interventions that improve women’s and male and female youth capacity to produce all or part of good-quality food requirements and generate cash income for their families, contributing to empowerment and social protection of women, especially women headed households. The project will give equal opportunities to all categories of the population including women, girls, boys and person with disabilities to access the provided livelihood support, addressing the rights of all population categories to decent work to restore their food security and livelihood situation. The targeted communities have been involved in dialogue through meetings, interviews and group discussions throughout all steps of this project design and preparation and will be directly involved in the implementation processing including beneficiaries selection, registration and monitoring of the implementation at the field and beneficiaries level. As explained in the above paragraphs, FAO follows the necessary guidance for promoting the four pillars of decent work for the beneficiaries in the targeted rural areas. The project is considering protection of women and girls depending on the social environment in the mentioned areas. They will provide the training and input distribution in a suitable location, raise the awareness on GBV and use of fuel-efficient stoves to avoid firewood collection in far unsafe places.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability is integrated into all aspects of the way that FAO works. Accountability is incorporated into the definition of results in the Strategic Framework and Medium Term Plan, and how FAO plans, implements, monitors and reports on its programme of work.FAO accountability towards affected people starts from selection of types, varieties and quantities of livestock and agricultural inputs. The identified inputs are selected in a participatory manner during the consultation phase. FAO encourages the local communities not only to participate in the implementation of the activities but also to ensure ownership of the project. As a result. FAO provides assistance to the beneficiaries for the formation of local committees and training of its members so that they can play an active role in the implementation and monitoring phases. Any beneficiary complaints about types, varieties and quantities of inputs will be captured during the monitoring missions. FAO shows continues interest to hear the voices of the beneficiaries about its standard packages of inputs which have been subject to change every year taking into account the views of the targeted people. FAO urged all its implementing partners to open hot lines with the beneficiaries to allow them to express their opinions about the intervention and give them the chance to bring out any grievances they may have about the interventions. The project will promote understanding between the different livelihood groups (pastoralists and farmers) through group meetings, discussions and other community platforms to reduce conflict and encourage mutual support. Special focus will be on enhancing the knowledge and skills of men, women, adolescent girls and other at risk-groups on natural resources management and leadership to ensure sustainable livelihood interventions; this will help in avoiding competition over natural resources.Some FAO staff are aware about the Secretary General’s recent Policy Brief on The Impact of COVID-19 on Women notes with concern that “as the COVID-19 pandemic deepens economic and social stress coupled with restricted movement and social isolation measures, gender-based violence is increasing,” and economic impacts are felt most severely by women and girls. The economic devastation of the pandemic is leaving women and girls from affected communities, already in vulnerable situations, at greater risk of SEA. SEA complaint mechanisms have become even harder to access due to the rise in self-isolation measures, such as physical distancing policies, curfews and quarantines. School closures have not only interrupted education, but also affected school-based services for children at risk and placed additional childcare responsibilities on women, further restricting women’s access to livelihoods and economic security. As the crisis continues, children can also be expected to face separation from their families due to illness, death and quarantine, leaving them, particularly girls.
