Project Summary

This project will contribute to the strategic objectives of the 2021 HRP and all three of the FSL cluster objectives through the support of CARE donors and FSL partners to support improved livelihoods during emergency, early recovery, and resilience building of 126,150 (21,025HH) vulnerable women, men, boys and girls by improving food access, consumption and dietary diversity. This is expected to increase agricultural production, protect and build productive assets and restore livelihoods. CARE will use both in-kind, cash and voucher modalities to deliver the much needed assistance to the vulnerable households affected by conflicts, impacts of COVID-19 and natural disasters.
CARE will promote positive coping mechanisms among the most vulnerable beneficiaries including returnees, IDPs and host communities. Targeted vulnerable beneficiaries (IPC phases 3, 4 and 5) will be identified, mobilized, assessed and verified based on a widely consulted agreed upon selection criteria for support through appropriate cash / food vouchers. For cash and voucher assistance, there will be a rapid market assessments conducted to determine the feasibility of the cash and voucher assistance (CVA).
In all the project sites CARE will organize community mobilization and consultation activities where households will be sensitized on expected project outputs and beneficiary entitlements. There will also be context specific messaging on proper utilization of livelihood inputs (for farmers and fisher folks), proper nutrition, hygiene and protection (for lactating / pregnant women and for children).
CARE will also provide season appropriate emergency livelihood inputs (crop, vegetable and fishing kits) after providing appropriate training and guidance.
CARE will facilitate, through consultation and collaboration during community based participatory planning process with the targeted communities, construction of community assets using the food/cash for work approach where appropriate. These activities will aim at improving livelihoods at household and community levels, as well as building longer term community resilience. Special attention will be given to female headed households in providing support for agricultural inputs, protecting and building productive assets and restoration of livelihoods.

1 - Needs

More than 7.24 million people (60% of total population) are projected to be severely food insecure across South Sudan (South Sudan IPC, Dec 2020) during the 2021 lean season (April to July 2021). The major drivers of food insecurity in most of the areas in South Sudan are due to shocks and stressors attributed to persistent inter/intra communal conflicts which disrupt livelihoods and agricultural activities, climatic extremes in forms of drought and flooding, associated impacts of COVID-19 pandemic, poor macro-economic conditions, poor agricultural production and productivity due to negative biotic and abiotic factors, and challenges faced in the delivery of humanitarian assistance, especially during the peak of the lean season (June – August), when food shortages are typically at their worst levels. According to the latest IPC report, during the 2021 lean season, the projected proportion of the population facing acute food insecurity in the target states are, Unity State (69%), Jonglei (85%), and Eastern Equatoria (46%). Compared to the same lean season during the last six years, the food security situation is described as the worst, with about 20% of the population in emergency IPC phase 4 and about 1% likelihood of being in catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). The counties of Bor South, Twic East, Duk and Pibor in Jongeli State are all in IPC phase 4, with very high proportion of the population in this phase during both harvest and post-harvest/lean season projections (Dec - March and April - July respectively). Meanwhile, Pariang and Koch counties in Unity state have similar trends. The counties of Ikotos, Torit and Magwi are not spared as large numbers of returnees are expected in these areas during the course of 2021. The inter/intra-communal fighting and flooding has displaced thousands of women, men, boys and girls in most of the target counties, creating huge need for humanitarian assistance. Recent analysis shows that more than 230,000 people have been displaced to a new location during the first nine months of 2020, including new displacement and movement of existing IDPs to the new areas of displacement (IOM DTM as of November 2020). As of March 2020, four in five displaced people are living with host communities, of whom more than 90% in rural areas. The rest are in camp-like settings. Access to basic needs, security along with live-saving services has been challenging specifically for IDPs. The protracted displacement and scarcity of resources has, most often, led to strained relationship between IDPs and host community. Looking at the counties in the states selected for the project, the number of those expected to return from the refugee camps in the neighboring countries and IDPs from POCs and other areas within the country, are expected to increase. This therefore calls for an increased support to households who have just returned and don’t have food and those planning to cultivate through timely provision of quality emergency food/cash assistance, livelihood inputs and agricultural extension services, which are currently limited.

Conduct community consultations for needs verification/prioritization and beneficiaries’ selection process1.2. Beneficiaries identification, verification and registration exercise. Food/cash distribution to the most vulnerable households (restricted, unconditional cash)1.6. Conduct PDM Result 2: Enhanced productive capacity of 9,700 vulnerable households through emergency livelihood support2.1. Beneficiaries identification and registration2.2. Conduct livelihood inputs availability assessment. This is done with the aim of ascertaining possibilities of conducting input trade fairs (seed fairs)2.3. Distribute crop, vegetable and fishing kits (direct distribution and input trade fairs)2.4. Conduct small ruminants (shoats) restocking2.5. Training on livelihoods (crop, vegetable and fishing). Trainings will be conducted on good agronomic practices for field crops and vegetables, fishing, fish handling, processing and preservation, and other livelihood activities identified by communities 2.6. Sensitize households (esp. women) on messages regarding proper nutrition, hygiene and protection, especially for lactating / pregnant women and for children2.7. Conduct PDM. Post distribution monitoring (PDM) will be conducted for any distribution conducted. Result 3: Improved positive livelihood coping mechanisms among 9,125 vulnerable IDPs/host community/returnee households and enhance their abilities to absorb shocks3.1. Conduct community consultation and community based participatory planning. This exercise will be carried out at the boma levels3.2. Beneficiaries identification, verification and registration3.3. Community and household assets creation process. Participants from households will be supported to develop both household and community assets3.4. Formation of voluntary saving and loan associations and training on IGAs and VSLA approaches3.5. Provision of start-up grants to IGAs and VSLAs. This will act as a boost to VSLAs savings and the grants will be provided to the groups so as to increase the funds available for loaning to members. 3.6. Formation of farmers’ producer groups and training on GAP through Farmer Field Business Schools (FFBS) and other extension methods. This involves a gendered farming as a business approach and covers season-long production, gender, nutrition and livelihood aspects. 3.7. Capacity building of government extension workers through refresher trainings. Existing County and Payam level agriculture extension workers will be supported with refresher trainings on topics to be identified by them through a training needs assessment in each of the target counties.3.8. Conduct participatory disaster risk assessment and develop disaster risk management plans. These activities will be conducted during the process of community based participatory planning exercise.3.9. Provide conditional cash/food to support household assets creation. This will follow a community based participatory planning approach to select household and community assets to be constructed3.11. Conduct training for women groups in nutrition-sensitive food production. Nutrition sensitive trainingactivities will be included in CARE's livelihoods and agriculture related trainings

Recent market assessments in the areas where CVA has been proposed indicated that the markets are either functional or have high likelihood of recovering within the project period. To avoid marginalizing the vulnerable community members, more than one channel will be made available focusing primarily on verbal rather than written communication and allowing confidentiality and safety. CARE will establish participatory feedback, complain and response mechanism in order to listen to feedback and address grievances from those who participate, supported or affected by our interventions.
