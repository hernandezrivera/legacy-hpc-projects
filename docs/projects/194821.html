Project Summary

The dynamism of this project is to save lives and sustain the livelihoods of 30,480 people through the provision of fundamental crop and vegetable production and productivity through the provision of crop and vegetable seeds, tools, and fishing gear including imparting basic skills and knowledge. ADA will fully involve Various local stakeholders sector offices, community leaders, women’s groups, youth groups, representatives of people living with disabilities, the elderly, and other social groups throughout the lifespan of the project. It has been understood that communities (host communities and IDPs) in Fangak, Ayod, Nyirol, Pibor, and Malakal of Jonglei and Upper Nile states have limited productive resources and capacity and require immediate assistance to tackle and Survive the crisis and emergency levels of food insecurity in Fangak, Nyirol, Ayod, and Pibor and Malakal Counties. ADA has a well-established presence and offices in the mentioned location and a strong relationship with the stakeholders, community, Partners, and chiefs, its presence has been paramount in identifying the community needs and it collaborates and coordinates its activities with other partners on the ground. In addition, using its presence on the ground if given this funding, ADA will continue to promote crop and vegetable production as well as fishing as a means of diversifying household diet to address food insecurity as well as malnutrition, which remains a major concern among the host community, returnees, and the IDPs, as further detailed in the humanitarian context analysis sector of this proposal. The provision of improved inputs like crop and vegetable seeds, tools, and fishing gear including imparting knowledge and skills promptly will significantly contribute to the availability and access to food from its production. The project will ensure that vulnerable people have direct access to more food through the provision of crop and vegetable seeds, Tools, and fishing gear including the basic knowledge and skills required. Based on the lessons learned on the ground and the experiences gained in the previous project implementation, specifically, ADA will involve more women in the roles of management, especially in the management, Committees’ leadership positions and as decision-makers, and ensure ownership, and active participation in the implementation and sustainability of this project.
ADA will be conducting training involving at least 65 percent of women and will take into consideration women’s domestic work and maternal responsibilities. This project will also mainstream GBV throughout its programming. It has been understood that there is limited awareness of GBV in project operation areas. Awareness creation is therefore a key to ADA's programming strategy. ADA will assess and analyze conflict-provoking factors to understand the level of conflict while implementing this project. ADA undertakes regular conflict assessments to ensure security and stability for its staff and beneficiaries before undertaking any activity.
To diversify household diets and address food insecurity and malnutrition, Children and pregnant and lactating women are most vulnerable and are therefore a primary focus in the humanitarian context analysis sector of this proposal. The provision of improved inputs, such as vegetable seeds, fishing gear, and agricultural tools including knowledge and skills, will support and improve the availability and access to food through household production. The project will ensure that vulnerable people have direct access to more food through the provision of vegetable seeds and tools and fishing equipment. The project will integrate and augment responses to immediate needs, restoration of livelihoods, and capacity-building to pave the path for long-term recovery to enhance communities’ ability to cope with and recover from the ongoing conflict and natural shocks.

1 - Needs

The persistent occurrence of Man-made Conflicts and Naturals disasters shock waves is keeping South Sudan in a state of emergency, Between July 2022 and June 2023, an estimated 1.4 million children under five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition based on analysis and estimations from the results of the SMART nutrition surveys, (FSNMS). the alarm is high in Jonglei and Upper Nile, in October 2022, 30 counties are classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical), and 15 counties in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious). During the post-harvest season, unlike in the past, not much improvement is expected. Instead, the situation is expected to deteriorate in 50 counties. Overall, in the post-harvest period, counties in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious) and IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical) will likely increase from 45 to 56. Therefore, despite the huge humanitarian support, Food insecurity is highest in Jonglei with 79% of the population will likely face severe acute food insecurity and Upper Nile (60%) with the highest level of severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4 or worse) at the peak of the lean season 2023. According to the FEWS NET classification date June 2022 to January 2023, Upper Nile, Malakal County falls under (IPC 4) which requires emergency support and The Inter-Agency Rapid Needs Assessment (IRNA) led by UNHCR and OCHA which was carried out in Malakal, Upper Nile, on 22nd September 2022 indicated more 30,000 individuals were in dare need of support. They are settled outside the POC due to the pressure of the limited resources within the POC, Limited wash Facilities, Space to accommodate them, and limited ration at the POC. During the assessment, the IPDs mentioned general food distribution, seeds, tools, fishing gear, and some pieces of training to help them restore hope and livelihood.The FEWS NET classification date June 2022 to January 2023 there will be credible Famine in (IPC Phase 5) areas of Fangak and Pibor given the high proportion of the population already facing acute food insecurity and there is still potential for shocks occurring in Fangak and Pibor. Therefore, more severe food insecurity is anticipated by 2023. Fangak and Pibor are among the areas of highest concern for this risk of Famine (IPC Phase 5), based on the severity of current food insecurity, the local population’s very high vulnerability to new shocks, and the likely exposure to severe floods and/or renewed conflict in 2022/2023. The needs of the people of Fangak and Pibor remained the same as they are in IPC 5 and assistance of seeds tools and pieces of training will ensure the availability of seeds, tools, and much-needed skills and knowledge for the people of Fangak and Pibor.The Continued floods and conflicts in Ayod and Nyirol keep the populace vulnerable and prone to food insecurity, according to the inter-agency assessment dated 24/10/2022, the findings indicate high levels of food insecurity, inadequate clean water south, and demand for peacebuilding and Protection interventions. The fact that Nyirol and Ayod are bordering Fangak and Pibor there is a possibility of Nyirol (IPC3) and Ayod (IPC 4), heading to IPC 4 and 5). This project is to address and control this Nyirol and Ayod from their current contexts. The project aims at addressing food insecurity and building resilience in the flood-affected communities in Fangak, Ayod, Nyirol, greater Pibor, Malakal, Counties of Jonglei, and Upper Nile State. The project targets an estimated 30,480 individuals 5080 HH, i.e. Fangak 2123 HH, Ayod 506HH, Nyirol 741HH, Pibor 1050 HH, and Malakal 660HH particularly women, children the elderly, and the poorest of the poor families, living in Counties of Fangak, Ayod, Nyirol, Pibor, and Malakal Counties. Assistance will help affected households to meet their immediate and long-term food and cash needs, as well as provide appropriate capacity-building to the target communities will restore their livelihood

2 - Project activities

To save the lives and return hopes of the conflict and flood-affected people in Fangak, Ayod, Nyirol, Pibor, and Malakal County, ADA will undertake the following activities to address the identified shocks. Conducting community mobilization and sensitization about the project at the county and Payam levels. Project launching at the county level to ascertain the stakeholders and the community about the project and the beneficiaries’ selection criteria. Formation of Accountability to the affected population/ Project management committee. Project beneficiaries’ identification and registration are segregated by gender and age. Distribution of crops, vegetables, fishing gear, and tools to the registered households. Conduct ToTs for selected f 150 lead farmers and trained, them on GAP, CSA and IPM who later will train the 2000 farmers in their respective areas. These will be the champions of GAP, IPM and CSA. Conducting Training GAP, CSA, and IPM. Establishment of 10 demonstration gardens for practical learning purposes. Training on seeds processing and preservation, training on how to prepare vegetable food correctly for the identified mothers with malnourished children. Conducting training on post-harvest handling and marketing. Conducting training on modern fishing methods, preservation and marketing. Carrying out post-distribution monitoring and post-planting monitoring. Transportation of Inputs. Monitoring and evaluation of the project. Hire of the temporary store. Printing of visibility activities.
