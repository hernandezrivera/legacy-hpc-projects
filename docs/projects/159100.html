Project Summary

Action Against Hunger (AAH) and partner Arab Center for Agricultural Development (ACAD) propose to reduce the vulnerability of poor and vulnerable households (HHs) residing in Gaza, North Gaza, Khan Younes, Middle Area and Rafah Governorates of Gaza Strip (GS), through a graduation approach focused on:
- increasing access to immediate basic needs and services: provision of Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA);
- supporting the strengthening or rehabilitation of Income Generating Activities (IGAs): provision of cash or in-kind support according to the needs.
This approach will allow to cover immediate basic needs in the short term while promoting livelihoods sustainable opportunities in the longer term, with a positive effect on the psychosocial well-being of the overall household.
The provision of cash assistance for either covering basic needs or supporting income generating activities, will be complemented by interventions from AAH together with partner ACAD:
- awareness campaigns on nutrition and food safety, especially targeting pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and children under 5;
- capacity building addressing business/IGAs related topics.
The project will target 800 vulnerable households with member(s) who got severely injured (and/or have a disability as a consequence) during the Great March of Return (GMR), benefiting some 4,560 individuals (average size of HHs is 5.7 according to the 2018 SefSec survey: 999 men, 1,273 women, 1,080 boys, 1,208 girls).
The project will contribute to increase the purchasing power of households through the provision of up to 6 months of unconditional unrestricted cash assistance averaging $277 USD/household/months to 750 GMR-affected households, with limited access to basic needs and with limited income opportunities, benefiting some 4,275 individuals. To leverage the impact of cash transfer on the food security of the households, the project will integrate targeted messaging aimed at maximizing the nutritional impact, particularly for children under 5, PLW and injured people in need or under rehabilitation, as well for food safety, especially considering the power cuts and the limited access to safe water.
At the same time, 50 women and men – prioritizing women and the youth), who got injured (and/or have a disability as a consequence) during the Great March of Return (GMR), will be supported to strengthen or start up an income generating activity. The type of activity will be selected, upon analysis of market opportunities and considerations related to risks and specific barriers for people with disabilities, with the beneficiary themselves. Engaging in a livelihood activity will also alleviate the psychosocial suffering brought to victims, at both personal and household level, by the injury. IGA beneficiaries will be trained following the Life Palestine – Business Shuttle methodology, a business development coaching program design by Action Against Hunger and successfully piloted in oPt - in order to develop technical, business and soft skills essential to make the supported IGA successful.
The initiative will also seek complementarities with psychosocial support initiatives and services in the area of intervention and will leverage on existing networks of focal points to mainstream protection, through referral of identified cases.

1 - Needs

Despite the relative absence of armed conflict since 2014, the humanitarian, human rights, security and political situation in GS continues to deteriorate. Poverty rates reached nearly 60% and deep poverty more than 42%. The unemployment rate increased to 54% (HNO 2019), and disproportionately impacts women and youth. 80% of Gazans depend on international aid (OCHA 2018). The level of food insecurity remains alarmingly high: 62% of HHs are severely or moderately food insecure with highest rates among refugees, and female-headed HHs, PLW are particularly vulnerable to food insecurity and malnutrition (HNO 2020).According to OCHA, 34,185 Palestinians injured during GMR protests (March 2018 - July 2019), including 8,002 injured by live ammunitions. 71.3% of injured are men, 5.9% female, 20.8% boys and 2% girls, distributed as follows: Khan Younes: 8,307, Gaza: 8,190, North Gaza: 7,391, Middle Area: 6,138, Rafah 4,159. According to the needs assessment conducted by the Palestinian Center for Democracy and Conflict Resolution (PCDCR, 2019), priority needs expressed by GMR-affected HHs are: food (69%), adequate health care (61.8%), personal security and safety (57.5%), job opportunities and income sources (57%), removal of physical and logistical mobility restrictions (54%), provision of essential NFIs (42.3%), adequate housing for people with disabilities (38.8%). Other needs include counseling, psychological support, medications/medical items, specialized surgery, physical therapy and rehabilitation. Women and children, identified as most vulnerable, need in-depth psychosocial support more so than adult men who have a higher coping capacity. Participants also recommended to consider specific gender-based needs when designing and implementing any intervention with this category.Preliminary results of the verification exercise AAH conducted in 2019 for ECHO-funded project among men and women (64% of them breadwinner) injured during the GMR, show that:•35% of surveyed HHs receive quarterly cash subsidies from MoSD, 83% food assistance/e-voucher from UNRWA/WFP (regular assistance)•Average monthly cash expenditure is USD292, average monthly on-credit expenditure is USD182. This coupled with limited income (average USD140/month/HH) leads to limited economic access to markets•Injury affected the ability to work for 89% of assessed caseload, whereby 47% lost income and 39% reduced it•34% received assistance related to the injury suffered during the GMR•For 92% of interviewed the negative impact of the injury on their livelihoods will continue for at least 6 months•Three main priorities: small business support, health support, food / nutritional assistance•In terms of cash, interviewed group declared to mainly need it to repay debts, buy productive inputs, and cover health and food needs.AAH also conducted 2 FGDs in Khan Younes and Rafah to assess the socioeconomic situation of people injured during the GMR, identify their priority needs, and triangulate the information collected. The main findings reveal that:•After the injury, socioeconomic problems increased, episodes of harassment and increased GBV by family members and accusation of default as the injured persons are no longer able to move, work or generate income (Women are particularly subject to abuse following the injury of one of their HH's members (PCDCD, 2019).•Before the injury most people were living off casual work (on daily/hourly basis) in agriculture, construction, and other sectors. After the injury, they are no longer able to work•Coping mechanism adopted: dependency on debts (increase in debts), waiting for assistance from NGOs, borrowing food from relatives and friends, buying only essential basic needs, buying low quality leftover from local market•Main challenges faced because of the injury: lack of income, increased expenditure on medications, increased tensions at home, no access to needed support•Cash for basic needs is a priority

2 - Activities

A1.1. Selection of beneficiariesThe project will target 800 HHs who have at least one member who suffered an injury during the GMR protests and is experiencing temporary or permanent disability, negatively impacting the whole HH. 750 will be supported with MPCA and 50 will receive technical and financial support to strengthen or create IGAs.AAH will coordinate with the relevant Clusters, the Disability Working Group and medical organizations to obtain lists of potential beneficiaries and/or make a first cross check on the caseload. For the selection of the 750 HHs benefiting from MPCA, AAH and ACAD will organize a verification exercise (HH visits).HHs will be selected and prioritized based on eligibility criteria (presence of one or more member(s) who – as a consequence of injuries suffered during the GMR protests – have temporary or permanent disability impacting the broader HH), HHs’ profile and socio-economic vulnerability criteria (family size and age groups, head of HH, marital status, presence of children under 5, PLW, elderly, income and expenditures, debts, aid received, coping strategies unemployment of family members). Specific SOPs for beneficiary selection – including the scoring system – will be developed and applied. For the selection of the 50 HHs to be supported to strengthen or start an IGA, AAH and ACAD will publish an announcement having as eligibility criteria to have suffered from an injury during the GMR. Applicants names will be cross-checked with the injured individuals’ lists and verified through HH visits. A1.2. Cash distribution to beneficiaries AAH will provide monthly cash transfers of 277 USD/HH to 750 GMR-affected HHs, for up to 6 months. The actual transfer values have been calculated – keeping into consideration the deep poverty line threshold, the average income of a family proceeding from both work and aid received, and gap in needs coverage. The amount enables a family of 5.7 members on average to cover up to 52% of the gap in basic needs (food, shelter and clothes).Cash assistance is considered an appropriate humanitarian assistance modality given the level of access to the markets, as well as the acceptable degree of critical market function across the GS. The cash will be collected by beneficiaries from Bank of Palestine upon presentation of beneficiary ID. In case the beneficiary cannot reach the bank for mobility issues, he/she can delegate another member of the HH. In order to maximize the impact of cash distribution on the food security of HHs, beneficiaries will receive nutrition awareness and food safety sessions by the AAH and ACAD teams. A1.3. Cash support to beneficiaries (IGAs) 50 households, prioritizing women and the youth, that suffered an injury during GMR protests will receive support to either reinforce an existing IGA or to start up a new one. The support will consist of both technical and financial (max USD 1870) support. For the selection of IGAs, an assessment will be done on projects proposed by potential beneficiaries, considering type of business, location, estimated budget, market size, market strategy, production and management plan, innovation. Life Palestine Business shuttle methodology– a business development coaching program - was adapted to Gaza context, since 2018 builds upon both individual and group development. The first encompasses both technical knowledge (such as business plan development, financial management and book keeping, and marketing) and soft skills (self-knowledge, self-confidence, self-control, communication, initiative and decision making, interpersonal relations, flexibility, tolerance to frustration, customer orientation, negotiation) development, while the second one focuses on cohesion, networking and synergies creation. Technical assistance will entail training tailored on the type of activities. The methodology was piloted in GS by AAH with positive feedback from participants who felt their self-confidence boosted.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Through the whole project cycle AAH ensures protection mainstreaming is guided by protection principles of Do No Harm. AAH considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. As a specific protection mainstreaming component AAH is in the process of developing its own protection mainstreaming toolkit and to start working with its own sector specific gender and protection mainstreaming checklists. Up to date AAH has made use of cluster specific Protection Mainstreaming Tip Sheets. Overall AAH’s aim is to take decisive humanitarian action with attention to risk mitigation thereby avoiding doing harm to the greatest extent possible. AAH in oPt prioritises this also through context specific (risk) analyses as well as the protection coordinator position stirring questions and discussion. In order to inform its assessments and implementation AAH also continuously works on understanding gender roles. By understanding gender roles and household dynamics, AAH believes that the organisation mitigates the risks of doing harm. In 2019, AAH conducted a masculinity study in both Gaza and the West Bank, focused on identities/roles of men in order to fully understand men, women, girls and boys and gender dynamics. AAH has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All AAH staff has the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct. AAH has in place focal points at base and mission levels. as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. Moreover, most of AAH staff have gone through protection cluster facilitated trainings with the aim to know how to detect and refer potential cases of GBV. As part of AAH accountability to affected populations, a feedback mechanism emphasizing fluid and transparent communication will be in place. It serves as a mechanism for project staff, beneficiaries, host communities, local authorities, and other key stakeholders to share and respond to project performance, data and information. Beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries alike will be able to reach out to the project with complaints, suggestions, and any other feedback. The information collected will be regularly fed into our programming to ensure no harm is done and to guarantee continuous improvements.The mechanism will consist of a mixture of channels: SMS/phone numbers, email addresses, and feedback and complaint desks when possible, accessible to people with disability. AAH will reach out to beneficiaries through regular consultation processes (assessments, FGDs, community meetings, PDM survey on a randomly selected sample, etc.).During the assessment at the inception phase, beneficiaries from different ages/genders/disabilities will be consulted to identify their preferences in terms of providing feedback. The mechanism will take into account the perception or possible reservations that the target population might have in relation to their use, especially for complaint mechanisms. Vulnerable groups will receive additional sensitization to make sure they feel comfortable in using these mechanisms. Beneficiary feedback will be analyzed and presented in a systematic way – also to project partners - and the MEAL Officer will be in continuous contact with the Coordination team. Data will be sex-and-age disaggregated to show possible trends and understand barriers that might need to be addressed. Emphasis will be placed on closing the feedback loop, meaning that the person who raised a complaint will always receive a response on their case and beneficiary data (keeping confidentiality) will be used to inform programmatic decision-making.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AAH uphold the humanitarian principles of Humanity, Impartiality, Neutrality and Independence. AAH is committed to operate in an ethical manner and on a programmatic risk mitigation strategy for risk of fraud, waste, and abuse within the proposed activities. As a global frame, we rely on the organizational Policy on Anti-Bribery, Corruption and Abuse of Power and Code of Conduct. Organizational whistle blowing mechanisms and procedure are in place, `ZERO TOLERANCE´ towards fraud, bribery or corruption is applied. Reporting mechanisms have also been implemented in order to report these behaviors to headquarters. Ethical Risks related to any kind of abuse (sexual, power, etc...) are mitigated by providing compulsory briefing to each new staff member on ethical policies. AAH Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. AAH has a Policy for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and two referent persons against Harassment in Gaza.Fraud Risks in activities such as cash assistance, is continuously monitored during activity implementation and post distribution monitoring. In 2019, AAH has conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation for which the consultant spent the month of March in oPt. Particular attention was paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. Also, AAH started engaging with Humanity & Inclusion as inclusion experts to sensitise and inform AAH’s programming. For instance, instead of asking directly if people have a disability, AAH is increasingly using ‘The Washington Group Short Set of Questions’ to detect people with disabilities. AAH is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age; though pending the reporting format, AAH usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. In addition, AAH started in its assessments to consider ‘barriers’ of particularly vulnerable groups and ‘enabling factors’ for people with specific needs. Through the implementation of beneficiary feedback mechanism, beneficiaries and members of the communities where AAH works can report irregularities in the beneficiary selection, cash or other assistance distribution process. The project will develop specific SOPs including a set of eligibility and selection criteria, as well as a score system. Potential beneficiaries – pre-identified by collecting lists from medical organizations, clusters (FSS, Protection, Health) and working groups (Disabilities working group), will go through a verification process jointly carried out by the technical team and social workers. Random checks will be carried out by the MEAL team members to ensure transparency, neutrality and objectivity and spot potential bias or incongruency.
