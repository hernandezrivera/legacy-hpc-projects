Project Summary

The educational system is overstretched, with insufficient classrooms, and costly for students due to transportation, supplies, and uniform. These factors contribute to low enrollment rates and high drop-out. Just 69% of school-aged children in Darfur are enrolled in schools, retention rates are low, and 94% are unable to access secondary education.
From the experience that CRS has when implemented the prospect phase one it was noticed that children out of school are severely exposed to diverse protection issues such as exploitation, armed force recruitment, harassment and similar. In particular it was noticed that the families send their boys to engage in several types of work away from home such as working in the market as casual labor exposing them to may protection challenges such as exploitation and abuse. Furthermore, its was noticed that girls are exposed to early marriage limiting their opportunities to follow education or other important activities.
According to the recent Humanitarian Needs Overview, East Darfur has 7 localities that are classified as protection hotspots, including El Dain and Assalaya. Furthermore the same source as well as ILO studies imply that: in East Darfur, 41% of girls are married before the age of 18; even though FGM was criminalized in April 2020, the prevalence remains extremely high in East Darfur (93.8%) ; 1/3 of East Darfur children are lacking a birth certificate; 25% of children engaged in child labor. Over 90% of Sudan’s localities lack specialized SGBV services. Since 2004, CRS has been implementing integrated programmes in Sudan across the emergency, recovery, and resilience spectrum. In East Darfur, CRS proposes to build upon this intervention, leveraging the investments, synergies, and learnings, in PROSPECT II. This project addresses key educational barriers by improving the learning environment and skills of education providers as well as addressing key protection challenges by working on both system-level change and improving capacity of service providers.
Through the implementation of the PD CRS will work closely with stakeholders; government, NGOs and UN agencies, including other PROSPECTS agencies and implementing partners, for more collaboration and pursue new opportunities to cover the existing gaps in specialized child protection services , UNICEF and CRS will collaborate to activate communities platform that will foster peace building and social cohesion among refugees, IDPs and host communities. More specifically in this round of intervention the targeted youth from the abovementioned categories will be brought together into youth forums. Refugees’ community leaders will be involved in the school committees to increase the representation and opportunity for participation in decision making also aiming the increase of enrollment of refugee children in the school system.
In the framework of C4D the project will target two new localities that will be selected in collaboration with UNICEF, CRS and local authorities based on the selected criteria that will be developed (in addition to the existing ones).

The project will include C4D component where UNICEF defines Communication for Development (C4D) as a systematic, planned and evidence-based strategic process to promote positive and measurable individual behavior and social change that is an integral part of development Programmes, policy advocacy and humanitarian work. C4D uses dialogue and consultation with, and participation of children, their families, and community through a mix of communication tools, channels, and approaches, including the adoption of the Adolescent Kit and supporting the activation of U-reporters to enhance youth participation and Accountability to affected populations.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

CRS is ensuring that accountability is considered across all activities, ensuring the inclusion of vulnerable groups at all program stages. as CRS has extensive experience promoting and implementing participants' accountability at all project stages. the agency takes a participant-oriented, active approach to accountability. In addition to the feedback collected during regular program monitoring (satisfaction surveys, exit interviews during distribution), CRS uses safe and inclusive feedback mechanisms (responsive to gender and other vulnerability considerations) based on input provided by affected communities on preferred methods of communication. CRS ensures that all feedback is addressed through appropriate follow-up by the project staff. This two-way communication ensures transparency throughout project design and implementation. CRS organizes project update meetings, incorporating local government, participants, and other NGOs. Additionally, CRS uses a community-led approach for selecting participants, which emphasizes communication and transparency with targeted communities across all steps of the implementation cycle.Moreover, CRS is encompass the inter-agency guides in its policies and SoPs, to effectively promote accountability to affected populations. this is very crucial and on the top of the agency prioritization covering all areas of protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.CRS is a complaint with the an inter-agency referrals while operating community feedback and response mechanism. and the SOPs cover how to receive, assess, refer, and follow-up on complaints between agencies in line with diverse confidentiality and data protection policies.
