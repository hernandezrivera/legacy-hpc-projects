Project Summary

In order to respond to emergency health needs of the conflict-affected population, PUI intends to continue its emergency intervention in Entry-Exit Check Points (EECPs) and in various rayons of Donetsk oblast both in government and non-government controlled areas.
In particular, to improve quality of care and stay conditions in health facilities (HF), PUI will ensure donation of medical/non-medical supplies, rehabilitations including Wash rehabilitations, NFI winterization support and capacity building of staff. In order to increase access to health services for the most vulnerable ones, PUI will provide a cash-based support to children < 5, to PLW, to adults > 60 and to individuals in need of emergency assistance. In addition, financial support will be provided to HF to ensure referrals of patients from the different levels of care. Where possible and relevant, PUI teams will also promote the use of alternative models of care such as e-health.
At EECP level, PUI will continue to ensure people have access to drinking water, sanitation facilities (incl. hand washing stations) and emergency health services. PUI will continue to work with authorities for the hand-over of these activities.
In order to support national response to COVID-19 and ensure HF/SI compliant with minimum WASH standards, PUI will support HF/SI via donation of essential medical/non-medical equipment, consumables, PPE sets and cleaning kits. Facilities will be supported in the implementation of screening and triage protocols. At EECP level, PUI will ensure that suspected COVID-19 cases are able to be temporarily isolated and properly referred to designated infrastructures. Both at facility and EECP level, activities will be completed with awareness raising activities related to COVID-19 and good hygiene practices. Staff of facilities will be briefed on how to ensure infection, prevention control and based on needs identified related rehabilitations/arrangements will be conducted. Individual material support in terms of hygiene kits will also be provided to vulnerable individuals including immobile and partially mobile people that would be identified by partner HF/SI. Synchronously with the integration of MHPSS into PHC in the framework of the current Health Reform, PUI will strengthen capacities of health care workers in mental health and psychosocial support. Individual psychosocial support will also be provided to people in need including community’s members and health providers.
Health is a fundamental human right and for this reason, project will aim to ensure that all people of the targeted areas have access to essential health services and that those services are of good quality. A specific focus will nevertheless be placed on the most vulnerable populations including children under 5, adults over 60, people with disabilities, PLW and health workers. It should be noted that women will constitute the biggest part of project beneficiaries considering that they are over represented among health workers and people over 60. Based on PUI’s field experience, women are also more eager to use MHPSS services. In addition, it is worth noting that among HF/SI, PUI will support in priority pediatric/maternity wards and nursing homes.
PUI multi-sectorial approach combining assistance in the sectors of health, WASH and NFI winterization will ensure people have access to health services of good quality and that health facilities offers safe and dignified conditions of visit/stay for patients and working conditions for health providers. Responding to wash gaps in HF will ensure the adoption of infection prevention and control (IPC) measures and the reduction of nosocomial infection risks. Similarly, PUI combination of health and wash assistance at EECP level, ensure that people crossing have access to comprehensive services including clean water, adequate sanitation facilities, first aid services and information related to COVID-19.

1 - Needs

Six years of the ongoing crisis in Eastern Ukraine and COVID-19 pandemic has further aggravated the suffering of people in conflict-affected Donetska and Luhanska oblasts. A total of 3.4 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection services including 1,53 million in GCA and 1,62 million in NGCA.In particular, 1.52 million people need lifesaving and essential health care service. Limited access to health care due to poor infrastructural and structural levels, lack of essential medical equipment, inadequacy of health workers and disrupted health system have a concerning impact on vulnerable populations already facing a range of barriers (limited mobility, limited financial means…). According to information shared by health department of DRSA which were confirmed by PUI teams in the field, a high proportion of COVID-19 cases among health workers, limited availability of PPE in HF, and low awareness of basic COVID-19 preventive behaviours have a negative interconnected impact and increase the burden on health system. Additionally, COVID-19 testing is overwhelming laboratories’ activities decreasing possibility to undergo other types of testing, which led to increase mortality of Non-Communicable Diseases rates especially among the elderly. In 2020, REACH assessment highlighted that 55% of households reported problems accessing health care “all the time” or “often” in the past year in rural and 45% in urban areas. Also, cost of medicine, distance to facility, Cost of travel to facility, cost of appointment, lack of facilities and irregular presence of doctors are reported as the most common barriers when it comes to health access.Mental health and psychosocial support remain largely unavailable in Ukraine at community level, due to low integration of mental health into primary healthcare and a low-functioning referral system. Improved access to information about mental health and psychosocial services as well as information on the stress-related symptoms and positive coping mechanisms has to be increased for all conflict-affected people, in particular for the most in need (women, children, elderly, people with disabilities, health workers). Overall, 2,9 Million people need WASH assistance. More than one in four families on both sides of the ’contact line’ reported that the water situation has deteriorated over the past five years. Water stoppages, unavailability, inability to treat water, and a lack of money to pay for water, sanitation and hygiene products are among the commonly identified critical problems. 1.7 million people need help with access to water (HNO 2021) when people’s ability to maintain hygiene and livelihoods became more critical given the current sanitary situation. The quality of water in the conflict-affected areas is another concern. In PUI’s HF assessment (2019), the majority of FMAPs and 50% of ambulatories did not have access to drinking water for staff nor patients, or where it was available, its quality could not be confirmed. According to health cluster in 2020 nearly all HF need assistance in in terms of hygiene and infection prevention control (IPC). In addition, price of hygiene products (soap, toilet paper) which were already high became higher since the beginning of the pandemic.Harsh Ukrainian winters continue to pose a threat to the lives and well-being of people on both sides of the ’contact line’. Almost 160,000 vulnerable households in both GCA and NGCA are struggling to adequately prepare for the winter. Situation in similar in health facilities and social institution where mattresses, warm beds linen and heaters are missing. At EECP level, support of NGOs to ensure adequate access to essential health and wash services is still needed as well as material assistance to limit further spread of COVID-19 for people crossing (sanitizers, PPE for EECP staff, isolation areas…) considering that as of today authorities are not able to offer these services.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome1. Healthcare system is supported in order to support national response and limit further spread of infectious diseases• Donation of essential medical/non-medical equipment to HF • Donation of PPE sets to health facilities• WASH improvement+ general rehabilitation of HF/SI ( wash : Provision of water dispensers/drinking water, waste management supplies incl. for medical waste, water testing, water supply systems repairs; installation of heating tanks, cleaning of boreholes; improvement of toilets pans/sinks, replacement of pipes/pumps; General rehabs such as improvement of roof/ceiling/soil conditions, change of doors/windows, painting and electricity works)• Donation of cleaning kits to HF/SI (kits are based on WASH cluster guidelines)• Donation of hygiene kits to vulnerable individuals including immobile and partially mobile people, people <60yo, COVID-19 case ( kits are based on WASH cluster guidelines)• Capacity building of staff in terms of WASH and IPC• Support in the implementation of screening and triage protocols• Awareness activities about COVID-19 and other infectious diseases (risks communication will be aligned with existing message shared by MOH/WHO/UNICEF) Outcome2 . Access to essential services (including first-aid, PFA and WaSH) is maintained in Mayorsk, Mariinka, Novotroitske and Hnutove EECPs in the framework of the COVID-19 pandemic• Provision of emergency health services (FPA) and support for referral when needed• Management of sanitation facilities incl. toilets and hand-washing stations• Provision of safe water • Creation of isolation areas for suspected COVID-19 cases Outcome3. Access to essential healthcare services for conflict-affected population in the framework of the COVID-19 pandemic is improved as well as when possible quality and sustainability of services • Cash based support (voucher for drugs/emergency kits) • Donation of medical equipment/consumables to HF• Development of alternative models of care (e-health)• Support referral system from the different levels of care• Provision of individual psychosocial support for community based members and health providers• Improvement of stay conditions for patients via winterization assistance to HF/SI (kits composed of matrasses, bed linen, blankets, pillows and heaters in line with NFI cluster suggestions and needs of facilities) Beneficiaries of kits and vouchers will be identified based on their vulnerability (<5 yo, >60yo, PLW, disability, health conditions) by health providers of supported HF/SI. Drugs available under voucher activity and value of the voucher were determined based beneficiaries needs and lessons learnt from the last years. Voucher and distribution activities will be followed by Post-distribution monitoring surveys, enabling beneficiaries to give feedback regarding the assistance received (relevance of drugs on the list/of items composing kits) and PUI to identify corrective actions/changes to be introduced. Accountability to beneficiaries and partners will also be ensured through PUI’s hotline, PUI’s feedback dedicated email account, feedback/complaint boxes and when feasible via group discussions. Information regarding feedback mechanisms will be shared orally by PUI teams and mentioned on several supports (collaboration agreements, flyers accompanying kits, posters explaining projects activities). Necessary precautions will be taken to ensure the Do No Harm principle is followed, for instance this include the opportunity for beneficiaries to anonymously provide feedback, the use of online trainings or the use of additional precaution measures for home deliveries.All medical procurements (drugs, medical equipment, consumables and PPE) undergo a specific procurement process as per PUI internal guidelines which include strict verification of items origins/suppliers/manufacturers. PUI Health Coordinator and PUI HQ pharmacist are involved in the process.
