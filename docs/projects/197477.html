Project Summary

The project aims to reduce the gap in access to quality education due to armed conflict in northern Mozambique and strengthen the resilience of children under 18 years of age by (I) promoting girls, boys, and CWD access to formal and non-formal education, (II) job training, (III) training and support of local teachers and leaders to detect potentially dangerous situations and effective communication with children, (IV) strengthen the capacity of the entire community to respond to educational challenges in a crisis context and mitigate the impact of the emergency on girls, boys and CWD, (V) GVB and sexual exploitation awareness campaigns. This initiative is expected to reach 2,000 children under the age of 18 in the Ibo district. This project specifically targets girls and displaced children. In addition, the activities will consider the needs of children with disabilities (CDW).

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

To achieve the main objectives of this project, the main activities proposed are as follows: (I) non-formal education and job training (carpenters, agricultural activities, IT training, others), (II) Cultural awareness activities (III) leisure and sports activities, (IV) back to school campaigns (V) purchase and distribution of school kits, and (VI) capacity-building and sensitization sessions to train team members, teachers, and members of the community. The activities will be carried out in Ibo Island, Ibo District, Cabo Delgado

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The activities have been designed taking into consideration gender, age, and the needs expressed by females, males, and children. Information regarding the beneficiaries of this intervention will be disaggregated by sex and age (IPD will be considered) and gathered for further data analysis. Ibo Foundation acknowledges the local traditions, hierarchy, and societal roles when it comes to designing activities that are gender inclusive. To work within the local socio-cultural barriers and achieve positive change, the Foundation will tailor the activities to meet the needs and expectations of the beneficiaries, to do so local participation is strongly encouraged, especially female participation through groups of women. (I) Local team, activists, and stakeholders will receive training on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation (PSEA), Gender Base Violence (GBV), and Violence against Children (VAC). Local and community workers/ volunteers must sign a document called “Ethic Code”. (II) Support the local school, teachers, and school personnel to assess risks and plan strategies to protect children and an emergency context. (III) Empowerment of boys, girls, and CWD through training in children's rights and gender equality. Creation of a smaller group of girls to discuss sensitive issues such as child marriage. (IV) Community engagement, involved and committed to the project, sensitization campaigns, local authorities, and leaders’ support. (V) Various systems will be followed to identify potential risks to beneficiaries, carry out semi-annual evaluation and monitoring surveys, activate an anonymous suggestions/complaints box; permanent consultation with beneficiaries’ groups during and through the activities; meetings with community leaders and community representatives; all incidents will be recorded to find solutions for each problem identified and meet the needs of affected groups.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The Ibo Foundation has been present in the Ibo district for almost 20 years and has its own infrastructure and a permanent team of expatriates and locals. This stability makes it possible to agree on objectives, scope, and activities with local leaders, and the target population of each project. In addition, within the framework of each intervention, there are different channels for stakeholders and the target population to express themselves, such as surveys, and general group discussions. Focus groups will be organized at regular intervals to correct problems or complaints expressed by beneficiaries or stakeholders.This, like other projects, promotes the active participation of the beneficiaries as promoters of their own change.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The needs of children with disabilities must first be identified. To do this, a working group will be set up to develop data collection and processing devices. Second, specific activities will be designed to respond to the demands expressed by the beneficiaries identified in step 1 or existing activities will be tailored to fit CDW needs and to promote integration.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Sensitization and awareness about respect for the environment. All waste resulting from the Foundation's projects are properly separated (each infrastructure has its own and properly identify ed locations), and then taken to the appropriate and prepared location on the island for this purpose. However, in all actions, the "three Rs" of waste management are promoted and taught: Reduce, Reuse and Recycle. In this context, some of the activities with children and teenagers are planned with only recycled materials. As a matter of principle, the Foundation gives preference to local products. If there is no product, the nearest market is used (the other islands, Pemba or Nampula), transporting the goods whenever possible by traditional sailing vessels (dhow). Also, at the level of employees, local people are privileged to reduce the impact and expenses of travel.All the infrastructures that will be used for the project are supplied with a rainwater collection system and its distribution so as not to overexploit the Ibo aquifer and preserve the water. Periodically, the Foundation's maintenance technician cleans and disinfects the tanks to ensure water quality. Likewise, the facilities are also provided with sanitation through latrines connected to septic tanks duly located and periodically cleaned.
