Project Summary

This project is aimed at ensuring full capacity of the Protection Sector to coordinate the protection response in Libya, by providing co-coordination resources.
Considering the critical and complex protection dimension of the humanitarian situation in Libya, the Protection Sector remains one of the key stakeholders in providing strong and effective coordination. Coordination aimed at ensuring safe, dignified, meaningful, and accountable access to impartial, rights and needs based assistance for persons in need, whether seeking protection or non-protection services.
Functional sectors support humanitarian actors with coordination, protection sensitive information management, including needs assessments and advocacy. Considering commitments to centrality of protection and protection mainstreaming, the sector plays also a key role in ensuring those principles are operationalized at intersectoral level.
To ensure inclusive coordination the co-leadership of NGOs is critical, as it streamlines the information flow, allows to timely identify common challenges in the response, exceeding the capacity of a single partner, understand better the perspectives of affected communities by creating a direct link with implementing agencies. Collaboration with national/International humanitarian actors and Human Rights organizations are critical to ensure that assistance is provided safely and respects people’s dignity.

1 - Needs

The sector approach aims to address gaps and to increase the effectiveness of the humanitarian response by building effective and efficient partnerships, where the government has limited or constrained capacity to do so. The main drivers of the humanitarian situation in Libya demonstrate its crucial protection dimension of it. Considering the fragmentation of the government administration and the internal dimension of the conflict in Libya as well as recognizing the sensitivity of protection and access to rights for Libyan and non - Libyan persons a strong protection coordination mechanism encompassing general protection, HLP, GBV, child protection mine action is crucial to support protection of affected persons from violence, coercion, abuse and neglect ability to access remedy and redress for violations suffered. Sector coordination is aimed at ensuring that international responses to humanitarian emergencies are predictable, accountable and have clear leadership role ensuring a balanced division of labour among organizations both UN agencies and NGOs.Cluster/sectors co-chaired by NGOs tend to create stronger partnerships, advocacy and information sharing and result in more inclusive engagement of all stakeholders. Yet, INGOs cochairing different sectors in Libya have limited financial capacity and it's often challenging to divert their already limited resources for coordination activities. To be able to provide dedicated capacity to the sector, there is need to provide the organizations with enough resources and support to avoid extra burden on the already tight INGOs structures.The responsibility of the Protection Sector is also to provide support at intersectoral level on key aspects of protection mainstreaming and centrality of protection, recognizing that these are collective responsibilities of all humanitarian actors. Considering the need for the Protection Sector to provide technical guidance also at interagency level, the robust internal coordination structure within the Protection sector (Areas of Responsibility) and responsibility to provide protection focused information to decision makers there is a justified need for dedicated resources from both the Global Lead Agency and NGOs to support coordination.

2 - Activities

In line with the IASC reference module on cluster coordination and the Principled of Partnership the co-coordinator will work along the coordinator from the lead agency to:1. Support service delivery by:▪ Providing a platform that ensures service delivery is driven by the HRP and strategic priorities.▪ Developing mechanisms to eliminate duplication of service delivery2. Inform the HC/HCT’s strategic decision-making by:▪ Preparing needs assessments and analysis of gaps to inform the setting of priorities particularly related to Centrality of Protection and Protection Mainstreaming▪ Identifying and finding solutions for (emerging) gaps, obstacles, duplication and cross-cutting issues.▪ Formulating priorities on the basis of analysis incl. supporting the review/redevelopment of the HCT Protection Strategy 3. Plan and implement cluster strategies by:▪ Developing sectoral plans, objectives and indicators that directly support realization of the overall response’s strategic objectives.▪ Applying and adhering to common standards and guidelines.▪ Clarifying funding requirements, helping to set priorities, and agreeing cluster contributions to the HC’s overall humanitarian funding proposals.4. Monitor and evaluate performance by:▪ Monitoring and reporting on activities and needs including implementation quality monitoring ▪ Measuring progress against the cluster strategy and agreed results.▪ Recommending corrective action where necessary.5. Build national capacity in preparedness and contingency planning6. Support robust advocacy by:▪ Identifying concerns, and contributing key information and messages to HC and HCT messaging and action on critical protection issues informed by protection sensitive evidence ▪ Undertaking advocacy on behalf of the cluster, cluster members, and affected people.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.3 - among groups likely to affected are persons who do not have their representation in decision making bodies. Mitigation measures include: (1) advocating for a government engagement strategy at HCT or a least ISCG level to ensure risk informed and coherent approach towards work with the government, (2) ensure the engagement of sector and its partners if informed by conflict analysis to support conflict sensitive approaches understanding the existing power dynamics and risks of exclusion or legitimization 4.6 - at risk are persons in need of specialized protection services (GBV, MHPSS, legal aid) whose personal data might be accidentally disclosed if data protection SOPs are not adhered to when sharing assessment, registration data. Additionally, service providers might be put at risk if their personal data is disclosed. To mitigate this risk the Protection Sector will (1) periodically revise its data sharing protocol and related SOPs (2) provide refresher training and orientation to protection and non - protection partners on data protection measures especially related to referrals, sharing assessment data (3) ensure no personal data is shared via email and all files containing personal information are password protected (4) managing access to the service mapping (5) following PIM principles of defined purpose, do no harm, data confidentiality when designing protection information products ensuring a cost/benefit analysis is conducted when determining the level of disaggregation at which information will be made publicly available (6) streamlining protection information management processes and increasing joint PIM initiatives to mitigate risks for individual agencies engaged in protection data collection4.15 - Currently, there is no harmonized approach to PSEA nor a PSEA network in Libya and many agencies rely on their agency specific mechanisms to respond to SEA. This creates a significant gap, which if not addressed is likely to affect particularly women and adolescent girls in need of assistance, recognizing men and boys can also be subjected to SEA. While coordinating a PSEA network is not the responsibility of the Protection Sector but a collective responsibility enforced at the HCT level the Protection Sector has the responsibility to continuously advocate and provide technical guidance ensuring harmonized approaches and SOPs are established and followed. Mitigation measures: (1) ensure continuous advocacy to set up an inclusive (UN, NGO incl. national/local organizations) PSEA network (2) provide technical guidance on streamlined SOPs for PSEA incl. working together with the CFM to establish ways in which the CFM can be used to support SEA prevention and response efforts.4.22 - the key responsibility of the Protection Sector is to ensure information related to access to specialized protection services and referral SOPs are made available to all responding partners protection and non-protection. Failure to do so puts at risk survivors and victims of rights abuses and violations. The key mitigation measures include: (1) maintaining, updating and developing further a robust service mapping tool (2) providing capacity support and ad hoc guidance to protection and non protection actors on inter-agency referral SOP (3) analyzing gaps in service coverage and identifying ways to close them through supporting in addressing root causes, strong coordination of sector partners and with OCHA access team, and when relevant advocacy with donors
