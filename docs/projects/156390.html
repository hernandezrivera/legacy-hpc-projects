Project Summary

The planned activities support CCCM strategic objective number one to improve physical conditions in IDP sites. Atma cluster considered one of the biggest IDPs sites clusters. Where more than 76,510 people living in the overall camps (20,012 girls 23,049 boys 17,726 women and 15,723 men) according to CCCM ISIMM December 2018. New extensions have been established inside the cluster and the humanitarian access to the site is difficult due to the road nature. The project will gravel the opened roads to these extensions. Global communities is the focal point in Atma for the CCCM cluster. The project will be implemented through contractors and GC will ensure the quality of the implementation to meet the CCCM guidelines and international standards.

Needs

According to HNO 2019, the NW of Syria has witnessed more than 1 million population movements in 2018, many of these movements were for IDPs who had been displaced multiple times. On average, more than 96,000 monthly population movements compared to 82,000 in 2017 and 40,000 in 2016 in north-west Syria. The main causes of these displacements were renewed military offensives in Idleb, Aleppo, Hama governorates (particularly in September-December 2017). CCCM cluster in Gaziantep through the ISIMM tool has monitored the IDPs sites situation and As of December 2018, there are 325 active IDP sites registered into the CCCM database, which are hosting a total of 464,509 IDPs/85,834 families. In December 2018, Cluster has coordinated the provision of a lifesaving multi-sectoral response to 446,159 IDPs living in the IDP sites. More than 76,510 people living in the overall camps (20,012 girls 23,049 boys 17,726 women and 15,723 men)are living in Atma camps. GC conducted needs assessment in Atma and results shows that there is a need to gravel 13,500 m2 of the key roads

Implementation of activities

From our office in Gaziantep and sub-office in Qah, GC will be responsible for overall program strategy and design, reporting, grant administration and compliance, procurement, vendor and sub-award payments, monitoring and evaluation (M&E) and quality assurance/quality control (QA/QC), and capacity building of staff. Program staff will oversee direct implementation of all proposed activities. Our Syrian staff will enter Syria as appropriate to monitor program implementation.

Protection Risk Analysis

Consistent with our standard approach to humanitarian programming, the Program will follow Sphere Guidelines in implementing activities. The Program is based on the "Do No Harm" principle, with activities designed to be culturally appropriate and tailored to the specific context while preventing competition over resources. In settings where humanitarian needs are extreme and resources are scarce, GC recognizes that providing any material assistance has the potential to cause conflict and protection concerns, or to negatively impact local economic conditions. We will make every effort to mitigate the potential for conflict stemming from program resources by maintaining a conflict-sensitive approach to implementation; and promoting open community engagement and transparency in beneficiary selection criteria and processes, and materials distribution and assistance. GC incorporates beneficiary feedback and complaint mechanisms into program design. Our existing mechanism includes providing beneficiaries with feedback forms and contact numbers when assistance is distributed. We publicly post our anonymous feedback hotline number at public and program locations (such as child-friendly spaces and women and girls safe spaces) in target communities and in Atmeh, so that community members may send feedback to us by phone, SMS or WhatsApp. We provide the hotline number to individual HHs during beneficiary verification, assistance distribution, and post-distribution monitoring (PDM) visits to each HH, to ensure that the most vulnerable or disadvantaged have access to the information and the ability to submit his/ her own feedback.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The award will include approvals as requested by GC at the time of proposal submission for restricted items such as long-term vehicle rental and equipment (i.e., generators). • The Bab al Hawa border crossing between Turkey and Syria will remain open and accessible for humanitarian goods and staff, without extended closures, and/or alternate border points are opened and accessible; • GC staff continue to have consistent access to target locations; • Active violence does not directly impact GC's offices in Gaziantep, or Qah for a sustained period of time; • Violence in the targeted areas remains at a level which allows GC to implement the program, with both armed and unarmed parties to the conflict allowing access to vulnerable HHs for deliveries of humanitarian aid, and allowing GC to maintain acceptable levels of independence, impartiality, and neutrality; • Camp managers, Local Councils, relief committees, and community stakeholders will collaborate with GC, and facilitate access for delivery of humanitarian assistance; and • GC is able to obtain appropriate export/re-export license renewals in time to allow for program implementation according to the approved work plan.
