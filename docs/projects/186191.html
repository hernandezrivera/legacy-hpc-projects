Project Summary

Following the latest humanitarian report provided by OCHA in Cameroon, out of 4,300,000 people identified as in need of humanitarian assistance, 2,300,000 are children under 18 years. Considering that protection issues for children prevailed prior to the crisis, they have regrettably escalated and expanded following the emergency outbreak.
Since the beginning of the conflict in 2016, children continue to be one of the most affected population categories by the ongoing violence fueled by the war in the South West and North West regions of Cameroon, and the subsequent multiple community displacements subjecting them to suffering and severe deterioration of emotional wellbeing. As the conflict continues, more cases of sexual violence, harassment, armed robbery, and forced and early marriages accompanied by unwanted pregnancies among children, are being reported in the Divisions of Manyu, Lebialem, and Kupe-Manenguba in the South West region of Cameroon. Back to school dreams are becoming a nightmare for children as many of them are being reportedly threatened, recruited into armed forces, and used by the armed groups. Moreover, as families try to cope and survive and due to insufficient humanitarian assistance, children are engaging in negative copping strategies in the labor market and sometimes in the worst forms of labor, including association with armed groups, sex for survival, early and force mirages, acting as key informants to both parties in the conflict, and armed robbery. These copping strategies pose long-term consequences to their emotional, psycho-social, spiritual, and physiological development, and making them extremely vulnerable as reports from the field confirm.
For some children, school shut down has landed them into informal education environment especially in rural and peri-rural areas caused mainly by the surge of violence of the war and the lack or threatened educational opportunities in the affected communities.
A number of available social structures have also been weakened. This continues to impose further vulnerability on the affected population resulting into other child protection risks e.g lack/loss of birth certificates, which continues to hinder young boys and girls access to basic services, when available, and exposing them to further risks of exploitation and arbitrary arrest/detention.
Also, due to numerous attacks and new Communities becoming soft spots to armed groups, there has been an increase in the number of Unaccompanied and Separated children.
In terms of access and acceptability in the affected communities, Caritas, has a track record of acceptability due to her operational presence in all communities and presence on the ground even before the outbreak of the war. CARITAS Mamfe has worked and created a lot of developmental impact in her area of coverage and has won the trust and confidence of the local population. Both parties in the conflict accord Caritas access in the execution of her work. Caritas has well-qualified, committed, discrete and professional staff that work to ensure quality results. Conscious of protections issues, Caritas ensures that children are always safe and we avoid going into areas that expose children and workers to any danger, whether real or perceived. In working with the children, we put standard operating procedures for prevention of covid-19 in place for every person. We also take care of inclusive service to people with disability and our operations are sensitive to gender inclusivity. Our integrated community based approach in all interventions, has given the community a sense of inclusion and participation in their own development and decisions that concerns them. An approach that takes into account all age, gender and diversity of the affected population in all its programming and implementation.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Children in Manyu, Lebialem and kupe-Manenguba Divisions are highly vulnerable. They are exposed to child protection and Gender based violence risks. The war has accelerated the vulnerability of families making them to adapt harmful practices like early or forced marriage, and other forms of exploitation as survival mechanisms to overcome their distress. Moreover, the existing protection structures are weak resulting in a low protective environment and a higher exposure to the various risks.• In this light, this project will seek to Strengthen Community-based child protection systems (within the COVID-19 context) to prevent and respond to child protection and gender-based violence risks among IDPs and host communities affected by the conflict that is causing humanitarian catastrophe. • The project shall also address the need for sensitization of community members (women, men, boys and girls) on Gender Based Violence/Child Protection risks and positive parenting. • The is the need for identification and documentation of unaccompanied and separated children, and subsequent family tracing and reunification• Need to provide case management to Children facing risks of abuse, violence and exploitation. • Provide psychosocial support to children and caregivers through Child Friendly spaces.• Advocating for and facilitating acquisition of civil documents for children without birth certificates or have lost documents either because it is burnt in the house or because it cannot be traced as a result of numerous displacements. This shall reduce administrative bottlenecks families experience due to lack of knowledge on administrative procedure to obtain civil status.• Need for basic hygiene materials. Many families live where it is hard in areas where basic hygiene materials are hard to find thereby exposing them to poor hygiene conditions. This project will also enable Caritas Mamfe to support women/girls with dignity kits and sensitize them and other community members on the importance and ways of maintaining proper hygiene practices for the betterment of the entire communities.• Rehabilitation. Many children are directly affected by this war either by association with armed groups or by being released from detention. These children need special attention and support. The project intends to provide this rehabilitation support to the children. Caritas Mamfe will identify suspected or verified children associated with armed groups including children released from detention and provide them with temporary care or give them family/community reintegration support.
