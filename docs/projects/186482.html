Project Summary

Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates for North East Nigeria, over 1.5 million people needed nutrition interventions in 2021, a number with the potential to increase, largely exacerbated by the ongoing insecurity and displacement in North East Nigeria.
GHIV Africa is proposing a multisectoral project approach which to providing lifesaving nutrition assistance to the most vulnerable groups who are at risk of malnutrition in, Damboa Konduga and Ngala LGAs. The primary objectives are
1. Rehabilitation MAM children from 6-59 months to prevent deterioration of MAM children to SAM, to reduce mortality and morbidity risk in the target group.
2. To reduce mortality and morbidity risk in the target group.
3. Support children who are successfully discharge from OTP to prevent relapse
4. Provide nutritional education and counselling.
To achieve the above objectives the following activities will be carried out:
1) Stakeholders mapping and engagement • (2) Community entry/diagnosis and Identification and selection of beneficiaries, (3) Identification and training of community nutrition mobilizers (CNMs) on preparation of Tom brown supplement and IYCF.(4)establishment of lead mothers,
Tom Brown supplementary feeding program for 6-59month MAM children which the primary purpose is to help children with MAM recuperate and to prevent their deterioration to SAM. This shall be done through 1) Stakeholders mapping and engagement (2) Community entry/diagnosis and Identification and selection of beneficiaries, (3) Identification and training of community nutrition volunteers (CNVs) on preparation of Tom brown supplement and IYCF. (4) establishment of lead mothers, through (5) screening and referral, (6) eight weeks of supplemental feeding, (7) Infant and young child feed (IYCF) (7) weekly MUAC monitoring.
For IYCF component activities include: (1.) Establishment of mother-to-mother support group (2). Establishment of father-to-father support group (3.) one on one and group skilled counselling (4.) Cooking demonstration.
Also, identified SAM without complication will be treated In OTP with RUTF while SAM with medical complication will be referred to Stabilization centre.

1 - Needs

The north-east conflict continues to directly worsen the affected population’s nutritional status, especially the most vulnerable and those in inaccessible locations. Global acute malnutrition (GAM) levels in children aged 6-59 months are on the increase across the BAY states when compared year-on-year: according to the ninth round of the NFSS, the GAM prevalence in Yobe and Borno states are above the emergency threshold of 10% (13.6% and 10.7% respectively as of October 2020). This includes about 300,000 children currently suffering from severe acute malnutrition (SAM) requiring urgent treatment, and over half a million children moderately malnourished. Without adequate treatment, one in every five of these children could die. The sector attributes this to the worsening food security and WASH situation in the area amidst access and security constraints.The crisis has also exacerbated pre-existing conditions of malnutrition, driven by a number of inter-related factors. These include weak health infrastructures; poor infant and young-child feeding (IYCF) practices; limited access to safe water and health and sanitation services; the prevalence of water-borne diseases; poor hygiene conditions; and seasonal food insecurity (2021, Humanitarian Needs Overview).In line with the above, to address the issue of malnutrition in Northeast Nigeria, other sector like WASH and food security components should be mainstreamed into Nutrition intervention.From this proposed action GHIV Africa shall be implementing Tom Brown programming and Infant and young child feeding. Tom brown supplement is a locally made supplement powder for 6-59months MAM children. The ingredients are made up of Maize, soyabeans and groundnuts which can be sourced locally. GHIV Africa will work with community nutrition volunteers (CNVs), individuals who live in the communities where Tom Brown programming is implemented. These mobilizers will be trained by GHIV Africa on preparation of Tom brown supplement and optimal IYCF practices. They will then screen children under 5 in their respective communities for acute malnutrition and refer and enroll those with MAM 6-59months into the Tom Brown program. Children 0-59months identified with SAM are referred to existing OTPs for treatment. Lead mothers will be chosen by the community, to oversee 12 caregivers of children with MAM. The will group meets with their respective mobilizers weekly for eight weeks to prepare and distribute Tom Brown powder and receive IYCF and hygiene messaging from the lead mothers, supported by mobilizers. The mobilizers also measure enrolled children’s mid upper arm circumference (MUAC) weekly for eight weeks to track their progress, conduct weekly home visits, and monitor preparation of Tom Brown powder and feeding practices. Oversight of caregivers by the mobilizers will promotes the children’s recovery and allows for any necessary adjustments during the program (e.g., hygiene, texture of Tom Brown, addressing any myths around IYCF the caregivers may have, etc.).

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

GHIV Africa will ensure that safety, dignity, accountability and do no harm procedures are implemented and respected during the entire period of implementation. GHIV Africa will constantly monitor the security situation of the area of intervention in order for the mobile teams to choose secured and authorized roads to access the vulnerable IDPs in need. The mobile teams will only organise their activities in places considered as safe, in areas agreed on with the local authorities to minimize the risks taken by beneficiaries of settling in areas of continuing conflict or areas that have not been cleared of landmines and unexploded ordnance.To support the recognition of protection concerns, GHIV Africa field staffs will receive an initial training on child protection and GBV mainstreaming before the implementation of the activities. There will also be an emphasis to ensure an equal number of project staff will be female to ease the communication and interaction with female beneficiaries. Mobile teams will allow a flexible access to various locations where IDPs will be established, even temporarily, protection services will thus be available during day time, ensure safety for children, and the teams are free to come back to any location if any urgent needs arise. In order to avoid the creation of tensions or added risk for the beneficiaries who will receive items through the distribution of NFIs and food distribution for in the project activities, GHIV Africa will ensure strong information sharing on selection criteria, the value of items and usage and distribution processes, and that beneficiaries’ specific needs and opinions are taken into account. GHIV Africa’s MEAL department will establish a feedback mechanism that beneficiaries can access.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GHIV Africa has established a Complaint and response mechanism (CRM) – a formalized process providing a safe confidential, accessible, transparent and effective channel for beneficiaries and other relevant stakeholders to raise concerns, register complaints, call for investigation and make suggestions. GHIV Africa will systematically investigate and follow up on complaints in a timely manner. The purpose of the CRM and relevant contact information (phone, email, physical address etc.) is communicated to beneficiaries through complaints boxes wherever relevant, project activities as well as on distributed materials and / or at distribution sites. Staff will explain the purpose to beneficiaries. Received complaints are categorized according to level of sensitivity and severity and responded to in accordance to GHIV Africa’s Standard Operating Procedures for Programme-related Complaints Response and Feedback Mechanism. GHIV Africa will inform beneficiaries and the broader community on the selection criteria as well as on the distribution process in order to avoid the creation of tension. GHIV Africa’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) mechanisms provide a two-way communication channel through which beneficiaries and affected populations can give feedback and complaints, which will enable GHIV Africa to adjust its intervention accordingly and address issues directly in a time-effective manner. GHIV Africa will also ensure that its staff will comply with its code of conduct and serious measures will be taken if staff violates it during the course of the project. Lastly, GHIV Africa's firm commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance.
