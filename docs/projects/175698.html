Project Summary

The project aims to increase equitable access to quality of education services in conflict affected communities of Upper Nile, Central Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria states. The project specific objectives include; (1)Improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 805,951 emergency and COVID-19 affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities, and youth (girls and boys) by 2021 in counties under IPC 4 and 5. (2)Improve the quality of education services of at all levels education system and protect the wellbeing of all children, including children with disabilities and teachers' capacity to deliver quality services and prevent the negative impact of the COVID-19 crisis on learning environments by 2021 in counties under IPC 4 and 5.
(3)Strengthen the education system’s capacity of the school community and education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergency and the transmission of. Therefore, the project targets 18200 children. UNKEA will establish 16 temporary learning spaces for children and renovate 10 spaces, a total of 10247 children will benefit from early childhood and development kits and over 900 school children will get access to gender-sensitive WASH facilities in the project. The project aimed to implement the following interventions; i) Establish 16 temporary learning spaces, ii) Build capacity of teachers, iii)Build capacity of local school management committees, iv) Create COVID-19 awareness messages in school v) Provide mental psychosocial support services for children in IDP camps, vi) Establish virtual learning environment for candidate classes to ensure catch-up or accelerated learning sessions for children vii) Provide gender sensitive and disability WASH facilities in schools viii) Run advocacy meetings with key stakeholders on girl-child education in camps ix) Provide Early Childhood Development Kits to children in IDP camps.

1 - Needs

Years of protracted crisis in South Sudan has left social services in deplorable state. Education remains most affected with over 2 million children estimated to be out of schools (Report on Out-of-School Children, UNESCO 2018). The combined impact of political conflicts, floods, and food shortage has made it significantly difficult for learning to take place in schools. Girls remain the most disadvantaged due to pressure of marriage, challenge of pregnancy and domestic duties (Nation Wide Assessment Report, 2018).It is estimated that over 3.4 million people in South Sudan are in need of education services and 51% are female and 100% are children of which 15% of the children have disability (Humanitarian report 2020). These combined factors led to 36% of the population in categorized in severe and 18% extreme and 3% of the population are in catastrophic States. Flooding in South Sudan particularly in July 2020 had profound impacts on the livelihood of the people in Upper Nile, Lake States and Eastern Equatoria. Many flood-affected people took shelter in schools, health facilities that affected reopening of schools as announced by Ministry of General education. In 2020, a total of 430 schools in 9 states have been damaged/destroyed by flood and violence (HNO,2020). Therefore, UNKEA will conduct accelerated learning opportunities for those children in IDP camps as well as host communities in order to catch-up with learning sessions. The project will also address shortage of acute qualified teachers in those conflict-affected Counties of Longechuk, Kajokeji, Nasir, Magwi and Morobo. A total of 10 learning spaces will be renovated and 16 constructed during the project lifespan. The persistent fragility and economic crisis in South Sudan forced about 98500 school-aged refugee children of whom 18000 children out of school coupled with negative impact if COVID-19 and subsequent school closure led to increased number of children in need of education support (School Attendance Monitoring System database 2019) The closure of schools and all learning institutions followed by COVID-19 had devastating effects on the education of all children including refugees and jeopardized critical gains made over the years. It also exposed them to different forms of gender-based violence including forced marriage and teenage pregnancies. Education needs remain high among South Sudanese spontaneous refugee returnees. Similarly, access to remote learning platforms and education opportunities by refugees from neighboring countries are limited by lack of electricity and connectivity, and required devices including radio sets and lighting. Post-secondary opportunities for refugee students remain limited. Refugees in urban areas lack the financial means to access the national education system services (FSNMS+,2020)The girl-child school enrolment in South Sudan is less than 45% and only 25% of girls in school receive secondary education reflecting only 19% of the total population are literate females in the Country (School Attendance Monitoring tool database,2019). It is therefore alarming trend. Girls are more likely to be excluded from receiving education as parents prefer to send boys to school than girls. This increases the risk of girls ending in an early marriage or teenage pregnancy and sexual gender based violence in conflict affected areas such as Kajokeji, Magwi, Longechuk,Mawuit and Nasir.The displaced people, refugees and returnees from Ethiopia, Uganda and Sudan are identified as vulnerable groups and the education need of children in these groups of people are higher compared to the host community as they have no access to formal education due to limited number of qualified teachers, lack of electricity, flooding, low payment of teachers and radio stations to disseminate education messages to children during the lock down. UNKEA will focus in improving equitable access to safe and protective education services for 39583 children

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project is a set of specific interventions that focus on strengthening the child protection system at the school and at the community level, thus empowering local communities to strengthen the protection of children from abuse, neglect, exploitation and other forms of violence. The model strengthens both the protective environment for children, as well as children themselves, in order to improve their well-being and fulfil their rights to protection. The primary beneficiaries of this project are children who are affected by conflicts and at risk of, or currently suffering in situations of abuse, exploitation, neglect, discrimination or other forms of violence within families or communities. UNKEA will use internal and external models developed to provide a comprehensive framework for child protection issues at the community level and to establish an evidence base for advocacy efforts at community, state and national levels. UNKEA will use a systems strengthening approach which emphasises prevention, protection and response, coordination between sectors and integrated responses that can benefit all school children. This project will incorporate the learning and experience from numerous community-based child protection, adapting rich experiences and practices from inside and outside the organization. In order to uphold ‘Do No Harm’ all persons engaged through the project will be subjected to sign a code of conduct to protect children, boys and girls from abuse. This will apply to staff, education personnel, contractors, and school/community structures that that shall take part in any of the project activities. Apart from this, UNKEA has zero tolerant policy to physical, sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) to children up to 18 years of age. This policy is disseminated to all staff and those rendering contractual services to UNKEA projects.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During project design, the project staff conducted consultation with various stakeholders among whom were the displaced, returnees and host people. guided by accessibility of the services to both the host community and displaced people. All services to be provided by the project are free of charge and thus accessible by anyone. In addition, the structured and frequent consultative dialogue meetings will ensure that the perspectives of displaced people continue to filter through and guide the subsequent project activities and interventions. The formal consultations will include focus group discussions, in-depth interviews with key informants that include local authority administrators, leaders of women groups, representatives of people living with disabilities, a youth representatives, structured dialogue meetings with community members in all the target implementation areas.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

UNKEA increasingly recognizes that listening to the views and priorities of people in aid recipient societies is fundamental to improving the effectiveness of assistance efforts. Her commitment to learning from and including the voices of recipients in an effort to improve the quality of programming and to engage them in decisions that directly affect their lives. This recognition has resulted in a great deal of rhetoric about effective participation and inclusion of recipients and some concerted efforts to put these commitments into practice.
