Project Summary

In 2020, 3.5 million population lacks consistent access to primary and secondary health care services. This is due to the reason that more than half of the functional health facilities in 2019 are closed due to the intensification of conflict and the onset of COVID19 in 2020. Lifesaving health services such as reproductive, maternal, newborn, child and adolescent Health (RMNCAH) and mental health services have been severely disrupted, neglected and underfunded in the past many years. The situation amid COVID19 pandemic is further compounded by lack of personal protective equipment (PPEs), delays in payment of salaries to the health care providers, and closer of many health facilities or the suspension of operations especially in maternity hospitals in urban areas. The supply chain management for medical supplies, equipment and pharmaceuticals are globally and locally disrupted and it has closed many public health facilities. Due to this situation, majority of the patients are now referring to private health care facilities, which is making out of pocket expenditure more catastrophic for the people, especially for IDPs, migrants and people living in conflict zones or along the migration routes in Libya.
This project is designed to maintain and provide emergency reproductive and sexual health services amid covid19 pandemic to 53,800 women of child bearing age, among conflict affected, IDPs and migrant population and host communities. The services are planned to be provided in severely affected municipalities / geographical areas ranked above 3 on the severity scale with a focus on un(der)served population. The project intends to provide EmONC services to an estimated population of 9,157 pregnant women. Services will mainly target migrants, refugees, returnees and IDPs and host communities in Tripoli, Benghazi, Sebha, Misurata and Sirt and people in need among none-displaced and migrants are more specifically targeted in affected municipalities or hard to reach areas of Ghat, Sirt, Murzuq, Alkufra and Aljufra.
Considering the humanitarian situation in the country, scarcity of midwives and OBGYN specialists, low capacity level and limited availability of female healthcare providers (especially specialized nurses and midwifes) to provide quality SRH services and unavailability of SRH supplies in the health facilities, there is clearly the need for a humanitarian complementary service package. The services that will be provided through this project are in line with minimum initial services package (MISP) for life saving sexual and reproductive health services of UNFPA and Essential Health Service (EHS) package of WHO issued for the context of COVID19 pandemic. UNFPA will work with its partner agencies, stakeholders, communities and local authorities for the following:
1. Provision of Lifesaving Essential Maternal, sexual and reproductive health services to prevent excess neonatal and maternal mortality and morbidity among affected population.
2. Coordinate Sexual and Reproductive Health Services to ensure continuous provision of essential SRH services
3. Provision of Essential medicine, medical supplies and equipment to sustain health SRH services
4. Increase access to SRH services through the deployment of mobile medical teams and enhance the performance of existing primary healthcare facilities and community health workers in targeted areas of vulnerable population.
5. Strengthen health sector and community resilience to ensure social protection and sustainable and equitable access to quality SRH services.

1 - Needs

Despite the recent peace truce of July 2020 in Libya, the country remains at high risk of conflict resumption and the spread of COVID19 due to the presence of a large number of foreign armed groups, human and drug trafficking, lack of rule of law, presence of corruption and uncontrolled boarders, the political instability and fragile health system. The last 10 years of protracted crises and the exponential increase in the number of COVDI19 cases to a more than 84,000, as reported by the National Center for Disease Control (NCDC) have left 3.5 million people without constant access to life-saving healthcare services and essential medicines. According to Humanitarian needs overview 2021; among these people, 1.2 million are IDPs, refugees, migrants, returnees are people living with most severe needs. This is the double of figures in 2020 that reflects the deteriorating health system and increased burden of diseases due to COVID19 pandemic. Tertiary, primary and secondary healthcare facilities and human resource across Libya are not rationally distributed according to population density and needs. More than half of the health facilities functional in 2019 are closed in 2020 due to conflict and COVID19 pandemic that is overstretching health sector resources. Essential health services such as sexual, reproductive and maternal and mental health services are not available in almost 90% of the primary healthcare facilities. Hospitals and primary health facilities across Libya have regularly suspended operations due to high rates of COVDI19 infection among the staff and patients and also due to the absence of Personal protective equipment (PPEs), lack of electricity and delays in salary payment to healthcare staff in Libya. The capacity gaps are also exacerbated by interruptions of logistics and supply chain management resulting in shortage of supplies, pharmaceuticals and equipment. The far-flung areas have no adequate skilled health workforce. Among the affected population of almost 1.2 million, there would be an average of 168 births per day, of which 15% would need emergency obstetric and neonatal care (EmONC), including at least 5% Caesarian section. Amid covid19 pandemic, increase in other morbidities can lead to miscarriages and fetal losses; malnutrition leading to anemia, further increases the death toll because of post-partum hemorrhage. This humanitarian situation has exposed people, especially women and girls to violence, extreme poverty, separation from families and communities. The lack of access to healthcare and community support has increased the risk of women and girls to sexual violence and exploitation. Family planning is not available in public health facilities and limitations to quality SRH services could lead to higher mortality rates among women and children. The lack of access to emergency obstetric care, the lack of access to critical supplies such as PPEs can increase the spread COVID19 and STIs, including HIV, and increase unsafe abortions, unwanted pregnancies and morbidity related to high fertility rates. The provision of Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) services including ante and postnatal care, family planning and the management of sexually transmitted infections have all collapsed; and there has been an alarming increase in rates of cesarean sections throughout the country. Mental health remains chronically neglected with only one mental health functional hospital in the entire country. Due to the closure of health facilities and lack of health staff, most of patients are now referring to private health facilities (if available) that is making out of pocket expenditure more catastrophic, especially for IDPs, migrants and people living in conflict zones. SRH is closely related to personal dignity, choice as well as to physical and mental health issue. Hence increasing access to SRH services with a holistic and rights-based approach in the context of Libya is the main objective of this project.

1. Provide Lifesaving SRH service to prevent excess neonatal and maternal mortality and morbidity.a. Establish isolation and treatment facilities for COVID19 positive pregnant women in 3 referral hospitals. b. Provide clinical management of rape survivors and strengthen referral mechanism from primary to higher level of health facilities (HF).c. Provide Psycho-social services at HF and community levels through female health workers. 2. Strengthen coordination to ensure continuous provision of essential SRH services a. Ensure coordination and leadership for the SRH Sub-working group with the Ministry of Health;b. Develop the capacity of health care providers and managers (with a focus on female health cadre) on coordination, assessment, planning and provision of basic and comprehensive SRH services. 3. Provision of Essential medicine medical supplies and equipment to sustain health SRH servicesa. Provide emergency RH kits, PPEs, medicines and supplies.b. Distribute of clean delivery kits to visibly pregnant women on the move. 4. Increase access to SRH services through enhancing the performance of PHC facilities and community health workers.a. Deploy mobile medical teams along with skilled birth attendance to provide EmONC servicesb. Strengthening referral system for emergency obstetric care servicesc. Provide focused trainings i.e. BEmONC and CEmONC, Family Planning, Prevention of COVID19 d. Prevent COVID19, Sexually transmitted infections (STIs) and HIV / AIDS through increasing capabilities of HFs and training of staff 5. Strengthen health sector and community resilience to ensure social protection, sustainable and equitable access to quality SRH services. a. Prevent COVID19 through information, education and behavior change communication activities to population and health care providersb. Strengthen female and community healthcare workers and establish a referral pathway from community to health facilities.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

UNFPA considers best practices from its experience across the globe and specific experience from Libya and will adhere to the safety and security measures for Risk 4.1 and 4.4 on protection of beneficiaries from COVID19 contamination by training and deploying community health workers mainly selected from among the vulnerable communities to report on the concerns of beneficiaries and also inform and educate communities on the prevention measures for COVID19. Each of the community health workers will be supported by locally identified 10 women health action group members who will assist her with the distribution of information, education and communication materials (brochures, posters, pamphlets and others). Risk 4.9 and 4.11 about the local needs and people with disabilities will be identified and addressed through the establishment of local committee formed with each of the muncipality and relevant health facility authorities. This committee will also be helpful in mobilization of local resources, ownership of the project, protection of project assets and sustainability of the action in the community.UNFPA has a rich experience in mitigating the risks about sexual violence and exploitations and has already engaged local communities, national non-governmental organizations and has active women and girls safe spaces with trained community workers to implement the project with opportunities for capacity building and hands on practice sessions for their staff on the application of global standards in programmatic and operational areas to prevent sexual exploitation and violence. The increased engagement of local actors and strong networking of with local communities will ensure the ownership and support through those actors and UNFPA will further strengthen the communication with both state and non-state actors to facilitate access for beneficiaries to the services and utilize the existing establish GBV referral pathways for the survivor of GBV from the health community to health facilities and other judiciary institutions where UNFPA has trained services providers.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

UNFPA designed this project to address the emergency health needs of women and children in humanitarian settings. This package of activities ensure access of women, girls, men and boys to basic and emergency SRH services and addresses SRH needs and rights equally amid COVID19 pandemic. UNFPA will ensure accountability through coordination with MOH, consultation with the target population and feedback from the communities. In addition, UNFPA will ensure accountability to the health cluster, UNOCHA and the donors through implementation of the project activities with full transparency and integrity and in a timely manner. Reports of the project activities will be prepared in close coordination with health cluster and implementing partners. Training activities for different RMNCH component such as BEmONC, COVID19 prevention, STI and MISP have been planned in collaboration with MoH and after a thorough capacity needs assessment and in consultation with health coordinator and stakeholders on the ground in the prioritized municipalities. Female and male community representatives will regularly be consulted at health facility level to reflect their opinions and advice in project implementation through a community council. Capacity building of health professionals, men and women with sufficient knowledge and skills will also increase access of affected women from affected communities to SRH services including services for clinical management of rape survivors. Increasing awareness of men will support in reducing gender based violence incidences and will improve referral of SRH and GBV survivors.Some other cross cutting issues like prevention of STI, COVID19, safe blood transfusion in Comprehensive EmNOC facilities will be addressed through provision of different RH Kits.WHO Waste management guidelines will be implemented for safe disposal. Monitoring checklist of relevant health facilities will be reviewed to ensure incinerators existence in HF supported by this project.
