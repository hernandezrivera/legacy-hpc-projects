Project Summary

According to UNICEF, 600 000 children are living with disabilities in Zimbabwe. Children with disabilities (CWD) are more vulnerable during disasters. CWD are more likely to be neglected as caregivers focus on food and may be away from home for days. CWD are likely to be left in the care of elderly grandparents as parents search for food and livelihoods.
Zimbabwe experienced torrential rainfall caused by Cyclone Idai in March 2019. Tropical Cyclone Idai caused high winds and heavy precipitation in Chimanimani, Chipinge, Buhera, Nyanga, Makoni, Mutare Rural, Bikita, Masvingo and Gutu Districts, causing flash flooding and subsequent deaths, destruction of livelihoods and properties. To date, Chimanimani district is the most affected. An estimated 50,000 households/270,000 people were affected by flooding and landslides in Chimanimani and Chipinge districts. 634 people were reported dead and 257 people reported to be missing.
The onset of the rains in November 2019 has already had a significant impact with 19 people confirmed dead due to violent storms, destruction of critical infrastructure including health facilities and homesteads, a total of 23 people have been displaced. An estimated 63 schools have suffered damage due to violent storms.
The economic situation in Zimbabwe has negatively impacted livelihoods and impaired capacities to withstand shocks. The crisis in the health sector which has seen a protracted industrial action by medical personnel coupled with acute shortage of essential medication has left many families vulnerable. The unavailability of clean potable water for urban populations has further worsened the risk for the outbreak of diarrhoeal diseases.
Given the situation above, the risk of injury and consequential disability becomes elevated for children, furthermore the effects of these multiple hazards are more acute for CWD who have impaired capacity to avoid injury.
Lessons learnt from Cyclone Idai have pointed to poor disaster awareness, preparedness and response. This provides a platform to further strengthen disaster awareness and preparedness through actively engaging communities on disaster risk analysis and creating mechanisms for improved risk mitigation, prevention and disaster response. One of the key takeaways from the cyclone was the importance of building community level disaster preparedness through working with key stakeholders in health, education the communities themselves.
The devastation from the cyclone and onset of thunderstorms with flash floods creates additional challenges for persons with disabilities and appropriate care arrangements must be made as some children have lost their caregivers. Assistive devices such as crutches as wheelchairs are required, but some may not be suitable due to terrain. Child friendly spaces must be inclusive and should provide extra staff who can monitor children with disabilities to free family members / care givers to access relief donations/activities. Training of community cadres will be required so that they share correct information and are equipped to work with affected families and children with disabilities. Need to strengthen linkage to health services for drugs for those with disabilities and chronic health conditions such as epilepsy, HIV which need adherence to avoid progression to disability. Need to share accessible information on child protection and prevention of sexual violence and abuse and how to access services. There is need for inclusive psychosocial support including counselling. Appropriate food/diet for those with special diets may also be required.
Disability specific needs (physiotherapy/assistive devices) are also likely to be ignored/neglected as parents divert the little income, they must acquire food. The project aims to capacitat communities and families of children with disabilities in DRR, and improve access to services like rehabilitation and assistive devices.
