Project Summary

Amidst political turmoil, Sudan’s children were already in the middle of devastating crises severely hampering their chances to survive. In a country with 49.7 million people population predominantly (72%) children, adolescents, and young adults aged 0 to 24 years;7 million children are out-of-school; 3 million children under five malnourished; 11 million people without access to safe water; 1 in 3 girls subjected to female genital mutilation; 38% of women married before their 18th birthday. With further deterioration by the 15 April 2023 conflict in Khartoum, as fighting quickly ignited across neighboring cities and states, particularly Aljazeera; The situation has since significantly escalated and worsened, as the humanitarian needs of children and women rapidly multiplied. Anticipated food shortages, combined with epidemics including measles, and cholera, interrupted health services, access to safe water, and one of the highest malnutrition rates in the world, will have significant implications on the health of children.
This puts Sudan on the brink of becoming home to the worst education crisis in the world, with nearly all school-aged children unable to receive quality learning. In addition to the 7 million out-of-school children, a sum of 6.5 million children lost access to school due to insecurity in their region. Many schools and educational facilities are not suitable for children to learn today, lacking furniture and other essential supplies or maintenance of water and sanitation facilities. 1,256 schools (8%) are reportedly being used as emergency shelters for the IDPs. Children across Sudan are in critical need of life-saving education interventions to enable their recovery, protection, and well-being. This humanitarian crisis is affecting children on a large scale, particularly girls and children with disabilities.
This project aims to fill the gap for 10,000 girls and boys (4,000 IDPs in gathering sites) in 4 states, Red Sea, Gadarif, Nile River, and The Northern State; providing a two-shift Alternative Learning Program ALP, rehabilitating 20 of the existing ALP centers or installing temporary spaces, in consideration to the accessibility for girls and the disabled; Coordinating with the MoE, and NCCW; Capacitating the participating teachers, psychological and social researchers, and volunteers.
Moreover, the most vulnerable 4,000 IDP girls and boys will be provided with structured mental health, psychological, and social support through the installment of 20 child-friendly spaces CFS (5 per state, with 50 to 200 children per space), and engaging in various activities, including drawing, music, theatre, drama, and sports activities in the CFS; Focusing on the estimated 2% disabled children placing them at the center of the project, ensuring their inclusion in all CFS activities, providing them with the psychological and social support they need, and by rehabilitating the facilities in the gathering sites around the CFS making it more accessible.
Simultaneously, cash-based intervention is conducted, targeting 4,000 individuals of the most vulnerable segments of IDPs in the gathering sites, especially disabled, lactating, and malnourished children and women(50%); Achieving food security, reducing child labor risks, and ensuring their inclusion in all the CFS and the ALP activities.
To address the abuse risks children and women face during the conflict and displacement, and to ensure a safe, healthy, and stress-free environment for children at home; a protection-focused intervention targeting up to 24,000 individuals from the community around the CFSs, forming protection committees, and conducting more than 240 sessions, FGDs, and awareness campaigns about child protection and GBV, working with community leaders, women, and youth (18 –25 years). The emphasis on targeting youth comes from their role in the displacement crisis as lead actors, and first responders to emergencies and critical issues regarding IDPs.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To ensure gender mainstreaming and protection in the programme, the Sudanese Coalition for Education for All (SCEFA) will adhere to the Do No Harm principles. And apply the Code of Conduct and apply all policies PESA, conflict of interest, financial regulations, and petty cash, and by activating the procurement committee, etc.... The Sudanese Coalition for Education for All (SCEFA) will also seek to achieve gender balance in the appointment of employees at all levels, provided that at least 50% of 50% women and 50% men. It will train staff responsible for providing services and input to participants on gender issues and gender-sensitive approaches. SCEFA's internal gender focal point will provide a solid foundation for the programme. Recognizing the critical role of women in household nutrition, specifically within food security and livelihood interventions. Limited access to educational services forces school-age children to walk long distances to reach schools. A lack of gender-based violence services often exposes women and girls to additional protection risks; GBV interventions will help reduce these risks by providing basic infrastructure, which will provide GBV services at the community level. In addition, SCEFA will ensure that women and girls are represented in community action groups – at least 50 percent of group members.
