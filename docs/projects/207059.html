Project Summary

The Far North region is facing exogenous and complex shocks resulting from the precarious security situation caused by Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG), security situation remains unstable in the Far North Region. Multiple attacks by non-state army groups were registered during the year 2023. Most attacks were concentrated around the Nigeria border with three divisions typically affected (Mayo-Sava, Logone-et-Chari, and Mayo-Tsanaga). Continuous displacement of population is reported. The Displacement Tracking Matrix Round 27 (DTM, October 2023) shows a total of 700 766 people in displacement with 453 661 IDPs, with 6% in transit? than the last report, 198 940 returnees and 48 165 out-of-camp refugees. The high number of mobile people displays the seriousness of the security situation in various areas, and that it is not safe enough yet for people to feel comfortable returning to their homes. The latest alert by the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) on 28 October 2023 was in Mayo-Tsanaga division where 542 households and 4696 people fled from their homes to have secure places around Mokolo town. 90% of interviewers confirmed that the main cause of displacement is permanent attacks from NSAGs followed by climate change impacts (7%) and community conflict (3%). About inter-communal conflictsin the Mayo Sava division, the displacement of 4,574 people was observed recently. This situation confirms the extent of the food insecurity in these divisions.
The impacts of climate change have contributed to decreased food production worsening the food security condition. Hazards and vulnerability constitute key drivers that directly affect availability, accessibility, utilization, and stability. Climate change has induced unusual disasters and shocks which reduce coping strategy capacity for people especially the vulnerable. As a fragile agro-ecological zone, the Far North Region has experienced 3 weeks of continuous drought at the reproductive phase of crops. This has caused the appearance of caterpillars in most divisions impacting cereals production which is the staple food. Data in the Mayo-Sava division reported a loss of 31.4% of cultivated cereals by drought associated with caterpillars (ENSAN, October 2023).
The region has also faced challenges such as reduced production of foods especially for staple foods.. For agricultural households, access to food (for direct consumption and income generation) was drastically reduced and affected their livelihood and nutritional status. More than 50% of households experienced soaring prices of staple food. Maize and sorghum have increased in price by about 85% over the past five years (ENSAN, October 2023).
FAO and IMC will work in integrating food security and livelihood interventions with nutrition-sensitive and nutrition-specific interventions to improve the nutritional status of the crisis affected populations and build their resilience. Integrating the nutrition interventions with FSL interventions will enhance the resilience of the crisis-affected populations. Nutrition is recognised to be both an input to and outcome of strengthened resilience. In emergency situations, households that are least resilient are at the greatest risk for malnutrition. Well-nourished individuals and households that are nutritionally secure can better withstand, endure and recover more quickly from external shocks
The project will focus on crisis affected vulnerable households such as IDPs, refugees out of the camp and returnees with focus on gender, age, socio-economic status, and location according to the food security analysis and the availability of persons with disabilities and out-off camp refugees that are not considered during food distributions analysis. Vulnerable host population will also be involved after the targeting process. Community activity such as nutrition education, community management of acute malnutrition will be carried out at the village level.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Children U5, pregnant and lactating women and adolescent girls represent the segment of society most affected by nutritional deficiencies. However, due to their roles in the household in terms of food production (60% of the agricultural workforce intended for food production) and preparation, they are considered as the main pillars for the fight against malnutrition at the household level. unfortunately, the combination of loss of livelihoods, low levels of economic opportunities, the effects of climate change and insecurity and limited access to hygiene and sanitary services exacerbate poor nutritional status of their children in this region. Strengthening women's empowerment in such a fragile context is therefore essential to help reverse the trend, especially since gender inequality is both a cause and a consequence of malnutrition. In addition, men with children 6-59 months will be educated and supported to be actively involved in household chores which are usually considered as female task as well as encourage them to include women in decision making concerning the management of household assets and other important family decisions. The production period in the Far North region is constituted of two seasons. Rainy season with mainly staple food crops and economic or cash crops, and dry season crops directed by the availability of water with high potential nutrition value. To respond to nutrition and food security problems, it is preferable to focus on crops which contribute to food security and sufficiently rich in nutrients. Species of crops with high nutrient values will be promoted to fulfil both objectives for this action: FSL and nutrition. The market gardening with short cycle production associated with long cycle production will be selected to provide availability in short and long-term periods. Vegetable and fruits species like nightshade, guinea sorrel, carrots, and tomatoes will help households with available food and cash (from the sales) to respond to other household needs and contribute to revenue diversification and increase. The production system will be supported by Farmer Field School (FFS) to acquire global knowledge at the community level. Since access to protein foods is usually a challenge to crisis affected vulnerable households, livestock farming will be promoted including above-ground fish farming and rearing of small ruminants like goats for protein food sources. Animal production, especially keeping of goats can be easy to realise by most households, even vulnerable people due to their adaptability capacity and low nutritional requirements. The Far North Region was also declared as a potential area for fish production. Contributing factors are high temperatures that accelerate fish food intake, availability of raw material such as maize, soya bean, cotton cake. The limiting factor is water availability which will be mitigated by practicing the above-ground fish farming. Water reserves are available to promote community production, but household water availability is also necessary to involve the vulnerable population in the activity. . The limiting factor is water availability which will be mitigated by construction of water points and put in place a system of drip irrigation in areas where there is water scarcity. These activities will be able to improve the nutritional status of the vulnerable populations through the improvement of access to nutritious foods, education on optimum MIYCF and WASH practices and appropriate healthcare services. Children less than 5 and pregnant and lactating women of the project implementation zones will be screened for malnutrition monthly to evaluate their nutritional status and the impact of the joint action. Quarterly mass screenings for malnutrition will be conducted in the entire health area benefiting from the project to evaluate the overall impact of the FSL and nutrition interventions.
