Project Summary

The IRC proposes a year project focusing on increasing access to and quality of integrated, gender-sensitive services in Askira Uba LGA in Borno State in Northeastern Nigeria. The proposed intervention would focus on integration of services at all intervention sites, GBV training, protection mainstreaming and gender sensitivity into all program activities. It would be an integrated, multi-pronged approach to service delivery in the hardest-to-reach areas in Askira Uba based on
1. Access to justice, to improve people’s experience of accessing justice, especially women and girls who have experienced violence. While the women protection and empowerment sector provide full and comprehensive case management services to survivors, the protection and rule of law sector will ensure they have access to justices, through legal case management services are able to seek redress in courts based on the various options that will be provided to them. Such survivors will be referred to formal justice actors such as the Nigerian Bar Association (NBA). Working with Protection Action Groups, Women Action Groups and existing community structures provide information on legal awareness on access to justice for women and girls.
2. Strengthen Access to justice Networks (a) amongst CSOs and humanitarian actors, (b) at the community level to ensure meaningful networking and dialogue on Access to justice and accessibility, and (c) between informal and formal justice mechanisms including and formal Courts.
3. Work with informal justice mechanism to provide more quality justice and linkages to formal justice systems/actors. Linkages and referrals between and amongst formal and informal justice systems/actors.
4. Capacity building for formal and informal justice systems: Train informal justice system actors including community leaders, women leaders, youth leaders, religious leaders on international human rights standards, leadership, procedural and substantive law as it relates to customary law, alternative dispute resolution mechanisms, and formal justice processes, emphasizing linkages to formal courts for serious crimes.
5. Civil Documentation. The lack of civil documentation continues to contribute to restriction in movements, exploitation, denial of services and violations of rights. In the increasingly insecure environment in the Northeast the lack of identify and civil documentation is a priority risk amongst displaced populations and must be addressed to enable safety and access to justice for those affected populations. Many Men, women, and children lost their civil documents (Birth Certificates and National ID Cards) while fleeing armed opposition group (AOGs) and these clients are at risk of violence at checkpoints and border towns during travels, and women and girls are at particular risk of GBV from Government forces.
