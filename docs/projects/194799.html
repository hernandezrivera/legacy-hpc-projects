Project Summary

In support of the Education Sector, UNICEF, through this project, in 2023, will further support its Generation Unlimited Initiative and the No Lost Generation Agenda by empowering vulnerable adolescents and youth (10-24 years), female and male including those with disabilities, to have access to age and gender sensitive, learning and skills building opportunities for improved learning, empowerment, and employability.
UNICEF will support access for young girls and boys to multi-sectoral services and opportunities, including technical vocational and education training (TVET), employability skills and life skills and citizenship education, leadership and literacy and numeracy. UNICEF will promote the efficiency and effectiveness in TVET education services nationwide through (i) expanded access and service delivery; (ii) enhanced quality and relevance of TVET provision; and (iii) improved TVET systems including strengthen the capacity of service providers and evidence generation.
The project will strengthen the access of 200, 000 young people (10-24 years) to skills development programming and meaningful engagement opportunities including the development of foundational, transferrable, technical and vocational, and entrepreneurship programming to prepare adolescents better for their future, and to unleash their potential and increase their learning, leadership, and employability opportunities as well as support their smooth transition from learning to earning and decent jobs in particular for young girls. UNICEF will continue its strategic partnerships with national and sub-national stakeholders, local and civic NGOs including youth led origination and youth network to achieve the desired results. The project will target the most marginalized adolescents and youth groups in particular those who are out of school, the poor, those from child- or female-headed households, returnees, IDPs, youth in the camps, adolescents with disabilities, girls headed family and young people affected by the crisis. The project plans to reach a total of 200,000 young people in the high severity areas such as Dara’a, Rural Damascus, Northern Homs, Southern Hama, East and Northeast of Aleppo, Deir-ez-Zor and Idleb. UNICEF will expand its support to young people in NES, focusing on returnees and displaced people in Raqqa, Deer EZour and Hasakah, including those in camps, collective shelters and urban settings. UNICEF will focus on the most deprived areas in rural and urban areas. These will include camps (Hol, Roj, Newroz, Areesha, Mahmoudli) in Al-Hasakeh and Raqqa, the collective shelters in Al-Hasakeh city, and the rural areas in Qamishli, Malikia, Yaroubia, and Raqqa Governorate. The project will implement the activities for the young people affected by the crisis to acquire the right skills, become empowered and resilient community members.

3 - Needs

Ongoing insecurity and the compounded impact of displacement, combined with worsening socio-economic conditions characterized by sharp currency depreciation, record-level price increases and cuts in subsidized goods have prompted an increase in the number of people in need. Moreover, COVID-19 pandemic has led to additional stress on basic services while also depriving young Syrians of their jobs and livelihoods. The combined effects of these concurrent crises contributed to a significant contraction of the overall economic activity, affecting people’s ability to sustain adequate living standards, seek decent livelihood opportunities, access essential services, and undermine social cohesion. Young people in Syria have missed out opportunities to develop and learn. They have limited access to skill-development opportunities and basic services including safe transition from school to work. Syrian girls are vulnerable to child and forced marriages and have limited of no access to services and learning opportunities (Humanitarian Action for Children/UNICEF2022). Syrian youth are struggling with high unemployment rates (20.9 per cent of total labour force, Arab Youth Survey2021),lack of matching between youth skills and labour market needs due to dramatic changes in the economy, poor skill- development programme, lack of access to formal education and poor quality of education, difficult transition from school to work, lack of alternative and flexible learning &educational pathways

4 - Implementation of activities

UNICEF will support 200,000 vulnerable adolescents and youth per year, female and male, especially those not in Education, Employment and Training (NEET), to access to structured capacity development sessions, including non-formal TVET, life skills and citizenship, entrepreneurship and leadership skills training. The project will support the employability of young people and provide them with opportunities for economic self- empowerment. include creating fora for adolescents and youth to generate economic or social value for them as individuals, or for their peer groups and communities. UNICEF will support the TVET system specially for the most vulnerable youth to be equipped with TVET and entrepreneurship skills and support their access to labour market. UNICEF will focus on the following pillars and related interventions: 1)support the TVET system including strengthening the institutional capacity and enhance the quality and access to services.2)support skills development and learning pathways to prepare adolescents and youth for smooth transition to adulthood and to labour market (foundational literacy and numeracy, leadership, life-skills and entrepreneurship).3)enhance evidence generation and youth engagement. UNICEF will report the results of the project to the Education Sector by sharing monthly 4Ws, human interest stories, and good practices. In addition, the project teams will share the project information and updates during the sector meetings.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection from sexual exploitation or abuse by humanitarian workers and service providers is high on the UNICEF agenda to ensure that no violations and abuse happen during implementation. UNICEF as co-lead for the inter-agency network (ICN) on PSEA, will continue to support and work closely with other agencies to enhance the PSEA portfolio. The main goal of the ICN is to improve coordination for the benefit of people at risk and ensure access to complaint mechanisms and specialized services for victims. Continuous capacity building for humanitarian workers on zero tolerance for SEA is required, as is awareness raising for the community on the right to humanitarian aid free of any exchange. As a pre-condition for contracting implementing partners, PSEA assurance and capacity building plans are guaranteed. Prevention, mitigation and response interventions include: • Capacity building for humanitarian workers and service providers on zero-tolerance for SEA and commitment to SEA prevention, mitigation, and response. • Trainings and awareness raising activities on PSEA principles to beneficiaries and communities. • Train, mentor, and coach staff on policies and procedures ensuring do not harm principles and PSEA. • Strengthened safe and confidential complaint mechanisms for beneficiaries and communities. • Immediate support to SEA survivors in line with survivor centered approach principles.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Targeting the most marginalized people may encounter many risks from safety and security to social acceptance and maintaining a quality and timely delivery of services. The spread of COVID-19 with its high likelihood may affect the participation of people in adolescents’ friendly spaces. UNICEF will promote the digital engagement of people as well as continue to respect measures to prevent the transmission of COVID-19, including wearing protective equipment while engaging with people, regular self- monitoring of health, consistent temperature checks and regular hand sanitation.Potential conflicts between IDPs and host communities as well as the instability of security situation in some areas have a medium likelihood. UNICEF will continue to promote social cohesion service and youth-led initiative and awareness-raising to mitigate such risks. Community resistance to change in many locations due to social norms leads to preventing young girls or people with disabilities from participating in the activities. UNICEF will continue to promote behaviour change and engagement of community through different pathways. UNICEF will continue to prioritize targeting the most disadvantaged people and will improve trust-building with local communities.
