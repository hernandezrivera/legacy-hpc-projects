Project Summary

The tribal districts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (ex FATA) are the most remote and food insecure areas in Pakistan. The food security and nutrition situation has been adversely affected by low production, limited livelihood opportunities, poor education and the prevailing challenges from the law and order situation for almost a decade.
Such a large scale return after years of displacement impacts the overall situation including markets, provision of basic services, livelihoods and food security. Markets are particularly important, because households depend on them for most of their food needs and other essential commodities for their livelihoods. Markets are also the places where they can sell their products and connect with traders, thus enhancing social connectivity. Markets in the KP-MD are less developed than other parts of the country such as KP and Punjab and have been affected by years of insecurity. However, markets are generally integrated and functioning, and play a very important role for people’s livelihoods.
The In-depth Food security and livelihood assessment (May 2017) estimates that overall 24 percent of the returned households are food insecure. Among them only 1 percent is severely, and 23 percent are moderately food insecure.
There is a significant and consistent reduction in the overall food insecurity rate from 44 percent in 2014 to 24 percent in 2017. The severe food insecurity rate decreased from 5 percent to 1 percent, and moderate food insecurity rate from 39 percent to 23 percent.
By gender of the household heads, there has been a very dramatic improvement in the food security situation of households headed by women. Improvements of food security are also found among households headed by men but at a lesser degree.
The assessment also mentioned that unlike other surveys in rural areas where households headed by women are found to be more likely food insecure, this assessment finds a significantly lower food insecurity rate among the households headed by women. Better food security among the households headed by women can be explained by the analyzed data showing that many more women heads have been engaged in more stable and regular income sources such as remittance, salary from regular jobs, or income support/Zakat than their male counterparts, both before displacement and after return.
The need for food security response shall be massive and well-coordinated during 2019. In order to make food security response more predictable and to design a mix of appropriate interventions, the Food Security Cluster aims to bridge the gap between the needs of the affected communities and the best possible allocation of available resources, while providing support to the Government. The Food Security Cluster members are the organizations implementing Food Security Interventions in the field in KP-MD and KP, include district, national and international NGOs, UN agencies, development partners, government authorities and line departments. These organizations as members of the FSC regularly attend the FSC meetings at the national and sub national levels, provide relevant information for the development of the 4W (Who, What, Where, When) matrix and benefit from the dissemination of relevant information for the planning and adaptation of their activities. The FSC remains the only structured coordination mechanism in the KP province.

Activities

The proposed action aims to 1) enhance and strengthen the coordination and support to the members while 2) improving the Nutrition outcomes of the FS Interventions and 3) contribute to the reduction of the striking gender imbalances. Information and Evidence: The Food Security Cluster (FSC) will promote and coordinate evidence generating as guidance for the actions on Food Security. The FSC will not undertake any assessment by its own means, but support other initiatives, for ex. by ACF. The FSC will promote gender disaggregation of data, gender sensitive surveying mechanisms and presentation of the findings that highlight specific gender issues. Capacity Building: The FSC will continue to enhance the capacity of humanitarian actors working in the area of Food Security. The Capacity Building Needs Identification will be based on the knowledge already developed by the cluster team throughout the years and will be adapted, complemented and documented by a Training Needs Assessment. On the job Capacity Building: The Food Security actors often lack capacity in specific, unique areas. In this case individual capacity building from experts, long term coaching or technical assistance are adequate responses. The FSC will have the opportunity to respond to the most prominent and impactful of these gaps by seconding experts to the organizations. Gender: The FSC will promote gender equity fourfold: 1) by promoting disaggregated data collection to capture the different needs; 2) by actively promoting participation of women in the coordination activities (meetings, missions, consultations etc); 2) by providing specialized technical assistance to FSC members to improve their gender approach; 3) by including gender issues in the training plan. Nutrition: To improve the nutritional status of the affected communities, especially girls and members of female HHs, the FSC will coordinate and promote nutrition mainstreaming in Agriculture and FS Interventions. Adequate support will be given by the specialized staff member, a qualified nutritionist. Moreover, technical trainings based on the guidelines developed by the FS WG in 2016 will also be imparted. Access: The security situation in the KP-MD should not be anymore an issue, but the access by humanitarian actors is still hindered. The proactivity of the security forces results in measures that limit the access for the humanitarian actors. The presence of FSC staff based in the agencies, their good communication with the authorities and the trust that they shall built, will result to a better understanding and acceptance of the humanitarian community. Transition to Development and Exit: The FSC will be in close collaboration with the development partners and the Merged District Secretariat to link humanitarian actors and longer term rehabilitation and development stakeholders. FSC will advocate within the FS Sector for recovery towards an improved quality of life and gender equality to enhance the sustainability of the emergency interventions. Structure: The operational structure will include four levels: a) local presence in the Agencies; b) in the closest provincial capital, Peshawar; c) in the national capital, Islamabad; d) support from the global headquarters of the co lead agencies, WFP and FAO in Rome. Five persons (including female staff) will be based in KP-MD to ensure good knowledge of the field and timely response. The FSC Coordinator, based in Peshawar, will ensure the coordination at the field offices of INGOs and UN agencies, the Merged Districts Secretariat and FDMA (FATA Disaster Management Authority). At the National Capital will operate an Information Management assistant, a statistician, a nutritionist and an operations assistant. WFP and FAO management will also contribute to the coordination with all stakeholders at national level. The FSC gender balanced staff will have also support from headquarters in Rome.
