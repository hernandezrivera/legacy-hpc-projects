Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to contribute to improving the nutrition status of the conflict-affected population, IDPs and Host
Community, in Borno State, Northeast Nigeria, by improving their access to curative and preventive nutritional services. This objective
aligns with the 2023 United Nations Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) and NHF first Reserve Allocation strategy to address the rapidly
deteriorating nutrition situation.
Curative nutritional services will be provided to children under 5 suffering from Severe or Moderate Acute Malnutrition (SAM or MAM).
SAM cases with medical complications will be provided 24/7 treatment in Pulka Stabilization Centre (SC), located in Pulka PHCC, that
PUI has been supporting since December 2022. The SC bed capacity will be increased from 20 to 40 beds to meet with the increasing
number of admissions. Referrals of cases to secondary health care will be ensured whenever needed, and transportation allowance
provided. Caretakers of SAM children with medical complications will be supported with the provision of food and hygiene supplies, to
avoid any negative coping mechanism. Children suffering from SAM without medical complications will be provided treatment in 2
Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTPs), one PUI supported between December 2022 and May 2023 in Damara MCH, and one PUI will
start supporting under this project in Pulka PHCC to meet the increased number of OTP admissions. Admitted children will receive
treatment based on the National CMAM guidelines. To ensure the quality of the assistance provided, PUI will ensure the training of MoH
staff on Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM), and of Community Nutrition Mobilizers (CNM) on community
mobilization/outreach component. This will also contribute to rooting the project into the community and ensuring the continuity of service
when or if PUI exits.
Treatment of children under 5 suffering from MAM will be provided through the Tom Brown approach, which PUI has been successfully
implementing since January 2021 in Monguno. PUI coordinated with another partner (ASHF) implementing TSFP program in Gwoza
LGA under NHF grant to ensure there is no duplication of TSFP services in Pulka. Following this approach, mothers / caregivers of
children suffering from MAM are gathered into groups where they are taught by Lead Mothers how to prepare a nutrient rich
supplemental food with local ingredients over a 12 to 13 weeks program. Along with this cooking recipe, mothers / caretakers are
sensitized on the use of clean water and hygiene practices. They are then encouraged to cascade the knowledge they have acquired to
other community members.
Preventive nutritional services will consist in the active screening of children aged 06-59 months to ensure the early detection of SAM
cases by 25 trained Community Nutrition Mobilizers (CNMs) living within the community. The CNMs will also ensure awareness raising
sessions within the community on Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) best practices, including exclusive breastfeeding
for the first six months of life, and conduct cooking demonstrations in the community. They will oversee 50 Mother Care Groups (MCGs)
of 15 mothers each, with lead mothers in charge of promoting child and mother health care services and the dissemination of key
nutrition messages.
The strong involvement of the community through the network of CNMs and MCGs will ensure the beneficiaries’ ownership of the
proposed project, as well as maximize its short- and long-term impacts, also in terms of social norm change.
The project will integrate Protection and Gender targeted activities to mainstream, among others GBV prevention and mitigation
measures in the nutrition response. Therefore, safe and dignified quality nutrition services will be accessible for all, including people at
heightened risk of violence; and child nutrition status will be improved through positive social norms.

1 - Needs

This action was designed based on the analysis of secondary data on the Food Security and Nutrition situation and primary data from theroutine monitoring of PUI ongoing Nutrition and Health intervention, both in Gwoza LGA. March 2023 Cadre Harmonise analysis classifiesGwoza as “in crisis” (IPC phase 3), and as projected to remain in this phase from June to September 2023. The 2022 IPC AMN (leanseason) classifies Gwoza as in phase 4; nutrition surveillance data indicate GAM and SAM rates of 16.7% and 6.9% respectively (HSMBulletin, April 2023). They confirm the concerning situation of malnutrition observed in Pulka where a July 2022 PUI SMART surveyshowed GAM and SAM rates of 15.3% and 2.6% respectively. Between Dec. 22 - May 2023, 563 children under 5 suffering from SAMwere admitted at PUI-supported SC and OTP in Pulka, a 131% achievement of the 480 target.Three main factors contribute directly to the high prevalence of malnutrition in Gwoza: 1) people’s limited ability to access an adequatefood diet due to the high food inflation rate (24.8% in May 2023 -Nigeria Statistic Bureau), which is expected to increase due to fuelsubsidies removal; 2) the weak access to quality health care services due to a lack of qualified staff and inadequate medical supplies inpublic health facilities; 3) low access to potable water as up to 60% of Gwoza population use unprotected source of drinking water (HSMBulletin). It is worth saying that cholera outbreaks are recurrent in Gwoza.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

According to PUI’s Gender and GBV analysis report (2020) mentioned above, Nigerian society is ruled by a pervasive patriarchal system, which supports male supremacy, leading to unequal gender role relations, and grants men power and control over women in both the domestic and the public spheres. A variety of gender-based violence have been reported including domestic violence (such as wife battering, rape, and assaults), sexual violence and harassment, violence against women and girls in conflict and post conflict situations, and harmful traditional practices against women and children (i.e. obnoxious widowhood rites, early marriages, and female genital mutilations among others). Because of the immediate and potentially life-threatening health consequences, coupled with the feasibility of preventing these consequences through medical care, addressing sexual violence is a priority in PUI’s areas of interventions. PUI ensures that survivors receive support and assistance across sectors in a safe, and accessible way and in accordance with IASC’s GBV Guidelines.PUI in this regard has taken some measure i to mitigate the SGBVs issues during humanitarian response:- PUI recognizes that every intervention aimed at tackling GBV can provoke harmful and potentially fatal consequences, and therefore aims at prioritizing first and foremost the survival of female beneficiaries through safety planning followed by adequate psychosocial support by trained PSS providers.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

PUI will ensure that women, girls, men, boys, older persons, people with disabilities, people living with HIV and affected by other vulnerabilities are regularly consulted to provide relevant and accessible information about their unmet needs and services.The monthly Community Committee Meeting (CCM) gave us information about where is the gap of which services that we could use to design a new project and for collective advocacy.Furthermore, their voices will be captured, engaged, and integrated through informed advocacy in cluster/ sectoral and coordination mechanisms within the humanitarian sector. The information gathering will be conducted through community meetings, focus group discussions and interviews with the community members. Affected populations will be consulted on project design, including the design of feedback and complaints mechanisms to enable PUI to provide efficient and effective programming that meets the needs of the vulnerable population and allows them to express feedback that creates sustainable implementation through community ownership of the program. PUI will also consult the affected population during the needs assessment and throughout program implementation about selection of recipient criteria, the location of service delivery points such as for instance the Individual protection Assistance to ensure that the distribution locations do not compromise with the safety of the people and caters for the people living with physical disabilities to access and minimize the risk of harming the beneficiaries.
