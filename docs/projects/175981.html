Project Summary

Estimates of IPC Dec 2020 indicate that about 7.7 million people in South Sudan will face acute food insecurity by the lean season of 2021. Of the 59% of people in extreme need (phase 4), about 287,318 are from Duk, Twic East, Bor South (Jonglei) and 51,727 from Awerial and Yirol East (Lakes) (FSLC PiN and severity ranking for lean season 2021). The vulnerable households in these phase 4 counties will need immediate and unhindered food assistance complemented with emergency livelihood support to strengthen their absorptive and adaptive capacities.
The main drivers of acute food insecurity in Jonglei and Lakes in particular, include the impact of COVID-19 on markets and movements of goods, increased sub-national violence, desert locusts, recurrent flooding, higher food prices and devaluating local currency. In addition, food security situation will be influenced by seasonality of livelihoods, grain stocks, movement of livestock and nutritious status of the people.
In order to contribute to FSL cluster CO1 : ''Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3) in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19'', CINA will reach individuals from the most vulnerable food insecure households with In-kind food assistance (including GFD: blanket/ seasonal/ targeted and GFD plus; More hybrid rations in-kind plus cash component; Cash & voucher-based transfers with food security/ nutrition objectives; Food, fishing & quick maturing vegetable seeds through IRRM; Conditional cash transfers (social skills & safety nets) through investments in assets, training, or communal work groups/ planning committees etc.; and Conditional food assistance through food for assets. To contribute to FSL CO2: '': Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID-19'', CINA will reach individuals from vulnerable households with Emergency food production through distribution of agriculture seeds & tools, fishing, vegetable & crop kits; Nutrition vouchers (plus poultry & shoat distribution and Seed fairs; Emergency food & livelihood support through distribution of fishing kits/ vegetable kits, rapid response kits (fish kit/ cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed); Livestock treatment and vaccination, livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers Training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment especially zoonoses e.g. RVF; Provision of livelihood support to returnee households and provision of timely food security information.
Using its current static presence in Lakes and Jonglei and good community relations, CINA will target project beneficiaries in line with vulnerability targeting using community based participatory planning (CBPP) approach. The blanket support will target children, women headed households and persons with disabilities. The agricultural inputs will be availed using FAO selection criteria for crop kits/seeds, vegetable kits/seeds, fishing kits. FSL activities will be integrated into famine prevention clusters to target households in catchment areas of health facilities, nutrition facilities and community improved water infrastructure. The project will integrate protection dimensions by caring out FSL activities that contribute to safety, dignity, and integrity of at-risk individuals/groups and do not increase the protection risks faced by the crisis-affected populations, while assessing their needs, barriers and risks faced by persons with disabilities as well as their capacities, views and priorities. CINA team will participate in data collection for FSNMS in hard-to-reach areas to monitor the impact of crisis and changes in context on food security and nutrition.

1 - Needs

About 7.7 million people in South Sudan will face acute insecurity in 2021 (IPC Dec 2020). South Sudan has one of the worst crisis context globally. The youngest East African nation ranks 185 out of 188 countries in the Human Development Index with 4 out of 5 people living under international poverty line. The GDP per capita dropped from $1,111 in 2014 to below $200 in 2017, with inflation rate rising to 40% and the standard food basket increasing by 42% in March 2020. About 91 per 1000 live births of under 5 children die. There is 300% increase in sub-national violence incidents from January to June 2020 compared to 2019 and more than 60% of roads become impassable during rainy season. In these situations, 54% of the people affected are children, 50% are female and 15% are persons with disability. (South Sudan HNO 2021). In particular, about 1.4 million South Sudanese children and 483,000 pregnant and lactating women will face acute malnutrition in 2021 due to food insecurity and decreased uptake and coverage of nutrition services, with GAM rate among under 5 children expected to remain below 15%. The DTM and FGD assessments in settlements (Sept 2020) show that men, women, youth and older persons identified lack of food as the primary challenge facing affected communities and that more displaced people live under poverty line than rural and urban residents. Presently, about 339,076 people are in extreme need of food assistance (77,673 from Duk, 149,100 from Bor South, 60,545 from Twic East, 31,292 from Yirol East and 20,435 from Awerial). (FSLC 2021 PiN and severity ranking). In addition, due to the negative impact of flood-related shocks and COVID-19 measures in combination with intensified sub-national violence on the people's resilience capacity, especially in Jonglei and Lakes, the priority counties are expected to have the highest number of people estimated to be in emergency or worse acute food insecurity as these multiple crises will underpin the limited household's capacity to absorb short term shocks, with female headed households mainly at greatest risk. Food insecurity is expected to persist by the lean season of 2021 in these counties due to recurrent emergencies (flooding and conflict), low production, lost of household incomes, depleted productive assets and fragile nutrition among populations. In addition, Jonglei has one of the highest population of IDPs and returnees in settlement without access to a food market due to impact of flooding and at least 25% of their population will rely in getting 50% or more of their caloric needs from humanitarian food assistance (South Sudan HNO 2021). It is worth noting that if immediate food assistance and close monitoring is not prioritized, there will be limited food consumption due to lack of household food stocks. This means that households will be sourcing food from markets in possible scenarios, but increased debt levels and limited incomes mean that some households will need to sell some assets. Therefore, CINA will require USD 400,000 to implement activities that will provide access to lifesaving, resilience and reduction of food insecurity and initiate activities that will cushion communities in time of disaster and help to reduce seasonal starvation and hunger among the lactating mothers and children.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

CINA Cash assistance will strive to provide the most appropriate, effective and efficient responses to the needs of people affected by a crisis. It will be provided in many ways depending on needs and context. The choice of appropriate transfers (in-kind, cash or vouchers), or their combination, must be based on a thorough assessment and context-specific analysis, including cost effectiveness and efficiency, secondary market impacts, the flexibility of the transfer, local availability of goods and services, prioritization and targeting, gender and protection considerations, and risks of insecurity and corruption. CINA in the past has been delivering Cash Based assistance to PSNs to address their livelihood needs, NFI needs such as soap and medicine, whereas now, it is increasingly providing cash to beneficiaries, often as a combination with cash and vouchers in order to meet this fundamental change in the way assistance was delivered. Therefore, Cash-based intervention has the potential to respond quickly and efficiently to a variety of urgent needs offering dignity, flexibility of choice to beneficiaries and supporting local markets growth for long term development. assistance covers both cash and vouchers that are provided to individuals, households or communities as emergency relief and which contribute to meeting their basic needs or to assisting them in protecting or reestablishing their livelihoods. Cash-based assistance, particularly when delivered in the form of unconditional cash is a people-centered approach

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CINA staff and volunteers will be trained to ensure adherence to IASC PSEA guidelines throughout the project cycle. Each will sign a Code of Conduct. All CINA staffs are subjected to enhanced reference checks up on taking job offer. A commitment statement on PSEA is found on CINA’s job adverts. CINA will ensure that staff are trained and uphold humanitarian principles including, of neutrality, impartiality and participation, and where feasible, CINA team will remain diverse as much as possible to achieve PSEA objective. CINA will ensure project beneficiaries are sensitized about the PSEA, and their role to identify and report it appropriately. CINA will display PSEA messages on the banner during awareness raising activities. A PSEA reporting form will be kept at Community centers, clubs and beneficiaries well be sensitized about it. They will use this to report cases directly to PSEA focal person at CINA's head Office. A hotline number and email address will also be displayed on the PSEA banner which beneficiaries can use to call and report PSEA cases in the community.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will target 15% People with disabilities, women, men and their caregivers. Their views and preferences will be used to realign provision of Cash Based intervention support in terms of blanket food, agricultural inputs. In particular, female headed households, persons with disabilities, child headed households, households with members critically and chronic sick, etc. will be targeted and support to get prompt information about the services.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community will be engaged through the CBPP process, CBPP committees and forums. Beneficiary Complaint mechanisms will be established at the community level and feedback will be collected using a form, complaint box or hotline number. Banners on SEA awareness and reporting will also be displayed at the distribution centers, will be used to receive feedback from community members. During the project inception at the community, beneficiaries will be informed about the project objectives, targets, activities, and their participation in the project. CBPP committees will be formed/strengthened to convey any project information to the beneficiaries during the implementation. Activities such as training on asset building, pests management and value addition will be used to pass important information about the activities of the project. CINA will also use feedback forms during M&E activities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The beneficiaries' feedback about services rendered by CINA in their location will be synthesized by CINA management and used during the project planning or implementation to adjust next phase of response.
