Project Summary

The main humanitarian needs in Sudan result from different underlying root causes and drivers including instability and multiple conflicts, recurrent natural disasters including droughts and floods as well as disease outbreaks. women and girls continue to suffer disproportionately from GBV due to violations of basic rights, insecurity, low economic status, denial of livelihood opportunities, lack of community awareness on women's rights and GBV as well as due to negative cultural and societal norms. Rape, physical violence, FGM, early/ forced marriages, denial of resources opportunities, and verbal abuse are some of the common violations perpetrated against women and girls. The 2021 HNO indicated that there are 2,287,484 People in need of GBV services, out of which the GBV Sub Sector is targeting 890,303.
As a continuation of the incredible achievements in GBV programming reported in 2020, UNFPA Sudan will contribute to the three GBV Sub -Sector objectives:
1- Strengthen the provision of life- saving specialized quality GBV services, for survivors of GBV and vulnerable women and girls. (with a focus on women and girls with disabilities and adolescent girls (Linked to HRP Strategic Objective 1), through the provision of different services to vulnerable women & girls including GBV survivors (Case management, PSS, referrals), assessments, community awareness-raising, and programming, provision of dignity kits, and capacity building of GBV frontline actors.
2-Enhance strategies for community resilience through the empowerment of women and girls and strengthened community-based interventions(Linked to HRP Strategic Objective 2), by supporting women centers, community protection networks, and training of women & girls on vocational skills,
3- Promote GBV risk mitigation into all aspects of the humanitarian response (Linked to HRP Strategic Objective 3), by training on GBV for Non-GBV actors.
Overall target : 400,000 (45% of GBV SS Target).

1 - Needs

In Sudan, Gender-Based Violence (GBV) is a key concern within the different communities affected by different emergencies i.e. conflicts, tribal clashes/tensions, floods, drought as well as disease outbreaks. Women and girls amongst the IDP communities and refugees are especially at risk of GBV. Survivors of GBV lack adequate access to quality specialized lifesaving services, reporting mechanisms, and legal/justice interventions with the majority of Sudan’s localities lacking specialized GBV services (such as clinical management of rape, specialized psychosocial support, and counseling, legal aid, and case management). In October 2020, the Sortoni protection need assessment was conducted (Annex 14). The mission was coordinated by OCHAand led by UNHCR. The main findings - PEP kits are available in the health facility where MWs are working, however, due to the rapid turnover of health staff currently only one trained MW is providing CMR service. No staff was trained on structured basic CMR training, during the visit additional PEP kits were provided, and on-the-job training was conducted for 6 health facility staff including 3 MWs, to ensure the provision of PEP treatment service timely. The assessment found that there is no clear mechanism for the Referral system of GBV cases. In central Darfur, due to the armed conflicts in Jebel Marra, in addition to other competing emergencies: heavy rains in Nertiti and Zalingie localities, in addition to the pandemic of COVID 19, the humanitarian community could not access most of the affected areas for IA need assessment. Access was denied due to movement restrictions made by armed rebels who are still out of the comprehensive peace agreement, like the SLA Abdelwahid faction. Only some NGOs have conducted limited need assessment with IDPs around Golo and Nertiti. UNAMID conducted a need assessment to the IDPs in Toga; near Golo in July 2020; they have reported that about 14 villages, estimated by 6000 HH (90% of communities were women and children) were compelled to flee their villages and settle in Toga village after armed conflicts took place between two factions of SLA. Basic humanitarian needs were widespread, mainly security, health, and nutrition which subsequently widespread GBV incidents with no official reports received as survivors were been prevented by their communities and armed SLA to seek support. As has been the case in most countries, the COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated key risk factors for GBV, such as food shortages, unemployment, economic insecurity, school closures, etc. The GBV helpline launched at the national level and in 6 states to meet the needs of GBV survivors during the lockdown received calls 165 calls from 20 April to 26 November. Most of the callers were women (99.4%), and more than half (53%) of them were young adults (18 – 29 years). The two most common types of GBV reported incidents from the hotline were physical abuse (70%) and verbal harassment (41%). According to the partial MSNA data, 87 percent of households prefer reporting GBV cases to the police, while 24 percent preferring leaders, four percent preferring social worker, and only one percent preferring Health staff, community midwife, Teacher, or NGO staff. The results indicated the need for more focusing on police as an entry point for GBV survivors. Also, the MSNA indicated the law awareness among women on the GBV available services and GBV prevention mechanism in their communities. UNFPA Sudan launched for the first time the VOICE assessment – a qualitative assessment that covers the 18 states of Sudan. The primary results indicated the huge need for quality of GBV services and more engagement of communities including men and boys on GBV risk mitigation and response.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The activities and implementation design considered the impact on the environment with mitigation and enhancement process, the planned women centers – if supported - will be rehabilitated using to the extent possible the no environmentally harmful materials. The promotion of fuel-efficient stoves - if supported - will contribute to the improved livelihood of vulnerable households while reducing environmental impact as fewer trees are cut for cooking fuel. In addition, during the dignity kits distribution, women and girls will be sensitized to environmentally friendly ways of disposing of the used sanitary wear. Through the women and girls’ empowerment program, the GBV AoR partners will explore the production of reusable sanitary pads as a replacement for disposable ones.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through this project, UNFPA is focusing on promoting effective participation of targeted communities across gender, age, and vulnerability throughout the project cycle to ensure GBV survivors and those at risk are not exposed to additional harm. 7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to the affected population is core in the UNFPA response and will be strengthened through continuous consultation with the affected population on the response modalities as well as their satisfaction with services provided. In addition, UNFPA is an active member of the national PSEA technical and coordination team and leads the survivor support task team and together is working with the team members to ensure that GBV referral mechanisms are accessible to survivors of SEA. UNFPA will equip implementing partners and targeted communities with information regarding the SEA, and how to report and access services including health, legal and psychosocial support services. UNFPA’s partners and beneficiaries play an active role in the decision-making process in regards to the planning, implementing, and evaluating of activities. This is done by conducting a focus group discussions with women and girls in women centers. The project will support the development of self-protection capacities and aim at improving beneficiaries’ daily lives through improved formal and informal protection mechanisms. This is considered the most effective way of tackling gender inequality as the root cause of GBV.
