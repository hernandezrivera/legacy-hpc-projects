Project Summary

Clashes between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF)
that erupted on 15 April 2023 are having devastating consequences for people across
Sudan. Life-saving responses related to WASH, Food Security, nutrition and agriculture
are urgently needed for conflict affected populations. Mercy Corps response plans aims
to provide humanitarian assistance to new IDPs and CAPs people..
Goal:
“To provide humanitarian assistance to conflict affected people in Sudan to meet
immediate needs for survival and work towards recovery”:
Objectives:
1. Save and protect lives
2. Improve capacity to withstand further shocks (e.g. flooding)
3. Prevent backsliding for vulnerable communities indirectly affected by the conflict
Mercy Corps will distribute seeds through a restricted value voucher system to conflict
households. Project participants will then be able to redeem their value voucher for their
choice of seeds at selected certified seed suppliers. Mercy Corps will provide basic and
refresher trainings on the CMAM guidelines and the Essential Nutrition Package to the
existing MoH staff and community volunteers. The training sessions will focus on the
four main components of CMAM, including community outreach, OTP, targeted
supplementary feeding program (TSFP), and inpatient care by the MoH. To ensure a
continuous high standard of care, Mercy Corps will provide biannual refresher training
sessions for the nutrition center staff. Mercy Corps will also conduct SAM treatment for
children 6-59m (without complication) in OTPs (first 6 months) as well as SAM treatment
for children 6-59m (without complication) in OTPs (Second 6 months).Through the
nutrition interventions about about 76% of the nutrition sector’s lifesaving response
activities will contribute to nutrition resilience. IYCF (MYCN), OTP and TSFP activities will also be included in the nutrition response along with AAP and GBV in nutrition context. The nutrition programming will also be integrated with WASH, monitoring and reporting, Mercy Corps will also support capacity building for nutrition staff.
Mercy Corps through implementing partners will provide GBV, Child protection training and as cross cutting activities across all other thematic areas. As part of the GBV strategy, Mercy Corps will promote
prevention of gender-based violence and will provide support to survivors and their
families as well as promoting responsive legal and justice systems. Mercy Corps will
ensure that children protection and GBV is an integral part of the programs in order to
ensure that women and girls are protected and given the opportunity to participate in the
project implementation. In the target locations, water collection is typically the
responsibility of women and girls. Limited access to water sources forces women and
girls to walk long distances in search of water, often exposing them to additional
protection risks. This intervention will help to reduce these risks through rehabilitating
infrastructure which will increase the availability of water and reduce the distance women
and girls are forced to walk to collect water. In addition, Mercy Corps will ensure that
women and girls are represented in the community-level WASH committees – minimum
50 percent of WASH committee members are women. Mercy Corps also plans to
encourage the involvement of women and girls in all elements of the community-based
operation and maintenance of the water sources. In terms of child protection Mercy
Corps will conduct awareness campaigns on children’s rights and protection concerns,
plus establishing community-based child protection committees trained on child
protection standards and referral mechanisms. Mercy Corps will address WASH needs
through rehabilitation of water sources for both community and institutions, and
formation and training of WASH committees to ensure improved access to clean and
safe water is sustained.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To ensure that gender and protection are mainstreamed in the program, Mercy Corpswill abide by Do No Harm principles. MC will also strive for gender balance in therecruitment of staff at all levels and will train officers, responsible for deliveringservices and inputs to participants, on gender issues and gender-sensitiveapproaches. These efforts will be supported by a Gender and SafeguardingCoordinator who will be partially funded through this project. Mercy Corps’ in-housegender expertise will provide a solid foundation for the program. Recognizing the keyrole of women in agriculture and household nutrition, a minimum of 40 percent ofparticipants will be women.
