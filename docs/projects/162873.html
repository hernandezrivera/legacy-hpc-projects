Project Summary

Sudan faces one of the highest rates of malnutrition in the regions and according to the 2020 draft HNO about 522,000 children suffer from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) in Sudan. This project aims to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with malnutrition among highly vulnerable population and communities through the provision of high impact nutrition interventions. In 2020 UNICEF will further scale up its support for the delivery of life saving treatment to children suffering from SAM, the prevention of malnutrition through community-based counselling on good infant and young child feeding (IYCF) and care practices while focusing on the quality of services delivered. This will include the provision of treatment to 290,467 children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) including the procurement of 348,560 cartons of Ready to Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) and support for operational support including the expansion of the current number of Outpatient Treatment Program (OTP) sites including capacity building of key cadres of staff responsible for service delivery to increase the coverage of services in high burden areas. UNICEF will further strengthen for the early case finding and referral by using Mothers Support groups (MSGs), community health workers, health facility staff, health promoters & hygiene promoters.
To prevent malnutrition more generally and reduce the risk of readmissions UNICEF will strengthen the linkage between the national curative and preventative nutrition services, specifically the linkages between CMAM and the provision of IYCF counselling by improving the referral mechanisms. UNICEF will further scale up the cIYCF program currently existing of more than 3000 Mothers Support Groups (MSGs) to counsel 860,785 caregivers while improving both the reporting system and introduce a supportive supervision structure. UNICEF will continue to provide vitamin A supplementation to 3,830,884 children aged 6-59 months through subnational and national vaccination campaigns while aiming to develop an alternative distribution strategy. To prevent anemia among vulnerable women during pregnancy, UNICEF will provide iron folate supplementation to 374,786 pregnant women in collaboration with the health sector.
UNICEF will support health system strengthening through efforts to strengthen supply chain management for RUFT from national level to health facility level, gradually moving towards a transitioning of the supply chain management from UNICEF to FMOH except for the procurement and by strengthening the national capacity for state and locality level monitoring of the CMAM program and continue to build capacity to utilize data for assessing and improving performance. UNICEF will also support the further strengthening of nutrition information systems to provide accurate and timely data from OTPs and MSGs. In new emergencies UNICEF will support the undertaking of timely rapid assessments to inform response actions.

1 - Needs

The general nutrition situation in Sudan has progressively worsened since 2016 with the additional influx of SSR and opening of new areas previously not accessible for humanitarian aid during the past 8 years combined with population growth. The national prevalence rate of global acute malnutrition (GAM) at 14.1% places Sudan above the very high threshold according to WHO criteria. About 522,000 are suffering from severe acute malnutrition (SAM) out of whom only 300,000 SAM children are targeted by the outpatient therapeutic programme. Since January 2018, Sudan has been facing a deteriorating macroeconomic situation which is worsening economic conditions for all Sudanese people, especially vulnerable families and children. According to the recent FEWS NET reports around 5.5 million people are severely food insecure in the last quarter of 2018, almost 1.7 million more than estimated. Considering the high rates of malnutrition before the crisis, the rising food insecurity will serve to exacerbate the already precarious nutritional status of children. Restricted humanitarian access and limited service delivery still persist in some areas, while conflicts and displacement remain risk factors for malnutrition in Sudan through the disruption of the livelihoods of people and predisposition to vulnerabilities that lead to malnutrition including reduced own crop production, high staple food prices, and disruptions to normal livelihood activities. The challenge of displacement includes the influx of South Sudanese Refugees (SSR), vast majority of which are living out of camps, facing multiple vulnerabilities remains a serious humanitarian concern. In addition to the burden of refugees, there are over 3 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in the Darfur Region. Some of the IDPs live in much worse conditions than some refugees and are at risk of multiple vulnerabilities, including acute malnutrition. Results from 2 SENS surveys conducted in West Kordofan and White Nile found critical levels of Global Acute malnutrition (GAM) amongst SSR both within and out of camps as well as high prevalence of diarrhea. Coverage of services was found to be low including issues with the continuity of care due to poor alignment and referrals between services (SC/OPT/TSFP) as well as low coverage of vitamin A supplementation, antenatal care services including provision of iron folate supplements. Anemia prevalence amongst children and women of reproductive age was also found to be high. Consumption of nutrition rich foods were low such as the consumption of iron rich foods and well as fruits and vegetables. Key Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices were also found to be poor.
