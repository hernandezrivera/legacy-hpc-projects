Project Summary

Health situation in target areas has presented poor health indicators with prevalence of diseases sustaining as high.
Afmadow and Kismayo districts have chronically high levels of acute malnutrition beyond the emergency threshold among both children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women, high prevalence of common communicable diseases especially among IDP (FSNAU,2020),limited access to health facilities and services especially among the rural population, few women with access to Maternal and child care services including delivery services and poor access to vaccination services (UNICEF ,2019).
Baidoa IDP remain some the most vulnerable in Somalia (Reach IDP profile 2019). IDP numbers continue to increase due to a number of factors including conflict, insecurity, flooding and drought among others. In early January 2020, the first influx of IDP fled from Tooswayne to Outskirts of Baidoa town following clashes with Al Shabaab community leaders on forced taxation and child recruitment. More IDPs continue to arrive in at the outskirts of Baidoa town since June 2020 (OCHA led Multisector assessment report, September 2020).
WRRS is currently having ongoing health and nutrition interventions in Afmadow and Kismayo that it intends to sustain in 2021. In Baidoa, WRRS is involved in camp management covering most new IDP. Having identified health gaps in those camps, the organization plans to scale up and include health interventions in the camps where it has operational presence.
In all target areas both mobile and fixed services will be offered. Mobile teams will cover rural areas where services hardly reach while fixed facilities will cover towns and IDP camps where the population can easily access services. CHW will also be employed to ensure that the population is encouraged to use these services and that some services are offered at village/community level.
Proposed interventions in all target areas will integrate nutrition, food security, WASH and protection activities.
Project will also include aspects of mental and psychological health, management of rape, identification and referral of protection victims, prevention of COVID 19 and hygiene promotion with distribution of hygiene kits and risk communication.
Proposed interventions will also include all gender groups, marginalized groups and the disabled who are often left out or given unequal treatment.
Direct beneficiaries will include 11240 women with: 100 for post rape clinical management and GBV services,1340 for ANC and delivery,1990 with PNC,3700 for outpatient consultations,200 female headed households with hygiene kits and 4010 with RCC. 17741 Children,130 with PRCM and GBV services,7240 with measles and Penta 3 vaccine,1340 with BCG vaccine,531 with treatment of complicated severe acute malnutrition and 4300 with RCC.
Indirect beneficiaries will include 12500 men with outpatient consultations, 500 male headed households with hygiene kits, 30 men for GBV services and 12500 with RCC , and 9 ministry of health officials involved in monitoring and supportive supervision.
Of these 320 (90 men,90 women, 40 boys , 40 girls and 60 elderly) will be disabled who have been given access to treatment, with 100 (30 men,30 women, 10 boys , 10 girls and 20 elderly) mentally disabled for mental health and psychosocial support.
All these will be done through 5 health centers, 4 PHU and 2 mobile teams.
Both facility based staff and community health workers will be trained on various aspects to build their capacity.
Disabled will participate at all levels of the project cycle. Staff will be trained on disability inclusion as to to eliminate discrimination. CHW will work with the community to identify disabled who need services. All data will be disaggregated according to Age ,gender and disability. The project will adhere to set government policy and guidelines and any other MoH protocol.

Mitigation Measures

Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.) Mitigation measure: WRRS will create awareness on Protection and GBV, for all the groups involved in the project, this will include mapping of referral centers where survivors will be informed of where to access services in case of a protection or GBV incident. WRRS will also ensure that information is available on GBV, Child protection through a help desk established at the health centers.Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities Mitigation measure: In many instances marginalized communities and people with disabilities have been excluded in project decision making and design, it is with this in mind that WRRS will ensure that all the beneficiaries including women, men, boys and girls, people living with disability are consulted and that are part of decision making of the project activities, consultations with different groups will be held separately to ensure that different groups participate inclusively. Opinions from different groups will be incorporated into the project design and implementation.Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms: Mitigation Measures: WRRS will establish a confidential complaints and feedback mechanism that will be accessible by all the beneficiaries, awareness will be created on the CFM to ensure that beneficiaries are aware/informed of its existence and use. A hot line will be established as well as suggestion box for access to beneficiaries who have no access to mobile phones.
