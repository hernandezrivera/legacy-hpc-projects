Project Summary

In 2024, 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. Of the 16.7 million people in need, 5.5 million are displaced, including over 2 million who live in last resort sites.
After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023. The earthquakes uprooted hundreds of thousands of families, resulted in almost 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 people injured in Syria, and severely damaged infrastructures already weakened by the conflict. Many families lost their main breadwinner due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, increasing the vulnerability of millions of people previously unable to meet their basic needs.
Bahar's 12-month strategic Water, Sanitation, Hygiene (WASH), and Shelter intervention seeks to address pressing challenges faced by communities in Idleb, catering to a total of 666,198 individuals.
The project employs a comprehensive approach, engaging communities, developing infrastructure, and raising awareness. In enhancing water services, Bahar aims to rehabilitate and expand water networks in Idleb, benefiting 451460individuals. An innovative touch involves the integration of solar-powered systems, contributing not only to efficiency but also to long-term sustainability.
Simultaneously, the project focuses on improving sanitation services for 196480individuals through the rehabilitation and expansion of sanitation networks in Idleb. The project recognizes schools as vital community hubs and targets 12,008 students and teachers, ensuring 100% access to WASH facilities and implementing hygiene awareness campaigns.
Recognizing the severe challenges posed by conflict and the earthquake in February 2023, Bahar's Shelter strategy in NWS aims to alleviate the vulnerability of affected populations. This includes providing Cash For Shelter (CFS) to 4,650 individuals living in damaged houses due to conflict and earthquake. Moreover, the project targets 1,600 individuals residing in informal settlements, aiming to increase access to comprehensive dignified living conditions by replacing the informal settlements with organized comprehensive dignified shelter units site which provide WASH essential services, social and educational services.
