Project Summary

INTERSOS as a protection agency aims to pilot, throughout 2019 a series of integrating strategies into its current programming. Following the submission of a proposal for partnership to WFP and in line with a general broad scale integration strategy INTERSOS aims to provide additional avenues through integrating FSL activities and Case management practice. Hence through this proposal the organisation would like to triangulate support of food production to vulnerable households in Jonglei state across current presences i.e. Ayod, Akobo, Lankien, e Pibor. Of great concern is former Greater Jonglei State, where food security is rapidly deteriorating, predominantly in the counties of Ayod, Canal/Pigi, Duk, Nyirol and Uror, which are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, with Ayod having an estimated 20,000 people experiencing Humanitarian Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) at least through July 2017. (IPC, 31 May 2017). An estimated 5.3 million people, 48 percent of the population, are currently facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, despite the harvest and continued large-scale assistance. (FEWSNET, 22 Mar 2018). Understanding, the current food insecurity and protection concerns, INTERSOS proposes: provision of material and support for improving livelihood but as an integral part of the gbv process, provision of Emergency food production: according to the locality's primary source of food production, the assessed capacities of individuals and in line with the protection risk assessment carried out - distribute main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; and in locations like Akobo and Lankien distribute fish kits; Vegetable and Crop kits to be determined according to the specific area of implementation, distribution and provision of Emergency food & livelihood support: primarily in the form of fishing kits/ rapid response kits to women and GBV survivors, particularly to SHH, and analyse and assess the possibility of supporting communities with Livestock vaccination; disease prevention & treatment especially zoonotic e.g. RVF;. Before the intervention, INTERSOS will carry out rapid protection assessments (including risk assessments) to determine the factors that drive GBV and to enable proper planning of GBV interventions in each of the target areas. the assessment will be intersected with the already existing case management services and potentially with any activity also implemented by the FLA holding WFP partner in the locality at the time of implementation. In addition to this, INTERSOS establish direct connection with the GBV case management system thus the established referral pathways, refer or identify individuals and households that as per criteria to be defined in accordance with assessments rolled out.

Needs

Conflict in Jonglei continues to have negative effects on the humanitarian situation of the population regardless of the ongoing peace process that has yet to take heed; clashes between armed groups & pervading insecurity particularly in the North continue to cause movement & affect populations bringing significant obstacles in fulfilment of basic needs. Tension & multiple displacements especially around areas like Greater Akobo & the ongoing evident flux (see Mawut, Panyikang & Guit counties together with displacement in Pieri, Motot, Yuai, Waat e Walgak) highlight a constant level of need. Conflict & clashes drive movement, together with violent activities such as cattle raiding, age-group battles (such as n Pibor); food scarcity & insecurity also drive displacement.Security concerns particularly in Akobo (hotspot for revenge killing incidents & clan fights) show the deep roots of conflict in Jonglei. Historically, Nuer, Dinka & Murle pastoralists all participated in cyclical cattle raiding & child abduction. Cattle are central not only to all communities’ livelihoods but also to their social & cultural systems. Conflict & violence often disrupt agricultural activities; as a result, food insecurity has increased dramatically across much of the state, with particularly severe effects in Akobo & Pibor. The latter & poverty are also major drivers of early marriage; idleness & lack of proper school functioning to engage young girls in studies also makes them more vulnerable. At the start of its intervention in Akobo & Lankien, through a 2016 joined assessment INTERSOS & other partners identified food security & livelihoods needs; while the response increased so have the numbers of households. While WFP food distributed to Ny&it or Bilkey (Akobo East County), the provision of seeds & tools by Nile Hope, & ACTED & an FFA program rolled out by Oxfam was significant it was & is difficult to cover the high number of households. In connection to the high impact of seasonality & rains, the limit in seed availability consequent limited l& being worked, lack of fishing tools in proportion to the percentage of people relying on the river constant needs are present. Reach’s Q1 assessment in Jonglei 2018 states that the proportion of assessed settlements reporting adequate access to food remained stable with 64% having adequate access, (like in December 2017 68%). However, when taken at county level, reported access to food in assessed settlements varied considerably especially looking at the extreme discrepancies in high risk localities such as Pibor (IPC 5 September 2018 assessment). Akobo reportedly depends on humanitarian assistance for food (53% March 2018) other striking examples that illustrate the deep need at county level are the coping mechanisms chosen such as in Nyirol, which reported the highest proportion of assessed settlements consuming less than one meal/day on average in February 38% & March 21% to cope with food scarcity. A striking data set for Jonglei other than its ongoing volatility, high transience & now increased IPC 4/5 areas is the persistence of GBV, remaining a major protection concern for women in Jonglei, & reported as the main protection concern for women by the highest proportion of REACH’s assessed settlements in March (46%),This project seeks to respond to gender impacts of the conflict in two ways. Firstly through integrating the GBV protection component with FSL activities, gearing them as solutions within the case management process, through specific action focused on empowerment & livelihoods, thus increasing the protection & safety of beneficiaries particularly in hard to reach locations where limited presence often results in low support targeted directly at Women Girls & Protection. Secondly, through strengthening community protection environment & support groups in hard-to-reach locations outside the PoCs that are less serviced due to their geographic location.

Activities

Firstly, INTERSOS will carry out rapid protection assessments (including risk assessments) to update (include recent context analysis data), contingency measures & the factors that determine & drive GBV & to enable proper planning livelihoods interventions in each of the target areas. The team willundertake a mapping of factors influencing livelihoods & opportunities. An initial analysis of livelihood assets & strategies used by households (especially female headed households) to engage in livelihood activities. The project will call for an initial analysis on the policy, legal & institutional context which includes host government policies, laws & regulations, & instruments that can support livelihoods, markets, services & commodities, local government, other organizations, community based organizations and more. Details of livelihood strategies (what they do for a living) should be captured through organized focus group discussion Analysis of the strategies will keep into account that livelihood strategies are used differently by men & women. It is of essential concern that the team Identifies and integrates protection challenge analysis arising from livelihood strategies as well as the specific needs of individuals & groups, such as women at risk, older persons, persons with disabilities.In consultation & coordination with a broad range of stakeholders INTERSOS will aim to work around obstructive norms & find ways to work within existing constraints to support the targeted groups.The intervention will aim to achieve short, medium & longer term objectives by implementing a range of activities, combining targeted & others with broader categories regardless of capacities. Combined as Livelihood protection interventions activities will have a direct & short to medium-term effect on people’s socio-economic wellbeing, this is also connected to the, individual and household skills training. The team will aim to connect to the protection intervention particularly in the light of Livelihood promotion interventions – moving away from assistentialism as a means to have an indirect & medium to long-term effect on people’s socio-economic wellbeing, through advocacy, building capacity.This intervention will be intersected with the already existing case management services & potentially with any activity also implemented by the FLA holding WFP partner in the locality at the time of implementation. INTERSOS will thus, through establishing direct conneciton with the GBV case management system & the established referral pathways, refer or identify individuals & households that as per criteria to be defined in accordance with assessments rolled out will carry out the followingProvide Emergency food production: according to the locality's primary source of food production, the assessed capacities of individuals & in line with the protection risk assessment carried out - distribute main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; & in locations like Akobo & Lankien distribute fish kits; Vegetable & Crop kits to be determined according to the specific area of implementationDistribute & provide Emergency food & livelihood support: primarily in the form of fishing kits/ rapid response kits to women & GBV survivors, particularly to SHH Provide training to identified households and roll out livelihood promotion activities aimed broadly at the community through discussion with CBCNs (in place from protection intervention)Analyse & assess the possibility of supporting communities with Livestock vaccination; disease prevention & treatment especially zoonotic e.g. RVF;
