Project Name

Project Summary

The project will focus on the provision and Increasing the equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection, treatment, prevention of acute malnutrition for children 6 -59 months of age and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) and elderly and people with disabilities by targeting populations of 38,710 (boys 12,109 and girls 13,119 age 6-59 months and 13,482 PLWs) in Fangak Canal/Pigi, counties of Jonglei, Tonj North county of Warrap State, Aweil South county of Northern Bhar el Ghazel State, Mayendit county in Unity state The population will include returns, IDPs in local community areas in protection of Civillian (POC sites in the respective counties. Healthcare Foundation Organization (HFO) project will contribute to the achievement of Nutrition Cluster strategic objectives for humanitarian response in 2021:
1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventive nutrition service delivery for children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women in the prioritized locations. There will community sentization and engagement in order to increase accesibility to services by beneficiaries.
2: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2021.
3: To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2021. HFO will involve i surveillance in early case detection for malnutrition as well as surveillance for COVID-19 suspected cases within the community. And such information will be shared with CHD/MoH and with other actors and ensure preventive measures and raising of alert suspected cases of COVID-19 and work with Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming monitoring and evaluation.
4: To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors. The nutrition staff will ensure MUAC screening for children 6-59 months, PLWs, issuing VAS to chldren 6-59 months to prevent and lower the VAD and as well counselling on exlcusive breastfeeding by MIYCN. The project, The will set up OTP TSFP, BSFP sites to treat SAM and MAM for children 6-59 months and MAM cases form PLWs, and all sites be made accessed by all beneficiaries and referrals for SAM + Meidical complication happen to SC health facilities. These will integrated into existing projects of health WASH, FSL NFI, and COVID-19 pandemic response and Protection that are either supported by HFO or other partners to energize the efforts of humanitarian response in 2021. HFO will mobilize qualified nutrition workers that will run the project right from Juba and field locations where the project will be implemented. The recruitment of community Nutrition workers, community nutrition volunteers and Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) counselors will be done in consultations with community members and local authority for their participation in the selection criteria by making sure location stakeholders are involved and gender balance is taken in to account of getting workers from both males and females where necessary and including people with disability or elderly.
The nutrition response will be designed to ensure that Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) is looked after, at all stages of the response. HFO staff will continue to use the Nutrition Cluster and Global Nutrition Cluster operational framework on AAP that includes minimum and/or major key activities and indicators focused on participation, establishment of complaints and feedback mechanisms, transparency and communications, planning and implementation of nutrition responses as well as monitoring.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has been experiencing worse humanitarian disasters in the world such as Conflict that resulted into civil war since 2013, flooding 2019-2020 in Jonglei and Upper Nile, some part of Equotoria and Bhar El Ghazel. The two disasters have caused displacement to population from South Sudan to Neighboring countries, and Internally displaced person within Protection of Civilians sites-POCs in country, destructions of infrastructure of health facilities, schools’ churches and homestead that are hard to rebuild due continuous communal violent all over the country. Despite the ceasefire between two warring parties, the economic meltdown is making life so difficult both in urban and rural areas where people are hardly putting meals on the table for everyone in the family. Harvest has been destroyed by heavy rain and serious flooding in the most part of the country specially Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity states.The most severe acute food insecurity conditions are in counties where chronic vulnerabilities have been exacerbated by a combination of shocks, including floods, insecurity incidents, the macro-economic crisis, and the effects of COVID-19 outbreak. Between October and November 2020, 34 counties across the country were classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Children are still getting malnourished despite strong efforts done by Nutrition cluster, donors and partners, South Sudan is at GAM rate of upto about 16% above WHO threshold of malnutrition. Donors partners and clsusters need to continue coming together the reduce the burden of malnutrition in South Sudan especially most affected areas. Greater Upper Nile region had 20 counties classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity (i.e. Akobo, Canal/Pigi, Fangak, in Jonglei State;, Mayendit in Unity State; Luakpiny/Nasir, Maiwut, Manyo, Panyikang and Ulang in Upper Nile State); Greater Bahr el Ghazal region has 10 counties classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity (i.e among them are Aweil South in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State; Tonj North in Warrap State that have 17.4% of the population that is about 2.10 million people were in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) acute food insecurity; and 35.0% of the population) cases of malnutriton are very high depites the fact that partners are stepping up heir effort to reduce the malnutriton rate; In the first projection period of December 2020 to March 2021, 22 counties are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, with the number likely to increase to 33 counties in the second projection period of April to July 2021 if nothing is done to prevent the deteriorating situations. With the current levels of HFA (Humanitarian Food Assistance), 0.2% of the population (about 24,000 people) were in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) acute food insecurity in Pibor County in Pibor Administrative Area, and About 1.4 million children under five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021 based on same season historical data of food security and nutrition monitoring system unless action is taken to response and prevent the hunger level and treatment of acute malnutrition early enough, SMART nutrition surveys and admission trends for 2020. This is the highest caseload for acute malnutrition since the start of the crisis in December 2013. The estimation of the caseload was based on the peak lean season historical data that provide higher caseload for better informed response planning. The high levels of acute food insecurity in the country are driven by flooding, insecurity, population displacements, declined crop production, diseases and pests. This project will make services available to vulnerable community esp. children 6-59 months, PLWs through OTPs, TSFPs, BSFPs and MIYNC services and training caregivers to have proper garden demonstration so that are able to have knowledge in generating food locally, to sustainably prevent malnutrition as part of durable solutions.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

HFO multi-sectoral approach helps in ensuring that we meet most of the needs of the targeted beneficiaries including protection, inclusion and equality. Healthcare Foundation Organization during the initial stages of the projects conducts a general risk assessment and once the project is assessed to cause risks to both the beneficiaries and the organization, mitigation measures are always employed. In previous projects, mitigation measures such as clear and open communication with the beneficiaries will help in protecting the beneficiaries from being exploited, choosing locations for registration and distribution which are close and safe for the beneficiaries and an inclusive decision making process with the beneficiaries. HFO also works with the government and the community leaders to ensure that adequate information reaches the beneficiaries and the community leaders usually take responsibility of managing the crowds as the community members listen to them more. During distributions and registrations, vulnerable groups such as sick, disabled, pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, children and the elderly are prioritized so that they spend little time in waiting. Confidentiality is also key to protecting the beneficiaries and HFO normally conducts thorough training for the staff before any activities in the field which includes data protection policies etc. 1. The project will make sure the service sites and distribution are free form any threat to beneficiaries, and protection partners will be mapped for referrals services assistance. 2. The project will be designed and implement in a way that everyone is safe and free from intimidation, harrassment, asualts and any other form of SEA. The project will have good and clear mapping of SGBV referral centers where survivors will be attended to. Mothers and caregivers who bring their children and relatives to health and nutrition centers will be accorded with protection. 3. Marginalized group will be involved in decision making of project implementation through their group and leadership. 4. All people (beneficiaries) will be allowed to access all services without hinderance as human right for all. The project will ensure safe audit on services delivery to assess any breach of regulations by health and nutrition workers. And that the beneficiaries are satisfied. Feedback mechanism will be used and be identical to all people voice their concerns.
