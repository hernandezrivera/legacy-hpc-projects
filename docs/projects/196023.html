Project Summary

This project focuses on the continuity of the ongoing project during the COVID 19 and Cholera pandemic, addressing the needs of both internally displaced persons and host communities including Persons with disabilities in the Idlib and western and northern countryside of Aleppo. SRD provides integrated protection activities in the health setting through prevention and risk mitigation activities like awareness-raising sessions, and response activities like case management approaches and consolidated referral pathways. PSS Services are provided in a safe place that is currently functioning and builds resilience of survivors and vulnerable men, women, boys and girls with disabilities. All activities are carried out in consideration of WHO recommended precautionary measures, health clusters, protection and COVID 19 and Cholera Task Force guidelines, but also acknowledging the COVID-19 pandemic has changed and as a result activities will need to be weighed in terms of their risks in the new context (post-vaccination). SRD works with community-based support services in the Idlib and Aleppo provinces through women and girls safe spaces (WGSS), community centers, or child-friendly spaces. SRD provides integrated services for GBV, psychosocial support, and early childhood development within health facilities. SRD provides center-based and outreach services in relevant areas, including community-based protection, CP, and GBV's structured awareness sessions through field activities. By integration approach, accessibility is improved and barriers to vulnerable groups are broken. This successful model will be replicated and continued within this project, as the Health Center has proven to be a secure and sensitive entry point for accessing GBV services. SRH services will include a focus on adolescents and family planning utilizing AMAL Initiative model through raising awareness of relevant services available which include psychosocial support, clinical management of rape cases and providing safe spaces for women and girls. Qualified and trained psychosocial workers will be present in the safe spaces and PSS rooms to manage the activities and provide private sessions for GBV survivors, referred cases, or other individuals seeking focused specialized PSS services. Work includes integrated case management , communication skills, community protection practices, behavioral change communication, maternal and child health, hygiene promotion, nutrition, support for survivors of gender-based violence. Includes guidance on referral routes. Protection awareness messages are delivered to beneficiaries through daily home visits, prioritizing messages on civil documents, HLP, child labor, child marriage and gender-based violence. Individuals in need of protection or other forms of support are referred to facilities and service providers within the referral channel to minimize the risk of breach. Considering what is best for your child through case management services (using GBV IMS+), an integrated emergency outreach team, cash and voucher assistance (CVA), structured and unstructured PSS, parenting skills, legal counseling, early childhood development, referrals, and child care. Provision of specialized and community-based child protection services considering what shall be in the best interests of the child through case management services, integrated Emergency Response Outreach Team, structured and unstructured PSS, parenting skills, early childhood Development, referrals, and addressing children and caregivers’ vulnerabilities that are related to disability, physical/psychological injuries, severe neglect, lack of appropriate care.

3 - Needs

According to the 2023 HNO, approximately 8.5 million people are in need of GBV assistance (1.2 million increase from 2022), of which 55.7 per cent are women and 37.5 per cent are girls. As of 17 December 2022, 500 lab-confirmed cholera cases and 16 deaths have been recorded by the Early Warning, Alert and Response Network.Women and girls in Syria are increasingly at risk of GBV. This is exacerbated by the economic and food crises, and reduced opportunities for women and girls to live in a place they consider safe. This has contributed to negative coping mechanisms among families, such as child marriage. According to the HNO sectoral analysis, Grave violations against children remain a major concern, including the risk of being killed, injured, used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted or sexually abused. By September 2022, 1,895 grave violations were verified by the MRM in Syria. Of those, 1,291 children were cases of recruitment and use of children (69 girls and 1,222 boys). In 2022, 69 per cent of households with members with disabilities reported lacking meaningful access to health services, with people in north-east Syria the most affected (87 per cent lacking access). The compounded effects of such health and protection risks in NWS over a period of 10 years requires the delivery of comprehensive, tailored, and integrated services packages to those most vulnerable, including women, adolescents, children, persons with disabilities (PWD), and the elderly.

4 - Implementation of activities

Centers continue offering the following services: Case management and referral, vocational training and prevention empowerment activities, GBV awareness sessions and protection monitoring. In order to remove physical barriers for women and girls with disabilities, in coordination with the local council, an additional caseworker is recruited that is specialized in disabilities. Plus, elderly and disabled people will be identified and helped with IPA cash module. Case management has CVA to cover the needs of vulnerable beneficiaries who cannot be referred to other services. Un/Structured PSS activities, CPM, parenting skills, and early childhood development are provided to children and their parents, identifying children with vulnerabilities to be referred to CP case managers. All the mobile staff will be trained on emergency plan and therefore ready to expand coverage in order to conduct rapid protection assessment and protection monitoring to identify risks faced by WMBGs, providing emergency service package for newly displaced households. persons with disabilities and older persons, persons with protection concerns; particularly woman- and child-headed households or recent IDPs are served through integrated community-based approach. Protection-specific M&E tools and SAG data segregation is in place led by trained ME team, referring to ME Tool adopted by SC and SRD.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

There are numerous risks, but we can mention the main risks below:Risk 1: Exposure to COVID-19 by staff while working at office or facility.Impact: mediumMitigation Measures: Ensure the application of IPC protocols; implementation of triage system; social distancing within the health facilities and in waiting areas; ensure the availability of PPE, acknowledging the pandemic has changed and as a result activities will need to be weighed in terms of their risks in the new context (post-vaccination).Risk 2: Lack of qualified staff and/or suppliers in areas of implementation.Impact: ModerateMitigation Measures: SRD has worked in NWS since 2011 and is very well positioned to implement the project. The project area is currently considered a relatively stable area, and SRD has access to big markets and various suppliers in the area of implementation. Risk 3: children, ado and PWDs face challenges in accessing the centers to attend sessions.Impact: MediumMitigation Measures: A designated vehicle will be provided to facilitate access for elderly, adolescents, and persons with disabilities to the centers.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The likelihood of Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups and its impact is rated high protection risk analysis. Moreover, Exploitation by service provider or by other actor in the community. And low knowledge on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report. SRD has policies, procedures, and other measures in place that relate to sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment. Sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment are never acceptable, under any circumstances and SRD has a zero-tolerance policy. SRD recognizes that achieving a significant reduction in SEAH is a long-term mission. Zero tolerance is not the same as zero incidents. Reports of incidents may increase as organizations improve safeguards and reporting channels. Increasing reports may indicate a growing awareness of SEAH and changing attitudes, with survivors feeling more comfortable to report and organisations more likely to take action. The reporting of incidents and responses is an indicator that the risk of SEAH is being managed. Such measures taken are train staff/service providers regularly with context appropriate information on code of conduct and PSEA, Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation reporting ways. Establish good relation with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles and stick to them. SRD is an active member in UN SEA Network.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mobilization activities take place to share information with the community focusing on IDPs caregivers esp women about the services safe spaces/CFS. Regular meetings are conducted with NGOs services providers to facilitate referral pathways and project staff are regularly updates on the up-to-date service map. One of the protection outreach trained on protection emergency response to respond rapidly to new displacements with safety Audit, PFA and recreational/dignity kits. Specific budget is allocated for transportation costs for the most at risk individuals. Tensions linked to overcrowding may lead to an escalation of partner violence and other forms of domestic violence these risks might not be identified and rectified. Advocating for appropriate and adequate household rehabilitation to be provided considering privacy. We will include women, girls with disability and at-risk groups to determine such things. Designated car will be provided to facilitate the access of children with disabilities to the centers. In consultation with caregivers community members ensure mobile tents or any other safe space that will mitigate the risks of violating confidentiality and privacy. Protection officer in charge of case management supervision. Budget and well trained SRD staff ensured for sustainable capacity building strategy that shows impacts with pre and post assessment, follow ups, mentoring, coaching initiatives and structured supervision of case managers and midwives.
