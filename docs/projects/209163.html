Project Summary

This project aims to provide Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) to complement the in-kind assistance to support crisis-affected individuals with protection risks across Sudan. IOM will be conducting comprehensive cash feasibility assessments, market assessments, risk mapping and evaluation, communication with communities consultations to confirm usage of Cash-Based Initiative (CBI) modality most applicable in this complex context and to identify and mitigate potential challenges such as the exacerbation of conflict which impacts initial assessment findings and accessibility to the target beneficiaries, inflation of prices which would impact the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB), fluctuation of exchange rates between currencies, and managing assistance in a conflict sensitive way that does not increase community tensions over resources and reduces aid diversion. Assistance will be based on assessments conducted on the specific needs of the targeted vulnerable households/individuals based on comprehensive eligibility criteria and case management recommendations. IOM is committed to using the best modalities and delivery mechanisms available to ensure effective and dignified assistance to vulnerable populations. Although IOM has established a system of mobile money with MTN in Sudan and successfully provided cash assistance through this modality. The type of modalities used for support in the action will include cash distribution, mobile money, bank transfers and a dedicated assessment exercise will be implemented to identify, evaluate, and select the most effective modality which will be most suitable for beneficiaries and best financial service provider (FSP) prior to distributions.
After the completion of the assessments and consultations with relevant local authorities and communities, eligible household/individuals will be registered and enrolled in the CBI programme based on predetermined criteria. The distribution of MPCA for beneficiaries to address food, non-food, communications, transportation, health care needs not already catered for through other plans or proposed responses. This support would assist crisis-affected beneficiaries with their immediate basic needs in coordination with the Cash Working Group (CWG) and other multisector partners to maximize impact of the assistance (i.e., providing basic services such as direct protection assistance, non-food and hygiene items along with cash assistance so that beneficiaries have increased purchasing power). The MPCA support will complement in-kind and direct assistance prioritizing newly displaced and vulnerable households focusing on people with special needs to reduce use of negative coping mechanisms such as selling assets to buy food, other items, or to access services; and mitigating risks of GBV when giving cash to women or female headed households (FHH) so that they have more purchasing power, decision making capacity, and are able to support their household's survival in the face of continuous shocks. Based on the latest assessment by the CWG the current MEB for household level is approximately 170,300 SDG/285 USD per household with an average of six persons and MEB for individuals is 28,387 SDG/50 USD per person. IOM will seek to provide between 3 rounds of support per household per year to support them in meeting their basic needs. The MEB and transfer fees for the financial service providers have also been factored into the calculations for the 10,000 households targeted by IOM for assistance to ensure costs for service provision are accurately reflected.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Cash assistance is critical for humanitarian response because it allows for a flexible assistance modality for vulnerable crisis-affected communities that improves the choice and dignity of beneficiaries. Cash assistance can support basic needs and subsidies livelihoods, which gives the recipients purchasing power and agency they may not have access to otherwise; helping ensure that vulnerable populations do not resort to negative coping mechanisms due to situational challenges and other barriers to access such as conflict and poverty. MPCA will be prioritized for crisis-affected persons such as IDPs who have lost assets and income generating options; youth, women, and people with disabilities or special needs with societal barriers to access who typically do not have decision making or purchasing power making them more susceptible to exploitation and abuse, especially in the current conflict, including trafficking, forced labour, child marriage, Gender-Based Violence (GBV). The planned MPCA will conduct specific assessments including through referrals from protection partners to propose cash assistance that mitigates protection risks and address the needs of the most vulnerable based on specific protection assessments. These referrals will be for individuals or HH at risk of immediate harm, as well as in cases where individuals and/or HHs face protection concerns that negatively impact well-being of affected persons; this includes providing emergency support post evacuation or displacement, covering additional protection related costs for which the value of the MEB is insufficient (such as paying for legal fees, family reunification, emergency shelter for survivors, or other identified services, or special items/equipment for people with disabilities). Although IOM has established a system of mobile money with MTN in Sudan and successfully provided cash assistance through this modality. The type of modalities used for MPCA distribution will include cash in hand distribution, mobile money, bank transfers and a dedicated assessment exercise will be implemented to identify, evaluate, and select the most effective modality which will be most suitable for beneficiaries and best financial service provider (FSP) prior to distributions.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

In the current context, IOM will be prioritizing the use of Communicating with Communities (CwC) to ensure that there is a continuous process of listening, responding, and adapting programs based on the needs of the community and factor in conflict sensitivity. The number of persons reached and number of rounds of MPCA will depend on inflation, the beneficiary needs, beneficiary access, registration and continued eligibility, and ability of field colleagues to contact registered persons repeatedly in the current context. This may be a challenge due to the unpredictability of the current situation, prolonged and/or expanding conflict and potential variability and sudden displacement or return of crisis-affected populations across Sudan. The distributions will take place after beneficiaries are selected and persons participating in the CBI programme (at least one person from the target households) will be required to attend awareness sessions for sensitization on practical uses for the MPCA and various modalities of the assistance that could best suit them. The distribution and awareness sessions will be organized for small groups which will mitigate overcrowding, and added security measures will be employed for distribution and awareness sessions where cash will be distributed in hand. During and after the distribution sessions satisfaction surveys including questionnaire on criteria, eligibility, distance to distribution sites, beneficiary use of funds, protection needs, effectiveness and efficiency of support provided will be conducted to establish baselines and set-up for beneficiary feedback that informs implementation and allows the CBI officer and staff to adjust programming as needed. IOM will also conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) missions of the MPCA to determine support for the beneficiaries, best practices, and lessons learnt. The PDMs will take place after each of the distribution rounds (between 1 to 3 months) depending on if 1 or 3 rounds of cash distribution took place. At least 30 per cent of target beneficiaries will be interviewed face-to-face or by telephone depending on availability to ascertain if they were able to receive and spend their CBI safely, if they were able to secure their basic needs, and highlight any challenges experienced. The PDM will be done in coordination with protection staff and services providers a to ensure the protection risks are mitigated and any additional needs for protection assistance are referred to protection case management specialists or relevant specialized partners or service providers. Cash assistance will mainstream protection and risk considerations across the cycle from assessment to distribution and post distribution stage, this will be supported by the protection teams who will conduct risk assessments and support the assessments and PDMs for assistance. The assessments and monitoring will include gender and vulnerability rapid analysis or protection analysis to understand the protection dynamics and risks cash assistance may impose and put in place immediate mitigation measures to address those risks.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

The project will ensure safe and accessible communication channels are available to the project participants, stakeholders, and partners to facilitate complaints, including on SEA. Where there are no active protection partners at the site level, the project will facilitate joint advocacy through the Protection Cluster to establish linkages with protection services and referral pathways. Beneficiary feedback will be collected periodically at three critical stages of the provision of MPCA assistance: (1) During assessments information will be collected about the selection process, beneficiary criteria, and additional beneficiary needs. (2) During distribution and awareness raising missions, feedback and complaints will be collected from beneficiaries through questionnaires about the selection process and report additional beneficiary needs. (3) During PDMs, information and complaints will be collected for beneficiaries through questionnaires about MPCA amounts, modalities, or other support provided during the distribution missions. There will be follow-ups at each of these stages to ascertain if beneficiary complaints were addressed either by IOM or by sector partners. This will improve the feedback mechanisms and allow engagement with the local community on their feedback and addressing their issues in coordination with other Agencies who may have provided additional direct support or referrals to other relevant service providers. IOM has a zero-tolerance policy on SEA, which is in line with the UN policy and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA as well as guidelines from IOM's Institutional GBV in crises framework on how to manage GBV related cases during crises. IOM staff will receive refresher training on how to report SEA and ethical and appropriate ways to refer GBV cases through the referral pathways. These guidelines will also be included and highlighted to any implementing partners to ensure compliance sessions with beneficiaries for MPCA and with those tasked with providing MPCA assistance.
