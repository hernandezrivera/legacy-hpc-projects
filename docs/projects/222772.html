Project Summary

"
The overall goal of this project is to protect children from all forms of violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect through improved knowledge and commitment, well-coordinated age, gender and disability-response services, and strong child protection structures through Prevention, mitigation and response intervention in the allocated camps for plan and adjacent host area. It will follow the socio-ecological model and will include 03 outcomes:
Outcome 1: Vulnerable children and adolescents, especially girls have improved knowledge, skills, capacities, and well-being to protect themselves from violence, neglect, abuse and exploitation
Children (5-11) and adolescents (12-17) in camps and host communities will participate in age and gender-tailored sessions on child rights and child protection risks. These sessions will enable them to identify, report, and respond to any potential child protection and GBV-related concerns. Adolescent clubs in camps will be established and strengthened through life skill education by using Champion of Change (CoC) curriculum (a globally recognized model of Plan International) that will also include youth. Champion of Change graduates will undertake participatory initiatives like campaigning, learning sharing and supporting the CBCPCs to ensure a protective environment for children. These clubs will initiate community activities for both refugees and host communities to promote social cohesion such as theater and sports events.
The project will provide psychosocial support for children and adolescents, including programs for survivors of sexual violence, Children Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups, early/forced/child marriage, children in labor and unaccompanied & separated children. They will receive PSS and PFA with the provision of structured and non-structured psychosocial support as well as emergency NFIs and dignity kits. A total of 46 CFS/MPC will be used for covering all the activities.
Outcome 2: Community-led child protection mechanism is strengthened and functional to ensure a protective environment for children and adolescents
The project aims to operationalize and empower a community-led child protection mechanism to act as a bridge among child protection actors, children and families. CBCPC will be strengthened with an alliance of community-level child protection networks, adolescent clubs, CoC groups and community leaders. Training for the CBCPC members will be provided. This structure will safeguard children and adolescents by preventing, mitigating, reporting and responding to Child protection and GBV concerns including child recruitment through community mobilization and identifying and referring children/adolescents at risk to appropriate mechanisms. This will promote community engagement, ownership and participation while promoting social cohesion, public trust, and accountability.
Community-led child protection mechanism will be strengthened with the support and collaboration of communities, child protection networks, adolescent clubs, and different stakeholder.
Outcome 3: Child protection system is strengthened through increased capacity and willingness of families and different stakeholders to meet children's needs and reduce protection risks.
Parents and caregivers will receive support on best parenting practices, including child marriage, child labor, trafficking, CAAFAG, gender equality and PSEA. The project will arrange ‘Fathers’ Café’ in camps, a successful model through which male caregivers are sensitized on gender equality, shared roles, SGBV and mental health.
As a part of the CP system strengthening, PIB will organize regular meetings with CBCPC, the local govt. Child welfare boards and train the non-CP actors on Child Protection and Gender Based Violence for ensuring referral mechanism to response in time. Coordination and collaboration with government departments like DSS, RRRC, and I/NGOs, and the sector will be the core of this initiative. "

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Bangladesh hosts 967,765 (Ref:UNHCR fact sheet) Rohingya refugees of which 52% are children. Entering the 8th year of the Rohingya humanitarian situation in Bangladesh the needs of children remains dreadful. The CPSS outlines the ongoing need for initiatives including strengthening child protection systems for children and adolescents, families, and communities to access age-appropriate and gender-sensitive child protection services. Urgent action is needed to address the specific needs of girls (including survivors and at risk of GBV), adolescents, and children with disabilities. The GBV Sub-Sector stresses the need to strengthen community prevention, risk mitigation, and response to GBV(Ref:https://rohingyaresponse.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/JRP-2024.pdf). These risks and needs were emphasized in a joint assessment by Plan International Bangladesh and TdH conducted in December 2023 in camps and host communities. The risk of Child trafficking was identified as the top-most critical issue among Rohingya respondents and the second most concerning issue among host community respondents. Child labor, child marriage, harassment, and Children’s involvement in criminal groups were also highlighted as significant concerns in both communities. 08 boys and 3 girls among a total of 86 refugees were identified as victim of trafficking by ATWG between July and September 2024.Due to the escalation of the conflict between the Myanmar Armed Forces and the Arakan Army in Rakhine since February a surge in child recruitment has been reported between late 2023 and early 2024, with children being used in hostilities, espionage, child labor, and drug smuggling. Families face threats from armed groups, leading some to send their children to host communities at night for safety (Ref: Child Recruitment Dynamics in Cox's Bazar: Risks and Realities. Risk Assessment - Dynamics of Child Recruitment) The Joint Protection monitoring report 2024 (Q3) reveals that children continued to face serious protection challenges such as child labor, child marriage, neglect, and mental health concerns. Neglect and physical maltreatment were reported as the most pressing concerns, with 63% of adult informants reporting instances of neglect by parents or caregivers. Economic hardship was cited as a significant driver of violence, coupled with limited awareness among caregivers. Data from the CPIMS+ shows that neglect constituted 31% of all reported cases in Q3 2024. Children with disabilities are especially vulnerable as they are frequently left isolated without adequate care. Girls are often expected to assume childcare responsibilities, limiting their participation in social activities. Children also reported experiencing violence at the hands of parents, teachers, and peers. Boys reported facing punishment from their fathers while girls reported being punished by mothers. Mental health and psychosocial challenges are a growing concern, with 58% of key informants identifying behavioral or psychological issues in children. CPIMS+ data showed that 12% of all reported cases involved psychological distress. The children identified family conflict, separation, restricted freedom, and feeling unheard by their elders as significant sources of stress. Uncertainty about their future, reliance on aid, and social exclusion further exacerbates their anxiety. Girls face additional stress due to restrictions on their movement and fear of violence. However, children reported that community activities, religious events, and support from child protection centers provide moments of relief and joy. Child marriage remains a deeply rooted issue with 29% of key informants acknowledging its prevalence in their communities. Cultural norms, family pressure, heightened security risks and fears about unsafe relationships drive families to arrange child marriage.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will be targeting the most vulnerable individuals, enhance safety, dignity and promote and protect the rights of people, encouraging inclusiveness without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation. Community feedback mechanism will be established and functional. It will apply the following principles of Community, Engagement and Accountability (CEA) mainstreaming in all of its activities:• Prioritize Safety and Dignity and Avoid Causing Harm: It will strongly prevent and minimize any adverse effects of its intervention which can increase people’s vulnerability and risks. • Meaningful Access: It will ensure access to assistance and services in proportion to need and without discrimination. It will pay special attention to individuals who have difficulty accessing assistance. • Accountability: It will set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can be involved in all participatory aspects of project cycle and establish measures for affected populations to measure the appropriateness of interventions and organizations can address concerns and complaints. • Participation and Empowerment: It will support the development of communities’ and individual capacities and assist people to claim their rights.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The proposed action focuses on creating transformative change that supports children, adolescent girls and boys and young women while enhancing community-based strategies and fortifying local protection systems. It will ensure that age- and disability-responsive measures are incorporated into all service points like CFS, MPC, youth club etc.The project will cover minimum 3% person/children with disability of total reach. Special measures will be taken for children with disability to ensure their participation in project intervention and access to required services as well. For instance, home based support will be provided for the children having movement difficulties, facilities like CFS/MPC will be made accessible for them etc.This project aligns with the CP Joint Response Plan (JRP) by addressing the specific needs and vulnerabilities of Rohingya refugees and host community members, with a strong emphasis on inclusivity for age and disability.PIB will ensure that communication is inclusive and transmitted through multiple media options including radio, visual guides, and community mobilization as well as in a diversity of languages like Rohingya and accessible formats when using technologies. Participation, Community engagements and consultation of most affected groups that includes women and youth groups, adolescent girls and boys’ groups, and those having age and disability-related concerns will be kept in account to ensure age and disability inclusive program.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Children friendly complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) will be established at the beginning of the project. CFM includes three channels of data flow that are a hotline number, opinion box and community reflection session with children, youth, parents, and caregivers and other community members. PIB recognizes that Child-Friendly Community Feedback Mechanisms (CFRM) play a key role in strengthening reporting mechanisms for PSEA and in safeguarding children and young people.Feedback received from different channels contributes in program adaptations in many ways including changing implementation strategies. Community members will be encouraged to raise their issues by engaging in project activities, phone call and feedback and information centers.Plan International Bangladesh will make the community people, volunteer, community structures to strengthen the capacity and resilience that will reduce the dependency on aid.Plan International will follow the Tip sheet for AAP made by CPSS at every stage of the project.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Plan International Bangladesh acknowledges that abuse of power is the root cause of Sexual exploitation, Abuse and safeguarding incidents over the survivor. As mainstreaming of PSEA emphasizes to integrating measures to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse into the core practices, policies, and procedures of organizations, hence for this proposed project Plan International Bangladesh is fully committed to ensure:Creating a safe and inclusive culture that allows children, programme participants, staff, associates and visitors to thrive and feel secure while engaging with Plan International; Addressing the drivers of gender inequality and unequal power relations that can lead to harm and violence, sexual harassment, sexual exploitation and sexual abuse (SHEA);Strengthening, cultivating and sharing the capacity of all staff, associates and visitors so they understand, and are supported in meeting, their PSHEA and safeguarding roles, responsibilities, and their duty to do no harm.This project will be promoting safe practices, approaches, interventions and environments which respect, recognize and respond to the specific PSEA and safeguarding needs of children and programme participant. It will also be encouraging, facilitating and receiving all reports related to safeguarding or SHEA concerns.Responding in a timely, effective, safe, comprehensive, procedurally fair and confidential manner to all PSEA and Safeguarding complaints, allegations. We have zero tolerance policy in action on safeguarding and SHEA reports.By integrating Safeguarding and PSEA Plan International Bangladesh will safeguard vulnerable groups (Children and adult programme participant), improve organization's efficacy & reputation, and build a safer environment for everyone involved by incorporating safeguarding and PSEA into the organizational structures.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Climate change adaptation and mitigation are vital for fostering resilience, social cohesion, and sustainable development, especially in areas like Cox’s Bazar. Plan-international Bangladesh will align with EEN (Energy, Environment Network) Climate action strategy (2023-2025) in coordination with CPSS (Child protection sub-sector) to initiate the adaptation and mitigation of climate change. On that path, the projects will provide awareness, and Theatre for Development (TfD) on the importance of navigating climate issues and environmental safety through increased community-based outreaching where Community-based child protection committees will be engaged. The projects will implement activities by keeping environmental sustainability at its core that will address the potential negative and unintended environmental consequences of its proposed activities. The projects will also emphasize using less plastic in packaging and distribution of inputs. To ensure safe waste management, communities will be mobilized to drive cleanliness with trained volunteers in place to support and monitor the waste management being done appropriately. To reduce plastic waste, the projects will not use banners and plastic water bottles in numerous proposed trainings, workshops, meetings and dialogues.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Plan International Bangladesh always consider the contingency plan while design the projects in coordination with EPR WG members and ISCG’s EPR focal point to address potential issues. In total of 30% out of the targeted programme participants (According to JRP) is the contingency capacity PIB holds. As a core child protection actor, we form a team for response during and after the disaster, we select the team member from the PIB existing child protection team and train them on emergency preparedness and response according to EPR guidelines and Standard operation system. To strengthen emergency preparedness in the local communities, the projects will emphasize on enhancing the awareness desiminating activities on DRR. We also will convert our CFS/MPC to emergency shelter – ECCC center for the caregivers and Children’s, PIB has allocated budget for providing food and others need based materials to ensure their safety, and well-being parallel with reduced distress or risk of exposure to further trauma especially children. PIB case management team (FTR- Family Tracing and Reunification) will stay actively functional for family tracing and renunciation of the unaccompanied and separated children caused by the disaster.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

PIB will arrange age and gender-transformative life skills and awareness sessions for 200 female and 200 male youths along with adolescents through Champions of Change (CoC) and Girls Shine modules to enhance their skills. The sessions will contribute to promoting their psychosocial well-being, health, and safety through addressing needs and risks, managing emotions, strengthening positive relations, developing safe and healthy relations, and knowledge about sexual and reproductive health, how to stay safe from GBV and how to seek for support from relevant stakeholders and actors. This session will be conducted in community-based structures such as youth corners, MPC, CFS, and learning centers.PIB will initiate gender and youth-friendly arts and crafts activities for youth to connect them with their cultural roots while encouraging creativity and ensuring mental wellbeing. Participants will engage in regular arts and crafts activities designed to foster self-expression and emotional release. These activities will include painting, drawing, embroidery, and crafting traditional items, helping youth connect with their cultural roots while encouraging creativity. These initiatives will also serve as informal support spaces where participants reflect on their experiences. Youth will participate in these arts and crafts activities who will then contribute to/support in organizing different cultural events in their communities. It will facilitate promoting social cohesion through community engagement and exchange and will also help to reduce protection concern of youth and support build the resilience.Youth will receive orientation on nutrition, drug addiction, human trafficking, and anti-social activities, child labor, early marriage, DRR, GBV and WFP assistance-related. After receiving the orientation, youths will prepare action plans on highlighting targeted activities, and disseminate message in their communities with a view to protect children from harms.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the projects of Plan international Bangladesh creates an enabling environment that empowers refugees to protect themselves against some of the most common risks, such as trafficking, child marriage, and conscription into armed groups. Refugees take active roles to promote child protection through the establishment of Community-Based Child Protection Committees. Local community members are thus empowered through the committee on child protection issues to identify problems and hence take appropriate action toward creation of an enabling environment. Youth initiatives, like the "Nurturing Youth in Camp", set up leadership, Champion of Change session and life skills, while PSS services are intended to work on mental health challenges for better handling of trauma. The overall project will help in developing a resilient, self-reliant community through the inclusion of refugee into local governance, social cohesion, and providing the means-skills needed to face challenges on their own

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, this project is linked with Social security, safety and gender dimensions under District Development and Growth Priorities of Cox's Bazar (Dec 2022). This proposed action will work improving social security systems. It will keep social security perspective in consideration. The project will work for ensuring the benefit from any one or another of 114 disparate social protection programs mentioned in the report of DDGP of Cox'sbazar (Dec 2022), primarily centered around vulnerable sections (widows, differently abled etc.,). This project will influence at demand level by empowering the community people to claim their rights, at the same time it will advocacy at supply level like Union Parishad, Upazila parishad etc.
