Project Summary

Mukti Cox’s Bazar believes that the Food Security Sector (FSS) facilitates the entry point to strengthen the Nutrition and economic development of the Rohingya refugees and the host communities of the Teknaf and Ukhiya upazila of Cox’s Bazar district. Under the ongoing global climate crisis and rapidly changing local climate, an effective FS programme reduces climate vulnerabilities and protection risks and enhances peace and stability. Mukti will work closely to engage 1,400 Rohingya refugees and 1880 host communities in small-scale agriculture, cash support, and plantation activities that will foster self-reliance, food security, dietary diversity, access to fresh foods and environmental sustainability. Agriculture activities focus mainly on homestead vegetable gardening and vertical gardening. The project will have a training component for the beneficiaries on improved agricultural production in small gardens. The technical training will be followed with distribution of seeds, fertilizers, fingerlings, and livestock components for the beneficiary households. The monitoring for the output will include key food security indicators on the supported households, as well as income generating indicators. Additionally, behaviour change communication (BCC) and awareness activities on climate change, gender and DRR will be incorporated with social and health messages.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The socioeconomic condition of Bangladesh was able to withstand the shock of the COVID-19 pandemic through its diversified agricultural and skills-based income generation activities (Tackling the Covid-19 Social and Economic Crisis in Bangladesh, UNICEF, 2021). However, the price hike of daily commodities created another pressure on the day-to-day life of lower- and middle-income families to meet their household needs. The current food inflation rate is down to 8.2% in July 2022 from the highest of 8.6 in June 2022 (WFP VAM report, July 2022). However, the aftermath of the COVID-19 pandemic is still observed in many communities due to the unstable market chain causing price hike of daily commodities. Access to foods, shelters, and income generation activities are three main priorities according to the Joint Multipurpose Needs Assessment (JMNA) in December 2021.Despite many humanitarian and development activities in Teknaf and Ukhiya, the resilience and capacity index of these two Upazilas are the lowest compared to Cox’s Bazar Sadar and Ramu (FAO Resilience Index Measurement and Analysis, 2022). The women are the most vulnerable observed in the assessment. In Bangladesh, the provision of the social safety net is very limited. Around 33% of the host community population had access to social safety net according to the findings. The support were mostly dedicated during the COVID-19 pandemic, however, the trend declined during the price hike of daily commodities when this safety net was also a requirement for the sustainability of the vulnerable population. Bangladesh has a long history of sheltering Rohingya refugees in Myanmar. In 2017, millions of Rohingya were forced to seek refuge in Bangladesh after the brutal ethnic violence initiated by Myanmar. These displaced Rohingyas are currently living in different makeshift camps in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh, waiting to return to their homeland. The total number of Rohingya in Bangladesh has increased from 767,324 (154,264 households) after the August 2017 influx to 901,703 (UNHCR population data, November 2021) in December 2021. These additional populations have a social, natural, environmental, legal, and financial impact on Bangladesh. The skills development support for the Rohingyas is mostly dependent on the humanitarian assistance provided by national and international donor organizations. The purpose of the support is to provide tailored vocational skills so that they can utilize the skills once they return to Myanmar. The overall vulnerability of the Rohingya population has increased since 2017 (Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment 2022). Nevertheless, the resilience of women is also low. According to the latest report from FAO (Resilience Index Measurement and Analysis, 2022), women have the lowest resilience capacity index compared to men in both the Host and Rohingya community. To ensure equal opportunity for women and increase their empowerment, Mukti will take wide ranges of activities focusing on women both in the camps and host communities. Persons with disabilities will also be included to enable them to contribute to society and family so that they can be considered as resources rather than burdens for their families.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This proposed programme is aligned with the rationalization principles of the FS Sector. The programme will ensure that common strategies, systems, protocols, and tools are used across all the camps. This will lead towards a standardised and cost-effective approach to providing equitable services to refugees and also ensure a minimum standard of service delivery by different partners.Mukti Cox’s Bazar will follow an integrated approach to ensure effective and impactful outcomes during project design and implementation. Mukti has been responding to the crisis since 2017. The learning and experience of the last five years show that an integrated approach incorporating other relevant sectors like Nutrition, Protection, Education and WASH will have a more holistic and integrated approach to have greater impact on the response of Rohingya and host communities.Moreover, Mukti has been ensuring stability in the refugee operation for the last five years and improving relations with refugees, the Government and other partners in the camps. As a result, Mukti is now considering multi-year funding, especially for critical activities that may require sustained longer-term engagement in Food Security.In addition, Mukti is committed to serving in the FS sector in the same areas for several years, which is mentioned in its 03 years humanitarian action plan that will support strengthening relations with government agencies, the refugees, and other implementing partners in the area. All activities are aligned with the JRP following current Sectoral/Working Group objectives and strategy. We also actively participate in the relevant Sector and/or Working Group and coordination meetings. Notably, Mukti has a strong government Liaison team to obtain the relevant approvals from the government promptly.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Mukti Cox’s Bazar will ensure mainstreaming of protection throughout the implementation of the activities. “Do no harm” is the guiding principle for implementing the activities for the vulnerable people. Privacy and confidentiality of the beneficiaries will be considered while designing and implementing the project. Beneficiaries will also receive awareness raising session to protect them from any unwanted incidents.Mukti Cox’s Bazar will deliver services and assistance in ways that preserve the physical integrity of individuals and communities, that are culturally appropriate and that avoid any unintended negative consequences. Mukti will provide assistance and services according to needs and not on the basis of age, sex, gender, nationality, race, ethnic allegiance and so on. Services and assistance are provided in an adequate scale, within safe and easy reach, are known by the affected individuals and accessible by all groups including medical cases, disabled individuals, discriminated groups and the like. Mukti beneficiaries and volunteers provide feedback through different channels, which allow us to improve the programme quality and prevent or mitigate situations.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Mukti Cox’s Bazar mainstreams an age, gender, and diversity (AGD) approach throughout the planning, implementation, and reporting processes of the proposed project. We collect data disaggregated by age, sex and disability for both analysis and programming purposes. As a result, operations are able to account for the differing needs of persons of concern with respect to their age, sex and disability. The proposed project will engage a variety of different groups, notably women, PwD, faith leaders, caregivers, parents, youth and adolescent from both Rohingya and Host Community, encouraging widespread participation. Awareness and mutual understanding will be raised through joint activities, the improvement of the shared environment and soft skills development. Mukti Cox’s Bazar will ensure that persons of concern and stakeholders receive information on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), including child protection, along with complaints and feedback mechanisms to ensure that persons of concern and stakeholders can provide feedback or raise concerns or complaints on project implementation. Mukti organisational Code of Conduct, PSEA policy, Regulations on Child Protection are binding for all employees and all collaborators need to commit to the key principles of the CoC. Moreover, Mukti has standardized recruitment process. Reference checks are carried out as a mandatory part of this process. During the design and implementation of the project, Mukti participatory process and complaints and feedback mechanism contribute to age and child safeguarding as well.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to affected populations (AAP) is an active commitment to use power responsibly by taking into account giving account to and being held to account by the people humanitarian organisations seek to assist. Mukti will provide beneficiaries with a meaningful influence over decision-making in inclusive, non-discriminatory arrangements for diverse communities. Besides this, Mukti will also put individual feedback mechanisms and innovative approaches to joint feedback mechanisms that can reinforce transparency and mutual accountability and have a positive impact. Project information will be shared with the beneficiaries effectively and transparently. Mukti will ensure the inclusion of persons with disabilities and special needs through the programme activities, which include the participation of the persons with disabilities, and taking the opinion during the designing & planning of any activities in the programme and ensuring the transparency of the programme.

As a PSEA network member, Mukti Cox’s Bazar has compliant policies prioritizing protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) with an emphasis on 'Do No Harm' to prevent unintended negative effects of any programme. 3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Mukti Cox’s Bazar has a comprehensive resource on Disaster Risk Management (DRM) to ensure children, communities are prepared, and through community groups, DRR messaging will be continually disseminated during monsoon and cyclone seasons. Mainstreaming environmental considerations is one of Mukti’s core principles to ensuring high quality programming. The project includes activities that may involve the transportation, storage materials, which have the potential to affect the quality of soil due to possible creation of waste/pollution. Mukti is committed to limiting negative environmental impacts through robust planning, risk mitigation and monitoring. With the implementation of prevention and mitigation measures, as well as the promotion of good practices, Mukti expects to have little impact on the environment.
