Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) will support the most vulnerable families in drought, locust and conflict affected internally displaced persons (IDP) camps and host communities of Somalia by addressing their urgent protection and prevention and response to gender-based violence (GBV). To ensure that children are at the center of every intervention and that they are protected from harm, exploitation, violence and abuse, child protection interventions will be mainstreamed into all sectors as well as direct responses to the child protection concerns by the child protection team. The humanitarian response will provide critical child protection services. To provide critical child protection services the response will focus on strengthening community-based child protection structures to raise awareness and prevent VAC, strengthen case workers and CWCs capacity for early identification of cases and effective referrals of cases of violence against children, providing timely and quality case management support and referral services to children with protection concerns including SGBV. Children living outside the family environment will be provided with FTR services to UASC and alternative care arrangements. To mitigate the impact psychological impacts of trauma SCI will build the capacity of frontline workers to offer immediate psychosocial support and referring families that require further support along the MHPSS pyramid In addition, caregiver capacity will be enhanced for parents under stress through the evidence based Parenting without violence sessions to prevent physical and humiliating punishment in the home. Trainings on child protection, SGBV and MPHSS using the IASC guidelines will be delivered to different stakeholders and partners, including sector staff (Health, Education, Nutrition, WASH and Livelihoods) to ensure mainstreaming of CP and improved coordination of services. Efforts will be made to ensure equal participation of minority groups like excluded clans, children in IDP camps, women, girls and as well as people and children with disabilities. Coordination will also be strengthened by placing a full time secondment to the co-chair role. SCI will strengthen the community /school linkage by training teachers and CCWs jointly on Child protection and MHPSS and support the role out safe schools for children. To improve the quality of case management SCI will conduct a refresher on the case management SOPs and roll of the CM CPMIS

1. - Projects activities

• Strengthen existing CFS to provide psychosocial support services for children affected by drought and conflict (17 CFS/area) to provide psychosocial support (including Psychological First Aid), especially age appropriate CFS activities that support children psychosocial wellbeing, as per international guidelines. (900 children in SL, 800 in PL and 1,200 in CSS) 50% of girls in all locations.• Support to caregivers and families (MHPSS, parenting without violence and other kind of support including child resilience programme) of children facing protection risks in order to enhance their capacity to care for and protect their children. (1,100 children in SL, 1,250 in PL and 1,500 in CSS)• Increasing the capacity of community-based child protection structures and mechanisms (CBCPM) to prevent and mitigate CP risks through multi-layered, community based psychosocial support interventions to reduce vulnerabilities and risks to children by building a protective environment at family and community levels. Peer to peer awareness raising will be led by CBCPMs, youth and community volunteers’ networks to prevent child protection risks, identify vulnerable children in need of support such as case management and emergency child protection care (i.e. alternative care or basic PSS activities. (Target 650 persons: 10 CWCs @10members and 10 CECs@10members in each area)• Provide protection and support to children at risk and child survivors including unaccompanied and separated through case management services that meets their unique needs, which include family tracing and reunification services, alternative care. The capacity of community volunteers will be strengthened to support the case management process, namely through identification of children at risk, making home monitoring visits and offering peer support to caregivers• Conduct awareness raising activities targeting community wide on prevention messages and awareness sessions including on recruitment/CAAFAG, SGBV, harmful practice, family separation, and Children’s rights. (SL: 18,000 children, 31,000 adults; PL 18,000 children, 25,000 adults, CSS: 25,000 children, 27000 adults)• Provision of gender sensitive dignity kits (underwear, sanitary towel and soap) for vulnerable girls and boys (SL, 1400 children, PL 1400 children, CSS, 1500 children)• In collaboration with SC health teams, train health service providers (female and male) on the clinical management of GBV including provision of Post Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) and Emergency Contraception (EC) and on PFA for GBV survivors. (1000 children in SL, 900 in PL and 1200 in CSS)• Provide support to children and adolescents’ survivors of sexual and gender -based violence in coordination with the Gender Based Violence service providers through referral and direct support. The capacity of case workers will continue to be built through learning strategies including case management training, mentoring and coaching. (SL: 1,500 children, 2,300 adults; PL 1,500 children, 2,200 adults, CSS: 2,200 children, 2,700 adults)• To improve coordination, SC will build the capacity of CP working groups and task forces, including GBV. SC will continue to co-chair the national CP AOR. In 2021, SC will enhance partner capacity to respond to safeguarding concerns by contextualising standards and training on humanitarian principles. • Train drought response staff (Health, FSL, Education, Nutrition Staff) on mainstreaming of CP in other sectors including on identification children with CP concerns, PFA and referral mechanisms (Target:190 SC staff (30/ Area). • Family strengthening through cash grants (link with child poverty sector) for extremely vulnerable children and survivors of gender-based violence and other protection concerns (1500 families: 500 per area)
