Project Summary

An estimated 9 million people including refugees in South Sudan, will experience critical needs in 2024-quarters of South Sudan's population – are projected to require humanitarian and protection assistance. Two million people remain displaced due to decades of conflict, intercommunal violence and extreme effects and events of climate change, such as flooding and dry spells. The impact of the Sudan crisis resulted in the arrival of vulnerable population which has worsen the already dire situations, overstretching the capacity.In this context, education plays a crucial role for South Sudan’s children including refugees. Education provides safety, keeping children and youth from physical harm, whilst also contributing to psychosocial wellbeing and health, when complemented by WASH and school feeding interventions. Schools provide safe spaces that also function as a platform to deliver lifesaving interventions, such as food, water, sanitation, mental and physical health services. Education programmes mitigate the impact of humanitarian crises by building resilience among children, young people and their communities. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) programmes in education curricula and preparedness plans equip and strengthen children’s ability to deal with emergencies and mitigate the impact of future disasters. WV will implement EiE projects in the counties where it has a strong operational presence, characterized with very high needs, especially in education and other related sectors: CES (Juba) Upper Nile (Manyo, Fashoda, Baliet, Renk, Malakal & Melut), WES (Yambio, Nzara, Tambura), Warrap (Tonj North, Twic, Gogrial East, Gogrial West, Tonj South and Tonj East), Northern Bahr El Ghazal (Aweil North, Aweil South) targeting populations affected by conflict or disaster in 2024.
WV is currently implementing education programs in partnership with ECHO, Education Can't Wait, EMPOWER Irish Aid, UNHCR in 3 States. To consolidate on current efforts while reaching further and deeper into the areas with dire needs by targeting most vulnerable children, in hard-to-reach areas WV will continue to focus on emergency affected rural and urban areas.
In line with the Education Cluster Response Plan, WV will ensure that through this project, 30,000 children will have access to safe and equitable quality education; 600 teachers, and 650 PTA’s will be trained and supported with supplies and materials; 1500 youth will be provided life skills and vocational training with starter kits. Out of the identified learners, the project will target about 400 children (200 boys, 200 girls) with disabilities. The learners will also be categorized under social/residential status as internally displaced, host community, returnees or refugees during the actual enrolment in the project.
WV will also prioritize establishment of climate resilience learning structures with gender-segregated, responsive and inclusive WASH facilities, and other supplies/materials/kits that are supportive and enhance motivation of children to go to and remain in school with a particular emphasis on girl’s education. In addition, recreational opportunities will be established and implemented to support the recovery of children and youth and promote their overall wellbeing. All learners will be given equal access to education despite Gender, disability or ethnic background.

1. Needs

In 2024, South Sudan will face significant humanitarian needs a alongside drastic cuts to aid budgets, 9.4 million people, 1 including 5 million children, 22.2 million women and 1.3 million people with disabilities will require humanitarian assistance. The needs indicated are: 2.8 million children are out of school; 23% of schools have classes under trees; 77% of schools reported a lack of separate toilets or washrooms for girls and boys; 60% of classrooms and 50% of latrines are not accessible. Less than 25% reported having a general protection referral mechanism; Only 34% of head teachers reported teachers had been provided with PSS training. The overview of needs highlights the pressing educational challenges in South Sudan, drawing insights from key data sources such as the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview and the Education Cluster's 2024 report. Around 2 million children and teenagers aged 6 to 17, with girls comprising 53 percent, and 1 million children aged 3 to 5 face significant barriers to education, including issues like early marriage and gender-based violence, especially affecting girls. The main goal for 2024 is to improve access to safe and conducive learning environments for vulnerable populations, notably internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. This effort aims to address the educational requirements of more than 311,000 IDPs, 1,535,000 settled communities, and 189,000 returnees. An additional critical concern is the shortage of qualified teachers, with one-third lacking proper training, leading to high pupil-teacher ratios averaging 93:1. In 2024, approximately 41,000 teachers, predominantly in remote areas, will need assistance with teaching resources and essential incentives to ensure the delivery of quality education, this is according to the Humanitarian Action. The overview of education needs presented here was validated and prioritized by members of the Education Cluster SAG as part of the strategy development process. WV will also prioritize establishment of learning structures with gender-segregated WASH facilities, and other supplies/materials/kits that will support and motivation of children to enroll and remain in school with a particular emphasis on girl’s education. Recreational opportunities will be established and implemented to support the recovery of children and youth and wellbeing. All learners will be given equal access to education despite gender, disability or ethnic background. WV through its partnership department, will enhance capacities of CSOs, CBOs and national organizations through collaborative implementation and knowledge sharing. WV will implement EiE projects in the following counties where it has strong and operationally meaningful presence, with very high needs, especially in education and other related sectors: CES (Juba) Upper Nile (Renk, Fashoda, Manyo, Baliet, Malakal, Melut), WES (Yambio, Nzara, Tambura), Warrap (Tonj North, Twic, Gogrial East, Gogrial West, Tonj South and Tonj East), North Bar El Ghazal (Aweil North, Aweil South & Awiel East) targeting populations affected by conflict or disaster in 2024.
