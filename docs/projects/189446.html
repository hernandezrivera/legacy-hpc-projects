Project Summary

The proposed project will cement on the work SLI is already doing on the ground in Renk. This emergency Water, Hygiene and sanitation (WASH) project will assist the most vulnerable households from the Returnees, IDPs and host communities to have access to safe drinking water through provision of raft of measures. In order to address WASH related gaps, this project will provide lifesaving WASH interventions by distributing essential WASH items which include WASH Hygiene kits, buckets, collapsible, Aqua tabs, Pur sachets and non-collapsible jerry cans and other items with support from WASH Core pipelines. The project will rehabilitate 24 water points, establish 24 latrine stances, and ensure 4 nutrition facilities have access to water, and provide 420 SAM children and PLW with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion. A total of 36000 Households classified as severe, extreme and catastrophe in Renk County. Disaggregation of beneficiaries shall be informed by actual needs during the intervention. Community members will be mobilized through the established community hygiene promoters (CHPs) and encouraged to avoid bad practices such as open defecation. Hygiene promotion activities including hand washing after visiting toilets, before and after eating food will be shared with households to minimize common water related diseases such as typhoid, cholera among others in the IDPs, returnees and host communities living in poor hygienic conditions. In covid19 context, hygiene promotion will be conducted at household, community and school levels to educate affected persons on transmission routes of Covid19 and how to protect themselves from Covid19 infection. Social distancing, washing hands with soap, use of sanitizers and masks are some of the activities to be conducted on Covid 19. The project will address and mitigate risks related to Gender Based Violence (GBV), which has badly affected women and girls. These groups of beneficiaries suffer multiple problems including sexual assaults while accessing water points, sexual assaults and rapes due to distances travelled as well as intimate partners violence (IPV) where women are subjected to beating for failing to bring enough water home in time and suspicion of having affairs with other men if delayed by the long queues in few boreholes or far away stream or river. Women and girls will get support and access to WASH MHM kits to assist them during menstrual periods. The project is designed to incorporate knowledge gained during the trainings on the accountability to affected persons (AAP) and widely consultation of beneficiaries, before coming up with WASH activities. Overall the project is designed in line with the WASH cluster’s objectives and overall the humanitarian response plan for 2022.

1 - Needs

The population of Renk have been left in highly vulnerable conditions due to four underlying factors including inter-communal violence, floods, scarcity of food as reported in the Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) report in May-July 2021, and the economic crisis. In the latest Humanitarian Needs Overview Report (2022), 26,200 Households classified as severe, extreme and catastrophe in Renk County. In the prevailing acute food security situation, vulnerable internally displaced persons and host community who have lost household assets as a result of conflict and flooding require in-kind NFIs support. Such support will enable them dedicate their remaining recourses to buy food and medicine instead of using the money to replenish lost household items. Additionally, the three counties are grappling with the impact of flooding and Covid 19. Due to flooding, the water sources such as boreholes and hand-dug wells were submerged and contaminated leading the population to resort to drinking untreated surface water. Displaced households lost their belongings to floods such as cooking utensils and water containers. The general hygiene condition in internally displaced sites is worsening and there are potential water borne diseases such as cholera, hepatitis A &E and intestinal worms. Assessment conducted in the county have also noted that the menstrual hygiene condition of women and girls is poor due to lack of sanitary kits and soap. In locations with water pumps, lack of pump mechanics has made it impossible to repair damaged water pumps and boreholes triggering water scarcity. Further, dysfunctional markets, lack of WASH enough WASH partners, absence of community based hygiene promoters and water management committees has negatively affected the capacity of the affected population to respond to disaster. During the present Covid-19 pandemic, displaced population who are living in overcrowded settings and lack personal protective materials (sanitizer, soap and masks), the opportunity for social distancing and limited access to correct information on transmission routes of Covid-19, are in danger of getting infected with Covid-19 if mitigation measures are not taken. In light of this, there is a need to carry out WASH intervention in Renk in order to save lives and alleviate suffering of the most vulnerable people using cluster guideline on Phase floods and Covid response. SLI will utilize the grant obtained through this call to conduct hygiene promotion at the community and household level including COVID-19 messaging and distribute lifesaving WASH items such as PUR sachets, filter cloth, aqua tabs, jerry cans, soap and buckets and menstrual hygiene kits for women and girls affected by floods, Covid 19 and are food insecure in targeted counties. The proposed emergency response is in agreement with the WASH Cluster Floods and Covid 19 Preparedness and response and Humanitarian Response Plan for South Sudan. The assistance will cover only returnees, internally displaced persons and most vulnerable host community members who fall under the following categories: IPC 4 and IPC5, elderly (given their advanced age, poor economic status coupled with inability to actively engage in income generating activities); pregnant and lactating women (they are exposed to various risks such as sickness, malnutrition and protection thus making them more vulnerable compared to any other group); children; unintegrated persons; people with no community links; severely sick persons; and individuals living with disabilities.

2 - Project activities

Activity 1.0: Community consultation and sensitization. SLI project team will conduct community consultation and sensitization meetings on this project with local authorities, IDPs, host community, women, youth and chiefs to brief them about the project and enumerate the role of the community and SLI in the implementation. Covid-19 protocols will be observed during the meetings. Activity 1.1 Form and train AAP committee. SLI will facilitate the formation of Accountability to the affected population (AAP) committees of nine members (5F and 4M) to ensure community participation in the implementation, enhance acceptability and sustainability. AAP will comprise of community leaders, women, youth and People with Special Needs alongside SLI’s staff will work closely to effectively deliver WASH response. Selection of AAPs will be done RRC and community leaders. SLI will work with the AAPs to select enumerators, Hygiene promoters, beneficiaries, distribution and monitoring. Activity 1.2: Selecting and training of 30 (20 F, 10 M) hygiene promoters. SLI and AAPs will select 30 hygiene promoters (20 Female, 10Male) with previous experience and knowledge of hygiene promotion. CHPs must be versed with local culture and should demonstrate capacity to read, write and communicate either in English, Arabic and Local dialect to enable clear and widespread information dissemination. Their training will be done in line with WASH cluster hygiene messaging and will be facilitated by SLI’ WASH Manager and WASH Officers. Training topics: safe water chain, safe excreta disposal, environmental hygiene, hand washing with clean water and soap/ash, how to make tippy taps, food hygiene, Menstrual Hygiene, Cholera Prevention messages and COVID-19 preventive messages as part of mainstreaming. Activity 1.3: Conducting house to house hygiene promotion CHPs will conduct house-to-house hygiene promotion to develop good hygiene practices, prevent disease and encourage positive hygiene practices among IDPs and host communities. Hygiene promotion will entail; Water purification, treatment and storage, Hygiene in the home, Hand washing and Menstrual Hygiene Management; sustainable water purification methods and hand washing and Covid19 awareness. SLI will utilize Health and hygiene promotion guidelines for covid-19, which has been developed by Global health and WASH Cluster. Activity 1.4: Selection of beneficiaries for WASH NFIs SLI, AAPs and enumerators will select 26200 HHs beneficiaries support distributing and conduct post distribution monitoring. Selection criteria will include households with elderly persons, caregivers of separated and unaccompanied children, unaccompanied minors, severely sick persons, individuals living with disability, individuals with no community links and women headed households and youths. 1.6. Distribute WASH NFIs beneficiaries Distribution of the items to 36000 HHs will be conducted through enumerators, Chief and AAPs. SLI WASH Officers will oversee the distribution process ensuring quality assurance and adherence to WASH cluster Covid-19 guidelines. Activity 1.7: Conduct post distribution monitoring. The PDM will be carried out using WASH NFI’s cluster PDM household questionnaires to assess the appropriateness as well as beneficiary satisfaction with amount & quality of items distributed, selection processes and quality of distribution. Respondents will be selected randomly among the beneficiary household with focus on the IDPs and vulnerable host communities. The project will employ a sample size of 20% (7200HHs) of the household who will benefit directly through the intervention. Activity 1.8: Provide 420 standard kits to 210 SAM children and 210 PLW provided with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion. Activity 1.9: Establish water points in 4 nutrition sites. Activity 1.10: Establish 24 latrines stances with flood resilient design, Activity 1.11: Establish 12 water points with flood resilient design.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

SLI is committed to the protection of human rights, therefore it has established mechanisms to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). SLI is dedicated to upholding IASC 6 core principles on PSEA and will integrate PSEA sessions in all its activities, SLI will have a full time Gender Officer tasked with mainstreaming protection and creating awareness on PSEA, training, field staffs and enumerators on PSEA and their policies pertaining to PSEA during project implementation. The Gender officer will provide information about PSEA during inception meetings with community leaders, IDPs and host community representative, during focus group discussions and meetings with women and girls, and during selection of beneficiaries, during distribution of Wash NFIs. Also, the Gender Officer will ensure PSEA messaging through banners, T-shirts and brochures during project activities. The Gender Officer will hold biweekly meetings with women and girls representatives to gauge their perception on protection concerns as well as seek their views on how best to safeguard their protection concerns. SLI gender officer will also play a prominent role through existing referral pathways in Renk. Necessary PSEA referrals will be done if and when they arise by the gender officer

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Information about the project (aim of the project, its objectives, targeted beneficiaries, project duration, project donors, and the kind of assistance and beneficiaries selection criteria) will be provided to community members, leaders and local authorities during inception meetings, review meetings and end of project stakeholders meeting. Project reports including progress reports, post distribution monitoring reports and end of project reports will be shared with community representatives, Wash NFIs cluster at State and National level and with the community leaders in Renk County. Information on feedback mechanism will be provided by SLI’s Gender Officer during meetings, training, and selection of beneficiaries and at distribution venues. The Gender Officer will conduct sensitization and awareness about the type of feedback that beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can submit and avenues of receiving feedback. As far as complaint mechanism, SLI will distribute a toll free number which will be under custody of Gender Officer and Protection Officer. The number will also be displayed prominently at registration sites as well as distribution sites. Complaint and Feedback desks will also be availed during selection of beneficiaries and distribution of shelter and non-food items.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SLI will create forums where project staff, its Gender and Protection Officer will conduct Focus Group Discussions, individuals and group meetings to obtain information from beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries on their perceptions regarding project implementation. In instances where a member of a family is disabled, SLI will conduct personalized meetings so that the individual or their caregivers can be able to communicate their preferences, submit complaints and received feedback. Also, SLI will seek to engage women in leadership positions so that their voices, concerns and preferences can be taken into account right from project planning, project implementation, project planning and evaluation. Further, Complaint and Feedback Data will be disaggregated by gender and age in order to measure the level of participation of females and those living in disability in the mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

SLI employs evidence-based programming in all its projects which will apply to this project through constant information sharing and feedback from the community. Project feedback and suggestions from the community will be gathered through multiple channels, especially hygiene promoters during hygiene promotion activities, monitoring activities e.g. post-distribution monitoring (PDM), safety audits (on protection and GBV). The information gathered will be used to inform future programming on the efficiency and effectiveness of the assistance, preferred quality and quantity of WASH/NFIs and protection concerns associated WASH/NFI in the targeted locations. The feedback/information gathered will also be used in advocacy for further actions from relevant parties in situation where SLI is unable to respond to the needs. Positive feedback will form part of documentation of the project success stories.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

On risks of beneficiaries being exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services, SLI will conduct a safety audit on all registration and distribution points to map existing and potential risks. Safety Audit Findings will be embedded in project work plan and safety matrix to ensure all project activities are conducted in locations and sites where no risk is posed to staff, project supplies or beneficiaries. SLI will ensure registration and distribution points are near settlement sites to reduce on distance travelled by beneficiaries to collect their shelter and non-food items. On risks of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance, SLI has a robust PSEA policy with all the staff properly trained on PSEA. SLI will conduct PSEA training targeting enumerators, community mobilizers and AAPs committees. Also, SLI’S gender and protection officers will conduct sensitization targeting women and girls, community leaders and local authorities on PSEA mitigation and reporting. On risks that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA, SLI’S Child Protection policy prohibits any form of child Labour and child exploitation. Gender Officer and the Protection Officer will conduct community sensitization and awareness to ensure child protection concerns are addressed and mitigated before they occur. Cases of Child Protection will be referred to appropriate child protection actors at project locations. On risks that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. The project targeting criteria is clear and targets these category of beneficiaries. Community communication and sensitization will be conducted targeting beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries in order to minimize of tensions arising from sections of the community who do not receive the assistance. To mitigate HLP related disputes and conflict, SLI through local authorities, protection partners and chiefs will conduct due diligence will be made on land tenure system to ascertain land ownership, proof of ownership and potential and underlying land and property concerns at the community level and IDPs sites. Training on HLP access issues and HLP dispute Resolutions will be ensured to mitigate HLP cases. Staff, local authorities and AAPs will assist to clarify tenure arrangements and authentication of HLP ownership documents/alternative proof of ownership prior to distribution of Shelter materials to beneficiaries.
