Project Summary

iMMAP supports the humanitarian actions in Nigeria with quality information management services, which include the complete data management cycle, analysis, capacity strengthening in IM, and other thematic expertise. iMMAP's interventions enable partners to make informed decisions that ultimately provide high-quality targeted assistance to those most in need in Northeast Nigeria.
Through the highly valued support from USAID Bureau for Humanitarian Assistance (BHA), iMMAP continues to provide critical support to partners across Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states. In coordination with OCHA, iMMAP supports sectors, working groups, coordination bodies, National and International NGOs, the Borno State Agency for the Coordination of Sustainable Development and Humanitarian Response (BoACSDHR), the University of Maiduguri (UNIMAID), and the American University of Nigeria (AUN) in Yola, Adamawa state. In addition, iMMAP acts as the hosting agency for the Nigeria International NGO Forum (NIF).
iMMAP also works closely with humanitarian partners to select participants – based on needs – for its training programs across the BAY states. Furthermore, iMMAP selects qualified and recently graduated candidates from different humanitarian organizations, the University of Maiduguri (UNIMAID), and government agencies that previously participated in our capacity-building sessions to take part in our internship program.

1 - Needs

Information Management Needs:Considering the gaps in the humanitarian operations across Northeast Nigeria and the number of humanitarian actors responding to the protracted crises, there is a need for coordinated response efforts through the availability of quality data to support programmatic decision-making, prioritization, coordination, and resource allocation. Also, iMMAP's activities will address the need for sustained information management support to humanitarian actors involved in the response, mostly the NGOs in order to facilitate a better understanding of the needs, response, gaps, and important trends.

2 - Activities

iMMAP builds on its strong support to the humanitarian response in Nigeria to further assist with the delivery of its objectives by relying primarily on the pool of national Information Management Officers (IMOs) whose capacity has been developed over the years to support the humanitarian response through the Humanitarian Information and Analysis Unit (HIAU). iMMAP strengthens the IM capacity of national and international Humanitarian NGO partners up to lower administrative levels, through the delivery of basic, advanced, and thematic ad hoc training sessions, as well as on-the-job coaching. iMMAP develops innovative IM tools to facilitate seamless inter-sectoral analysis and monitoring of the humanitarian response. iMMAP facilitates joint mapping activities or “mapathons” for the humanitarian response sectors’ members and other actors to develop new information products and tools from remote sensing, spatial and statistical datasets, in support of coordinated and evidence-based prioritization of support to vulnerable communities. iMMAP collaborates and provides technical support such as capacity building, product design, and data management that enable effective response monitoring for Borno state government through the Borno State Agency for the Coordination of Sustainable Development and Humanitarian Response (BoACSDHR). iMMAP collaborates with the University of Maiduguri (UNIMAID), and the American University of Nigeria (AUN), in Adamawa state to conduct its capacity building programs. iMMAP also works closely with humanitarian partners to select participants – based on needs – for its training programs across the BAY states. Furthermore, iMMAP selects qualified and recently graduated candidates from different humanitarian organizations, the University of Maiduguri (UNIMAID), and government agencies that previously participated in our capacity-building sessions to take part in the internship program.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

iMMAP promotes protection mainstreaming by encouraging operational actors to collect and share disaggregated population data (e.g., sex, age, disability, legal status, socio-economic) that may help refine the common analysis, mindful of the need to protect sensitive data appropriately. Additionally, iMMAP’s information management and analysis efforts systematically seek and promote the inclusion of specific information relevant to population groups deemed extra vulnerable, e.g., discriminated groups, children, elderly, and persons with disabilities, with the aim that such groups benefit equitably from the collective response. iMMAP includes in its strategies the following guidelines for the inclusion of extra-vulnerable population groups:1. Encouraging and supporting governmental bodies and organizations representing people with disabilities or older people to identify those who are not accessing services. 2. Identifying barriers and potential solutions to discrimination or exclusion by applying a gender analysis. 3. Supporting agencies and governmental bodies in ensuring people with disabilities and older people participate directly in needs assessments, consultations, and feedback mechanisms to inform programming. 4. Utilizing appropriate communication methods in engagement activities, for example, the use of pictures or photos, audio, large print, visual demonstrations, face-to-face explanations, clear/slower speech, and simple language. iMMAP’s capacity-building activities promotes protection mainstreaming, equity and inclusion as key concepts of context and response analysis. While iMMAP does not explicitly target organizations that focus exclusively on specific vulnerable groups, all stakeholders involved in the humanitarian response are encouraged to participate in the available training workshops.
