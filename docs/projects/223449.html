Project Summary

The 2024 Nigerian Humanitarian Needs Overview projects that 7.9 million people (including 2.3 million internally displaced persons (IDPs)) will require urgent humanitarian and protection assistance. Alarmingly, nearly 5.4 million children aged 0-59 months are currently suffering—and are projected to continue suffering—from acute malnutrition through 2025. Among these, 1.8 million children are affected by Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM), as indicated by the weight-for-height z-score (WHZ). The highest GAM prevalence was recorded in Borno (15.7%), followed by Yobe (11.9%) and Adamawa (6.6%), as per the NFSS Round 14 report.
In Yobe State, Northern Yobe recorded the highest GAM prevalence at 14.9%, with LGAs such as Karasuwa, Machina, Nguru, Yunusari, and Yusufari particularly affected. Southern Yobe, with LGAs including Potiskum, Damaturu, Fika, Fune, Gubja, Gujba, Gulani, Nangare, and Tarmuwa, reported a GAM prevalence of 11.4%.
Historical data from the 2024 Population in Need (PiN) assessment further highlights the severe nutritional burden among children in Yusufari LGA, Yobe State:
• Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) cases requiring Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP):
o 146 IDPs, 138 returnees, and 19,626 host community members.
• Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) (children aged 6-59 months):
o 121 IDPs, 115 returnees, and 16,330 host community members.
• MAM among Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW):
o 9 IDPs, 8 returnees, and 1,162 host community members.
• Micronutrient Powder (MNP), Small Quantity Lipid-based Nutrient Supplements (SQ-LNS), and Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) needs:
o 195 IDPs, 185 returnees, and 26,195 host community members.
To address these alarming nutrition challenges, this proposed intervention builds on the Jireh Doo Foundation's (JDF) past achievements in improving nutritional outcomes within project locations. This project aligns with the objectives of the National Multi-Sectoral Plan of Action for Food and Nutrition (NMPFAN) 2021–2025, targeting a reduction in malnutrition prevalence across Nigeria.
The primary beneficiaries for this nutrition-integrated project include 65,720 women, men, boys, and girls, with priority given to pregnant and lactating women (PLW), children aged 0–59 months, adolescents, persons with disabilities, and the elderly—particularly those affected by conflict and living in vulnerable communities. Beneficiaries are categorized as follows:
• Total Direct Beneficiaries: 65,720
o Women: 11,931
o Men: 5,000
o Boys: 20,000
o Girls: 23,120
o Elderly Men: 1,500
o Elderly Women: 1,500
• Total Indirect Beneficiaries: 105,822
The project is designed to address the critical nutritional needs of vulnerable groups, including children under five, adolescents, and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs). Jireh Doo Foundation (JDF) will adopt a community-based, participatory approach, ensuring all stakeholders understand project goals, implementation strategies, and beneficiary entitlements.
The urgency is heightened by rising malnutrition rates, with a 23% increase in children requiring treatment and a 69% rise in severe acute malnutrition (SAM) cases. Additionally, 787,000 PLWs now require immediate nutritional support, a 35% increase since 2023.
The project aims to reduce malnutrition prevalence, strengthen community-based nutritional practices through education and interventions, and enhance community resilience, particularly in conflict-affected areas. By addressing acute and chronic malnutrition, this initiative consolidates JDF’s efforts in Yobe State and aligns with Nigeria’s National Multi-Sectoral Plan of Action for Food and Nutrition (2021–2025).

2 - Activities

1. Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM):o Implementation of Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTPs) in strategic locations across the 10 wards of the target LGAs.o Integration of mobile clinics and community outreach programs to serve hard-to-reach areas with limited access to fixed OTP sites or health facilities.o Admission of children with SAM into OTPs, with referrals of complicated cases to stabilization centers (SC).2. Active Case Finding and Screening:o Deployment of trained community nutrition mobilizers (CNMs) living in the wards to identify children under five with SAM or MAM using MUAC screening.o Introduction of the Mother/Family MUAC strategy to empower caregivers to screen children at home for timely identification and admission.3. Treatment and Supplementation:o Provision of Ready-to-Use Supplementary Food (RUSF) and Micronutrient Powders (MNP) to children with MAM to prevent relapse into SAM.o Treatment of opportunistic infections such as diarrhea and malaria to improve recovery outcomes.o Support for Vitamin A supplementation and zinc fortification to improve immunity and overall nutrition.4. Maternal, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN):o Establishment of breastfeeding corners in OTPs to promote exclusive breastfeeding practices.o Conducting Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) sessions targeting pregnant and lactating women (PLWs).o Health sensitization campaigns and nutrition talks within communities to foster behavior change and promote healthy feeding practices.o Formation of mother-to-mother and father-to-father support groups and food demonstrations for sustained positive health outcomes.5. Capacity Building and System Strengthening:o Training of health workers and community mobilizers on CMAM, MIYCN, and Family MUAC strategies to ensure maximum quality and timely service delivery.o Strengthening of referral systems to enhance coordination and follow-up of complicated cases.6. Collaboration with State Partners:o Close collaboration with the Yobe State Primary Health Care Development Agency (SPHCDA) to align efforts with state health programs and ensure sustainable impacts.Rationale for Activities• The deployment of community nutrition mobilizers within wards reduces the burden on caregivers traveling to distant health centers, ensuring equitable access to services.• The mobile clinics and outreach programs allow for better coverage of underserved and hard-to-reach areas, addressing gaps in access caused by difficult terrain.• The MUAC screening strategy, including the Mother/Family MUAC approach, ensures early detection and admission, preventing the worsening of malnutrition cases.• Behavior change interventions through sensitization and support groups foster long-term improvements in nutrition and health practices.• The capacity-building component ensures a sustainable impact, equipping local health workers and CNMs with the skills needed to continue service delivery beyond the project period.Target BeneficiariesThe program will reach the following groups across IDP, returnee, and host communities:• IDPs: 162 girls, 150 boys, and 150 women.• Returnees: 150 girls, 120 boys, and 168 women.• Host Community Members: 18,000 girls, 16,000 boys, and 28,150 women.This holistic approach integrates prevention, treatment, and capacity-building measures to effectively reduce malnutrition rates and improve health outcomes, contributing to the National Multi-Sectoral Plan of Action for Food and Nutrition (2021–2025).

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project will integrate protection mainstreaming through activities designed to promote gender equity, community inclusiveness, and long-term sustainability: Formation of Women/Mother Support Groups: These groups will provide a platform for women to exchange ideas, share experiences, and support each other in improving family and child nutrition. They will also help address gender barriers by empowering women with knowledge and practical skills to manage their families’ nutritional needs.Breastfeeding Corners: Establishing breastfeeding-friendly spaces in health facilities will encourage pregnant and lactating women to practice exclusive breastfeeding, promoting healthier outcomes for their infants while creating a safe and supportive environment for women.Awareness and Inclusion of Men: Awareness campaigns targeting men will be conducted through focus group discussions, town hall meetings, home visits, and support groups. These initiatives will emphasize the importance of exclusive breastfeeding, nutritious meal preparation, and men’s supportive role in improving household nutrition.Community Education on Local Food Solutions: Leveraging JDF’s decade-long experience in managing Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) using locally fortified supplementary foods, the project will teach community members how to prepare nutritious meals using locally available ingredients. This practical knowledge will ensure the sustainability of nutritional improvements and reduce dependency on external resources.These measures will ensure that all stakeholders—women, men, and community leaders—are engaged in fostering positive, sustainable health outcomes while addressing equity and protection concerns in the targeted communities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure effective feedback collection from the most vulnerable community members, Jireh Doo Foundation (JDF) will implement the Community-Based Accountability and Feedback System (CoBAFS). This system is designed to prioritize inclusivity, transparency, and accessibility while fostering trust and accountability. The key components are as follows: CoBAFS Reporting Library: A physical project library, placed in a safe and accessible location within the community (e.g., Bulama, Lawan), will house JDF's project documents, including a large-print implementation work plan and monthly reports.Reports will be converted into pictorial animations and charts in local languages, with support from Translators Without Borders, making them easily understandable by all, including children and persons with disabilities (PWDs). These animated reports will also be distributed house-to-house to ensure accessibility for vulnerable individuals.Community-Based Accountability Committee (CoBAC): A feedback committee will oversee the accountability board, which displays deliverables and timelines in large print behind a glass enclosure for community visibility.CoBAC will organize monthly feedback and quality implementation review meetings, engaging community stakeholders, affected persons, sector representatives, and government officials.This approach ensures that vulnerable groups, including those with limited education, disabilities, or other barriers, can actively participate in feedback and monitoring processes. It also builds trust through upward and downward accountability while aligning with JDF’s commitment to Accountability to Affected Persons (AAP) as part of its CHS Alliance and AAP/CEAL Working Group membership.
