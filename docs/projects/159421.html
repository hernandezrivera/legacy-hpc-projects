Project Summary

The project aims at securing sufficient and safe drinking water quantities for 345 households (2,377 individuals) of the targeted communities and families living in Area “C” of the West Bank for household purposes, including 1,134 women and girls.
The initiative responds to needs highlighted in the 2019 HNO and it is also fully in line with the oPt. Response plan, contributing to the WASH Cluster Objective 1: ensuring equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable communities. Access to safe WASH service is a main concern in the West Bank, specifically In Area C, where around 95,000 people receive less than 50 liters of water per capita per day, while over 83,000 people receive bad quality drinking water or are unconnected to a water network.
The initiative has been endorsed by PWA, it is in line with the PWA Strategic Plan (2017-22) and in compliance with the Water Master Plan for Area C. The action is in line with the Sustainable Development Goals, specifically contributing with “Goal 6: Ensure access to water and sanitation for all”.
Further, the initiative allows to reduce and more effectively address the core vulnerabilities of people in need, increasing water access for unconnected and underserved communities in area “C” of the West Bank, precisely in Ras al Tin and Wadi As Seeq in Ramallah Gov., AL Farisiya, Al Hadidiya, Hammamat al Maleh, Humsa, Khirbet Samra and Mak-hul in Tubas Gov. and in Turat al Mujdarin (al A'uja), in Jericho & Al-Aghwar Governorate.

1 - Needs

PHG conducted a thorough needs assessment in March 2018 in all West Bank. The assessment covered 115 vulnerable communities in Area C in addition to 2017 need assessment covered 130 vulnerable communities in addition to the ongoing 2019 WASH assessment for 62 communities in Nablus and Tubas Governorates. It was based on; field visits, interviews and questionnaires with potential beneficiaries including Bedouin communities, local authorities and community based organizations including women CBO’s. The assessment was completed in coordination with the PWA and other water related parties. Around 180 women and girls have been included and interviewed within the two assessments. The targeted communities have been selected considering people highly affected by water access (under served and/or un served communities) and affected by forcible transfer. They are those more affected by the impact of drought events, increase of water costs during the dry period and lack of access to water. The targeted households are those who suffer from water shortage and cutoff. Many are not connected to a water network. In some locations people are using primary plastic pipes to deliver water to houses. In addition, part of the proposed humanitarian needs have been inspired and modeled based on previous studies and water master plans such as water master plan for Area C (2017-2035) completed by ACF and GVC in full coordination with PWA. Therefore, it was clear from PHG assessments carried with the local authorities and community based organizations including women CBO’s that approximately 345 households/ families (2,377 individuals – 1,134 females and 1,243 males) are in need of the below humanitarian needs in the West Bank, Below are some of the assessment highlights: - Communities in the northern Jordan Valley (Tubas and western Ramallah Bedouins) are buying water tankers from the surrounding water springs and filling points. On average water cost is as high as 20-30 NIS/m3 and they are using open steel barrels to store water for daily use. This practice exposes the water to contamination. Proposed activity to address the high costs is supplying and installing plastic water tanks of 1.5 m3 capacity. - The available water networks are in need of rehabilitation in Turat al Mujdarin (al A'uja) inJericho. - Around 2,067 inhabitants located in Area “C” are unable to access enough water, the use level is well below humanitarian standards (35-55 liters depending on locality). It is therefore suggested to provide them with plastic water tanks of 5m3 capacity as well to provide them with water quantities through mobile tankers to increase their access to water. - A number Bedouins and herders’ communities are not connected to water networks or they are using primary plastic pipes to get water to houses. They are mainly dependent on mobile water tankers to fulfill their needs of water for domestic uses and for livestock. The average water price by water tinkering is 25-30 NIS/m3, water tankers are filled from the sounding filling points existing in the neighboring localities. Suggested intervention to improve water availability and reduce the high cost. All interventions and activities will target communities either fully or partially located in area C and East Jerusalem, while excluding others with less pronounced protection concerns. The targeted households suffer from continuous marginalization, water shortage and cutoff. Many are not connected to a water network. In some locations people are using primary plastic pipes to deliver water to houses. It is worthwhile noting that some interventions are in area B. However their main aim is to serve communities totally or partially located in Area “C”. This is particularly valid for the following activities: mobile water trucks, water networks construction and rehabilitation.

2 - Activities

All project activities and interventions have been reviewed, evaluated and coordinated with the below parties: - National and international NGOs active in the geographic area - PWA and WBWD - Local water providers (regarding water distribution activity) As to water distribution, PHG agreed to use same procedures and mechanism in line with the PWA/ WASH cluster modality, in case of any activities overlapping at community level PHG is committed to coordinate this issue over families level and beneficiaries with other organization in field. Major activities or outputs of the project are: O1: access to basic water services is improved. A1.1 Supplying and installation of 65 plastic water storage tanks of 5,000 liters for the benefit of 116 households (371 females and 410 males). The water storage tanks localities are distributed as follow: 14 in Al Hadidiya, 21 in Humsa in Tubas Gov., 20 in Ras al Tin, 10 in Wadi as Seeq in Ramallah Gov., tanks suppling and installation will be completed by local contractors and suppliers with the coordination of the project committee and community based organizations. A1.2 To install 1.5 km of 2” Water Supply Pipesline to connect Turat al Mujdarin (al-Auja) in Jericho with water from Al Auja for the benefit of 40 households (152 female and 158 Male). A1.3 Distributing 7,720 cubic meters of water by mobile tanks for the benefit of 189 families (611 females and 675 males). The water distribution locations are: 540 m3 in Al Hadidiya, 1,410 m3 in Al Farisiya, 1,200 m3 in Hammamat al Maleh, 920 m3 in Humsa, 240 m3 in Khirbet Samra, 210 m3 in Mak-hul in Tubas Gov., 1,670 m3 in Ras al Tin and 1,530 m3 in Wadi as Seeq in Ramallah Gov. A1.4 Conducting 25 workshops for the benefit of 625 persons in WASH awareness and promotion campaigns to cover all topics related to water handling, water management and saving in addition to training about facilities operation and maintenance as well as to include hygiene promotion topics of the above mentioned interventions., such WASH awareness will enhance water right of marginalized people protected and focused on gender equality and women ’s empowerment in household water management and water sanitation. The other household members (children, youth, men, disabled persons, and elders) have improved their water consumption and sanitation practices due to the increased amount of available water at their houses . PHG is committed to consult women in each targeted household on operational details (e.g. precise location of storage tanks), considering that they will be the primary beneficiaries of interventions. Female PHG staff will be part of the implementation team enabling an open environment for female beneficiaries to voice their concerns. In general women and girls will be affected positively after improving the WASH conditions and facilities on the household level and this will be reflected on reducing daily chores, more time allocated to girls’ education, and improved feminine hygiene. Appropriate WASH facilities should also result in reduced conflict in the household, improving quality of life, and significant decline in health problems (especially for children), time and money saving by the family allowing for greater focus on children's education. Women are more vulnerable to lack of access to water. In the same time inadequate availability of water, collecting water from unprotected sources will expose women, children, and old people in particular to high risks of waterborne diseases and other illnesses. Thus, the project will contribute to secure more clean water and will give more weights to female headed households in beneficiary selection process.

8.6.b - If 'Other', please specify

Community engagement at all levels of the project is essential and falls within the policy and vision of PHG as well PHG is keen to involve and engage the community of all age groups, males and females, starting from the planning and design phase of the project through communication with local authorities, civil society institutions and beneficiaries to discuss the needs and challenges in the field of water, sanitation and public health by collecting information, statistics and evidences which have already been done during the design phase of the proposal, and will involve the community in all categories during the implementation phase of the project through the local project committee, the project committee will include representatives from the community , women, municipality, local committees, the role of the committee is to facilitate work of the project staff in the field particularly to solve any conflict if raised through implementation period , the women whom representing CBOs and community will be active and expected to contribute equally in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project side by side with the committee members, they will be given a significant role in implementation from the beginning. In addition, community engagement and feedback will be strengthen through participation in awareness-raising, training and capacity-building workshops by different ages including both males and females.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

PHG aim with all of its projects is to ‘Do-No-Harm’ and to maximize positive impacts. In conjunction with this policy, PHG has ensured these practices by focusing on female heads of household, empowering the most vulnerable of families. By doing community outreach, PHG is able to help normalize female participation in public spaces. By providing home visits involving female evaluators, women can feel more safe in their own homes.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The approach of implementation will starts by formulating a project committee that consists of 5-7 person , which will include representatives from the community , women, municipality, local committees, the role of the committee is to assist in collecting contribution if any (local members) , also will facilitate work of the project staff in the fieldparticularly to solve any conflict if raised through implementation period , the women whom representing CBOs and community will be active and expected to contribute equally in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project side by side with the committee members, they will be given a significant role in implementation from the beginning .During the awareness sessions PHG team will ensure that health and hygiene will not be considered as a “women issues”. In this way, men and boys will be encouraged to be actually engaged in their promotion, thus contributing to the improvement of the health and hygiene in their households and communities. The awareness sessions will promote equal knowledge and participation between female and male of good hygiene practices and WASH management in the household. protection approach will be followed throughout the whole implementation phase to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level. PHG staff and project committee role throughout the whole project will that that of guaranteeing compliance and respect of safety and privacy of eachindividual, especially for the most vulnerable groups (female farmers, female-headed households, households with people with disabilities).Beneficiaries will be consulted from the project onset regarding their preferred type of assistance and will be invited to participate in fully empowered committees. Awareness raising: in order to maximize the project’s impact, beneficiaries will be informed and sensitized by PHG staff and project committee on the prevention of major health related risks and best hygiene practice
