4 - Needs

Primary HealthCare:According to the last HeRAM report, there are 76 Primary Health Care facilities operating in Idleb and 77 in Aleppo. The number of facilities not functioning increased compared to the previous monitoring period of the same year, showing a decreased trend of funding for the health establishments. Furthermore, the gap in medical staff (doctors-nurses) for Azaz district is 75%, Idleb 44% and for Ariha district of 63% and of 84%, 78% and 95% in terms of health workers. Operation of a comprehensive trauma center;According to HNO 2019, the health sector’s current capacity to provide supplies for trauma and burns, as well as surgery and postoperative rehabilitation care is insufficient to meet the existing need across the country. Referral of such cases to the small number of hospitals offering specialized services is a significant burden on the health system and on the ability to provide predictable quality services. Indeed, According to the last available HeRAMS report, only 43 hospitals are operating in Idleb and 20 in Aleppo for an estimated population of 4.2 million, and an average of 130,327 new and follow-up trauma cases was treated in health facilities supported by humanitarian partners in 2019. An assessment in parts of northern Syria showed that an average of 45 percent of persons injured during the Syria crisis is expected to sustain a permanent impairment.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Gender-Based Violence GBV: - Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention. Accordingly, affected population should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package. - Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. - Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor. - The pres of female staff in health facilities increases access of women and girls to health services. 7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Unforeseen changes in the context: Conflict is such that the organization cannot safely access locations, staff gets besieged in one area of control or operational assets are lost. -Management and mitigation of this risk: -Continue to gather information from a range of sources. -Develop a contingency plan to work in an alternative location if the conflict continues. -Continuous efforts by the access teams to gain new assurances and connections to secure humanitarian space. 2-Detention / Arrest of the staff: This has been one of the most reoccurring security incidents in Syria since 2011 in the regime, and opposition areas. Detentions can be for any reason or duration. All must be managed on a case-by-case basis. Detention can be both of individuals and of materials. The mistreatment of detainees is a real threat. - Management and mitigation of this risk: Staff tracking and daily check-ins. -Actor mapping and connections established in all areas. -Staff training on visibility and communication practices. 3-Loss of acceptance or registration by Turkish authorities in the destination area: Beginning in the fall of 2014, Turkish governmental relations are good and increasing, for the work in Turkey and in northwest Syria areas controlled by Turkey. Management and mitigation of this risk: -Continue generating options: local procurement, stock-piling inside certain areas in Syria and seeking alternate border possibilities.
