Project Summary

This proposed project is intended to deliver a mix of static primary health care and outreach services to increase and expand equitable coverage and access, especially for hard to reach populations and is expected to improve access to low-cost, high-impact health services to the communities in Kajo keji, morobo, lainya and yei Counties, and generally improve the health of the population in the area.
The project will complement ongoing interventions by South Sudan Health Association(SSUHA) supporting health service provision and responding to the effects of floods and COVID 19 pandemic. The proposed project will be implemented through the already existing Government -MoH systems to enhance sustainability (durable solutions) through strengthening of the health system to offer quality health services. This, combined with emergency preparedness and response, will ensure service continuity and will play a crucial role in achieving the Health Sector Development Plan’s overall objective of “increasing access, utilization and quality of health and nutrition services, with emphasis on maternal and child health and with attention to effectiveness, efficiency, and equity” (Ministry of Health National Health Policy, 2016-2026). Proposed interventions will directly contribute to Cluster objectives 1, 2, and 3.
South Sudan Health Association will seek to reduce excess morbidity and mortality as a result of climatic shocks and conflicts. Proposed activities will seek to contribute to improvement in the physical and mental wellbeing of the population affected by conflict and displacement including people with disabilities. It further, seeks to improve equitable access to quality emergency and essential lifesaving health services for the crisis-affected population. Static facilities and mobile outreach teams will be set up and strengthened through the provision of medical supplies and the improvement of staff capacity. Remote villages will be reached through mobile clinics; community mobilizers and Community Health Workers (CHWs) will be recruited and trained. Among the activities that SSUHA will conduct includes; supplemental vaccination campaign to under 5 children (boys and girls) and Women of childbearing age (WCBA); Provision of antenatal and post-natal care to pregnant and lactating mothers, strengthening physical and mental healthcare system. Treatment and control of communicable diseases targeting boys, girls, PLW, WCBA, and men; Strengthening of a referral system between Primary Healthcare facilities, the community (remote villages) and the regional hospitals; Establishment of mobile clinics; Creating Disease Surveillance and Response plan; Training of health workers on clinical management of raped survivors, IMCI, Basic Emergency Obstetric Care and disease surveillance and response. Training will be conducted for community health workers in integrated community case management on communicable diseases such as pneumonia, diarrhea, and malaria.

1 - Needs

The weakened health system compounded by multiple shocks will impact the health and wellbeing of an estimated 4.9 million South Sudanese in 2021 (HNO 2021). Only 7.5% of the 1,763 reporting health facilities provide basic health services (Ministry of Health and WHO health facility functionality September 2020 ). Specialized mental health services are scarce and limited to a few urban areas like Juba and the disruption in accessing medication and treatment aggravated the already poor health condition of people with mental disorders. OCHA January report indicates that more than 600,000 people are in need of health support, the immediate needs which includes health awareness to prevent water-borne diseases, prepositioning of medical supplies in readiness to increased needs of flood affected population and strengthening of health outreach services. WHO reports a rise in COVID 19 cases with a cumulative of 5,922 and 79 deaths as of February 18th 2021.Many health facilities still remains shut down or not fully functional even when the current peace agreement shows some glimpse of stability, due to lack of funds increasing the risk of outbreaks due to lack of healthcare services and health surveillance (UNICEF Social Assessment Report Sept 2020). Kajokeji County has only 11 functional health facilities of which 4 are supported by health partners, while the other 27 healthcare centers have been vandalized and remain nonfunctional. Lainya with a total of 23 health facilities only 10 are functional and supported under the current HPF funding, Yei has 39 health facilities and only 10 are fully supported by the current HPF project. Of the 14 facilities that were active in Morobo County, only 4 remains functional; the other 10 have been destroyed and non functional, roads are impassible during the rainy season, cutting off large areas from the rest of the country. These counties, with an estimated population of 343,400 (approximately 13,736 children under five years of age and 78,982 pregnant and lactating women) are in great need of increased access to healthcare services. For instance, many people living far away from health facilities must walk for several hours to access medical services. For this reason, the population is more likely to try and solve health problems through traditional medicine, which usually causes further complications. The proposed project is intended to deliver a mix of static primary health care and mobile/outreach services to increase and expand equitable coverage and access, especially for hard to reach populations and is expected to improve access to low-cost, high-impact health services to the communities in Kajo keji, morobo, lainya and yei Counties, and generally improve the health of the population in the area. The project will complement ongoing interventions by South Sudan Health Association(SSUHA) supporting health service provision and responding to the effects of floods and COVID 19 pandemic. The proposed project will be implemented through the already existing Government -MoH systems to enhance sustainability (durable solutions) through the strengthening of the health system and capacity building to staffs to offer quality health services. This, combined with emergency preparedness and response, will ensure service continuity and will play a crucial role in achieving the Health Sector Development Plan’s overall objective of “increasing access, utilization and quality of health and nutrition services, with emphasis on maternal and child health and with attention to effectiveness, efficiency, and equity” (Ministry of Health National Health Policy, 2016-2026)

2 - Project activities

South Sudan Health Association will provide life saving health interventions which are in line with the South Sudan Ministry of Health National Health Policy 2016-2026 which conforms with the cluster objectives. The outreach/mobile clinic will improve the quality of life by providing access to health services in remote and conflict affected hard to reach areas particularly access to maternal child health, general consultation, screening and information on COVID 19 prevention and treatment of diseases. The main activities will include; prevention, control and treatment of communicable and non communicable diseases including epidemic-prone diseases in 25 static health facilities and 22 outreach/mobile sites.• Support reporting for communicable disease surveillance and Response Early Warning and Response Network including COVID 19 •Capacity building for health facility staffs on Essential package of Health Services (EPHS) core services including disease surveillance, infection prevention and control, clinical management of rape and emergency obstetric care •Provision of integrated primary health care services through medical screening, consultations and referral of critical cases both at outreach and fixed health facilities. • Providing adequate environment for urgent medical consultations including timely treatment of survivors of GBV and delivery of regular primary health care services for returnees, displaced as well as host communities. •Providing early detection, diagnosis, and treatment of emergency and/or life threatening injuries and other health conditions such as diarrhea, fever, respiratory infections, malnutrition among others. • Support national vaccination campaigns in addition to routine immunization services for under fives, and pregnant and lactating mothers at 25 health facilities and 22 outreach/mobile sites and facilitate access and utilization of the immunization services. •Provide risk communications and community engagement for COVID 19 through a network of Boma Health workers and community committees • Focus on the special needs of especially vulnerable groups (children, pregnant or post partum women, newborns, the elderly, mental illnesses and the critically injured). • Facilitate medical referrals including survivors of GBV and transfer of patients through ambulance to secondary or tertiary health facility as needed. • Procurement of essential drugs and other pharmaceutical equipment to support the project •Conduct 22 monthly outreach/mobile clinics services in targeted locations in Kajo keji, Lainya, Morobo and Yei counties to increase access and utilization of health services by community members. • Provide focused antenatal care and postnatal checkups to attending mothers during outreach and in static health facilities. • Conduct normal deliveries in all the 9 PHCCs and refer complications for comprehensive maternal health services in the nearest hospital. • Provide family planning services and awareness raising in all the established facilities. • Provision of equitable access to to ANC services, skilled facility delivery and post natal care services at 25 static and 22 mobile sites • Provide Basic management of survivors of gender based violence (PEP, CMR, treatment for STI, wound care etc) • Provide training to 30 health workers on Psychological Support and Psychological First Aid for people affected by the conflict (Children, women, and people of special needs). • Conduct RRT training for 50 health workers on integrated Health, WASH, Protection and nutrition response. • Train 30 health workers including county health department on emergency preparedness and response.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Protection will be mainstreamed through prioritizing safety and dignity and avoiding causing harm by delivering services and assistance in ways that preserve the physical integrity of individuals and communities are culturally appropriate and avoid any unintended negative consequences. Ensuring equitable access according to needs and not based on age, sex, gender, nationality, race, ethnic allegiance. Services and assistance will be provided in an adequate balance, within safe and easy reach to all including People Living with Disabilities, and those with mental disorders. SSUHA will ensure zero tolerance on sexual exploitation and abuse and all project staff are expected to sign the organization’s Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policy before commencement of work. To ensure ethical compliance in implementation, project staff will also sign the SSUHA staff code of conduct, child-safeguarding policy in addition to other organizational policy documents. The project will take into consideration inclusive guidelines without regard to disability, gender, social/economic status, and ethnicity to ensure all members of the affected communities benefit from our activities. Implementation of the project will also involve the participation of a proportional number of qualified women (50%-50% of the total workforce) to challenge the notion of discriminating against women in the work place.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SSUHA will include persons with disabilities, women and girls as well as boys and men to ensure that their unique needs are covered. Age sex, and vulnerability criteria disaggregated data will be used to track inclusion, promote accountability and reduce exclusion of those who qualify for assistance. SSUHA will identify barriers to accessing assistance and services for women, men, boys, girls and persons with disabilities including individuals who are not literate and will design interventions that are contextually and culturally appropriate to their needs. SSUHA will ensure that specific groups are considered e.g PLWs, persons living with disabilities, unaccompanied minors, the elderly etc with relevant mitigation measures to prevent risks and harm. The project will incorporate measures to proactively include persons with disabilities and elderly including individuals who are not literate in the program design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Beneficiaries' safety and security, particularly women and girls, persons with disabilities including individuals who are not literate will be regularly monitored through feedback and accountability mechanisms and field visit reports. SSUHA will ensure that facilities working hours and setting do not pose risk for vulnerable groups. SSUHA will strengthen its feedback and accountability measures to ensure that beneficiaries are aware of how to report and will strengthen the management systems to respond to such complaints if they occur. Feed back mechanisms will include; toll free hotline numbers, use of GBV focal points to identify and report cases of assaults' and regular community sessions with relevant groups

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Implementing feedback mechanism is a standard procedure in SSUHA projects as one of our systems to monitor quality, promote participation and improve accountability. We use different tools adapted to the language, context and culture to avoid barriers preventing the usage and facilitate the access. We also follow strict confidentiality protocols in order to assure the privacy of the informant and the information is processed by the correct staff. Actions to inform about the feed back mechanism existence are taken. Before implementing the system, the pertinent committee is trained on how to compile, share and process the information and afterwards lead/follow up actions of response. The information compiled is a valuable resource to monitor the project, better adapt it to the needs of the population, improve current and future intervention and open a fundamental participation channel with the population.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Accountability to the Populations will be emphasized and implemented to ensure affected individuals and communities play an active role in the measurement of the quality of interventions, and ensuring transparency throughout the project development and implementation while encouraging Inter-agency feedback and complaints mechanisms as part of an integrated M&E process. SSUHA already has a Robust feedback mechanism which is regularly employed to get feedback from beneficiaries and other stakeholder using a variety of methods including: Live chat, Emails, Telephone calls, media, written documents, surveys, FGDs and meeting with community leaders and other project beneficiaries.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

South Sudan Health Association (SSUHA) will provide refresher training to all staff involved in project implementation on preventive measures against sexual exploitation and abuse/protection mainstreaming and ensure that they sign the code of conduct on physical sexual exploitation and abuse as a commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients. SSUHA has a safeguarding policy in place to ensure continued sensitization, awareness and follow up on sexual exploitation and abuse of the beneficiaries. Boma Health Workers, community volunteers and health/nutrition workers will strive to identify passive cases in the community and report using the existing reporting mechanism and also continue dissemination of behavior change communication key messages on sexual violence and exploitation. SSUHA shall carry out in-depth research and analysis to identify the different risk factors for the project and strategies to address these risks. Research suggests “the most effective strategies are the result of indigenous movements that advocate for the overall well-being of women and girls and target all members of the community. SSUHA will continue to work closely with the local government structures, community leaders and reinforce good relationship on the ground to ensure ownership of the project, SSUHA will maintain contact with UNOCHA and South Sudan Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (SSRRC) coordinators to discuss and mitigate issues like forced taxation of humanitarian assistance, recruitment etc that may arise and affect the project implementation. SSUHA will also maintain active participation in the United Nations Department of Safety and Security briefings where project staffs will be informed of areas with UXOs like landmines, unexploded bombs etc. These areas will be avoided until when its declared safe by UNDSS for project activities. SSUHA will also conduct integrated mobile outreach to take services nearer to the communities with no health facility structures, this will reduce the distance patients have to move to access health services in the nearest health facility, also reduces the risk of exposure to violence as a result of traveling long distance on the road and in areas which are bushy, this is especially risky for women and girls.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The intervention will develop a functional referral pathways which will be shared with all the partners to ensure that GBV survivors receive comprehensive multi-sectoral services. SSUHA will use four main approaches that will guide the project implementation as described below; • Partnership: With this strategy, the emphasis will be on building partnership with and between different sectors (health, social, security and legal) and working with communities including men and boys to sensitize communities on seeking timely GBV services. • System strengthening: SSUHA will put more focus on system strengthening health, police and legal institutions and supporting closer collaborations between them. • Skills building and development: Within this strategy, comprehensive training of service providers, eg health workers, lawyers, police, teachers, community leaders etc is envisioned particularly to build and strengthen a multi-sectoral response for GBV survivors at the community level. The survivors will be referred for survivor skills development to empower them economically to support their families and contribute to household income eg, horticulture (farming), tailoring, bead making, bee keeping etc. • Service delivery: the emphasis shall be on GBV screening, treatment, psychosocial support, counseling and legal aid. SSUHA will use the above approach which integrates international human rights and humanitarian law norms, standards and principles into plans, policies, services and process of humanitarian intervention and development related to violence against women both at national and local/community levels aimed at putting in place and reinforcing both responsive and preventive mechanisms essential to promoting and protecting the rights and welfare of women.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

SSUHA's interventions will coordinate and collaborate with other sectors like nutrition and food security and livelihood sectors and will participate in needs assessments and actively share humanitarian situation reports including IDSR with the nutrition and health clusters for prompt planning and actions.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

SSUHA has a gender and social inclusion policy in place and will ensure that everyone (women, children, elderly, persons with mental health and persons with disabilities) are included in the project activities and delivery. SSUHA will also closely coordinate and collaborate with other partners/agencies such as those supporting persons living with disabilities to support the needs of persons with disabilities. SSUHA will also ensure that the project is implemented in a gender sensitive and culturally sensitive way with a DO NO HARM approach

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

SSUHA will ensure that the nutritional status of all the pregnant mothers and children under the age of five years who report for services in all the supported facilities and mobile outreach sites are assessed using the MUAC and growth monitoring tools while applying the COVID 19 adaptations to reduce the risk of transmissions and also will ensure that vitamin A supplementation, deworming and immunization are provided to all the children who are due. The Boma Health Workers (BHWs) including the CNVs will continue to conduct community screening of both the children under the age of five years and pregnant and lactating mothers and refer to the nearest outpatient therapeutic centres for admission. The project will also provide infant and young child feeding education to attending patients both at OPD in the fixed facilities and in mobile sites. SSUHA will also conduct and participate in the joint nutrition assessments that will be conducted every six months.
