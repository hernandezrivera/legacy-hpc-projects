1 - Needs

In its eleventh years, the crisis in the North-East Nigeria remains one of the most severe protracted crises in the world . In the most severely affected Borno state and two adjacent states, a population of 1.9 million are displaced as internally displaced persons (IDPs). One in four of the affected population is under 5 years of age. Women and children are the face of the crisis, constituting 81% of the overall crisis population and 87% of the new displacements. The gendered dimensions of displacement are having a significant impact on the status of women and girls within the context of fragmentation of households, demoralization and rampant Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) . In addition, under spread of COVID-19, critical incidents and attacks continue and are even increasing, with 2,614 persons killed under the incidents or attacks in the three regions between the beginning of 2020 to 17 October 2020, while the total fatality there in 2019 was 2,221 . Despite the challenges, return of IDPs to the communities are ongoing in a number of locations. Communities therefore have an opportunity to transition from a humanitarian context, construct their livelihood systems, restoring access to public services, while strengthening their resilience for building and sustaining positive peace in the locations of return. This aligned with the Humanitarian Response Plan Nigeria 2021. One of the approaches emphasized in the plan is effective Humanitarian-Development-Peace (HDP) nexus. Through the approach, support for conflict-affected communities through the approach should be further extended.

2 - Activities

The activities will be done for 4 priority areas as follows: 1) Restoration of basic services through the repair and reconstruction of infrastructure essential for the pursuit of lives with diginity in communities that were devastated by conﬂict, and the strengthening of basic services provision. The restoration of basic services will be achieved through partnerships between the government and international actors for the provision of basic services. Priority needs will be conﬁrmed through consultations with communities, security groups, and local governments (both traditional and formal representatives). Key activities include: -Reconstruction/rehabilitation of housing and shelter for returnees - Construction/rehabilitation of key infrastructure (e.g., water, schools, clinic, markets) - Promotion of renewable technologies such as solar power for basic services and environment protection, such as waste management - Coordination of service provision and capacity building, as well as the provision of effective maintenance systems Priority Area 2) Livelihoods Stabilization infused with opportunities for immediate employment and income generation including, but not limited to, cash for work (CfW) programmes. Interventions will focus on both emergency income generation measures (e.g, CfW, agri-input provision) and sustainable job creation. Key activities include: - Cash for work for construction/waste management labour - Provision of crop production inputs including tools, seeds, and fertilizers – Support for micro and small businesses both in agri- and non-agricultural areas - Re-establishment of market structures and community saving schemes - Grants for women and youth led small and micro-enterprise recovery. -Vocational training for vulnerable people, especially female-headed households. Priority Area 3) Rebuilding social cohesion and peaceful coexistence among IDPs in Host Communities, Returnees, and Host communities through systematic social engagement and community security initiatives. Restoring community stability, social cohesion and trust is the most critical precondition to recovery and peace building, given the deep impact of the conﬂict. The programme will strengthen community cohesion through building the sense of shared responsibility for and ownership of the reconstruction process and well as inclusive dialogues among different population groups in the communities Key activities include: - Strengthen the existing community mobilization mechanisms, in particular with traditional conﬂict resolution mechanisms, and access to the formal legal chain - Strengthening community-level peace-building activities, including cultural activities, peace dialogues, joint communal activities and trust-building between the community members and security/police actors. – Conduct stakeholder dialogue on counter narratives on radicalization - Conduct sensitization for religious and traditional leaders on radicalization - Research and knowledge development for prevention of violent extremism (PVE) Priority Area 4) Reinstating effective and accountable local governance systems through, community participation and capacity building activities. The programme will aim at building capacity of the local government to support for effective coordination and monitoring of existing Recovery Plans and Programs and gradually assume the responsibility of basic services provision and managing recovery efforts. Key activities include: - Mapping and proﬁling of local government structures and communities in places of both displacement and origin - Establishment of community platforms to serve as a forum to discuss community needs, grievances and security; and building a bottom-up planning model - Capacity building for state and local governments on budget planning and execution, and monitoring and reporting - Development of a transparency and accountability model through community participation (social accountability mechanisms)

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The project will contribute to Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus, complementing and creating synergy with the humanitarian assistance ongoing in the target areas which will lead to the development and the peace building of the communities. Recovery of these communities will reduce the needs of humanitarian assistance such as food assistance and prevent recurrence of displacement which could eventually become basis for further peace building.
