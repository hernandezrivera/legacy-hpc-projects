Project Summary

FSNAU October to December 2020 report that moderate to heavy rains cause substantial flooding in low-lying areas along the Shabelle and Juba rivers, resulting in several deaths and significant damage to infrastructure, crops, property, and livestock, affecting more than a half million people across 17 districts in ten regions in the three states of Jubaland, Hirshabelle, and Southwest, with over 400,000 people displaced from their homes. Poverty and human rights violations in the COVID 19 context among the minority and general poor populations in Hiraan. High impunity against Disabled girls and women during this COVID-19 pandemic.
While COVID-19 is posing a risk for people of all ages and gender, however, pregnant women are at an increased risk of COVID-19 due to the physiological weakness during pregnancy and also due to the challenges faced while fulfilling the expected chores in rural community. Most of the communities, pregnant women and lactating mothers are struggling with psychological, social and economic problem.
COVID 19 has worsened the situation in Hiraan.The customary legal mechanisms are fraught with fundamental flaws being discriminatory towards women, minorities, IDPs, Returnees and youth; lack transparency and accountability; and are vulnerable to elite and political mafia capture. In the field of land law, it is also regularly questioned whether customary tenure systems provide farmers with sufficient security and clearly defined individual rights in land to encourage them to invest and attempt to increase productivity.
When human rights violations take place, in the absence of any properly functioning mechanisms for the rule of law, individuals from “minority” or weak clans often have little recourse to systems of justice – whether through customary law (xeer) or religious law (sharia). The subtleties of political, economic and social discrimination remain largely hidden to outsiders. Minorities such as Bantus are prone to theft of their land, rape, forced labor, and a range of discriminatory behavior.
Although individuals buy land privately, generally land belongs to the community and so are communal mechanisms in solving and mitigating the land related conflicts in concert with the local authorities. Bi-cultural separated Somali minors who are returned to their homeland under duress or through deception are in danger of harassment, extortion, rape and murder. Female Genital Mutilation is rampant just as gender based violence. Women and widows along with child-headed households face dispossession and disinheritance off their properties. Gender Inequality Index for Somalia is 0.776 (with a maximum of 1 denoting complete inequality; being fourth highest globally according to the UNDP 2010 report.
CEWI promotes to SDG Target 10.3 which clearly articulates the linkages between law, policy and practice and reducing inequality, calling on States to “ensure equal opportunity and reduce inequalities of outcome, including by eliminating discriminatory laws, policies and practices and promoting appropriate legislation, policies and action in this regard”.
intervention modalities:
coordinate with universities and colleges teaching law as well as Government judicial offices in enhancing harmonious realisation of the project objectives
in addressing the gender based injustices such as disinheritances and dispossession of properties.
We set up local village and District land adjucation and arbitration committees able to mitigate, prevent and resolve land injustices among the afflicted segments of the population. The Land Committees are concerned with property rights to land - the right to use the land, to manage the land, to generate an income from the land, to exclude others from the land, to transfer land and the right to compensation.
Set up Truth and Peace committees at village levels
pro-poor and vulnerable income generation

Mitigation Measures

CEWI defines 'Risk’ as the effect of uncertainty on objectives. This is influenced by insecurity and criminality, supplier / partner capacity and integrity, non -contractual compliance, Gender based violence and discrimination and Staff ability and capacity for project implementation.cognizant of the Coronavirus impact on humanitarian, human rights and development interventions consequently adhere to the risk mitigation frameworks under the DO NO HARM CONCEPT.Communicating and consulting with internal and external stakeholders as appropriate at each stage of the risk management process and concerning the process as a whole. Establishing the context – establish the external, internal and risk management context. Identifying and Analysing risks – identify where, when, why and how events could prevent, degrade, delay or enhance the achievement of the objectives. Evaluating and monitoring risks against the pre-established criteria and consider the balance between potential benefits and adverse outcomes. We coordinate with all stakeholders including Persons with Disabilities, landless, IDPs and Returnees.Additional risk mitigations• ensure accessibility through universal design and reasonable accommodation in order to ensure dignity. • raise awareness on the need to provide education without discrimination. • ensure that a variety of options are available to access the feedback and complaint mechanism (e.g., accept complaints in sign language with a trusted interpreter or with the assistance of a support person). • collection of both quantitative and qualitative data is crucial at all stages when it comes to identifying the specific risks affecting the minority clans, landless, Returnees, Women and persons with disabilities and the barriers they face.
