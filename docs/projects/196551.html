Project Summary

Children are generally vulnerable during the large-scale Syrian crisis, particularly those unaccompanied and separated or living with older or disabled caregivers. An increased vulnerability of families and households aggravates poverty and lack of access to essential services.
These deteriorating conditions force families to embrace coping strategies that are disproportionately harmful to children, such as child labor, early marriage, and school dropout.
In addition to the negative impact of deteriorating family conditions on children, there is a general lack of knowledge on child’s rights and how to secure them by caregivers, families, and community members. This can lead to child rights violations, unregistered children (that is, preventing children from having access to essential elementary services such as education), (physical, sexual or emotional) abuse, or homelessness. SOS believes that a better understanding of and awareness of child’s rights and how to react to cases of child rights violations is a crucial step to creating an environment that is respectful to the rights of these children.
The Project aims to reduce the risk of family separation by strengthening 240 families (960 children,240 Adults) to access their basic needs and raise awareness among their communities to fulfill and advocate for children's rights (22800 beneficiaries).
The project will invest in raising awareness, knowledge, skills, and support services regarding childcare, and welfare, such as parenting program training and counseling.
Furthermore, ensure the community’s active participation to prevent and respond to child protection issues, and collaborate with local associations and government entities to promote Child Protection activities.
The project will target children and young people at risk of losing parental care aged 0-17 years and their families. Special attention will be made to include children with disabilities, as they are more vulnerable to being kept out of school to domestic violence and abuse; disadvantaged households, including vulnerable groups, e.g., marginalized family-headed females, Community Actors, including community leaders, and local associations’ representatives.

3 - Needs

According to the 2023 Syria HNO sector chapter draft, more than 6 million children, including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse, and exploitation and need critical child protection services. The ongoing hostilities, protracted displacement, and limited access to basic services continue to significantly affect the physical, mental, and social well-being of children and caregivers.Insecurity and economic hardship exacerbate child protection concerns, fuelling harmful coping mechanisms. 85% of people live in communities where child labor is reported. 84% of people live in communities where child marriage is an issue for adolescent girls (15 -17 years old) and 36% of people live in communities where family separation is reported. Psychosocial distress among children is reported in 28% of surveyed households.Based on SOS Children’s Villages' need assessment for Alternative Care and Separation Prevention Services in Damascus and its Countryside 2022, in Rural Damascus, 25,568 children living in inadequate care and subjected to severe violations and 105,991 children are at risk of separation.SOS team identified the need for this intervention within 12 targeted areas that scored regarding severity as follows: Rural Damascus, sahnaya : 3, Qudsya: 3 kisweh : 3, Markaz Darayya: 3, Qatana:5 , and Jaramana: 4,Aleppo, Jebel Saman 4, Tall Ed-daman 4, Haritan 4, Zarbah 4, Maskana 4, Hadher 3.

4 - Implementation of activities

Through our network partners (local associations’ representatives) within the targeted areas, SOS will receive their support in selecting the most vulnerable families that match the project criteria.The project will focus on the following major activities,Provide families with 10 months cash assistance through a cash transferring company to secure their education,home rent,clothes ,and heating fuel, Provide family members with psychosocial support,support caregivers to improve their childcare & parenting skills, e.g. through workshops on child development & parenting skills;caregiver mentoring, Provide Awareness Building to community leaders and community members on Child Rights law and Protection issues,Ensure the community’s active participation to prevent and respond to child protection issues, and Collaborate with local associations,and government entities to promote Child Protection activities.The project's indicators will be monitored and evaluated, through SOS M&E adviser as well as it alignment with the overall processes of the SOS policy.This process will include monitoring field visits,surveys,staff meetings,participatory reviews,and participation through communication channels such as complaint hotline and regular follow-up visits.quarter-year review of family development plan,SOS standards monitoring tools and technics for data collection tools are Surveys, Questionnaires,Focus Group Discussion,Observation reports.Monthly 4WS report,Annual report, and BVA report.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Cash assistance may cause tension within the family, including risks of physical violence and disruption of the family dynamic ,Likelihood:Low,Impact:High, SOS team will establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities by consulting with actors on the ground that provide such protection services, and seek the Protection Sector support to increase the harmonization of targeting criteria, Follow Guidelines adopted by the protection actors and ensure that they are properly applied in all project locations, ensure a holistic family based approach where possible and proper consultation and communication, conduct family level analysis of impact of service and attempt provision of comparable service to all family members.Risk that existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles Likelihood:High,Impact:High, regular engagement with communities.Risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific individuals at the exclusion of other individuals.Likelihood:Low,Impact: High, SOS team will communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for, in addition to conduct regular training to staff for code of conduct and PSEA. Risk to physical safety of child protection service providers if perceived to be interfering in family-based concerns Likelihood: High,Impact: High, SOS team will coordinate with relevant authorities.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Based on the SEA & Humanitarian Assistance in Syria report on Household perceptions of favors in exchange for aid, October 2021, approximately 5 percent of Households that received humanitarian assistance stated that they or their family members were asked for favors in exchange for humanitarian assistance or services, such as requests for cash (84%); labor ( 9%); personal relationships (emotional/physical 9%); or other favors.Female-headed Households were nine times more likely to be asked for personal relationships in exchange for humanitarian assistance and services, support will be provided by SOS team for the prevention of Sexual Exploitation & Abuse mechanisms by continuing awareness raising in communities and among beneficiaries on PSEA, standards of conduct of aid providers and available complaint mechanisms( hotline, SMS, and focal points), with emphasis on women and girls and female-headed Households.The prevention of Sexual Exploitation & Abuse must include the full range of actors engaged in aid activities in addition to the humanitarian actors such as local authorities, community leaders, private vendors and distributors, and others participating in aid provision to beneficiaries.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The chance that security events in the targeted areas may disrupt the project and its activities. The probability of such a risk is medium, the impact is very high.However, we always follow up on the political and security situation in the governorates where we operate to take any necessary actions.Project activities will take place in safe spaces.The risk of an unstable economic crisis with high probability.This will lead to instability in the expenses needed. However,the project budget was established after studying the price changes in the region in addition to the rate of inflation during the previous years. Conservative approaches to the responsibilities of adults in providing care for vulnerable children can block their participation or crucial involvement in the project’s activities.The project will include the different stakeholders in the awareness sessions on child protection topics and the importance of children and adults' participation, Cash assistance is constantly subjected to the risk of theft, primarily when it is physically delivered to the beneficiaries.However,cash transfer programming through official money transfer companies brings immediate improvement by reducing and managing fraud risks. Finally, a delay might happen in obtaining the project’s official approvals from the Ministry Of Social Affairs.This risk will be mitigated by ensuring good coordination with MOSA and informing them of the project implementation plan.
