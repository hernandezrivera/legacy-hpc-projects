Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to strengthen capacity of Ministry of Health, National Center for Disease Control and health sector partners for rapid detection of public health risks, prompt risk assessment, notification and response through enhanced early warning and rapid response mechanism. Furthermore the project will support the collection, processing, analysis and dissemination of critical health information including access to and availability of life-saving services.
The project is in line with objective 3 of the health chapter of Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). It is also in line with strategic objective 1 of the same document.
In this project, WHO will address the capacity gaps to ensure compliance of all health facilities and sentinel sites with the minimum standards of disease surveillance reporting. Vulnerable groups including migrants, returnees, displaced families and poor host communities will remain the direct beneficiaries of the project as it will detect, respond to and contain outbreaks at an early stage.

Needs

Currently, the Libyan EWARN covers 125 sentinels sites, however not all sites report regularly due to capacity gaps, inadequate equipment and lack of backups during power cuts. The rapid response capacity is limited owing to limited capacity and lack of recurring support. The entire surveillance program depends on volunteers that compromises the quality of the surveillance data. The country is currently experiencing a measles outbreak, while preparations for a national campaign are underway, it has been difficult to intervene with a rapid response. Partners are currently using different reporting templates that lessened any chance for consolidation, analysis and draw conclusion for quick actions. Different reporting templates are currently being used by different partners which necessitate an activation of the DHIS system.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

During the community consultation and needs assessment activities, the project identified the gender implications and gendered health determinants influencing health inequities between men, women, boys and girls. Vulnerabilities and strengths of men, women, girls and boys; older persons; persons with disabilities; persons belonging to minority groups and persons with mental disorders were also identified through joint assessments. The project will improve access of affected people to services by making services readily available round the clock. Gender equality in health service provision will be taken into consideration in order to warrant equity in health access and promote the Universal Health Coverage.WHO will organize a full orientation session on the accountability to affected people and protection for the service providers. A complaint mechanism will be established to receive and address concerns of beneficiaries.
