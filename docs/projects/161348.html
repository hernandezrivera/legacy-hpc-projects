Project Summary

Protracted conflict and fresh outbreak of fighting in South Sudan’s rural areas in September 2019 (OCHA September, 2019) and frequent calamities such as flooding and drought has exacerbated the already poor sanitation and hygiene situation. Majority (80%) of food insecure populations are in Northern Bahr El Ghazal (NBEG) among other States (South Sudan IPC update, September 2019). Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in States such as NBEG have shot up to 33.3%, the worst in the country with at least 61% (280,000) of populations facing emergency acute food insecurity (FAO/ WFP FSNMS Update July 2019; South Sudan IPC update, September 2019).
Higher than normal flooding in NBEG could signify flood prone areas such as Aweil South (NBEG) likely to further disrupt livelihoods and fuel further displacements. Current flooding in these areas and prevalent drought presents a major challenge particularly to the IDPs and Host communities residing in these areas due to migration and camp congregation; coupled with restricted movements (South Sudan IPC update, September 2019).
Analysis indicate deficient key water supply infrastructure due to lack of both maintenance and damage sustained during the active months of conflict, compounded by recent mass flooding in Northern Bahr El Ghazal (NBEG). Open defecation is widespread and risks exposure to water borne diseases coursing diarrhea and Acute Watery diarrhea as it has been the case in many parts experiencing flooding. Untreated water from Rivers including makeshift ponds has been attributed as the main cause of water borne diseases; however, poor sanitation coverage including schools and lack of hand washing facilities, poor knowledge and practices on personal and environmental further fuel the situation.
The project will target 8,000 girls, 10,000 boys, 13,000 men and 20,000 women drawn from a total of 177,170 (people in need) and 3,000,000 (population in target) out of 5,243,193 population just arriving internally displaced persons, host community members, children in schools. This project will target most vulnerable populations such as elderly persons, pregnant and lactating women, and people with disabilities all drawn from the most vulnerable households. Among these will be beneficiaries at risk of gender based violence and sexual abuse, communities with poor access to nutrition and health, SAM/ MAM children. Inter-cluster synergies will be explored in targeted by close working with partners in nutrition, health, FSL and Protection. PH-SS has been operational and is currently implementing an FSL and WASH project funded by CCI Church AID on seed multiplication training techniques and site demonstration and sensitizing communities on emergency sanitation (CATS, OD fields, trenches etc). Through these, the organization has fostered strong linkages with communities, state/county line ministries of water and irrigation. This is in addition to strong and competent team including staff redeployment to scale up interventions based on needs.
Activities will ensure delivering a WASH integrated with Nutrition packages to address the high level of malnutrition, by targeting SAM/MAM children and their families. These will be further integrated with other Health-related priorities, acknowledging that poor WASH services are a key driver to poor health, and ultimately resulting in high malnutrition. This is in addition to regular assessments to mitigate GBV risks including sexual exploitation and abuse.
The project funding will be used to reduce spread of water related diseases and provision of adequate safe water through: Rehabilitation of Borehole, construction of institutional latrine with hand wash facilities, training of water management committee, training to Community Hygiene Promoter, distribution of bar soap and aquatab to vulnerable households, distribution of buckets.

1 - Needs

Poor hygiene practices, rampant open defecation with no suitable defecation location and unavailability of adequate safe water supplies continue to exacerbate the already poor access to WASH services and facilities in addition to contributing to high malnutrition levels in Northern Bahal el Gazal. Despite other partners being on ground and responding to the hygiene and sanitation needs, the current ranked humanitarian situation poses a significant threat more so in far and hard to reach locations which are often cut off from easy to reach locations due to poor infrastructure conditions and difficult terrain in the rainy season. Water points have been damaged during recent flooding and in some cases vandalized or destroyed during cycles of conflict thereby forcing women and girls to walk for long distances to fetch drinking water during the dry season or ponds or nearby rivers in the dry season thus not only exposing them to protection risks and water borne diseases/potentially cholera. Use of river or swamp water as primary source of drinking water appeared dirty, cloudy, and brown in color and had a foul smell, likely expose the populations to water-borne diseases. 2019 Protection Trends indicate rising cases of gender based violence and abuse including child abductions, recruitment of children into armed groups/ forces in addition girls and women facing sexual violence and rape as they trek long distances in search of water and food. This is in addition to unprecedented levels of food insecurity forcing girls to get married at an early age or engaging in transaction sex, further exposing them to negative reproductive health consequences and worse incidences of GBV.The project funding will be used to reduce spread of water related diseases and provision of adequate safe water through: Rehabilitation of Borehole, construction of institutional latrine with hand wash facilities, training of water management committee, refresher training to Community Hygiene Promoter, distribution of bar soap and aquatab to vulnerable households, distribution of buckets.

2 - Activities

• Rehabitation of boreholes• Construction of hand dug water ponds• Constructions of Institutional latrines with hand wash facilities• Training of water management committee• Training of Community hygiene Promoters• Distribution of washing bar soaps• Distribution of hygiene water buckets and aquatabs

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The displaced in the targeted location and all those affected by the South Sudan conflict since 2013 to date who live in Aweil East and West are beneficiaries to the project. Other groups include those with disabilities, the elderly, pregnant and lactating mothers, returnees, host community, men, women, boys, girls and youth. Those who are most vulnerable will be included.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Peace holding South Sudan will work in collaboration with the local authorities and the national government ensuring that the project objectives are in line with the overall government plan for the location. The community members will be engaged in the project at all stages of the project cycle from the planning to implementation and monitoring. The community will provide feed back on the project through feedback committee to inform future planning.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned to national objective of social protection of the beneficiaries. Every citizen has a right to access basic commodities and facilities. The project will provide emergency WASH services to the most vulnerable populations of Aweil East and West Counties and in this case, improved water, hygiene and sanitation by provision of water purifiers, construction of pit latrines and hygiene promotion. Provision of these facilities is in line with the overall government objective which is to increase investments in water supply and sanitation and higher prioritization for the sector when budget allocation decisions are made. The government states that there is need for water and sanitation is fundamental to all citizens and increased coverage of these essential services will significantly contribute to population welfare as well as the wealth and stability of South Sudan.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. The construction of latrines in the schools and within the community in public places like the market place and hospitals will contribute towards keeping the environment clean as it will minimize open defecation. Hygiene promotion will provide knowledge amongst the communities on the value of not just personal hygiene but also environmental cleanliness that includes cleanliness at home. The hygiene promoters will among other things move from house to house and will also hold awareness sessions with different groups within the community to educate them on hygiene issues. They will organise activities such as cleaning of the markets and gabbage collection.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes this project includes sustainability hand over. The community is designed to involve the community at all stages of the project. This means that the community is given an opportunity to own the project because they will become part of the implementation team hence will gain understanding of how the project works. They will grow with the project facing the challenges and being part of the solution providers. They will monitor the activities and other aspects of the project and at the end of the project they will come up with lessons learnt. This way, once the donors pull out, they will be able to carry on the project with understanding. Training of the hygiene promoters and the water, sanitation and hygiene committees on how to manage the facilities like the latrines is a sure way of ensuring that the project handover is smooth and that the project is sustainable.
