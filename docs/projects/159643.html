Project Summary

Protection concerns remain for the population in Lower Shabelle due to the increased conflicts and natural disasters, mentionable of the ongoing military operations for the SNA and AMISOM against AS, the airstrikes and clan conflicts, contributing in the worsening of the protection situation of the population, forced above 9000 HHs out of their houses to suffer for protection and GBV risks, According to Protection HNO report 2019, The lack of protection from violence, abuse and neglect remains a key consequence of the current humanitarian crisis in Somalia for up to 3.2 million people. Out of those, 1.9 million are living in displacement and 1.2 million people belong to host communities. Whether directly exposed to armed conflicts or violence, or the destitution resulting from climatic shocks, affected communities face serious risks to their physical safety and integrity, and access to their rights. The number of people in need of protection has increased since 2018. Lower Shabelle is most areas producing the IDPs, due to recurrence of conflicts, droughts and increased military operations led by the SFG against AS. From January to June 2019, the Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting (CTFMR) verified and documented. 1,473 cases (1,272 boys and 201 girls) of violence against children, with abduction and recruitment/use of children accounting for 41% and 55 % of the overall number of children affected. According to AYUUB Assessments in Marka and Lower Shabelle in June and Sept 2019, above 100,000 of the 250,000 of IDPs displaced in 2019 have fled due to the deteriorating drought conditions. According to AYUUB Multi-Sectoral Assessment report on June 2019, Marka, Qoryooley, and Wanla Weyn were among the most areas hosted highest IPDs movement, mainly escaped from the droughts, military operations and airstrikes in Lower Shabelle region, representing 27% of the total population in each district, due to the ongoing security operation and airstrikes mainly in Bariire and Aw dheegle areas of Lower Shabelle, an estimated of ( 1500 HHs) (9000 IDPs indiv.) fled to Marka and Afgooye, settling in the Afgooye corridor and Marka IDPs camps, lacking the basic of humanitarian assistance. The Project delivers protection services that encompasses of Child protection case management through reporting of violations affecting the newly displaced people in Marka, Qoryooley and Kurtunwaarey districts due to the ongoing military operations, airstrikes and droughts in Lower Shabelle region, and supporting these IDPs with Child friendly spaces and psychosocial support (CP) and referral responses of GBV and six grave violations affected individuals to the services facilities in the areas, the project will also enhance the activities with community mobilization through; targeting the various community members, respecting gender equality inclusion, disabled members, women, youths, IMAMs and community leaders to effectively achieve the project goals. Bearing in mind that SNA and AMSIOM forces sometimes backed by US technical troops increased the military operations against AS in Lower Shabelle region, mainly in Bariire, Awdheegle, Shaan Garre, Malable, Moordiinle, Sabiid and Anoole villages, resulting in the IDPs influx, arrived in parts of Marka and Afgooye districts, lacking the primary services, where protection represents the priority one for the affected community. The project will provide Family tracing & reunification (CP) to the separated children during the displacement and providing Child friendly spaces service awaiting to reunify to the caregivers. The Project targets 2,333HHs of 14000 people (4200 girls, 2940 boys, 4480 women and 2380 men the various districts mentioned above. The project in collaboration with Child Protection sub-cluster will be delivering CP Referral and Response services, Community based CP Awareness activities in the IDP camps.

Project Activities

Activity 1: Assessment, monitoring, awareness raising and outreach (Child Protection) and reporting of displacement and general protection violations from the IDP camps in Kurtunwaarey, Marka and Qoryooley districts over the project period.Activity 2: Access to Basic General Protection Services including Case management, family tracing and reunification for minors lost during conflict including reintegration for ex-child soldiers and referral services to 14000 beneficiaries (8,400 females) vulnerable and IDPs in Kurtunwaarey, Marka and Qoryooley districts over the project period.Activity 3: Protection oriented assistance (on off Cash based assistance, solar lamps and dignity kits) for 2,333 HHs with specific needs or heightened vulnerability targeting Kurtunwaarey, Marka and Qoryooley districts.Activity 4: Establish Child friendly spaces and psychosocial support in Merka, Qoryooley and Kurtunwarey districts to reach some 7140 boys and girls.

Please provide further explanation

The protection environment in Marka is sufficiently safe and stable to enable durable solutions. This is why AYUUB is proposing activities linked to Somalia governments' SDGs especially for long term interventions aimed at alleviating poverty through child protection and child safeguarding lenses and other general protection services. These interventions have also been aligned to national objectives for the Somalia Protection sector. The proposed activities are also sustainable and upon completion will be handed over to the communities through existing management structures. The challenges of displacement on the affected communities has been incorporated into the programme design.
