Project Summary

Relates to Strategic objective #2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL, and to health cluster objective #1: Ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to the most vulnerable communities in Gaza and West Bank/EJ, including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building.
This project will aim to address the urgent needs of Limb Reconstruction patients in Gaza, especially those who suffered from traumatic LR injuries since the start of the Great Return Marches (GRM) events. This category has been neglected by organisations and left for the under-resourced MoH to deal with as these cases require years of treatment and are financially overwhelming. Plus, the skills' set available in Gaza are not able to meet such great demands.
MAP has been active since 2014 establishing the first dedicated LR unit in Palestine, however, this project is designed to meet the urgent needs of the very recent and late-complex limb injuries of the Great Return Marches (GRM) as these far exceed any local capacity that exists in Gaza.

Needs

Since the start of the GRM events on 30 March 2018, majority of the injuries via live ammunition were lower limb injuries as can be shown by the latest info-graphic of the MoH covering until 8 September 2018. These mount to 6373 injuries (56.1%) of the total number of injuries. Out of these, and as per the recent assessment that MAP conducted after 14 May, and as per the cluster's calculations; the number of limb reconstruction patients will reach 1,500 patients by the end of December if the GRM demonstrations stop by the end of the year. These LR patients are patients that would require 6 months to 3 years of treatment, 2-8 surgeries, 12 - 18 physiotherapy sessions, and as much as 30 physiotherapy sessions (MAP, 2018). The only economical study done by King's College Hospital about these patients indicates that each case would cost around 30,000 sterling pounds which mounts to 40,000 USD. The figures above exclude upper limb injuries which constitute 1,495 cases (13.2%). Now, with regards to the current capacity at MoH, it is true that MAP, along with partners like IDEALS, and NORWAC, assisted in creating the first dedicated LR unit in Palestine, however, the capacity of the local team is way beyond the 1,500 injuries to be treated locally. The current team is composed of 2 consultant orthopedic surgeons (lower limbs only), 2 nurses, and 2 physios (male and female). This team is also supported by medical missions that MAP manages to bring to Gaza every quarter to treat the most complex cases that the local team cannot handle. This comes on top of the fact that only 23% of the GRM patient applications to cross outside of Gaza for treatment are approved with the rest of applications being denied, or delayed (Health cluster, 23 SEP - 06 OCT situational report). The aforementioned is a clear indication of how much medical teams are needed and also how much support needs to be given to the local units at Gaza hospitals. To add, and according to the trauma working group, MAP is the only organisation that deals with late and complex LR injuries in Gaza Strip assisting the MoH, as other organisations deal only with early construction and avoid the late stages that are risky, lengthy, and financially are overwhelming. One of our surgeons was astonished by the nature of the injuries of the GRM events and indicated that even the best equipped hospital in Europe can never handle the influx of injuries that the local teams are treating. In addition, the upper limb injuries are mostly left for the MAP consultants as the local team are experts in lower injuries only. All the above comes in top of a staggering crisis of zero-stock items and delays in getting life-saving tools and drugs into Gaza. Also the fact that the LR service does not have dedicated physical space to use such as a dedicated theater, or dedicated OPC. Please note that the LR service helps both male and female patients and all ages according to their specific needs. We provide physio services to both genders considering the cultural settings via a male and female physios. Also please note that this project address all ages and does not segregate between age groups unless their needs dictates otherwise. The treatment of pediatric cases, for example, is dependent upon the availability of the consultants coming with the missions, if these cases cannot be treated locally.

Activities

MAP is proposing to continue its vital support to the LR unit in Gaza via the below: * Bring 8 medical missions to Gaza consisting of lower, upper limb, pediatric ortho. and plastic surgeons to assess patients and conduct complex LR surgeries.* Procure the urgently needed sets of items such as TSF, and Ex-Fixes, and the needed bone substitutes (cement, STIMULAN) considering age difference when applicable.* Procure the needed implants and disposables needed urgently for the LR service.* Provide on-the-job training to health practitioners in Gaza via the missions and medical symposiums. Please note that the local teams are engaged during all phases of the project in terms of determining the needs, designing the shape of each mission and it activities, the selection of cases for assessments and surgeries, and the M&E aspect. We have a regular bi-weekly Skype session with the medical teams to discuss such issues regularly.
