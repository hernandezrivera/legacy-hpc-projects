Project Summary

This addendum to the FSS Project proposed will provide a buffer for the currently proposed Food Security intervention for CARE and will also serve as an avenue to integrate Covid-19 response plans and preparedness activities.
Activities for the Covid-19 Integrated Response Include:
1. Training's for All beneficiaries targeted by the project, Staff, Volunteers, Community Leaders/Committees etc on Covid-19 preparedness, prevention, hygiene awareness.
2. Installation of Hand Washing Points at the proposed Communities of Intervention in Borno and Yobe State.
3. Provision of PPE for All 60 Staff
4. Distribution of Hand Wash - 10,000 pcs.
5. Distribution of Hand Sanitizers - 10,000 pcs.
6. Training of selected 1,000 beneficiaries on Soap Production, Production of Hand Sanitizers, Hand Wash & Face Masks; supply of inputs for production.
7. Printing of IEC Materials, Posters, Leaflets; Radio Jingles targeting the entire population of the proposed intervention areas.

1 - Needs

Since the emergence of the index case of Covid-19 on 27th February 2020 as announced officially by Nigeria's Minister of Health, there has been increased cases recorded. As at 6th May 2020 Nigeria has 2,950 confirmed cases and 98 deaths recorded with already confirmed positive cases and deaths recorded in the North East. On the other hand Yobe State has also recorded 13 confirmed cases and 1 death - according to NCDC. The Northeastern part of Nigeria which is ravaged by Insurgency since 2009 and has been trying to recover is facing double crisis with the emergence of Covid-19. With so many viulnerable HHs that depend on Humanitarian Aid coupled with high illiteracy levels, the North-East is a key area that requires undivided attention. Here, there is need to integrated Covid-19 response and preparedness activities to create awareness and sensitise the people on the virus and to equip them with preventive measures; how to identify suspected cases and what to do when you feel sick.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Complaint & Feedback Mechanism: A variety of ways will be used to ensure effective complaint and feedback mechanisms and these will include the following: - Setting up of help desks and the purpose and use of the help desks well explained to the affected populations, local leadership/stakeholders - Through engaging the Project Management Committees (PMCs) who will ensure that beneficiaries are consulted in decisions that affect them - Recruitment of Gender, Protection, Complaint and Feedback staff whose main focus is to ensure that all protection issues affecting beneficiaries are addressed to ensure safety and dignity of the affected populations. - Establishment of hotlines and suggestion boxes where relevant - CARE will strengthen its feedback and complaint mechanisms to receive continuous feedback from affected populations on the interventions. CARE will ensure that it is accountable to the affected populations through implementation and adoption of a range of strategies, approaches and processes in the designing, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project. These include the following: - Affected Persons will be involved throughout the project cycle where they will actively participate in all aspects from design phases. - Explaining the project activities, participants’/beneficiaries identification and selection criteria, ration entitlements and the right to provide complaints and receive feedback. - Facilitate the establishment of PMCs who are representatives of the wide range of beneficiaries from the various communities and the PMCs will constitute at least 50% women while representing all categories of the beneficiaries (women, men, youth, people with special needs (eg disabled). - The inclusion of all categories of the affected populations (women, men, youth and people representing people with special needs, will ensure that the concerns of all people are addressed and information dissemination is done in a way that will ensure equal access.
