Project Summary

Project Summary
The project is one faced: it aims at address the food insecurity among the IDPs and host communities in Hirshebelle region by providing conditional and unconditional food to the food insecure and at risk population as well as screening and reporting of the responses and provision of applicable skills that would enable them develop resilience once the situation is under control. It aims to undertake this by using tools to gather base data, in addition to the available FSNA, FEWSNET, and other analysis and assessment results, to informs it action. Provision of food is a short gap measures stringent upon the food security situation until the condition improves

Needs

Needs In the recent past South Central Somalia, including Hiran and Middle Shebelle region of Hirshebelle State, has faced a number of natural challenges ranging from the prolonged back to back drought going back to at least three previous years coupled by rainfall failures. This year rains pounded most parts of the south leading to flooding, decimation of the assets including sources of livelihood in farms and livestock and pushing the communities to different risks in food security health and even protection. As a result of the natural disasters, drought coupled by the floods there was widespread migration of people and families with many families ending up in IDP camps. Add to the volatility of several areas in South in terms of insecurity and the communities have been left without tangible source of livelihood. This, according to FSNAU latest update, increased vulnerabilities of the communities to malnutrition which if not addressed urgently can lead to other health complications and humanitarian disasters. This led to acute food security. According to FSNAU/FEWS NET, an estimated 3.1 million people, 25 per cent of the population, are expected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and Crisis (IPC Phase 3) through at least December 2018. In addition, nearly 3.1 million people are classified as food security ‘stressed’ (IPC Phase 2). An assessment made by GHF on 7th to 12th of October 2018 in Beledweyne, Bulaburte and Jowhar Districts of Hirshabelle state.

Activities

(a) Food Security Programs. (i) Provision of food vouchers (Maize, Sugar, Oil & pulses) to 1300 HHs in emergency (586 men headed households & 714 women headed households) each household receiving a voucher worth 75 USD per month. Total amount of food provision is 60 mt of Maize, 8 mt sugar,6 mt Oil & 9 mt Pulses. (ii) Provision of food vouchers (Maize, Sugar, Oil & pulses) to 500HHs in IDP camps (220 men headed households & 280 women headed households) each household receiving a voucher worth 75USD per month. Total amount of food provision is 20 mt of Maize, 3 mt sugar,2 mt Oil & 3 mt Pulses (iii) Provision of cash relief worth 150 USD per month for 2 months to 800HHs (352 men headed households & 448 women headed households) (iv) Provision of seeds (maize seeds, sorghum seeds, cow peas, mung beans, onion ,tomatoes, lettuce) farm tools & land preparation to 3150 hh (1390 men headed households & 1760 women headed households); (v) Creating of temporary job opportunities to 600 households (264 men headed households & 337 women headed households) through cash for work activities . (vi) Provision of daily meals at school (CSB in the morning hours & rice at noon) to 400 school going children for 3 months in the year

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Food provision has capacity building and provision of farm inputs and skills transfer to ensure long-term and sustainability of the project and contribution towards resilience. Additionally it supports one of the pillars of the Federal Government 2017 National Development Plan which identifies food security as resilience as a key pillar in the stabilization of the country. While at it the project especially the provision of agricultural inputs will play a role in environmental preservation through creation of soil cover, protection from erosion as well as enrichment through organic development. Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Constant migration in search of the livelihood opportunities and to escape poverty, drought and other natural disasters is a common practice in Somalia and within the South Central areas including within the Hirshebelle states, parts of which GHF operates. The project operates withing an already identified community consisting of host communities and the IDPs. Within that framework there is no category envisaged within. Those migrating from their homes because o the issues the project seeks to address will most certainly end up in the IDPs Camps, as has been witnessed in the past and will be accessed. Although this presents planning challenges the organization will nonetheless work with stakeholders to address these challenges.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

GHF has a policy of equality and equity and as such does not condone such practices. It doesn't discriminate any person based on any orientation whether clan based, gender, age, colour or any other differentiating attributes. The organization works with all the stakeholders including IDP leaders and the host communities to ensure the inclusivity at all levels provided the population was originally targeted in the inclusive planning process.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The possibility of attacks from the militia Al-Shabaab is one challenge the program team always deals with on a daily basis. However in the recent past the host communities and IDP within which the project takes place has witnessed tremendous level of peace and tranquility. GHF will continue to work closely with the local community to ensure safety of its staff and the project beneficiaries..
