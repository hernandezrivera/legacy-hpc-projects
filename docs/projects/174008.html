Project Summary

The proposed intervention will target a total of 14,802 individuals: 11500 children (28050 girls, 3450 boys) and 3,302 adults (1,936 women and 1366 men). It is estimated that the project will indirectly benefit the population of the targeted communities as a whole, i.e. more than 450,000 people. Beneficiaries will be selected by community child protection committees and other stakeholders on the basis of specific child protection vulnerabilities. They will include children and young people at-risk or having experienced violence, abuse or neglect, including UASC, survivors of SGBV, CAAFAG, adolescent mothers, and children engaged in child labour. The number of girls and women are expected to be higher than boys and men due to the assessed protection needs where they have been deemed as more vulnerable and affected
Plan International will also continue to strengthen and provide community child protection services through the proposed project. Plan International will contribute to increased access to a protective environment by providing Child Friendly Spaces with Psychosocial Support to boys and girls below the age of 18 including children with disabilities and the Mbororos. Case Management & referral services will be provided to at least 1500 children in affected communities, and contribute to strengthening the Community Based Child Protection mechanisms to prevent and respond to violence including SGBV.
Complain and Feedback committees shall also be put in place to get regular reactions from the target communities and to improve on the quality of services provided

1 - Besoins / Needs

In 2020, Cameroon remained the most affected country by the conflict in the Lake Chad Basin, after Nigeria. The Far North region has seen a sharp upsurge in violence. Ongoing hostilities have forcibly displaced a total of 559,699 individuals2 (114,496 Nigerian refugees, 321,886 internally displaced people (hereafter IDP), and 123,489 IDP returnees) and continue to push more people to flee their villages. Among the forcibly displaced population, 64% are children calling for a dedicated protection response. The number of IDPs increased from 297,000 at the end 2019 to 321,000 as of September 2020.The affected population is regularly subjected to armed attacks, kidnappings, including kidnapping of children, looting and destruction of property and infrastructure. Protection risks are often different, depending on gender, age and diversity. Security threats also include arbitrary arrests, forced recruitment into armed groups and physical violence, primarily affecting men and boys. Girls and women remain particularly vulnerable to violence including sexual and gender-based violence due to the combined effect of pre-existing cultural and traditional discriminatory norms and practices, gender discrimination and socio-economic vulnerability caused by the crisis. Security threats are exacerbated during the heavy rainy season that limits movement of law enforcement forces and road access in many areas of the three departments bordering Nigeria, Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga. As of September 2020, close to 160,000 people had been affected by the floods in the Far North Region. From January to October 2019, a total of 2,622 GBV cases were reported to GBVIMS. The data analyzed revealed significant gaps in the provision of essential GBV services.
