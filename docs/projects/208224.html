Project Summary

Mozambique has been experiencing a significant influx of IDPs, who are seeking refugee in safer areas across different regions. These displacements are typically the result of either natural disasters or conflicts, both of which have left communities more vulnerable, particularly regarding shelter and housing conditions. The latest IOM/DTM Base line report (Round 19, August 2023) shows that around 850,589 people are displaced in Mozambique, and nearly 79% (668,929 people) are displaced in the northern Mozambique.
In the same report, shelter is indicated as one of the top three priority needs reported by IDPs in Cabo Delgado. This highlights the significance of shelter in ensuring the safety and better accommodation of affected families as well as the basic needs of the people.
This project aims to increase the community resilience in Cabo Delgado, Nampula, and Niassa Provinces through crisis and disaster preparedness, response, and recovery actions based in people's process to ensure better appropriation and sustainability.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project takes into account the latest data and trends in population displacement in the northern region of Mozambique, and considers at the forefront the need for a sustainable transition from Humanitarian to Development and focuses on three geographic foci, i) areas of displacement (Ibo, Mecúfi, Metuge and Montepuez) covering 14,523 people, ii) areas of return (Mocimboa Praia, Mueda and Paleda) covering 19,986 people and iii) areas affected by natural disasters (Ilha de Moçambique, Lichinga and Palma). 523 people, ii) areas of return (Mocimboa da Praia, Mueda and Palma) covering 19,986 people and iii) areas affected by natural disasters (Ilha de Moçambique, Lichinga and Cuamba) covering 720 people. This project is centered on a holistic approach, so this initiative integrates climate-resilient shelter solutions, gender-inclusive practices design and constructions, and community participation to strengthen resilience and self-recovery of shelter and housing by offering support in following (3) three components: (1) Site Planning, Development and Awareness Sessions on DRR/DRM and Resilience;(2) Community Capacity Building;(3) Technical assistance to communities and partners. Awareness-raising activities will be the strong component of this intervention, which combined with on-the-job training in expeditious planning and settlement design, will contribute to building the climate and socio-economic resilience of the target community. The settlement maintenance committees will be established or strengthened and equipped with tools and expertise in land demarcation and delimitation, access road alignments and the construction and maintenance of dwellings and/or shelters. Therefore, the strengthening of local capacities in terms of resistant and incrementary shelters will play an important role, especially in the areas of return, where it is hoped that the population will be able to settle permanently. The project basis on building the capacity of the different stakeholders in the process of building community resilience with a view to reducing the consequences of natural disasters and armed conflicts. The implementation of this intervention will be based on UN-Habitat's past experiences, which emphasise a people-centred process as a way of better conducting community resilience building.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Gender inequalities are accentuated in crisis contexts because women, girls, boys and men all have different experiences, needs, abilities and priorities. Potential risks for women and girls, boys and men may be related to the failure to include their specific needs in shelter design. Women may be underrepresented in community consultations. The material produced may not be easy to understand due to the high level of illiteracy among women, girls, boys and men. Women's participation in these participatory spaces could trigger gender-based violence because of their domestic roles and responsibility. In order to prevent the project from creating situations that could harm women, the project will: 1. Collect information from both women and men, of different ages and social groups; focus group discussion with women separately from men; 2. Guarantee gender-balance on assessment teams;3. Take into account the more limited time and mobility of certain groups (older persons, person with disability, etc) when asking for their participation. Workloads, domestic roles, physical ability, and childcare will be factored in when planning for inclusion. 4. Production of IEC material based on illustrative images and translation of the main awareness messages into local languages.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

To ensure gender inclusivity, and meaningful access to assistance the project sets a clear target of 45% women and 55% men (which includes elderly persons) among the 29,518 people to be reached in all activities. Outcome 1 emphasizes increasing community awareness about disaster risks through DRR/Resilience Awareness Sessions, specifically targeting youth, women, and persons with disabilities. This ensures that vulnerable groups, including elderly persons, are actively engaged in understanding and responding to risks. Furthermore, activity 1.1 involves a participatory settlement and shelter assessment, acknowledging the dynamics and challenges faced by diverse groups. The project aims to understand the specific needs of women and elderly persons in shelter adaptation and disaster response.Acknowledging the unique needs of women, the project commits to guaranteeing a minimum of 40% women's participation in trainings and awareness sessions. Capacity-building endeavors, including focus group discussions, will be tailored to ensure meaningful participation, acknowledging the varied roles and responsibilities of women and elderly persons.Additionally, women will receive specialized training in Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Resilience, and Early Recovery Shelter. This not only equips them with essential skills but also positions them for future employment opportunities, contributing to sustained empowerment and economic independence. In Activity 3.1 involves community workshops and co-creation of participatory shelter designs, actively engaging community members, including women and elderly persons, in shaping resilient and sustainable housing solutions based on their needs, climate conditions, and land topography.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project's commitment to inclusivity spans all activities and outcomes, ensuring individuals with disabilities actively engage in and benefit from the project's objectives of fostering improved community resilience and sustainable development.Throughout the project, a comprehensive strategy for disability inclusion is noticeable. The focal group discussions in Outcome 1 actively seek to identify people with disabilities among vulnerable populations, informing the development of inclusive shelter elements. Outcome 1's socially inclusive approach fosters awareness and dismantles barriers for individuals with disabilities. In Outcome 2, insights from people with disabilities shape the crafting of a training package prioritizing shelter construction, aligning with the project's emphasis on Disaster Risk Reduction, Resilience, and Emergency Solutions. Consultations in Activity 2 of Outcome 2 ensure a nuanced understanding of community dynamics, including challenges faced by individuals with disabilities. Focused on providing vital support, incorporates Activity 2, emphasizing the creation of dual-purpose Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials. These materials are intentionally inclusive, covering Resilience, House Land and Property, Disaster Risk Reduction, and Building Back Safer, tailored for various audiences, including those with disabilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Taking into account the project description, as well as the local reference framework in terms of policies, standards and regulations and the potential risks and impacts related to specific activities, the Environmental and Social sphere of the project within the specific implementation location of the districts of implementation in the northern region will need to be evaluated and studied, in parallel with the participatory process, in aspects related to the Environment and the Social dimension. Thus the project will be using UN-Habitat’s standard guidelines related to ESSS, ensuring that, in addition to consultations, training can integrate environmental prevention components such as Biodiversity Conservation and Sustainable Management of Living Natural Resources, Climate Change Resilience, Community Health, Safety and Security, Zero-Carbon Development, Pollution Prevention and Resource Efficiency, among others.
