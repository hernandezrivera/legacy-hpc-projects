Project Summary

Within the framework of the ERP 2021-2022, Tdh in consultation with their local partner organizations Sanabel Al Janoub (SJ), SAMA, Al Ghad and Human Charity for Relief and Development (HCRD) have jointly developed a project to strengthen the Child Protection (CP) and Gender-based violence (GBV) response services for the most vulnerable Lebanese, Palestinian Refugees from Lebanon (PRL) and migrants in remote and underserved areas in the South and Nabatieh Governorates.
The proposed intervention will take place in the Governorate of South, particularly in Saida and Tyre districts, covering 30 cadasters and in the Governorate of Nabatieh, specifically in Bent Jbeil and Nabatieh Tahta districts, covering 9 cadasters. In addition, the project will target five Palestinian Camps of South (Bourj Chamali, Al Bass, Rashdieh, Ein el Helwi and Mieh Mieh).
The locations were identified and targeted in consultation with the Union of Municipalities in Tyre, Saida, Sarafand and Nabatieh, Popular Committees and Child Protection Networks (CPNs) in the five Palestinian Camps and based on the recent needs’ assessments of the local partners that highlighted the gaps in terms of CP/GBV services, trends, and vulnerabilities of the targeted populations. Furthermore, the previous prevention and outreach activities within the Social Behavioral Change Initiatives of Tdh in South and Nabatieh were factors that supported the identification of the locations and pointed out the areas with the highest CP/GBV needs.
The specific localities were also selected based on Tdh and partners’ ability to access remote and underserved areas (such as Bent Jbeil and Nabatieh) and areas that project’s partners have more than 44 years constant presence and working experience.
The primary purpose of the project is to provide a life-saving response in a context of significantly increased needs due to complex humanitarian crisis in Lebanon. Tdh and local partners have identified the vulnerability criteria through needs assessments, CP Realtime Monitoring Reports, analysis of the recent referrals but also using as a baseline the data from the case management specialized services of the previous and/or ongoing projects that target partially the CP/GBV concerns of Lebanese and PRL populations. As a response, the project is proposing to target the most vulnerable individuals with the following criteria: 1. Children exposed to neglect, psychical, psychological, and emotional abuse, exploitation, including all types of child labour, 2. Children separated by their parents/caregivers or at risk of separation due to COVID19, medical or socioeconomical reasons, 3. Children exposed to poverty, malnutrition, hidden malnutrition, and school’s drop out due to socioeconomical situation in Lebanon. 4. Children in conflict and in contact with Law and exposed to substance abuse. 5. Children (girls and boys) and young women (until 24 years old) exposed to GBV, including child marriage, sexual abuse, intimate partner violence and domestic violence. 6. Female single headed households with children.
This project will provide specialized response services to GBV cases, including Case Management, provision of Emergency Cash Assistance (ECA), Legal Assistance, as well as Focused PSS sessions to vulnerable individuals. The project will have a holistic approach, including Community-based PSS sessions in Safe Spaces and distribution of Dignity Kits within prevention activities. Moreover, specialized response services related to CP cases will be provided such as Case Management, referrals to the cash assistance under the Haddi program, and focused PSS sessions for children and caregivers. Community-based PSS sessions, Positive Parenting sessions for caregivers and Social behavior Change/child protection initiatives will be also implemented during the project duration of 11 months.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

The Child Safeguarding, Gender and Diversity and PSEA policies and Code of Conduct will be implemented as followed: 1. Staff and partners will be trained on and commit to policies by signature, 2. Policies and reporting mechanisms will be explained to beneficiaries, 3. Consult individuals on their needs and preferences in order to access the reporting mechanisms, 4. Three independent focal points monitor the implementation of the policies, receive, and address the complaints, 5. Tdh will continue active participation in the PSEA national network, 6. Ensure an active and clear appeal process with the program team and the communities, 7. Recruitment of female and male will ensure the access to beneficiaries of all gender, 8. Tdh and HCRD, that is specialized on disability, will ensure the active and tailored participation of people with disabilities in the protection risk mitigation plan.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Tdh and its partners ensure that beneficiaries engage with the project planning, design, implementation, and M&E and learning practices. All beneficiaries will be encouraged to work with Tdh in the phases of the project cycle: in delivering messages, identifying vulnerable children and families and sharing general data on trends and gaps. Through close contact with the communities Tdh ensures access to relevant and clear information about the program. This information includes the project plans and outcomes, staff roles and responsibilities, contact information, beneficiary selection criteria and entry points. Feedback around the quality of service received and any faced challenges from the beneficiaries will be collected through outreach activities, focus group discussions and satisfaction surveys to improve the activities implementation. Feedback and complaints of beneficiaries will be collected through feedback boxes at static centers, hotlines, and email addresses.
