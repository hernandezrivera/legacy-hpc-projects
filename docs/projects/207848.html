Project Summary

The main goal of this program is to collaborate with the youths both male and female in the host community to create sustainable livelihood opportunities that are oriented towards the market. Additionally, it aims to foster social cohesion by involving them in volunteer work for development. The project will assist 600 both male and female young individuals to acquire the necessary skills for either finding employment or starting their own businesses. The project places significant emphasis on empowering women and girls, recognizing that there are power dynamics within families and communities that need to be addressed in order to promote gender equality. The training and mentoring support provided also incorporate elements focused on maintaining peace and harmony within and between communities, preventing sexual and gender-based violence, promoting female leadership opportunities, and preventing further victimization of women and girls. ACF will enhance livelihood opportunities for youth from vulnerable households in the host community through IGA and vocational skills training. ACF have already conducted an assessment to identify the preferred skills for training among the community's youth. ACF will offer support for a four-week short course by engaging technical institutes or resource persons in areas such as Mobile Phone Repairing, Electrical House Wiring, AC/Refrigerators Technician, and Car Driving based on the needs and preferences of the youths. After completing the training, participants will receive the necessary technical tools and equipment and/or start-up capital. ACF will facilitate non-formal technical training through internships, on-the-job training, or job placements in professions like Barber, Carpenter, Auto Rickshaw Mechanics, Plumber, Mason, and tile mason, again based on the needs and preferences of the youths. ACF will also provide support for off-farm Income Generating Activities (IGA), such as fish drying, small business where participants will receive two days of training, along with start-up capital and inputs. Financial literacy and disaster risk reduction actions will be integrated into all types of training sessions. Under this project, ACF aims to target Hnila, Whykong and Sabrang union of Teknaf Upazila. The project participants will be selected from the most vulnerable households, including those headed by females, households with persons with disabilities, orphan-hosted households, and households with elderly individuals. Job fairs will be organized to connect employers with vocational training participants, creating opportunities for employment. This will establish a connection between the participants, financial service providers, and employers. ACF's Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) team will develop tools based on the project's output and outcome. The MEAL unit will conduct periodic surveys, focus group discussions, and participatory development meetings with the targeted population. In the project implementation area, an effective complaints and feedback mechanism will be established to ensure community participation, transparency, and accountability. Additionally, a contingency plan will be included in the project to address any disasters that may occur during the project period, and all staff will be equipped with the necessary tools and protective gear to respond to emergencies within 72 hours. The proposed project will adhere to the Livelihoods and Skill Development Sector policy and guidance.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Bangladesh hosts around one million FDMNs, most of whom fled for their lives from Myanmar in 2017. The country set an admirable example of solidarity with the refugees despite its resource constraints, especially in Cox’ Bazar region, where most refugees are located. However, in the host communities is a perceptible shift from this rather unconditional solidarity to an anti-refugee sentiment. Over the years, solidarity with the plight of FDMNs evolved because of economic instability as a result of the wage fall and price hike, unequal access to humanitarian aid and uneven distribution of resource opportunities created through substantial donor support to the FDMNs. Because of the disrupted economy that comes with hosting so many refugees, the tension between host communities and refugees increase. To address these tensions, supporting youth and women in host communities in livelihoods and peaceful coexistence is key. According to REVA-6, in the Bangladeshi communities, insufficient food consumption saw a slight decrease to 34 percent in 2022, down from 38 percent in 2021. However, this still represents a decline from 21 percent in 2019, indicating ongoing challenges faced by Bangladeshi communities in meeting their food consumption needs since the onset of the pandemic, and their slow recovery from job and livelihood losses. Both Rohingya and Bangladeshi households are grappling with difficulties in providing enough food, often resorting to negative coping strategies. Approximately 65 percent of households in the camps and 55 percent in the Bangladeshi community turned to less preferred or lower-cost food options as their most common coping strategy. The economic vulnerability of Bangladeshi community households increased to 35 percent in 2022, up from 32 percent in 2021, already surpassing the pre-COVID-19 level of 26 percent in 2019. This decline underscores the challenges households face in light of the ongoing global food crisis, the continued economic impacts of the pandemic, and the 2022 price increases of essential commodities in Bangladesh. In the Bangladeshi community, the proportion of households not employing any coping strategies to secure food increased to 29 percent, marking an 11 percentage point rise from the previous year (18 percent), yet still 10 percentage points lower than the base year of 2019. The percentage of Bangladeshi community households experiencing income deprivation has decreased to 18 percent. As per the MSNA 2023 report, approximately 36% of households in the host community faced diverse challenges in accessing income-generating prospects. Moreover, 29% of households indicated zero income (in Sabrang Union, this percentage was 37%). In the 18-25 age bracket, 58% of individuals were employed out of the total population that reported working, with only 8% being female. According to RIMA II-2022 report, In comparison to 2021, the resilience index of Ukhia and Teknaf has significantly increased. Besides, a negative impact of shock specially landslides and price hike was observed on the resilience capacity of the host community. The households not affected by shock were found having a higher resilience capacity (37) compared to those affected by shocks (32).

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Action Against Hunger (ACF) is a global humanitarian organization that takes decisive action against the causes and effects of hunger. ACF has been providing support to communities in Bangladesh since 2007 and has been active in the Cox's Bazar District since 2009, where it offers high-quality assistance to both host and Rohingya refugee community. Over its extensive presence in the country, particularly in Cox's Bazar, ACF has championed numerous innovative initiatives in the field of Food Security and Livelihoods, aiming to bolster the resilience of both host and refugee communities. ACF boasts a wealth of experience in partnerships with UN agencies, including UNHCR, UNICEF, IOM, WHO, UNOPS, and WFP. Additionally, ACF has collaborated with local NGOs such as Mukti Cox's Bazar, FIVDB, Sushilan, Friendship, SHED and SARPV etc. ACF's dedicated team includes technical experts in each sector, including Food Security and Livelihoods, who provide invaluable support to program teams on the ground, ensuring the quality of interventions. Through an integrated approach, ACF aims to enhance inter-sectoral coordination within camps and local administration, improve service delivery to refugees and host communities, and maintain strong relationships with all relevant stakeholders. ACF is already implementing a Skill Development project in Sabrang union under Teknaf Upazila, offering training in areas such as electrical work, refrigerator/AC repair, mobile phone repairing, driving, and fish drying. This initiative will continue until 2025. Throughout the project cycle, ACF will reinforce coordination with WaSH, Health, Shelter, FSS, GBV, and other actors. ACF is an active member of the PSEA network, and all staff members will receive further training on safeguarding policy and PSEA guidelines. The proposed interventions under this project align seamlessly with the Livelihoods and Skill Development sector priorities and the strategies set forth by the Bangladesh Government.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

GBV risk mitigation also integrated throughout the project cycle. ACF will ensure to mainstreaming GBV issues across all activities, following a survivor-centered approach to ensure vulnerabilities of women and girls are integrated into all analyses and approaches. ACF works for protection mainstreaming, for person with disabilities, children and on gender-related issues. ACF will provide integrated, multi-sectoral trainings to ensure that non-protection actors are equipped with basic knowledge and skills to recognize vulnerable individuals and refer them to protection services. Child protection will be included in vulnerable household selection by identifying SAM/MAM/ health cases at high risk of child abuse, to be referred and prioritized for comprehensive child protection assessment and case management. There will be a Protection Hotline for reporting, complaints and feedback. A conflict-sensitive lens is also applied throughout and risk/security assessments are conducted to ensure protection e.g. to ensure interventions do not increase tensions; or risk the safety of staff or beneficiaries

3.3 - Age & Disability

ACF maintain Sex, Age and diversity Disaggregated Data (SADDD) and Washington Group Questions (WGQ) for targeting beneficiary in order to address the specific needs of the each beneficiary group. ACF abide by the local laws and by applicable international instruments, including the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child and International Labor Organization conventions. The activities will be enabled for age group 18+ under the project. ACF provides priority for person with disability in selection process and implementation of activities. ACF will provide further training for staffs on SADDD and WGQ. ACF will ensure referral mechanism to ensure rehabilitation services for every person with disabilities and establish facilities after conducting accessibility assessment.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project beneficiaries will be actively engaged in the assessments conducted by ACF like post distribution monitoring (PDM), food security surveillance survey (FSS). The beneficiary selection will be done by community consultations and the affected people will decide to select beneficiaries as per criteria of project interventions. Household with PLW, person with disability, children under 5 and elderly are prioritized in the criteria of selection of beneficiaries. Communities also will provide their feedback on ACF led project activities formally through the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) using hotline number, complaint box and email. And informally through meetings and individual interactions based on which actions will be taken to rectify the project activities if needed. Apart from this, ACF will prepared a Project Monitoring Committee (PMC) by the local community, who will provide feedback and learnings.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

ACF is committed to the mainstreaming of the PSEA principles and has own policy. This entails embedding a robust framework that prioritizes the safety, dignity, and well-being of all participants, particularly those who may be vulnerable or at-risk. ACF will take initiative to aware community people, staffs, beneficiary and all stakeholders involved, equipping them with the knowledge and skills to recognize signs of vulnerability and report any incidents promptly. ACF have clear reporting mechanisms, including a confidential hotline, complaint box and email to encourage open communication and facilitate immediate response to any concerns raised. Additionally, ongoing monitoring and evaluation will be conducted through a PSEA lens, ensuring that interventions remain aligned with best practices and uphold the highest standards of protection for all individuals involved in the program.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, for the past 14 years, ACF has been actively engaged in disaster risk reduction efforts in Cox's Bazar. ACF played a pivotal role in developing and reviewing Community Risk Assessments (CRA) and Risk Reduction Action Plans (RRAP) for Union Parishads. Additionally, ACF has taken proactive steps towards implementing small-scale mitigation measures based on RRAP recommendations. ACF have also established a Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) task force focused on early warning systems, search and rescue operations, evacuation procedures, and first aid, with support from CPP at different Upazila level of Cox’s Bazar. Acf has organized disaster drills in collaboration with CPP and fire drills in partnership with the Fire Service and Civil Defense. ACF has worked towards strengthening the capacity of UDMC and WDMC members. As part of this program, ACF will continue to facilitate the organization of community development initiatives by Youth Clubs through campaign. These campaigns will cover various aspects, including raising awareness about early warning systems, conducting search and rescue operations, practicing evacuation procedures, promoting hygiene practices, tree plantation drives, maintaining cleanliness in the area, discouraging the use of polythene, combatting drug abuse, and administering livestock vaccinations, all in response to community-identified issues.Youth clubs will be provided with orientation and will be connected with CPP volunteers to support DRR activities. Youth CPP volunteers will receive priority consideration for membership in the youth clubs. ACF will also be responsible for developing and periodically reviewing contingency plans to ensure the safety and well-being of project participants in the event of a crisis. Furthermore, all participants in the training activities will receive instruction on disaster preparedness, environmental awareness, and climate change mitigation topics.
