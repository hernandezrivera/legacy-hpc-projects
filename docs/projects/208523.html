Project Summary

The project will focus to improve and uninterrupted access to life-saving Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) care services to the IDPs and vulnerable women and adolescent girls, including the returnees and those from the host community in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States (BAY States). The minimum initial service, and comprehensive SRH services covering but not limited to antenatal care, postnatal care, support for delivery services, clinical management of rape and family planning services to women and girls in need will be strengthened through the supported health facilities and partners. In addition, the project will also strengthen the integration of MHPSS in SRH service provision and make sure that clear referral channels are available for those with the severe psychosis. The survivors of sexual violence and women with obstetric emergencies who need tertiary-level care will also benefit from the specialized referral support services of the project. UNFPA will also procure inter-agency emergency reproductive health kits, including clinical management of rape kits and other essential medical items to support uninterrupted SRH service delivery, as well as for the prepositioning. UNFPA will ensure the last mile distribution of the kits and medical commodities to all agreed Health facilities and mobile outreaches.
The unpredictable situations of cholera outbreaks will also be considered in the response while also ensuring the continued provision of vital SRH services. In order to do so, UNFPA will provide capacity building trainings to different service providers and partners. The efforts of advocacy and leadership of the SRH sub-working group under the health sector will be strengthened to maximize the momentum of SRH response, including the preparedness for any emergency across BAY States. In addition, the clinical management of the rape task force (CMR TF) under SRH SWG will be strengthened and ensure the integration of SRH and GBV, to ensure the GBV survivors receive appropriate, timely health care services and mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS). The ultimate aim of the project is to ensure the continuation and strengthening of SRH services through the provision of reproductive health supplies, capacity building and enhancing coordination across the BAY States.

Implementing Partners

There are two levels of partnership, first is the direct implementing collaboration with the Royal Health Heritage Foundation (RHHF), Nigerian Red Cross Society (NRCS), Center for Population and Reproductive Health (CPRH), Action Health Incorporated (AHI), Norwegian Church Aid (NCA), International Rescue Committee (IRC). In addition, UNFPA will work with local CSO including women led organizations sub-contracted under each of the main IPs. Moreover, UNFPA will also work with State Ministries as the key strategic partners (Ministry of planning and finance, Ministry of Health, State Primary Health Care Development Agency, Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development, etc.)
The second level of partnership relates to SRH coordination and leadership across the sub-sector, ensuring supplies of the Inter-Agency Reproductive Health Kits, as well as supervising the management of utilization and quality of services, including mentorship and capacity building.

1 - Needs

The crisis in Northeast Nigeria has been ongoing for 13 years, leading to the dire need for essential SRH services. The region of Nigeria has the highest maternal mortality in Nigeria according to a recent 2022 smart survey, estimated at 1849 per 100,000 live births, which is higher than the national average of 512 per 100,000 live births. According to the WHO/UNFPA report, a third of these mortalities can be averted by ensuring access to and using modern contraceptives. According to the multiple indicator cluster survey (2022), the modern contraceptive use among married women in Northeast is around 11%, while the unmet need for family planning is 28.3% of which the unmet need is the highest in Borno State with 35.2%. Due to the pre-existing inequality, traditional norms and harmful practices compounded by the humanitarian crisis, women and girls, including adolescents and people living with disabilities (PWID), are more disadvantaged by the events which leave them more vulnerable to be able to exercise their fundamental rights. In addition, natural disasters, such as devastating floods, have resulted in thousands of new displacements, and subsequent cholera and Diphtheria outbreak has placed an extra burden on the already weakened public health system. The Floods crisis has exacerbated the vulnerabilities of people affected by the conflicts and displacements. According to the NEMA, the floods have affected over 210,000 people in the Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States in 2022. The living conditions in the displacement sites, such as overcrowding, insecurity, lack of privacy and inadequate/unsafe WASH facilities, lack of shelter, malnourishment and lack of health functioning facilities, have resulted in increased stress. Therefore, it is noticed that there is an increasing need for humanitarian assistance, including the MHPSS services in the North-East of Nigeria, where the people are already struggling to access quality health care services. Maternal mental health and distress during pregnancy and immediate postpartum have negative impacts for both mother and baby. WHO states that 1 in 5 women will experience a mental health condition during pregnancy or in the year after the delivery. The UNFPA assessment on MHPSS also reported the need to strengthen integration of MHPSS as part of SRH service provision, since many of the frontline health service providers are not aware nor trained on mental issues around SRH. Currently, the number of people in need of health assistance is 5.3 million, and 3.6 million will be targeted in 2023, of which 1.3 million people are women and girls of reproductive age (15-49 years) and 641,148 adolescent girls (10-19 years) who are most in needs of SRH information and services. (projected by MISP Calculator)Moreover, 144,258 currently pregnant women will give birth in the next 12 months, who will require lifesaving support and referral services. The insufficient infrastructure, the gaps in medical commodities and staff capacities are the identified challenges of the healthcare system to meet the population's demand. The funding had been a significant challenge in 2023, primarily for procurement of family planning related kits and delivery to the local and international NGOs and government partners for the uninterrupted SRH service provision. As a result, some health facilities and humanitarian organizations had some gap in SRH-related commodities. Despite the significant progress made in the past years, there are still notable gaps in SRH service provision and improvement of access across the BAY States.

2 - Activities

The proposal focuses on the following Activities.1. Procurements, prepositioning and supplies of the standard Inter-Agency Sexual Reproductive Health Kits in crisis and delivered them at the last mile to all identified Health facilities within the BAY States. UNFPA will ensure that the distribution plan is prioritized on need bases in collaboration with key stakeholders and that the utilization is well monitored. In addition, supplies of the SRH will ensure the beneficiaries have access to timely health care services, which is crucial for women and girls to avoid the three delays in maternal mortality. 2. UNFPA Strengthening SRH coordination within the standard sub-sector framework to ensure the delivery and access to quality SRH and GBV services; This also includes Hosting regular SRH / CMR Task Force Coordination meetings with all relevant stakeholders to facilitate coordinated action to ensure the implementation of the SRH. In tandem with health/GBV/HIV coordination mechanisms, mapping and analysis of existing SRH services, supportive supervision, monitoring and mentoring local partners and government agencies implementing SRH and leading joint inter-agency assessment. UNFPA will prioritize Health system strengthening and ensure planning for comprehensive SRH for a more structural response phase for the humanitarian-development nexus3. Training and capacity building of Local Health Care providers/organizations on Minimum Initial Ser Package (MISP), Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), Emergency maternal and neonatal Obstetric Care, Family Planning (FP), Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) within BAY State and most especially new areas of displacement or return. Training primary health workers to collect information on maternal death and review for the facilitation of targeted implementation to address the underlying cause of maternal death4. Provision of essential specialized GBV Integrated SRH services in IDP camp clinics, Referral centers and Mobile medical outreaches in hard-to-reach communities. Services include adolescent Sexual Reproductive Health services, CMR, management of Obstetric Complications and contraceptives. Consideration for people living with disabilities to access SRH services. Provision of technical guidance on reinforcing the infection control measures and universal precautions as guided by MISP for SRH service provisions in the health facilities and the clinics to ensure the critical SRH life-saving SRH services are continued even in the context of the flood and cholera outbreak5. Community engagement and improved awareness of Key SRH issues to create demand and promote accountability to the affected population. Promotional messages in local spoken languages will be developed and shared, ensuring feedback from the community members is considered. Behavioral change initiatives to mitigate harmful traditional practices and promote the acceptability of life-saving SRH services. UNFPA will continue to coordinate and work with the State Government in the BAY States and partners to prioritize the SRH needs of women and girls, including adolescent girls and boys, and Persons with Disabilities (PWD) for inclusivity , ensuring no one is left behind.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure that the concerns of women and girls are addressed from the beginning of the project, focus group discussions (FGDs) with them will be organized. Community mobilization and awareness activities will also ensure the participation of beneficiaries during the implementation of the project. Additionally, the project will also strengthen the integration of Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) coordination mechanisms to ensure that protection concerns are taken into account in the health programming. To prevent Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH), all community mobilizers, including volunteers, will be trained on the principles of PSEAH, and the community will be made aware of it.
