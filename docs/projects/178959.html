Project Summary

This program aims at supporting the resilience of the conflict affected people in northwest
Syria, particularly in Idleb and Aleppo governorate. Shafak's proposed is designed to
contribute to supporting the transition to the early recovery phase, as it supports the
targeted communities with long-term assistance activities, programs that focus on
building/restoring the communities' infrastructure, local economic empowerment, and
initiatives that ensure engagement and participation of the community in recovering from
the protracted crisis. This program will achieve its objectives through the following:
a) Providing short-term work opportunities through Cash-for-Work activities to
3500 HHs.
b) Establishing or expanding Small-Micro Enterprises through provision of business skills training and financial or in-kind support to 1000 selected beneficiaries from IDPs and Host communities. Grant total will be between 600-1000 USD per beneficiary.
c) Providing vocational and skills training to 800 youth, men and women applicants and
then support successful trainees (Expecting 500 trainees) with small-scale cash grants to
enable them restart/initiate their small enterprises, each grant total will be between 600-1000 USD per
beneficiary.
d) Rehabilitating the local economic, public and social infrastructure (15 Projects) through
linking with the Cash-for-Work activity or through service providers. Each project cost will
be about 15000 USD
e) Improve access to basic services and Markets through construction of Markets, Roads, rehabilitation Electricity and Water net work, Community led priorities through initiatives. 4 markets will be established
e) Supporting local community and social cohesion initiatives.
f) VSLA: Establishment of Village Savings and Loan Associations VSLA: an estimated 25 Savings and Loan Groups, each consisting of approximately 10-15 members, will be facilitated to establish. Shafak will follow VSLA methodology, developed by CARE, however, this will be adapted to the Syria and emergency context. Community volunteers will be selected directly from the targeted communities as Savings and Loan Group Facilitators and trained on the formation of the groups. Gender and protection norms will be considered, the groups will constitute a common fund for members to save and borrow to finance a business or pay for living expenses. Groups will follow a standard savings and loan groups methodology, however, this will be adapted to the Syria context. Participants will elect their own group leaders, set their own rules.

4 - Needs

The ten years of the crisis has impacted peoples’ negative coping mechanisms and led to widespread poverty a poor economic context, low employment levels, limited access to livelihoods and income generating sources. The crisis in Syria continues to strain the socio-economic foundations of Syrian people, thereby affecting their ability to sustain adequate standards of living, seek decent livelihood opportunities, and access essential infrastructure and services. These factors have deepened poverty and weakened communities’ resilience to cope with internal and external shocks. An estimated 83% of Syrians live below the poverty line. In northwest Syria (NWS), approximately two million people rely on monthly food assistance due to persistent conflict in Idleb and Aleppo. . In NWS, poverty has been exacerbated by the escalation of armed hostilities that have limited Syrians’ access to markets, the agricultural output of farming businesses, and sharply reduced levels of food security and increased unemployment. Lack of access to sufficient electricity has constrained, for instance, the supply of clean drinking water, management of essential health services and a sense of safety in private and public spaces after sunset, particularly for females. Geographically, 72 sub-districts show a severe shortage of electricity. Likewise, the assessment indicates that up to 25 per cent of basic social infrastructure remains destroyed, negatively impacting provision and availability of and sustained access to essential services such as physical access, water supply, sewerage, energy, and solid waste management The opportunities and abilities for the affectedpopulation to end

5 - Implementation of activities

Shafak has its implementation approach with regards to the early recovery projects, Shafak starts its programs with community mobilization activities, orientation sessions about the project activities, approaches and assistance modalities. For the CFW, this proposed action will work with communities, to identify priority projects in the community that would help restore or protect assets, particularly related to livelihoods, and public infrastructure. Unskilled laborers will be employed for a duration of 20 days in each round, they will receive wages on rates agreed as per the Cluster's guidance. Regarding small business activities, Shafak aims at supporting a group of selected applicants with vocational training, receive their business plans, based on the evaluation of which a narrower group will receive the cash small grant. Both CFW and small business cash support will be in the voucher-based approach. As for rehabilitation of infrastructure SO will identify 20 top community projects to be implemented through service provider or.Shafak will insure proper inclusion of PWD and Women on two levels: 1) prioritization: Automatic inclusion of women and PWD will be ensured as those two groups among the most vulnerable people, 2) Designing of activities: By conducting proper assessment to identify the barriers and opportunities for wider inclusion, Special types of activities will be in place to ensure safe access and participation of those groups considering the level of impairment for the PWD and considering the culture norms and sensitivity of the context when designing activities for women and girls including having CFW,VT and SMEs that suit the women capacity and ensure women more empowerment and participation.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In all of its program, SO conducts a risk analysis to identify the internal and external risks associated with the proposed and identify actions that could help mitigate the risks for vulnerable people including women and PWD. The following risks mitigation measures: 1-Interference and Aid diversion by Armed Opposition Groups: As mitigation measures Shafak will do the following: Share the distribution plan with safety and security department to get clearance and advice. Train staff to make them prepared for like scenario, negotiating with this group in a manner to avoid jeopardizing the safety of staff, Make Sure Complaint mechanism active and accessible by all people. Stopping the distribution and withdrawal from the community then report the subject case to our donor/ OCHA/ Access working group.3. Forgery of vouchers; The security features included in the vouchers will prevent these attempts. 4. Deterioration of security (military, civil: riots, bombing, airstrikes) in the operational area; ending the process and evacuate the area from staff and beneficiaries and reschedule the distribution dates. 5. Beneficiaries re-displaced; By making to list of beneficiaries Most Vulnerable and less vulnerable, to replace the re-displaced with another from the List 6. Sexual exploitation and abuse by the project personnel Mitigation (refer to PSEA strategy section) 7- Risks on Accountability: Shafak will work to enhance and streamline existing accountability mechanisms with a greater focus on transparent information sharing, and efficient complaints & feedback mechanisms. Information sharing will focus on beneficiary entitlements, services available through the project and how to channel feedback and complaints.
