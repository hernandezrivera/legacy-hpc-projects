Project Summary

Reduction of child protection cases in Slemani province (Darbandixan, Slemani City, Chamchamal, Rania, Halabja districts) through prevention and response interventions.
Specialised Child protection case management for 400 children, 200 boys, 200 girls, in non camps in Slemani province through mobile teams of trained social workers. Purpose is to reduce the number of child protection cases in the province and increase access to child protection services.
Awareness on child protection principles for 25,000 community members in Slemani Province, 10,000 adults including parents and 15,000 adolescents, with special attention to equal participation of women, men, girls and boys, on common child protection issues according to concerns prevalent in the communities such as access to education, child marriage and child labour. Purpose is to strengthen knowledge and understanding of community of CP issues to contribute to prevention, changing behaviours and response for a reduction of child protection cases.
Strengthening and supporting 20 community based structures, 10 adult structures, 10 adolescent groups and training 200 individuals, 100 adults, 100 adolescents in CP for a sustainable approach to protecting the rights of a child.
Training a further 100 individuals, 25 NGO staff, 25 government staff and 25 non governmental adults and 25 adolescents on CP principles for a sustainable response for a stronger and better equipped governmental and community workforce to respond to and mitigate child protection risks.
Total target is 25,700 individuals in Slemani province over 12 months.

Needs

According to the HNO 2019 protection cluster, Slemani province is an area where IDPs in need of protection assistance are concentrated in, based on IOM DTM data July 2018. All the activities proposed will be implemented in Slemani to address childrens protection needs in this province. Amongst IDP children, child labour, child marriage and other poverty induced child protection risks and violence at home are high in prevalence according to the (MCNA VI). This project will directly address children with child protection concerns such as those mentioned above through comprehensive case management services for children by trained social Slemani province. Commonly, non camp IDP s find it harder to access CP services therefore through our mobile teams and community interventions we hope to identify and manage children out of camp with child protection risks in the priority districts of the province (Chamchamal, Slemani City, Rania, Halabja, Darbandixan). Childrens needs under 18yrs differ between communities, locations, genders, ages. In an assessment by KSC, 82% of child labour cases were boys, and 77% are children aged 0-13yrs, 22% aged 14-17yrs whereas typically girls encounter child marriage more than boys. Children under 5yrs cannot typically communicate their needs, and children 5-16yrs should be enrolled in and have access to formal education. In accordance with the law, it is illegal for children under 15yrs to work and children working 15-18yrs should be supervised and in no hazardous environments. The law also states child marriage is illegal. Culturally girls have a quieter voice in the community and this is the same for mothers too. Fathers are usually the main decision makers of each family so it is essential to consider the roles of mothers and fathers when addressing the needs of children. Both genders have a high prevalence of school drop outs and no enrolment in formal or non formal education, usually due to financial constraints. Children with disabilities, both boys and girls have difficulty accessing services due to social or physical barriers. As mentioned, each district of the province faces different challenges and different prevalences due to the different administrations, organisational presence, geographical, historical, tribal, religious, racial and societal traditions in each district. In Darbandixan 22.7% of IDP households with children above 6yrs have had at least 1 child working in the past 30 days, 15.7% of households in Chamchamal, 12.7% of households in Rania and 10% of households in Slemani City (MCNA Vl September 2018). 35.9% of IDP households in Chamchamal 26.7% in Darbandixan, 23% in Rania, 19.6% in Slemani city 16.3% in Halabja have at least one child 6-17yrs not enrolled in formal or non formal education. 2.4% of household with children 12-17yrs in Slemani City have at least 1 child marriage, 1.5% of IDP households in Halabja, 1.4% of IDP households in Rania, 1.3% of IDP households in Darbandixan. Although the DTM protection data has not separately assessed child exploitation, KSC is continually referred cases of abuse and exploitation such as cases of children in need of healthcare exploited for cash assistance.Aside from these, psychological distress is also immensely prevalent in the district and should be addressed. As evident, geographical location, gender and age of children are factors that must be considered when dealing child protection issues in the community. A lack of child protection actors with mobile case management services have exasperated the situation and left cases unattended too and a large number of cases referred to our organisation without the resources to attend to. Awareness for parents and adolescents in all districts to address societal barriers and a strong response. Community structures support and training is not readily available in this province with the reduction of recent funding and downsizing of national and international actors therefore past efforts have been somewhat unsustainable. Activities

Individual child protection needs for 400 out of camp children, (200 girls 200 boys) under 18yrs will be addressed directly across the prioritized districts of the province through mobile teams qualified to perform comprehensive high-quality case management, to support children and risk and survivors in accordance with international standards. CP concerns include but are not limited to child labour, non-enrolment in formal or non-formal education, child marriage, exploitation, violence and abuse. The needs will alter according to children ages, genders, role of mothers, fathers or guardians and this will be addressed on a case by case basis by professionals.Cases of trauma will be referred to and monitored with existing PSS services in the areas. We aim to strengthen the PSS response in the community through community structure activities, particularly PSS module trainings and Youth/Child resilience trainings for a sustainable approach to PSS for children. The mobile team will respond to out of camp cases referred by other actors and coordinate efforts with CP sub cluster. The mobile teams will also be on hand for out of camp rapid CP needs assessments in case of disasters or emergencies given the unstable context.A separate team of social workers will engage the community and work on behavioural or societal CP barriers through awareness raising activities for 25,000 individuals, (10000 adults 15000 adolescents) on CP principles in priority districts of the province, strengthening the communities response to CP issues and preventing occurrences of new CP concerns; especially those associated with disabilities, non enrolment in schools girls forced to marry, boys exploited to work, violence at homes and other often cultural norms. Schools would be the access points for students, teachers, social workers and parents. Community centres would be access points for school drop outs, youths and community members. Through an intensive schedule we would reach our target of 25000 community members. From recent awareness raising experience, participation is approx. 350 students per school per day, in one month we would reach 7000 children and 560 teachers. Community centres are spaces we use regularly for activities, so it is common for us to engage this target no. of community members.Societal barriers often come from parents however it is difficult for parents to commit time and efforts to parenting programs and often the parents most inconsiderate of CP issues refrain from such engagement. So as not to run the risk of disengagement or not targeting the parents of those children in need, we will take a subtle approach to parents/ caregiver participation; through the participation in their childs case management, through FGDs held for methods and priorities of awareness and community support, through awareness activities for parents in schools and community centers, and through direct participation in community structures. We hope to fully engage parents without highlighting any sensitivities but still get the CP priority messages across. We would be able to make parent referrals to other actors such as MC or IRC for specialized parenting programmes if or when required.The project aims to strengthen responses and preventions of CP risks and concerns through building the capacity of men, women, boys and girls by training 25 NGO staff (frontline workers), 25 governmental staff (social workforce), 25 community members and 25 adolescents on CP issues and childrens rights. Also, establishing and supporting 20 community structures (10 adult structures,10 adolescent structures), expected 200 individuals, in priority districts, to better respond to CP survivors and prevent CP risks for a sustainable reduction of CP concerns. Community structure support would include teacher trainings on integrating PSS in schools for long term support for students. KSC youth centers and childrens activity centres in Darbandixan, Rania, Slemani City, Chamchamal would be other community structures supported on PSS and youth resilience trainings to improve the quality of existing community programmes. CP cases with trauma, particularly those not in school, could be referred to these centers for ongoing PSS activities under monitor. Depending on concerns in priority districts, religious leaders- particularly for child marriage, and community leaders will be encouraged for participation in community structures.KSC has a strong establishment with other projects including child sponsorship, income generation, juvenile justice, healthcare assistance, PSS, 6 NFE centers, center for children with special needs, 9 youth centers. Where need be, internal referrals will be made to different departments to follow up with any indirect CP issues to have a greater positive impact on a childs life. The project will have a monitoring and evaluation plan and assessment to ensure the needs of the children are met and the impact of the project is on track.

Protection Risk Analysis

Humanitarian actors may incur security and safety risks travelling across the province, particularly the Garmyan administration where political and societal tensions can arise. Beneficiaries- particularly children under 18 may be at risk of restricted access to services or abuse if parents/ guardians and community leaders are not supportive of humanitarian interventions. Beneficiaries- particularly children survivors of child marriage, exploitation and abuse could be at risk of privacy exposure which could lead to other concerns. Misconduct is also alway a risk that exists with human resources.

Mitigation Measures

KSC has been established since 1991 and has a large network thereby reducing the risks associated with restricted access. We will ensure that our cars have the correct documentation and qualified drivers to mitigate any safety risks. We will ensure that we are regularly updated with any insecurities and instabilities in districts through our networks and staff already operating in the field in those locations. We will ensure that children, parents, governmental administrations, and community leaders are communicated with and engaged in the project for support for interventions. If at any time abuse or restricted access is suspected, KSC will follow protocols to manage the situation discreetly and efficiently. Specific GBV risks or concerns will be referred to GBV actors. Risks of case exposures will be mitigated through our data protection policy, non disclosure agreements, and general protection principles of do no harm. If at any time we feel exposure is a threat KSC will follow practise to disarm the situation and protect the beneficiaries at risk. The staff implementing this project will be closely monitored and supervised by staff with 15-20yrs experience and the project will be closely coordinated with the CP sub cluster thereby limiting any misconduct or risks.

How is your project accountable to affected population

This project is initiated based on the request for KSC to address the needs of multiple CP cases a month by parents, community members and also children whereby KSC is not currently in a position to respond to all these needs.Individual child protection needs for 400 out of camp girls and boys under 18yrs will be addressed directly across the entire province through mobile teams qualified to perform comprehensive case management to support children and risk and survivors. The needs will alter according to the ages of children, the genders, the role of mothers and fathers or guardians and this will be addressed on a case by case basis by professionals. Our project will consider the needs of children under 5yrs who cannot communicate their own needs through trained social workers. During the case management interventions, parents and children will be responsible for contributing to the case plan as per any proper case management protocol. Feedback forms on the quality of CM service will be made available for all beneficiaries to complete as per other KSC projects. These will then be analysed and taken into consideration during regularly project review. Before the awareness raising activities, FGDs will be held with each group district (adolescent boys and girls, adult and parent women and men) to better know which activities will be better heard and which CP principles will need to be more strongly focused on in the different districts. The awareness raising activities will be tailored to address women, men, girls and boys as seen fit after the FGDs. With regards to staff hiring, strong consideration will be given to affected populations and gender balance . Each training session will have a feedback discussion and feedback form to ensure the training is successful. The community structures will also strongly be based on affected population participation, influence and feedback since they would be part made up from affected populations and supported and trained to do so.
