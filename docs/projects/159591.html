Project Summary

The Somalia Food Security Cluster, showed that there has been a significant scale up of emergency food assistance since May 2019. 1.2 to 1.4 million people were reached each month from May to June, 1.9 million people were reached in July and planned assistance is expected to continue at similar levels in August and September. Even though sustained assistance is preventing worse food security there are still areas that are still classified as stressed and others are in crisis (IPC 3) such as Hiraan, Galgaduud and Jubas. Further reports show that many households are still recovering from the severe 2016/2017 drought or have been affected by conflict, and 2.6 million people remain displaced. Most of the displaced people are in crisis (IPC phase 3 or Stressed (IPC Phase 2). According to the FSNAU FEWSNET Post Gu Technical Release Up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps, high acute malnutrition, or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3)1 or worse outcomes through December 2019.In this Case Yme has designed a project that will respond to the existing food security gaps. The project will ensure that it averts the deterioration of the situation further. Yme will offer timely and appropriate assistance to the affected population in the southern and central regions of Somalia especially in Mudug. The target beneficiaries are from different livelihoods that have been affected by the drought and are in need of assistance. The interventions that we will offer will address the immediate needs of the people such as lack of food and other household needs. This will be done through providing food aid through vouchers to 450 Households (2700 beneficiaries) to the most vulnerable households. Also we will provide temporary employment as well as income by having cash for work activities before the rains in Lower Shabelle. The cash for work activities, will target 377 households and we providing 5USD daily rate to the laborers which will increase their income for two months. The infrastructures will contribute to provision of water and also facilitate transaction of businesses through feeder roads. Since most of these infrastructures such as canals, water catchments and feeder roads and not well maintained we will train the existing committees and if they are not there we will organize new committees to be in place. These activities will address the immediate food security needs and support the communities to create assets that will strengthen their livelihoods. The advantage of Cash for Work is that it empowers the beneficiaries to buy food or other necessities for immediate use and according to the priorities of the individual. Yme will further work with the communities to build their resilience by supporting the farmers in the targeted regions. Yme will provide agricultural inputs that is crop and vegetable seed to 520 households as well as land preparation of 1 hectare per farmer before the rains. The farmers will receive improved farming techniques training on how they can improve their crop production. In most of these activities we will target at least 60% women headed households as they are the most vulnerable groups in the community. Yme has a technical vocational training centre in Galkacyo which has graduated over 600 students and over 80 percent are estimated to be in gainful employment. Young unemployed men and Women in Galkacyo will be targetted to be trained on selected vocational training to help them start their own businesses. By supporting the young people we will be empowering them to become self-reliant and responsible men and women in the future.

Project Activities

Community mobilization will be conducted at the start to create awareness on the project including selection criteria involved. The activities that will be carried out will be the provision of food aid through vouchers which includes sugar, oil, rice and pulses to 450 households (2700 beneficiaries) with over 50% going to women headed households. We will target 200 pastoralists and do restocking of 2 goats and 2 sheep in each household. We will target 520 Households (3120 beneficiaries) with agricultural inputs of crop, legume and vegetable seeds together with urea. Then 220 farmers will receive 1 hectare land preparation and selected 150 farmers will be trained on Good Farming Practices.Creating temporary job opportunities for 377 households through rehabilitation of irrigation canals, water catchments and road rehabilitation. 85 Unemployed youths in IDP areas will be selected to be trained vocational skills.

Mitigation Measures

Yme sees some potential risks during implementation and has also though about some mitigation measures:2b. Exclusion and marginalization: Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processesYme will ensure that during project inception as well as during formation of committees women will be included in the decision making processes. This is a hard target for project implementation and it is expected to yield the desired results which will be that specific project activities are better achieved because of women participation. Boys and girls are also consulted when relevant.3b. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance.Yme has strict procedures regarding beneficiary selection and Yme staff is instructed to adhere to the code of conduct which does not allow to ask favours for assistance. There is also a hotline with a staff outside the project at the other side of the line. During the inception phase of the project beneficiaries are informed about the project and what they can expect and also that there is never any payment towards humanitarian staff. 3c. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and servicesYme will conduct most activities during daytime and in the mornings so that beneficiaries who may need to travel to areas where there are activities, are there during the more secure times of the day. The locations will also be selected with the community, including women to ensure the more secure areas are used for project activities. 4b. Protection mainstreaming: Beneficiaries risk exposure to violence during registration/distributionDuring the inception phase a list of beneficiaries will be made with the community representatives including women based on the selection criteria of the project. Also all groups will be in this list, including men in order to do no harm and cause tension. 4d. Protection mainstreaming: Barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanismsAs mentioned before, a hotline with a staff member not directly involved in this project will be established. This phone number will be communicated to all potential beneficiaries at the start of the project. Yme has clear procedures on how to go about any complaints with a beneficiary centred approach where always a final conclusion or result will be communicated.

Yme will involve the key stakeholders to participate in the programming so that together we can find durable solutions that will benefit both the host communities and the IDPS.
