Project Summary

The Health systems and indices in the Northeastern Nigeria has been severely devastated by the years of violent conflict that left devastations on the health infrastructure, looting of equipment and massive displacement of health workforce. The risk of disease outbreak has more particularly increased in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe, affecting 14 million people with 1.4 million Internally Displaced People (IDP) and 5.2 million people in need are taking shelter in overcrowded and unsanitary camps and dwellings with limited or no access to clean water and adequate sanitation. The ongoing conflict and continued displacement has a serious impact particularly on the most vulnerable population including women, unaccompanied/ separated children, adolescent boys, pregnant/ lactating women and abductees. Since the declaration of humanitarian Emergency in the Northeast states, health service delivery has focused majorly on the delivery of rapid emergency lifesaving healthcare services through the temporary health structures and mobile teams. The result is that most of the health infrastructures still remains in dire need of rehabilitation and full restoration of health services with outbreaks of cholera and other epidemic disease increasing due to lack of infection prevention and control structures, effective waste management, water as well as sanitation facilities . The Health systems availability monitoring systems (HeRAMS) estimates that only a marginal improvement in functionality of the health infrastructure has been achieved between 2016 and 2018 across the 3 states especially in Borno state.
This project aims to restore comprehensive healthcare service delivery to the most vulnerable displaced population and returnees including women and children in the North east states of Borno, Yobe and Adamawa. The program will focus on revitalization of sustainable basic service delivery in the areas of health, the full rehabilitation and equipping of health facilities would be carried out based on the minimum standards provided by the National Primary Healthcare Development Agency (NDPCDA) and adopted by the state ministries of Health with the concept of building back better. Health training institutions would also be strengthened to produce quality health workers who would be engaged and deployed to the rehabilitated health facilities through provision of equipment to the school practical demonstration rooms, classroom furniture, library equipment, office equipment and other teaching aids.
This project will support the following:
• Rehabilitation of health facilities as per the Nigerian MoH norms and standards, and best practice recommended by WHO ( 8 primary health centres in Borno, and 4 primary health centres each for Adamawa and Yobe states )
• Procurement and distribution of medical equipment and (limited and time-bound) supplies for 16 rehabilitated health care centres.
• Establishment of standard waste management systems for infection prevention in 40 primary healthcare facilities in Borno Yobe and Adamawa
• Provision of equipment and teaching aids for the 6 departments of college of health technology
• Assessment of Workload indicator of Staff Needs
• Support the HDN sensitization Meeting with stakeholders and partners at the national level
• Set up a technical working group on HDN in the health sector in the three states on the North East Nigeria
• Support the HDN coordination meetings at the LGAs in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states

Needs

Health infrastructures in the north east have been adversely affected by the activities of the insurgents who often target both the physical structures and the health workers leaving behind a totally devastated health facilities, looting of equipment and supplies, and displacement of staff which has resulted in almost complete health system collapse and lack of provision of health service outside the few towns within the secured areas, that are dependent on humanitarian support. Ensuring full rehabilitation of health infrastructures in a number of primary and secondary health facilities, equipping the health facilities and training of the health staff would help in restoring comprehensive health care service delivery and contribute to the overall resilience of the affected populationHuman resource is one of the greatest challenge in achieving the health sector service delivery goals especially in Borno state. The displacement of health workers from the state leading to acute shortage of health workforce has a far reaching implications on the restoration of health services in the state. Shortage and maldistribution of health professional already existing pre-insurgence has worsened during the crisis, with most staff leaving the conflict affected states and LGAs. Borno state’s health workforce ratio is only 5.8/10,000, grossly inadequate compared to the WHO standard of 23/10,000. Shortages of skilled HR for all cadres cut across from basic skills to specialist level, such as radiologist mental health professionals. There is shortage of health workers both in the quantity and the right mix of the professional categories. For instance 90% of the health facilities in Borno state especially in rural communities lack the right mix of professionals from the various departments such as pharmacy, radiology, Laboratory, Environmental health, health information etc. The implication is that the few health workers are forced to undertake tasks for which they are not trained and this also further increases their workload and demotivates the few health workers serving in this most challenging context.Furthermore, the current security challenge has negatively influenced the production, management and development of the much needed human resources to serve in the state. The current administration of the state has identified Human Resource for Health (HRH) as a major impediment to achieving its health goals, with the health training institutions losing accreditation, the production of the required health workforce has become a challenge. As a result of the low capacity and quality of the state health professional schools, health professionals trained in Borno state are considered sub-standard. Some nursing and midwifery schools as well as the colleges of health technology with the six (6) departments are not accredited and produce sub-standard health professional. Borno plans to include laboratory, radiology, physiotherapy, etc. trainings when upgrading their nursing schools to health professional colleges. Borno is seeking support for this upgrading and achieving accreditation standards, which includes capacity building of trainers, revision of training curricula, provision of training equipment, and infrastructure. They intend to train mainly locally recruited students with knowledge of local language and culture and increased changes of remaining in the state their LGAs of origin.The overall goal of the Humanitarian Development Nexus(HDN)- within the health sector would be to promote the implementation of the New Way of Working(NWOW) between Humanitarian and Development partners/stakeholders: facilitate dialogue and coordination amongst key health sector humanitarian and development partners to harmonize financial and technical support for the implementation of a common collective outcome to end need, reduce risk and vulnerability in the North East; avoid duplication and parallel processes; promote synergies in joint analysis, joint plan

Activities

WHO will in collaboration with the state ministries of health in the three states of Borno, Yobe and Adamawa rebuild, repair or renovate 16 primary health facilities that are linked to secondary health facilities through a functional and free emergency referral systems for each of the LGAs. The selection process will be done in close consultation with the SMoH and SPHCDA of the three states informed by the ongoing Health Resources Availability Mapping (HeRAMS). There is an ongoing (HeRAMS) exercise which commenced in June 2018 and the preliminary findings shows only a marginal improvement has been recorded in terms of rehabilitation of health infrastructures across the three states of the north east. This HeRAMS data will serve as a basis for the selection of health facilities for this project and for the other partners involved in health infrastructure rehabilitation. Consultative meetings will be organized regularly with key partners involved in rehabilitation/construction of health facilities. In addition progress on implementation will be discussed during the cluster meetings to avoid duplication and share best practices. All facilities will be rehabilitated and equipped following national recommendations, guidelines and standards.Key activities under this output are: • Identification of primary health facilities for rehabilitation• Rehabilitation of health facilities as per the Nigerian MoH norms and standards, and best practice recommended by WHO ( 8 primary health centres in Borno, and 4 primary health centres each for Adamawa and Yobe states )• Regular Supervision of rehabilitation and continued monitoring of the operational status of the health facilities, including inventory control. • Procurement and distribution of medical equipment and (limited and time-bound) supplies for 16 rehabilitated health care centres.• Establishment of standard waste management systems for infection prevention in 40 primary healthcare facilities in Borno Yobe and Adamawa• Design of refresher training courses for existing health staff• Monitor the provision of health care service delivery in the rehabilitated health facilitiesWHO will work closely with the SM0H to strengthen health human resources (HRH) management strategies and interventions in the SMoH and its agencies. This will include the establishment of Borno HRH information system, development of gender-balanced recruitment policies, staff redeployment systems including institutionalization of the Workload Indicator of Staffing Needs (WISN) tool to guide equitable redistribution of staff. WHO will also provide a package of assistance to the College of Health Technology Maiduguri in all the six existing departments such as Health Information management, Medical Laboratory, Community health, Environmental Health, Pharmacy, Radiology as well as the staff offices. The assistance package will entail classroom furniture, science laboratory equipment, library equipment, books and teaching aids as well as minor rehabilitation of the buildings and offices where applicable.Key Activities under this output are: • Provision of equipment and teaching aids for the 6 departments of college of health technology• Assessment of Workload indicator of Staff NeedsWHO will seek to promote the implementation of the New Way of Working(NWOW) between Humanitarian and Development partners/stakeholders. this would promote synergies in joint analysis, joint planning, joint implementation, joint monitoring and review of health sector programs; trengthen/reinforce local/State capacities ;and provide consistent policy inputs to the Governments of the 3 North East States experiencing protracted crisis. The key activities would be to: • Support the HDN sensitization Meeting with stakeholders and partners at the national level• Set up a technical working group on HDN in the health sector in the three states on the North East Nigeria• Support the HDN coordination meeting at the LGAs
