Project Summary

Health: The integrated primary health care interventions will be provided with key objective to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with disease among the children under five years, men, women, adolescent boys and girls, women of child bearing age, elderly men and women and people with disability. Alight has been implementing a comprehensive integrated primary health care life-saving intervention supporting 24 primary health care centers in South and East Darfur. Under this project, Alight will continue to provide basic primary health care services; treatment and diagnosis of communicable and non-communicable diseases, reproductive health, support of referrals for both medical and obstetric emergencies to tertiary level, emergency preparedness and response, disease prevention activities through health promotion activities by community volunteers, routine immunization, provision of essential drugs, medical and non-medical supplies, clinical management of rape to gender-based violence survivors and provision of Psychological First Aid (PFA) services. Alight will also strengthen community-based approaches to promote positive health-seeking behaviors, recognition of illnesses, and care-seeking practices through trained community structures, community relief development committees and health, nutrition and WASH volunteers network. The project will strengthen institutional capacity through on-the-job training and mentoring to implement sustainable and affordable health interventions that meet the needs of men, women, boys, girls, children, elderly, and persons with special needs.
Nutrition: The nutrition project is designed to meet the needs of nutrition services for internally displaced people and vulnerable host community in Belil, Alsalam, Gereida and Dimso localities in South and Assalaya, Adilla, Abukarinka, Elferdous and Yassin in East Darfur states. ALIGHT will provide nutrition services integrated to the primary health care facilities supported by ALIGHT in South and East Darfur states. The nutrition activities will be implemented in an integrated approach with health, WASH and protection. The goal of this project is to contribute to the reduction in nutrition-related mortality and morbidity, and improve access to high-quality multi-sectoral life-saving nutrition interventions for children under five and pregnant and lactating women who are above the emergency threshold. The project will support 24 Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programs, 24 Outpatient Therapeutic Programs and 3 Stabilization Centers (SC) including referrals to secondary health care and provision of essential drugs. ALIGHT will follow CMAM approach to implement the intended interventions. ALIGHT will use the mother support group strategy to promote appropriate Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices and Social Behavior Change and Communication (SBCC) among the target communities. ALIGHT will also enhance the technical capacity of its staff and Ministry of health staff through conducting training on CMAM package, IYCF and training on monitoring and reporting of the nutrition activities. All activities will be implemented in close collaboration with local institutions/ line ministries and communities such Community Relief and Development Committees (CRDC) to ensure sustainability and active community participation.

1 - Needs

The health system struggles to handle the multiple emergencies affecting the country. Health needs are exacerbated by the deteriorating economy, conflict, floods, and disease outbreaks. In September 2020, WHO classified Sudan as grade 2 acute health emergency (CRRP, 2021). Almost every year there are occurrences of diseases outbreaks such as cholera, chikungunya, dysentery, dengue, malaria, measles, diphtheria and rift valley fever, adding to the stress of an already-weakened and fractured health care system. With recently reported cases of Polio after almost a decade in eradication process, there is a need to reinforce epidemiological surveillance for timely informed interventions and strategic interventions to deal with shortages in immunization coverage in several areas. The supply chain of essential medicines and medical supplies remains inconsistent and the pandemic of COVID-19 worsen the situation. According to draft nutrition sector Humanitarian Response plan (HRP) 2022, The nutrition situation in Sudan in 2022 is projected to remain at elevated high levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) of 13.6 percent. The situation is most critical in 64 localities that have global acute malnutrition prevalence above the WHO emergency level classification of 15% and above, and 9 localities with GAM prevalence above 30%, classifying the situation as catastrophic. In addition to acute malnutrition, a recent sector analysis revealed high prevalence of stunting and micronutrient deficiencies, particularly iron-deficiency anaemia at 36% and 48% of under-fives, respectively. According to draft Nutrition Sector Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO)2022, the drivers of malnutrition are multi-sectoral that include high income poverty, poor WASH conditions leading to epidemics, limited access to health services, illiteracy and high food prices contributing to inadequate food intake and dietary diversity at household level. Inappropriate feeding practices and cultural norms affect children’s growth and development from an early age. A reported 29% increase in displacement due to floods or inter-communal clashes in 2021 compared to 2020 can exacerbate the main drivers of malnutrition as it disrupts affected communities’, access to health and wash services and therefore increasing risks to disease outbreaks and food insecurity due to limited access to farming land, availability, access to and utilization of food. COVID-19 continues to contribute to loss of livelihoods, disrupting access to food and the health care system. The overall nutrition sector people in need (PIN) has slightly increased by 8.8% from 3.6 million in 2021 to 3.9 million in 2022 with under-five children accounting for 77% and 23% pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) in need of life-saving treatment and preventive nutrition responses.A needs assessment survey conducted in the Alight target localities also highlighted challenges in the health and nutrition sector, putting men, women and children at higher risk. Challenges in medical supplies, lack of adequate human resources, inadequate and in some cases lack of infrastructure, lack of community awareness has affected provision of basic life-saving basic health care services. Poor investments in preventive services even makes the situation more complicated and harder to respond to outbreaks in time. Gaps in community engagement has affected provision of community interventions including nutrition. In additional, the 2021 Program reports show drop- out in nutrition programs mainly in hard to reach areas which is attributed to mobility of parents, and difficult to access the facility in rainy season, thus a need to reinforce the community engagement through community volunteers to help to trace and follow –up such cases at community level. There is a need for coordinated efforts to ensure integration and inter-connection of different sectors for successful results as well as strong monitoring and timely feedback.

2 - Activities/Outputs

To reduce morbidity and mortality rates, Alight will continue provision of life saving primary health care services following key primary health care pillars: patient consultations, maternal reproductive health, child health, community health awareness, capacity building, health information systems, reinforcing pharmaceutical supply chain, upgrading and maintenance of facilities infrastructure and equipment. Alight will build the capacity of health care providers and community based structure based on needs and gaps and this will be done through trainings, mentorship and supervision. Based on guidelines and protocols Alight will ensure cases that are beyond PHCCs capacity are referred to secondary health care services for timely management and where resources allow, referred patients will be supported to ensure the secondary health care are affordable. Immunization services will be provided as per national guidelines and to ensure high coverage, immunization outreaches for hard to reach areas will be organized. To ensure community health is promoted in both health and nutrition, Alight will continue to work with trained community volunteers as well as community leaders to ensure health promotion and disease prevention at individual and community level while awareness and vaccination campaigns will be organized to tackle key public issues including outbreak such as COVID-19. Alight will continue to implement curative and preventive nutrition interventions to Cu5 and PLWs. Services will be provided through 24 outpatient (OTP) and 24 targeted supplementary feeding (TSFP) and 3 SC centers into eleven localities in East and South Darfur. All MAM cases will be admitted to TSFP, and all SAM cases without medical complications will be admitted to OTP while critically ill, severely malnourished children with medical complications will be managed through SC and where Alight doesn’t have a SC, cases will be referred to nearest SC and care takers will be supported to afford the referral cost and their food.Alight will coordinate with UN agencies including WFP, UNICEF to ensure non –interrupted supply of nutrition commodities for different nutrition programs including OTP, TSFP, home fortification program, FBPM programs as well as Vitamin A, iron and folic acids. This include availing commodities, their transportation to targeted sites with prepositioning of supplies in remote areas prior to rain season, distribution and stock monitoring to prevent leakage as well as post distribution follow -up of beneficiaries.Alight will ensure community awareness and IYCN best practices are promoted though engaging community cadres such as community leaders, religious leaders, community volunteers as well as mother to mother support groups. For timely identification and management of malnourished children, Alight will implement active case finding through regular MUAC screening .For integration of persons living with disability, Alight will train frontlines staff on types of nutrition disabilities and good nutrition practices for this particular groups and awareness sessions will be organized at facility and community level on nutrition disabilities and available support.As part of integrated services, for health, screening of malnutrition /undernutrition will be conducted to children visiting clinics and pregnant and lactating mothers including growth monitoring in EPI with immediate referral to nutrition services for eligible persons, also IYCN will be integrated in maternal and child health services including ANC, maternity ward and PNC . For WASH integration, hand washing facilities will be availed at each facility and education sessions on hygiene organized regularly, also as part of integration , where possible latrines will be gender segregated while staff will be trained to identify beneficiaries with protection risks and refer them to appropriate services.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

ALIGHT places protection at the center of its programming by undertaking efforts forlifesaving activities throughout the project cycle and consistently working with theaffected communities to ensure all services are delivered based on needs withoutharming the target beneficiaries. ALIGHT has comprehensive and collective approachto mainstream protection in order to enhance the overall ability of our staff to analyze,prioritize and respond effectively to protection needs of the affectedpopulations including Refugees, IDPs, returnees and host communities. ALIGHTprovides special consideration to making its services (Livelihood, WASH, Health andNutrition services) accessible to everyone as well as persons with disabilities andpeople with specific needs and ensure their accessibility across the age categories andvulnerability scenarios, with special provisions for pregnant women/ new mothers andinfants, older, women headed families, widow’s orphans and severely ill and peoplewith disability. ALIGHT has a dedicated protection team to work with all thematicsectors to strengthen the capacity of staff in protection mainstreaming but not limitedto child protection, older persons, and people with disability, GBV prevention andpsychosocial support. The protection team will support project teams in adoptingpractical ideas for, protection of identified vulnerable people in all ALIGHT programs aswell as continuously monitoring protection outputs in all sectors.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

In regard to AAP: Alight will employ the AGD policy that will be implemented through community based, participatory approaches that are already well established in the communities. The Project will ensure that participation, feedback and complaints mechanisms are integrated in all strategies and plans all through the project cycle. This is to ensure the project decisions and actions are responsive to the expressed priorities, needs, capacities and views of all persons of concern irrespective of Age, Gender, Diversity and Disability backgrounds. This is aimed at ensuring meaningful participation, enhanced communication and transparency, improved feedback and response. ALIGHT will ensure a balanced boy, girls, women and men in all project activities with relative 60% female and 40% male. Data for beneficiaries will be disaggregated by gender, age groups and location, as the project activities are tailored for different groups in line with developmental stages, needs and vulnerabilities and basing on relevant parameters appropriate in reverence to heightened protection risks of the persons of concern. The Project will ensure at all persons of concern have equal and non- discriminatory access to protection, assistance and solutions, this will build on their resilience and recovery. The project will engage much better with boys, girls, men and women while in their safe spaces. Integration of other Alight protection projects will be enhanced such that activities like awareness raising, dialogues, will be integrated in our health and nutrition approaches. Alight will maintain effective feedback systems (including comments, suggestions and complaints) using a variety of communication channels that are accessible to all persons of concern and that are appropriate for both sensitive and non- sensitive feedback e.g. use of complaints boxes, toll free hot lines. Committees called CRDC’s will be engaged in all health and nutrition intervention planning and feedback. For nutrition, information on the services provided will be posted at nutrition facilities in accessible formats and local languages.All Alight staffs have committed to and signed PSEA policy, Child Protection policy, Code of Conduct as well as the engaged community based networks Alight is a member of National and State levels PSEA task force and regularly coordinates with other partners in relevant PSEA forums. Under the Project, we will conduct Safeguarding and SEA risk assessments to mitigate any potential or identified risks of SEA. Alight has contributed to Inter Agency Action Plan for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and will ensure Zero Tolerance - No Excuse to SEA in all its operations and project. Jointly with the PSEA task force, we will develop relevant SEA IEC materials and distribute them in communities, raise awareness on SEA, strengthen community based complaints mechanisms, enhance reporting and ensure appropriate measures are undertaken for investigations.
