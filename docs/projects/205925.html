Project Summary

This action seeks to address protection risks and increase the resilience of population at-risks, particularly returnees, refugees, IDPs and residents in the Greater Upper Nile region (Jonglei, Upper and Unity States), Lake, Eastern and Central Equatoria States. The project particularly targets vulnerable households, including female-headed households, children, families with persons with a medical condition, the persons living with disabilities, the elderly, and families with specific legal and physical protection needs, amongst others with;
1) General protection assistance, including protection risks monitoring and referrals, individualised protection assistance (IPA) and awareness raising on key protection concerns/risks;
2) GBV interventions implemented through needs and evidence-based static and mobile response focusing preventing and responding to protection risks of women, children, older persons and persons with disabilities;
3) support to the peaceful returns and resettlements of the IDPs, returnees and other persons of concerns through Housing, Land and Property (HLP) rights interventions.
By focusing on these core intervention pillars, the intervention contributes directly on reinforcing the general protection mandate through provision of life-saving general protection services for returnees, refugees, IDPs and other asylum seekers, particularly those forcefully displaced by the Sudan conflict as well as South Sudanese returnees from other countries.
Overall, the action reinforces the three-core strategic HRP objectives to reduce morbidity and mortality among vulnerable groups through equitable and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs; address protection risks of those facing protection issues; and enhance the resilience and peaceful co-existence of population affected by conflict and climate-induced shocks
(SO1) - Vulnerable people who experience multi-sectoral severity levels of 4 and 5 have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral essential services to meet their needs. This is achieved through general protection emergency and non-emergency activities of carrying out protection monitoring to identify persons with specific needs and respond through individualized protection assistance for the specific objective of general protection to “Reduce the vulnerability of women, men, girls and boys at increased risk of mortality and morbidity and protection incidents/threats in priority areas through protection monitoring, advocacy, awareness-raising and prevention and response services.”
(SO 2) - Vulnerable people are exposed to fewer protection threats and incidents, and those who are exposed have safe, tailored, timely and dignified access to appropriate services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action that promotes centrality of protection priorities. This objective is the major emphasis of our protection programing in both general protection, Gender-based violence and housing, land and property. The setting up of protection desks to identify GBV and other protection cases, safety audits of places of residence for the displacement affected persons to ensure safety and appropriate response to any protection risks. This ties with the GBV Specific Objective of “Reduce the suffering of girls, boys, women, men, the elderly, persons with disabilities, and other persons with specific needs at risk of or who experienced violence, including gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, exploitation, and neglect through the provision of specialized protection and multi-sectoral services”
(SO3) - Vulnerable people withstand and recover from shocks, have their resilience to shocks and stresses built, and seek solutions that respect their rights. Provision of HLP programming activities as part of solutions and ensuring that IDPs and returnees have access to lands and other HLP rights.

1. Needs

In the resident communities of the targeted locations, the South Sudan HNO 2024 established that women and children including older persons and persons with disabilities/chronic health conditions are likely to continue facing violence and abuses, socio-economic well-being concerns, physical security, along with limited health services and justice mechanisms, this is especially for those living in displacement sites and hard-to-reach areas. Likewise, the increasing returns presents significant challenges in terms of settlements solutions, particularly on the raising housing, land and property issues, compounding the already weak implementation of legal frameworks on Housing, Land and Property rights. Considering the unfolding conflict in Sudan causing the large-scale influx of returnees and refugees as well as recognizing the protracted nature of the crisis, and the need for durable protection solutions for returnees, refugees, IDPs and residents in Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Central and Eastern Equatoria states, HDC foresees direct live-saving protection assistances and durable solutions to 81,540 population at-risk, mainly (48,924 women, 9,418 girls, 7,705 boys and 15,493 men). The main focus will be; 1) The new arriving returnees from Sudan and other neighbouring countries; 2) IDPs living in protracted, including those newly displaced; (3) vulnerable resident communities affected by disasters and/or conflict and in locations with limited services. Specific actions will focus on mitigating GBV risks and supporting GBV/CRSV survivors with integrated package, addressing child protection issues and rights violations, including establishing and reinforcing community-based protection and reaching at-risk populations, customary and formal HLP structures with both awareness-raising sessions, trainings, Alternative Dispute Resolution mechanisms and individualized legal aid and counselling.Women in South Sudan are the ones majorly bearing the impact of consequences of conflict and failed economy in the country. They are left widowed or negligence from their husbands, to take care of the huge families that often includes orphaned children of their sisters or brothers.The protracted IDP situation and violent nature of the country breeds sexual and gender-based violence. Many lack proper jobs and resort to negative coping mechanisms that put them in conflict with their partners. Cases of adultery are increasingly common. Men continue to neglect their families and partners due to lack of proper jobs. The traditional norms discourage men from being stigmatized as weak when they do meaner chores to earn a living, yet there is decreasing number of white collar jobs in the country.Older persons and persons with disabilities are increasing disadvantaged; the schools have no brails, no one teaches sign language at school. To some older persons are seen as vulnerable rather than asset, a library to learn from the rich culture.This is just a brief over view of the nature of needs in South Sudan that require protection response as general protection, gender-based violence response and prevention and housing land property rights programming

2. Project activities

HDC’s 2024 protection response plan will focus on general protection, GBV, CP and HLP Areas of Responsibility. The proposed sub-sectors actions will ensure provision of critical protection related assistance through an integrated approach in addressing the priority needs of returnees, refugees, IDPs and residents, specifically focusing on the needs of women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach locations and areas with high returns, as well as protection risks. The action will as well provide the returnees and vulnerable residents with self-sustaining solutions, and increase their resilience to overcome shocks, enhance their safe and dignified environments with respect to their rights. Specifically, under General Protection, HDC will: 1) carry out protection monitoring, assessments and analysis in areas with high returns, hard-to reach locations, as well as informal settlement sites and resident communities. This will be done both using mobile and static response mechanisms, covering both non-technical analyses (NTA) and technical Analyses (TA) such as protection risk analysis, conflict analysis, safety audits, and gender and inclusion analysis to inform referrals, protection assistances and advoacy; 2) provide survivor-centred cash and non-cash Individual protection assistance (IPA) to address immediate protection risks, empower survivors as well as their caregivers; 3) strengthen referral systems and link survivors to available response services and support.For GBV AoR, HDC will undertake the following activities, 1) Carry out protection information and awareness raising activities focusing on GBV risks, available response services, GBV referral pathways, Sexual Exploitation and Abuses (SEA) prevention, and timely reporting); 2) provision of cash assistance (individual protection assistance) to persons at GBV survivors and other persons at heightened risks including referral of GBV survivors for response services; 3) training frontline workers and community focal points on basic GBV concepts, GBV guiding principles and responses services; 4)establishing/strengthening community-based protection structures such as the community-based protection networks and committees; 5)undertaking non-clinical GBV case Management (GBV) and (CP)case management, including provision of legal aid and counselling, psychosocial support through structural and non-structural mechanisms; 6) Provision of livelihood and economic support, including through Cash and Voucher Assistance to vulnerable women and girls and GBV survivors as part of a multi-sectoral GBV responses.Under Housing, Land and Property (HLP) AoR, HDC will implement the following activities, 1) carry out gender-sensitive awareness raising on social cohesion and Alternative dispute resolution mechanisms, land rights issues, and locally available services for beneficiaries on specific entitlements through legal aid and counselling; 2) undertake capacity building of the existing capacity of community structures through training community leaders and local authorities to increase understanding of HLP, the Legal Frameworks and practices in line with South Sudan HLP legal framework and International enactments; 3) Support 6 newly established National and State level Task Forces on Solutions with their advocacy at National and State levels on HLP issues and in supporting HLP solutions for those returning home spontaneously; and 4) Provide legal aid to most vulnerable returnees, and IDPs, particularly female-headed households, elderly-headed households and those headed by persons with disabilities to enable them address legal issues to acquire legal documents. This includes legal advice and representation to acquire property ownership documents or address legal disputes.
