Project Summary

Peace Corps proposes an integrated emergency education programme focused on provision of life saving safe, equitable access to protective and quality education interventions to conflict/flood affected vulnerable school going children and youth in Counties of Tonj East and Tonj North counties of Warrap State, Wullu County of Lakes State and Pibor County of Jonglei State. This project targets 9,206 beneficiaries of which 8773(4,858 boys, 3,916 girls) children and Youth aged 3-17 years and 435 adult (144women and 291men) teachers, PTA/SMC. This education project will incorporate provision of school facilities, referral mechanism, PSS and availability of gender-sensitive WASH facilities to deliver a comprehensive package of services. This project will run for a period of twelve months covering the three terms and the target will be 15 schools in selected counties.
Key activities of this project will include enrolling 8,773(4,506 girls) children in the learning centres, establishing/rehabilitating of 15 temporary learning spaces/classroom, providing 600 (360 girls) children aged 3-5 years with ECD kits, establishing 30 gender segregated latrines equipped with hand washing facilities, procuring and supplying of 30 sets of sanitation and hygiene materials(hand washing soap etc) to all the targeted schools, Providing emergency-affected 8,773 children with 15 sets of quality education recreational materials, acquire and distribute 164 boxes of teaching and learning materials to all the 15 targeted schools, providing sanitation and hygiene materials (MHM) to 1600 adolescents school going girls, training of 270 (98 female) teachers in EiE, pedagogical skills and child- cantered methodologies to improve teaching-learning process for emergency affected children, 270 (98 Female) teachers trained and updated on PSS and on the impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanism, 66(26 Female) teachers and education personnel trained on protection,GBV,COVID-19,health and Nutrition, Training of 600 (320 girls) youths and adolescent(14-17)years on life- skills and basic vocational, training of 165 (65 F)) community education committee members and local education officials school management, PSS and DRR, provision of appropriate IEC prevention and mitigation materials to 15 targeted schools, reaching population of 9208 teachers/children/PTAs/SMC and education personnel. Conducting 8 awareness creation campaign on back to school learning in Tonj East, Tonj North, Wullu and Pibor Counties. PCO will also set-up a simple, safe accessible community based complaints mechanism for reporting PSEA and protection of school children to add efficiency to strengthen reporting mechanism by incorporating local solutions into overall PSEA reporting system.
PCO is the education cluster Co-Lead in WBeG and currently implementing UNICEF funded construction of 10 Semi - Permanent TLS in 10 schools in Wau and Jur River counties since July 2022 to January 2023, Also on-going Education project targeting 5 schools under UNOCHA 2022 SA1 funding in Twic County being supported by SSHF 2022 and Multi Year Response Education Program (MYRP) project in 46 schools in Gogrial West and Tonj North Counties funded by Education Cannot Wait (ECW) since May 2020 to December 2022. PCO is also implementing FNS REPRO project in Jur River and Wau Counties of WBeG state being supported by FAO/USAID, Camp and Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) Project in Pibor GPAA of Jonglei supported by SSHF 2023. PCO in partnership with NPA has a current running FSL Program in Rumbek East County of Lake state supported by NORAD. PCO has maintained uninterrupted programmatic presence, strong network and linkages with the local authority in targeted counties since 2012 and proposed action will scale up Education initiatives. The organization has a functional and experienced emergency response team that has been working in coordination with the states as well as national and is better placed to respond.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education services. According to HNO 2021 report the education system and infrastructure in South Sudan are fragile, with limited equitable access to quality education for children and youth (age 3-17) who represent 44% of the population. This includes an estimated 3.4 million (51 per cent girls) school- aged girls and boys who are estimated to be out of school and have no access to attend normal education in 2021. The education system has been negatively affected and the main barrier to access education in 2022 includes protracted conflict, economic crisis, limited sanitation facilities at schools, displacement of teachers and children, killings of teachers and the risk of revenge killings of boys in schools, limited financial resources within communities to send their children to school, lack of learning and teaching material, lack of school fees and uniforms. The other challenge will be to ensure girls return to school, lack of and delayed teacher salaries and incentives results in teachers apathy, high turnover and poor quality teaching, girls face additional challenges, including cultural perceptions within communities that being educated would lead them to engage in criminal activities or risk becoming pregnant. According to UNESCO 2018 Report, at least 2.2 million children in South Sudan are not receiving an education representing one of the highest rates of out-of-school children in the world. 68% of schools in South Sudan lack proper WASH facilities according to Nationwide Education Cluster Assessment (November 2018), lack of proper WASH in schools exposes learners to risks of diseases and weak immune and exposing the community to be vulnerable to COVID-19 pandemic. The findings of the multi-sectorial need assessment conducted in October 2020 in Counties of Tonj East and Tonj South by partners indicate that there were destruction of education infrastructures as there are lack of access to learning spaces for a population of 66,101 school going children aged 3-18 years in Tonj East and Tonj South who learn under trees without WASH facilities, lack of teaching and learning materials, and low enrollment displacement as a result of the intra/inter communal conflict and floods, insufficient trained teachers on PSS, no trained SMC/PTAs members and Education Officials on roles, responsibilities and on DRR, no incentives to teachers. Humanitarian needs assessment conducted in Pibor by PAH between (16th and 18th February 2022) indicates that none of the schools are functional due to COVID-19 and hunger outbreak forcing families to search for food with their children and lack of teaching materials, 75% of the sample pupils interviewed in schools mentioned using stream and river water, 25% uses borehole water.This project is linked to the general strategy of saving lives and providing timely integrated multisectorial assistance and reinforcing protection, particularly connected to the 2022 education cluster strategy by ensuring that children and youth (3-17 years) have access to quality formal and non-formal education and that girls and boys already in school are retained, improved school attendance, increase of children, survival rates, and nutrition and food security by promoting emergency WASH in schools and mainstreaming child protection, psycho-social support, hygiene, and GBV. The project will also contribute to lower the risk of abuse and exploitation of children including early marriage, child labour and forced recruitment into armed groups. In addition, PCO will create synergies with Health, Nutrition, WASH, FSL and Protection among others.

2 - Project activities

The overall objective of this proposed plan is to provide equitable access to protective and quality education in emergency and learning outcomes across gender and disability for 8,773 children and youth children (45% girls) through implementation of the 3 cluster objectives. Objective 1: Improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 8,773 (4,506 girls) emergency and COVID-19 affected children including those with disabilities and youth (girls and boys) by 2022 in Tonj East, Tonj North, Wullu & Pibor Counties under IPC 3 and 4. Activities: 1. Enroll 8,773 (51% girls) children in schools/learning centers 2. Establish/rehabilitate 15 temporary learning spaces and classrooms. 3. Provide 600 (360 girls) children aged 3-5 children with ECD kits. 4. Establish and rehabilitate 30 gender-segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion 5. Distribute sanitation and hygiene kits (MHM) to 1600 adolescent girls. 6. Train 600(280 girls) adolescent and youth on life skill and vocational courses. Objective. 6. Improve the quality of education services at all levels and protect the well-being of 8,773 (4,506 girls) children, including children with disabilities and teachers' capacity to deliver quality services and prevent the negative impact of the COVID-19 crisis on learning environments by 2022 in Tonj East, Tonj North, Wullu & Pibor Counties. Activities: 1. Provide emergency-affected 8,173 (4,146 girls) children with 15 set of quality education recreational materials 2. Train 270 (98 female) teachers in EiE basic pedagogy and learners centered methodology and updated on PSS and the impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanisms to improve the teaching-learning process for emergency-affected children 3. Train 66(26 females) teachers and education personnel on referral pathways on protection/GBV, COVID-19, health and nutrition, 4. Train 600 (280girls) youths and adolescents (14-17 years) on life –skills and basic vocational. Objective 3: Strengthen the education system’s response capacity of the school community and education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergency and the transmission of COVID-19 pandemic on 8,773 learners. Activities 1. Train 165 (65 female) Community education committee members and local education Officials on school management, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) 2. Print and distribute appropriate Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials) to 15 targeted schools targeting 9,273 learners, Teachers, PTAs and Education personnel. 3. Organize 8 back to learning campaigns in Tonj East, Tonj North, Wullu and Pibor Counties. PCO will also ensure that normal routine monitoring of all the activities implemented in the 15 supported schools throughout the entire project life cycle by both PCO staff and other externally by stakeholders to ensure activities are in line with the project design document and are successfully achieved. Reporting will also be done as required donor and by the cluster through use of 5Ws and shared monthly.

3 - Project indicators

The project intends to measure its success through monitoring of the indicators and reports submitted upon implementation. The use of standardized tools and data collection tools will ensure data collection is of good quality. The analysis of this information will inform the indicators performance and therefore the determining success of the activity performance. Measuring the impact of the education intervention will be done by a robust MEAL plan developed to measure both outcome and outputs of the project in a quantitative and qualitative way. The measure of outcome results will include the collection of a baseline, to have a base against which to measure the success of the intervention. 7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

PCO has presence in all the targeted locations and hence there is already community engagement and trust with beneficiaries as well as the local authorities, who already had given some of the needs to help design this project. PCO will spend some time with beneficiaries at the inception of the project creating an awareness of the project and its goal including how to report complains and feedback and how they will be addressed. PCO will use the following Information sharing mechanisms such as monthly focus group discussions and community meetings to share information; weekly or bi-monthly announcements through notice boards, quarterly printing and distribution of leaflets and posters and monthly child participatory activities to share information and solicit feedback from children about the support they receive or the spaces they attend (using simple child participatory tools, such as the assessment to generate useful information for quick decision-making). The Project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members, through, Surveys and FGDs with specific age groups, women, men, students, youth out of school and potentially vulnerable or marginalized youth will be conducted on a regular basis. Quarterly focus group discussions will be conducted with children and communities to assess child protection knowledge, attitudes and practices; including focused FGDs with girls and young women with participatory assessment. Bi-annual public meetings will be organized to gain beneficiaries’feedback in a structured format, enabling different stakeholders to discuss and exchange views on specific topics, often adopting a face-to-face format which allows for brainstorming and testing ideas. In addition, in-depth interviews with individual stakeholders will be used to get a sense of stakeholders’ perspectives and key informants/stakeholder assessments will be used occasionally to explore an issue in depth and permit related issues to be introduced into the discussion. PCO will use its internal tools of checklists (pre-prepared lists of items to assess numerous activities rapidly, suitable for self-assessments, classroom observations and audit purposes), activity logs (records kept by teachers/administrators of specific activities such as instructional practices, learner-teacher interactions, learner behaviour and assessment of classroom layouts and infrastructure audits to keep track of its information-sharing strategy. PCO will use FGD/community meetings, help desks, suggestion boxes (including information on how the boxes will be handled) and child-friendly feedback mechanism. PCO will maintain and raise awareness on its Complaints and Feedback Mechanism. This mechanism will follow PCO guidelines, ensuring multiple methods for submitting complaints and receiving responses, including CFM boxes in schools and safe spaces as well as through static helpdesks. PCO’s Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) will provide continuous information and feedback on the quality of services provided, gaps and ways forward to strengthen the response. It will also be part of monitoring exercises in schools/TLSs and lead consultation meetings at school-level. Complaint desks with a focal point shall be made known to all stakeholders in targeted Counties of Warrap and Jonglei States to voice their concerns and the desk focal points shall make field visits at intervals to hear from the beneficiaries. During these visits women and girls shall be given priority to air out their views in complete confidentiality. PCO’s MEAL Team will identify risks, classify any high-risk activities, and document steps being taken to reduce or remove these risks for school children, develop and implement an action plan to ensure the child’s/children’s safety. Child-friendly information will be made available to ensure children understand about their safety and protection and feel comfortable about speaking to someone if they have any concerns.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

PCO will adopt a consultative approach in the implementation of this project and this will enable the implementing partners to receive feedback on the project progress. All feedback regarding project-related matters will provide self-reflection and providing actionable plans to ensure the project is adjusted to suit recommendations provided during consultation sessions. All the beneficiaries (men, women, boys and girls) will be directly involved in the entire response cycle from inception until exit and also be informed on the duration, activities and the roles of different stakeholders in the project. This will be done by ensuring a two-way communication and dialogue throughout the project. Details on recruitment and other arising issues will be communicated in forums such as public meetings and Focus Group Discussions which will be held with the different segregated groups of people (Men, women, girls and boys) to ensure community gets timely information which enhances active involvement and sense of ownership of the project. The local leaders among other relevant authorities and other key stakeholders will also be invited during project launch and since they are the representatives of the community, they will give the community information about the assistance to be provided. The local leaders and other community representatives will give their opinions and complaints as well as possible recommendations. Feedback obtained from project activities will be consolidated and analysed at the state and Juba level offices and feedback into the program unit used to realign activities to planned standard.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will establish a range of Beneficiary Communication and Accountability mechanisms to strengthen community engagement and feedback throughout the project life cycle. This will be developed during the project’s inception phase, and will be the primary responsibility of Project Management Committee comprises of diverse representatives such as pupils representative, PTA/SMC, Teachers and with support from project staff. In order to provide beneficiaries with information about PSEA, all training will incorporate elements of PSEA, indicating the engagement levels of the project staff with the community, reporting avenues available, their rights to benefit without being compromised. The project will Identify and minimize the risk of harm to children and adults from staff. The beneficiaries will be reminded that PCO staff interactions with children or with adult beneficiaries, or with personal data about such persons, will comply with the PCO’s Safeguarding Behaviour Protocols and PSEA policy. The project will Include GBV mitigation as part of staff training & conduct. Any incidents of harm or risk of harm to children or to adult beneficiaries will be reported immediately through channels that will be explained and demonstrated to the community. Beneficiaries will be reminded of all issues related to PSEA including reported cases being treated with utmost confidentiality, investigated and action taken. All actions taken will be discussed with the complainant after a specified time (feedback).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

PCO have developed a tool for feedback and complaint mechanism which we have been using in the previous similar projects and have always proven to be efficient. The local leaders and other community representatives will give their opinions and complaints as well as possible recommendations. PCO have locally recruited and trained Community Health Worker and Agriculture Extension Officer who vaccinate animals and train beneficiaries on agronomics respectively and are able to get feedback directly from the beneficiaries and they are addressed immediately depending on the urgency. We also have sessions with the beneficiaries during our field supervisions where we discuss the challenges, project impacts and the areas which they feel requires more concern and the best approach. We also have suggestion boxes where beneficiaries can drop their complaints for which will be registered in the complaint book and reviewed by Project Management Committee and give feedback to respective beneficiary. All the views from the beneficiaries are then screened and processed further and we use these criteria as a complaint/ feedback mechanism.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

PCO will develop mitigation measures for potential protection risks associated with implementing of this proposed project. PCO is mainstreaming protection in all its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such as PWDs, elderly, receive protection when they are providing information at any stage of the project cycle and that the whole process is confidential. In the event of closure of schools and disruption of learning as a result of social distancing guidelines as a measure to contain the spread of COVID 19, PCO will mobilize and sensitize the school community and stakeholders (teachers, students, parents’ committees) about COVID 19 disease through use of megaphones, house to house visits but maintaining the MoH standard of physical distance; develop, print and distribute appropriate IEC materials (i.e. posters, leaflets) on COVID 19 prevention and mitigation measures and train volunteers/teachers on distance learning and related methods. In the event of finding difficulties for women to participate in the intervention, PCO will conduct community outreach to explain the project’s objectives and the importance of engaging women and girls. Where safety in schools (and traveling to and from school) for girls, other vulnerable groups is the issue, PCO will include school safety in training for CEDs and PTAs; provision of toilets in schools and/or establish community support for school participation, for example by encouraging community mechanisms that arrange to accompany groups of children to school if routes are considered unsafe. Risks of women, girls and boys without and with disabilities exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance, Mitigation measure: staff will have to sign the code of conduct when signing the contract and before activities start all teams will receive PSEA and Child protection training. Additionally, specific PSEA complaint mechanisms will be put in place through the PSEA focal points within the CRM committee to ensure complaints reach Juba and the internal audit as early as possible. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Mitigation measures: in each CRM it will be ensured at least on representative of each marginalized group is represented in the committee to guarantee feedback from these vulnerable groups is received. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.). PCO will establish help desks as well as EIC material aiming at raising awareness on protection risks specially GBV and CP as well as referral pathways to the services available will be made available in every school. PCO, together with other agencies will ensure that the meeting sites or locations are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Barriers to access particularly the meeting locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups (constituencies) before it will be agreed on where everyone is comfortable to access.
