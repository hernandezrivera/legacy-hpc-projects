Project Summary

The 2019 Gu rains were the 3rd driest on record since 1980s, resulting in widespread crop failure and accelerated decline in livestock productivity, pushing communities in the worst-affected areas into acute food insecurity phase or worse. A spike in drought-induced displacement continues as rural communities flee to urban areas and increased military activities, insecurity and clan conflict further amplify displacement. As of September 2019, 2.6 million people remain displaced.
Rainfall in most of central and southern Somalia was 25-40% below average and the Shabelle and Juba river levels remained very low. An increase in rainfall from mid-May to mid-June led to improved browse and water conditions in northern and southern Somalia. However, the short duration, low amount and erratic distribution of the rains, as well as low river levels, led to significant reductions in area planted and yield of cereal crops. Severe acute malnutrition rates among children are increasing, mainly among IDPs. With an estimated 1 million children under 5 likely to be acutely malnourished through mid-2020.
According to FAO, an estimate 5.4 million Somali face acute food insecurity through September 2019; this includes 2.1 million people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4).
FSNAU estimates national maize and sorghum Gu production are expected to be 68% of normal in agropastoral areas, leaving most poor agropastoral and rivering households unable to meet their minimum food needs due to below-average food stocks and income from seasonal agricultural employment. Despite some improvements in herd sizes and livestock body conditions after the onset of the Gu rains, pastoral households are still recovering from extended impacts of the 2016/17 severe drought, contributing to poor food security outcomes.
The average number of beneficiaries reached with food assistance from January to May 2019 declined 47% compared to those reached from August to December 2018, according to FSNAU.
Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are expected to be widespread in northern and central agropastoral and pastoral livelihood zones as distressed sales of livestock will outpace births. However, seasonal milk availability and restored livestock body conditional and value, along with agricultural labor opportunities will offer some food and income. Emergency (IPC Phase 4) is most likely predicted in Guban Pastoral livelihood zone as births will not occur until Dec-Jan and in Bay-Bakool Lower Potential Agropastoral livelihood zone given the severity of the Gu food deficits.
This project is aimed at improving the ability of vulnerable HHs to meet their basic needs and reduce consumption gaps through life-saving humanitarian Unconditional Cash Transfers (UCTs) in the most vulnerable communities in Southern Somalia and Somaliland. The project will target populations in IPC 3, 4 and 5 and especially female-headed HHs, heads of household with disabilities and elderly headed households. Support will be provided through monthly multi-purpose cash transfers to 8,900HHs via Mobile Money Transfer.

Project Activities

CWW will target the most vulnerable HHs with multi-purpose unconditional cash transfers. The project will target populations in IPC 3+ especially newly displaced and worst-affected pastoralist and agropastoralist communities. UCT is the preferred emergency modality as it can be implemented within a short timeframe, respects dignity and choice of the households to prioritize their own needs and supports functioning markets. Mobile money also maximises the amount of funds directly reaching beneficiaries by drastically cutting the cost of delivery by eliminating intermediaries such as cash facilitators. Markets across Somalia have also proven to be very resilient through periods of drought and reactive to increase in commodities demand during influx of new populations. Geographical targeting will be done through a systematic approach where districts with highest IPC and IDP populations are prioritized. After regions and districts are selected, settlement targeting will involve consulting local authorities and other stakeholders, such as partner agencies and relevant clusters. Selection criteria includes: areas with new IDPs, low food security, malnutrition, rural areas severely affected by crisis not receiving aid, areas without cash/food assistance, minority communities, newly liberated areas and other criteria based on the context.Targeting will take place in February-March for cash assistance that will be provide to beneficiaries in April, May and June. In July-August, a second round of targeting will take place for cash assistance that will be provided in September, October and November, aligning with seasonal rainfall patterns and lean seasons. During these seasons HHs face a hunger gap in which food is scarcer and food prices rise while purchasing power lowers following the Jilaal and Hagaa dry seasons.Beneficiary targeting will follow a community-based process by which representative community committees discuss and identify the most vulnerable HHs in their community based on pre-set vulnerability criteria agreed between them and CWW. These reflect negative coping mechanism use, poverty, head of HH characteristics linked to access to livelihood opportunities and difficulties accessing food/basic needs and additional criteria the community leadership identifies. Priority will be given to female-headed HHs, HHs with people with disabilities, HHs with older/chronically ill people, pregnant/lactating women, poor HHs with many young children and no source of income and HHs from marginalized groups. The project aims to actively target women within the HHs to be registered to receive UCT since women are more likely to spend money towards the overall HH wellbeing. Targeting women is widely accepted in Somalia as women’s traditional role is that of the HH manager, responsible for the daily subsistence spending, including food.After the list of proposed beneficiaries is announced, community members will have the opportunity to provide feedback and suggest changes before the list is finalized and the process of verification of eligibility by CWW staff begins. CWW CRM toll free line will be publicized during community sensitization. CRM is made available to both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries to enable complaints about inclusion/exclusion. The transfer value will vary based on geographic location, according to the CWG recommended transfer values.A baseline will be conducted prior the release of the first transfer while PDMs will be undertaken 2 weeks after every other transfer. The PDM will track intra-HH decision-making on expenditure to ensure cash benefits all HH members and females have a say in the decision makingCWW intervention aims at improving beneficiaries’ access to goods while also preventing HHs from resorting to negative coping mechanisms which erode resilience in the longer term. UCT is flexible, allowing HHs to prioritise their own needs and tailor assistance to the particular vulnerabilities they face.

Cash transfers to the most poor in the community can also mean less chance of recruitment of the children by Armed Opposition Forces (AOGs) and offset early marriage or unsafe livelihood activities.PDMs track intra-HH decision making on expenditure to ensure cash benefits all HH members and females have a say in these decisions. PDMs will also track whether cash caused inter-HH disputes due to disagreements on how to spend cash. Concern will ensure that VRCs include female representatives and representatives from a broad age-range and from different groups to ensure balance and a range of perspectives in community-level targeting and verification. 4c. Forced taxation/extortion or theft of humanitarian assistanceThe risks is mitigated by the registration approach and mechanism of mobile money cash transfers. Partners make a money transfer to a national telecommunication company each month. They provide a list of names and phone numbers of the intended beneficiaries and the company makes the transfer of the specified amount to each account. Once cash is transferred, the beneficiary receives a code; they use this to collect the cash for official distribution points located close to their homes or maintain the money in an account on their SIM card for future purchases. There are no middlemen or gatekeepers who transport or hold cash during the process. The telecom companies provide CWW with official documentation upon successful completion of transfer to each SIM. PDM surveys include a focus on diversion of aid at HH level, asking whether HHs had to pay someone in order to access the cash (e.g. gatekeepers, elders) and whether they were pressured to share the cash. Beneficiaries are sensitized on their rights before receiving the cash (i.e. nobody has the right to take the cash from them) and on how to access the CRM hotline, allowing them to report diversion for timely follow up by CWW.
