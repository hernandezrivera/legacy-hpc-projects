Project Summary

The humanitarian situation, particularly in the Idleb de-escalation area, continues to deteriorate as a result of the hostilities and economic situation. The overall estimated Sector PIN for 2022 – of food insecure and those at risk of food insecurity – is estimated at 13.9 million, with 12 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.9 million people at risk of sliding into food insecurity. The acute food insecure figure includes 2 million people living in camps and deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure. FSA sector foresees an increase in the number of food-insecure households in 2023.
Through this project, IR Syria plans to reach vulnerable families in host communities and who are newly internally displaced in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. Through this project, IR Syria plans to reach 22,700 families (113,500 individuals) who are newly IDP and host communities in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. The targeted families will be assisted with:
 1.5 kg of baked bread daily to 2,700 households: free bread will be distributed daily to the most vulnerable groups at the targeted IDPs camps
 Monthly food parcels to the most vulnerable groups will be distributed monthly to BNFs meets the selection criteria of 1. Female headed-households 2. Child headed-households. 3. Elderly headed-household. 4. Households has people with disability
 Emergency food parcels in a time of emergency: a one time off distribution of emergency food parcels in response to the seasonal emergency situation (floods/snowfall/ harsh winter)
 Provision of livestock: targeted households will receive also veterinary services (treatment livestock and provide suitable drugs) and that will decrease the number of livestock deaths, raise the health level of animals, increase animal production and increase income generation for families, and help them to keep their livestock as a productive asset by providing veterinary services.
 Provision of agriculture kits: include (pesticide, fungicide) and support with the agricultural kit (fertilizers and harvests bags). These families will be provided with agricultural training to improve their knowledge and practices to get the best yields. The families will further be supported through links with the markets so that the crops can be marketed well and ultimately the sale of which can increase the household level of income.
 Veterinary clinic and vaccination.
The project team will design agricultural tool kits and livestock kits through the community consultation involving the experts/experience individuals in the target areas and by capturing male and female views and reflecting them in the project design. Over the course of the project implementation period the feedback of the project audience will be collected using the appropriate types of surveys and interviews also making sure that the CRM is consistently accessible for the main stockholders throughout the project period.
Islamic Relief MEAL department has the capacity and the experience to implement the MEAL-related task, senior MEAL officer, MEAL officer and three MEAL assistant in addition to the enumerators that will collect part of the required data such as data for the beneficiary verification against the criteria of selection etc, All the elements of the MEAL framework will be considered in during planning and budgeting and throughout the project time

3 - Needs

Syria continues to suffer from complex socioeconomic difficulties. According to 2023 HNO, 77 per cent of households reported buying less expensive and less preferred foods resulting in poorer diets (63 per cent with low and medium diets). In addition, more than 48 per cent of households reported never consuming animal proteins, further deteriorating the quality of diets as well as increasing risks of nutrient deficiency for many. As many as 90 per cent of households reported relying on markets as main sources of consumed foods. High food price increases, hyperinflation, and underperforming agriculture seasons resonated with reported coping mechanisms. Lastly, ongoing electricity and fuel rationing compound with declining economies affected several businesses, resulting in reduced income opportunities for Syrians. For example, in April 2022, as many as 46 per cent of households reported having experienced income loss or reduction (mainly due to reduced working hours or salary gains).Livestock has not also been spared from the impact of the prevailing water shortage and scarcity, limited access to livestock production inputs especially feed. Substantial harvest losses combined with chronic economic deprivation and displacement have contributed to mounting food insecurity and associated negative food and livelihood coping strategies. MAAR stated that total area planted with wheat in 2021/2022 was 1.2 million hectares. Furthermore, the harvested area is less than 60%.

4 - Implementation of activities

In close coordination with the FSL Cluster, local council, camp management, local community and other humanitarian organizations active in the field, the geographic areas where a large number of new IDP families arrived will be mapped. The newly displaced families who are residing in the camps, unfinished buildings, informal settlements and have not received baked bread will be provided with daily baked bread. As recommended by the FSL cluster, after the initial weeks of displacement the families need monthly food parcel support to sustain minimum food security requirements without adopting harmful negative coping strategies. Each of the targeted families will receive monthly food parcel in emergencies. IR will conduct a detailed household-level need assessment to identify the most vulnerable families to provide agricultural vouchers including pesticides, fungicide and support with the agricultural kit. These families will be provided with agricultural training to improve their knowledge and practices to get the best yields. The families will further be supported through links with the markets so that the crops can be marketed well and ultimately the sale of which can increase the household level of income. Furthermore, IR will support targeted households with feed, Veterinary kit, the breeders need these vet materials continuously to treat and control some pests which attack their livestock.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

There is a risk of community tension arising from the beneficiary selection process. IR will ensure to inform the beneficiary selection criteria clearly to all the stakeholders. Local councils and community committees will be engaged in beneficiary selection to minimise the tension arising from targeting for IDPs versus Host communities. The risk includes inclusion and exclusion errors leading to tensions and sexual exploitation of IDP females. This will be mitigated through local beneficiary selection committees with equitable representation of both IDPs and host communities. Clear/precise guidance to the staff on the beneficiary selection process and criteria. Effective communication as regards the guidelines include communication materials in local language. Targeting for IDPs/Returnees for livelihood projects. The risks include access to land/assets. Risk to secondary rights holders e.g., female headed households. This will be mitigated through engaging with local authorities to verify status and through work alongside other complementary programs on HLP. In provision of general food assistance there is a risk of violence from the beneﬁciaries among themselves as well as the humanitarian staff. Also, there is a risk of SEA by humanitarian workers male dominated, gate keepers and potential other beneﬁciaries. This risk will be mitigated through establishing a robust complaint and feedback mechanism so that any such cases are appropriately reported and followed up.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Conflict settings place women and girls at acute risk of poor health outcomes and sexual and gender-based violence. IR’s CFM uses multiple channels to collect beneficiary complaints and feedback including a complaint box, hotlines, during-distribution and post-distribution monitoring, community committees, casual enumerators and program staff. In addition, during activity implementations, random interviews, spot checks and dialogue will allow beneficiaries to report concerns. The received complaints and feedbacks will be classified and handled as per IR policy for CFM and high-risk complaints, such as those relating to PSEA, child protection, or corruption, will be prioritized and promptly managed. Confidentiality and data protection are ensured as per Islamic Relief’s data protection policy. IR’s commitment towards safeguarding against misconduct will be upheld, and options for safe access to IR’s feedback mechanism, especially to the most marginalized groups such as women, children, persons with disability, and those who cannot read and write, will be sought through WhatsApp, telegram, Skype and SMS.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Potential conflict between the beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. This is a medium level risk. Islamic Relief, in cooperation with the Community Representative will choose the beneficiaries according to specific criteria to serve this community and will adopt a clear and transparent policy in project implementation. 2. Takeover of trucks moving the agriculture and livestock inputs. This is a medium level risk. IR will make continuous assessment on accessible safe routes prior to transportation. Appropriate time of truck movement will be assessed and chosen. The movement will take place only on the main and frequently used roads during daylight and journeys will be planned beforehand and based on the latest security and accessibility information. 3. The existence of many checkpoints inside Syria, which may limit the access to the target area. This is a medium level risk. IR will coordinate with local partners and local committees in facilitating the movement of the items to the target areas. All required documentation will accompany all shipments of goods along with experienced staff who are familiar with the situation. 4. The closure of Bab al Hawa border crossing between Turkey and Syria. IR will use a second border crossing i.e., Bab al Salamah. This will slightly increase the transport cost of the deliverables. IR will then also assess the possibility of buying materials from inside Syria.
