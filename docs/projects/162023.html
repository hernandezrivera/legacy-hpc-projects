Project Summary

Through the proposed project, Tdh aims to contribute to a protective environment for Rohingya and host communities in Cox’s Bazar district by ensuring a safe and dignified access to a life-saving integrated package of services in camps 26, in Teknaf upazila.
The aim of the project, is firstly to improve access to emergency primary health care, with focus on children under 5 and pregnant and lactating (PLW), for individuals in camp 26 through operating a primary health centre (PHC) in the community and outreach activities.
Secondly Tdh will improve quality of healthcare services available to the refugee and host communities by improving infection prevention and control measures, adherence to international standard protocols and providing non communicable disease (NCD) care to the affected population in the PHC.

Needs

Needs of refugeesEnsuring adherence to quality, respectful implementation of the minimum standards for services in health facilities is a continuous challenge, in part due to large case load in health facilities and high staff turnover. There are shortages in Non-Communicable Disease (NCD) management, laboratory diagnostic capacity, mental health and psychosocial support services (including inpatient/psychiatric care) and specialized services (eye-care, geriatric care, oral healthcare and services for people with disabilities and palliative needs). An overall health care waste management solution is needed including safe disposal of expired medicines. Rational use of medicines to reduce risk for antimicrobial resistance is an emerging priority which requires engaging with private drug shops as well as health facilities in the camps. Overall, 55% of refugee households are reportedly taking on debt to cover health care (MSNA 2019). There is need for clearer understanding on what is driving this, and the types of expenditures. There are critical gaps in comprehensive emergency obstetric care, newborn care, family planning provision (particularly long acting methods), availability and timely access to 24/7 comprehensive clinical management of rape/intimate partner violence services and first line support for gender-based violence survivors. Mobility restrictions among women may also impact their health seeking behaviors with 20% of married women and 28% of unmarried women reportedly not able to move in the camps unaccompanied (MSNA 2019). Risks for communicable disease persist due to living conditions in the camps. Immunization coverage for Pentavalent 3 among children under 1 year old has improved from 67% at the start of 2019 to 78% by end of September 2019 but requires substantive improvements to achieve >95%. The refugee population remains vulnerable to weather-related emergencies which also impact disease trends. 31% of adult refugee men reported to smoke cigarettes every day (MSNA 2019). Referrals for emergency medical and obstetric cases, surgery, trauma and complications for NCD/chronic conditions remains challenging and costly. This is further compounded by recent communication/network coverage issues in the camps. Availability of quality data to guide the response is needed. Needs of host communitiesMany of the same needs are observed in the host community, particularly regarding healthcare waste management, rational use of medicine, facility-based deliveries, risk factors for communicable diseases and vulnerability to weather-related emergencies. Notably, while the proportion of household who reported taking on debt in the previous month was lower among the host community (45%), the debt levels for medical expenses are higher suggesting insufficient access to free health services. Mobility restrictions also exist in the host community, with 15% of married women and 20% of unmarried women reportedly not able to move unaccompanied (MSNA 2019), with potential implications for health seeking. S27% of adult refugee men reported to smoke cigarettes every day (MSNA 2019). NGO-clinics in host community areas need to engage in outreach among both host and refugee populations to encourage utilization of their services. Mental health and psychosocial support among the host community population are insufficiently addressed. There is need for strong coordination of host community support to ensure that the gaps are adequately filled.

Activities or outputs

Output 1: Improved access to emergency primary health care, with focus on children under 5 and PLW. Activity 1: Provide quality and complete primary care to community members with health needs including for Non-communicable diseases Activity 2: Ensure an inclusive and accountable medical service provision through improved access to disadvantaged community groups and real time feedback collection Activity 3: Provide preventive community services through tailored outreach to mitigate risks of outbreak and infections. Activity 4: Manage, maintain and strengthen medical, diagnostic and pharmacy services at the primary health centre.
