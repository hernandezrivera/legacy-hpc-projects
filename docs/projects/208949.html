Project Summary

The project aims at improving equitable access to quality lifesaving curative and preventive services through the provision of primary health care and nutrition interventions. This will contribute at reducing morbidity and mortality including those related to vaccine preventable diseases, maternal complications and malnutrition. The project tends to address the health and nutrition needs mainly targeting people affected by displacements resulting from conflicts that erupted from 15 April 2023, causing displacements, affecting health system which was already weak and resulting in inaccessibility to essential services to IDPs and hosting communities as well as people residing in conflicts affected areas. The targeted states are Kassala, Gedaref, East Darfur, South Darfur, Al Jazirah and Khartoum states which are either conflicts affected areas or hosting many IDPs all in need to access health and nutrition services.
Under health, the project tends to support 24 static health facilities and 4 mobile clinics that will provide lifesaving health care services including outpatient department for communicable diseases and common non communicable diseases, provide EPI services, provide reproductive health and maternal health services, outbreak prevention and response and mental health and psychosocial support.
Under nutrition the project will provide nutrition services including (i)screening of malnutrition for early detection and treatment of malnourished children and PLWs; (ii) Outpatient therapeutic feeding, (iii)Targeted supplementary feeding, (iv) Infant and young child nutrition. In addition, Alight plans to conduct one nutrition smart survey in East Darfur, the locality will be determined based on nutrition cluster priority to inform on nutrition situation and required efforts. The target is to support 24 fixed OTP/TSFP sites, 3 stabilization centers and 4 mobile clinics.
Considering that some facilities have been looted or damaged, a deep analysis will be done, and the project tends to rehabilitate/refurnish health facilities accordingly.
Alight will collaborate with stakeholders including FMOH, SMOH, HAC, health cluster and nutrition cluster at national and state level to ensure coordinated efforts and interventions based on the most in needs for rationale use of resources. Considering volatile situation in Sudan, Alight will rely on 4 Ws and capacity and gaps mapping from health and nutrition clusters. Where necessary the project will adapt to evolving situation including change of locations or modality to ensure the project responds to emergency health and nutrition to the most in needs.
Alight will also coordinate with UN agencies such as WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA and WFP either directly or through SMOH to ensure a constant supply chain of essential supplies such as medicines, vaccines, nutrition supplies and transport of supplies to Alight supported facilities will be arranged though this project.
Alight plans to ensure integrated approach is used as much as possible, where other sectors such as WASH, protection are available, referral mechanism will be discussed, and all relevant staff will be oriented. As part of integration, WASH education will be integrated in IEC at facility level, latrines and hand washing facilities rehabilitated/renovated while waste will be collected and disposed in safe manner. IYCF counseling will be integrated in IMCI, ANC, EPI and post-natal care. While on the other hand, parents attending nutrition services will be targeted with messages on EPI as well as risk communication and community engagements on any outbreak that may occur in targeted locations.
The project targets 342,056 people (310,000 for health and 32,056 for nutrition) among them 40% are IDPs, 31% are host communities and 27% are non -IDPs but residing in conflicts affected areas. The project target 47% female and 53% male while children cover more than 50% of targeted people. The project includes people with disabilities.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

As part of protection mainstreaming, ALIGHT will consider persons ages, gender, and other characteristics for better assessment of their risks, capacities, solutions and provide meaningful services to all without discrimination but rather inclusion for a stronger response. The services will be designed to ensure safe and dignified environment for service users, including a balanced gender team especially for reproductive health services, ensure services user information are kept in confidential way and where applicable the privacy is ensured, and culture or religious sensitivity is considered. high risks people such as persons with disability, elderly, and supported to access the services, this may include assigning volunteers to reach out to them at their home and using community structure to support them in reaching the facility. ALIGHT will train the project staffs on protection mainstreaming and orient them on referral pathways where other services are available. ALIGHT ensures policy on Prevention and response to, sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment (PRSEAH) are in place and ensure new staff, consultants and contractors are oriented while existing ones are refreshed and will sign their commitment to prevent and report such incidents.Beneficiaries will also be oriented on prevention and reporting mechanisms as part of their education. If any feedback on risk of PSEAH, From analysis, Alight recognize increased protection risks due to ongoing conflicts and intercommunal conflicts, women, girls and children are at high protection risks including child abuse and GBV. In additional, access to essential services have been affected increasing the vulnerability, for example women are exposed to high rate of maternal deaths due to inaccessibly to CEmONC, children, PLWs at high risk of malnutrition due to inaccessibility to services and food insecurity.Alight will work with other stakeholders including protection partners, community leaders, beneficiaries to mitigate the risks and improve the environment that minimize protection risks, and staff oriented to use a human centered approach when dealing and referring such protection cases. At community level, when educating on health and nutrition, community members will be engaged to identify protection risks and their ideas on how they can be mitigated. This may include mapping and considering community calendar and engage the most at risks when deciding days for nutrition supplies distribution, selection of sites of mobile clinics...During design, monitoring of implementation and evaluation of outcomes of the project, the specific groups with high protection risks will be considered and ensure their opinions are sought on how the project is considering and minimizing their risk and how they can be served better and where applicable their feedback with be triangulated into action with follow -up mechanism.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

ALIGHT is focused on implementing systems and methods that reveals customer experience of organizational services so that it can align a response and re-establish trust between ALIGHT, refugees, IDPs and the host communities. Trust works in both directions and must be built on the foundation of honesty and mutual accountability. ALIGHT commitment to accountability to the affected population will focus on the following: - governance, transparency, feedback, participation, design, monitoring and evaluation. ALIGHT will work with community structures in increasing the role of the community participation in maintaining the services. In addition, through the established community feedback and response mechanism, ALIGHT will continue to facilitate community consultation meetings to ensure active community participation, transparency and handling community feedback. The community members and beneficiaries provide feedback and seek responses in relation to activities provided by ALIGHT and other development partners in their communities in a manner that is safe, non-threatening and accessible to improve accountability across ALIGHT operational areas. ALIGHT’s Feedback/Complaint Response Mechanism (F/CRM) provides a formal channel through which ALIGHT beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can register voices and complaints. The F/CRM is centrally operated through feedback forms and a toll-free hotline number from all mobile phone networks companies (Sudani, Zain and MTN). ALIGHT has developed awareness materials including banners and flyers placed at ALIGHT project service delivery points such as health centers, water points etc. with the hotline number and explanations on how the FCRM works as well as information about our accountability standards and practices to which customers should hold ALIGHT accountable. In addition, through the MEAL department, ALIGHT conducts regular customer interviews and post service/distribution monitoring using standard survey feedback forms. As per ALIGHT procedures, the analysis of feedback information from communities is fed directly to senior management for action. These mechanisms have proven to be an effective tool in providing beneficiaries and communities with a voice and ultimately improving ALIGHT operations and identifying misconduct within the organization. A well-structured FCRM also enhances lessons learned and good practices across our network.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

The Project will ensure that participation, feedback and complaints mechanisms are integrated in all strategies and plans all throughout the project cycle. This is to ensure the project decisions and actions are responsive to the expressed priorities, needs, capacities and views of all persons of concern irrespective of Age, Gender, Diversity and Disability backgrounds. This is aimed at ensuring meaningful participation, enhanced communication and transparency, improved feedback and response. ALIGHT will ensure a balanced boys, girls, women and men in all project activities. Data for beneficiaries will be disaggregated by gender, age groups and location, as the project activities are tailored for different groups in line with developmental stages, needs and vulnerabilities and basing on relevant parameters appropriate in reverence to heightened protection risks of the persons of concern and issue affecting their access to essential services. The Project will ensure all persons of concern have equitable and non- discriminatory access to health and nutrition services. ALIGHT recognizes that in a context of a humanitarian emergency of the kind currently affecting the project locations, risks of SEAH is inherent. These include the potential of discrimination of IDPs and prioritization of host community members by care providers in the supported health facilities, the extorsion of resources or favors (including sexual) in exchange for preferential treatment at the service delivery point, the psychological abuse through language or attitude and the transfer of anger as both care providers and beneficiaries are psychologically affected by the effects of violence. In response, ALIGHT will ensure robust context-relevant systems are developed with all participants to ensure consistent accountability mechanisms are in place and guarantee the safety, access, dignity and inclusion of all persons eligible for services offered by the project. Alight will train all staff involved in the implementation of the project (including care providers at health facility levels) on Humanitarian code of Conduct and Core Humanitarian Standards and sensitize members of the IDPs and host communities on SEAH. Communities will be empowered to recognize and report deviations from expected attitudes and behaviors by all person involved in the implementation of the project, as well as avenues to report them safely and effectively. This will include through dedicated SEAH-themed IEC materials displayed at service delivery points, using illustrations and language that are sensitive to the current context and culture. ALIGHT’s PRSEAH Officer will monitor a CRM (complaints & Feedback mechanism) on a daily, weekly, monthly, and quarterly bases to ensure prompt and fitting response to suspected SEAH cases. Additionally, a quarterly PRSEAH ‘health check’ will be proactively conducted to diagnose the established system and verify its effectiveness.
