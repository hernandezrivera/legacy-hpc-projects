Project Summary

The conflict in the North East Nigeria which started some nine years ago had left an estimated 1.8 million people displaced in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe and around 40 percent (0.8 million) of that population is comprised of school aged children between the ages 3-17 years displaced across the three states. While the number of displaced persons and returnees increased compared to the previous year, the total number of children and education personnel in need decreased from 2.9 million for 2018 to 2.2 million for 2019 based on education sector estimates (EiEWG HNO, 2019).
It is also clear that because of the displacement and destruction caused by the armed conflict, North East has the highest number of out of school children, and high pupil to qualified teacher ratios which standards at 152: 1. Additional 59,074 teachers are needed to reach the required pupil teacher ratio of 40:1. Schools have been destroyed and the few that are existing are grossly congested making it hard for children to learning and this combined with other factors explains the low literacy levels. Parents, children and teachers have been traumatised and in great need of psychosocial support and Social and emotional learning. If these issues are not addressed, they will further fuel conflict in the region.
The goal of Save the Children’s project is to increase Access to Education in a safe and quality learning environment for children affected by the armed conflict. Save the Children will work with implementing partners such as Gender Equality, Peace and Development Center (GEPaDC), Federation opf Moslem Women Association (FOMWAN) and Ekklesiyar Yaúw A Nigeria (EYN) projects to reach 50,000 school age children (5-13):
The project will particularly target 50,000 (26,400 girls and 23,600 boys) in selected Local Government Areas of Maiduguri, Jere, Konduga, Mafa, Biu and Damboa in Borno and Damaturu, Gujba, Potiskum and Fune of Yobe. This figure will comprise of IDPs: 25,000; Returnees & Hots Communities: 24,000; Teachers: 900; School/ Centre Based Management Committees: 80; State Quality Assurance Department: 15 and Colleges of Education: 5.

Needs

Owing to the displacement and destruction caused by the armed conflict, North East has the highest number of out of school children, and high pupil to qualified teacher ratios which standards at 152: 1. Additional 59,074 teachers are needed to reach the required pupil teacher ratio of 40:1. Schools have been destroyed and the few that are existing are grossly congested making it hard for children to learning and this combined with other factors explains the low literacy levels. Parents, children and teachers have been traumatized and in great need of psychosocial support and Social and emotional learning. If these issues are not addressed, they will further fuel conflict in the region. Recent data on the key education indices in the NE still indicate a dire situation in education with Yobe recording one of the highest rates of children out of school at 42.2% compared with 27.2% of the estimated children out of school in the whole of Nigeria. Borno is reported in the same survey (MICS 2016-17) as having the highest number of girls out of school in Nigeria at 71%. Education sector faces an upward task of infrastructural rehabilitation and expansion, education management stretching and the need to address emerging education needs such as skills acquisition and protection of education against attacks. In a Human Rights Watch Report 2016, “They Set the Classrooms on Fire: Attacks on Education in the North East Nigeria” it is evident that the deliberate and systematic destruction of learning institutions, killing and maiming of learners and teachers had adverse impact that is deeply embedded in the communities. Children and teachers in all three affected states are in dire need of psycho-social support to cope with the negative effects of the conflict. There is an urgent need to engage the local communities in supporting education as a longer-term strategy for recovery and peace building. Also to strengthen system of education managers for sustainability. Teacher factor is very critical in providing quality education. The project will also focus on teacher training to ensure that Conflict-affected children and adolescents receive quality and conflict sensitive educational services to enhance their resilience.
