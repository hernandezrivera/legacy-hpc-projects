Project Summary

This Project aims to create access and provide emergency essential life-saving integrated primary healthcare services to affected vulnerable populations of IDPs, Returnees and Residents in Bor South and Duk counties of Jonglei state. SMC will focus on integrated primary healthcare services including maternal, child health (MCH) through static and mobile health facilities as key intervention approaches in our project locations. The project seeks to bridge significant gaps in the affected areas by flood and conflicts, targeting 19,666 people in needs, 53% (10,423) are children and 47% (9,243) adult. Segregated as follow: 24 %( 4,720) Women and 23% (4,523) men, 5,113 Boys (27%) and 5,310 Girls (26%). The Host community represents 70% (13,766), IPDs 16% (3,147) and 14% (2,753) are returnees with 15% (2950) representing people with disabilities Segregated in Both Counties.
The Vast majority are from Duk County with 65% (12,783 people,) with 8,948 Host, 2,045 IDPs and 1790 Returnees) segregated as children 53% (6,775) and adult 47% (6,008), with 3,068 Women, 2,940 Men, girls are 3,451 and boys 3,324 with 15% (1,918) representing persons with disabilities. Bor County represents 35%, 6,883 people of the total target with 4,818 host, 1,101 IDPs, and 964 Returnees. Children are 3,648 and 3,235 adults. Women are 1652, 1,583 Men, girls 27% (1,858) and boys 26% (1,858) with 15% (1,032) representing persons with disabilities.
Our focus will be on integrated life-saving interventions with Health, Nutrition, WASH and Protection (GBV) services. Through the supported facilities, the project will provide the following activities; Curative consultations, Preventative and promotional as well as capacity building of health workers, Screening, treatment, referral of MAM/SAM cases with complicated medical conditions and training of health workers on MIYCN best practices, Conduct WASH-IPCs, Distribution of reproductive health kits and sanitary items and provision of sanitary facilities, and provision of GBV-oriented services. SMC project will support 9 health facilities; The Intervention focus will be through, 3 PHCCs, (2PHCCs in Duk and 1 in Bor South) 2 PHCUs (1 per a county) and 4 community-based mobiles (2 in Bor South and 2 in Duk). The above proposed project intervention has the budget estimate of 600,000 USD.

1 - Needs

The country’s health system relies heavily on international humanitarian assistance and is still unable to cope and respond to humanitarian emergencies and provide essential life-saving health services. The drivers of humanitarian shocks include devastating years of political instability, recurrent inter-communal violence, unprecedented seasonal flooding, chronic hyperinflation compounded by the scourge of COVID-19 pandemic and outbreaks of cholera, measles and EVD also drive humanitarian need. (HNO-2023). The main barriers are lack of medicine, staff, Equipment's, transportation cost, insecurity, flood and long distance, according to ISNA 2022, . The contribution to health services was 1.9% in 2020/2021 to 7.9 % in 2021/22 national Budget. According to South Sudan health service functionality report (Oct.2022) out of 1980 health facilities, only 11 percent are providing the full package of BPHNS and 46% are moderately functional, implying inadequate access and unavailability of some health services many parts of the country, particularly in the flood affected state of Jonglei, Upper Nile, and unity and northern states. Maternal Mortality remain among the highest in the world with 789 per 100,000 live birth. Only 39% Pregnant women attend four Antenatal Care Services, and institutionalized delivery at 14.7 % by skilled birth attendance. Extreme low Immunization coverage (49% Penta 3) has made children vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases. Malaria, remain the leading cause of morbidity and mortality in 2021, high Infant Mortality rate at 90.7 deaths per 1,000 live births, while the reported cases of AWDs increased and 20 counties are classified as cholera hotspots, THE current outbreak in Unity ,remain a high treat to our operational areas of Duk and Bor Counties. (HNO-23) Several health facilities were relocated from their Original sites in both counties due to the current floods, where the populations are displaced to relative highlands along the canal, Islands (Koyom) and Pagaak Payam in Duk and all facilities in Jalle payam of Bor South are severed with populations displaced to Pabial Bor town currently being served by our Mobile clinics (MdM & SMC need assessment, Duk/Koyom IRNA 2021). The Community impact of recurrent flooding contributed to the destruction and damage health & water facilities, livelihood, and Education infrastructure, leaving vulnerable people in urgent need of basic services and intensified existing vulnerability, which include high poverty rates, limited access to basic services including health and Nutrition, high prevalence of diseases outbreaks, particularly in Duk and Bor Counties. Out of 19,666 people in needs, 10,423 are children and 9,243 adults. Segregated as follow: 4,720 Women and 4,523 men, 5,113 Boys and 5,310 Girls. The Vast majority are from Duk County with 65% (12,783 people,) with 8,948 Host, 2,045 IDPs and 1790 Returnees) segregated as children 6,775 and 6,008 adults, with 3,068 Women, 2,940 Men, girls are 3,451 and boys 3,324 with 15% (1,918) representing person’s disabilities. Bor County represents 35%, 6,883 people of the total target with 4,818 host, 1,101 IDPs, and 964 Returnees. Children are 3,648 and 3,235 adults. Women are 1652, 1,583 Men, 1858 girls and 1790 boys with 1,032 disable persons. We will close current gaps and will focus on essential life-saving integrated health care services, support ongoing maternal & child health, Immunization, GBV and mental health services provision through static and Medical mobile teams. Disease outbreaks detection, investigation and respond to, by supporting the surveillance systems, maintain the core- pipelines for essential medicines and supplies to respond to emergency health needs, rebuilt infrastructure and sanitation system will be vital in all interventions sites. Immunization will be improved,COVID-19 vaccination and awareness activities will be adopted.

2 - Project activities

1.Provision of maternal and childhood healthcare services; including management of communicable and Non-communicable diseases through static (Hospital, PHCC and PHCU) and Mobile facilities.2.Conduct routine EPI through static and outreach immunization services against vaccine preventable diseases targeting children less than 5yrs and Mothers in their child bear ages.3.Provision of basic medical Equipment and supplies to the supported health facilities during the Project period.4.Conduct community social mobilization, sensitization and awareness campaigns through health Education and behavioural changes sessions in our facilities and surrounding villages.5.Carry out integrated capacity-building of health workers through refresher training on case management, integrated disease surveillance and response & EWARS, CMR, MHPSS, WASH- IPCs, hygiene promotion and health education, and MIYCN best practices at the facilities.6.Rehabilitation of Health facilities targeting 2 PHCCs and 4 PHCUs in Duk and Bor South Counties of Jonglei7.Provision of Solar system to 4 heath facilities( 2 PHCs and 2PHCUs)8.Construction and rehabilitation of sanitary facilities and WASH-NFIs in our target locations including latrines, incinerator facilities and hand-washing and PPE items.9.Conduct screening, treatment and referral of all under 5 children and Pregnant and lactating women (PLW) for MAM/SAM cases at our facilities and surrounding villages.10.Conduct clinical management of rape cases for SGBV victims, referral and MHPSS services in our facilities and surrounding villages.11.Conduct community consultative and evaluative meetings with the project communities regularly and implement feedback mechanisms to ensure compliance.12.Provide technical and operational assistance to health facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure service quality and compliance to project standards at our facilities
