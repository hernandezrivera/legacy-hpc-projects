Project Summary

The displacement context in South Sudan remains fluid. The country continues to experience conflict and natural disaster-driven displacements in communities in both urban and hard-to-reach areas. PoCs and collective sites continue to host IDPs in a protracted displacement situation across the country. Concurrent with this are spontaneous returns and relocations of IDPs and refugees, the latter of which can end up finding themselves in a new displacement situation once back in South Sudan.
IOM will target 568,623 of the 800,000 beneficiaries targeted by the Cluster. IOM will focus on providing camp coordination and camp management under the following areas:
I. Static Operations (PoCs and Collective Centers): As CM agency in Bentiu and Wau, and care and maintenance focal point in Malakal, IOM will continue to improve the living conditions and meet the basic needs of affected populations in these sites.
Output 1: IDPs living in PoCs and CCs have improved and dignified living conditions in support of a conducive environment for durable solutions and with meaningful community engagement.
II. Deploy mobile teams to new and unreached displacement sites as prioritized by the Cluster.
Output 2: Deployment of sustainable mobile responses to unmanaged sites to ensure coordination and community ownership in camp-like settings or spontaneous settlements.
II. Co-Lead the Camp Coordination and Camp Management Cluster
Output 3: Fully functional, staffed, and well-managed CCCM Cluster supports partner agencies in accountable CCCM responses and systems

1 - Needs

The displacement context in South Sudan remains fluid. The country continues to experience conflict and natural disaster-driven displacements in urban, peri-urban, and rural areas. PoCs and collective sites continue to host IDPs in a protracted displacement situation. Concurrent with this are spontaneous returns and relocations of IDPs and refugees, the latter of which can end up finding themselves in a new displacement situation once back in South Sudan. Although stability has returned to some areas, the outlook for the peace process remains uncertain, with many communities having limited access to basic services and facing threats of intercommunal conflict over HLP. In 2020, complex and overlapping displacement trends are likely to continue, with the potential for a deterioration in the peace process, which could result in mass displacement. IDPs depend on humanitarian service provision to meet their basic needs. Central to service provision is camp management, the core services of which facilitate life-saving service delivery that is targeted, accountable, participatory, and accessible. In static and mobile approaches, CCCM interventions will be in line with Cluster and HRP Strategic Objectives. IDPs in PoC sites are likely to remain displaced due to multiple factors, including security constraints, limited livelihood opportunities, lack of services, and HLP issues. Though some IDPs have decided to leave the sites, the requirement for continued humanitarian assistance for the remaining population continues in order to maintain site safety with dignified living conditions. Without core CCCM services and engineering investments, sites will experience a substantial deterioration, including vis-à-vis their protective environment. Without specific efforts to include persons with disabilities (PWD) and other marginalized groups, further barriers to service access will persist. Recognizing that the majority of displaced persons reside outside of formal camps, IOM will continue to reach out to populations that reside among host communities. Many of these sites lack formal services and have sporadic and generally uncoordinated humanitarian programs. Left to manage their own displacement, populations often settle on undesirable, difficult to access and marginal land, serving to increase protection risks, especially during rainy season. Alongside the Cluster, IOM mobile teams will support rapid multi-sector assessments and responses, promoting and engaging community governance structures with time-bound camp management interventions. An exit strategy plan will be approved by the cluster. IOM will ensure mobile teams coordinate with the CCCM cluster to ensure information is shared among cluster partners. IOM mobile responses will also explore multisectoral capacities with other clusters and will engage with national organizations where possible to support the localization of Camp Management services. Multi-sectoral needs assessments reveal that displaced persons from within and outside the country are moving to reclaim former homes or settle in new areas. A proportion of this population end up in further displacement and face acute needs resulting in vulnerability and insecurity. Communal clashes, economic and climatic shocks – further exacerbated by fragile stability – continue to threaten spontaneous “return areas.”. Given the linkages of many returnees to CCCM-managed displacement sites, IOM is well positioned to support transitional and durable solutions through service coordination, information dissemination, community engagement, inclusive participation, and site care and maintenance. IOM teams are technically diverse, ensuring responses – static and mobile – are comprehensive, coordinated and cost effective, providing IDPs with the information and tools to enable voluntary, dignified and informed decisions regarding the possibility to achieve acceptable solutions to their displacement situation.

2 - Activities

IOM will focus on the following CCCM activities: 1. Through static operations, continue to improve living conditions and meet basic needs of affected populations in PoCs and CCs in support of a conducive environment for durable solutions and meaningful community engagement• Provide CM and coordinate humanitarian services at Bentiu and Wau PoCs to ensure equal and needs based access• Ensure services are delivered in a manner consistent with humanitarian principles• Ensure site ownership and enable participation of communities living in sites through inclusive governance structures and community protection structures, to expand participation of women, youth and PWD• Maintain and expand existing information management systems, to track service provision and gaps, population counts, and surveys (e.g. intention surveys) to inform programs and provide consistent information to partners and communities• Provide two-way information channels through information desks in PoCs, and AAP initiatives, such as CwC, Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms, and Town Hall Meetings, to ensure access of communities to information on areas of return to enable voluntary decisions on remaining or leaving PoCs• Implement site care and maintenance of infrastructure and, if necessary, implement further improvement / expansion of IDP sites to alleviate congestion and reduce health risks, sanitation and protection concerns, and improve accessible infrastructure to PWDs • Collaborate with UNMISS security and protection actors to identify, mitigate and respond to protection risks• Implement updated contingency plans, including risk prevention, alongside partners and Cluster• Alongside Cluster, prioritize mapping of displacement sites in IOM AOR• Promote inclusive programming through linkages with development partners to enhance livelihood opportunities, promote resilience, and contribute to durable solutions, including piloting CBI 2. Deploy sustainable mobile responses to new and unreached displacement sites prioritized by Cluster to ensure coordination and community ownership in camp-like settings or spontaneous settlements• Participate in inter-cluster assessments and other assessments triggered • Produce site mappings, 3Ws and service provision gap assessments;• Set up sustainable CBCM, respecting do no harm principles• Provide technical support to site governance structures to promote participation of women, youth and PWD, and site ownership and conflict sensitivity• Provide technical training for site management committees on basic site care and maintenance, focused on flood and fire prevention and infrastructure adaptation for PWD• Set up community led initiatives to attend community identified needs• Build capacity of NNGOS / local organizations to ensure sustainability of responses by securing CCCM focal points in unreached areas• Operationally support inter-agency / -Cluster efforts to support displaced persons with achieving durable solutions, including returnees• Implement pilot CBI alongside Cluster, following best practices 3. Co-Lead the Camp Coordination and Camp Management Cluster to support partner agencies in accountable responses and systems• Coordinate CM partners to define strategies, harmonize best practices, develop relevant tools and resources, and coordinate responses at national, state, and site level• Support Cluster partners through support deployments, oversight, and capacity building• Provide information management, including site lists, displacement trends, partner service tracking, and M&E systems• Support national level coordination through Cluster Information Management and Cluster Co-Coordinator• Support state level coordination through State Focal Points in Upper Nile, Jonglei, and Western Bahr el Ghazal • Support Inter-Cluster Coordination forums including ICWG and co-lead state-level Solutions Working Groups• Co-Lead Communication and Community Engagement WG

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Core to CCCM projects and activities is integrating the voice of the affected community into humanitarian service provision. Coordination with beneficiaries is facilitated through a multitude of institutionalized platforms and governance structures, such as weekly camp committee meetings, block leaders’ meetings, sector leaders’ meetings, service providers meeting, Youth Forum meetings, Women’s Forum meetings, Community Leadership Committee meetings, Inter-Church Committee meetings and Trade Union meetings for market traders.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

CCCM interventions in the area of support for spontaneous returnees and durable solutions are in line with the 2019 South Sudan government framework on returns. On a community level, these operations are planned and implemented following extensive consultations with local communities, displaced persons, state and local authorities, and affected persons in order to ensure long-term integration, risk mitigation, and do no harm.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

CCCM interventions are in line with Cluster priorities as detailed in the CCCM Cluster Strategy and guidance documents on mobile response.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Overall, many parts of South Sudan are classified as not safe enough for return. However, pockets of stability are emerging and may continue to emerge as implementation of the peace process solidifies. In many areas, spontaneous returns are taking place on a large scale as formerly displaced persons seek their own durable solutions to displacement.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

A core function of CCCM interventions is to foster community self-management structures. CCCM mobile missions are needs-based and time-bound, with mission planning including capacity building to local authorities, community self-management structures to ensure community ownership and continuation of core CCCM functions including information sharing, advocacy, and service coordination. IOM will continue engaging in forums including Solutions Working Groups (SWG); Future of PoC process, and other collaborative and inter-agency forums through support to CCCM Cluster and CCCM operations to explore interventions and solutions to protracted displacement in PoC sites.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

CCCM will promote representation of marginalized communities in decision-making and governance structures. Through CwC, CM will ensure all groups are reached with public awareness, information on project activities and concerns regarding access to services. With the accessible complaints and feedback mechanism marginalized groups will be able to address issues of exclusion/lack of information, and lack of representation For intervention in new collective sites, CM will conduct a risk assessment to identify possible risk of disputes on landownership and will consult with different actors including community leaders, authorities, OCHA and partners regarding camp administration and land usage to ensure that the displaced population will not be forcibly evicted. IOM CCCM will continue to support spontaneous returnees and durable solutions programming coordinated with state and national-level Solutions Working Group carried out in compliance with ICWG-endorsed guidelines. Where returns are affected by HLP issues CM will link them with HLP actors for assistance. IOM will expand its CCCM mobile services to improve living conditions and reduce protection risks of IDPs in unmanaged sites through community self-management, equitable CCCM access to displacement areas, maintaining AAP standards. Thus, contribute to less number of population moving from their habitual places to the already congested sites with concentrated services and reducing the collective negative impact. Through support to Cluster and CCCM operations, engage in forums including Solutions and CCE Working Groups; Future of PoC process, and other forums to address service gaps in return and displacement areas. CCCM will participate in safety audits to identify and address GBV risks. CM will collaborate with protection and security actors for the development of community based protection measures that enhance the safety of women and girls, including site planning and maintenance to reduce GBV risk.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Camp Management will promote the representation and participation of vulnerable members including persons with disabilities, women and girls, older persons and persons with chronic illnesses in community structures and CCCM forums to raise issues of exclusion, stigmatization and access constraints to services. Messages and meetings minutes will be context-specific and tailored for the targeted audience respecting age, gender, and diversity translated and shared via notice boards keeping in mind cultural context and target beneficiaries. Messages to specific groups such as women and persons with disability, are developed in consultation with specialized partners. In 2020, IOM will ensure messages are more tailored for people with visual, hearing and speech impairments and will place specific messages from Camp Management in women friendly spaces and community gathering facilities. Through the satisfaction surveys CCCM will be able to collect beneficiaries’ feedback on services, CwC activities and effectiveness of the CFM in channeling their complaints. Focus Groups Discussions are also conducted on an ad hoc basis, which enable consultations concerning the immediate impact of interventions, such as infrastructure improvement for persons with disabilities, women empowerment livelihood and youth activities and utilization of public spaces in the sites. Assessments involving all categories of affected population are also considered as a tool to collect accurate information about their specific needs.
