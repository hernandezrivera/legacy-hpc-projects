Project Summary

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) aims to provide sector endorsed all WASH activities in camp 4 (F-block) and camp 17 (B-block) covering a total of 2,928 HHs. The major activities include construction of latrine, deep tube-well/water networks and bathing spaces, repair and maintenance of latrine, tube-well and bathing spaces, water quality test, hygiene promotion, solid waste management and desludging of latrine.
Within the camp and host community area, 28% of bathing facilities are out of order due to unavailability of water facilities, uncleanliness, distance from the shelter, no privacy, and broken door/fence and drainage problems. CB will conduct necessary repair and maintenance of the facilities. More than 65% of people use communal bathing facilities. Most of the people do not have designated bathing facilities, and thus use tube-well platforms. On the other hand, around 35% of latrines are non-functional and not in use due to a lack of privacy/no separation between men and women, toilets being full, not being safe (no door, no lock, etc.), and being too far away. Repair of latrines will be done with sector standard design and effective coordination and communication will be maintained with other actors of WASH sector. These challenges around latrines and bathing places especially affect women because they did not feel safe using the facilities at night and there was no segregation of facilities for men and women, a problem due to cultural norms. Additionally, females’ s menstrual hygiene needs are largely unmet. Also, COVID-19 is a major risk within the camp which will be addressed in hygiene promotion as the targeted population will receive hygiene kits and awareness sessions. Moreover, the potential diseases in the camps (i.e. AWD) are major concerns which will also be addressed in coordination with other actors.
In a joint agency gender analysis on the response, children and adolescents also raised concerns about safety when accessing WASH facilities. Girls were prevented from using latrines that were being used by both men and women, due to the lack of segregation and privacy. Girls also had problems with long queues, overcrowding and the lack of lighting at night at latrines. With the WASH related activities, greater emphasis will be provided to ensure safe and dignified access to WASH facilities. Boys raised concerns around the proper maintenance of latrines and lack of lighting and felt unsafe when using wells because of the long queues. All girls surveyed felt that fetching water from tube wells was unsafe because of the distance from home.

1 - Identified Needs

As per the ISCG situation report of the Rohingya Refugee Crisis, 34% of the people in camps still do not have access to safe drinking water, 32% of the people have not received WASH & Hygiene Kits and 35% of the people do not have access to latrine facilities. As per ISCG/WASH-Sector reports non-functionality of water options (tube-well) is 27% in camp 17 while 17% in camp 4. It was expressed that non-functionality of tube-wells stems from declining water table, having no head and broken pipes. 54 % of refugees in the camps are now accessing chlorinated water through piped distribution networks. The remainder continue to access water from tube-wells fitted with handpumps. Water quality surveillance shows that 75% of water analysis done at household level were without fecal contamination and 58% with adequate chlorine residual. 88% households reported having enough water to meet all domestic needs but local disparities exist especially in Teknaf area 55% refugees reporting enough water in camp 26.Despite 99 percent of households reporting using pit latrines in the camps and acceptable quantitative standards reached, many qualitative challenges remain with 22% of sanitation infrastructures requiring O&M and 10% being built in flooding areas. Sanitation coverage remains challenging due to lack of space in the camps with the highest density (e.g: 1E, 1W, 2E, 2W, 3, 9, 10). 68% of households reported having a private space inside or attached to their shelter for bathing but those bathing places sometimes associated with laundry (56%) and dishwashing places (52% ) are unformal and often unhygienic. One of the major concerns regarding sanitation is the quality of the fecal sludge treatment9 as facilities require upgrades and improvements. With 500 cubic meters of Solid Waste produce daily in the targeted area10, SMW remains one of the main challenges as only 51 percent of the refugee households are disposing waste in designated area and 27 percent reporting the presence of garbage near their houses.Appropriated hygiene practices remain low despite the fact that progresses have been observed partly due to fear from Covid-19 and focus on hand-washing promotion. As a result, 98% of the refugees increased handwashing practices since the COVID-19 outbreak and 95% reporting having access to soap. Those improvements need to be confirmed on longer-term through both practice on knowledge as only 60% of the refugees can mention three critical time to wash their hands. In respect of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM), 1.3 MHM kits were provided to women and girls on average between January to September11 instead of one every trimester. In the host community, 88% of the households has access water from tubewells / handpumps, but only 77% of households reporting having enough water for their domestic need (only 74% in Teknaf where groundwater is scared).Over the past year, this figure has significantly increased(from 63% in 2019) Despite high pumping in the aquifers below mega-camp, groundwater availability has been proven sustainable however, impact on the surrounding communities is significative due to depression from pumping cone requiring deeper tubewells and better handpumps in host communities (especially in the north of Kutupalong camp).63% of household have at least basic access to sanitation leading to 14% of the HH practicing sometimes open defecation. Regarding waste management, 11% are reporting having often visible waste in the vicinity of their house. Host communities families have been less supported by hygiene items and campaign, but findings show encouraging data as 96% of the HH reported having access to soap. Hygiene knowledge seems also better than in camps as 81% of HC respondents being able to mention 3 critical times for handwashing.
