Project Summary

The project contributes to the prevention of Severe Acute Malnutrition, in Yobe and Borno States, among children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) by ensuring adequate food intake, improved diet diversification, early detection of SAM and referral, improved Infant and Young Children Feeding Practices (IYCF).
In addition the project intends to contribute to the nutrition sector of HRP for Nigeria by:
- Strengthening in scale the availability of quality services managing acute malnutrition for children (boys and girls 6-59months) and pregnant and breastfeeding women through increasing access to treatment.
- Increase capacity to identify malnourished children and refer them for treatment as well as promote services preventing under-nutrition while supplementing for micro-nutrients deficiencies.
- Enhance routine monitoring of the emergency nutrition situation through regular assessment analysis of data, while strengthening coordination within the nutrition partners and other sectors.
- Capacity building and training for MoH health workers in Jakusko and community mobilizers in all the target areas.

Needs

Malnutrition is still a public health concern in the North-East Nigeria and a key priority for emergency response. Acute malnutrition is among the leading causes of death weather directly or indirectly for children under the age of five. It is estimated that 1,094,467 children aged 6-59 across the BAY states are undernourished with 367,526 severely malnourished (SAM) while 726,941 with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM). There has been a reduction in the prevalence of SAM cases from 439,520 in 2018 to 367,076 in 2019 following sustained scale up of services the same is not the case for MAM where cases increased from 502,536 in 2018 to 723,326 in 2019 . The nutrition situation has further been exacerbated by protection concerns related to new displacement following on going military operations as well as outbreak of diseases such as cholera where 18 LGAS were affected by cholera outbreak in 2018 with 4,250 cases reported The HRP 2018 indicates that women and children continue to bear the brunt of malnutrition in north-east Nigeria, where the conflict has acted as an important driver of hunger. Recent assessments have identified pockets of high malnutrition, with a critical rate of global acute malnutrition (GAM) above the 15 per cent emergency threshold in six LGAs in northern Yobe (Jakusko, Karasuwa, Machina, Nguru, Yunusari and Yusufari). The Nutrition sector estimates that 3.5 million women and children are in need of nutrition interventions. The majority of these (2.7 million) live in host communities, while a remaining 440,000 are internally displaced persons and 340,000 are returnees. According to the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance, round IV carried out by the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) in coordination with the National Population Commission (NPC), the Federal Ministry of Health (FMOH), there is a continued concern about emergency levels of acute malnutrition in several regions of Yobe. GAM prevalence indicates a serious situation (>10%) in all Yobe domains. The mortality remains above emergency levels: crude mortality rate is above 1 deaths/10,000/day North Yobe (1.35) and Under 5 mortality rate is above 2 deaths / 10,000/ day in North and Central Yobe (3.72 and 2.07 respectively). According to the Nigeria nutrition cluster surveillance, the nutrition situation in Borno and Yobe as continued to be perilous with GAM rates exceeding the WHO threshold of 10% for “serious” : Central Borno, MMC/Jere, Northern Yobe, Central Yobe, and Southern Yobe. Prevalence of GAM was highest in Northern Yobe both as assessed by weight for height and/or oedema (14.3%), and as assessed by MUAC (10.5%).The nutrition situation in the North East has further been aggravated by high food insecurity, sub optimal infant and young children feeding practices such as negative coping strategies, increasing spread of endemic diseases, low coverage of programs targeting children with acute malnutrition, limited dietary diversity, loss of livelihoods, disruption of access to quality water and optimal sanitation, population displacement and destruction of housing, compromising the privacy necessary for breastfeeding; and the poor and deteriorating health care system.
