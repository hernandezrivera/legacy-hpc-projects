Project Summary

The Cox’s Bazar in south-east region of Bangladesh has been hosting Forcibly Displaced Myanmar Nationals (FDMN) for several decades since 1978 with the largest influx in 2017. The total population identified so far by the latest JNA of UNHCR-Govt is 8, 96,879 living in two Official Camps in 34 extremely congested makeshift camps. While this region is the most poverty stricken with very limited livelihood opportunities, hilly & uncultivable lands due to salinity and proneness to numerous natural disasters. Given the context, this massive influx also created extra pressure on the overall conditions of the host communities as well. This proposed project is targeting to address one of priority needs Shelter, will construct a total of 1368 Shelters for FDMN. Each targeted HHs will receive solar lamps and participatory activity planning will be ensured to provide HH level site improvement.
The possible risk factors will be taken into consideration and necessary mitigation measures will be taken during project implementation. YPSA will develop IEC materials with the support of HRF in order for message dissemination on different protection issues including risk mitigation, Covid-19 awareness and hygiene etc. Issues based community meetings will be done by the staff. During this meeting social distancing and safety measures will be ensured at all stages.
YPSA will set up a complaint mechanism to know about the opinions from the beneficiaries about provided assistance. In this regards YPSA will develop a context based feedback/complaint mechanism. YPSA will ensure all the staff and contractor are complying to PSEA policies and are trained on the PSEA.

1 - Identified Needs

Rohingya Population: Shelter interventions have had a positive impact, but shelter conditions remain a concern. The main materials used – untreated bamboo and tarpaulin – are not long-lasting and deteriorate over time. Seventy-one percent of Rohingya households advise that the roofs of their shelters leak, while 46 percent report rotten or damaged bamboo. Improved shelter solutions are needed that provide enhanced structural strength, protection from the elements and resistance to insect attack. The 2021 monsoon rains caused slope failures that highlighted the need for shelter-level site improvement work that is not currently 100% covered by the Site Management and Site Development Sector. Fifty-one percent of households reported access difficulties. Works such as slope protection, protection wall, raising plinths on which shelter stands, improved household drainage and the improvement of stairs and paths giving access is needed. On the other hand for FDMN, considering their safety and security issue in the project implementing area solar panels will be distributed as a project interventions for ensuring security related issue and mitigating the risk of fire incident of the Rohingya community and community sensitization on COVID-19, protection, DRR, community safety and security related issue will be ensured during project period. Required message will be delivered through IEC materials and project field staffs will directly involve to ensure this in collaboration with community people.
