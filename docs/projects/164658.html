Project Summary

GC has proposed this plan to build upon the ERL components of its OCHA-funded, and FFP-Funded activities where we rehabilitated 12 km of asphalt roads in Atmeh Camp and Atmeh town, and as part of the wheat value chain project, we also rehabilitated mill and bakeries, including the building rehabilitation and equipment maintenance and replacement, we also created short-term employment opportunities under the activities, the field team conducted assessment, and during and after the implementation, we could observe the needs for expanding the activities geographically.
Cash-for-work is as an effective early recovery approach to support vulnerable families who struggle to meet their immediate needs. By providing short-term employment opportunities, Cash-for-work projects allow participants to generate a resilience income through which they are able to access basic needs such as food and medicine. It therefore contributes to immediately support the food security of vulnerable people, and improves the well-being of the community by rehabilitating or improving communal infrastructures.
The plan aims to address multiple Early Recovery sector objectives, Global Communities will provide critically needed upgrades/rehabilitation/construction to the neglected and crisis-affected infrastructure as follows:
- Rehabilitation of 7.5km of the main roads in and outside the camp (new asphalt layer) with all the manholes and drainage infrastructure.
- Implementing sidewalks through cash for work approach next to the new rehabilitated roads
- Establishing and training six rescue teams during the rainy season to assist in responding to the floods risks
- Rehabilitating 5 bakeries.
-Rehabilitating 10 schools.
The total required budget will be $778,500, and this will serve 75,000 people.

4 - Needs

According to ERL assessments were conducted in 6,700 communities and 500 camps across Syria to inform the 2020 HNO, there are 9.9 million people inside Syria requiring Early Recovery and Livelihoods support given the cumulative impact of crisis on the population and broader macro-economic developments. While areas exposed to active hostilities have shrunk over time, populations continue to experience poor living standards. More than half of the working-age population are without access to sustained employment leading to poverty and contributing poor living standards. Youth without reliable employment may be exposed to a variety of risks including involvement in crime and trafficking. 25 per cent of basic and social infrastructure is destroyed, putting stress on communities’ access to water, education, health services, reliable energy and social safety. Lack of access to such services and livelihoods erodes households' living standards and weakens their capacity to cope with internal and external shocks. According to the same assessment;Idleb showed high Income Severity, Idleb and northern Hama showed high rates of severity for the market. Northern Aleppo showed high severity for the basic infrastructure.The prolonged conflict in Syria and the resulting humanitarian crisis have long-lasting effects on the lives of Syrians. The crisis notably highly affected the labor market, with millions of people been pushed into unemployment. Families thus suffer from a lack of income, while the prices of food and goods on the market are increasing.According to the ACU 5th edition of the annual thematic report “Schools in Syria” 12% of the schools used for teaching purposes are partially destroyed, and 24% of the classrooms need repairs of varying levels.

5 - Implementation of activities

Global Communities will provide critically needed upgrades/rehabilitation/construction to flood-affected infrastructure as follows: - Rehabilitation of 7.5 km of the main roads in and outside the camp (new asphalt layer) with all the manholes and drainage infrastructure- Implementing sidewalks through cash for work approach next to the new rehabilitated roads- Establishing and training six rescue teams during the rainy season to assist in responding to the floods risks-Rehabilitating 5 local economic infrastructure for increasing their capacity for covering the newly arrivals.-Rehabilitating 10 education facility. The rehabilitation listed above will target damaged and neglected infrastructure to improve drainage capacity as well as the population's access to basic services. The activities will include; 1.Community engagement: collecting detailed information about the specific roads and infrastructures require rehabilitation, through FGDs, KIIs, Observations with all the stakeholders. 2.Topographic surveys for the selected locations, developing the BoQs and the technical specification. 3. Employ youth in cash for work schemes to rehabilitate basic infrastructure4.HLP due diligence for the targeted locations with the proposed rehabilitation activities. 4.Procurement process: through transparent procurement process, advertising and receiving offers from the bidders, opening bids, TEC, and signing the contract with the selected contractor. 5.Implementation for the roads rehabilitation, drainage systems rehabilitation and construction, and supervising the implementation of the rehabilitation and construction works. Along the implementation of the activities we will have an ongoing monitoring for the implementation.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

During the implementation of the planned activities, we expect the following risks; For the CFW, there is a high probability of risk of increased community tension due to different assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria or poorly timed distributions leading to a feeling of discrimination. Tension can also arise between host community and IDPs, due to a lack of assistance to the host community. this can be mitigated by 1. Standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners. 2. Ensure beneficiary selection criteria is uniform/agreed among sector members and with the government and is clearly communicated widely within a community and among different groups. 3. Establish complaint mechanism at community level. 4. Training to humanitarian staffs. Method and location of distribution of cash or vouchers can put vulnerable beneficiaries at risk of robbery or extortion. Risk of cash and or voucher value diversion to non-humanitarian purposes, this can be mitigated by 1. Distribution methodology with clear protection risks analyzed and mitigated.2. Home delivered rather than at distribution location where beneficiaries could be targeted.3. Verified beneficiary list to prevent cash or voucher value being misused for other non humanitarian purposes. There is medium likelihood for the risk of child labour and sexual exploitation, this can be mitigated by advocating for provision of alternative accommodation/expansion of existing sites, raising the awareness and building capacities of communities and authorities in the prevention of gender-based violence, particularly sexual violence, and the provision of appropriate support to victims. Identify detrimental coping mechanisms such as child labour or survival sex, and develop appropriate preventive and responsive measures in collaboration with communities, authorities and other relevant actors.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The following risks and assumptions are expected while implementing the activities; 1.Fake beneficiaries / Duplication / Non-adherence to selection criteria. 2.Selecting service provider with low capacity (technical/financial/project management) 3.Implementing of unneeded rehabilitation works or over reporting. 4.Procuring with high price, Over budget, A contractor with low capacity/quality 5.Safety and Security risk during the implementation 6.Market failure - Limited supply and large demand The existing and planned mitigating measures; -Review assessments by senior staff and return to field team with correction action -Feedback mechanism -Beneficiary registration and verification process -Post Distribution Monitoring -Proper inventory management -Reviewing the data/ purchasing requests by senior technical team member, send correction notes to the field. -Precise market assessments -Negotiation process -Safety procedures. One of the red lines defined in GC risks matrix is requests for payment of taxes or duties on aid deliveries or humanitarian services to beneficiaries, this can be mitigated by market assessment, security analysis and risk mitigation, improving programming across the board, and ensuring that programmes are adapted to context and that the most appropriate intervention is implemented. PDMs should be completed in a timely manner Good/trusted communication [always to be better developed] Do No Harm/accountability guidelines/policy.
