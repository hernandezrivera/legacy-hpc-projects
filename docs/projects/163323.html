Project Summary

Grace Chin Foundation through this project aims to provide sanitary pads to schoolgirls in formal and informal learning centers from the crisis areas of Cameroon to increase the rate of school attendance despite the period flow which causes discomfort during classes when there are no sanitary pads. Adolescents in the crisis areas of Cameroon find it difficult going through extremely painful periods, which affects their attendance and performance in school. The lack of sanitary pads restricts movements of the girls during their periods; they feel ashamed and unclean which also contributes to them skipping classes, hence poor performances in school. The age group of girls to receive these sanitary pads ranges from 10-18years; they are victims from the crisis, most especially the girls from the bushes, orphans and displaced girls. This project will cover the whole of Fako division including towns and villages like Buea, Limbe, Ekona, Bonakanda, Bokwai, Bova, Muea, Tiko, Tole and a host of other communities in this division. The project also aims at training young girls from ages 16 and above including teachers and parents on producing organic sanitary pads that are reusable, this is to ensure a constant supply of comfortable and healthy sanitary pads to the girls to help boost their confidence and participation while they study. In order to improve the quality of education, this project aims at educating young girls, mothers and the entire community on menstruation, to end the myth, stigma and harmful gender norms that are affecting women in these region. To teach them on menstrual hygiene and curb the negative ideas they have about the natural occurrence. The project is targeting 5,000 girls to receive the sanitary pads. 2,000 parents including teachers and girls will be trained on producing organic sanitary pads in the different communities we will be visiting. The project will also provide teaching and learning materials to those partaking in the trainings. A total of 200 learning centers will be visited in the South West region precisely in Fako Division for teaching and provision of sanitary pads.

Besoins / Needs

1) Needs Girls from the ages of 10-18 years in the crisis affected areas of Cameroon undergo a lot of struggles in terms of providing sanitary pads for themselves especially orphans who have lost their parents in the crisis. This affects the level of interaction and performance in school as many find it hard sitting without sanitary pads in class for fear of embarrassment; they chose to stay at home during school hours to evade the shame. Many of these girls miss school during their periods. A research done by United Nations estimates suggest that 1 in 10 girls from Sub-Saharan Africa miss school during their menstrual period, with some missing out on 20% of their education, this is a general estimate without a crisis but in the two crisis affected regions in Cameroon, the case is worst as most of the people are displaced, some not working, others are orphans and single mothers who can barely fend for themselves, and so there are a lot of difficulties providing sanitary pads which are expensive and inaccessible to the young girls attending school. By giving them the knowledge and means to manage their menstrual hygiene effectively, we’re making sure they do not miss out on their studies. 80% of young girls in these crisis affected areas have little or no knowledge about menstruation and menstrual hygiene, most parents find it hard discussing the natural occurrence with the young girls and those without parents to teach them end up confused. Menstruation is seen as a taboo to discuss within the people including the young girls in Africa most especially Cameroon, it is seen as an embarrassment, a shameful thing. It causes exclusion from school, family life and social activities as some of the girls feel ashamed and unclean to even participate in class most especially when they lack sanitary pads to hold in the period flow. This is the case in most areas affected by the crisis. Since the ending of 2016, schools have not been effective with most of the girls living in bushes using leaves to pad themselves, in the long run may cause health challenges if not properly handled. With the coming of safe centers to rehabilitate them, they need to be educated on menstrual hygiene and sanitation so as to help them know more about menstruation and reduce their fear of embarrassment and shame which will result to an increase in their performance in school, causing them to feel comfortable and participate more in class in order to boost their level of education. More than 30 million girls and women are currently displaced due to conflict and disasters across the world. A very common and visible challenge they face is the ability to manage their menstruation safely, comfortably and with dignity. In an emergency setting like South West region with an ongoing crisis, the lack of basic materials like sanitary pads to hold in their period flow is frustrating and traumatizing to many. A research done by Plan International shows that 1 in 2 girls experience issues around the affordability of sanitary products and also that 1 in 2 girls did not find school helpful for providing information on menstruation reason why we need to teach teachers on the sensitive nature of menstruation to these young girls. Plan international also concluded from its research that 6 in 10 girls are embarrassed to talk about their periods with family or teacher. With adequate knowledge and a period-shame free society, we can improve attendance and performance at school, break down taboos and misconceptions around menstruation, raise the self esteem of girls and enable girls to fully participate in all aspects of society.
