Project Summary


The shelter and NFI assistance project will target IDPs and returnees in three BAY states; Borno, Adamawa and Yobe with provision of new emergency and transitional shelters, emergency shelter repairs, shelter kits and Non-food Items (NFIs) to contribute to the physical well-being and dignity of PoCs and as protection assistance aligned with Shelter/ NFI/ CCCM Sector strategic objectives for the HRP 2022. The project will also ensure holistic durable solutions approach led by the Government, mainly to enable local integration of IDPs in Borno State, reintegration of refugee returnees from Adamawa origin and support to the host community in building resilience and ease the pressure on them.
Therefore in 2022, UNHCR shall build upon progress being made in 2021 in providing shelter and NFI support to respond to unmet needs in Adamawa (Mubi North, Mubi South, Madagali, Michika, Yola South and Yola north); Borno (Bama, Banki, Damasak, Damboa, Dikwa, Jere, konduga, Gwoza, Pulka, Maiduguri, Mobbar, Monguno, Rann and Ngala) and Yobe State (Damaturu and Gujba,) through provision of; (1) 20,000 emergency shelters (2) 1000 reinforced/transitional, (3 ) 25,000 NFIs kits and (4) 15,000 emergency repairs targeting IDPs and Returnees households both in formal and informal settlements, suffering from protracted displacement and those returning home. To assess and ascertain the level of beneficiary satisfaction with regards to shelter and NFI support, UNHR plans to carry out 5 post construction/distribution monitoring.

1 - Needs

In line with the strategic objective 1 of the 2019-2021 Humanitarian Response Strategy - Save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and protection interventions to the most vulnerable, UNHCR intends to contribute to fostering a favorable protection environment as the crisis in the northeast, continues to unravel with no end in sight; Internally Displaced populations, particularly in the BAY states, continue to face immense needs regarding Shelter and NFIs. The revised 2019-2021 HRP highlights that out of the 7.9 million people in need, more than 1 million are in need of shelter support. According to Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Round 37 (August 2021), there were a total of 2,191,193 Internally Displaced Persons (445,852 households) across Adamawa, Bauchi, Borno, Gombe, taraba and Yobe states. This signifying an increase of 0.3 per cent (or 6,939 individuals) IDPs, compared to the previous round (36 of DTM assessments) when 2,184,254 IDPs were recorded (May 2021). An estimated 91% of the displacement was attributed to the ongoing insurgency and counter insurgency operations with over 75% of displaced persons living in Borno State. Out of all six states, Borno state continued to be the only state where the number of people residing in camps or camp-like settings exceeded the number of IDPs living in host communities. Fifty-two per cent of IDPs in Borno lived in camps or camp- like settings while 48 per cent of IDPs lived among host communities. These displaced populations are hosted across 32 official Government run camps and over 300 settlements. Close to 35% of the IDPs reside in three LGAs in Borno (Maiduguri, Jere and Monguno). The statistics show that more than 50% of all IDPs in camps (some 426,115 IDPs) live in inadequate shelters and living spaces. The current spacing in IDPS camps is well below the Sphere standard (45sqm/person). Out of the 273 camps in the BAY states, 73 camps and sites (26.7%) housing 424,515 individuals are considered highly congested where IDPs are living in less than 19sqm/person. The CCCM/Shelter/NFIs Cluster estimates that globally, about half of IDPs households live in inadequate shelters or do not have access to shelter altogether. In 2022, UNHCR plans to cover an estimated 24% of all shelter/NFI needs. There will remain a gap of some 76% that need to be bridged. According to the Sector (CCCM/Shelter/NFI) site tracker, there were over 137,000 IDP Households in need of NFIs as of the middle of August 2021. The needs of IDPs in the Northeast for basic and domestic items is an indicator of PoCs vulnerabilities and this has continued to deteriorate with no abating. The combined multiple efforts by humanitarian actors has reduced the number of IDPs in need of NFIs, which, however, is still an immense representation of the brutal impact of suffering and disruption of normal life in the affected LGAs.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The key protection principles underpinning the provision of humanitarian assistance informed the design of this project and as such will be observed during the project implementation. UNHCR will adopt a rights-based approach in the planning, implementation and evaluation during the entirety of the project. To ensure comprehensive protection mainstreaming across this project, the following measures will be taken for each of the protection principles:• Prioritize safety and dignity and avoid doing harm during the project implementation, UNHCR will closely monitor the security situation of the area of intervention, and mobile teams will use safe and authorized roads to access people in need.• To ensure meaningful and equal access, in the identification of beneficiaries, men, women, boys and girls will be prioritized based on need alone. Particularly vulnerable groups such as female- and child-headed households, but also elderly and persons with disability will be prioritized. The composition of the assessment teams will reflect gender balance, to facilitate contact with women and girls.• If necessary, additional measures will be taken to reach persons with disabilities and others extremely vulnerable. This can be the case of disabled persons unable to reach the protection desks.• In order to promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries, beneficiaries’ participation to the project will be promoted by involving, to the widest extent possible, men, women, boys and girls of different ages in the community-based structures. • Establish measures aimed at preventing and responding to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, bearing in mind the UN zero tolerance policy.• All along project implementation, UNHCR will monitor protection, age, and gender aspects and will address any arising issues. To ensure a comprehensive response, all the findings will be shared with the Protection Sector as well as other relevant Sectors, with whom UNHCR will maintain close coordination.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR will strengthen community-based complaint/feedback mechanisms, which will allow men, women, boys and girls to express any complaint; beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanisms, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. Child friendly complaint boxes will be introduced in child friendly areas as well as in educational structures. The existing tollfree number and email will be vulgarized and peer to peer approach will be established to support reporting of complaint. Focus group discussions with people from different age, gender and diverse origin will be conducted regularly to collect feedback from affected population on available services and as well as protection risks. UNHCR will ensure that committees of women, men, boys and girls are in place in order to participate fully in the implementation of activities. Through these, UNHCR will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention as well as to the attention of other partners, for intervention. UNHCR will leverage on its active membership of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and its Age Gender Diversity mainstreaming policy too. UNHCR and partners will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the SG’s special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.
