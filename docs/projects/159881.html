Project Summary

The project will provide essential and life-saving integrated health assistance to vulnerable communities in Awdal, Bakool, Banadir, Bari, Bay, Galgaduud, Hiran, Lower Juba, Mudug, Nugaal, Sanaag, Sool and aims to reach a total of 265,800 IDPs and host communities of the cluster target in the targeted geographies.
The project will seek to contribute to a 5% reduction in excess morbidity and mortality rates from crisis-driven diseases and outbreaks among target vulnerable communities by end of 2020 while scaling up provision of regular health services and providing safe, dignified and meaningful access to life-saving assistance (provided directly or through referral) with sensitivity to age, gender and disability status.
This will be achieved by supporting the Somalia governments and communities to be able to delivery essential package of health free services to communities with acute humanitarian needs using development programs as a platform for immediate responses. The required budget for the delivery of the project is USD 3.6 million.

Mitigation Measures

Lense 1: Protection of civilians SC will ensure that beneficiaries will not be exposed to danger when receiving assistance including landmines or explosive remnants of war. SC will not be asking beneficiaries to travel to far places in order to receive assistance. In addition, SC will provide assistance where communities are. To minimize the risk of making humanitarian assistance as a pull factor for people to access it where it is being offered, SC will employ geo targeting where support will be channeled to places where the need is greatest e.g. areas of IPC 3 or worse and to IDPs particularly. SC is aiming to cover as many beneficiaries as possible so that those left will not have to migrate. In addition, one of the criteria of admitting beneficiaries to the program will be that the person receiving assistance is from the target community.Lense 2: Exclusion and marginalization In Somalia IDPs and minority groups are marginalized. Clans who believe are better and more powerful take the lions share if not all of the resources. Host communities or gatekeepers in the IDP camps may sometimes manipulate IDPs. In both IDPs and host communities, families headed by categorically vulnerable and weak individuals including the elderly, children, women and disabled are marginalized and their rights are taken by so called more physical and/or societally powerful. The project will deliberately prevent risk of exclusion of most vulnerable and marginalized groups (based on gender, age, minority clans, IDPs, returnees, persons with disabilities, etc) by taking the following:• Awareness raising and sensitization: SC will start sensitizing stakeholders including community leaders and local authorities on the objective, processes and targets of the project. The organization will than meet people in target communities in public places where we will ensure all those who can attend come to the meeting. In these meetings, information about the project including objective, entitlements, duration, targeting criteria, targeting approach will be shared. • Setting community response mechanism: The action will establish community response and feedback mechanism. A hotline will be provided and time will be given before registration. During verification and mobilization, SC MEAL team will provide a help desk. SC will mitigate the impacts of conflict and violence by targeting IDPs and returnees including the ones evicted from urban areas. The organization will take a context assessment where it will identify the hot spots of new arrivals in the target vicinity, and new evicted communities. Lense 3: Interference of external stakeholders in impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries SC will avoid community tensions between displaced and host communities by providing the local/host community with access to services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of health services. SC will consult both displaced and host communities about the health needs and assess whether assistance could cause tension, harassment or conflict between communities. The organization will engage both groups during community mobilization, project sensitization and will ensure that all groups are represented in any project structures. SC will use vulnerability criteria to enroll access of people to the services, which will be made very clear during community engagement. Communities will be given sufficient information about the project including how they can access, selection criteria, selection process and entitlements. SC will sensitize communities on how best they can provide their feedback about the program and therefore establish safe and accessible CRM. Beneficiary data will be segregated sex and gender but will keep lists of beneficiaries and personal data confidential and safe from unintended use - apply effective data protection measures. Please provide further explanation

Due to the protracted nature of displacement in Somalia, past assistance to IDPs has mainly focused on sustaining humanitarian responses with limited efforts in supporting IDPs to become self-reliant. Hence the proposed Health response will go beyond provision of access to health services and will strive for transformation. Health as a critical need and priority during emergency, early recovery and longer term recovery efforts in Somalia. The recovery measures to be undertaken by the project will be creating of sustainable Health services as well as community and institutional capacity building. The project is designed in such a way that while providing emergency lifesaving interventions, it will also provide longer term sustainable solutions for affected populations. To ensure the emergency interventions are linked to durable solutions, the project includes establishment and equipping of emergency trauma centers and promotion of community based health services.
