Project Summary

Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) report (November 2024) indicates that 769 million South Sudanese (57%) of the population will face severe food insecurity (IPC 3+) by end of July 2025. In Fangak, 132,000 (over 65%) of the people will face severe food insecurity. In Koch County, 76,000 (over 74.6%), 60,000 in Leer (over 74.8%) while in Canal/Pigi 76,000 people (over 69%) of the people will face IPC 3+ conditions.
The main causes of food insecurity are climate related shocks, manly floods which have destroyed farms, livelihoods and resulted in displaced populations. Over four consecutive years of flooding have been experienced in these counties. Crops were destroyed before harvesting and in storage. Livestock died through drowning and starvation as grazing grounds were flooded. Conflicts witnessed at both sub-national levels and at regional level including spillover effects of conflict in Sudan that has increased pressure on humanitarian needs. Coupled with flooding, are intensified sub-national violence arising from political instability, cattle raids and revenge killings. Violence causes instability limiting access to land for cultivation but also results in deaths of productive members of the HH. Agricultural productivity has been persistently low and coping capacity of these populations has been eroded. At the Macro-economic level, depreciation of the local currency and effects of War in Ukraine have made food more expensive and scarcer. Coping strategies of affected households have been exhausted and household assets eroded. Without timely humanitarian intervention, this situation could worsen and plunge populations into famine situation.
The goal of this intervention is to support the vulnerable populations in these selected counties with livelihood kits and training so as to help them restore and sustain their livelihoods. This intervention will be guided by two cluster objectives (CO). The second CO which is to enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock. The third CO which is to enhance resilience among the affected population and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. It is expected that through this intervention, target populations will be able to supplement food provided through general food distribution which has proved to be irregular and therefore unreliable but also increases dependency to external support systems. The intervention will also contribute towards reduction in flood related displacements in the target counties. This will be achieved through disaster risk reduction strategies that will build the community capacity on early warning signs and mitigation measures including construction of dykes to control flow of water. The project will also ensure minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.
A total of 163,440 HH will be reached including 40,080HH in Koch, 80,400HH in Fangak and 42,960HH in Canal/Pigi who are facing IPC 3+ food insecurity will be targeted for this intervention, comprising of 60% women, elderly, youth, disabled and other vulnerable groups will be targeted for crop and vegetable kits. Out of this number, a total of 24,516HHs will be targeted for training on improved agronomic methods, post-harvest handling and utilization. Similarly, 16,344HHs will be targeted with fishing kits, including training on appropriate harvesting methods and fish preservation. To help improve sustainable access to quality seeds, the project will work with 900HHs (300 in each county) to produce high quality seeds and avail them to other farmers for subsequent use.
The intervention will run from January to December of 2025.

1. Needs

The Greater Upper Nile (GUN) region, particularly the counties of Koch in Unity State as well as Fangak and Pigi/Canal in Jonglei have persistently poor food security and livelihoods indicators. This situation is as a result of floods that have persisted in the country for more than four consecutive years. Floods affected crops in farms and reduced the cultivated areas across the GUN region. Livestock have also been affected by the floods leading to massive deaths or relocation of livestock to raised areas away from population settlement. Floods have also destroyed physical infrastructure, particularly roads. This has affected flow of goods. Adok port which is main point of entry into southern unity has remained flooded and unavailable for docking. Inflow of basic food items has been hindered leading to unavailability of food and high food prices. Similarly, this region has continued to be affected by intercommunal conflicts which have affected livelihood activities. Inter and intra-communal conflicts manifesting as revenge killings and cattle raids lead to population displacements. This displacement greatly affects farming activities. Conflict also greatly affects availability of food commodities. Supply routes and Market closures affect supply of food in markets. Tonga market in Panyikang county is a main source of food supplies in pigi/canal and greater Fangak. Sporadic conflicts in upper Nile state affect supply of food to this market which worsens the food insecurity situation. Tension between Koch, Mayendit and Leer communities in Southern Unity over land issues has continued to hinder agricultural production as communities feel unsafe to access land for cultivation. Conflicts and effects of climate change within the greater horn of Africa also negatively affect livelihoods activities in South Sudan. The conflict in Sudan let to influx of returnees and refugees into South Sudan. Koch and Leer counties in Unity State as well as Fangak and Pigi/Canal in Jonglei State have received continued to receive returnees since the onset of conflict in Sudan. These refugees/returnees continue to exert pressure of food resources that are already scarce. conflicts in other parts of the world, including Ukraine and Palestine have continued to affect humanitarian assistance. Humanitarian aid is redirected to these needs and therefore further compromising the food needs in the country. conflicts also affect flow of food supplies in the world market and therefore making food scarce or more expensive. Elnino conditions which are currently affecting the whole of greater horn of Africa region will have the net effect of reducing agricultural production due to floods or prolonged dry spells which affect crop and livestock production. Crop and Food Security Assessment Mission Report of 2023 notes that food production across the country will still be in deficit. Communities are forced to adopt copying strategies including collecting wild foods in order to survive. The most vulnerable segments of the community are the worst affected by the food insecurity. The elderly, disabled, children, pregnant and lactating women are worse affected and are the target of this intervention.

2. Project activities

Project activities will be structured around cluster objectives two and three. Cluster Objective two: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock. Under this objective, main activity will Emergency food production focusing on main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fishing, vegetable & crop kits. Dry season activities will include Emergency livelihood support through provision of fishing kits/ vegetable kits; and at least three variety of fast maturing vegetable seeds. Dry season farming will focus on water efficient farming methods to produce sufficient and nutritious vegetables for consumption and possibly sell for some cash. This is to ensure increased availability of food even during the dry season where availability of vegetables becomes a challenge and therefore complicates the food insecurity and malnutrition situation. Main season activities will concentrate on crop production specifically maize, sorghum and legumes. Fisheries activities will cut across the main season and dry season as there are sufficient fishing grounds for both seasons. Cluster Objective three: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. The main activity will be Training and capacity building on both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production targeting, farmers through field schools and on demonstration plots as well as the agropastoralists. WR will also involve communities in construction of community assets through conditional food assistance. Intended activities will include construction of dykes around farms to prevent perennial flooding, excavation of drainage channels, construction/rehabilitation of access roads, de-silting water pans to collect water during the rainy season, excavation of shallow wells. Food For Assets intervention will help communities establish communal assets, mitigate effects of flooding on food production and enhance preparedness and response to flooding which has become a yearly occurrence in the target counties.All activities will be implemented so as to establish a nexus between Humanitarian response, Peace building (conflict sensitivity) and Resilience programming. A strong emphasis will be laid on accountability to affected population to ensure the do-no-harm principle is observed. There will be a strong coordination at county level with other partners to reduce duplication and ensure efficiency and wider coverage. The project will emphasis integration with other sectors to achieve greater impact in meeting household needs. For instance, the project will support establishment of kitchen garden within Nutrition centers and target mothers and caregivers of children enrolled in nutrition program with training and input support. This is to ensure that those children discharged from nutrition program do not relapse but have sufficient and nutritious food at home. The project will also incorporate WASH messaging at home, especially with reference to preparation and utilization of food, human waste disposal and provision of WASH items including water containers and purification tablets, cooking utensils and food preservation containers from other partners. To improve access to milk and household income, the project will provide small ruminants to caregivers of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) children within the Nutrition centres. This will be implemented through the mother-to-mother strategy and Care groups for both distributions and training. The project will carry out regular monitoring with participation of Boma management committees, and will develop and share project reports on monthly, quarterly and annual basis
