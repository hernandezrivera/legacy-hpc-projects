Project Summary

In South Sudan, only 36 per cent of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. The remaining 64 per cent of people across the country rely on unimproved or surface water sources (41 per cent), either take more than 30 minutes to reach an improved water source (19 per cent), or are able to reach an improved source in less than 30 minutes but face protection concerns while accessing the water (4 per cent). The highest proportion of households relying on surface water were found in Upper Nile, with 66 per cent, followed by Jonglei with 43 per cent and Central Equatoria with 41 per cent. The quantity of water available per day is also below standards in many locations, with 17 per cent of people unable to collect enough water for drinking, and 39 per cent of people reporting insufficient access to water for hand washing.
Access to sanitation remained low, with only 17 per cent of households reporting owning a latrine in their compound, 4 per cent have access to a shared latrine, and 2 per cent have access to a communal/institutional latrine. This means that 77 per cent of people do not have access to a latrine. In 34 counties, zero to 10 per cent of households reported using latrines. At the country level, open defecation is practiced by 73 per cent of the population.
Regional access to sanitation varied greatly, from 75 per cent in Western Bahr el Ghazal to 8 per cent in Warrap, 9 per cent in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and 10 per cent in Jonglei;
Poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continue to have a detrimental impact on the health of children, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases. Counties reporting high GAM rates have also high WASH needs. Mothers and care takers of malnourished children lack basic hygiene knowledge and access to safe water at domicile jeopardizing the benefits again.in nutrition programs, WVISS intends to reach the IPDs and Host community with an integrated WASH intervention which will target the population of Upper Nile (Melut, Renk, Malakal, Kodok, Baliet, Panikang counties), Western Equatoria (Yambio, Nzara, Ezo, Nagero, Tambura), Northern Bahr El Ghazal (Aweil North and East) Warrap ( Twic, Tonj North, Gogrial West and Gogrial East), and Central Equatoria (Juba) states. The proposed project by WVISS is aiming to achieve the following specific objectives: Vulnerable people who experience multi-sectoral severity levels of 4 and 5 have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral essential services to meet their needs. Vulnerable people are exposed to fewer protection threats and incidents, and those who are exposed have access to tailored, timely, appropriate services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action. Vulnerable people withstand and recover from shocks, have their resilience to shocks and stresses built, and seek solutions that respect their rights.
To realize the WASH cluster objectives, WVISS will provide lifesaving WASH emergency interventions in the targeted Counties in (Melut, Renk, Malakal, Kodok, Baliet, Panikang counties), Western Equatoria (Yambio, Nzara, Ezo, Nagero, Tambura), Northern Bahr El Ghazal (Aweil North and East) Warrap ( Twic, Tonj North, Gogrial West and Gogrial East), and Central Equatoria (Juba) states. The WASH emergency interventions will improve access to safe water, secured and gender appropriate sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion, which will be integrated in other sectors, among them health, nutrition, Food security and livelihood, and child protection/ GBV.

1 - Needs

The protracted armed conflict which started since December 2013 in the Republic of South Sudan has resulted in multiple, acute, and chronic needs among the conflict affected population, and stretched the population’s coping capacity. Two years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, its implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people. Lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services are holding people back from stability and sustainable development. According to the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview, the living standard and wellbeing of 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence andRecurring floods in many parts of the Country. These shocks have led to the continued displacement of people and restricted their access to already limited and functional WASH infrastructure. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites and creates barriers for WASH partners when implementing activities. 7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

In line with the Core Humanitarian Standards, WVI will maintain its commitment to engage with affected communities at all phases of the project cycle, through focus group discussions with women, men, boys and girls on issues concerning their education, WASH, and food security concerns. Establishing/strengthening local structures such as Project Management Committee is one example of how WVI engages the community in a sustainable and accountable manner to determine and support appropriate need-based responses. WVI's Quality Assurance framework ensures that each project implemented is carried out effectively and continually reviewed in line with community needs and in line with other humanitarian frameworks.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will conduct needs analysis of identified prior to implementation of project activities to identify needs of vulnerable groups. From this phase of project, World Vision will establish a system (complaint feedback mechanism) that makes smooth communication of every beneficiary’s feedback to World Vision. Individuals with disabilities and those who are illiterate will be given special attention by mobilizing them to participate in activities and provide feedback. Local translators will be trained on the project and asked to disseminate information to the illiterate beneficiaries. On the other hand, they would return feedback that were gathered to World Vision for analysis and use in the project. Local project committees that are formed in each area will be representatives from vulnerable groups including disabled, illiterate, women and girls.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Complaints regarding any protection-related issue are handled by a Protection Officer to ensure confidentiality and to be able to determine an appropriate response to the feedback received, particularly in the event that such responses require CP or GBV intervention from the case management team. Any complaint involving sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) will be immediately escalated to the human resources team in Juba. All WVI staff are trained on SEA and all have signed WVI ’s code of conduct. Cases of SEA will be investigated by a team led by WVI South Sudan’s human resources staff in collaboration with the Safeguarding Manager in a safe and confidential manner.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

World Vision will ensure that all staff are involved in the implementation of this project will involve and inform the beneficiaries and other key stakeholders at all stages of the project management cycle starting from the initial assessment, design, implementation and close out stages. The project participants will also be sensitized on their rights to launch complaints, express grievances, and give other forms of feedback about the implementation process, quantity and quality of services they are getting from the project. Communities are asked which complaints and feedback mechanisms they prefer, with efforts to ensure that unique needs of vulnerable populations are met (for example, considering child- and gender-friendly methods of collecting feedback, accounting for literacy or individuals’ safety, protection, and access to mechanisms, etc.) Project participant feedback is compiled and shared with the project teams on a weekly basis. The feedback is then verified and responded to by the relevant teams. To accurately respond to feedback and complaints, an investigation is conducted on each feedback item submitted. Thus, after the team verifies the concerns raised, corrective measures are taken and incorporated into project implementation.
