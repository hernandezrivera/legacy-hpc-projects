Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to increase access to lifesaving emergency healthcare services to vulnerable populations in Fangak and Nyirol. This project seeks to scale up the provision of Lifesaving emergency healthcare access to vulnerable populations focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality. According to HC bulletin (July 2018), a total of 7 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance. Out of this, it is estimated that 4,058,392 women, men and children will need humanitarian healthcare services in 2019. People in need are 82,791 in Fangak and 91,861 in Nyirol, including 26,200 IDPs in Fangak and 53,590 in Nyirol integrated into host communities. Cluster targets are 46,363 in Fangak and 51,442 in Nyirol (Cluster PIN 25 Oct 18). The payams in greatest need are Manajang, Paguir in Fangak and Waat, Pultruk and Pading in Nyirol.
This objective will be achieved by: 1). Increasing access to essential PHC and an integrated multi-sector assistance and services which address life threatening diseases as well as the medical complications associated with malnutrition, WASH and SGBV 2). Strengthening protection of and increasing access to specialized and integrated services for vulnerable women, men, girls and boys 3. Scaling up support to the most vulnerable as well as at-risk communities to sustain their resilience to recurrent shocks including promotion of emergency health protection for people with disabilities and 4). Improving prompt and quick response to outbreaks such as to cholera, kala-azar and other highly infectious diseases
To offer quality services, the project will prioritize capacity building of health and nutrition actors on standard procedures for screening and referral of SAM children with medical complications as well as Malaria RDT training. In the spirit of integration, the project will promote water quality testing and Monitoring in health facilities to mitigate disease spread and implement the integration of Health education messages across sectors on risk communication. Further, the project will promote collaboration with protection on Mapping of SGBV services and referrals.
This project is aligned to both the HRP as well as the health cluster strategic objectives and deliver services through PHCC, PHCU and mobile modalities. Activities will revolve around screening/detection for both health and nutrition problems, management of common illnesses, referral, prevention and surveillance/ EWARN reporting. It is envisaged that a successful implementation of these activities would significantly result in the overall improvement of access and scaling-up of responsiveness in the two counties as well as prevention, detection and response to epidemic prone diseases. Project activities will also achieve the dividends of an integrated response involving key sectors such as health, Wash, Nutrition, food security and protection.
For purposes of Gender and Protection Mainstreaming, the project will applying IASC guidance, measures to identify specific needs by: 1. involving the at-risk group women in project planning, implementation and monitoring 2. Implementing strategies that increase the safety, availability and accessibility of health and nutrition services for women, girls, boys and other at-risk groups especially pregnant adolescent girls and work with them to plan schedules that are convenient and safe for at-risk women, girls and boys 3. Implementing proactive strategies to meet the protection needs of most-at-risk groups accessing nutrition services 4.Designating a facility-based safety focal person to conduct safety audits and ensure staff know the referral pathway for victims of SGBV 5. Providing health education and gender awareness training to men and women on SGBV risk and the need for protection to secure that the most-at-risk groups have unimpeded access to health services.

Needs

Whereas challenges to health care provision in South Sudan are more or less similar, there are specific counties where the services have been compromised and hence bear a higher burden of disease prevalence. Nyirol and Fangak counties fall within this category. Through assessment and experience, CMA has established that the critical health needs in these two counties are:• Increased access to essential PHC through an integrated multi-sector assistance and services which address acute needs, life threatening diseases as well as the medical complications associated with malnutrition, WASH and human conflicts such as SGBV.• Improved protection of and access to specialized and integrated services by the vulnerable women, men, girls and boys• Scaling up support to the most vulnerable as well as at-risk communities to promote and sustain their resilience to recurrent shocks including promotion of emergency health protection for people with disabilities.• Strengthening a prompt and quick response to outbreaks such as to cholera, kala-azar and other highly infectious diseases through mobile approaches. In Fangak and Nyirol, poor access to healthcare services have been due to multiple factors including, conflicts, displacements, malnutrition, high disease burden from vaccine preventable and communicable diseases. Similarly, seasonal outbreaks of communicable diseases including cholera and measles with continued endemicity of malaria pose a challenge to a health system which is already fragile. These disease outbreaks tend to last longer hence weakening already vulnerable people’s ability to cope with multiple shocks. Conflict has caused continued breakdown of health facilities and resulted in interruption of essential services and remains one of the main drivers of morbidity and mortality in these counties. Displaced populations such as those in Chuil and Waat in Nyirol face the most complex challenges in accessing health care services. Health issues have further been compounded by malnutrition as conflict continues to disrupt the cereal markets. According to FEWSNET outlook (June-Sept 2018), Fangak and Nyirol are among the counties feared to be at risk of having households slump into Catastrophic (IPC Phase 5) soon. A SMART survey conducted in Nyirol in March found a GAM (WHZ) prevalence of 25.7%, close to the Famine threshold of 30%, and a SAM (WHZ) of 6.6 %. The Crude Death Rate was 1.70, with % of deaths due to trauma unknown. In the same survey, 4.1% of households reported severe HHS and 77% reported moderate hunger. This indicates a sharp deterioration which requires intervention. According to WHO report (Feb 2018), though the third dose of pentavalent vaccine coverage increased marginally from 45% in 2016 to 57% in 2017, the coverage for areas seriously hit by the crisis such as Fangak and Nyirol remains at 16%. Access to EPI services is constrained by inadequate static and outreach sessions, lack of appropriate planning for routine immunization in HFs; low capacity of EPI staff at all levels; shortages of vaccinators at facility level; sub-optimal functioning of the cold chain system etc. In targeted counties, 70% of HF structures are in need of rehabilitation and lack a secure place to store pharmaceuticals and equipment for delivery of quality healthcare. The communities in greatest need are where IDPs have integrated and settled, and where services have been interrupted due to conflict and floods. To serve this population, a scale up in delivery of outreach services delivered from functional facilities and through mobile teams is needed. The rise in SGBV has heightened the need for comprehensive care of victims of SGBV. Women, adolescent girls and children are most at risk. This reality coupled with conflict, insecurity and flooding causes severe restrictions to access health services. Special medical outreaches and community-based protection measures are needed to ensure PLW and children have access to services.

Activities

This project will be aligned to both the HRP as well as the health cluster strategic objectives. This project will implement activities planned around key clinical packages such as PHCCs, PHCUs and mobile team approach with emphasis on ICCM/CHP. The activities will revolve around screening/detection for both health and nutrition problems, essential health care package, management of common illnesses, referral, prevention and surveillance/ EWARN reporting. In order to establish a disease causal organism with precision under Screening/detection, the project will leverage on technology and utilize the same to conduct screening for malnutrition with MUAC, rapid testing for malaria for under 5 and pregnant women, screening for pregnancy, and screening for uncomplicated diseases. On the other hand, under Essential Health care Package, the project will focus on key activities namely management of uncomplicated MAM in areas with severe food insecurity through OTP, TSFP, Integrated community case management (ICCM) involving Management of uncomplicated cases of diarhoea, Management of uncomplicated cases of respiratory infection and Management of uncomplicated cases of malaria, and when required provison of RUTF for SAM cases with medical complications. Under Referral, the project will embark and therefore promote activities such as referral of SAM cases to PHCC with Stabilization Centre or County Hospital, referral of pregnant women for ANC to PHCU, referral for delivery by skilled health care worker, referral of SGBV survivors and referral of complicated cases. In order to consolidate activities under prevention, the project will focus on the provision of Folic Acid distribution for pregnant women, Vit A supplementation for under 5 every 6 months, Malaria prophylaxis for pregnant women, deworming treatment for under 5 every 6 months, training CHW on HIV, Health promotion/risk communication Under Surveillance/EWARN Reporting, the project will engage in the activities such as active case-finding (epidemic-prone diseases and TB), results of screening, family records: birth and death registration (including maternal deaths) It is envisaged that a successful implementation of these activities would significantly result in the overall improvement of access and scaling-up of responsiveness in the two counties as well as prevention, detection and response to epidemic prone diseases. Besides improving resilience among vulnerable populations, the activities are further expected to achieve the dividends of an integrated response involving key sectors such as health, Wash, Nutrition, food security and protection. Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Through quarterly meetings (more frequent if required), the project keeps the community well informed about the assistance being provided, their rights and entitlement as well as detailed plans for outreaches and other implementation concern. Further, community meetings are used to seek the community’s support to identify vulnerable households for action by project personnel.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In the regular meetings with Women Groups and in counseling victims and survivors of SGBV, messages are tailored to specific target group needs. When men are part of the target group for health education, messages are adapted to their gender roles and responsibilities for child and pregnant mother’s health

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project addresses protection from sexual abuse at two levels: (1) in the community as the threat of abuse and exploitation is a barrier to accessing health and nutrition services and (2) at the facility level in terms of safety of the facility as patients access services. In the community, the project seeks to reach those with access issues through outreaches and by promoting community-based protection approaches to ensure all have equal access. At the facility, the time-of-day of service delivery is planned in consultation with patients to ensure it is appropriate for women, boys and girls and does not expose them to risk of sexual abuse, and the conduct of personnel are closely monitored to ensure there is no coercion or sexual exploitation of patients.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Project community consultations go well beyond community leaders and the usual constituencies. The project reaches men and women in focus groups and on an individual basis as key informants. Where committees which are made up of key community leaders have been set up to monitor and offer protection to the vulnerable, these groups are clearly very important in broadening consultation as they provide vital information on household and gender related realities that inform project plans and strategies. Similarly, their leadership positions also make them accessible to the vulnerable who perceive them as sources of protection.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Each health facility supported under this project will have a feedback and complaint mechanism established as a pillar of project approach to the accountability to affected populations. Again, in the complaints and feedback mechanism, the project will consult with users on an individual, key informant and focus group basis as well as through community meetings to establish a viable, transparent and effective mechanism for delivering feedback and complaints and to seek input to finding resolution to complaints. In areas where the Boma Health Committees (BHC)/Boma Health Initiatives (BHI) have been established, such institutions are treated as an authentic layer for community representation in as far as their grievances/complaints are concerned. Through a joint meeting with the project team the complaints are enumerated, discussed and feedback provided.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Both the nature and quality of community engagement are important elements of project monitoring and evaluation. Community engagement is monitored as part of the ongoing process of project implementation. This is done through the regular community meetings, meetings with BHCs and also through interviews with key informants, focus group discussions and gathering the perspectives of community leaders.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

At each location, the project has a strong awareness of protection risks of vulnerable populations (elderly, PLW, adolescent girls, boys and girls especially for IDPs) due to experience gained through both nutrition and health programming. However a formal protection risk assessment as presented in the SS Protection Mainstreaming Toolkit (final edition) Dec 2015, has not yet been undertaken. Resources have been allocated to undertake protection risk assessments at each project site in 2019. The assessments, risk mapping and analysis will inform ongoing programme planning beginning 2019.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Project personnel have a wealth of knowledge on barriers to access to health and nutrition services based on experience at the field level in both health and nutrition programming. IDPs, PLW and children all face these barriers. More will be learned through completing the protection risk mapping and assessment, which will help inform location specific protection plans applying the protection mainstreaming toolkit. Presently, to mitigate these access barriers, CMA conducts a robust outreach approach to serve populations facing access barriers, and promotes community-based protection approaches to enable vulnerable people to access nutrition services. On the other hand, Community leaders are constantly engaged so as to ensure that their jurisdictions are free from unnecessary barriers to the vulnerable who are desirous of seeking access to both health and nutrition services.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Project activities do address inequalities and protection threats mainly through a robust outreach approach to serve populations facing access barriers and by promoting community-based protection approaches to enable vulnerable people to access health and nutrition services. Completion of the protection risk assessment and analysis will better inform project strategy and plans. Further, at the facility (employee) level, the protection focal person is responsible to ensure that all personnel conduct their duties in an ethical manner without threat of coercion, violence or mistreatment of patients in anyway.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project measures beneficiary access to health services through facility-based and community based safety audits/assessment including community consultations, focus group discussions and gathering knowledge from key informants. Through this approach, both the reasons for access constraints and measures that need to be put in place to mitigate access constraints are learned.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Safety issues pertaining to beneficiaries are discussed with local authorities especially the payam and boma administrators/authorities. Nevertheless, this project will conduct facility-based and community-based safety audits/assessment to give clear measure of safety issues. Presently without a completed formal protection risk analysis, the project relies on local leaders, focus group sessions and responses of key informants for knowledge on safety issues faced by nutrition patients as they access services. Women Support Groups have been critical in the identification of safety issues. This same group of local representatives also help to mitigate and resolve recognised safety issues of beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

As with beneficiary safety, issues of dignity of beneficiaries is also monitored through consultations with local leaders, focus groups and responses of key informants especially Women Groups. These community-based structures have been very valuable resources for recognizing dignity issues, especially for the ultra-vulnerable mentally handicapped, orphaned children, women headed households etc. At the employee level, CMA defines clearly how personnel are to conduct themselves in delivery of services, and any reported mistreatment or misconduct of project personnel that shows a lack of respect for patients is dealt with quickly. The project will conduct safety audits/assessments at facility level and community level which will provide more insight into dignity issues.
