Project Summary

Building on experience of project interventions in Syria since 2013, Oxfam is planning to
work with local authorities supporting the most vulnerable populations in districts/sub-districts of Aleppo, Hama, Idlib, Rural Damascus, Dar’a and Deir E Zor, based on the severity of needs, and in response to further emerging needs in NES and NWS. The sub-districts that will be targeted by this project have all been heavily affected by
conflict and require WASH support. This is based on several rapid Oxfam Key Informant assessments in some of the selected villages, consultations with LNGOs, INGOs, and secondary data, including the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2019. The project is designed to meet specific life-saving needs around water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), and is strongly aligned to the WASH Sector. It is gender mainstreamed, with a well-defined response strategy suitable for the access situation and security challenges; implementation engages reliable and experienced local site engineers, community resource people and focal points, carefully screened private contractors. There is strong collaborations with other actors. The total estimated population, as per UN-OCHA datasets in these sub-districts, is over 1.1m people. This estimation is initially a rough guide, given that many people have been displaced, with many IDP and returnee fluctuations within Governorates and Districts across Syria, both before, during and post intervention. Target locations have been proposed according to severity of humanitarian needs and
number of population in need. They may be altered depending on the evolution of the context on the ground, access and people's movement. The project aims to support life-saving WASH interventions that additionally pay attention to ensuring locally sustained maintenance of repaired existing WASH systems: The objectives of the project are; a) provide emergency response to WASH needs through provision of WASH equipment and materials, including NFI distribution to the most vulnerable; b) support the enabling environment to deliver, operate and maintain WASH services at the technical level; c) improve the target communities' access to safe water and sewage infrastructure and solid waste management services, d) provide conservation and hygiene awareness, and standardised and inclusive WASH facilities at schools and community health facilities. The type of interventions strongly align with: SO1, SO2 and SO4 which captures the sector efforts to respond and assist local water establishments in restoring WASH services to basic functionality for communities and schools. Oxfam will target all age groups for water and sanitation interventions. Hygiene interventions, will target head of households and adolescent girls with a concerted focus on children as a proportion of the activities will be conducted at school level. Accurate dissegregation of population data is still to be defined. To ascertain this, WASH assessments and interventions will be facilitated and supervised by three area office WASH teams, utilising household surveys, and key informant interviews with ministries, directorates, LWE officials, local authorities, and other key community leaders and WASH Sector actors to ensure coordinated and relevant data and appropriate interventions. For some years, Oxfam has had unique MOUs with several ministries with which to gain trust, strong relations and process for approval from relevant government authorities. bi-monthly meetings at the MoWR allow insight and agreement for selection of prioritised possible activities and discussion of operational challenges.

4 - Needs

An estimated 15.6m Syrians require WASH assistance, 6.2m of whom are considered in acute need. Average water consumption during 2018 was estimated at 71 liters / capita / day. but those relying exclusively on commercial water vendors consumed an average 57 liters / capita / day, with 10 per cent consuming less than 30 liters / capita / day. Families with no free available water can spend on average 10 per cent of their income on water, with 3 per cent of the population spending more than 20 per cent of their income (Nawa). In extreme cases up to 40 per cent (Areesha). Families living in informal settlements can spend more than 50 per cent of their incomes on water. Reduced incomes and purchasing power can lead to adoption of harmful coping strategies, with potentially significant implications on health and hygiene behaviour/practices. There is strong evidence linking poor water-sanitation with nutrition and development, especially in young children. At least 80 per cent of sewage is discharged untreated while at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional. Outbreaks of acute bloody diarrhea have been observed in some areas of Deir-ez-Zor. Poor solid waste management typically contributes to increased incidence of leishmaniosis which has been observed by WHO since the beginning of 2018, especially in Deir E Zor and Aleppo. 23 per cent of garbage is inappropriately disposed within communities. local water establishment resources and equipment have deteriorated drastically since the war, making operation and maintenance of WASH services extremely problematic. WASH facilities in schools have suffered in much the same way as community WASH infrastructure, leading to lower education access and unsafe conditions of hygiene facilities. Through a lack of resources and prioritisation there has been little if any attention paid to the needs of adolescent girls or disabled children.

5 - Implementation of activities

For emergency response, Oxfam can provide water trucking, sewage pipe unblocking, provision of bins and essential NFIs. For more resilient outcomes, Oxfam rehabilitates Wat/San infrastructure, and supports basic solid waste management services. Oxfam will continue to rehabilitate inclusive WASH facilities in schools to minimum standards, and sub-district health centre WASH facilities & medical waste disposal. We support local Wat/San technical staff at district level with capacity building activities in consultation with MoWR & LWEs, providing relevant operation and maintenance materials and training during project implementation. Oxfam has some unique governorate approvals to carry out household surveys, triangulating assessment findings with data gathered from representatives of LWEs, mukhtars and other community representatives. Oxfam has a strong MEAL methodology supported by a large team of field engineers and volunteers who are trained in Oxfam principles, standards and WASH practice. Projects are prioritised in communities with high number and ratio of returnees, high levels of vulnerability and poor coping mechanisms, for both returnees and static residents. Household level needs are based on the following criteria: 1.Female-headed HHs; 2. HHs with persons with disabilities, including mobility related to old age; 3. HHs with high number of children, both school age children and under 5 years of age; 4. Spontaneous returnee households who have lost assets and property through the conflict; Oxfam will build upon during and post intervention community satisfaction surveys, facilitating full community representation and participation in decision making, most vulnerable population selection ensuring voices of women and those marginalised are heard. Oxfam will aim to target communities with large voluntary returnees, addressing social cohesion (connectors / dividers). All project data will be disegregated to gender, disability and community (host/returnee/IDP).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Oxfam's humanitarian programming has several risk tools during planning and monitoring; Oxfam Minimum Standards for Gender in Emergencies, Gender and Age Monitoring tool, Humanitarian Gender Action Plan (updated quarterly for more coherent response to immediate needs), and reinforces with capacity building of all staff in these approaches. Activities include; multi-sector and multi-level rapid gender analysis to inform our programming and advocacy, Gender and GBV/Child Protection training for all staff and volunteers, developing partnerships and strengthening coordination with the Protection Sector including Child Protection and GBV Sub-sectors. Due to limited local capacity ofprotection service providers, Oxfam engages with specialized agencies (UNHCR,UNICEF and UNFPA and their local partners) to gain better insight on protection risks aswell as define Oxfam’s role in supporting referral pathways. Oxfam incorporates conflict sensitivity analysis. in its choosing of activities and locations, updating guidelines for staff and volunteers to support this, Child Protection, Safeguarding (PSEA) and fraud, to further promote staff and volunteers’ awareness of protection risks and vulnerabilities. Oxfam engages communities in a conflict sensitive manner, taking care not to exacerbate tensions - while supporting local capacities for peace, particularly among women-ledinitiatives, wherever possible. Oxfam will ensure that each stakeholder involved in the project can safely participate to its activities. Safe programming (or protection mainstreaming) is implemented as part of the project cycle and staff, volunteers and implementing partners are trained in safe programming, risk assessments including identification of risks, possible targets, and risk prevention, mitigation and management measures. Particular attention will be paid in addressing risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse, aid diversion, durable solutions and conflict sensitivity.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Instability factors in project locations limits the ability of Oxfam to achieve project objectives. Mitigation Measures: Maintain engagement with country security networks and humanitarian organisations to monitor and evaluate the changing security environment, conduct regular risk assessments and adopt mitigation measures where appropriate, allocate funds to security personnel and equipment (first aid kits,security alarms, safes, etc. to address shortcomings,adopt strict safety procedures forpersonnel, especially when travelling outside of Damascus, collaborate closely withUNOCHA and other appropriate organizations on the ground to share operational detailson Oxfam's programming. Risk: Difficulty in accessing communities for data collection in order to assess their actual needs and/or measure impact.Mitigation Measures: Oxfam has strong relationships with MOFA, MOWR, MOSAL, MOLA to help aid in datacollection and assessment at the local level directly. To reach vulnerable groups in HTRAs,coordinate and collaborate with private contractors and partners which enhances capacitiesto access these areas, adopting remote reporting techniques to allow for data collectionand reporting. Oxfam uses local personnel as site engineers and focal points at thegovernorate level, who also aid in data collection and monitoring, closely guided by theMEAL Officer.Risk: Inflation and exchange rates are unstable, complicating spending andexpenditures that must be made in Syrian Pounds (SYP) within Syria. Mitigation Measures: Explore options to overcome this challenge employed by other NGOs andinternational organizations despite the government requirement for contracts with localsupplies to be denominated in SYP,make payments to vendors in tranches to ensure thatsuppliers remain interested and that neither Oxfam nor the supplier is affected too muchby the exchange rate fluctuations) Exchange rate gains are reported to the donor.
