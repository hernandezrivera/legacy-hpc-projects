Project Summary

BRD will establish Two CFS centers in Dana (Sub-District) to reach to the affected people in mentioned communities in Idleb and Aleppo.
will focus on reducing risks and strengthening protective factors for children to ensure their physical, social and emotional development that will contribute to the cluster priority of protecting children and mitigating the effects of violence and displacement. The proposed interventions are aimed to reduce/mitigate the impact of threats to children’s physical and psychosocial security in Given the seriousness of the protection threats experienced by children affected by conflict and displacement, the child protection interventions proposed are one of the frontline services that are critical to the physical and psychosocial survival of conflict-affected and displaced children. This project will promote girls’ participation in the CFSs and provide them with opportunities to safely talk about sexual violence and other gender-specific CP concerns.
BRD will also enhance the ability of CP staff to detect and support specific vulnerabilities amongst girls and boys through comprehensive case management and referral. CAAFAG (will be approached by BRD case managers), and children with complicated mental health problems will be referred to Other service providers.
Children involved or at risk of hazardous labor and in the same line, the center will provide parenting programs to the parents and caregivers. According to HNO22 In 81 percent of assessed communities, respondents reported child labor preventing school attendance was an issue of concern. This issue will be approached by BRD case managers to comprehensively be managed by working with children and their caregivers to promote minimal standards and involve children in BRD's CFSs.
The activities will aim at increasing boys’ and girls' knowledge and awareness of possible threats, allowing them to better protect themselves.
BRD will also be involved in raising awareness on the prevention of recruitment of children by armed groups, children involved in hazardous labor and prevention of family separation and will provide cash in case management to who needs regarding set criteria for ( labor, exploitation, recruitment, and who needs some special equipment). The main target will be parents, caregivers and community stakeholders such as religious leaders, local council members, teachers, medical point’s directors, and etc.
BRD will train caseworkers to identify and respond to the needs of such children. BRD will use the CP sub-cluster SOPs and referral pathway to provide the needed services through case management. In the child-friendly space (CFS), dedicated time and space will be created for adolescent girls where they can be together and where they do not have to compete with boys. BRD will ensure that a female facilitator is available to work with the girls, who can offer confidentiality, warmth, and advice.
BRD will seek also to link CFS to other services such as education, health, and social services to enhance referral of children in need of special assistance.
BRD seek to build capacities of child protection staff and community stakeholders to provide protection services for children according to CPMS. These Interventions will aim at addressing vulnerabilities related to child labor, child recruitment, child marriage, and family separation. This project will work with leaders and Community Based Protection to prevent labor, exploitation, and recruitment.
MEAL team will keep on track the CP services, satisfied children for (centers, supplies, materials,..etc), training of CP workers How they dealing with children, satisfied of children in CP environmental. A lesson learning event will be conducted at end of project lifecycle to draw some lessons and best practices from the project, using CP cub cluster materials and tools, guidance to ensure the quality of services provided
BRD committed to 4ws on monthly basis.

4 - Needs

About 12 years of conflict in Syria, have increased the suffering of civilians including the suffering of armed conflict, blockade, homelessness, Recent displacement occurred (total number of IDPs 591K) toward NS, Euphrates shield and Afrin, has exacerbated family stresses, disrupted normal patterns of living and crumbled social support systems exposing children to protection threats and psychosocial distress. Events leading to family separation indicate the interconnectedness and diversity of vulnerability factors with death (26 per cent for boys and 28 per cent for G), economic need (20 per cent for B), child marriage (22 per cent for girls) and child recruitment (14 per cent forB) among top cited reasons for family separations . The absence of a continuum of alternative care options puts some children at increased risk of additional protection concerns. these children will be forced to process their conflict experience alone, which may prevent them from being able to fully engage in daily activities such as playing, learning, or basic social interaction.(HNO22) the crisis producted child labor, unaccompanied children..etc. the psychological disorders and distress of both children and caregivers. Child labor section focuses on the proportions of communities that have the phenomenon of child labor, including Band G labor, which restricts them from going to school. Focus was on two special types of cases, child-headed household and the families that contain PWDs.

5 - Implementation of activities

BRD will Increase access for b and g to quality CP interventions in line with the CPMS according to the principles of CP sub-cluster to establish CFS, according to SOPs and CPMS. Activities will take into consideration the balance and gender between children and PWDs, BRD will hire specialist for each age group and gender (children - M/w) to deal with them depend upon their needs. BRD will secure services either outreach or vehicle which transport them to/from the center. Firstly Establishing children (B:2320,G:2310) and youth (B1600:,G:1500) safe spaces. Activities will focus on mitigating the risks of child labor and encouraging them to return to school, through awareness sessions about the risks of child labor and help them to learn a job that does not affect on their life, development, and education. It will also target caregivers (M:525,W:545) at the same line through awareness sessions parenting skills on the dangers of child labor. BRD will provide structured psychosocial support for groups of children. Activating the program of the resilience of children and youth to measure their adaptation in new situations and psycho-drama activities using puppets theatre to introduce some solutions to their problems, activities will conduct by psychologist. BRD will provide a library, stories, recreational activities. specialized child protection services (CM) for G/B. supervision skills and FTR in emergencies and some specialized training related to focused PSS and PFA for C.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Prohibition of (PSEA), BRD continues to stand firm on zero tolerance for (sexual) misconduct. Therefore, BRD has PSEA CoC it’s included on to set forth the expectations of conduct and mutual respect in regard to PSEA and the process of complaint if these expectations are not met or violated. Risk of doing SEA by not PSEA trained staff utilizing the authority (Provision of the services): Likelihood: H Impact: H Mitigation measures: 1.BRD has developed PSEA CoC in line with PSEA network. All recruited staff should read and sign the PSEA CoC. 2. Recruited staff should attend PSEA training provides by BRD’s PSEA Focal Point. 3. Establish a PSEA reporting mechanism for BNs to repot any misconduct by staff. give feedback and complaints even to the LC or field staff. In addition, will include (phone numbers, e-mails, verbal reporting and complaints’ box) in any awareness materials. 4.distribute awareness images inside centers in addition to use child satisfaction forms (expressive images and tacit questions) to make sure that no abuse has been done. Parents will also be encouraged to register any complaints expressed by children. BRD tries to touch on the specific risks faced by PwDs, such as isolation and limited mobility, by reaching out to communities, by improving CP centers, BRD during implementation will prepare centers to receive PWDs, through ramp, exiting, entering tracks, toilets, separate toilets with locks, lamps, and other.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

CP facilities could be attacked, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a CP workers, direct attack on workers or children, children or military use of the centers. Mitigating Measures CP facilities can be de-conflicted using the de-confliction mechanism administered by UNOCHA. All attacks will be reported to CP cub cluster. CP facilities will have safety protocols in place. These protocols should be adequately resourced and regularly practiced. Resources may include walkies-talkies, basic firefighting equipment, basic first aid supplies, flash lights, exit signs, etc. Caregivers should know CP facilities’s safety protocols and what will happen in case of a security event. Preparedness training such as self-care, first aid, fire drills, evacuation drills, should be regularly practiced. These processes and procedures will be appropriate for age of the involved children. Where relevant, children, CP personnel will be trained on unexplored ordinance risk awareness. children, their caregivers, workers and other CP personnel will be provided PSS. Those services will have capacity, ability and resources to address basic needs. For needs that are beyond what will be reasonably expected from CP services there will be a specialized protection services. Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by CP Workers, CP personnel, organization staff or related entities. Mitigating Measure Child Safeguarding polices and systems in place
