Project Summary

WVSR plans to continue provide life-saving comprehensive WASH package for IDPs living in displacement sites across NWS/NES. Water trucking, solid waste management and hygiene supplies/cash/voucher distribution will be provided in the subdistricts of Azaz, Idleb, Dana (Alhanan, Alaramel, AFAD camps), Ras Al Ain (Tal Halaf village), Tell Abıad (Amin village)and future sites. In some of these locations such as Dana and Azaz there are plans for new water networks in the settlements and towns through connection to existing and new water points/elevated storage tanks to allow for the phasing out of water trucking to a more sustained and durable solution, 9 water stations will be supported in subdistricts Bennish(1), Idleb(1), Taftenaz(1), Maaret Tamsrin(3), Dana(1), and Tell Abıad and Ras-AlEin (2), through the operation (staffing, chlorine and fuel supply), and maintenance. Some of these sites have potential to provide a solarized alternative of energy to support using hibernate system decreasing the electricity costs of running the systems. In addition to O&M support, there will also be capacity building and feedback consultative meetings arranged to serve as a knowledge sharing with local council, water station management and community focal points on various topics such as cost recovery, maintenance and reporting. Desludging activities are proposed in IDP sites in Azaz (Al-Ryan, Talil sham), and Dana subdistrict. Sewer network rehabilitation and expansions are proposed in the subdistricts of Azaz (6 IDP locations), Dana, and Ras Al Ain. In 2018, WV notably piloted the construction of a wastewater treatment plant (first of its kind) in Sejjo camp with private funds where treated water it acceptable for agricultural purposes. From learnings and ongoing O&M WV. On the institutional setting, WV plans to rehabilitate and construct WASH facilities in 6 schools in Harim, Atareb, & Daret Azza, supporting WASH needs for persons with disability in 3 camps in Azaz. There will be 4 Healthcare facilities in Ras-Alein will be supported with medical waste management services. for hygiene Promotion, and RCCE sessions in various formats (from school messaging, posters in streets, on water tanks, Solid Waste vehicles, social media) with tailored messaging to the appropriate setting and age groups. WV is focusing more on ‘nudges and cues’ such as painted steps to hand washing stations at the school setting to social distancing markers at communal water point to serve as reminders for healthy behaviors. For children, WV will use its existing partnership with Sesame Workshop, in 3 informal learning centers in IDP camps in Azaz, with plans to expand to the formal school setting. The Sesame Workshop program educates and empowers children to healthy lives, through promotion of behavioral changes in sanitation and hygiene through children play-based learning materials, and multimedia content program developed through Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation. In summary, all of these interventions will improve access to essential WASH services, reduce harmful coping strategies; that have adverse cascading effects on individuals and communities in terms of public health, environment, inequality, poverty, and provide a foundation for the prevention of Cholera outbreak and reduce transmission of other water-borne illnesses and ultimately save lives.

3 - Needs

Access to WASH services will remain a challenge for more than 2 million people in IDP Sites. Water trucking are the only source of water for 73% of IDPs, only 39% HHs are connected to simple sewer networks, indicating a need for investment in sustainable, affordable, and safer WASH services in IDPs sites. The dysfunctionality of water and sanitation systems is closely linked with widespread electricity shortages.[1] WASH gaps include the need for water system rehabilitation/operational support (521,000), water trucking support (335,000 IDPs), sewerage rehabilitation or construction (68,000), and SWM support (256,000).[2] Over 15% of garbage is inappropriately disposed of or collected with low frequency[3] At least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated, and 50% of the sewerage systems are not functional. Lack of WASH facilities increases the risk of students dropout. WASH in School needs are growing – 56% of students complained to their parents about WASH-related issues. Poor sanitation conditions, hygiene practices, and water quality have a direct impact on increasing public health risks, including Cholera. 16% of HHs did not have soap, water, or handwashing stations at all, and 59% do have access to all hygiene items.[1] Most all WVSR proposed sites have the WASH severity score of 3 to 4. [1] HNO 2023 Draft. [2] WASH Cluster Needs and Gaps, Nov 2022. [3] WoS – WASH Assessment (1st round 2022).

4 - Implementation of activities

This intervention will follow a mixed methodology (direct and indirect) of implementation where WVSR and its implementing partner will divide the roles and responsibilities to ensure the best support and maximum impact provided for the targeted beneficiaries. The implementation partner will be responsible for the camps/community's selection, announcement, registration and beneficiaries’ registration management. WV will be responsible for quality assurance, financial and data management, vendor contracting and regular market mentoring. For construction works this will be implementing through qualified only contractors whom are selected being lowest price, all design and specifications with be in accordance and coordinated with the local municipal departments water,sanitation,health which during implementation contractors will be monitored by engineers of WVSR and its implementing partners to ensure quality of construction and rehabilitation per designs from the bids tendered. For school settings, WV will ensure coordination with the Ministry of Education on targeted schools and ensure approvals for new curriculum such as the Sesame hygiene approach along with construction works in rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools. WASH in Healthcare will also fall in accordance to ministry of health. WVSR will not do standalone WASH in HCF without guaranteeing that another partner, Ministry of health is also providing health specific support in that particular healthcare facility

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The WASH Sector will ensure that the ’do no harm’ principle is systematically applied.Potential protection risks related to the implementation of the WASH related activities,along with a description of the relevant mitigation measures and resources requiredfor monitoring those risks have been identified. All WASH partners will take adequatemeasures to minimize exposure of the most vulnerable groups, including women,adolescent girls, children, people with disabilities, female-headed households, togreater protection risks, particularly GBV. WASH staff will be trained on key GBVconcepts and referral pathways. Women, adolescent girls and people with disabilitieswill be consulted during project cycle steps to facilitate feedback. High level ofexplosive hazard contamination close to water and sanitation infrastructure, orcontamination of accumulated garbage/rubbles, which potentially put humanitarianstaff in danger during WASH assessments, rehabilitation works, andoperation/maintenance will be considered. Where relevant, WASH staff will be trainedon explosive hazard risk mitigation, while risk education sessions and awareness raisingmaterials for beneficiaries will be integrated as much as possible into WASHprogramming. Risk of underage labor are mitigated through proper registrationsystem implementers; add zero tolerance disclosure on child safeguarding and laborin the contracts; check and register civil documentation.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WVSR adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards SEA and it is committedresponsibility at all times to ensure that all people – women, girls, boys and men aretreated with dignity and respect, furthermore, uphold humanitarian principles andensure the integrity of the humanitarian operation. Support will be provided for thePrevention of Sexual Exploitation & Abuse mechanisms to protect beneficiaries andtheir caregivers which include the technical support related to the safeguarding andPSEA available policy review and development with partners, ToT and training to beprovided for first line workers, assigning field focal points with partners in additionWVSR field and national focal points, raising the awareness of the community inproper way including child friendly material, regular reporting on all related activitiesand incidents in coordination with other clusters in addition of PSEA network which isled by IOM. Child safety concerns are identified and monitored through regularchild/beneficiary checklists which are used by WVSR field staff which all is constantlymonitored through the project lifecycle. Furthermore, WVSR has its own incidentpreparedness plan which is understood by all staff and partners and is summarized bythe procedures and actions related to proper responding to safeguarding and PSEAincidents in multiple levels and categories, in addition WVSR uses a standard onlineplatform which is called Ethics Point that ensures the proper and timely response


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

WVSR will consider conflict patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions forestablishing WASH service provision, ensure safety audit is conducted to ensure safeaccess, engage with local authorities on safety and security in the community, relatedto certain activities. The risks for water, solid waste and desludging trucks timing ofdelivery being not appropriate/reliable which can be mitigated through consultationwith targeted communities (separately women & girls) regarding location, deliveryroutes and its schedule. Also, service provision takes security and safety considerationsby avoiding narrow streets, congested areas; school areas as well as conducting safetytraining with truck drivers/operators. Health risk related to construction and supportof municipal water supply/sewage due to exposure to chemicals which will bemitigated by staff/partners/contractors involved in construction works or working withchemicals wear appropriate PPE and follow established safety regulations. This is inaddition to conducting and repeating safety training for staff/partners/contractors.Unequal distribution of WASH services inadvertently strengthen the position of armedgroups, thus WVSR will establish strong links with authorities and operators topromote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time; Include community andauthorities in targeting; Ensure sensitive information is handled with care.
