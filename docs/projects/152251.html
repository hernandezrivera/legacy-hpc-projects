Project Summary

Summary of Plan International´s overall response strategy
Plan International’s regional Lake Chad response covers humanitarian responses in Nigeria, Cameroon
and Niger. The regional multi country response strategy focuses on providing integrated child protection,
SGBV, education and livelihoods support to support the survival, well-being and development of girls and
boys affected by the Boko Haram crisis.
Plan International Nigeria’s response strategy is in line with both the regional response strategy as well as
the Nigeria HRP and identifies three priority intervention areas:
1) Education in Emergencies: Plan International in Nigeria is committed to increase access to early
childhood care for children under five and quality basic education opportunities for children and youth
affected by the crisis. This is done through creating safe and secure learning environments through
rehabilitation of schools, set-up of Temporary- Learning Spaces (TLS) or the provision of mobile
education units especially for hard to reach areas. The organization builds capacity of teachers,
provides psychosocial support to them as well as teaching material. Plan International Nigeria also
offers alternative forms of education including Accelerated Learning Program for out of school and
drop out adolescents aged 12 to 24 years with emphasis on adolescent girls.
2) Protection (Child Protection and SGBV): Under the area of protection Plan International in Nigeria
focuses on providing protection to children as well as SGBV- interventions for girls and women as
well as boys. Plan’s strategy focuses on prevention through good parenting education, establishment
and strengthening of community based protections systems but also the provision of protection
services through psychosocial support, family tracing and reunification, case management and
establishment of static and mobile Child Friendly Spaces and Women-centers.
3) Livelihoods: Under the livelihood sector Plan International focuses on provision of skills and
livelihood assets/cash to SGBV-survivors and vulnerable women, the provision of vouchers/cash for
school materials and Youth Economic Empowerment (YEE) to target out of school older adolescents
(15-17 years) and youth and to enable them to build a foundation for their future.

Needs

The Lake Chad crisis is one of Africa’s largest crises. In Northeast Nigeria round 1.8 million peopleremain displaced as a result of violence inflicted by Boko Haram. In its eight year, the humanitarian crisisremains severe due to ongoing conflict, continued internal displacement and the unpredictable return ofrefugees from neighboring countries. (ACAPS, OCHA). A total of 26 million people remain affected, with atotal of 8.5 million people, of which over half (57%) are children, in urgent need of humanitarianassistance in the three worst affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe17.Nearly 70% of the internally IDPs are hosted in Borno state, placing huge restraints on the already limitedresources and infrastructure in the region. The mass displacement has led to loss of livelihoods, land andlivestock. Only 20% of IDPs has access to livelihoods while the vast majority is solely dependent onhumanitarian aid. At the same time, humanitarian access to affected areas remains a challenge asattacks and hostilities continue to cause unpredicted population displacement. The ongoing instability inthe region has severely disrupted basic services for children and families. Key infrastructure such ashealth, education, and livelihood facilities remain significantly damaged from the ongoing conflict,although government as duty bearers tries to reconstruct the rehabilitation it is insufficient (ACAPS).Protection of the affected population continues to be a major concern in the whole Northeast region. 6 outof 10 women and girls in conflict areas in Northeast Nigeria are reported to have experienced one ormore forms of SGBV with rape accounting for 85% of all women who sought medical assistance forSGBV in 2017. At the same time, in the HRP Protection is only 27.3% funded out of the required $88.3million, while the overall funding rate of the 2017 HRP was 70%. Analysis of needs and gaps for childrenThe humanitarian needs of girls and boys remain extremely high, as the Boko Haram crisis is entering itseighth year. Plan International conducted a comprehensive Child Protection and Education NeedsAssessment in Borno and Adamawa states in September 201718 that indicated prevalence of a highnumber of child protection concerns including of family separation, sexual and gender-based violenceincluding child marriage, child labor and sexual exploitation. In addition, access to education is in manyaffected areas extremely challenging due to ongoing insecurity, lack of infrastructure and teachingpersonnel.Child ProtectionFamily separation: Mass displacement has led to widespread family separation. An estimated 32,000children have become separated from their caregivers, including 9,600 children who are unaccompanied,without any other family member to take care of them. These children are at extreme risk of violence,abuse and exploitation.Child labor: The increasing food insecurity also has a devastating impact on the protection of children.Children are reported to be in child labor to contribute to family income, including the worst forms; reportsof ‘survival sex’ have revealed sexual exploitation of women, girls and boys to obtain food and other itemsneeded to survive. IOM reports that children in food insecure households are at heightened risk oftrafficking.Grave violations against children in armed conflict: Grave violations have been committed againstchildren by Boko Haram groups, including widespread killings, maiming, abduction, sexual violence andattacks on schools. Girls and boys (formerly) associated with Boko Haram including children born out ofsexual violence and marriages within the armed groups are faced with serious stigma and rejection as they return home.Sexual and Gender-Based Violence: The ongoing conflict and scarcity of resources in North-East Nigeriaplaces girls and young women are at heightened risk of sexual and gender-based violence. Protectionservices report a wide range of sexual violence.

Activities

1) Promoting adolescent girls’ and boys’ participation, life skills and social cohesion: adolescentpeer groups will be supported to organize their own activities, participate in life skill trainings and inrecreational activities that promote social cohesion.2) Strengthening family- and community-based protection structures: Plan International willsupport community-based groups, consisting of key community members and youth representatives,who are mandated to mobilize communities; raise awareness on protection issues; identify, report andrefer protection cases, and promote social cohesion in their locality. Plan International will also intensifyengagement with parent groups and with local authorities including religious leaders to raise sensitiveissues, as they are highly influential in their locality. Plan International will develop strategies to engagewith these key community members and initiate dialogues about child rights and the impact of harmfulpractices such as early marriage and other forms of GBV on children, particularly girls, andprogressively to engage with them in efforts to prevent and respond to protection concerns in their ownlocality.3) Safe community spaces offer children aged 6-17 and particularly adolescents girls (11-18) andyoung women, with essential psychosocial, play and learning support. The safe spaces will be ran bytrained community volunteers, supported by Plan International and provide safe and confidential placefor the identification of children with special protection needs including separated children, survivors ofviolence, early married girls and child laborers. To reach the most marginalized children, during the firstyear, a CFS management committee will be put in place and trained. They will be supported intensivelyduring the second year to enable them continue the functioning of the CFS after the end of the project.4) Case management for survivors of (sexual and gender-based) violence: As a key child protectionstrategy to respond to violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation, Plan International and implementingpartner will provide direct case management services. This process follows pre-defined steps of caseidentification, registration, documentation, assessment, case planning, provision of direct assistance andreferrals, monitoring and follow-up and case closure when key protection concerns are addressed.5) Strengthening formal systems: Underlying all child protection in emergencies work, is an emphasison systems strengthening. In this project, Plan International and implementing partner will recruit and train NGO and Government Case Workers onCase Management guidelines and Caring for Child Survivors, in order to ensure age appropriate, gendersensitive case management, and psychosocial services for both child and adult GBV survivors.
