Project Summary

In South Sudan, 19.33 million m² of land is suspected of being contaminated with landmine and explosive ordnances (EO). The highest level of contamination is predominantly located in the Equatorias, along the main routes and areas for returnees from Uganda.
Explosive hazards inhibit civilians from collecting water or firewood, cultivating land, attending school, and receiving health care services, among other critical daily needs. They further hinder humanitarian organizations from accessing vulnerable communities or providing lifesaving assistance in conflict-affected areas. While substantial progress has been made in the monitoring and clearing of explosive contamination, landmines and EO continue to pose a threat to civilians and humanitarians in South Sudan.
In 2021, more than 654,800 people are estimated to be at risk of injury or death from landmines and EO. Magwi is one of the eight counties with the highest level of recorded contamination in Equatorias. The majority of the counties within the Equatorias are considered to be main access routes for returnees as well as sites for initial returns to arrive and commence re-establishment of their lives in South Sudan. EORE prepares communities to resume cultivation and livelihoods, and to conduct activities related to education and shelter. It further reduces tension between various communities, as well as environmental stress, around natural resources and the availability of land. With the projection of returns in a majority of the currently contaminated areas, the EORE is critical to ensure that safety of places of settlement is guaranteed.
CINA’s EORE project will be implemented in Magwi, which is one of level 5 locations for HRP 2021 where 35,790 people from host communities, IDPs and returnees will be targeted. From its existing EORE presence in Magwi, CINA reported 11 hazards and reached over 4600 people with EORE messages in the second half of 2020. CINA's EORE project in 2021 will further seek to reduce the risk of injury from Explosive Ordnance by raising awareness and promoting behavioral change, including public information dissemination, education and training at community level. It will be the role of Risk Educators to move from community to community, schools, churches etc. sensitizing and raising the awareness on safety and prevention messages about the Explosive Ordnance. Sad to note, the conflicts which have been experienced by the people of South Sudan have made civilians to lose life, fight one another, and many other distractions, leaving behind many explosive hazards which pose further threats to human lives, livelihoods and movements.
To reach the people in catastrophic need, CINA estimates a budget of $ 400,000 to provide Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) activities in Magwi, Eastern Equatoria State in 2021.

1 - Needs

Continuing political instability, lack of physical protection and insecurity intersects with key protection issues. Competition for local resources is often driven by organized politically motivated attempts to obtain resources in the form of sub-national organized violence. This, in turn, is leading to increased rate of incidents of conflict-related sexual violence, heightening children’s vulnerabilities and protection risks linked to repeated displacements. Conflict-related injuries lead to disabilities, while the conflict simultaneously reduces options for individuals living with disabilities. Conflict, economic and climatic factors have placed South Sudan one of four countries on the brink of famine, which disproportionately affects women and children. Selective implementation of the R-ARCSS remains a challenge to sustainable peace. Lack of capacity of the government to conduct reforms delays transitional security arrangements, formation of the unified forces. Paradoxically, if the peace agreement is progressed, protection concerns may increase, after return of some of South Sudanese refugees, further testing the economic and social resilience of a weakened nation-state. Potential returnees, particularly to the Equatorias, face arriving to areas with high levels of explosive hazard contamination, it will be critical to respond through survey and clearance, as well as EORE, to ensure protective environment enabling to re-establish daily lives, accessibility to critical services such as health, education, food security, livelihoods.

2 - Project activities

Activity 1. Conduct three community-based EORE training to 50 (26women, 24men ) caregivers of EO victims, community leaders, local authority leader, women and youth groups representatives, etc. in Magwi. The topics to be covered will include but not limited to education on dangers of EO, learning about local markings around suspected UXOs, how and who to report any spotted EO, what to do when one comes across an UXO, informing others about which areas are safe/unsafe, information on small arms and light weapons (SALW), etc. Activity 2. Convene two days training to 60 (20 women, 40 men) teachers/PTAs/SMCs on EORE messages to impart knowledge to the children at school and their families in both locations. Members of Parents and Teachers Association (PTAs), School Management Committees (SMCs) who will be trained on key EORE messages will act as RE Mentors in schools, homes and places of residence and in their communities in both counties. Activity 3. Provide two training to 60 (30girls, 30boys) adolescent peer educators on EORE messaging to support in delivery of RE safety messages in schools and community. Most of the at-risk populations are often children. Training and support to peer educators will not only help in disseminating risk education messages to large number of people but will help in project sustainability Activity 4. Establish/strengthen one Disabled People's Organizations, comprised of 30 members (16 women, 14 men) to advocate for the rights of mine/ERWs survivors, PWDs among other minority groups. After establishment/strengthening of DPOs, training of the DPOs members will help in the sustainability of the project after its closure. The DPO members will be empowered to act as agents of change and keep informing the communities of the dangers of EOs and importance of risk education to the community members, IDPs and Returnee, there will be one DPO. Activity 5. Conduct awareness raising sessions to 35,790 people (8500 girls, 10,500 boys, 8,000 women, and 8,790 men) (including 15% people with disabilities) on key EORE messages in Magwi County. Every EORE Session in school will take approximately 45 mins each. Observe COVID-19 measures by conducting sessions on radio, clustering of participants into groups of 8-10 members. When COVID-19 measures are fully lifted and schools reopen, children are expected to report to schools in large numbers. Arrangements will be made with teachers to present EORE messages during free sessions. The schools normally accept 3 days visit in every week and the attendance per class is usually between 45 - 50 pupils. Activity 6. Carry out three EORE awareness sessions to 20 staff (8 women, 12 men) from humanitarian actors including GBV, child protection, HLP, Shelter, FSL, CCCM, to ensure safety of humanitarian workers in the project locations. Having an integrated approach across all sectors will help CINA actors receive information related to EO fast and help in effective decision making as well as protection mainstreaming. Equipping every sector with key EORE messages will help the humanitarian actors take care of their own safety together with others as they respond in their project locations Activity 7. Carry out monitoring visits to the field by 25 program staff (10 women, 15men), train project staff and assess the project status in Magwi county. The Program team will travel to the project locations in each county from Juba to do training, monitor and evaluate project activities twice during the implementation period.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During implementation of activities, banners with PSEA messages and telephone number will be displayed during EORE sessions in the community, and beneficiaries will be sensitized about importance of reporting any suspected PSEA case using the PSEA form or telephone call. Feedback will be relayed back through CINA PSEA focal person or the Project Manager.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The EORE project team will use messages translated in local language in order to get feedback from all community members. In particular, members of DPOs, women, girls who can not read or write will be targeted during EORE activities and specifically asked to make a feedback whenever necessary to ensure that they have understood, and to know if they have concerns to report.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The EORE project will ensure that beneficiaries get information about the project through use of information awareness sessions, use of Disabled People's Organizations, Peer Educators and Community Liaison. Community Complaint and Feedback boxes will be displayed at the sites during EORE sessions and members sensitized to drop in their complaints or feedback using a form. A telephone number will be displayed on EORE banners where community members can call and report their complaints/feedback. In addition, feedback will be collected during M&E activities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CINA EORE team will have a two-way communication between the affected population and the Team members. The affected population will inform the EORE team about locations of spotted mines/UXOs and as CINA EORE team also educate them on messages that change behaviour. During CINA monitoring and evaluation activities, the project will be able to have an interactive communication with all stakeholders and views analyzed and shared. The EORE teams will encourage beneficiaries to give suggestions on areas of improvement or to report any malpractices. EORE Teams will be empowered to maximize transparency and ensuring that complaints and concerns are addressed, feedback accessible and participation is inclusive because different people can be affected differently.
