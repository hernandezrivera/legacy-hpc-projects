Project Summary

This project aims at providing the following services to IDPs sites:
a) upgrading infrastructure in IDPs sites, such as drainage systems, benefiting 30,000 beneficiaries (age and gender disaggregated) in 3 -4 sites;
b) As part of the emergency response, Shafak will conduct care and maintenance of Shafak's supported camps in case of floods, fire incidents ...etc. This includes maintenance of alleys, tents and floors among tents; this will be for existing sites.
c) equipping and training emergency responders and IDPs committees in three IDP sites; and replacement of
100 tent in case of fire/floods.
e) care and maintenance for two IDP sites (technicians and labor can conduct light to medium maintenance services such as for electricity, water, and sewage network in the camps).

4 - Needs

IDP sites continue to provide immediate humanitarian lifesaving assistance to the most vulnerable IDPS, particularly in situations of sudden mass displacements. Children and women make up 73 per cent of camp population in north-west Syria. Although IDP sites (including planned camps, informal sites, transit/reception centres and collective centres) are designed to act as an option of last-resort, the past year has witnessed an increase by 16 per cent in the number of IDPS seeking shelter in these sites. During sudden displacements, IDPs have often no choice but to seek shelter in planned camps, informal sites, transit/reception centres and collective centres. Of the estimated 6.2 million internally displaced persons in Syria, an estimated 871,150, or 14 per cent of all IDPs, are living in IDP sites where they receive humanitarian assistance and services. (HNO, 2019) Although IDP sites (including planned camps, informal sites, transit/reception centres and collective centres) are designed to act as an option of last-resort, the past year has witnessed an increase by 16 per cent in the number of IDPs seeking shelter in these sites. Against the backdrop of overcrowding, there is limited absorption capacity in IDP sites. Some sites are running by more than 400 per cent above their actual capacity. The absence of accountable camp management and maintenance of facilities in IDP sites, particularly for informal/self-established IDP sites, are deeply concerning and contribute to alarming reports of uncovered needs in these sites. According to HNO 2019, between September, 2017, and August, 2018, the CCCM cluster has tracked almost 1.1 million population movements across northern Syria – a significant increase from the 804,300 reported in the 2018 HNO. During this time period, an estimated 18% of IDPs resorted to, or transited through, IDP sites.

5 - Implementation of activities

Shafak will implement the proposed activities depending on it usual practices and SOPs derived from working with different INGO and UN Agencies. Shafak will follow the following implementation steps: - Needs assessment to determine the IDP sites affected by any humanitarian disasters which are in need for maintenance, emergency services, and/or care services. - Technical assessment and preparing BoQs. - Contracting with service providers to implement the maintenance activities in the IDPs sites linking with Cash-for-Work activities if applicable with a particular focus on enrolling youth and mid-age adults. - rehabilitation activities will include, and will not be limited to: 1) maintenance and rehabilitation of water and drainage systems as well as graveling and preparing alleys between tents to benefit 30,000 beneficiaries. 2) Equip and train emergency responders and IDPs committees in three IDP sites. 3) tent replacement and distribution for 500 tents. - Monitoring and evaluation for the activity. - Exit strategy for the project and hand over to the camp management. It is worth mentioning that Shafak has good experience in implementing such activities, as Shafak has previously implemented tent replacement, and rehabilitation of infra-structure.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk (1): Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), including harassment, against women and girls by distributors and male beneficiaries while queuing and before/after distributions Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: - recruit male and female volunteers who are respected in the community and independent of armed groups. No children to be employed (under 18 years old) - make sure staff are already trained on Do No Harm, protection from SEA (PSEA), and child safeguarding; and sign and adhere to code of conduct that respects and uphold the rights of beneficiaries and PSEA - set up communication and complaints mechanisms or desks that are clearly marked; face interviews with beneficiaries, including women and children Risk (2): Tension between host community and IDPs (who were evacuated) Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: - clearly communicate selection criteria to the community, community leaders and beneficiaries prior to the announcement of the activity. - harmonize the assistance delivered as per Cluster instructions and guidelines. - coordinate with local council in the targeted area as well as being active in field coordination Risk (3): Difficult access for women, unaccompanied children, elderly, people with disabilities (PWD) Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: - Register all adult women, unaccompanied children, elderly, people with disabilities as main recipients of Kits distributed - During post -distribution follow-up, understand how women, children, and other groups accessed the distributed goods and their feedback on the process and materials Risk (4): Resistance to gender mainstreaming from male leadership structures. Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: - Programme activities which engage women will be endorsed by community and religious leaders to gain support from male community members to reduce the likelihood of resistance to these activities.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1st Risk: lack of approval from property owner which may prevent from implementing the activity. Likelihood: High Mitigation: a) No start of the activity in the camp without obtaining the approval from the land owner or the stakeholders (local authorities). b) Include HLP documentation into the MoUs or agreements signed, 2nd Risk: risk of PSEA and CSG. Likelihood: High Mitigation: a) Clear complaint mechanism in place b) Train staff on code of conduct and PSEA and CSG policies c) field staff should be accompanied with female staff d) Incorporate strong consideration for protecting the rights of the beneficiaries and maintaining their dignity, e) Safe recruitment of staff f) Safe response standards.
