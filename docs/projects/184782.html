Project Summary

An estimated 6.4 million people, 40% of the Somali population need emergency life-saving WASH assistance of which 3.5 million will need safe drinking water, 5.1 million sanitation services, and 6.4 million hygiene supplies. 16% of these are females, children for 64% and the elderly for 4%. This presents an increase of 39 per cent from 2021, in which 4.6 million were estimated to be in need of WASH services across Somalia. This increase is due to adjusted population figures of Somalia423, in combination with continuous failed rainy seasons which are expected to further strain existing limited WASH services, especially in rural areas . The population in need of WASH services in 2022 includes approximately 1,619,415 IDPs and 4,818,944 vulnerable host communities. Estimated that 6,058,603 people (38 per cent) the Severe category” lack access to safe water and proper sanitation services and are highly vulnerable to AWD and cholera and around 379,757 people (2 percent) are in “extreme condition as they access water directly from rivers and surface bonds, practice open defecation and lack access to soap and other essential hygiene materials and are susceptible to communicable diseases . It is also estimated that 965,754 (15 per cent) of persons with disabilities and 254,465 (4 per cent) of elderly persons face the risk of exclusion from assistance due to the barriers they face when accessing WASH services. A recent assessment confirmed that 40% of females and 75% of males with disability never collected water for their households due to distance and in-accessibility of water points and rely on neighbors and children to collect water for them.
According to the severity of WASH need, displaced people in drought and floods hotspot districts are disproportionally affected by drought. About 72% of IDP sites don’t access enough water (15L/p/d) for drinking and domestic use. As a result, they resort to negative coping mechanisms which are detrimental for women and girls. The majority of affected people take more than 30 mins to reach the nearest water sources which are overcrowded creating access barriers. Limited access to WASH services is also linked to malnutrition among children. About 68% of AWD/cholera cases reported between June/May 2021 were <5 children from flood-prone districts whose population depends on unprotected water sources, these districts have also high GAM prevalence rates of >15 per cent.
SRC action will serve the community through the construction and rehabilitation of shallow wells in the affected locations situated approximately 25 KM from Awdhegle town, construction of flood-resistant latrines, provision of trucking through vouchers, training of WASH Management Committees and conducting comprehensive hygiene sensitization. To ensure the success of this market-based approach, SRC will work closely work with communities, local vendors to strengthen the demand and supply of comprehensive WASH services in Awdhegle district.

1. - Projects activities

1.1 Identify and engage potential water vendor and sign memorandum of understanding with them to provide water voucher to 1400 drought, COVID-19 affected households benefiting 8,400 persons to increase immediate access to clean and safe water for human and domestic use through water voucher system and conduct distribution of vouchers, which they can redeem for water at the specified water vendor. Vouchers will be distributed to all beneficiary families to ensure an equitable water distribution. Each voucher can be used to redeem specified quantity of chlorinated water of 7.5 liters per person per day (45 liters/household/day) for a period of 30 days. WASH Cluster guidelines recommend 7.5 l/person/day during emergencies. Distribution will take into consideration the most affected target IDPs and areas far from any available water point and these affected by drought. New displacement point will be given consideration as first priority as well as vulnerable households. A central location will be identified for distribution as women children, disables are expected to collect water from trucks, SRC will ensure to take into consideration the protection risk of the vulnerable population and will put in place compliant mechanism such as call center to ensure the target beneficiaries concerns are attended immediately1.2 Continuous capacity building of community institutions (water committees and pump operators) in operation and maintenance of developed water infrastructure shall be throughout the project implementation period. At least 30 Water management committees (15 from Ceelbarde and 15 from Jalalaqsi district) will be given training in order to manage rehabilitated/constructed water point, Each committee to consist of 5 people and at least 50% being women, SRC will also consider the disable and minority group interest in the WASH committee to enable ensure inclusivity of the target groups. The training will include basic administration, operation and maintenance of the WASH infrastructures. The water committees shall also receive on job training during construction phase of the water infrastructure. These trainings will ensure there are local technical skills to ensure sustainability of developed water facilities beyond the project1.3 SRC will construct 200 semi- permanent dislodge able latrines (100 latrine blocks of 2) in sites hosting newly arrived and evicted IDPs in Galgadud region to enhance hygiene and sanitation levels. It is anticipated that new sanitation facilities will benefit 660 households (3,960 IDP persons) in accordance with the WASH Sphere Standards for people in emergencies. At project kick-off, consultations will be made the local authorities, the protection cluster, camp and community leaders to ensure that latrines are built in sites where there is need and eviction is unlikely. Prior to the construction of latrines, consultations will be made with community leaders, especially women and girls, to incorporate female privacy and protection needs in the design. Access requirements for children and persons with disabilities will also be considered during the design stage. Separate latrine blocks will be constructed for men & women with entrance on opposite sides and clearly marked signs in the Somali language. Each latrine block will be lockable from inside and will be equipped with a hand-washing station. Hygiene promoters will stress the importance of hand-washing to mitigate the risk of AWD and other hygiene-related diseases as part of a comprehensive hygiene campaign planned under this project.
