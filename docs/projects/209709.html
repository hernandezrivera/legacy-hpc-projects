Project Summary

In Syria, protracted conflict, economic crisis and the consequences of earthquake in 2023 continue to have dramatic consequences on local population and a disproportionate impact on most vulnerable groups. According to the 2024 HNO, the Syrians in need of FSA assistance are 15.4M, a data steadily worsening in the last three years, and 124 sub-districts are considered as in severe need.
In 2024-2025, leveraging its extensive humanitarian experience and insights gained over the years, COOPI aims to persistently address and broaden its response to the escalating challenges of food security and agriculture in Aleppo, Rural Aleppo, Dar’a, Hama, Homs, Idleb, Raqqa, and Rural Damascus, Latakia. Aligned with the FSA sector's 2024 strategy, this proposed humanitarian project is a continuation of the one endorsed in 2023 HRP.
Its primary goal is to address SO1 by scaling up and sustaining minimum food consumption needs through life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance. Additionally, it aims to respond to SO2 and support the self-reliance of affected households by providing urgent and time-sensitive emergency agriculture inputs for protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income-generating opportunities to save and sustain lives. Finally, it will contribute to the achievement of SO3, enhancing communities' resilience and capacity to sustain household livelihoods by improving linkages with the value chain through the light rehabilitation of productive and economic infrastructure in the FSA sector.
This initiative will specifically target the most vulnerable households, with a focus on FHHs, elderly members, PwD, returnees/IDPs, families with a high number of children, PLW in areas with high needs (Severity scale 3 and above).
Throughout the project's implementation, beneficiary participation will be actively promoted, incorporating their input during market assessments, trader surveys, focus group discussions, monthly redemption activities, and feedback mechanisms. To ensure high-quality project implementation and effective feedback mechanisms, COOPI staff and volunteers will undergo training in Protection for PSEA, MEAL tools, and CfF processes. A particular attention will be devoted to protection mainstreaming throughout the programming.
Programmatic monitoring will be conducted through regular field visits, PDMs, and FGDs. COOPI field staff and MEAL teams will be present at all distributions and redemptions. Three rounds of PDM, including FCS and CSI surveys at each location, will be carried out (base-, mid- and end-line). Traders' monitoring and price surveys will precede every CfF distribution to ensure alignment with community prices, as compared to the WFP price bulletin.
To facilitate collaboration with various stakeholders and promote a synergistic, multi-sectoral approach, COOPI will ensure coordination with local authorities, as well as the FSA sector, ERL Sector, CWG and international and national actors active in the same target areas. Following an integrated area-based strategy, COOPI's FSL project will be implemented in areas where COOPI is already involved in protection activities. Strong coordination will be maintained to refer and advocate for needs in other areas identified during implementation. Through this area-based approach, COOPI will continue to collaborate with local authorities and actively coordinate at sub and national levels, referring identified needs and gaps found during implementation that cannot be addressed within COOPI's scope.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

COOPI has selected the communities of intervention in the target governorates according to the severity of needs identified in the HNO 2024, and by giving priority to the communities where COOPI already has access, presence, networks and staff. Moreover, the multi-sectoral need assessment conducted by COOPI in September 2023 contributed to gather valuable information and to propose activities tailored to the specific needs in the target communities. The intervention will provide emergency response to crisis-affected vulnerable people through short-term food assistance modalities (CA1) and monthly food assistance for assessed food-insecure individuals (CA2). Additionally, supplementary food assistance will be provided to those at risk of malnutrition, including children, PLW, elderly, and PwDs (CA3). According to FSA sector recommendations, the employed modality will be CfF distributions, with minimum 5 distributions ensuring coverage of 70% of the food SMEB price to meet daily caloric intake per person. To enhance food security at the household level, COOPI will also supply flour or bread directly to households or bakeries (CA4) and distribute to vulnerable households without stable incomes but with access to land and irrigation means agricultural inputs such as seeds, fertilizer, pesticides, and equipment, coupled with related training on post-harvest loss reduction techniques, composting, fertilizer use, and improved farming practices (CA5). Furthermore, protection food rations will be provided to vulnerable farming HHs (CA6), and support will be given to small-scale food production activities such as horticulture, poultry, seedling production, and market household vegetable production (CA7). Livestock production will be boosted through activities such as providing small livestock, animal feed, and training for veterinary services (CA9), including emergency livestock treatment and seasonal vaccinations. Market-driven IGAs will also be supported, including vocational training in food processing and input production units (CA11). In addition to these activities, COOPI will focus on the rehabilitation of irrigation systems, canals, wells, and water systems (CA13) and the establishment/strengthening of infrastructure services for local communities, including early warning and DRR systems and renewable energy (CA14). Collective productive units at the community level will also be established/strengthened, focusing on food processing, fodder, compost, dairy collection/processing, governance, renewable energy, and training (CA15). By taking advantage of the synergies with COOPI’s Protection ongoing interventions in the same communities, through the close collaboration with the Protection clusters, and thanks to specific training for all the staffs, the mainstreaming of protection approach will be ensured throughout the FSA programming.
