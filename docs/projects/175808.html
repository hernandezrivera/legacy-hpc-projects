Project Summary

About 8.3 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021 including 8,000,000 South Sudanese IDPs and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers, with 61% being children. People affected face multiple protection threats and vulnerabilities resulting from floods, active intercommunal violence, displacement, and breakdown of community protection systems and structures exacerbated by COVID-19 measures, economic decline, acute food insecurity, lack of other basic services and disease burden (Protection Cluster HRP 2021Strategy).
During these crises, 2.7 million children and their families are under extreme protection threats including increasing caseload of family separations and other child vulnerabilities, lack of psychosocial, recreational and learning opportunities for affected children, precarious incidents of sexual and physical violence against (SGBV) children and adolescents, use of children by armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG), and increase in child marriages, child exploitation and child abduction.
Without appropriate care and family-based support, affected children are forced to process their crisis experiences alone which may prevent them from being able to fully engage in daily activities such as playing, learning, or basic social interaction. Of the children in need, 35,000 children and their families will be targeted and provided with lifesaving child protection assistance in Duk, (Jonglei State), and Awerial and Yirol East(Lakes State) in 2021.
To ensure that boys and girls facing extreme protection threats and risks are protected, CINA will provide comprehensive case management including family tracing and reunification (FTR) for separated and unaccompanied children (UASC), case management support to other vulnerable children, including through cash assistance. These other vulnerable children (OVC) include children associated with armed forces/groups, child survivors of SGBV, children with severe psychosocial distress, children living with disabilities, chronically sick children and children in conflict with law. CINA will support caregivers and caseworkers to provide the critical child protection care and structured psychosocial support (PSS) services through emergency child friendly spaces, PSS in the community and in schools through support to teachers/PTAs, CP helpdesks and life skills and resilience services for adolescents. In order to increase access and strengthen resilience to well-coordinated age and gender-sensitive child protection and preventive services, CINA will conduct child protection risk awareness sessions, including mitigation of risks associated with floods, COVID-19 and flights during conflicts. CP messages will be provided including via radios, use of community-based child protection structures and adolescent clubs and peer support groups to prevent threats and risks facing children.
CINA will use its existing child protection expertise and long term presence in Jonglei (Duk, Twic East, Bor South) and Lakes (Awerial, Yirol East and Yirol West) to provide time-sensitive, static and field-based child protection response. To ensure centrality of protection, this project will address risks facing UASC, OVC, child headed households, adolescent girls, and children living with disabilities, etc. through strengthened community safety audits and community-based child protection mechanisms. CINA’s staff will sign and adhere to code of conduct and PSEA policy, and CP policy to ensure safety, dignity and avoid causing harm. The team will conduct community and caregivers’ consultations on sites for CFS, etc. and conduct monthly beneficiary feedback mechanisms on quality of CP services delivered by CINA as a means for Accountability to affected Populations. The child protection needs in Duk, Awerial and Yirol East are very extreme and USD 500,000 is therefore urgently required to save lives, protect dignity and future of the children and families affected by multiple crises there.

1 - Needs

An estimated 2.7 million people, including 2.3 million children, are at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect and in acute need of immediate child protection in 2021. Children continue to be disproportionately affected by crisis and exposed to multiple protection risks including recruitment by armed groups, psychosocial distress, family separation, violence and exploitation in 61 most affected counties. High level of stress, loss of friends and family members are also taking a toll on mental health and emotional development of children, further exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic. Studies suggest that at least one in three respondents met the criteria for a mental disorder. A recent FSNMS assessment found that 30 per cent of children had behavioral change, showing signs of distress due to repeated exposure to conﬂict and shocks.Since the outbreak of violence in December 2013, about 26,184 (12,214girls and 13,970 boys) cases of UASC, missing and other vulnerable children have been documented in South Sudan and only6,348 reunified, with up to 4,000 children yet to be reunited. In 2020, the government of South Sudan re-committed itself to addressing grave violations against children by signing a comprehensive Action Plan on all six grave violations. The priority now is to support the implementation of the action plan and ensure sustained provision of critical child protection services. Due to various humanitarian shocks, the community care strategy to prevent and avoid sexual violence against girls and preventing early marriage still needs to be rolled out. There are 486,874 people in need of protection in Awerial, Yirol East and Duk and 179,486 of them are children. The risks faced by children are intensified by heavy flooding which smash Duk, resulting to destruction of shelters, crops, drinking water sources, child friendly spaces, schools, health facilities and market centers and impeding humanitarian’s ability to move across the affected areas. In all these contexts, children are the most affected. CINA has active caseload of 811 UASC and other vulnerable children in the inter-agency CPIMS+ database for Awerial, Yirol East, Bor South, Twic East awaiting case management. Children continue to face a multitude of risks, including abduction, GBV and other forms of violence, recruitment into armed groups and separation from families. These risks are further challenged by COVID-19 with reduced access to schools and the disruption of environments where children grow and develop. Economic pressures from COVID-19 have forced some families to rely on negative coping mechanisms, including child labor and early marriage. Decades of insecurity and upheaval have damaged traditional social structures and weakened justice mechanisms, leaving children more vulnerable to protection risks. Children are exposed to widespread family separations, recruitment by armed forces and groups, sexual abuse and exploitation. Family and community support systems have been devastated and harmful cultural practices including girl marriage and other forms of violence against children have heightened including increased number of boys and girls being exposed to harsh labour, living in child-headed households and working on the streets.Precisely, these needs are urgent and require immediate and unhindered intervention to save the lives and childhood of the affected South Sudanese children.

2 - Project activities

In accordance with South Sudan CPAoR strategy for HRP 2021, this project is going to focus on the following interventions: Provision of Comprehensive case management including for 300 UASC (160 girls, 140 boys) to be reached with family tracing and reunification services and 600 other vulnerable children (320 girls, 280 boys) for case management including with use of cash based intervention. This will also include service mapping and establishment of safe referral pathways, etc. CINA will use its current CBI methods for general protection to implement CBI in child protection case management. This will follow the Case Management SOPs, vulnerability criteria; use of banking agent, risks analysis for cash in childprotection and use of other existing guidance tools for cash which will be explored; 30 children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG) will be provided with comprehensive case management support including reintegration, education and livelihood opportunities. Provision of Psychosocial Support targeting 32000 children, caregivers and community members (12000 girls, 10000 boys, 6000 women, 4000 men) including provision of caregivers support sessions. This will be provided using community based initiatives, with particular emphasis to PSS in schools, CFS and youth resilience centers and working with youth as members of community-based child protection committees; Capacity building on Child Protection including PSS , Case management and child protection in emergencies (CPiE) capacity building will be provided to 250 frontline child protection staff, facilitators and community-based child protection committees (100 men, 150 women) to promote local approaches and project sustainability; 1000 caregivers will be reached with PSS and parenting sessions. 4 referral pathways will be strengthen to support child protection case management referrals. Child Protection awareness raising and prevention messaging will be conducted targeting 35,000 children and community members (13000 girls, 12000 boys, 6000 women, 4000men) in the schools and community using different forums and means, integrated into multi-sectoral outreach activities including WASH, education, nutrition, and health. All beneficiaries (35,000 people in total) will be reached both with PSS and CP risks messaging; Strengthening of community structures and mechanisms including community based child protection committees (CBCPC), Child Protection desks, etc. and engaging youth and adolescents in peace building, CP risks mitigation and recreational activities to foster harmony; Timely, Child Protection monitoring , coordination and assessments will be conducted within the deep locations of the prioritized counties using inter-agency tools to inform child protection response and advocacy as well as providing guidance to appropriate multi-sectoral interventions. CINA will make use of existing operational child protection tools including revised CPMS, CPIMS+, case management SOPs, PSS manual and Practical guidance for working girls associated with armed forces and groups and support to child survivors initiative during project planning, implementation and coordination.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

From CINA's ongoing interventions and markets assessments in priority locations, the local markets are served from Juba in dry season when roads are accessible. Cash transfer assistance will meet essential Case Management support for unaccompanied and separated children and other vulnerable children such as child mothers, survivors of GBV, injuries to children, CAAFAG, etc. in IPC 4 locations who lack access to basic services due to floods, active conflicts, roads infrastructure. Cash support will be provided within family-based care arrangements following vulnerability criteria and protection risks assessment to avoid causing harm

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CINA will ensure project beneficiaries are sensitized about the PSEA, and their role to identify and report it appropriately. CINA will display PSEA messages on the banner and/or disseminate them during awareness raising activities. A PSEA reporting form will be kept at CFS, Help Desk, etc. and beneficiaries well sensitized about it. They will use this to report cases directly to PSEA focal person at CINA's head office. A hotline number will also be displayed on the PSEA banner which beneficiaries can use to call and report PSEA cases in the community.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will target 15% children with disabilities, women caregivers and equal number of girls as boys. Their views and preferences will be used to realign provision of case management support in terms of material support such as wheelchairs, eyeglasses, as well as incorporation of awareness messages such as on non-discrimination, gender equality, equal play and learning opportunities for girls and boys, rights to girl's education, ending child marriages, etc. into child protection risks messaging.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During the project inception at the community, beneficiaries will be informed about the project objectives, targets, activities, and their participation in the project. Community-based child protection networks will be formed/strengthened to convey any project information to the beneficiaries during the implementation. Activities such as awareness raising, caregivers sessions, and PSS activities in CFS will be used to pass important information about the activities of the project. CINA will strengthen its existing community beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanisms through display of complaints box at CFS, schools, Helpdesks, and during awareness sessions. CINA will also use feedback forms during M&E activities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The beneficiaries' feedback about services rendered by CINA in their location will be synthesized by CINA management and used during the project planning or implementation to adjust next phase of response.
