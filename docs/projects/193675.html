Project Summary

The project will support 400 vulnerable women-headed households (Target 581 children, 1123 adult)
through a 9 months intervention aiming at providing shelter rehabilitation/repair and NFIs to protect people in substandard housing conditions, exposed to protection concerns. The priority target will be focused on HHs having PWDs, elderlies, persons with chronic diseases. The project comes in line with HRP 2023 strategic objective 2 and it falls under the Shelter and NFIs Cluster Objective 2”vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity”.
The project has been designed based on SIF and its partner Beit Lahia Development Association (BLDA) extensive experience in the field and in close consultation with beneficiaries as well as the shelter cluster. SIF and BLDA will ensure coordination with and participation in the Cash Working Group (CWG). SIF will manage the grant, monitor, carry out Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), and protection activities, and report the achievements. BLDA will carry out all the rehabilitation activities. The project will target 400 women headed HHs in North Gaza and Gaza governorates living in substandard housing and overcrowded conditions. These HHs have been identified by SIF through multiple shelters needs assessment in August 2022. In September 2022, SIF and BLDA collected more qualitative data from 29 women who participated in two focus group discussions (FGDs) in North Gaza and Gaza governorates . The assessment found a need for urgent rehabilitation/repair and NFIs assistance to improve safety and privacy and address the protection concerns. Based on the assessment, 200 women will be assisted to rehabilitate of damaged and/or substandard shelters to meet shelter basic needs and minimum standards, including adaptation for the PWDs and upgrade/separate shared toilet and kitchen facilities with an average of $3,000 for each HH (either through contracting or self- help/restricted cash modality.
200 women at risk of exposure or affected by natural or manmade hazards will receive cash assistance average of $ 400 per HH for winterization/summarization assistance or shelter NFIs.

The project will assist the targeted HHs by improving housing condition through technical consultancies and guidance, capacity building in planning and negotiation skills with the support from SIF, BLDA’s team and community committees (CCs) in the targeted locations. BLDA’s site engineers will assist each HH to develop an individualized rehabilitation plan and NFI package corresponding to the specific needs and priority of their shelter gaps to comply with shelter upgrading minimum standards.
SIF and BLDA will apply a mix of response modalities, including shelf help/cash and or contracting service for shelter rehabilitation and repair. BLDA will consult with all beneficiaries on their preferred modality based on their capacity to undertake the repair works. SIF and BLDA will also comply with COVID19 protective measures(if exists) to ensure safety, and do no harm and child safeguarding policy to the targeted beneficiaries’ and other involved stakeholders.
The project will ensure that women, girl, children, PWDs and all beneficiaries are protected according to PSEA network recommendations and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) principles including training, dissemination of awareness materials, and safe access to complaints channel. SIF’s protection team (Protection manager/ Child Safeguarding Policy Focal Point (CSPFP) and Counselors) will carefully monitor the protection concerns (GBV, abuse, exploitation, and child labor) by building the capacity of the project’s team, partner, contractor and suppliers staff, and targeted beneficiaries and applying referral approach.

1 - Needs

According to shelter and NFI HNO 2023, 591000 Palestinians in Gaza need humanitarian assistance in order to access adequate shelter including basic HH supplies. The MSNA 2022 assessment has found that 59.8% of the assessed households in Gaza Strip reported existing shelter damage, defects, or issues at their shelters, such as: Cracks in some walls in 42.7%, Opening or cracks in the roof at 30.3%, Broken or cracked windows in 17.3%, Lack of or bad condition of kitchen 12.7%, and Lack of or bad condition of bathroom 9.2%. Moreover, the assessment reveals that 60520 female-headed households suffer from shelter damage and defects, or issues at their shelters. Women and girls living in substandard, overcrowded, unsafe shelters with lack of privacy and limited access to hygiene facilities are highly exposed to the risk of gender-based violence (GBV). These conditions are likely to increase protection related concerns and consequent negative coping mechanisms such as early marriages, and greater exposure to GBV-related risks. MOPWH shelter assessment in 2020 reveals that 21 500 housing units in Gaza are classified as substandard and do not meet minimum requirements of living space, weather protection, hygiene facilities, and privacy, require different degrees of rehabilitations. In July 2022, SIF conducted an annual needs assessment for 2410 women-headed households in North Gaza (785 HH) and Gaza (1625 HH) governorates who are registered in SIF’s sponsorship program. Of those, 800 HH were identified as living under substandard shelter conditions. In August 2022, SIF conducted a social and shelter vulnerability assessment for a sample of 10% out of 800. The main findings are presented as following:- 89% of sampled HHs suffer from humidity or mouldily in walls.- 52% are exposed partially to rain in winter due to holes or leakage.- 34% of HHs suffer from hot or cold weather in summer or winter.- 22% of HHs are dark and gloomy, 20% of HHs are poorly ventilated and suffer from bad smell.- 39% of HHs lack proper sanitation installations, and 32% of HHs have incomplete kitchens and lack essential items.- 40% of sampled HHs live in overcrowded houses and lack privacy for cloth changing and no separation between male and female family members for sleeping which expose them to risk of gender based violence. - 24% of sampled HHs are living in unsafe or unsecure houses lacking top roof fence, unlocked doors, and damaged electricity installation.In September 2022, SIF collected more qualitative data from 29 women (including 14 elderly) through two focus groups discussion (FGDs) in North Gaza (1) and Gaza (1 FGD) Governorates. The results of the FGs discussion has confirmed the quantitative findings that resulted from the shelter needs assessment. All participant women reported that students (boys and girls) struggle to find an adequate and separate space for studying and they suffer from noise and poor lighting. Youth (boys and girls) also feel shy to invite their friends to their homes. Children do not have enough space to play. Children frequently get sick because of inadequate ventilation and lighting. Water leaking in winter is another layer of burdens for all family members. Particularly, women and girls need to do extra cleaning works due to untilled floors and rough walls, particularly in bathrooms and kitchens. Elderly and people with disability (PWDs) reported that they are at risk of pursuing daily live activities due to lack of adaptations or accessories in toilets and entrances.Participant young women and participants who have youth and/or children (girls and boys) reported that their housing units do not provide sufficient privacy for cloth changing and no separation between female and male family members for sleeping. All participant women reported that there is a need for NFIs, such as, blankets, carpets, beds, cupboards, mattresses, kitchen tools, washing machines, ovens, and refrigerators. The rehabilitation modality has been set on the basis of the women preferences, as 50% of participants women prefer to receive cash assistance to carry out the rehabilitation through self-help approach and the other 50% prefer contracting modality as they cannot manage to do it by themselves. Based on this assessment, SIF and BLDA has designed the project to respond to the needs for 400 women-headed HHs, of which 200 HH will benefit from rehabilitation or repair works and 200 HH will benefit from shelter NFIs.

2 - Activities

SIF and BLDA will implement the project activities based on experience and available shelter needs assessment of potential beneficiaries. The project will respond to the need of rehabilitation and NFIs for 400 female-headed HHs through the following activities:1- Rehabilitation of damaged and/or substandard shelters to meet shelter basic needs and minimum standards, including adaptation interventions for the persons with disabilities, elderly and upgrade/separate shared toilet and kitchen facilities.SIF and BLDA will conduct beneficiary selection based on the social and shelter vulnerability criteria (minimum standard) adopted by the shelter cluster including, scoring, and ranking system. BLDA site engineers will carry out shelter technical assessment and SIF MEAL will conduct data quality assurance and scoring system. BLDA’s site engineers (2 males and 2 females) will assist each HH to develop an individual rehabilitation/NFIs plan based on HH specific needs. A working plan will be revised and the cost of each HH repair works/NFIs will be determined based prices market survey. BLDA will sign an agreement with selected beneficiaries enclosed with an individual rehabilitation/NFIs plan that determine the amount of the cash assistance that each HH will receive or will be implemented by contractor. The 200 HH will receive either cash assistance for rehabilitation with an average of $3,000 per HH by self-help modality or rehabilitation service with an average of $3000/ HH by contracting modality based on beneficiaries’ preferences. 2-Provision of winterization/summarization assistance or shelter/household Non-Food Items (NFIs) to vulnerable households at risk200 HHs will receive cash for NFIs for manage overcrowding and improve access to basic household supplies with an average of $400/ HH. Each HH will develop a diversified NFIs package based on HH’s priority needs. NFIs cash will be disbursed at one off payment.SIF will promote applying innovative methods where possible, such as, using sustainable repair solution and durable and energy saving materials that can mitigate climate change impact (isolation, false ceiling or any relevant materials available in the market) as well as promote Build Back Better approach to reduce shelter vulnerability, specifically for HHs with loose roofs, such as (metal sheets and asbestos). The rehabilitation works will include shelter adaptation to meet the needs of PWDs and elderly and enhance privacy, improve WASH facilities, and repair leaking roof, doors and windows to protect the families from extreme weather. BLDA will disburse the cash through multiple payments based on progress and the completion of all the repair works. The first payment is an advanced payment amounts 30% of the total cost, 2nd payment amounts 30% based on progress, and the final payment amounts 40% will be disbursed after the monitoring visits by BLDA team confirming the progress and the completion of all the repair works according to the individual plan agreed with BLDA. This disbursement method is subjected to modification by which BLDA and SIF ensure effective monitoring for the best utilization of the cash assistance. SIF and BLDA will conduct one-day coaching session for beneficiaries in small groups to introduce the project, agreement clauses, implementation plan and provide awareness related to SIF complaint and feedback system , child protection, and PSEA, considering other protection mainstreaming issues. BLDA will build the women capacity in negotiation skills with labor and vendors to efficiently use the cash assistance either for rehabilitation or for buying the NFIs.SIF will effectively engage the community and enhance the participation through BLDA’s community committees (CCs). The CCs will participate in the design and implementation of the project activities through assessment, and feedback and monitoring and evaluation. They will support the women by selecting the skilled labors and vendors in or outside the area. Engineers will provide further technical instructions/training to help the beneficiaries reach the required quality of materials and repair works and will conduct monitoring visits to ensure that the beneficiaries efficiently use the cash installments according to their repair plan.The project will ensure that women, girl, children, PWDs and all population are protected from PSEA according to PSEA network recommendation and IASC principles through PSEA activities.The Project has adopted a safe complaint channel(s) for beneficiaries based on previous consultations with women in SIF sponsorship program and accessible to different groups by complaint boxes, generic phone, or write directly to SIF’s STOP e-mail. 9.1c - How will feedback be used?

SIF takes into account the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) to build inclusive beneficiaries’ communication, participation, and feedback mechanisms over the project cycle including sharing information, listening carefully to targeted beneficiaries and involving them in decision-making to contribute to effective intervention and improve the quality of the delivered services. Beneficiaries’ feedback will support a real-time adaptation of the project to precisely fit their specific needs. It will be used to hold the project team and the contractor accountable and to inform upward accountability to senior management. Each beneficiary will decide on the priority need of rehabilitation by drafting individual rehabilitation plan. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

SIF and BLAD will prioritize the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and consider the principles of Do No Harm. At the begging of the project SIF and BLDA will do further analysis of the protection context, identification and mitigation of risks to people’s safety and wellbeing that the project might inadvertently exacerbate.Base on the needs assessment, beneficiaries identified the gaps of shelter that negatively impacted their safety and raised protection concerns. SIF will train BLDA and SIF staff who takes part in assessments/response on the protection implications and risks and how to response to protection issues. Despite the methodology of the project implementation - either through the contracting approach or shelf-help and cash modality, the beneficiaries will be given full freedom to decide on their specific and priority shelter needs in a way that maintain their dignity. Also it will foster build back better approach to provide long standing rehabilitation works. The beneficiaries will participate in the implementation of the rehabilitation works to ensure that rehabilitation works meet their needs and they will be able to input how the rehabilitation works can further address their protection needs. In the case of the contracting modality, SIF will oversee the contractor’s works to ensure there is no discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, exploitation or any other harm done to beneficiaries and it holds the contractor accountable. A specific emphasis will be done on applying protective measures and behaviors related to rehabilitation works to ensure the protection and safety of all people involved in the project (project team, beneficiaries and laborer). SIF will ensure compliance by all parties to its protection Code of Conduct and protective measures related to COVID 19 (if exist). SIF mainstreams the Abusive Behavioral Procedure (ABP) among all project stakeholders including the beneficiaries as anyone can submit a real, clear, and accurate description of the facts of abuse or misconduct incident by writing directly to stop@secours-islamigue.org. The ABP documents will be an essential part of the contract document with the beneficiaries. All will sign agreement to undertake their understanding and compliance with SIF CSP.
