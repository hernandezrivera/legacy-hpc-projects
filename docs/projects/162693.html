Project Summary


Zimbabwe is going through one of its worst food security crises, with a total of 5.8. million people (4.1 million rural, 1.6 million urban) estimated to be in need of emergency food assistance. According to the IPC Zimbabwe Food Insecurity report for June to December 2019, at least 65% of the population in Gokwe North district in Midlands Province were unable to meet their immediate food needs between October and December 2019, and the district is classified in emergency phase. In Gokwe urban 35.7% of the population (about 13 873 people) is considered food insecure (ZIMVAC Urban Livelihoods Assessment 2019). These people are facing moderate to large food consumption gaps or meet their basic food needs through depleting essential assets or employing other emergency coping strategies. This situation is largely attributed to a poor 2018/19 season which was characterized by erratic rainfall patterns and Fall Army-Worm pest infestation. The Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWSNET) considers Zimbabwe’s 2018/2019 agricultural season among the nation’s worst droughts in the last four decades. The drought resulted in low harvests and limited carry over stock into 2019. Reduced income levels compared to same period last year has resulted in reduced household purchasing power. In addition, the prices of most basic goods have gone beyond the reach of many. Although most of the country received good rains in November adequate for planting, limited availability and affordability of agro-inputs has negatively affected planting activities. This poor start to the 2019/20 planting season increases the chances of a bad season and therefore continued increase in vulnerability of the rural population.
Welthungerhilfe will, guided by the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification, build on its work in Gokwe North and urban under the Livelihoods and Food Security Program to respond to the food insecurity crisis through the implementation of a 12 months project whose overall objective is to improve food security for the most vulnerable households in Gokwe. The first component of the project will meet the immediate food security needs of 13 873 individuals in 6 wards in Gokwe Urban using the food voucher modality, with the goal of ensuring that beneficiaries have access to a food basket comprising of 7.5kg cereal, 1.5kg pulses and 0.75kg vegetable oil per person per month. The voucher system is a suitable market based assistance delivery mechanism feasible for Gokwe urban, based on the WFP Market Monitoring reports of 9 December 2019, which indicates 100% availability of maize meal, pulses and oil on the market in Gokwe Urban. WHH will identify and contract reputable and established suppliers for the specified commodities, including Metro Peech, N. Richards, Gain and other local retailer. Beneficiary selection will be done through community meetings, during which the communities identify most affected households, with the facilitation local traditional leaders. The project will specifically target the most food insecure and vulnerable households, including child headed households, female headed households, households with chronically ill members, households headed by older persons as well as persons with disabilities. The second component of the action will provide support for emergency agricultural production and rebuilding household food security for 20 000 rural households in Gokwe North. Activities under this component will include provision of seed packs, training in Good Agronomic Practices (GAP) and rehabilitation of community assets to ensure continuous agriculture productivity.
WHH response takes into account protection principles which include safety and dignity, meaningful access and participation as well as accountability. A Complaints Response Mechanism will be set up which includes a help desk during distribution days and a telephone number which will be availed to the beneficiaries to call and provide feedback.

1 - Activities

Based on beneficiary feedback collected during the implementation of existing projects by WHH in Gokwe , the food security needs of men, women, boys and girls there will be met through the provision of food parcels. Community consultations will be done to explain the criteria of targeting for the most vulnerable households, as well as the modality i.e. vouchers. Dissemination of information on how the distributions will be done in line with the Do No Harm principle. Redemption points will be distributed in such a way as to ensure ease of access, particularly for women and people living with disabilities. Outcome: Improved food consumption and agricultural production for target households Objective 1: To save lives by providing support to 13 873 people in Gokwe urban to meet their immediate food needs. Activities under objective 1 will be carried out between January and April 2020. Activities 1.1Identification/Registration of vulnerable food-insecure households in Gokwe urban 1.2 Identification of reputable local suppliers: These will be responsible for aggregation of the commodities and will enter into contracts with WHH for the duration of the food distribution period. 1.3 Monthly voucher distribution and redemption: The project will make use of pre-loaded smartcards that work with a point-of-sale device. These cards will be given to the beneficiaries who will use them to purchase the specified commodities in select retail locations. Subsequent top-ups to the cards will be made for new distribution cycles. 1.4 Post distribution monitoring for collecting data on household level consumption and expenditure data. 1.5 Cooking demonstrations-these will be conducted during voucher distributions with the intention to raise community awareness, provide information and knowledge exchange on maternal, child and household nutrition. WHH will maximise on its experience in nutrition using its Linking Agriculture and Natural Resource Management towards Nutrition Security (LANN+) approach, developed to strengthen the capacity of households to combat malnutrition Objective 2: Provide support for emergency agricultural production and rebuild household food security for 20 000 households This objective will be ongoing from January 2020 to December 2020. Under this objective, the following activities are proposed: 2.1 Support small scale vegetable production for 20 000 households in Gokwe North: In the first half of the project, beneficiary households will receive standard seed packs including rape, tomatoes, onion seed. 2.2 Support 20 000 food insecure households with sorghum seed: In preparation for the 2020/21 season, 20 000 households will each be supported with 5kgs of seed, and 3 bags of fertiliser, sufficient for 0.5 ha production. Selection of farmers will be based on availability of labour and adequate farming land. Small grains are drought tolerant and will be grown primarily for household consumption, contributing to improved nutrition. 2.3 Community consultations to identify locations for boreholes and irrigation schemes to be rehabilitated by the project 2.4 Facilitate rehabilitation of 72 boreholes (3x24 wards) and 15 irrigation schemes. This activity will ensure continuous water supply for the communities and enhance productivity in the household gardens which will be supported in activity 2.1. 2.5 WHH together with Agritex will conduct 2 day trainings for 4800 farmers on Good Agricultural Practices, and post-harvest handling and storage to ensure better harvests for food security.2.6 Progress monitoring and quality assurance.The Project Head will be responsible for provision of oversight and support to staff in the implementation and monitoring of activities under both objectives. The project will incorporate WFP’s SCOPE platform because it is an already established system and therefore more cost effective, and it can be used throughout the programme intervention cycle e.g. for beneficiaryregistration .

2 - Indicators

Monitoring activities will be done monthly to collect data on the following: Objective 1;Indicator 1.2. Improved average number of meals eaten per day at householdIndicator 1.2 Improved availability and access to food through the voucher intervention.Objective 2; This objective will be monitored quarterly, with indicator 2.2 monitored annually;Indicator 2.2 Household with improved dietary diversity scoreIndicator 2.3 Number of farmers who adopted improved post-harvest handling and storage methods.Indicator 2.4 Number of farmers with improved Knowledge on Good Agricultural Practices These outcome indicators relating to household food consumption patterns will give an indication of whether food consumption has stabilized, increased or decreased during the implementation period. They also give an indication on whether dietary diversity has improved over the project period. Post distribution monitoring will be done two weeks after each distribution. The project will closely monitor the markets in the two districts and will make reference to WFP weekly monitoring reports, in addition to on going market monitoring by field staff. This information will be used by the project team to make any necessary adjustments to activities during the implementation period.
