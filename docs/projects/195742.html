Project Summary

The project is focused on Emergency Food security support assistance intervention geared to support mainly 20000ulnerable households in Gogrial West, Tonj North ( Warrap State ) , Mayendit and Guitt Counties ( Unity State) that are largely affected by unprecendented climatic shocks that led to flooding and localized fighting for 2022 cropping season . The project will be implemented from January, 2023 to December, 2023. The key objective of this project is to enhance and sustain Emergency Food Production (increase people’s access to food and availability of food) through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inuts , fishing and livestock. and sales. The project targets will be vulnerable Households & populations: women, girls, men & boys, elderly, disabled, PLW, children U5, HIV & chronic sick
The project will target 120,000 families ( 20,000 Households in these four most highly food insecure communities . According to the recent IPC report , the targeted population in mayendit and mayom Counties in Unity State are s 32000 and 31,000 people respectively and in Warrap , Gogrial west and Tonj North are 65,000 and 66,000 people. All of these ppopulation as currently are currently in Phase four (4) of the Emergency Food security phase (IPC report Oct. 2022 . In the two targeted states the respective Counties are likely to erode into into Catastrophic Food insecurity Phase Five if urgent action is not taken. The proposed project will in addition to enhancing Agricultural food production, reduction in post harvest losses, restoration and support of fishing activities, focuses on restoring , strengthening and sustaining [livelihoods ( IPC 3 and IPC 4); Women’s and female youth , needs , voice and rights; accountability with information provision , participation and complaint and feedback mechanism and access to support returnees. The key indicative activities of this project will be Emergency distribution of seeds, fishing kits and tools distribution, livestock treatment and vaccination, livelihood support mainly to returnees and provision of timely food security information
The project is line with the current HRP strategic objective 2 ( SO2) and is in support of FSL cluster objective 2 and FSL cluster objective 4.
The main programmatic approach is through direct distribution of inputs, provision of technical agronomic messages, working with accountability to affected committees (AAP) and providing key messaging on how to prevent the spread of COVID-19 to the affected population. The project activities falls within the seasonal calendar and within the livelihood zones of Warrap state and Unity State ; hence it is a viable and very much contextualized.
Centre to the project implementation is the consideration given to address protection concern and the involve of the accountability to the affected community. The needs of Women, boys and children are focused and will be robustly addressed in this project. The proposed project will have strong synergy and it will integrate with the nutrition cluster wherein the caretaker or mothers of malnourished children will be trained and supported with high nutritious vegetable seeds for production. The project cash value is 200, 000 USD and the project is expected to get in-kind contribution through FAO pipeline for the core inputs.

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian Situation in South Sudan is unprecedented deteriorating instead of improving .According to 2023 Humanitarian needs Overview contributed to by the Food Security and Livelihood Cluster, An estimated 8.2[ Comprises FSL PiN 7.7 million without Abyei plus refugees (300K) and Abyei southern & northern sector (188K);] million people, including refugees and Abyei, are likely to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2023 lean season (May – July)[ The IPC analysis conducted in October 2022 estimates that 7.76 million people (excluding Panyikang County’s population but includes Abyei southern sector PiN) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between April and July 2023.]. This is comparable with the situation described in the FSL sectoral analysis (December 2021) and IPC analysis (March 2022)[ The IPC analysis conducted in March 2022 estimated that 7.74 million people would likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between April and July 2022.] where food security needs remained large and severe. Food insecurity is highest in Jonglei where 79% of the population will likely face severe acute food insecurity, followed by Unity (75%), Warrap (70%), Northern Bahr El Ghazal (66%), Lakes (61%) and Upper Nile (60%) with the highest levels of severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) at the peak of the lean season 2023. The main drivers continue to be: (1) covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks (such as floods and dry spells, inflation and rising food prices, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to conflict and insecurity); and (2) household-level idiosyncratic stressors (such as death of breadwinner, gender-based violence, morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene, and lack of access to essential basic services) which are greatly amplified by the compounded effect of shocks.Unprecedented flooding this year caused by the Climate change is the main contributing factors responsible for high food insecurity. Above normal rainfall for the third consecutive times in n year 2022 led to prolonged flooding, which impacted areas that had not flooded in previous years. An estimated 835,000 people were affected by severe flooding between May and October 2022, and 80 per cent of those affected were from Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile states. Many flood-displaced people, including those who were displaced by the 2021 flood were unable to return to their homes until early 2022,. Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei states were the most affected in terms of crop and livestock production. The UN’s Food and Agricultural Organization (FAO) assessment findings indicated that 65,107 hectares of land planted with cereals had been damaged, with an estimated loss of 37,624 tonnes of grain in the flood-affected areas.According to October 2022 IPC released report, Nationally, food insecurity remains high with 62.7% of the population (7.76 million people) facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity.The three most food insecure states during the three analysis periods are as below, with the % showing the proportion of population that is severely food insecure (IPC Phase 3 or worse) in the state:o October – November 2022:§ Jonglei (68.0% of the population in IPC Phase 3+)§ Unity (66.7% of the population in IPC Phase 3+)§ Northern Bahr el Ghazal (61.9% of the population in IPC Phase 3+). The key drivers of food insecurity are: Climatic Shocks, Conflict and insecurity the continued poor performance of the economy, economic impacts of the conflict in Ukraine that saw the increase of fuel and food prices in South Sudan, ncreasing vulnerability / poverty because of the depleted / depleting asset base caused by the prolonged “crisis”, Reduced humanitarian investment that has led to scaling down of response to the population in need hence there is limited / or no respite for the severely affected populations.

The key indicative activities are:Main season seeds and tools distirbuton; fishing , vegetable and crop kits;distribution of lean season dry season vegetable seeds, tools, fishing kits Distribution of rapid response kits, provision of technical messages in production of crops, vaccination, treatment of livestock, establishment and management of cold chain, provision of key messages that will help mitigate climate change and provision of timely food security information

Number of livestock vaccine cold chain established . Number of national and state level food security and livelihood coordination meetings; The success of the project will be measured through impact assessment studies . The key contributing data to this measurement will be data or reports generated from regular field visits in monitoring the progress of the intervention, beneficiary list, field visit monitoring data, Quarterly and final reports, and Success stories
