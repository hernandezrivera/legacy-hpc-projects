Project Summary

Takaful will support formal and non-formal education for 40000 (5-17, 20800 boys, 19200 girls) who are dropping out or at risk of school drop-out, in addition to 1416 (778 males and 638 females) education personal through supporting schools and educational centers that have ended fund or will end in 2019, in addition to providing the dropped-out children with non-formal education.
This project is critical due to its significance in mitigating social risks that might lead to educational gaps if they were not dealt with immediately. These risks include early marriage, recruitment into armed groups, and child labor.
The project is based on three major interventions:
1-Takaful will support formal education in 24 schools where those schools include 10800 students (5616 boys and 5184 girls) and their fund has ended or will end soon. The intervention minimizes the risk of students dropping out, where monthly incentives to teachers will be provided, as they don’t have any alternative fund in addition to providing schools with heating materials, and operational costs.
2-Takaful will support the educational process through providing 6000 Out of School Children (OOSC), (3120 boys and 2880 girls), (65% IDPs and 35% HCs) with non-formal education in 24 schools through catch up classes using curriculum B. Those intensive sessions will help OOSC to catch up with their peers in the formal education. Teachers will be provided with incentives, as they don't receive any financial support from any other NGOs, monthly operational costs, and heating materials to face the cold of winter. PSS weekly sessions will also help children, who spent long time away from school, ridding of the harsh experience of war conditions and motivate them to continue their study at school. The 24 targeted schools will be equipped with safety and security tools, also, the project will involve 400 children with disability , who drop out of school, and overcome the difficulties that they may face through providing them with transportation service in addition to providing schools with tools for children with disability. Learning materials will be distributed to students, boys and girls. distributing teaching materials to teachers and teaching resources for school. Moreover, teachers will receive light training sessions on non-formal education, curriculum B, child protection, and PSS.
3-Takaful will support the formal and non-formal education within 48 schools that include 21600 students, boys and girls, IDPs and HCs where those schools suffer from poor capacities and need urgent assistance in order to increase their absorption capacity for more students. Where receiving more dropped out students can be guaranteed through providing; safety and security tools, disability requirement,
student kits will be distributed students, teacher kits and teaching resources, light training sessions on non-formal education curriculum B, child protection, and PSS.
At the beginning of the project Takaful will conduct a meeting with local community and local actors to
ensure their participation in the project, explain the project goals and take their suggestions and notes
to develop the project activities and implementation.
Takaful will coordinate with Education cluster and with other organization who works in the same sector and field to reach the right beneficiaries and avoid any duplication.
Plan B will be implemented if we couldn't reach some schools or they receive support from another NGO, by selecting schools with the same criteria in other sub-districts where we have full access.
We coordinated with protection cluster to provide protection activities in the project. A methodology was adopted according to the cluster's guidance, taking into account safe access to children with disability.

Needs

The protracted crisis in Syria, now in its eighth year, has forced 2.1 million, over one third of children, out-of-school and has put a further 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Over 5.8 million school-aged children and about 245,000 education personnel are in need of education assistance inside Syria, 61 per cent of whom are in acute and immediate need. (WoS Education Severity needs analysis). Increased internal displacement has put the current infrastructure under enormous pressure. 2.6 million Children have been displaced within Syria. Schools in IDP/returnee receiving communities have limited absorption capacity for newly arriving students, causing overcrowding. (OACH population Data as September 2018). Takaful Alsham Assessment team conducted in October 2018 multi sector need assessment, In order to respond to the most vulnerable needs of population including to alleviate their suffering, this assessment included education needs conducted in 13 sub-districts in north west Syria Idleb and Aleppo Governorate. 158 Key informant interviews were conducted with Education Key informants (males and females) from education bodies. The most important require needs are summarized as; 28% providing teachers with incentives, 20% establishment or rehabilitation additional classrooms or education centers, and 17% providing with school books, other additional required needs were the need for supporting stationary costs, rehab WASH facilities and teaching supplies. 90% of assessed schools in targeted sub-districts operating schools. 48% of them need maintenance, 38% of the schools working two shifts (morning and afternoon). The percentage of dropping out was 34% of total school age children in assessed sub-districts this rate differs between IDPs and host communities, 46% of dropping out children of schools are from age 15 to 17 year. 41% of total school age children had received psychosocial support while 59% of them haven’t received. Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat 1: Changes in the security situation, particularly in project target areas, affects the safety/mobility/ displacement of beneficiaries/staff and results in being unable to participate in project. This includes the risk of targeted attacks and bombing of schools/ communities and the risks of mines, airstrikes and cross fire.Likelihood: HighImpact: HighMitigating Measure: Closely monitor the security situation in coordination with partners and relevant stakeholders and conduct a security assessments. They will ensure that staff members and teachers are trained in safety and security best practices, including mine risk education.If and when the local security situation deteriorates: a) Immediately inform internal emergency focal point on the situation; b) Coordinate with partners, cluster and hibernate/suspend the operations if necessary; c) Examine the possibilities of adopting different modalities to implement the project. All important documents will be saved as soft copies, so that they will not get lost due to an attack. Decisions are taken in advance whether hard copies will be destroyed or not. • Communication with donors to accept flexibilityProtection Risk/Threat 2: Attacks on schools Likelihood: mediumImpact: mediumMitigating Measure:Security/safety training for organization, students and school staff Protection Risk/Threat 3: Risk of long distance and young children walking alone. Children may get lost, be attacked or lose interest in learningLikelihood: mediumImpact: mediumMitigating Measure: Establish ECCE within the neighbourhoods whereby children will be dropped to the center by their parents. Protection Risk/Threat : Risk of children dropping out of formal education to attend SLP centres Likelihood: HighImpact: HighMitigating Measure: Identify specific criteria to all implementing partners for accepting children into the program Protection Risk/Threat :Protection risks of mixing different age groups in the same centre/classLikelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigating Measure: Create separate times of attendance for each age group. Protection Risk/Threat : Resistance to gender mainstreaming from male leadership structures.Likelihood: LowImpact: LowMitigating Measure:The project will benefit all members of the community and will always seek prior agreement and active involvement of the traditional leadership structure.Mitigating Measure: Programme activities which engage women will be endorsed by community and religious leaders to gain support from male community members to reduce the likelihood of resistance to these activities. Protection Risk/Threat : Increased risk as some locations do not accept SLP materials with MoE logos. This may pose danger to the children and the service delivererLikelihood: HighImpact: HighMitigating Measure: Identify locations that don’t accept SLP materials with MoE logos and print the ones without logos. Liaise with local partners and use their volunteers to deliver the services of SLP in HTR (Hard to Reach) areas.
