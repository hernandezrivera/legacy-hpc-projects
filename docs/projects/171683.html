Project Summary

The project will ensure equal access and needs based assistance to improve the quality of integrated services for populations affected by displacement, well-coordinated and integrated humanitarian intervention to 30,000 IDPs (5000 men, 15 000 women, 3000 boys and 7000 girls) to improve their well-being, their resilience and participation in decision-making processes. It will respond to emergency needs by empowering community and strengthening the capacities of local actors to have access to both essentials camp management service. Several strategies will be used including joint interventions with other clusters such as FSL, WASH, ES/NFI, and ensuring that our activities and the whole intervention complements other sectors and partners activities and not duplication. MRF using CCCM cluster strategy and initiative of cash for work will provide a cash-based intervention to enhance resilience building and copying mechanisms. MRF through a multi-sectoral approach will mainstream and integrate protection, health, WASH, Nutrition, Education, FSL in its intervention through intentional massaging, joint missions among others and will integrate MHPSS, PSEA, and GBV issues and ensure gender balanced teams. MRF organization has experience working in Garowe and Bosasso implementing CCCM and protection. If funded, the project will contribute significantly to service delivery in hard-to-reach location through organizing, carry out multi-sectoral needs assessments, create site level service map and protection of people with special needs in coordination with actors; and strengthening community structure by training community members on CCCM and humanitarian response.

Project Activities

1 – NeedsPopulations in Somalia continue to be affected by climatic shocks and insecurity, often leading to their displacement. Urban areas receive newly displaced people, who move into private IDP sites with poor living conditions and little access to basic services. IDP returns to home are extremely limited as many displaced families have lost livelihoods and are reliant on humanitarian services. People displaced to informal sites are living in precarious conditions and are not having their basic needs met due to inconsistent service provision or exclusion from accessing humanitarian support. There are over 2,400 recorded IDP sites across Somalia. An approximate, 85% of the sites are informal settlements on private land and about 74% of them are in urban areas according to the Detailed Site Assessment (DSA) of the CCCM Cluster. Existing coordination and management mechanisms are largely informal, with most sites being managed by landlords or gatekeepers. These mechanisms have limited accountability and little adherence to minimum standards. An estimated 2.6 million people are displaced in Somalia. The displaced population’s coping mechanisms have been affected over successive years of drought and conflict, leaving them vulnerable to shocks and inhibiting a sustainable return to places of origin. Of the total displaced population, 2,341,411 million people are living in IDP sites with the most severe needs. An estimated 351,211 of them are persons with various disabilities while 47% of the displaced are women. IDP sites lack dignified living conditions, sufficient access to basic services, land tenure and adequate resources to sustain their populations. Marginalized clans and vulnerable groups such as women and people with disabilities are reported to be particularly affected and at risk of discrimination. Persons with disability face several barriers such as separation from family members, loss of assistive devices, access to information and humanitarian assistance during displacement in the informal and unplanned settlements. 2 - Activities MRF will focus on the following cluster objectives and activities in the project target locations: CO1: Objective 1: Strengthen safe access to multi sectorial services at site level through improved site management and coordination. • Conduct site maintenance activities. • Carry out multi-sectoral needs assessments in the areas. • Conduct capacity building training targeting local community governance structures • Set-up or establish basic community engagement and inclusive governance structures. CO2: Objective 2: Improve living conditions of displaced people through site development, care and maintenance and decongestion initiatives . • Set up basic community engagement and inclusive governance structures. • Establish coordination mechanisms with all sector partners in the sites. CO3: Objective 3: Strengthen community self-management and access to information for displaced populations • Conduct community based care and maintenance in the sites. • Facilitate essential site maintenance activities. CO4: Objective 4: Support opportunities for displaced people to achieve durable solutions. •Conduct beneficiary satisfaction/intention/disability access surveys in the sites. •Set-up Community Based Complaint Mechanism (CBCM) for informal settlements •Carry out service monitoring exercises in the established sites.

Mitigation Measures

This project is conceptualized based on protection risk analysis and will mean-stream protection at every level. MRF is mainstreaming protection in all its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such as Persons with disabilities (PWDs), elderly, receive protection when they are providing information at any stage of the project cycle and that the whole process is confidential. MRF is a lead national NGO on domestic violence prevention and response included SGBV in Puntland and will employ a protection advisor for this project to work closely with established committees to guide the process with a serious protection lens. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. MRF with other partners have developed a referral pathway in Garowe, Gardo, Bandarbayle, Burtinle, Lascanod and Bosasso and will ensure that the meeting sites or locations are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Barriers to access particularly the meeting locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups (constituencies) before it will be agreed on where everyone is comfortable to access. Monitoring tools MRF will use have a section of monitoring safety of beneficiaries, and other barriers to access as protection is mean streamed in the tool and also have a section that asks questions as regards the use of the items support and whether the beneficiaries feels comfortable using them. The project activities will address inequalities and protection threats by analyzing power dynamics in the community. Local leaders will not be in-charge of determining who will benefit but beneficiaries with support from our staff. MRF’s program leaders had already taken PSEA course via online, nevertheless, MRF staff will also sign PSEA before engagement in the project. Our actions will address both basic needs that improve protection of those at risk of violations. MRF is also a very active member of Protection Cluster.

the project will employed area -based approach to restore the rights of affected populations and place them at the center of the process whereby the IDPs are in a position to make voluntary and informed decisions and IDPs are engaged and involved in processes that directly impact their well-being and participate in the planning and management of such processes and IDP's, have access to humanitarian and development actors and IDP's have effective monitoring mechanisms and as well as establish community led initiative like (sewing club, livelihood and income generation club).Transitional Durable solutions that enable IDPs to improve their protection environment and take steps that will lead to more sustainable solutions need to be taken into consideration and supported. Protection driven support to individuals, households, and communities could enable IDPs exercise their right to choose the best of available solutions for their displacement /. A area based approach to displacement seeks to restore the rights of affected populations and place them at the center of the process whereby, IDPs are in a position to make voluntary and informed decisions, IDPs are engaged and involved in processes that directly impact their well-being and participate in the planning and management of such processes, IDPs have access to humanitarian and development actors, IDPs have effective monitoring mechanisms. Protection assessments will be done for areas of concentrated movement, with active participation from the host community and including analysis of housing, land and property concerns, service mapping, and identification of conditions that would compromise progress toward achieving more sustainable solutions. Assessment will also include locations that are currently not conducive for solutions to monitor the situation, identify potential risk, and to collect information to share with IDPs. Protection risks along the potential routes of travel will also be taken into account.All interventions to assist IDPs in towards a durable solutions will uphold humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, neutrality, independence, and non-discrimination and will ensure that interventions are based on the voluntary and informed decisions and undertaken in a manner that upholds the safety and dignity of the population of concern. All assessment, analysis, and any intervention will be cognizant of the specific needs and vulnerabilities of particular individuals and communities.The length of displacement in Somalia continue to increase. The protection environment in areas where IDPs have fled remains fluid but has generally declined and there are no locations that provide absolute security. Throughout the displacements, some IDP have spontaneously returned or relocated to find better and safer solutions to their displacement. The capacity of IDPs to act on their intentions is varied with some requiring more assistance than others. IDPs have been able to pursue better solutions when they have the resources and conditions necessary to do so. Many IDPs find themselves in areas where their resources and coping mechanisms depleted, of which limit their ability to decide where they will reside and to act on their intentions.The project does not to any degree detract from duty that the Government has as the primary responsible party to protect IDPs and enable them to find solutions for displacement. All activities undertaken by this project will be coupled with robust advocacy to the Government to uphold their responsibilities does not supersede existing guidelines and established procedures for protection-mandated organizations to facilitate returns and relocations to address specific protection risks at the individual or household level.
