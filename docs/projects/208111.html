Project Summary

CARE International (CI) is a global confederation of fourteen independent non-profit and non-sectarian humanitarian organizations working together to fight poverty and aid in the face of humanitarian emergencies. Founded in 1945, CARE works collaboratively through its 14 members to end poverty. CARE has been working in Mozambique since 1984, delivering lifesaving assistance, building resilience and disaster management capacities, and empowering girls and women from vulnerable communities. CARE has offices in Maputo, Pemba, Nacala, and Maxixe, and works across the country through partnerships with civil society organizations.
Mozambique is Africa’s most vulnerable country to climate change, a country which previously recorded cyclones and other climate shocks over a period of years, changing weather cyclones have seen Mozambique exposed to weather events such as cyclones, tropical storms, floods, and drought on a yearly basis. Between 2019 and the first quarter of 2023, the south-eastern African country has registered 2 tropical storms and 5 cyclones, with the most recent being that of Cyclone Freddy – an exceptionally long-lived, powerful, and deadly storm that traversed the southern Indian Ocean for more than five weeks in February and March 2023. Freddy is both the longest-lasting and highest-ACE-producing tropical cyclone ever recorded worldwide, additionally it is the third-deadliest tropical cyclone in the Southern Hemisphere, only behind 2019’s Cyclone Idai which also hit Mozambique. One of the provinces affected by cyclones and climate shocks in recent years is the northernmost province – Cabo Delgado. In addition to the climate shocks, Cabo Delgado has been the backdrop of armed conflict since 2017, and as a major consequence, more than 1.5 million people have been displaced and a humanitarian crisis erupted. Loss of life, livelihood, patrimony in general and the eruption of all sorts of diseases coming from the consumption of untreated water and non-nutrition food became the reality of hundreds of thousands in Cabo Delgado, some districts of Nampula and Niassa.
As to expect in conflict contexts, women and girls were the most affected. From abandonment and inability to provide from themselves, all the way to aggravated risks of PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) and GBV (Gender Based Violence), girls and women have been made an even more vulnerable conflict due to both the armed conflict and the natural disasters striking Cabo Delgado in the last years. It is of utmost importance to keep working towards alleviating the suffering of the displaced communities, the host communities, and now, the returnees.

The aim of the PRCIC project is to prevent, mitigate and respond to imminent protection risks the populations of northern districts in Cabo Delgado go through in the aftermath of both conflict and natural disaster-oriented displacement and needs.

Focusing on vulnerable groups in the districts of Macomia, Mocimboa da Praia & Quissanga, PRCIC aims to alleviate the suffering of the targeted communities through a triple pillar approach on Protection

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

PRCIC is a one-year project, designed to alleviate the suffering of displaced, local and returned communities who survived natural disasters or the armed conflict in Cabo Delgado. This is a protection-oriented project, targeting 5 thousand people (vulnerable groups – women, girls, boys, men and people with disabilities), from the northern districts of Cabo Delgado, mainly – Macomia, Mocimboa da Praia & Quissanga.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Needs assessment to feed the project implementation; Training for community mobilizers and case workers; Recreative activities for WGFS; Case management; Dignity kit distribution; Community PSS activities; Life skills and capacity building/intensive courses on sewing, handcraft, etc. For girls, women, boys & men; Awareness raising activities in the community; Sensibilization activities around GBV and PSEA; Installation of an accountability committee as a referral pathway channel; All of these activities will happen simultaneously in Quissanga, Mocimboa da Praia and Macomia

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

An assessment will be made to identify potential risks and from there, a contingency plan will be properly tailored.For now, building a gender and age balanced and sensitive team, a diverse one culturally and religiously speaking, will ensure representativeness. Another aspect is the installment of Accountability Committees to allow the community to reach the implementing organization with feedback and complaints that will be followed up, diminishing the number of PSEA and VBG cases, hopefully.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

the present project, in its design phase, will ensure a gender balanced selection of participants and beneficiaries. through activities of Mental Health and economic empowerment, the project aims to emotionally rehabilitate the community, with a focus on girls and women (who are often responsible for the education and upbringing of an entire family, community, society, etc), and then put them through a series of trainings to maximize capacity building on life skills which can be mastered and then used as a source of income. so, besides teaching the community how to sew, cook, tailor, handcraft, plumb and much more, the project intends to, at a later stage of implementation, ensure partnership with different organizations in order to establish a channel through which the ''doers'' can have their products sold, either through monthly fairs, or through a proper market where their products will be sold at.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will count on its early assessment to understand what are the most imminent needs of people with disabilities, and what barriers they face, to then tailor its strategy to overcome said barriers. The base idea, is to ensure our WGFS are physically accessible to these vulnerable groups, and that the distributions (of items like dignity kits) do not pose a bigger challenge by, for instance, recurring to a door to door deliver to this specific group, if this makes the group more comfortable rather than excluded. the idea is to constantly consult the group itself to ensure a deliver they deserve.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The plan is to have an assessment on how the aid materials of previous projects have been used and how have they superficially impacted the community and its surroundings. This will not be a profound assessment on environment.
