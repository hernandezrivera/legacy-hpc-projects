Project Summary

UNICEF’s WASH humanitarian response will focus on the provision of and sustaining
access to lifesaving basic water sources and sanitation facilities and promoting personal
and environmental hygiene for 1,350,000 most vulnerable conflict, WASH related epidemics
and natural disaster affected non-refugees’ population and hosting communities in North, South, East, West and Central Darfur, South, West and North Kordofan, Blue Nile, White Nile, Sennar, Kassala,
Gedarif and Red Sea States and Abyei.

1 - Needs

As per Sudan 2020 HNO (draft) WASH sector estimates that about 6.5 million emergency affected people need improved water, sanitation and hygiene services. These people include IDPs, returnees, WASH related epidemics affected population and non-displaced, host communities who are vulnerable with limited access to improved WASH services. As per estimates by Joint Monitoring Report , 2019 about 40 per cent of the population do not have access to basic drinking water services, 63 per cent do not have access to basic sanitation, and 23 per cent do not have access to a handwashing facility with soap and water. Taken together just about a third have simultaneous access to water and sanitation in the country with wide disparities between states, between urban-rural and between the richest and poorest. Access to WASH in Institutions (schools and primary health centers) is also limited. fifty percent of the existing schools do not have improved sanitation facilities and a fifty five percent of them do not have access to clean water This is further compounded by the lack of gender segregated sanitation facilities including for menstrual hygiene management; and ease of access to the physically disabled children. This negatively impacts children’s attendance and enrollment, especially girls. The impacts of poor WASH are measurable through a multi-sector view. WASH has a direct impact on health and well-being, the poor WASH situation contribute to a very concerning public health situation, for example with GAM rate as high as 45 in Haya locality, Red sea state can be attributed to poor water access as 40 percent do not have access to improved water, 91 percent do not have access to improved sanitation and 99 percent of Household do not have a place for hand washing with soap, the locality also has a highest risk ranking when it comes to prevalence of AWD. After a year of civil unrest and despite people’s bartered political change, WASH needs continue to rise. The situation of people displaced by decades of conflict is unresolved. IDPs and returnees continue to need WASH assistance both in camps and communities. The key drivers are deepening economic crisis, following years of stagnation, and little investment in already-weak and ageing WASH services, poor knowledge, attitude and practices related to water, sanitation and hygiene (especially hygiene and sanitation), lack of community governance of WASH infrastructures in rural areas, huge disparities among rich and the poor, and climate change affect are some of the key reasons of growing WASH needs. There are about 1.87 million IDPs and 324,328 returnees out of which 1.7 million IDPs and 291,000 returnees are in dire need of WASH in the country which is about 26 percent of the total PIN. The remaining 5.5 million in need of WASH are non-displaced conflict affected communities, WASH related epidemics affected population as well people identified vulnerable elsewhere in the countrySouth, North, Central Darfur’s, North and South Kordofan’s, Red Sea, Kassala and Gedaref have very acute as well as chronic WASH needs (severe, very high, high and medium on severity scale). Nile and Sennar state show acute WASH needs (severe and very high on severity scale). Blue Nile and East Darfur shows very acute as well as chronic WASH needs (severe, high and medium on severity scale). West Darfur and West Kordofan shows acute and chronic WASH needs (very high, high and medium on a severity scale). Whereas White Nile and Al Gezira shows acute as well chronic WASH needs (Very high and high on severity scale). 1.59 M people spread in 25 localities are in severe needs of WASH, 1.27 M spread in 37 localities are in very high need of WASH, 2.12 in 39 localities are in high need of WASH, remaining 2. 6 million in 89 localities are in medium to low need of WASH assistance.

2 - Activities / Outputs

-Strengthening WASH Sector humanitarian coordination forum with focus on the coordination and harmonization of WASH sector planning, advocacy, implementation, monitoring and resource mobilization.-Activation and strengthening evidence-based M&E and IM systems with gender sensitive information using up to date, innovative, monitoring tools.-Institutions and human resources capacity building for sector partners with focus on managerial, technical, equipment, logistic and motivation capacities.-Construction/rehabilitation of gender and disabled people sensitive sustainable basic water sources with due consideration to the Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM) and climate change adaptation. -Enhance decision makers and communities’ awareness on IWRM and climate change adaptation and develop geohydrological IMS with focus on the groundwater vulnerable areas.-Construction/rehabilitation of schools and health/nutrition centres WASH services.-Operation and maintenance and water chlorination for the existing water supply sources, transportation means and HH level.-Empowering affected populations using Community Management for Operation and Maintenance (CMOM) approach with focus on women, girls and adolescents.-Construction/rehabilitation of gender and disabled people sensitive household and community sanitation facilities.-Community mobilization for household latrines construction using Community Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) approach within focus on women, girls and adolescents contribution.-Personal and environmental Hygiene promotion campaigns with household visits focusing on AWD prevention and handwashing with soap using up-to-date C4D approaches.
