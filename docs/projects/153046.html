Project Summary

The project will target 8,000 vulnerable IDPS, Returnees and Host communities in Koch, Leer, Mayendit and Panyijiar Counties with support to help rebuild the ability of communities to produce their own food and thereby safeguard their livelihoods. It is envisaged that for the project to achieve its overall goals,CH shall integrate and mainstream across the various activities elements of GBV, general protection,conflict transformation and WASH to have holistic results.Where necessary, CH will work in close collaboration with other partners where we do not have experience and expertise in achieving the overall goal of mainstreaming and integration to maximise on resources as well as benefits.The project is expected to address urgent short and medium term support to most vulnerable members of the communities in concentrated IDPS sites such as in islands and other hard to reach areas as well as providing basic support for them as some start to return slowly to their homes.
CH will provide agricultural inputs for vegetables and crop seed to the returning farmers. Given the potentially devastating risk of fall armyworm infestation, CH will support development an Early Warning System (EWS) to ensure farmers are able to prepare and deal with this risk appropriately. Alongside this, CH will also provide transitional livelihood kits in the form of fishing rods, fast maturing vegetable crop seeds, and other agricultural inputs to enable them farmers cultivate food for their families and some for sale to the market.
In order to ensure livestock diseases are managed effectively, CH will support livestock vaccination against disease prevention and treatment especially targeting Zoonotic diseases as Rift Valley Fever, Anthrax, Haemorrhagic septicaemia (HS), black quarter which are common in these areas. CH will also strengthen the provision of extension support to enable farmers adopt best practice in agricultural production and livestock management in order to increase their productivity. This with other tactics involve working with the farmer field schools to train farmers in each of the payams ensuring that they can optimise their farms under cultivation.

Needs

The conflict in South Sudan has had negative impacts on the entire population with particular adverse impact experienced by women and children. Since the beginning of the current conflict in 2013, millions have been displaced internally with hundreds of thousands (majority of them women and children) crossing the border as refugees. The three counties of Leer,Mayendit and Koch were the epicenter of this conflict. Panyijiar acted as based on IDPS stretching their coping mechanism on limited resources. On several occasional, different locations in the country have changed control between government an opposition forces. The rich oil deposits in the country make it an automatic region of interest for both sides of the conflict. Displacement in Leer,Mayendit and Koch has disrupted the livelihoods of many in those counties. Large areas of the state, specifically in Leer and Mayendit and Koch have experienced looting and forceful cattle rustling in the heat of war, leaving thousands of families without the only means of livelihoods they know – cattle. When this is combined with drought challenges, which are increasingly becoming commonplace, people must learn new ways of life and do it quick or else they will perpetually depend on external aid for their very survival. According to the recent IPC of food security findings in September 2018, majority of the population in Leer, Mayendit and Koch County lies in IPC 4-emergency phase. Of the 47,000 found in catastrophe phase or (IPC 5), 19,000, 10,000,10,000 population from the projected periods from September, December and January to March 2019 are in Leer, and Mayendit Counties. People are unable to farm because of insecurity and fear of being killed or kidnapped in their farms.These Counties did not get adequate harvests this year due to devastating conflict hence worse hit by food insecurity. According to Reach report in April to June 2018 approximately 90% of assessed settlements in Mayendit, Leer Counties reported inadequate access to food. The report further pointed out the populations in Leer and Mayendit Counties especially lacked sufficient livelihood activities to access adequate food in the absence of humanitarian assistance. The above findings were confirmed by an internal Coalition for Humanity assessment conducted in Leer and Mayendit County in September-October 2018, which found that 90% of the population in those counties has not been able to farm and therefore rely on food assistance. Most have fled their homes and currently living in IDPS settings especially in islands where they sheltered for safety. Those islands and other host communities have limited capacity to accommodate the huge IDPS numbers and this has overstretched their meagre resources and stretched their coping mechanism.In Leer County, IDPS are hosted in islands and hard to reach locations such as Tochriah, Meer, Kok,Adok ,Rubchai to the South and in the northern part of Leer include locations such as Paah,Leah, Luom, Padeah, Gandor and Guat. According to our findings, people in these locations have resorted to skipping meals due to shortages, with devastating effects on pregnant and lactating women and children under the age of five years that has resulted in malnutrition.With recently signed R-ARCSS and observed cessation of hostilities in effect, it is expected that local population will start moving from those hard to reach areas closer to their homes where they will start their lives completely from nothing. The project shall integrate and mainstream across the various activities elements of GBV, general protection and WASH to have holistic results.CH will work in close collaboration with other partners where we do not have experience and expertise in achieving the overall goal of mainstreaming and integration to maximise on resources as well as benefits. This project an opportunity to provide life-saving intervention to vulnerable members of the community to rebuild their lives.

Activities

• Cluster Objective 2: Food Production for the most Vulnerable o 3,000 farmers in Leer, 2,000 in Mayendit, 1,000 in Koch and 2,000 in Panyijiar (total of 8,000 farmers, 1333 HHs) supported with quality agricultural inputs (vegetables and crops seeds).o 50 farmers each in Leer, Mayendit, Koch and Panyijiar trained (TOT) on identification and early warning system of fall armyworm surveillance and reporting.o Train 200 Agricultural extension workers , 50 each per county on pest and crop disease control e.g. detection, prevention & eradicationo 100 farmers each in Leer, Mayendit, Koch and Panyijiar provided with farm level support on improved production/modern farming methods.o Provide 2,000 farmers with emergency livelihoods support such as fishing kits and fast maturing vegetable seeds (60% women and 40% men) in Leer, Mayendit, Koch and Panyijiar Counties.o Protection of livelihoods: providing famers on time with season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; provision of seed protection rations & seed multiplication sites; vegetable & crop kits; livestock vaccination & treatment.o Train or give capacity building for 400 farmers, 100 each per county on improved farming production through farmer field schools, demonstration plots, income generation (business skills).o Training community animal health workers (CAHW) on livestock diseases, prevention & treatment especially zoonotic e.g. RVF, Anthrax, Haemorrhagic septicaemia (HS), black quarter among others that are common in these four counties.• Cluster Objective 4: Dependency Reduction on Food Assistanceo Encourage and support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed from nearby counties; seed security & multiplication strengthening.o Support value chain addition e.g. through milk & fish preservation & marketing for people living along river Nile and other swampy areas.o Train livestock keepers on early warning systems and disaster risk Reduction through formed county committees.o Strengthen the role of Community peace committees for indigenous conflict mitigation institutions to promote peaceful co-existence among IDPS, returnees and host communities. to enable smooth farming.
