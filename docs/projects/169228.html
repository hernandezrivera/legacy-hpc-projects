Project Summary

In response to the Beirut Explosion that happened on August the 4th and based on a needs assessment, HelpAge International is proposing a project to reach out to older people and persons with disabilities affected by the explosion and support them to access services focusing on their protection needs. The project will target a total of 800 older people and persons with disabilities living in the affected areas through 1) identifying the most at risk and provide them with information on health and protection services, 2) raising their awareness on Covid-19 prevention measures, 3) referring them to health and protection services including support to transportation, 4) home visits by trained community volunteers for home-bound older people to support them in their daily living activities and provide them with psychosocial support, 5) provision of multipurpose cash assistance to most at-risk households with older people, 6) provision of psychological one-on-one counseling and psycho-social support through home visits by psychologists, and 7) operating an MHPSS hotline and referring to in-clinic consultations for older people and other household members in need for further intervention. The project will be linked up with other projects implemented by HelpAge under the Health and WASH sectors to complement the intervention and ensure a comprehensive response to the emerged needs.

1 - Needs

HelpAge international, Amel Association international, Dorcas, and Makassed foundation implemented a joint assessment targeting 802 households in the areas affected by the Beirut explosion on 10-11 August. Findings showed that 66% of the respondents were older people (50 years and above) and 33% of respondents were between 18-50, of which 68% were older person headed households and 32% were adult headed households. This reflects the high number of older people living in the affected areas, and the fact that many of them do not wish or are not able to leave their house.The main needs identified as a priority by the affected population were cash assistance (26%), food kits (13.71%), hygiene items (11.93%), and medications (12.65%). In this immensely challenging process of recovery and rehabilitation, there are protection risks and 26% of respondents felt that their mental health was a major health concern, especially for older people (25%) and among Syrian refugees (32%). There are also reported cases of the exploitation of older people by others posing as volunteers which raise an alert to the increased risk of abuse. Some older people have reported that they are unaware of any services provided by the humanitarian response or that they do not have access to the services.

2 - Activities

Through a community-based intervention, HelpAge International (HAI) will reach out to older people in the affected communities and support them in accessing the information and services they need. Community-based volunteers (2 volunteers, one male one female, per targeted neighborhoods) will be trained on protection of older people and identifying specific needs (including MHPSS needs) and cases of abuse, in order to refer older people to relevant activities or external services. Support for referrals (transportation costs) will be provided when needed. During home visits, volunteers will also share awareness-raising messages on Covid-19 to all older people visited. They will do home visits for homebound beneficiaries (support with activities of daily living as well as procuring food/medicine delivery, if needed), while those with specific needs will be referred externally to Humanity & Inclusion for physical rehabilitation and assistive devices.The most at-risk households with older people or people with disabilities will be recommended for cash assistance to enable access to goods and services, provided by the project in line with recommendations from the cash working-group.A psychologist from Amel Association will also provide home-based consultations following referrals by volunteers or the medical team. HAI’s partner IDRAAC will hold an MHPSS hotline and will provide phone and in-clinic consultations for older people and other household members in need referred by the project. All project staff and volunteers will be trained on PSEA, safeguarding and code of conduct, which will be signed by all staff and volunteers. A complaint and response mechanism will be set up to allow beneficiaries and other people living in target locations to raise any feedback or concerns on the project or related to safeguarding issues. The project will also link up with HelpAge’s projects under the WASH and the Health Sector:- under the Health Sector, partners AMEL and Makassed’s medical roving team, composed of one doctor and two nurses (one male one female), will visit older people who are not able to reach PHCs by themselves and provide nursing care and medical consultation, as well as refer them to other relevant medical services.- Under the WASH sector, hygiene kits will also be distributed to older people at risk who are receiving awareness sessions on Covid-19.
