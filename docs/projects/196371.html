Project Summary

Caritas employs robust protection and gender mainstreaming strategy and apply age/gender/diversity lenses to all aspects of implementation at all phases of the project cycle, including paying special attention to particularly vulnerable groups in this context. It utilizes the Protection Mainstreaming Framework and is committed to follow the guidance and practical tip-sheets and strategy developed by the WASH Sector by incorporating the principles of Safety and Dignity, Do No Harm, Meaningful Access, Accountability, Participation and Empowerment throughout the response. Under this project, CB will offer full WASH package to the people of concern in the targeted blocks that includes operation & maintenance of facilities, provision of water supply and hygiene promotion etc.
CB’s national policy for PSEA, is adapted and complaints will be collected through the several entry points and are directly processed by the dedicated PSEA focal point. Trainings on PSEA, Safeguarding and how to report is organize for volunteers and staff.
CB closely works with Government like- NGOAB, Deputy Commissioner, CiC and sectors for approvals and coordination. Regularly submits 5Ws and Sitrep to sectors and as active member it participates in Education Sector Coordination meetings Cox’s Bazar level and camp level CiC meetings and education sector meetings. CB Intends to participate and work with Water, Sanitation and Solid waste TiWG.
These challenges are due to a lack of strategic focus on operation and maintenance, quality, and durable solutions of all essential WASH Services, building resilience with a community-engaged approach. Therefore, CB intends to address the above challenges to keep operating Maintenance capacity ensuring facilities remain functional.
In a joint agency gender analysis on the response, children and adolescents also raised concerns about safety when accessing WASH facilities. Girls were prevented from using latrines that were being used by both men and women, due to the lack of segregation and privacy. It was alarming that a good number of HHs with persons with disabilities not reporting having used an improved water source. CB intends to ensure inclusive WASH service too.
The projects aim to mainstream protection for ensuring that beneficiaries safely access their services, without causing harm, and for promoting meaningful access, accountability, and participation of beneficiaries in the provision of WASH assistance and it will maximize the positive impacts of WASH programs on people’s safety and dignity and support affected populations access and enjoy their rights and strengthen participation and empowerment.
The program includes DRR components integrated into WASH activities that will have special attention for upholding environmental sustainability. CB will integrate site improvement and construction will be implemented for the improvement of the WASH facilities. The community people will be motivated to protect the soil erosion through the roadside plantation. The community people will be motivated not to do open defecation. They will also be made known and motivated not to cut trees indiscriminately and to plant trees at their homesteads. Besides. All DRR activities will employ an Environmental Stewardship tool which is being developed under the DRR Coordination group comprised of CRS, Caritas Australia, CAFOD and Trocaire.
Finally, the project will support social cohesion through the localization process, through developed proactive communication strategies, including challenging negative stereotyping within and between communities. Through community engagement across all WASH components activities, sports events, outings, and welcome events, as part of wider strategies to promote community cohesion and community engagement.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

As per the ISCG situation and WaSH Sector report of the Rohingya Refugee Crisis, 12% of FDMS and 26% of HC reported not having enough water to meet domestic needs on the Sanitation component only 8% of faecal sludge treatment plants meet standards while 7% of sanitation infrastructure require major maintenance and 51 FDMNs on average share bathing facilities due to lack of space. Waste: Only 51% of refugees are disposing of waste in the designated area resulting of 27% of households reporting garbage near their shelters. Hygiene: Only 66% of women and girls accessing MHM kit as per standards. Regarding out outbreaks, scabies and dengue have respectively been multiplied by three and by twelve in 2022 compared to 202112% of FDMS and 26% of HC reported not having enough water to meet domestic needs. Water quality in households will be prioritized through a water safety plan and ensure a safe water chain, provisions made for gender-specific safe WASH spaces and institutional needs. Along with the WASH sector strategy, CB will be aligned with an improved distribution network operating Water Networks, and boreholes and water facilities will be monitored periodically and keep a keen eye prioritizing environmentally sustainable and climate-resilient systems.Access to Sanitation. Latrine coverage at a camp level provides access at 1:20 in line with sphere standards, but there is a gap due to the high population density with a camp-wide estimated at 8,500 latrines. There is a 7% sanitation infrastructure gap and is a continual process for partners. Women and girls face issues of overcrowding and lack of privacy when using latrines and bathing spaces during the day, while at night they risk harassment and violence with 95% preferring private bathing spaces. There is a gap and need to improve latrines and bathing cubicles through upgrades and maintenance, enhance monitoring, ensure safety, and privacy and address specific needs for vulnerable communities, integrate handwashing facilities, improve fecal sludge manage effectively and efficiently. Solid Waste produce daily in the targeted area, SMW remains one of the main challenges as only 51% of the refugee households are disposing of waste in the designated area and 27% reported disposing of waste near to the shelters. So, strengthening solid waste management through community engagement is a priority at all levels. Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement. Hygiene knowledge and access to soap remain high although behavior change remains an issue that requires further research to assess the impact of knowledge on hygiene habits. Only 66% of Women and girls’ access MHM as per kit standards. Regarding outbreaks, cases of scabies and dengue have respectively been multiplied by three and by twelve in 2022 compared to 2021.Needs of host communities In August 2021 heavier than usual seasonal rains caused widespread flooding in the host community, resulting in a request for support from DPHE to assist in the assessment and the response. Approximately 23% of the HC population was affected by floods, affecting both water and sanitation facilities. So, for HC, access to quality WASH services will be ensured by aligning with National Strategies, and plans policies through solar power water distribution for water, Sanitation through Community Approaches such Community Led Sanitation Scaleup, SWM activities, and community engagement in schools, market, and household level with collaboration with Government of Bangladesh.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Caritas Bangladesh (CB) is one of the first responders and has been working in the camps following the Rohingya influx in Bangladesh since 2017. WASH services were integrated with Shelter program. CB has now one-year PPA partnership with UNHCR in WASH and has been implementing WASH program with own funds since 2018. CB regularly communicate and coordinate with camp level sector and CiC office to avoid duplication and settled the issue in mutual understanding and collaboration. CB has been the part of JRP for Shelter, Site Improvement & NFI, WASH, Protection, Child protection, GBV from the beginning and Education JRP for 2021 & 2022. CB regularly attends the Sector Coordination meeting, and TWiG meetings at Cox’s Bazar and camp level and also participated trainings, workshops and learning sharing meeting. CB is an active member of PSEA Network and have PSEA Focal, Safeguarding Focal within the organization. CB is the Focal of Protection, Child Protection, Shelter, WASH in a good number of camps and has wider experience of camp-level inter-sectoral coordination and networking & collaboration with CiC Office. In the mentioned camps CB has Child Protection, GBV, Shelter & Site Improvement and WASH programs besides Education. CB has been implementing activities with the support and approval from RRRC & CiC office and never found any objection so far from any Government authorities. CB has field office in Ukhia and is able to share resources across different programs. All of CB’s interventions and projects have been continuing since inception and expanding day by day. CB has now increased capacity to perform WASH activities and donor interest is increasing gradually in WASH. CB organizes partner meeting each year at Cox’s Bazar to raise the fund and get a commitment for funding for the well-being of Rohingya population and host community as well. This year CB expects multi-year funding from the donor community.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Caritas is concerned with unique and immense risks to the safety and dignity of Rohingya persons, especially those with an elevated vulnerability like women, girls, boys, persons with disabilities, elderly persons, and other diversified groups including LGBTQ, etc. WASH facilities are in a safe location, accessible for all, access roads of wash facilities, risky slopes and isolated areas, and Gender Segregation facilities. PSEA and Safeguarding are the major concern areas of humanitarian response. Solar streetlights to the access road of latrines will be ensured safety at night. A community-centred approach is followed to ensure participation, empowerment, and community strengthening that reduces risks and hazards to promote resilience. The MEAL structure and CFRM mechanism will maintain AAP and community responsiveness. Our Protection team will ensure Protection mainstreamed approach for integrated and inclusive programming

3.2 - Gender

CB adheres to a comprehensive Gender Policy in humanitarian responses and gender is mainstreamed in the project, participation of gender-diversified groups including LGBTQ decision-making processes, train volunteers, and gender-segregated WASH facilities including MHM situation will be improved by engaging the participation of all groups. Deploy female technicians and volunteers for girls-only sessions, especially for hygiene promotion and MHM sessions and adapt services culturally responsive and follow gender principles. All project staff will be trained and equipped on gender-sensitive programming. The program will also emphasize enhancing a women-friendly and gender-equal environment in the WASH programming with safety and dignity. Proper mitigation mechanisms will be established to address gender issues related to WASH and aligned with CFRM and MEAL structure. Organize community consultation to remove the cultural barriers and involve men to improve gender discrimination.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

CB follows a comprehensive Child and Vulnerable Adult Safeguarding Policy in this humanitarian context and has Safeguarding Committee and one dedicated Safeguarding Focal in all offices, including Cox’s Bazar office. Staff and volunteers have trained on Safeguarding, Code of Conduct, and PSEA orientation will be organized each year for all. They must sign safeguarding, Code of Conduct, and self-declaration forms during joining and reference checks before offering the job. Beneficiaries are oriented on safeguarding policy through providing orientation before any service delivery, or distribution. Agenda on safeguarding keeps in all meetings, training, and workshops. Bengali version of the Safeguarding policy and one pager distributed among the staff and host volunteers informed visitors, advisors and vendors and ensure sign the safeguarding form before visiting the camps. All the obligations are applicable to the staff, technical staff, and volunteers under this project.

3.4 - Diversity

Affected people, Children, adolescents, and youth both male and female including persons with disabilities and other diverse groups/LGBTQ, receive effective WaSH services accordingly. PWDs are systematically targeted, and their inclusivity is consistently mainstreamed in the program. There will be resolved by creating accessible facilities through handrails, special commodes, access roads, ramps and other assistive devices as required. Individual Plans will be taken for persons with disabilities for promoting inclusive WASH in the working area. 7.14% of people in Bangladesh have functional difficulties in different domains based on the Washington Group on Disability Statistics module. Referred other specialized org will be consulted to ensure their needs. The project ensured the inclusion of children, adolescents, and youths from a diverse group of population in the WASH services, will make sure that the facilities are accessible to all and include diverse groups.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The systemic MEAL mechanism ensures AAP principles, CFRM and stakeholder consultation, and periodic meetings with stakeholders are designed in the project towards improving the quality of WASH services. CB aims at capacitating staff and volunteers to facilitate cultural programming with a perspective of sustainability and potential replication and to promote the empowerment of the community. The project engages the community in all processes of project implementation. Involvement of community members in needs assessment through Participatory Action Research (PAR) methods. Sharing impact findings with beneficiaries, organizing periodical meetings and consultations, and taking decisions on the ways to ensure quality WASH services. Their feedback will be considered/addressed importantly. The proposed project keeps the door open to community participation, like coordinated and inclusive mechanisms during the COVID-19 lockdown.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Emergency preparedness and response activities are in place in the proposed project. CB will continue to provide services so that each WASH facility has DRR contingency plan and continue following the plan accordingly with community people. Capacity building of staff, Community Volunteers, user groups, WASH committees, and community stakeholders, and their active engagement in DRR programme management is the crucial part of the proposed project to prevent and mitigate the crisis. Their knowledge and skills development in the decision-making process and implementation ultimately help the community to be sustainable and resilient.
