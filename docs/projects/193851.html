Project Summary

Access challenges differ greatly across the oPt, primarily due to the range of political and administrative measures implemented by Israeli and Palestinian authorities. Access and movement restrictions are severe, widespread, costly and difficult to overcome. They increase the impoverishment and vulnerability of Palestinian women, girls, boys, and men within the oPt, and directly impede humanitarian/development operations. They make reaching vulnerable communities difficult and compromise the quality of programming and impact the effectiveness of humanitarian and development interventions. These challenges are particularly severe for local NGO personnel and for communities to reach services. The Access Coordination Unit (ACU) provides a more systematic approach to these challenges and improves access of humanitarian personnel and goods in Israel, Gaza and the WB. Overall, 56% of ACU's UN staff beneficiaries are female (3350 from 6000). Working over all access points, the ACU provides direct engagement and advocacy with relevant authorities as well as direct and timely support, information and advice to international organizations and their personnel. The ACU also facilitates the movement of Palestinian staff serving at duty stations around the world, supporting family reunification and career development of humanitarian personnel. The ACU formulates common policies with a coordinated approach to alleviate constraints to access and to negotiate changes to access policies and procedures with the relevant authorities including Israel, the PA, Gaza authorities, Egypt and Jordan. The ACU currently supports the work of 24 UN agencies, over 200 NGOs, and 65 diplomatic missions whose access to populations in need is impeded by a series of political, physical and bureaucratic restrictions on access and movement imposed by authorities. In addition, an estimated 2.1 M Palestinians in need of assistance throughout the WB and Gaza will indirectly benefit through the more efficient and timely delivery of humanitarian assistance. One of the primary services of the ACU is a 24/7 access hotline where staff can call the ACU to conduct real-time interventions with relevant authorities to resolve access incidents at checkpoints and crossings for both personnel and goods. The ACU also manages the ACULive mobile app which has 312 active users from 95 organizations, and which enables humanitarian and development personnel and agencies in the oPt to obtain timely and effective access information, guidance and support. The ACU also provides information and advice on procedures for obtaining visas, permits, project approvals and materials coordination as well as conditions and accessibility at checkpoints and crossings via the hotline and beneficiary-specific mailing list which includes 644 individuals working in UN, NGOs, and Diplomatic delegations. During emergencies and contingency situations, the ACU deploys to key field and coordination centres to conduct access and civil-military coordination with the relevant authorities. During the July-August 2014 escalation, the ACU facilitated evacuation of over 1,300 foreign and dual nationals from Gaza to Jordan as well as coordinating other humanitarian activities. ACU coordinated the exit of dual nationals from Gaza during the C19 restrictions, as well as the entry and exit of humanitarian personnel. The humanitarian locations notification system developed by the ACU was launched in 2019 and contains over 900 locations that are regularly shared with the Israeli authorities. During the May 2021 Gaza Escalation the ACU provided support to 1,185 UN, INGO, and diplomatic missions. Finally, information, analysis and messaging on restrictions to humanitarian space are made publicly available to support advocacy efforts undertaken by the humanitarian community and other stakeholders on access.

1 - Needs

Unhindered humanitarian access is the cornerstone to ensuring humanitarians - and others - have access to vulnerable populations including women, girls, boys, and men in need. This is required to implement activities and programmes which meet needs and reduce vulnerability. The 2023 HRP for oPt has identified 2.1 million people across the oPt as being in need of assistance (PIN) of which 61% are in Gaza and 39% in the WB. This includes 31,000 females aged 65+; 65,000 males 65+; 592,000 females 18-65;550,000 males 18-65; 458,000 females under 18; and 476,000 males under 18. Of the 2.1 million people in need, 1.8 million or 86% are severely impacted. As underlined in the 2023 HRP, the nature of these needs can be grouped into three categories: Those related to lack of protection and displacement (19% of PIN); limited access to essential services (64%); and erosion of resilience and preparedness (17%). Most of these needs require multi-sectorial and gender and age responsive approaches and most of the people in need have multiple needs across the different clusters which means that the total number of people in need by cluster will be higher than the total PIN for the HRP as a whole. The following are the total number of people requiring assistance under each Cluster: - Protection: 1.8 M; 64% Gaza and 36% WB - Health and Nutrition: 1.5 M; 67% Gaza and 33% WB - Food Security: 1.6 M; 73% Gaza and 27% WB - Education: 0.56 M; 74% Gaza and 26% WB - WASH: 1.36 M; 49% Gaza and 51% WB - Shelter and NFI: 0.94 M; 92% Gaza and 8% WB. The Access Coordination Unit will be supporting 24 UN agencies, over 200 international and national NGOs, and 65 diplomatic missions in accessing the 2.1 million people in need in both Gaza and the West Bank in order for them to implement projects across all six clusters listed above and to meet their needs as listed in the HRP. This will reflect positively on the ability of those organizations to respond to affected population’s needs and address vulnerabilities, including gender-specific vulnerabilities, with multisectoral approaches. The needs of these organisations vis-a-vis access are related to three main areas: 1. Direct support to access specific areas; 2. Information, data, and strategies to advocate for access; 3. Training and capacity building to improve access negotiation skills. In an environment already highly restrictive due to a range of political and administrative measures implemented by Israeli and Palestinian authorities, the COVID-19 pandemic has accentuated access needs in many ways. These measures have restricted access into the oPt and between Gaza and the West Bank, in addition to movement restrictions inside Gaza and the West Bank, particularly between East Jerusalem and the rest of the West Bank. The ACU negotiated and facilitated access for humanitarian agencies and personnel during lockdowns and other restrictions. It is expected that COVID-19 access restrictions will not continue into 2023. Closer coordination and cooperation by the ACU and OCHA in an evolving and more restrictive environment also in the West Bank is needed to ensure increasing humanitarian needs there continue to be addressed by all humanitarian partners, local and international. The ACU will continue to support the coordination of humanitarian personnel and goods as required, through its membership and contribution to the HCT, and the logistics cluster, as well as direct support to humanitarian partners. The planned merger of the ACU with the GRM Project Management Unit (PMU), will also be implemented during this reporting period. In 2023 particular attention will be paid to access of local NGOs to PIN and access of communities to services. The ACU will continue playing an important role in advocating for the right to humanitarian assistance and to access to health with the relevant authorities. The activities listed below will meet these needs and ultimately the overall objective of having access to PIN across the oPt in order to meet their humanitarian needs. Please note that in addition to these humanitarian and recovery needs, there are similar access requirements for development actors which are supported outside of this HRP project.

2 - Activities

- Management of a 24/7 access hotline where staff can call the ACU to conduct real-time interventions with relevant authorities to resolve access incidents at checkpoints and terminals for both personnel and goods. The ACU has a gender balanced team and makes sure that ACU female personnel can be assigned to provide assistance to female personnel when the need arises. - Provision of general information, advice, and support on the movement of personnel and goods. Information is collected in online and off-line databases and is provided through reports, the ACU website, ACU Twitter feed and a new ACULive mobile which enables humanitarian and development personnel and agencies in the oPt to obtain timely and effective access information, guidance and support. - Provision of information and advice via the hotline and beneficiary-specific mailing lists. This information includes procedures for obtaining visas, permits, project approvals and materials coordination as well as conditions and accessibility at checkpoints and terminals. - Provision of direct support to the UN, international and Palestinian NGOs, as well as diplomatic missions, to obtain visas, permits, special coordination and project approvals; Through a closer integration of the ACU into OCHA, in 2023 more support will be given to local NGOs which find accessing PIN particularly difficult and to communities to access humanitarian services. - Deployment to key field and coordination centres to conduct access and civil- military coordination with the relevant authorities. In preparation for such events the ACU directly trains COGAT staff and IDF humanitarian affairs officers and conducts preparatory exercises in civil-military coordination activities during emergencies. - The running of a humanitarian locations notification system containing over 900 humanitarian locations that are regularly shared with the Israeli authorities to ensure they are not targeted during escalations. - Provision of a range of access briefings and trainings to UN and INGO staff and to other interlocutors including personnel manning checkpoints controlled by Gaza de facto authorities to familiarize participants to the variety of access-related issues, humanitarian principles and UN operations. - Provision of trainings in humanitarian negotiation to support partner colleagues involved in negotiations with duty bearers. - Support to the HC/RC, UN Country Team (UNCT) and Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) with a mechanism for common policies and coordinated approaches for negotiating access with the related interlocutors, including during the COVID-19 response - if needed again in the future - and escalations of hostilities. This coordination role includes producing or assisting in the production of concept notes, leading, or participating in negotiations and coordinating amongst the relevant stakeholders. - Regular tracking of access conditions to identify the extent and impact of access constraints. The ACU then works with the HC/RC and affected agencies either bilaterally or through coordination forums such as the UNCT, HCT and AIDA (Association of International Development Agencies) - as well as COVID-19 coordination bodies such as the HC/RC led task team and coordination groups should there be a need again in 2023 - to determine priority issues for and ways of engagement. The ACU is well-placed to support a common approach that assists in ensuring that humanitarian movement is facilitated and opened-up as much and as soon as it is safe to do so, and the temporary restrictions do not become permanent. Challenges relate especially to movement within Gaza, between East Jerusalem and the rest of the West Bank, and in and out of Gaza. - Provision of access information and analysis for public reporting, internal working documents, policy papers, advocacy documents and talking points. Regular contributions are made to public UN reports, including the Ad Hoc Liaison Committee report, monthly Security Council briefings and the UNSCO socio-economic report. Contributions are made to OCHA reports, as well as humanitarian updates and other external reports produced by UN agencies, INGOs and diplomatic missions. This would include access briefings and field visits to diplomatic missions in addition to coordinating their access. - Provision of regular updates on access trends, developments, and ongoing engagements with authorities in regular reports to the HC/RC, including in relation to COVID-19 related restrictions. In addition to reports, provision of regular verbal briefings at different forums, including UNCT, SMT, HCT, OMT (Operations Management Team), AIDA, Security Cell and donor meetings in Jerusalem and their equivalents in Gaza.

3 - Indicators

The success of each activity and of the overall project will be measured through a series of performance indicators as follows: - Number of UN and NGO staff members (disaggregated by sex) receiving technical advice and support during access incidents via 24/7 hotline. Target: 150 per month - Number of UN and NGO staff (disaggregated by sex) receiving support in obtaining visas, permits, approvals and/or prior coordination. Target: 300 per month - Number of personnel (disaggregated by sex) receiving access briefs as part of their training. Target: 120 per quarter - Number of updates to UN and NGO personnel with information and analysis of access conditions, , through advisories, twitter feeds and email alerts, and ACULive mobile app notifications. Target: 15 per month - Number of negotiations on improving humanitarian access initiated and maintained. Target: 15 per quarter. - Number of internal policy reports including information on issues affecting humanitarian space, brought to the attention of HC/RC in a monthly report with recommendations for action. Target: 12 per annum. - Number of access related contribution to policy level public reports. Target: 12 per annum.

9.1a - Specify the mechanisms for feedback and/or complaints

a - Generic email,b - Project hotline,d - Satisfaction survey

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Regular feedback received through the 24/7 hotline and by email is acted upon immediately; for example, if an NGO staff member coordinated through the ACU to undertake a critical mission in Gaza is stopped and not allowed to proceed by the authorities, he/she can call the hotline immediately and action will be taken to contact the authorities and rectify the problem. If s/he does not make contact immediately but only after the incident occurs, an ACU official will still contact the authorities in Gaza to explain what happened, understand why it happened and ensure it does not happen again. The ACU would then call back the NGO staff member to explain what happened. If the feedback is received through a survey or an evaluation - usually conducted once a year - these would be analysed and corrective action taken as required. The action would then be communicated back to all the direct beneficiaries usually by email.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :
9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

The ACU's feedback mechanisms are designed for it's direct beneficiaries, i.e. the humanitarian community and not for population groups. this question is therefore not strictly speaking applicable to this project. However, the ACU ensures that all it's beneficiary groups, i.e. local and international NGOs, CBOs, UN agencies, Diplomatic Missions, and to some extent journalists, are targeted equally by reaching out to them through various means such as social media, emails, a website, an App, briefings at meetings such as AIDA and PNGO General meetings, the HCT, UNCT, UNGT, SMT, etc. the ACU also runs a 24/7 hotline so it can be reached by all groups equally.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project is designed to assist all humanitarian actors regardless of their nationality, ethnic group, religion, social status, gender, sexual orientation, or race in delivering humanitarian assistance to people in need in the oPt. The modality of ensuring that the direct beneficiaries - the PIN - are assisted in a safe and dignified manner in adherence to the Do-No-Harm approach and the Centrality of Protection principles is decided by the implementing agency. Being part of the cluster system and having a seat in the ICCG, the HCT, the UNCT and other important fora ensures proper monitoring of the activities of all the implementing partners and corrective action by the ACU if needed.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project is designed to assist all humanitarian actors regardless of their nationality, ethnic group, religion, social status, gender, sexual orientation, or race in delivering humanitarian assistance to people in need in the oPt. The modality of ensuring that the direct beneficiaries - the PIN - have equal and impartial access to assistance and services and vulnerable groups, including women and girls and PWDs are specifically targeted is decided by the implementing agency. Being part of the cluster system and having a seat in the ICCG, the HCT, the UNCT and other important fora ensures proper monitoring of the activities of all the implementing partners and corrective action by the ACU if needed.
