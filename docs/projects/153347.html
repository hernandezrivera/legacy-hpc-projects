Needs

Basic services including primary health care, reproductive health, referral of emergency cases immunization, prompt management of malnutrition, control of communicable diseases are still the highest priorities in poor vulnerable settings, camps, destroyed villages, informal settlements and high density of vulnerable populations. Although humanitarian actors were able to serve around half of the needs in 2018. The average amount of monthly health consultations in 2018 was 250,000 only by 158 functional health care centers in the post conflict governorate out of 447 pre-conflict PHCs. In 2014, Iraq health indicators were considered subordinate to neighboring countries, this situation was highly deteriorated during years of war, displacement and decrease in the living standards. Neonatal mortality rate 31.3, Infant mortality rate 38.5, and under-5 mortality rate was 21.7, the present situation is now more drastic, as per the detracted conditions especially in the Governorates that were subject to the conflict and in underserved rural regions. Diarrheal and acute respiratory infections, compounded by malnutrition account for two thirds of deaths among children under 5 years of age.By 2016, two years after the rise of ISIS, who imposed harsh version of Islam Iraq’s early marriage figure jumped from 15% to 24 %, 5 % who married before age 15. This contributed to negative consequences both for young girls and the whole society, with profound physical, intellectual, psychological and emotional impacts; cutting off educational and employment opportunities and chances of personal growth. In addition to negative consequences on their children, population pressure, health care costs and lost opportunities of human development are growing burdens that society shoulders because of teenage pregnancies. Endemic communicable diseases related to substandard environmental conditions in settlements, conflict affected governorates and underserved rural regions have increased impacting the health of children, pregnant lactating women, elderly and fragile. Leishmaniosis, cholera, diarrhea, upper respiratory infections, water borne diseases were reported on weekly bases, and as long as the environmental conditions are favorable with persisting lack of health services, these may sprout to an uncontrolled status, affecting the lives of children and poor households living in below standard conditions in tents. The severe weather fluctuation , poor housing, unsafe water, rodent population, insect infestations, and lack of nourishing foods majorly exacerbate the health conditions of the community. Although Ministry of Health figures indicate that 63% of pregnant women attended at least one ANC visit, three pilot studies conducted by ASB in 2018 shows that in North, South Ninawa and East Anbar, 32% of women had their first ANC visit at the time of delivery, 21% claimed that during their ANC visit no screening for anemia, hypertension/glucose level were performed, and 90% of the maternal medical files lacked health data. The same for postnatal coverage (41%). It is reported that 22.3% of births occur outside health institutions, with 22% of deliveries at high risk and in need of advanced medical support.The total unmet need for contraception is high. Family planning services are offered in less than 5% of primary health care centers and family commodities are rarely available except through private pharmacies at a high cost. Short birth spacing is associated with elevated risk of maternal death, increased neonatal, infant and under-five mortality, and child malnutrition. According to a study conducted in North and South Ninawa and Anbar, the reasons that women do not intend to use contraception include: lack of knowledge; ambivalence about future childbearing; and disapproval of family planning, fear of side-effects and low risk of conception, price of contraceptives and their unavailability.MICS carried out in 2012 showed acute malnutrition (wasting) at 7%, underweight at 8% and chronic malnutrition at 22%. A new screening by MSF covering 9 governorates in 2017, indicated that the prevalence of severe malnourishment among infants aged 1–5 months was 26%. Based on a screening study by ASB in one PHC during 2017 -2018, the prevalence of anaemia among women of reproductive age (15– 49 years) is 37.5%, and 43% among pregnant women.Primary health Care delivery services that survived the fighting are often poorly maintained and chronically short of supplies, medication, equipment, and qualified medical staff. This adds to the challenges of providing emergency care and limits the ability to provide essential needs among underserved communities. Access to essential health care services by IDPs residing in substandard condition remains an important pillar for lifesaving, increases productivity, decreases morbidity and related disabilities, decreases mortality in all age groups, decreases the health bills, and increases resilience.

Activities

The main objective of this project is to support sustain the continuity and provision of high quality primary health care, safe referral, nutritional, health awareness, maternal health, case management and mental health services for IDPs in Bersive 1, and insuring continues provision of PHC services to IDPs in Bersive 2, whereby the INGO Maltese will withdraw at the end of January 2019, Charmisko and Darkar . The total number of vulnerable that will benefit from the project is estimated as 55000 IDPS and 5000 of the most vulnerable nearby host communities. 1. Conduct RNA to understand their information needs and access to communication channels in the served communities and camps.2. Provide essential basic preventive and curative care and treatment of diseases to IDP camps and host communities in the supported health facilities.3. Support the health facility with technical, financial, staff and capacity building.4. Undergo community awareness and health promotion activities.5. Provide comprehensive reproductive health services for females in the reproductive age, ( 14- 45) pregnant females including ANC, PNC, provision of micro - nutrient, vaccination and health promotion, STI and family planning.6. Engage in immunization and vaccination activities with for children < 5 years, school children.7. Conduct Growth monitoring & Nutritional assessment to neonates ( 0- 1month), infants and children under five years.8. Provide nutritional treatment and supplements for children with malnutrition.9. Active Screening for non- communicable diseases and provide prompt management and refer complicated cases.10. Conduct training for health care professionals on disease management, infection control, use of medications, screening and reporting.13. Train and mobilize female community health workers.16. Conduct rapid assessment and response to communicable diseases outbreaks with Prompt reproting EWARN and management of detected diseases.17. Provide medical and curative services for patients including mental health (MHPSS) treatment and referral services. 21. Provide medical and psychological support to survivors of sexual violence22. Training health staff on sexual violence protocols for the clinical management of rape; supply drugs and materials. 25. Establish and support patient referral system for patients to PHCs and other specialized health services27. Provide and maintain the provision of safe water to the PHCCs. 28. Provide PHCCs with medical waste management

Mitigation Measures

1. Women face problems in the community due to family planning and reproductive health sessions:a) Prior understanding of roles and responsibilities in relation to gender in the community. b) Raise general awareness on the importance of reproductive health and family planning on women, child, and family health. c) Incorporate community figures and women associations in sexual and reproductive health training. 2. Most vulnerables ( widows, poor females, elderly, poor children, People with disabilities) are not able to access the services:a) Friendly construction of PHC and giving priority to disabled patients.b) Home visits for most vulnerablec) Transportation and referral system in place3. Community resistance to female work force:a) Prior understanding of roles and responsibilities in relation to gender in the community. b) Raise general awareness on the importance of the role of females in the health care field and the role of women in income generation. c) Inclusion of the community in implementing the training component4. Spread of health facility acquired infectiona) Advocate for personal hygiene.b) Ensure following the quality assurance standards by staff. c) Training staff on quality health care provision and prevention of nosocomial infections. 5. Abuse of vulnerable patients by PHC staffa) Daily/weekly follow up on PHC reports and incidents. b) Training staff on patient centered services and dealing with vulnerable populations. c) Ensure PHC staff following of accountability measures.d) Ensure the presence of hotline/phone number for feedback.6. Abuse of health care providers by the patientsa) Daily/weekly follow up on PHCC reports and incidents.b) Communicate with health care providers on their rights and responsibilities.c)Train health care providers on PSEA d) Set feedback mechanism7. Epidmices and disease out breaksa) Follow EWARN updates surveillance of disease prevalence at the PHCC levelb) Awareness campaigns c) Coordinate with different sectors to minimize risks8. Emergence of conflict between beneficiaries and health providers due to the difference in cultural and social perspectives/values. a) Recruit health care providers from the same area/community of the beneficiaries to ensure similar values and social perspectivesb) Conduct training for health care providers to further familiarize them with the cultural and social perspectives and views of the beneficiaries.9. Breach of privacy and confidentiality of beneficiaries. a) Mandate and train health care providers on the code of conduct

How is your project accountable to affected population

The project components are designed to fulfill the perceived needs of the beneficiaries by conducting need assessment in the areas of intervention. The services provided considered peoples interest, needs and requirements by their direct participation in prioritizing the services provided.ASB is applying variety of strategies to ensure accountability and open dialogue with beneficiaries, stakeholders and partners by:A rapid need assessment will be conducted in the intiation phase.CwC procedures and actvities will be followed and documented on routine bases.Monthly meetings and FGD with Community leaders, Camp management, partner and ASB staff facilitate a dialogue between ASB and its affected and served populations.Feedback and complaints mechanisms have been developed to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints.A hotline will be provided for beneficiaries to call ASB call center and in active coordination with IDP hotline.A complain box will be available in each PHCC, and will be promoted for beneficiaries feedback.Frequent and contineous monitorng visits and reports by ASB staff will be assessed on timely basesExit satisfaction survey as on-going activity to monitor quality and relevance of services provided.Beneficary complain logbook will be present and timely reveiwed in each camp.The project exit strategy is well planned and will be followed: The exit strategy will end by handing over the PHCCs to the Directorate of Health in Duhok, through schedualed steps: capacity building of the PHCCs and the local health authorities.The project will be phased out into ongoing DOH programs, progressively transferring responsibility to DOH, PHC program. The scale of operations will be reduced progressively ensuring equality in treatment for all beneficiaries. At the end stage the delivery of residual health services will be provided on the basis of need, and avoid leaving vacuums. The strategy is to prevent any gap of services at the final phase when the DOH will take over, the ASB will be following the timetable set for this strategy.On completion of the project, materials and equipment will be handed over as a donation to the DOH;• Equipment and materials purchased with project funds will be properly documented and properly transferred to DOH.
