Project Summary

The overall goal of this project is to target 16,667 direct and indirect beneficiaries including (4,650 Girls and 4,650 Boys) Children, adolescent children and (3,650 men and 3,717 Women) in Ayod 4,400, Canal/Pigi 4,400 Counties in Jonglei State and Longochuk 3,467 and Maiwut 4,400 in Upper Nile State, aimed to ensure the provision of critical protection-related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas and prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience caused by the flood and conflict, through provision of timely comprehensive case management and referrals including CBI, Mobile and static protection monitoring and assessment, Psychosocial Support Services, protection awareness-raising and prevention messaging and CBP preparedness and protection risk mitigation.
UNCDR, with community participation, will involve the beneficiaries, local authorities, CBCPN and other stakeholders in project implementation and capacities building on the three approaches that will use to achieve more results as the Current flood, conflicts and COVID-19 significantly impact the children’s psycho-social well-being and development, which exposes them to violence, loss of lives, or separation from, family members and friends, deterioration in living conditions, inability to provide for one’s self and family, and lack of access to services that can all have immediate and long-term consequences on children, families, and communities and impair their ability to function and be fulfilled.
UNCDR will provide crucial psychosocial support for children during emergency situations to help them overcome such difficult experiences. These efforts include culturally and age-appropriate, safe and stimulating activities such as games and sports to develop life skills, coping mechanisms, and support resiliency.
UNCDR will also use extensive comprehensive case management forms to document all cases in accordance to the CP principles into the database and report it to the relevant services, this will be done in conjunction with the community-based child protection committees or Networks (CBCPC/N, which will be recruited and trained to provide quality case management and proper coordination of the FTR in the local context. The project will also strengthen the ability of community members to support and provide comprehensive quality of services to the most vulnerable children, UASC, communities and families with their neighbors with keys messages on how to cope with this situations through a variety of channels including the existing community structures, youth centers and use of child friendly spaces (CFS), organize activities to the children and adolescent youths in a safe and stimulating environment where affected communities and children can be supported, provide specialized referral pathways to access the services base on the need, or who may need extra support, so that appropriate networks of care may be engaged.
UNCDR will recruit and train the CP staff on the child protection concerns in order to speed the implementation to provide quality activities and services to support the, identification, registration, documentation of the Unaccompanied and separated children in the selected counties and conduct assessment, planning, implementation follow up and reporting in accordance with the IASC, SOPs and ISPs to improve FTR and case management in line with minimum standards of Child protection in emergencies services.

1 - Needs

Reference to 2022 HNO 2.9 million are children and adolescents, including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse and exploitation and need critical child protection services in 65 counties, and high in severity in nine counties. An estimated 20% increase in the number of children in need of immediate child protection services compared to 2.3 million children in 2021. According to the CPIMS+, the number of documented child protection cases including UASC increased from 26,009 (12,128 girls and 13,881 boys) in 2020 to 30,441 (14,185 girls and 16,256 boys) by the end of 2021, with Unity, Jonglei, Upper Nile and Lakes states having the highest number of documented child protection cases, including UASC, child neglect, child labour, domestic violence and child trafficking1. This situation has been exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic, protracted displacement, and limited access to basic services which continues to significantly affect the physical, mental, and social well-being of children, adolescents and caregivers(2022-HNO). A recent 2021 FSNMS+ survey results show that 3.7% of interviewed households indicated that their children were missing or not living in the household at the time of the assessment and are at risk of abuse, neglect,, and exploitation. The areas affected include; Ayod, Canal/Pigi counties of Jonglei; Longochuk, and Maiwut of Upper Nile. Children and adolescents also continue to face a multitude of risks, including grave violations, abduction, GBV and other forms of violence, recruitment into armed groups and separation from families. Economic pressures from COVID-19 have forced some families to rely on negative coping mechanisms, including child labor and early marriage. Decades of insecurity and upheaval have damaged traditional social structures and weakened justice mechanisms, leaving children more vulnerable to protection risks. The prolonged conflict in the listed counties of Ayod, Canal in Jonglei and Longochuk and Maiwut Counties in Upper Nile where it has had a catastrophic effect on the population especially children being the most affected due to increased exposures to protection risks which include separation from caregivers, widespread of GBV concerns, loss of immediate caregivers, forced recruitment and marriage, massive destruction of schools and TLS, abuse, exploitation of the vulnerable children among other protection concerns. UNCDR intends to continue addressing protection risks issues through a response strategy which includes: Provision of comprehensive case management and referrals including CBI to children under support with material and other medical concerns. UNCDR will increase its Static and Mobile respond to reach hard to reach areas with protection monitoring and assessment, strengthening the Psycho-social Support Services and Protection awareness-raising and prevention messaging to the affected communities within the selected counties, strengthening CBCPC/N, increase capacity building program to staff and communities to support the children during the exit of the organization, to continue improving the well-being of the children that have gone through difficulty and in the community’s centers in order to complement the current multi-sectoral interventions with mainstream CP and GBV into Education, WASH, Nutrition, Health and FSL Referral pathways will also be established to strengthen and support implementation of case management and link children to other services and address the gaps in coordination within the targeted counties were UNCDR operates.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

UNCDR mobile protection team will use different methodologies of collecting feedback from beneficiaries. We pay more focus on the most vulnerable individuals’ women, men, boys, girls including elderly person and people living with disabilities, our routine protection monitoring and house-to-house visit has been an effective way of getting feedback from people who cannot easily access the complaint desk. UNCDR also has been using FGD and KII to get feedback from vulnerable individuals or groups and address their needs accordingly.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The community feedback mechanism will be in place and accessible to beneficiaries. In the previous projects, UNCDR had established four CBPNs centers in Ayod in Jonglei state as well as Maiwut in Upper Nile State where the centers were used as protection desk to attend to any complaint or other protection concern, the same approach will be used in this project to ensure that community feedback is collected and addressed amicably. At the beginning of the project, UNCDR will carry out rapid protection needs assessments, including KII, and FGD with beneficiaries and key stakeholders to have a better understanding of the context, and this will inform the basis of our intervention. UNCDR will introduce a monthly stakeholders meeting as part of our effort to ensure that feedback from the affected community is put into consideration for effective response.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

UNCDR will ensure clear communication with beneficiaries include the provision of the hotline for reporting the cases of sexual exploitation and abuse committed against beneficiaries and will put in place a high degree of confidentiality so that informants are protected against any retaliation from the perpetrators. Same will be made clear to UNCDR staff, volunteers and constructors that any attempt / sexual exploitation against beneficiaries including failure to report can attract administrative measures including dismissal when the investigators will have reasonable ground to believe that staff member has committed PSEA or was aware when another colleague members committed and he/she failed to report it. It will be communicated to the beneficiaries as well that any attempt or exchange of core relief items and money for sexual favor constitutes sexual exploitation and misuse of power, therefore, should be reported immediately for further investigation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

UNCDR will immediately look into the feedback from beneficiaries and will consider corrective measures accordingly. Beneficiaries' participation in all stages of the project cycles is a crucial part of project implementation including the design of the project. UNCDR will ensure the project assistance genuinely reflect the true needs of the beneficiaries and they will be involved in all stages of the project cycles including post-distribution monitoring and evaluations.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Safety and dignity and avoid causing harm: UNCDR will ensure that services are designed to prevent as much as possible any unintended negative effects which can increase children’s vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks. In particular, all staff of UNCDR and community volunteers will sign and adhere to UNCDR’s Child Protection Policy to protect children from any eventual abuse and advocate for viable solutions. Meaningful Access: UNCDR will ensure that all children including vulnerable children in the community have access to CP services in the CFS or direct support without any discrimination. In particular, children living with disabilities, children living with HIV/AIDS, child-headed households, child mothers, children demobilized from armed forces/groups, SGBV survivors, etc. will be accorded with necessary assistance and services. Equal participation and empowerment: Adolescent girls and boys (peer educators) will be trained on life skills education to protect themselves and also help support other small children. Adolescents and child rights clubs will be formed in schools to help children develop self-protection capacities to enable them to claim their rights, including rights to education, health, sanitation, Food/NFIs, shelter, etc. The team will conduct throughout an in-depth protection analysis using the protection risk equation, and will utilize the use of the cluster assessment template and the report will be shared with the national cluster and Sub-Cluster coordination team. UNCDR will insure do no harm’ approach is observe for the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries as it is the core of the UNCDR Policy, the protection team will assess the distribution sites and will advise the project team on adjustments if needed. Traveling routes and time to the distribution sites will be taken into consideration as well.
