1 - Needs

Since 2011, waves of conflict, political and economic instability in Libya caused severe deterioration in the standard of living on the community and individual levels and the escalation of this conflict since April 2019 has worsened these conditions. Damage to housing and infrastructure has been observed across Libya, mostly on the coastal areas including Sirt, Benghazi, Misrata and Tripoli. According to the 2019 Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA), over 296,938 people in Libya are presently in need of shelter and NFI (SNFI) support. Approximately 127,909 migrants and refugees need shelter and non-food items support. Lack of appropriate shelter and NFIs including clothing, bedding, and hygiene items negatively affects all population groups (Libyan and non-Libyan) regardless of their age (children, young adults, vulnerable adults and elderly) and gender (men, women, boys and girls) worsening their wellbeing, with negative impacts on health, protection and access to basic services. atThe dire economic situation, limited access to livelihood opportunities coupled with the inflation and limitations on cash withdrawals increases the difficulty of making rent payments and can consequently increase the risk of eviction. Shelter represents 50% of domestic expenditures for most households. The Libyan population faces a combination of liquidity constraints and a shortage of housing, which has led to very high rental costs. Those living in public and informal settings, experience lack of access to basic services, poor water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) conditions, overcrowding, and a lack of privacy. Informal settlements are often places of physical insecurity due to crime and other forms of violence. In return areas, thousands of people without adequate shelter are living in damaged and/or unfinished buildings and houses, or in collective centres. These living conditions make people more vulnerable and increases the need for appropriate shelter responses. The shelter situation for migrants and refugees is also very challenging considering the transitory and often undocumented nature of their stay in Libya combined with various forms of protection concerns and lack of livelihood opportunities. The majority of the non-Libyan population cannot afford dignified and secure shelters, often sharing living spaces with others in overcrowded conditions without access to basic services including hygiene and sanitation services.According to DTM round 27, 60% of all IDPs identified in Libya were reported to be residing in privately rented accommodation, while 23% were staying with host families without paying rent, and 6% are taking shelter in schools and other public buildings. The majority of displaced households were identified in different neighborhoods of Tripoli, the Nafusa Mountains and various locations along the coastal line of Western Libya. While the main frontlines are situated in the greater Tripoli region, the consequences of the conflict are felt heavily in the Southern protracted areas. For IDPs residing in informal settlements, essentially in urban areas, humanitarian assistance continues to be central to maintain a basic standard of living. Among the migrant and refugee populations, approximately 5,000 individuals remain in detention centres across Libya.

2 - Activities

This project will include need-based provision of emergency shelter and non-food items to most vulnerable Libyan and non-Libyan population including IDPs, returnees, host communities and migrants in detention centers and urban setting. IOM will collaborate with government, S/NFI sector members as well as partners under the Rapid Response Mechanisms in the provision of life-saving assistance. This project aims to support a total of 30,079 people - 4,300 migrants and 25,779 IDPs and IDP returnees urgently requiring life-saving shelter and NFI supplies. The total requested amount to cover the needs of all 30,079 beneficiaries split between two proposals is 2,855,200 USD – 40% under RMM and 60% under this proposal.18,079 will be supported under this proposal, while the remaining 12,000 of the caseload will be supported through the Rapid Response Mechanism under the joint inter-agency project.

4 - Protection risk analysis

During assistance to both Libyans and non-Libyans, the process of need identification, prioritization and distribution of SNFI in the context of emergencies could potentially create security risks, including the risk of aid diversion and the potential for violence. When commodities or assistance are in short supply, tensions can run high during delivery and distributions. Vulnerable individuals and groups (Libyan and non-Libyan) may be unable to obtain their entitlement or may have it taken from them by force. SNFI assistance that targets only one group could also pose a serious risk including discrimination and retaliation. IOM will be assessing in advance and adopt mitigation measures, in terms of selection criteria and to minimize the risks. This will involve verification of requests through the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), adequate supervision of distributions and careful selection of distribution points as well as sensitization and consultation of the communities to prevent tension among different groups.

5 - Mitigation measures

The project will put in place mitigation measures to prevent, monitor and respond to safety and protection risks including gender-based violence or sexual exploitation associated with distribution of Shelter and NFIs. This will include avoiding distribution points located nearby the presence of large numbers of men and consider scheduling distribution times for women or men only to minimize exposing women and girls to sexual harassment within distribution areas. Physical security risk due to conflict activity and access barriers for certain groups including women and girls will be considered. Moreover, IOM follow a zero tolerance policy for sexual exploitation and other forms of abuse of power by aid workers or those collaborating in provision of SNFI (including community volunteers). IOM will also make use of the existing coordination mechanisms including the sector coordination and Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) for integrated response and maximize reach in cases where the required needs are too high. Safe and confidential complaints and response mechanisms would also be in place to respond to any reported incidents of intimidation, violence and sexual exploitation (that have been committed by community members and/or staff). Gender balance of assessment and distribution teams for both sectors will be considered to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point in the distribution areas.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

Consultations will be made with community representatives and local partners to identify the critical needs and needs of vulnerable groups. Proper identification of needs will be conducted through IOM’s DTM prior to planning any distributions. Consultation with communities and local authorities will be conducted to collectively agree on places and time of distribution to ensure safety, security and accessibility. Safe and confidential complaints and response mechanisms would also be put in place to respond to any reported incidents of intimidation, violence and sexual exploitation. IOM will also conduct post distribution monitoring to gauge beneficiary satisfaction, appropriateness and usefulness of supplies provided.
