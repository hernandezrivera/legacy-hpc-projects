Project Summary

Under the FATA transition plan, SIF aims at providing immediate food security support, revival of agriculture system and livelihoods support to 28,350 returnees in Upper Orakzai and South Waziristan tribal districts who have lost these resources during years of displacement. SIF aims at ensuring sustainable return of the population of tribal districts by restoring their agriculture and livelihoods sources and assets through proposed project. SIF will ensure that:
Targeted women, men, girls and boys have better access to lifesaving relief food assistance to address their immediate hunger needs and nutritional requirements through:
a. 200 families have received in kind/ cash grants on restoring 50 agriculture assets including rehabilitation of pavements, irrigation channels and other identified schemes
b. 200 farmers have access to agricultural inputs and started crops production.
c. 600 women headed, orphans, elders, persons with disabilities and extremely vulnerable households have started vegetable production at household level through kitchen garden support.
d. 600 women headed, orphans, elders, persons with disabilities and extremely vulnerable households have restocked chicken birds for food production.
e. 10,000 fruit/ indigenous trees are planted through conditional cash grants to strengthen agroforestry and reduce the impact of wind storms.
Targeted vulnerable households have restored and recovered critical livelihood assets and capacity building trainings for:
f. In kind/ cash grants to 3,000 most vulnerable families for restoring and recovering critical livelihood assets.
g. 50 youth have restored and recovered their livelihoods by establishing small enterprises.
h. Capacity building of field/ extension officers of agriculture and livestock departments to ensure continuity of services and sustainability.
SIF will recruit its local qualified staff for the implementation of proposed project. SIF will establish two field offices for both tribal districts to ensure close field supervision, monitoring and effective project management. SIF will strengthen community based organizations to ensure their contribution in each proposed activity. Female participation will be also ensured by strengthening female community based organizations. SIF will ensure close coordination with all relevant stakeholders to avoid duplication of resources and build synergies with other partners. The project exit strategy is closely linked with FATA transition plan where rehabilitation support will be provided by building the local capacities and sustaining project interventions.

Needs

The food security & livelihoods assessment conducted by Food Security Cluster in 2017, and local dynamics study and multisector assessment conducted in March and July 2018 by SIF team respectively testified the hardships of the returned families. During the needs assessment, 7 villages of returning families were assessed. To date, 35,823 verified families have returned to the Orakzai tribal district. Around 29,000 families are yet to return in the different areas of tribal districts in 2019. Despite official return, the families are still unable to return due to the lack of basic services in the area, such as: 1. The Coping Strategy Index results revealed that retuned families are adopting negative coping strategies for survival to manage their food needs. 88% of assessed households are relying on less preferred and less expensive food; 73% households purchase food on credit. 68% of households borrow food or rely on help from friends/ relatives; and 62% of households send household members to eat elsewhere. 2. Though majority of the households still own agriculture land but they cannot use it due to lack of financial and technical resources, inputs and land degradation. At the time of assessment, 100% of assessed farmers in Upper Orakzai were unable to grow agriculture land due to lack of basic resources. 3. Before displacements agriculture and livestock breeding were main income generation sources in tribal districts. After displacement and return phase, 59% persons are found engaged in income generation works e.g. labor work, small entrepreneurship; remaining 41% are not doing any income generation work. Livestock lost during displacement has negatively impacted on households’ income. 94% families do not have large ruminants and 65% families do not own small ruminants. Similarly, 33% families also do not own poultry. The significant reduction has occurred to the livestock population in the region as significant number of livestock was sold during displacement on extremely low price to the vendors; and some of livestock was lost due to death, injuries or sickness, which is also impacting on the household income. Through the proposed project, SIF aims at improving access to food, agriculture and livelihoods for the returned population of Upper Orakzai and South Waziristan. SIF has been working in Orakzai district since November 2017 providing agriculture and livestock support to 118 and 209 vulnerable families respectively; improved livelihood of 21 male, female entrepreneurs, plants nursery establishment, and rehabilitation of 35 WaSH schemes. SIF has support 2,160 families in 11 villages of Upper Orakzai, Ismailzai and Mamouzai through its multi-sectoral support to Temporarily Displaced Persons (TDP) project. These interventions are contributing in ensuring sustainable return of the vulnerable population and they have started getting benefit from such services by improving their access to improved livelihood, agriculture, livestock support and WaSH services.Moreover, SIF is planning to launch its Integrated Emergency Assistance (IDEA) project to support 1,060 vulnerable families in Upper Orakzai from January 2019 until August 2019. The project aims at reviving agriculture land to improve food security and livelihood conditions. SIF also aims at improving access to WASH communal and domestic facilities and increased hygiene awareness. The SIF funded project will complement the proposed project and expand its response coverage to serve the communities at larger level. SIF aims at expanding its geographical coverage to other tribal districts as well including South Waziristan. SIF aims at providing emergency WaSH services to the returnees of South Waziristan tribal district, in Sararogha sub district, in addition to Orakzai. The area is selected based on recent return taking place there and recommendations received from provincial authorities to expand SIF operations in other tribal districts as well.

Activities

The proposed project’s objective is to improve better access to food resources, revive agriculture and livelihoods for 28,350 returned persons in Orakzai and South Waziristan Tribal Districts. Following activities will be carried out to meet the set objective:1. SIF aims at rehabilitating the degraded agriculture land for 200 vulnerable farmers in both tribal districts so that these families are able to cultivate crops and seasonal vegetable to meet their livelihood and food needs. Local farmers who do not have financial capacity to rehabilitate their cultivable land will be targeted for this activity. Community members and agriculture department will be involved in the land rehabilitation process to learn best practices. Farmers will be provided agriculture packages having appropriate equipment and seeds. Technical backstopping will be provided through SIF technical staff and agriculture department. Female participation will be encouraged but culturally appropriate practices will be adopted.2. In kind/ cash grants on restoring agriculture infrastructure will be provided to 200 families in both tribal districts. These will include rehabilitation of 50 irrigation channels, pavement schemes. Skilled and unskilled labor will be identified and engaged into this work. The agriculture infrastructure schemes will be identified in close coordination with local community, farmers and district agriculture departments. Cluster guidelines will be adopted while conducting the proposed activity. Duplication will be avoided by establishing strong coordination with relevant stakeholders.3. Kitchen gardening support will be provided to 600 women/ child headed and extremely vulnerable families in both tribal districts to improve access to food and meet nutritional requirements. Such families will provide land for establishing kitchen gardens. Kitchen gardening package will include provision of tool kits, seeds and technical training to grow kitchen gardens. Agriculture department will be taken on board to contribute in providing technical support.4. Poultry restocking support will be provided to 600 women/ child headed families in both tribal districts to improve access to food and also meet nutritional requirements. The package will include 10 chicken birds, hen house and technical training on disease identification, prevention and medication. Livestock department will be taken on board to provide technical assistance and medication before and during disease outbreak.5. Plantation of 10,000 fruit/ indigenous trees will be done in both targeted districts to ensure agroforestry and raise awareness on risks associated with deforestation. It will reduce soil erosion, increase soil fertilization and support in disaster prevention e.g. wind storms, drought. Plantation will be done at household and communal levels through conditional cash grants. 6. In kind/ cash grants will be provided to 3,000 most vulnerable women headed, elders and families having persons with disabilities for restoring and recovering critical livelihood assets. These grants will also strengthen the local market and economy. Cluster guidelines will be adopted while implementing the proposed intervention. Duplication will be avoided by establishing strong coordination with relevant stakeholders.7. Entrepreneurship, vocational and skills development trainings will be delivered to 50 vulnerable families for restoring/ recovering livelihood. SIF will identify and select both male and female beneficiaries and train them as per trades prioritized at local level. Cash grants will be provided to trained beneficiaries to establish small entities for generating livelihoods. 8. Capacities of local agriculture and livestock department will be developed to ensure their technical support and engagement with communities in a longer and sustainable manner. Their capacities will be developed to supplement FATA transition plan for ensuring timely and quality services delivery.
