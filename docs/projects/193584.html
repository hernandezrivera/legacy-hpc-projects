Project Summary

The project addresses main problem "rise in GBV, discrimination & harmful practices against vulnerable women 18-59 and>59years/ girls 14-17years including with disabilities & cancer patients affected by blockade, conflict-related violence, violence in HHs, protracted crisis in Gaza & their limited access to safe/dignified multi-sectoral GBV services." It addresses protection/GBV risks, access barriers and needs of women/girls identified in needs assessment (NA)/ gender analysis/GBV risk analysis conducted on 11-16/10/2022. Findings of NA included exposure of women/girls to physical, psychological, social, economic & sexual violence, early marriage; patriarchal culture and gender-biased attitudes; legal illiteracy; women’s reproductive role in HHs; lack of livelihoods/ income; deprived access to basic needs; exclusion of WWDs/GWDs and cancer patients; lack of women’s awareness on their rights, GBV services and PSEA; and their limited access to safe GBV services. Its objective is "Acutely vulnerable women>18-59 and >59 years/ girls 14-17 years including with disabilities GBV survivors, have meaningful access to safe, dignified, gender responsive& inclusive multi-sectoral services based on GBV Minimum Standards” and outcomes are: 1. Acutely vulnerable women/girls GBV survivors manifested psychological well-being, good health, met basic needs, safety/dignity, knowledge, changed attitudes/behaviors toward reporting GBV, resilience & positive coping; 2. Front-line staff of GBV actors are better able to detect, refer/provide safe/ dignified multi-sectoral services; and 3. Local/national duty bearers sensitized and act to protect acutely vulnerable women/girls GBV survivors via community engagement & advocacy. The project targets directly 9810 beneficiaries (5790 women, 640 men, 3220 girls, 160 boys, out of them 489 PwDs) identified based on vulnerability criteria: with disabilities, cancer, elderly, IDPs, refugees, pregnant women, FHHs, GBV survivors & poverty according to their location, status, protection risks & needs severity. Beneficiaries are selected from Jabalya (Tal Za'tar/Twam) and Beit Lahya (A'tatra/Salatin) by ACHA; Gaza City (Shijaia, Sh-Radwan) & Nuseirat camp by WAC; Kh-Younis-Mawasi& Qarara; Rafah-Mawasi by CFTA & ARAs coordinated with GBV SWG. The project links to MSNA/HNO 2022, HRP SO1, PC obj 3 eligible activities/indicators. Partners consolidate their experience in geographical& thematic areas and coordinate with CBOs to address women/girls GBV survivors needs. They implement activities: A) Provide PSS counseling via partners’ helplines to 2700 women/660 girls & individual PSS to 780 women/180 girls & group PSS to 180 women/45 girls; therapy to 150 couples; specialized MH to 48 women/12 girls; recreational days to 420 women/180 girls; legal counseling to 150 women/30 girls; dignity kits to 360 women; legal representation to 60 women/15 girls; unconditional protection cash assistance to 300 women/30 girls as a complimentary measure to holistic GBV services tailored on specific needs of each women/girl to produce a protection outcome by preventing, reducing or mitigating their exposure to protection risks, reducing vulnerabilities& enhancing capacities; economic enablement to 39 women; case management (MHPSS, legal aid, health care to pregnant women/ cancer patients/WWDs, economic support, referral to shelter) to 81 women/9 girls; 1 GBV service directory; PSEA awareness sessions to 160 women/60 girls/160 men/60 boys; B) self-care & PSEA training for 54 partners staff & 120 frontline staff of specialized/non-specialized GBV actors & community members; C) community engagement& advocacy: 1 radio ad, 6 press releases, 9 community meetings, pamphlet on PSEA, 1 fact sheet, 3 position papers, PSEA complaints integrated into overall FCM, integrate PSEA into contracting; advocacy initiative. Partners ensure right-based & survivor-centered approaches, GBV Minimum Standards, SADDD, GBV risk mitigation to uphold do no harm

1 - Needs

Partners conducted needs assessment (NA) on 11-16/10/2022 including gender analysis (GEM A) to gather gender-related Sex/Age/Disability Disaggregated Data (SADDD) (GEM B) especially gender relations, roles and responsibilities, productive/ reproductive work, access to & control over resources, decision making at household and community, issues/barriers, social norms, protection and GBV risks/ needs for women 18-59 and>59 years and girls 14-17 years GBV survivors WWDs/GWDs; cancer survivors; IDPs; refugees; pregnant/lactating women; FHHs: widows, divorced, bereaved, separated), men 18-59 and>59 years and boys 14-17 years including PwDs. The NA /gender analysis covers Jabalya (Tal Za'tar, Twam) & Beit Lahya (A'tatra, Salatin); Gaza City (Shijaia, Sheikh Radwan); north Nuseirat camp; KhanYounis- Mawasi & Qarara; Rafah Mawasi & ARAs. It employed secondary data collection (review of MSNA, HNO, UNWOMEN) and primary data collection methods including: phone calls to 35 women/15 girls of them 11 WWDs/5 GWDs, and 4 women cancer survivors and interviews with 15 female/male frontline staff(25-55) years of GBV specialized/non-specialized actors, community members 35-65 years & suppliers of goods/services 30-60 years. Women 18-59 and>59 years/ girls 14-17 years reflected: 1) long blockade, GMR & escalations in Gaza as their homes and livelihoods were damaged/destroyed, internally displaced, family members are injured/lost/permanent disability, bereaved/widow women, orphaned children, restricted movement and access to ARAs; 2) strong patriarchal culture and gender-biased social attitudes; 3) exposure to physical, psychological, social, economic & sexual violence; 4) early marriage; 5) deprived access to basic needs; 6) legal illiteracy on Palestinian Laws &legal procedures in courts; 7) limited access to inclusive multisectoral GBV services; 8) limited access to, control over resources (land, income/employment, livelihoods, grants, credits, information) to meet their practical and strategic needs; 9) responsibility for household chores (child care, cooking, cleaning, washing), caring for elderly; 10) engagement in reproductive work in household more than productive work that generates income & community work; 11) inadequate women’s involvement in decision-making at household level (having children, children education, domestic food purchases) and community organizations; 12) limited participation and influence in decision making during project management; and 13) WWDs/GWDs face double discrimination for being women and with disabilities, and women/girls cancer survivors are excluded in their communities. Women 18-59 and>59 years and girls 14-17 years GBV survivors including with disabilities and cancer survivors need awareness raising on their rights, GBV, PSEA, complaint system, protection mechanisms; information on PSEA & GBV service directory on available GBV services; access to MHPSS (helpline to provide PSS counseling to Leave No Behind in remote communities, individual/ group PSS, couple therapy, recreational days & specialized MH); legal counselling & court representation; dignity kits to provide basic items to interact comfortably in public and maintain personal menstrual hygiene toward preserving dignity/ protection and coping in current stressful humanitariansituation; economic enablement; unconditional protection cash assistance to prevent, reduce, or mitigate exposure to protection risks, or limit the effects of violations; case management (MHPSS, legal aid, SRH, cancer treatment/rehabilitation, disability health care) and referral to shelter. Men 18-59 and>59 years and boys 14-17 years need awareness on PSEA, women’s rights, GBV, protection mechanisms. Partners staff 25-55 years explained that they were affected by working with GBV survivors which can be traumatic and can result in vicarious/ secondary trauma. They expressed that they need training on self-care to manage stress, monthly supervision & PSS support and training on PSEA and integrating PSEA in procurement/recruitment/ contracting procedures. Front line staff of specialized/non-specialized GBV service providers need training on protection mainstreaming, PSEA, complaint mechanism, risk mitigation/response. Community members need training on GBV prevention, risk mitigation/ response. Suppliers of goods/services need training on PSEA. MSNA (2021) reflects that 40% of HHs reported at least one member of their household experience psychosocial distress of trauma. 17.8% of HHs reported that a member of their household had experienced difficulties in accessing one or more services due to mental or physical difficulty. 86.1% of HHs reporting that women of reproductive age (15-49 years) had no access barriers to specialized reproductive health services. 11.4% of HHs reporting areas in their location where women/girls felt unsafe. 56% and 12% of HHs employing crisis and emergency livelihood coping strategies. HNO (2022) indicates that 37.5% of women 18-64 years experienced violence. Of those, 56.6% experienced psychological violence, 17.8% physical violence, 8.8% sexual violence, 32.5% social violence, 41.1% economic violence, 8% cyber violence & 24% early marriage. 19% and 17% of households are concerned about safety/ security of girls & women respectively. 8,250 persons were internally displaced need legal aid for GBV survivors and widowed women relating to denial of resources, custody of children and loss of identity documents; girls<18 yeas who are still displaced need PSS; 58% of WWDs lack information on GBV services; socio-cultural patriarchal norms; and limited access to multi-sectoral GBV services. UN WOMEN (2022) states that most common types of violence against women observed in Gaza include domestic violence, sexual harassment, early marriage and femicide. Outdated/discriminatory laws hinder GBV survivors from accessing gender-responsive services and obtaining justice.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Partners apply a functional, accessible and inclusive feedback/complaints mechanism (FCM) as a critical component of humanitarian accountability and community engagement. FCM is designed to allow affected populations including with disabilities and cancer patients to exercise their right to share their views/ perceptions/ thoughts (positive or negative) using different channels (generic email, hotline, complaint box, satisfaction survey, field visit, FGDs) during needs assessment, project design, implementation (delivering assistance), M & E and collected information is reviewed, analyzed and findings, conclusions and recommendations to advocate for change and capitalize on generated knowledge to inform decision making on taking corrective actions and improve programs making changes that communities see that are needed. Partners will present feedback information in the meetings of protection cluster, sub working groups (GBV, MHPSS) and LTF, which enables them to capitalize on shared experience and bring proposals to improve referral services and future actions.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

-Partners conducted needs assessment/ gender analysis including analysis of protection context, identifying and mitigating risks, particularly GBV, to women/girls’ safety and well-being that the project might inadvertently exacerbate. -Partners respect beneficiary informed consent, confidentiality and privacy in all forms of consultation, counseling and personal information sharing.-Partners train their staff, front-line staff of specialized and non-specialized GBV actors, CBOs, and suppliers of goods/services on PSEA to avoid exposing target women/girls to further harm and to enhance their safety/dignity. -Partners increase awareness of beneficiaries on PSEA via pamphlets and awareness sessions.- Partners undertake a built-in clear process for receipt and referral of PSEA complaints integrated into overall feedback and complaint mechanism.-Partners integrate PSEA in their procurement, recruitment and contracting procedures.-Partners ensure that the project will not be used to fuel further conflict, or to disadvantage a particular social group or to lead to increased human rights violations.- The location of partners’ facilities and the routes to them are safe and distant fromthe risk or threat of GBV. - Partners have infrastructure adaptations including fitting ramps and railings to facilities and provide WWDs/GWDs with assistive mobile devices and hearing devices so that they can access and use facilities and services in safety and with dignity. - Protection mitigation measures for protection cash, such as: i) Tailored women visits to ensure that cash assistance does not cause harm; ii) Financial coaching for women; iii) A communication channel in case harm resulted from the cash intervention.

-Partners ensure that all beneficiaries disaggregated by gender (women, girls, men, boys)، age & disability can equitably access project GBV services.-Partners provide a blend of in-person and remote (helpline) activities to overcome any barriers that beneficiaries may encounter. -Partners provide GBV services at an adequate time when all targeted women 18-59 and 59 years/ girls 14-17 years can realistically access them. -Partners facilitate access to GBV services for persons with reduced mobility (e.g., persons withphysical disabilities, the elderly, bed-ridden individuals) via making access paths smooth and fit ramps for wheelchair access. -Partners facilitate access to GBV services for persons with non-mobility related disabilities (e.g., the blind, hard of hearing, intellectually disabled) via providing information about services in both verbal and written forms and developing pictorial representations and explanations- Partners raise awareness of direct beneficiaries about their rights and GBV services and service providers via disseminating service directory. -Partners monitor access and discrimination through disaggregated indicators (# of individuals accessing services disaggregated by age, gender, and disability).-Partners use their helplines to facilitate access of women and girls in remote and inaccessible communities (e.g. ARAs) to GBV services towards “LEAVING NO ONE BEHIND”. -Partners involve women CBOs and local communities to keep up implementation of project activities during all times including times of crisis. -Partners’ access approach is consistent with humanitarian principles: 1) Humanity in addressing human suffering of vulnerable women/girls; 2) Neutrality so that humanitarian aid must not favor any political side; 3) Impartiality in providing services to women/ girls on the basis of need & without discrimination; 4) Independence in advancing autonomy of humanitarian project objectives from political, economic or other objectives.

EXIT STRATEGY & SUSTAINABILITY (WAC inserts this item here because there is no specific item in HRP application, while it is required in first round comments)Partners developed a participatory exit strategy to ensure the sustainability of the project. Sustainability factors are: ₋ Partners will build up the capacities of women CBOs to be service providers/information/ referral points in targeted locations to improve access of women/girls GBV survivors to protection services and ensure broader geographical coverage and continuation of protection services beyond the end of funding. ₋ Partners have competent and high skilled staff who will be able to continue providing similar services and benefits to beneficiaries beyond the end of funding.₋ Partners will engage men and boys in GBV prevention and response to promote positive masculinity. ₋ Partners will ensure effective participation of stakeholders (particularly target groups: women and girls GBV survivors, front line staff of protection specialized/non-specialized actors) from the outset to ensure their ownership of the project objectives and achievements. ₋ Partners will ensure consistency of the project activities with traditions and norms of local communities across Gaza Strip (ARAs). ₋ The budget of the project activities is adequate for achieving the planned outcome. ₋ The cost effectiveness (value for money) of provided services is maximized. ₋ The institutionalization of project objectives and activities within partners' core programmes and organizational structures and staff capabilities and competencies enable partners to continue delivering the services and benefits to target groups after the project end. - WAC has high capacity in writing proposals and raising funds for future interventions. ₋ Partners mainstream crosscutting issues like: 1) gender equality through applying gender analysis of aspects and priorities related to women, girls, boys and men, demonstrated by SADDD, tailored activities to address barriers and GBV risks, adequate Influence and participation of beneficiaries, access to feedback/complaint mechanism, transparency/access to information, benefits and satisfaction of beneficiaries; 2) environmental protection via using MIS to greatly reduce use of papers; and use of ICT/digital technology tools for communication like internet, skype, social media and zoom/team meetings to minimize use of traditional transportation and travel; collecting and disposing medical waste in safe boxes to central incinerators at Shifa hospital; conscious use of resources like scarce water and energy produced by using fossil fuel; using solar based energy to cover current deficit between electricity supply and demand; reducing generation of wastewater and solid waste; and focus on circular and green economy and climate resilient activities in supporting micro income generating businesses; and 3) disability and elderly inclusion via providing information on services, transportation cost and facilitating access to adapted facilities. ₋ Appropriate risk assessment, monitoring and mitigation measures to ensure the achievement of project results. ₋ Partners have friendly spaces where they provide women and girls with protection services, while ACHA has primary health clinic, mental health clinic, mobile clinic, laboratory medical testing, imaging center, and pharmacy.
