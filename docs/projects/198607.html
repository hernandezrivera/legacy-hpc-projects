Project Summary

This project is in line with WHO's strategic shift to the Global Programme of Work (GPW-13) objectives of triple billion target. In order to achieve UHC, timely respond to health emergencies, and promote healthier populations, WHO will adopt an integrated strategy that maximizes interdependencies between each of the three strategic priorities. In order to promote value for money and sustainability, this project will support an integrated approach to increasing the capacity of the States and partners (International and National NGOs) to guarantee continuity of essential health and nutrition services. By Building local capacity and building a strong referral mechanism and boosting the health system's ability to handle life-saving interventions and needs. WHO will progressively integrate health service delivery platforms into stronger local health systems by setting up a referral mechanism and increasing the capacity of the health system to cope with life-saving interventions and needs through the following activities:
• Conduct a training for trainers on the in-patient management of SAM with medical complications in the BAY states in line with the 2021 updated protocol of WHO for a pool of master trainers. In order to ensure that the knowledge and abilities gained during the training of trainers are applied to ensure the delivery of high-quality nutrition services, WHO will work with the State Ministries of Health on a regular basis to provide joint supportive supervision.
• Advocate for an integrated approach to SAM management as a required component of all maternal, new-born, and child health (MNCH) services.
• Increase government capability for monthly supportive supervision, monitoring and on-the- job coaching of nutrition services. The joint supportive supervision will focus on in-patient services. The supervisors/coaches will spend two to three days at the facility providing support through the administration of a standard checklist and conducting feedback sessions with the facility staff. To ensure that children are developing on schedule, support the systematic implementation of the Nurturing Care Framework in healthcare facilities and give people in areas with access to comprehensive and highly effective health
• Support the systematic implementation of the Nurturing Care Framework in health facilities to ensure that children are developmentally on track
• Provide access to comprehensive and high-impact health and nutrition services for people in areas with low/no health service coverage in the BAY states through the H2R mobile health teams(linked to functional PHCs) and CORPs/CHIPS delivery platforms. Under this approach, WHO will deliver extended basic and essential health packages in areas with low/no access. This will be through systematic screening for malnutrition and referral of all identified cases to treatment centers while those at risk will be counseled through infant and young child feeding (IYCF) in addition to treatment for minor illnesses, vaccination, vitamin A supplementation and deworming of children. Nutrition screening is done by MUAC (mid-upper arm circumference) measurement using a simple coloured plastic strip, a quick and simple way to determine whether or not a child is malnourished. MUAC is suitable to use on children from the age of 12 months up to 59 months.
• Enhance nutrition surveillance, prevention and control across newly accessible areas and the conflict-affected LGAs in the BAY states. This will be through the expansion of the WHO Early Warning Alert and Response System (EWARS). EWARS deployment entails the restoration and stimulation of existing disease surveillance through a network of focal points and key informants to collect weekly report on a subset of 17 key diseases, including measles, malaria, cholera and SAM.

1 - Needs

The ongoing conflict in northeast Nigeria has triggered a large-scale humanitarian crisis over the past 12 years in the 3 BAY states of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe, displacing millions of civilians, and resulting in disruption in provisions of essential services. With the associated potentiating effect of flooding, diseases outbreaks (Measles, Cholera and AWD), camps closure, inflation and reduced of access to health care, the crisis has been worsened significantly in 2022. According to the nutrition sector, the nutrition situation in has worsened in the lean season of 2022 compared to the same period last year. In some of the LGAs, Gwoza, Bama, MMC, Jere, the situation is described as critical with acute needs also identified in the health sectors.The BAY states have 4.2 million people in need of nutrition services (PLW and U5 Children) including 2.9 million children under the age of five who require life-saving nutrition services, according to the 2023HRP. The nutrition sector is targeting 2.4 million for life saving interventions. The most vulnerable people are women and children who live in the Hard-to-Reach areas because they have not had access to nutrition services. When compared to children who are well-nourished, children who are severely malnourished have a 12 times higher risk of dying. In comparison to exclusively breastfed infants under 6 months, non-breastfed infants have a 14 times higher risk of dying from pneumonia or diarrhea. Findings from Round 12 of the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance provide evidence that Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence among children under 5 years is still of public health significance in Borno and Yobe while in Adamawa there has not been any significant improvement in the last 6 years. Though the situation has improved in Yobe (10.6%) compared to (14.3%) during the lean season of 2021, the situation in Borno and Adamawa has not improved with numbers higher than the pre-emergency period of 2016. Out of the 63 LGAs included in the latest IPC AMN analysis (October,2022), between May and September 2022, 13 were classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical), 17 in Phase 3 (Serious), 30 in Phase 2 (Alert) and 3 in Phase 1 (Acceptable). This shows that nearly half (48%) of the LGAs included in the analysis were either in Phase 3 or 4. A reduction in partners’ presence in all key Local Government Areas (LGAs) is part of the consequence despite increased risk of Climate change (floodings and draught), communicable diseases, GBV, mental health and SAM (Severe Acute Malnutrition) amidst disruption of health services and damaged health facilities. The closure of IDP (Internally Displaced Person) camps without a secured return to IDPs (Internal Displaced Persons)’ settlement areas of origin is likely to put further stress on host communities with unofficial IDP camps, especially in Maiduguri Metropolis and LGA (Local Government Areas) satellite towns. This can fuel ongoing needs.

2 - Activities

Through the following activities, WHO will gradually integrate health service delivery platforms into stronger local health systems by establishing a referral mechanism and increasing the health system's capacity to deal with life-saving interventions and needs: •Support and strengthen the capacity of staff at the stabilization centres in effectively treating severely acute malnourished children with medical complications. This will be through the training of healthcare workers on in-patient management of SAM using 2021 WHO global guideline. Healthcare workers from Government and partners providing nutritional services will be selected from Stabilization centers across project locations. •Provide essential medicines and supplies to the stabilization centres for the treatment of severely acute malnourished children with medical complications through procurement and distribution of WHO 2020 PAED-SAM KITS. A single kit is expected to support the treatment of 50 complicated cases through availability of life-saving drugs.•Conduct joint-supportive supervision visits and on-job coaching& mentoring sessions for SC Staff: This activity would be conducted by WHO and MoH technical staff to project locations on a bi-weekly basis, this would entail monitoring project outputs, providing supportive supervision and on-the-job coaching.• Encourage the use of an integrated approach to SAM management as a minimum package of maternal, newborn, and child health (MNCH) services. • Provide access to comprehensive and high-impact health and nutrition services for people in areas with low/no health service coverage in the BAY states through the H2R mobile health teams and CORPs delivery platforms. Under this strategy, WHO will provide extended basic and essential health care packages in areas with limited or no access. This will be accomplished through sessions of systematic screening for malnutrition and referral of all identified cases to treatment centers, while those at risk will be counseled through infant and young child feeding (IYCF) in addition to minor illness treatment, vaccination, vitamin A supplementation, and child deworming.Through these interventions, 69,859 SAM children and 186,524 will be reached.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The beneficiaries through their care givers(Mothers) will be enlightened to gain an understanding of PSEA including the reporting mechanisms.The project is designed to prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of accessing health interventions. In addition, WHO treatment standards will be followed to ensure interventions achieve the desired effect and not cause harm. There will be no discrimination and anyone within the targeted areas can access the services. Patients seeking treatment will be treated with respect and dignity. Also, training on GBV and PSEA policy/standards of conduct will be integrated into all WHO trainings sessions.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

WHO’s Community health champions and Community Resource Persons(CORPs) will be engaged to continuously interact with the community members to inform them about the projects and services obtainable at the SCs, they will also support community mobilization as well as nutrition screening and referrals. Since the CORPs reside 24/7 at the communities, they will also update us on the feedback on the quality of service delivery. These will be analyzed with the MoH focal persons during the monthly CORPS supervisors review meetings. And feedback shared with the community leaders.Also the Ureport system already establishedin several locations will be utilized for feedback and complaints. The complaint desk of WHO through its dedicated toll-free lines that is stationed at the public health EOC will also closely monitor the activity by closely analysis the complaints sent and it will be followed by visit to the field to verify and provide feedback to the community.This project will ensure that beneficiaries are not harmed.WHO will periodically solicit opinions and thoughts of beneficiaries (including of the vulnerable groups and those with special needs- including people living with disability) through community engagement sessions. Feedbacks from the community will serve to guide the project implementation. • Through established network of LGA facilitators, the beneficiaries will be able to give feedback on the quality of service delivered and how well the activities have addressed their needs. This feedback will be used to modify services and activities implementation to the vulnerable population.
