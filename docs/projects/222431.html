Project Summary

Through this project WV will provide emergency life-saving primary healthcare interventions through a combination of static and mobile health facilities at the IDP camps and affected communities in the target locations. WV will work in collaboration with the State MoH, Health Facility and other health actors' staff and community health volunteers to provide quality health services. This project will strengthen technical capacity of health workers in disease surveillance, control, management, and community sensitization.
This project aims to address emerging humanitarian needs and health gaps that have persisted despite existing health projects in Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, and Western Equatoria States (WES) not fully meeting the requirements. World Vision International – South Sudan (WVI-SS) will collaborate with key partners such as the World Health Organization (WHO), UNICEF, and UNFPA to ensure the secure procurement and delivery of essential medicines and reproductive health commodities to health facilities, maintaining an efficient cold chain system. The project will provide static primary healthcare services and mobile outreach programs offering critical health interventions including the diagnosis and management of common illnesses, normal deliveries, family planning services, routine immunizations, and health education campaigns.
Aligned with the 2025 Health Cluster Objective 1, the project will focus on enhancing staff capacity in disease surveillance, Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM), Basic and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BeMONC/CeMONC), cholera case management, and infection prevention and control. Leveraging existing structures such as Boma Health Workers, community nutrition volunteers, mother-to-mother support groups, and champion groups, WVI-SS will deliver health education and promote disease prevention among Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and host communities.
Furthermore, the project will ensure integration of health response with Protection, Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL), Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH), and nutrition interventions by providing access to sanitation and water supplies at selected healthcare units and mobile clinics. Cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) identified at health facilities and mobile clinics will be treated or referred to appropriate nutrition programs, with complicated SAM cases directed to stabilization centers supported by WVI-SS. The project will procure medicines and essential equipment to bolster the health response and mitigate service delivery gaps.
Additionally, in alignment with the 2024 Health Cluster Objective 2, WVI-SS will conduct reactive measles campaigns and establish community feedback mechanisms. Under the 2025 Health Cluster Objective 3, WVI-SS will facilitate quarterly review meetings with humanitarian organizations and the community to address pressing issues affecting the target locations. This project aims to complement existing funding from sources like the Health Pooled Fund (HPF), Global Affairs Canada, UNICEF, Bureau of Humanitarian Assistance (BHA), WVUS, and WVI-SS Ireland, focusing on health system strengthening with an emphasis on maternal and child health and nutrition in the specified locations.

1. Needs

In 2025, the humanitarian situation in South Sudan continues to worsen due to a combination of factors like political conflicts, intercommunal disputes, and climate change-induced natural disasters such as floods and droughts. Approximately 4.3 million children under the age of five, as well as pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, are in urgent need of life-saving interventions and preventive healthcare. This includes 0.65 million children facing severe acute malnutrition, 1.4 million at risk of moderate acute malnutrition, and 1.1 million mothers requiring immediate treatment.Throughout 2024, South Sudan faced numerous disease outbreaks, including measles, yellow fever, poliovirus, meningitis, hepatitis E, and cholera. The ongoing conflict in Sudan, coupled with these outbreaks, poses a risk of imported cases, such as the detection of cholera at the Joda border crossing in Renk. Measles and Penta 3 vaccine coverage rates stand at 72% and 73% respectively, leading to multiple measles outbreaks across the country. Access to healthcare remains a challenge, with only 57% of the population able to reach the nearest health facility within an hour, while 44% face long distances as a barrier to healthcare access.World Vision is actively responding to the crisis in various counties, addressing critical needs in different regions. In Renk Transit Camp in Upper Nile, the continuous influx of returnees and refugees presents significant challenges to health and nutrition. World Vision's health facility in the camp serves thousands of individuals, with a notable percentage requiring nutrition services. In Aweil West and Aweil East in Northern Bhar-el-Ghazal, over 22,770 returnees need health, water, sanitation, and nutrition support, with alarming malnutrition rates recorded in some areas.Melut County continues to host internally displaced persons (IDPs) fleeing floods and conflict, with pressing needs for food, shelter, nutrition, and healthcare. In Tonga, Panyikang county, and regions like Tambura, Ezo, and Nagero, ongoing intercommunal violence has led to population displacement and critical needs for health, nutrition, shelter, and water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services. Despite efforts by organizations like World Vision, challenges persist, including shortages of medicines and healthcare staff, as evidenced by incidents like the recent shooting of a health worker behind Tambura hospital on November 24.

2. Project activities

WV will work in collaboration with the State MoH staff, Health Facility staff and other health actors and community health volunteers to provide quality health services. This project seeks to strengthen the technical capacity of health workers in disease surveillance, control, management, and community sensitization. WV will provide emergency primary healthcare to 51,915 individuals through a combination of static & mobile health facilities at the IDP camps & affected communities in the target locations in Upper Nile, Northern Bhar el ghazal, Warrap and Western Equatoria. In line with CO1 (To improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child & adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations), this project will build the capacity of staff in disease surveillance, CMR, BeMONC/CeMONC, and strengthen and use existing structures such as the Boma Health Workers, CNV, MTMSG, men & women champion groups to deliver educational health messages on health promotion and diseases prevention in IDPs & host communities. Project will be integrated with Protection, FSL, WASH and nutrition interventions. Beneficiaries will have access to sanitation & water supplies in selected PHCUs, mobile clinics. CO2 (Specialized protection and multi-sectoral services that are sensitive to gender, age and disability are provided to vulnerable groups, including survivors of gender-based violence, neglect, and sexual exploitation and abuse) in line with this objective WV will support the static and mobile clinics with IHK kits, conduct reactive measles campaigns, produce and distribute IEC materials and establish community feedback mechanisms. C03 (To enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms, WV will facilitate quarterly review meetings with other humanitarians and the community to discuss humanitarian matters affecting the target locations.) in alignment to this objective WV seeks to complement/leverage the existing funding from HSTP, BHA, WVUS, GFFO, BMZ projects in Upper Nile, Warrap, Northern Bharelghazal and Western Equatoria. These projects have a focus on health system strengthening with emphasis on maternal child health and nutrition.
