1 - Needs

The Corona virus otherwise known as COVID-19 continued to change the environments in which people live. The disease outbreak is having both short term and far-reaching implication for families are particularly vulnerable. Disruptions to families and the wider community can have negative consequences for Peoples living with Disabilities well-being and development. As the virus continues to spread across the world, individuals are facing multiple new stresses, including physical and psychological health risks, school and business closures, limited access to basic services, family confinement, isolation and economic vulnerability. Through all of that, children, persons with disabilities (PWDs), windows and Child headed hold and Aged population are particularly vulnerable. nationwide lockdowns, and government policies closing markets and restricting public gatherings, could be disruptive not just for the traders but for the public who will likely struggle to access markets and continue small-medium scale trade for MSMEs.Inclusion of older people and people with disabilities. (sphere standard)To complement the guidance in Sphere, it is important to ensure we leave no one behind and the following guidance from the Humanitarian Inclusion Standards for Older People and People with Disabilities address the following relevant issues: • Older people and people with disabilities and/or chronic illnesses face further risks and consequences, as well as anxieties, adding to the ongoing challenge of surviving a humanitarian setting. • The people most often cited as being at serious risk are largely older people, people with disabilities and/or chronic illnesses. They are often excluded in a humanitarian setting. • Being excluded, it can be harder for older people and people with disability in the humanitarian setting to take the most effective steps to protect themselves from the coronavirus outbreak. COVID-19 coronavirus threatens not only older people and people with disabilities’ health, but their independence.
