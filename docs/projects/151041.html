Project Summary

The project aims at saving lives of 2100 residents of Ceel Buur District in Galgaaduud Region in Somalia who are currently in food crisis condition. The overall objective for this project is to reduce the percentage of people in severe acute food insecurity. The assistance will provide hope especially to women, men and elderly as well as lactating mothers and the sick for a period of 3 months. The key programme outputs are :
 Vulnerability assessment conducted
 Number of contract signed with vendors for supplies of food to the
beneficiaries
 Number of households registered for the project
 Training project facilitated for stakeholders to participate in the project
design, M&E and number of consultations
 Number of lactating mothers enrolled into the project
 Number of women, girls and orphaned registered in the projects
 Number of community meetings held with local areas security
management systems to support the project

Needs

Humanitarian needs in Somalia persist due to climatic shock, large-scale displacement, and lack of access to basic services and, at its root, ongoing conflict. In the first half of 2018, the humanitarian situation in Somalia remained grave due to residual impacts of drought, ongoing displacement, conflict, and seasonal flooding. The unprecedented drought spanning over four consecutive poor rainy seasons has, however, severely aggravated the crisis and this was followed by heavy rainfall in April, and May 2018 resulted in major parts of the regions experiencing severe flooding which resulted to destroyed crops, shelters, and social services; including roads, water facilities, education facilities and over 290,000 temporarily displaced. This project will provide emergency food supplies to 2100 people in Ceel Buur District in Galgaduud Region who are currently in a food crisis and require immediate food aid. The proposed project targets those who lost most of their animals during the drought, orphanedchildren and women headed families and those with malnourished children as well as lactatingmothers. This area currently is controlled by the Al-Shabaab Islamist Group, and therefore it isinaccessible to International UN Agencies thus hindering quick humanitarian response in aid tosupport these people. However, local Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs) such as FamilyEmpowerment & Relief Organization (FERO) who have developed elaborate network in the areathrough local clan leaders, areas chiefs and all key stakeholders who manage the locals; security systems in the area will use such networks to bring humanitarian aid to the people. In an emergency such as what is facing the target population at Ceel-Buur, the focus is on meeting the primary needs through the delivery of aid as quickly as possible. However, distributing food directly may carry more risks and storage expenses as well as hiring security personnel to manage the stores.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The design of the project from problem analysis will be done by stakeholders and it will centre on developing Somalia led solutions in solving the crisis for now and future. Component of project sustainability will be prioritised at all phases of the design process, solution analysis and project strategy development. Local vendors will be utilised to supply food which will also create employment to local women in the area. Women will be appointed as area representative and this will give boost to the fight for gender representation which is very low in the area. Most families all decisions are madeby men however the project intend to explain to the local men that women have more experience in food matters and the project will utilise that experience in training them on responsibility and leadership. After the program has improved health of locals, they will be trained based on availableresources they can actively engaged in to raise income for feeding their families.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Risk: engagement with displaced communities who have since resettled in the project area thosefrom marginalized communities’ risks being interfered with the local leadersLikelihood of risk: High and impact: HighMitigation measures FERO shall explain to the local leaders during debriefing early sessions of the projectsthe need for equal representation of Internally displaced persons, women, men and youthsin the programme All program activities including recruitment of locals as field officers each person pervillage will be done through an open process and each village will be represented amongthe stakeholders who will be entrusted of making critical decisions FERO also will try as much as possible to utilise their existing close workingrelationships with the local leaderships to influence the outcome of several decisions infavour of equal representations and prioritisation based on persons at high risk Key opinion leaders and representatives from the beneficiaries will undergo trainingwhere the programme objectives will be thoroughly articulated and the danger of noaction plan to the targeted beneficiaries will be explained.  Feedbacks links of communications will also be established through the office of projectcoordinator. This will assists in addressing emerging issues before they escalate to amajor issue which can hamper the implementation process. There will not be any special treatment given to any beneficiaries neither will any personbe given more vouchers more than the set limits. Equality will be practiced throughoutthe period the food will be distributed. Likewise the process of vetting for vendors will betransparent and criteria will be explained to all the stakeholders to avoid instances wherethe project will aggravate tensions within the community and even to the IDPs. IDPs, men, women and youths will equally be represented at all phases of project cycleand at all levels of decisions making.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Risks: engaging with the vulnerable members of the community, women, orphaned children, the disabled and vulnerable and those who risks being neglected by the local leaders. FERO intends to work with an all stakeholders to assist in identifying the most vulnerable women, orphaned children, disabled among the target population and organize, reach them through our field officers and coordinate how their food supplies will be distributed to them. This will also include those who are sick and of old age will also be included. The identification of these vulnerable groups will begin during households beneficiaries mapping. FERO also intends to incorporate proper representation of women and men from recruitment of vendors to supply food there will be 4 men vendors and 4 women vendors, filling of vacancies for 4 field officers will also take into consideration gender balance and qualification especially those with skills of mobilizing groups these the position for field officers will be reserved for local residence at least one person per area. Registering all disabled individuals and the sick who are at most risks and discussing with the local leadership on persons who will be responsible of delivering their food supplies.

At the same time the Al-Shabaab government collects taxesfrom the local people The project can easily aggravate the exposure of targeted communities if elements ofdiscrimination is seen during food distribution process or if the FERO hires a store in thearea to keep food supplies. That why we are recommending food voucher systems as anappropriate channel by which food will reach the local. The risks of keeping the suppliesis with the vendor and payment is only given to the vendor upon collection of the voucherby our field officers from the vendors indicating that they have supplied food to thebeneficiary who is the bearer of the voucher serial number.  Ensuring that all solutions are decided by community through community organisedmeetings with representatives from the church, the local administration and selectedproject leaders.
