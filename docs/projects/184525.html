Project Summary

According to HNO report (Oct. 2021), Conflict and insecurity have forced hundreds of thousands of people to flee their homes in 2021 and are expected to remain key drivers of displacement in 2022. Humanitarian access is hampered by ongoing hostilities and movement and security restrictions. Somalia remains on the frontline of climate change, which continues to induce crises resulting in widespread displacement, rapid urbanization, food insecurity, and increased poverty. Critically, climate change is also increasingly understood as a major driver of conflict in Somalia as the struggle for dwindling resources intensifies clan divisions and inter-clan conflict. Currently, the country is forecast to experience its third consecutive season of below-average rainfall, which has already resulted in a sharp increase in food insecurity, especially in rural areas. Without humanitarian assistance, nearly 3.5 million are expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through the end of 2021. There is a serious risk that the combined impact of consecutive seasons of below-average to poor rainfall can develop into a major drought by early to mid-2022. At the same time, large parts of Somalia remain prone to severe riverine and flash flooding. A total of 7.7 million Somali women, men and children are estimated to require humanitarian assistance in 2022. According to the WASH cluster report, many parts of Somalia are experiencing prolonged drought conditions and water shortages since June, with thousands of people affected. This project aims to reduce people’s needs, risks, and vulnerabilities and increase access to durable and sustainable safe water, adequate sanitation facilities and hygiene education to the crisis affected IDPs, returnees and host communities and areas that are a high risk of AWD/Cholera due to extremely low water and/or sanitation in Somalia. Under this project, CoDHNet proposes to carry out the following in Gedo, and Lower Shabelle regions:
1. Provision of lifesaving safe water through water voucher to crisis affected 1,000HHs (IDPs and host communities), benefiting around 6,000 beneficiaries (900 men, 1500 women, 1750 boys and 1750 girls including 15% people with disabilities. Each HH will receive 45l/day for a period of 60 days.
2. Rehabilitate two non-functional boreholes and its infrastructures and replace the generator powered pump into hybrid system. Benefitting around 12,000 affected beneficiaries (1800 men, 3000 women, 3600 boys and 3600 girls including 15% people with disabilities.
3. Rehabilitate 20 flood proof shallow well which are accessible to people with disabilities and children. Benefitting around 10,000 affected people (1500 men, 2500 women, 3000 boys and 3000 girls including 15% people with disabilities).
4. Conduct capacity building training to 22 WASH committees and 44 wells' operators to keep the sustainability of the WASH services.
5. Construct 200 flood proof latrines with washing points and lockable doors to keep the privacy of women and children and avoid the risk of GBV against women and girls (10% will be accessible to disabilities).
6. Conduct comprehensive hygiene promotion and covid-19 mitigation awareness campaigns to the target locations to reach 22,000 affected people (3300 men, 5500 women, 6600 boys and 6600 girls including 15% people with disabilities).
7. Conduct 3 days training to 20 community hygiene promoters (15 women and 5 men) on proper hygiene promotion practices and Covid-19 mitigation measures.
8. Distribute 1500 hygiene promotion kits to 1500 new arrived vulnerable IDPs.
CoDHNet will employ community led beneficiary selection processes and ensure adequate targeting and representation of extremely vulnerable and minority groups in community committees. Interventions proposed will employ a Do No Harm approach with a gender that ensures conflict sensitivity to reduce protection risks for all individuals, and to mitigate the risk of conflict or community.

1. - Projects activities

1.1: Conduct Community Consultation workshops to 180 community stakeholders shared equally men and women including minorities, and disabilities to familiarize them with the project objectives, activities, feedback, and complaint mechanisms for AAP purposes;1.2: CoDHNet will provide 60 days distribution of lifesaving safe water through vouchers to 1,000 IDPs and host community households in Gedo and L/Shabelle regions benefiting 6,000 beneficiaries (900 men, 1500 women, 1750 boys and 1750 girls including 15% people with disabilities) who have no access of safe water due to the floods, conflict and drought.. Each HH will receive 45l/day for a period of 60 days. 1.3: Rehabilitate/construct 20 flood proof shallow wells with hand pumps, benefiting 10,000 people (1500 men, 2500 women, 3000 girls, and 3000 boys including 15% of disabilities). CoDHNet will consult with women/girls at the location of the shallow wells to reduce the risk of GBV against women/girls.1.4: Upgrade 2 boreholes equipped with hybrid systemsand rehabilitate borehole infrastructures; benefiting 12,000 affected people (3000 women, 2160 men, 2700 girls, and 3240 boys);1.5: Chlorinate 22 water points and conduct daily monitor the free chlorine residual testing(FCR) to prevent waterborne diseases and reduce AWD/Cholera outbreaks. 1.6: Conduct capacity-building training to 22 WASH committees and 44 water sources operators shared equally men and women, each WASH committee is composed (3 women and 4 men) on water source management and operation & maintenance of the water sources to sustain its continuity after the project is faced out. As cross-cutting, conduct community training on DRR and conflict resolution mechanisms. 2.1: Construct 200 flood-proof, desludging, and gender-sensitive latrines with lockable doors and hand washing basins to new arrived IDP settlements and host communities’ HHs to reduce open defecation and keep the privacy of women and children to avoid the risk of GBV against women and girls (10% of the latrines will be accessible to disabilities). The latrines will benefit around 6,000 affected men, women and children. 3.1: Conduct Hygiene Promotion activities and Covid-19 mitigation measures to reach 22,000 people (3300 men, 5500 women, 6600 boys and 6600 girls including 15% disabilities) though visiting house-to-house, schools and health centers emphasizing hand washing with soap, use of mask and social distancing. CoDHNet will conduct two KAP surveys at the inception and completion of the project to identify the gaps of HP and the impact of the hygiene promotion activities respectively. 3.2: Conduct 3 days training to 20 community hygiene promoters (15 women and 5 men) on proper hygiene promotion practices and Covid-19 mitigation measures. 3.3: Distribute 1500 hygiene promotion kits to 1500 new arrived vulnerable IDPs. Each HP kit contains (200 aqua tabs, 1 Jerry can (20L), 1 bucket (20L), 3 bars of soaps, and female sanitation materials).CoDHNet will ensure adequate targeting and representation of vulnerable and minority groups in community committees. 4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.1. CoDHNet will include women, men, boys, and girls including people with disabilities and minorities in all the consultation and decision-making meetings at the inception of the project, and during the project implementation. In the WASH committees, well operators, and community hygiene promotions, men and women will share equally. All the WASH services will be accessible to men, women, and children including people with disabilities and minorities. 4.4. CoDHNet will locate water sources visible, central locations, and not more than 500 meters from settlement and install lights near communal WASH facilities which significantly increase the security of women and girls fetching the water. Involve women and girls insisting locations for communal WASH facilities considering their security and safety and ensure the location and access routes to WASH facilities and distribution points are safe. At the same time, latrines will be lockable and the location of the new latrines is discussed with the women and girls to increase security in regards to the risk of GBV. CoDHNet will work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from the use of or lack of WASH facilities.
