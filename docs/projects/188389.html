Project Summary

The intervention aims to ensure increased civilian population’s security and sustainable returns enabled by protecting lives civilian infrastructure and productive assets and supporting livelihoods and income generation. The project focuses on the reduction of impacts by explosive hazards and is a contribution towards a lesser dependency of the population in Al Hasakah Governorate on humanitarian aid and supporting their long-term resilience. It further focuses on emergency humanitarian mine action intervention in high-priority areas of Deir ez Zour Governorate. The proposed area of operations are mainly former agricultural areas or contaminated arable land in Al Hasakah Governorate, primarily along the Khabur River or to the west of Hasakah City towards the Iraq border, where limited access to vital assets due to explosive hazards contamination is limiting people’s abilities to rebuild their lives. Operations are also planned in Deir ez Zour, particularly in areas along the East Line. Precise locations, however, will depend on the developments in the field, priorities highlighted by local communities/authorities, and complementary activities of other HMA operators in the region. To remove and mitigate the threats against lives and limbs of the civilian population, Team ITF proposes an integrated humanitarian mine action approach in close coordination with the relevant local authorities, communities, and other humanitarian actors. The project envisages the continuation of non-technical survey, mapping, marking, and clearance conducted on suspected or confirmed explosive hazards contaminated high-priority areas (schools, infrastructure) or former agricultural land (including agricultural infrastructure, e.g. irrigation channels) in ISIS liberated areas, and return of cleared land and infrastructure to the local population for their safe and productive use. Estimated at approximately 3 million m2 of cleared land in areas assessed as a priority in a participatory process with local authorities and communities. These activities shall be augmented through explosive ordnance risk education/community liaison, targeting approximately 10,000 at-risk boys, girls, men, and women with safety messaging, and providing the ‘eyes and ears’ for the clearance teams
on the ground. Through continuous outreach, the latter shall ensure that the team’s efforts are genuinely community-based and community-focused. When combined, this package of efforts shall help revitalize the social fabric in the community and bring a sense of normalcy to a war-ravaged and impoverished region. This intervention will build on first-hand knowledge and understanding of the specific local context in the Al Hasakah and Deir ez Zour Governorates, and on experience gained during explosive hazards clearance activities that are being implemented in the region since 2020. The intervention will aim to encompass the ethnic and religious diversity and secure equal opportunities in terms of employment, project implementation structure, and decision-making to all, regardless of their social status or personal background. To the greatest extent practicable, it will specifically address the needs of the most marginalized groups. By aiming to benefit the residents of Al Hasakah and Deir ez Zour Governorate through this specific program, this intervention is prioritizing assistance to the most vulnerable groups.

4 - Needs

In Syria, including the Northeast, levels of explosive hazard contamination continue to beconsidered as very high and even massive, especially in areas where there has beenintense fighting, aerial bombardments, and ground shelling like Ar Raqqa, Deir ez Zour,and Al Hasakah. According to the 2021 Syria HRP, one in two people in Syria is considered to be at risk from explosive hazards. UNMAS reports that the destruction orcontamination of key infrastructure, such as hospitals, has deprived civilians of basicservices, and the presence of explosive hazards is a lethal barrier to movement, thedelivery of humanitarian aid, and to those seeking refuge from violence, as well as tothose wishing to cultivate their land. In 2018 1,465 mine/explosive remnants of warvictims were recorded in Syria by the Landmine and Cluster Munition Monitor fromlandmines and ERW, though due to underreporting the true figure is thought to besignificantly higher. The majority were caused by improvised victim-activated devices orIEDs. In addition to the loss of civilian life, explosive hazards are the cause of lifechanginginjuries, trauma, loss of livelihoods, for instance through farming, or hinderedaccess to medical provision or education. Further compounding the needs of people inNES, are the COVID-19 pandemic and recent economic collapse, leading to increasingpoverty rates and major food insecurity.

5 - Implementation of activities

Non-technical survey (NTS) will assess land, map contamination, and provide categorization as a suspected or confirmed hazardous area (SHA/CHA) and will serve as the foundation for subsequentprocesses of cancelling, reducing, marking or clearing land for productive use. Clearance teams will be deployed using various methodologies to release land tosafe and productive use by local residents. Technical survey (TS) will be the primarymethod for defining accurately and with confidence the extent of areas that requireclearance. TS will be implemented hand-in-hand with actual clearance as the systematic andcontrolled clearance of hazardous area, including former military positions and temporary storage ammunition storage positions, or munitions simply dropped or abandoned, munitions that have been primed, fuzed, armed or otherwise pre-pared for use but have not been fired (or fired but failed tofunction, inclusive of sub-munitions), and booby traps (failed, or abandoned IEDs leftbehind after hostilities have ceased). Explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) will beembedded with the survey and clearance teams to share safety messaging within localcommunities. They also take on the role of community liaison (CL), and will work ontraining a number of local Community Focal Points.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1. Presence of explosive hazards limiting access and posing a threat to the physicalsafety and security to those delivering and those benefitting from services; R2.Creation of wrong expectations among beneficiaries, who attribute to EORE teams the ability of removing explosive ordnance, leading to frustration and tension between beneficiaries and service providers; R3. Risk of interventions not reaching all vulnerable groups because of lack of understanding of social norms, gender considerations; R4. Familiarization with messages delivered at EORE sessions resulting in beneficiaries becoming overconfident and engaging in risky behaviours. R5: Damage to housing, land or properties or eruption of HLP disputes due to survey/clearance. M1. Delivery of EORE training to humanitarian personnel to increase awareness of the explosive threat; include first aid component in risk education sessions; delivery of training of trainers (ToT) to ensure large-scale awareness and sustainability of efforts; M2. Clear explanation of intervention and objectives to beneficiaries; close cooperation between clearance and EORE/CL personnel; M3. Involvement of community focal points in the planning and delivery of risk education sessions; tailoring of messages and EORE materials according to the audience; M4. Involvement of survivors as role model for behaviour change; linking EORE with clearance interventions. M6: Clear explanation of intervention to beneficiaries, land remediation.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

R1: Teams exposed to/guilty of GBV, sexual exploitation, and abuse. M1: training all project staff trained on PSEA, gender mainstreaming, and Do No Harm; ensuring access of beneficiaries and staff to feedback and complaint mechanisms.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Disinterest of local authorities/lack of community acceptance. M1: maintain communication with local authorities and communities, employ lessons learned from previous projects in the area, deployment of CL. R2: Inability to ensure relatively safe access of staff to the areas and inability to operate in a manner that does not disproportionately endanger the lives of the project staff. M2: Contingency planning, scenario development for remote management with enhanced capacity mentorship of local staff. R3: Non-compliance of project personnel with SOPs or lower than expectedknowledge and competencies of the staff. M3: Re-training provided to staff failing to live up to the expectations; disciplinary processes for repeated failures to comply; feedback and complaint mechanism in place. R4: Deteriorating trajectory of the COVID-19 pandemic. M4: Adopting social distancing and other recommended measures during the implementation of activities; strict adherence to health prevention measures by the work team and the provision of appropriate PPE, testing kits; encouraging beneficiaries to use prevention methods and social distancing, and include COVID-19 messaging in activities. R5: Unexpected weather patterns and conditions related to climate change, may renderland clearance activities impossible. M5: Maintaining flexible working patterns and adapting the timing of mine action activities to the weather conditions or pausing activities temporarily.
