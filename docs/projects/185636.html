Project Summary

1. Providing essential primary health care services at the Primary Health Care center:
・ Provision of healthcare services: Providing counselling and treatment for maternal and child health, SRH, NCDs, EPI, communicable diseases, general OPD and 24/7 emergency care services for both the FDMNs and HCs. Consultations rooms are separated by gender to ensure privacy and female patients will be attended by a female doctor. Triage system is in place to provide immediate care for those with emergent needs. Patients requiring further care will be referred according to the referral pathways. Patients with suspected infections will be isolated from other patients in the isolation room.
・ Ensure adherence to the minimum package of essential health services for primary healthcare facilities in the Rohingya/ FDMN camps where feasible. Health sector, government authorities including CiCs, other health sector partners will be consulted to achieve this when needed.
・ Clinic based health awareness sessions: Health care workers will organize awareness sessions at the PHC to the patients based on the patient trend and/or the seasonal trend of the patients in our PHC and in the camps to enable timely awareness as well as foster enhanced communications among patients and clinic staff.
・ Data analysis and monitoring: Patient data is routinely collected through the patient records, and analyzed /monitored to capture the disease trend of the serving communities as well as timely shared/reported with/to the health sector to contribute to surveillance. Patient feedback is collected through suggestion boxes and interviews. All data gathered will be disaggregated by sex and age for analysis and monitoring.
・ Our PHC will contribute to the health sector campaigns (e.g. COVID-19 vaccination, Oral Cholera Vaccine campaign etc)
2. Community based health activities:
・ Focus Group Discussion (FGD): FGDs on health and hygiene will be organized to enable the community to identify what kind of health issues lie within their community and share challenges/ good practices among them. FGDs sessions will be organized for both the FDMNs and HCs and will carried out separately by gender to enable to capture the voices from both genders and enhance open discussion and active engagement. Community Health Workers (CHWs)/ Community Volunteers (CVs) who are familiar with the community will facilitate the FGD. FGD reports will be produced separately by gender so both opinions are equally heard.
・ Health awareness activities: The topics of the health awareness will be selected based on the key findings from the FGD as well as the disease trend. Awareness materials such as flipcharts will be developed for each topic. Depending on the topic, hygiene items such as soap and cloth masks will be distributed along with the awareness. The CHWs/ CVs who are familiar with the community will facilitate the awareness and enhance interaction among participants so that they can express their opinion and discuss challenges and solutions related to the topic.
・ Community networking: Due to difficulties in physical access to health facilities and limited access to correct health information, vulnerable people in the community such as pregnant women, elderlies, people with disabilities who have higher health risks are more likely to be left out in case of emergencies. Along with the above activities, the CHWs/CVs will identify the vulnerable individuals and provide them with the necessary information to promote health and well-being, and about available health services accessible from their community, how they can get there, whom to contact, so that they can live a healthy life and be supported in case of emergencies. Community volunteers will serve as the key person in their community that the community people can rely on when they need health related information. Community volunteers will be remunerated according to the progress and achievements.

1 - Identified Needs

Although 4 years have already passed since the influx, the circumstances surrounding the refugees are yet challenging and the displacement has become a protracted crisis. The total population of FDMNs and HCs in need of assistance is projected to be more than 1.4 million (JRP 2022 Sector objectives and Monitoring framework). The massive influx has brought negative effects on the HCs in the form of increased cost of living, loss of employment opportunity, exploitation of natural resources. This situation created a degree of tension among the HC, the aid agencies and the refugee population. Thus, a need for improving equitable services in the HC is acknowledged and incorporated in to the JRP. As the preparation of JRP 2022 is underway, improving equitable access and utilization of essential health services, preparation and prevention of communicable diseases outbreaks, as well as promoting health and wellbeing are set as a priority for response to health needs. There are still certain gaps in the number of health facilities available compared to the number in need indicating that equitable access to healthcare services remains a challenge, which has resulted to both the FDMNs and HC experiencing barriers when needing to access health care (J-MSNA preliminary findings -Health). Individuals with special needs, women, woman headed households, elderlies are more likely to face these challenges to access health facilities as well as access to correct health related information related. The congested and unhygienic living condition increases the risk of communicable diseases, which worsens during the monsoon season in the form of outbreaks of water-borne diseases. The year 2020 was significantly affected by COVID-19 and especially have posed a negative impact on people’s health seeking behavior, which were generated by rumors and misinformation about COVID-19 as well as distrust to health services. A modelling study conducted by the Johns Hopkins University initially estimated that over 2,000 deaths would occur within 12 months after a single introduction of the virus in the camp, however the actual figures up to now remains significantly lower than the estimate. This is a result of the consolidated effort of all health partners who thoroughly followed and implemented the necessary infectious prevention control measures whilst continuing the essential health services, as well as the COVID-19 awareness implemented by the CHWs/CVs at the community level to disseminate correct information and means of prevention. Health seeking behavior has shown some improvement compared to 2020, and has slightly reversed the negative impact due to COVID-19, however we need to continue to address the barriers to improve health seeking behavior and enable beneficiaries to access health facilities when needed. Active engagement of the community including the vulnerable individuals as well as the influential persons such as majhis and imams would be an effective approach to build trust to the health service and disseminating awareness messages and necessary information. PWJ acknowledges the importance of local knowledge to implement a successful project and thus it partnered with local organizations such as Dhaka Community Hospital Trust (DCHT). PWJ/DCHT team coordinates and closely collaborate its activities in the field with local authorities including CiCs, majhis as well as the health sector. All kinds of information and opinions are gathered during daily interaction with the beneficiaries, and incorporated into the project development and implementation. A suggestions and complaints mechanism is in practice at our health center. In line with JRP 2022, our project will provide the much-needed essential primary health care services and create awareness of health and hygiene through an all-inclusive approach where not only the FDMNs but also the host communities will be beneficiaries.
