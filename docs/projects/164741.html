4 - Needs

According to HNO 2020, the crisis in Syria continues to strain the socio-economic foundations of Syrian people, thereby affecting their ability to sustain adequate standards of living, seek decent livelihood opportunities, and access essential infrastructure and services. These factors have deepened poverty and weakened communities’ resilience to cope with internal and external shocks. Against this backdrop, there are 9.9 million people inside Syria requiring Early Recovery and Livelihoods support. While areas exposed to active hostilities have shrunk over time, populations continue to experience poor living standards, with 83% of the population living below the poverty line (HNO 2019). In NWS, poverty has been exacerbated by the escalation of armed hostilities that have limited Syrians’ access to markets, the agricultural output of farming businesses, and sharply reduced levels of food security. Currently, early recovery assistance, livelihoods, and cash assistance remain critical needs for 57% of recently displaced persons or an estimated 167,000 people in NWS (IDP Snapshot 2020). 35% of recently displaced persons cited their most important reason for moving was improved access to livelihood opportunities (HNAP MNM Report 2019).

5 - Implementation of activities

• Provide Business Support Package (BSP) to support business set-up or enhancement of small micro enterprises • Provide Vocational Training (VT) and Employability Support Packages (ESP) to selected beneficiaries (male and female)• Provide Business Development Training to selected beneficiaries to build their capacity and skills toward entrepreneurship and to encourage productive economic activities• Implementation of Community Support Projects (CSPs) to improve access to basic and social services in the communities through light construction/rehabilitation of infrastructures and/or provision of specialized equipment. Concerning rehabilitation of schools, before the project begins collaboration with the Education Cluster will be established.• Set up short-term employment opportunities (Cash for Work (CFW)) related to community public services and initiatives• Establish Community Engagement Teams (CETs) in each selected community to ensure community participation and engagement • Provide capacity building to Community Engagement Teams (CETs) on conducting voluntary work, improving self-reliance and effectively engaging with the community on events• Support community initiatives to promote social cohesion in the selected communities

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks:1. IOM response activities unintentionally lead to intra-community tensions arising from the beneficiary selection or limited resources and response capacities.2. The principal of “Do no harm” is not guaranteed due to unclear objective based on the community needs, validated through a transparent community consultation and participation process (or unrealistic expectations of income).3. Cultural and social norms may prevent women from participating in certain livelihood-generating activities (SME, VTs, FTs, CFW).4. Risk of violence (e.g. protests) or from deteriorating security situation5. Pressure on the service provider from the local authorities on who should or should not receive the service.6. Established Community Engagement Teams (CETs) might not be accepted by some local councils.7. Engaging in sexual exploitation and abuse and child labor by service provider or by other actor in the community. Mitigation measures:1. Perform conflict analysis before planning the response, ensure that a comprehensive needs analysis informs the response formulation, and that this is openly documented and transparent. 2. Communicate the planned response, and the rationale behind to the community or communities concerned and keep them informed of the project implementation. 3. Obtain validation of the prioritization and of the formulated response from CETs, or through well-selected key informants making sure that there is an equitable representation of diverse gender and age groups among them. 4. Ensure registrations, activities and distributions are well organized 5. Inform partners about the importance of principled humanitarian assistance, and make clear the ways to report any attempted interference6. Explain the purpose of the CETs and ensure suitable representation 7. IOM will ensure that selected IPs are sensitized on PSEA and aware of child labour risks. IPs will build the capacity of CETs to be able to identify and report any allegations/incidents.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:1. Security situation in Aleppo and Idleb Governorate allows IOM to implement response activities.2. Activities can be implemented in coordination with the local councils.3. Proper beneficiary selection processes are followed.4. Beneficiaries start their business after receiving their support packages. 5. Vendors in targeted areas have sufficient capacity. 6. Active community participation and engagement. 7. IOM interventions contribute to community cohesion. Risks:1. Security situation in Aleppo and/or in Idleb do not allow for viable IOM response activities to be undertaken.2. Local authorities are not in favour of the proposed intervention3. Local authorities seek to exert undue influence on IOM response activities (e.g. beneficiary selection). 4. Support packages are not in line with beneficiaries' requirements, or support packages are sold to gain immediate cash assistance.5. Vendors lack the capacity requested to provide required items 6. Dis-engagement of communities during the assessment, planning and implementation stages.7. Social tension between the different groups resulted from the partial inclusion of community members Mitigation measures:1. Establish a security and accessibility plan for the targeted areas, including contingency plan to target alternative locations2. Implementing Partners (IPs) ensure local authorities are engaged from initial stage 3. IOM's beneficiary selection process is clearly explained to implementing partners and verified against pre-determined selection criteria. Process is explained to local community members.4. Support packages are designed based on the beneficiaries’ business requirements 5. Well-designed market assessment, enough time to grantee procurement process6. IPs work closely with the communities to prioritize main sectors to be targeted based on the findings of the community assessment7. Transparent beneficiary selection process followed
