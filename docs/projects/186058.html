Project Summary

The project is a one year project focusing on addressing the identified child protection gaps and needs of most vulnerable girls, boys and their communities affected by conflict in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa States of North East Nigeria. The total project target is 51,000 persons (16,524 girls, 14,076 boys, 11,016 women and 9,384 men). At least 5% of the overall beneficiaries will be persons living with disability (PLWD). Plan International Nigeria (Plan) proposes to implement a child protection (CP) project that aims at improving children's access to quality and inclusive case management services including alternative care for unaccompanied and separated children, reintegration support for former children associated with armed forced and groups and referrals; gender and age appropriate psychosocial support interventions such as positive parenting sessions, life skills, recreational and counseling support for children affected by conflict. It will also include ensuring a protective environment for children through the functionality of community-based child protection mechanisms and awareness raising on key child protection issues/risks and existing services. Plan will ensure girls and boys have access to quality child protection services adapted to their needs, and will work with Ministry of Women and Social Welfare, UNICEF and the Child Protection sub-sector to enhance the accessibility of the child protection services. Plan will also work closely with case management task force (CMTF), Government Social Workers and ICRC in the tracing and reunification of unaccompanied and separated children. Plan is also a member of the child protection sub-sector and will coordinate closely with all members especially on the use of standardized tool/resources, joint trainings and referral mechanisms. Further, Plan will ensure an integrated approach by working closely with Education, Food Assistance, Livelihoods and Nutrition sectors. For instance, Plan will run its school based child friendly spaces in schools supported by Education sector within Plan. Also, caregivers of malnourished children enrolled in our nutrition program will receive positive parenting sessions and child protection awareness. In adherence with Covid-19 measures and guidelines Plan will employ both static and mobile approach in implementing their interventions. Plan will also set up child friendly complaints and feedback mechanisms to enable persons of concerns to report any concerns and receive feedback.

1 - Needs

According to HRP 2022, there are about 1.2 M persons in need of child protection services of which 404,287 are girls, 351,852 are boys, 285,580 women and 248,406 men. Due to conflict and displacement mental health and psychosocial distress was reported among IDPs (MSNA 2021). This is further compounded by secondary displacement due to forced returns and relocations. To address the psychosocial needs, Plan reached about 29,430 (17,658B,11,772G) children who experienced psychosocial distress between October 2020 till November 2021. However, Plan's MSNA 2021 has flagged an increase in mental health and psychosocial (PSS) distress indicating huge needs for PSS in 2022. Consultations held with children, revealed that children would like recreational spaces in schools. An estimated 1 in 50 children have been separated from their families due to the conflict, and urgently require family tracing, case management and alternative care (MSNA). According to Plan CPIMS+ data 602 children need case management services. Of which 243 (134 girls, 109 boys) are Unaccompanied and Separated children (UASC) and 139 (58 girls, 81 boys) are children associated with armed actors/groups. Furthermore, due to insecurity and breakdown of local protection mechanisms, about 60% of children in NE Nigeria have experienced some form of violence (UNICEF). Boys aged 13-17 are at risk of conflict related violence including abduction, killing and recruitment. Girls are engaging in survival sex as a coping mechanism due to lack of food and other basic supplies with the household/family. Displaced persons are at risk of violence, GBV due to poor shelter especially in the IDP camps (DTM informants). Adolescent girls (10-17 years) living the camps are particularly at risk of sexual violence and denial of realization of their rights. Community support structures such as schools, religious places, hospitals, livelihoods, family and community networks have been broken down as people living in IDP camps are separated from their families creating an environment where human rights are not respected. Social and gender roles or responsibilities have also changed: some women and girls have taken on new roles within households and communities. Food insecurity is a contributing factor to school drop out and increased child labour (MSNA) for children aged 8-16 years consequently affecting the wellbeing of children. In Yobe the main protection risks for children include killing and maiming of children (18%); child labor (13%); forced recruitment of children by the insurgents; abduction of children (10%); sexual violence against children (9%); neglect (8%); attack on schools and hospitals by suspected insurgents (7%); forced marriages and denial of access to humanitarian support (3%). In Borno, the State Government is continuing to facilitate the return of IDPs living in Maiduguri to their local government areas (LGAs) of origin by December 2021. Most of those returning are children and women headed households. This move is likely to result to increased child protection needs due to limited child protection services include schools, hospitals and livelihoods in locations of return particularly in deep field locations.

2 - Activities

To address the above mentioned needs, Plan will conduct the following interventions:1. Support family tracing and reunification of separated and unaccompanied boys and girls (30G, 20G). This activity will also entail training of case workers for 5 days on case management process including on how to use CPIMS+ database. Trained case workers will identify and register cases in the CPIMS+/ primero, conduct comprehensive assessment of the cases, and develop care plans for the UASC. The reunification will be done in coordination with UNICEF.2. Provide community based alternative care placement for 38 unaccompanied boys and girls affected by protection risks (20 boys and 18 girls). The case workers with support from Government social workers will identify caregivers in the communities and train them on alternative care and child protection in order to equip them with skills on caring for UASC before placing children under their care.3. Provide children living with disabilities (CLWD) with appropriate needs-based child protection services and referrals (25 boys, 25 girls). Plan will provide cash based support to CLWD.4. Provide life skill education for adolescent boys and girls (10-14 years) and (15-19 years) based on Plan's adolescent programming toolkit. The sessions will be gender age and disaggregated. It will be conducted in schools and in the communities to ensure in school and out school adolescents are reached. Plan trained case workers will conduct the sessions. Each adolescent will participate in 10 sessions which are in the module. 5. Provide age and gender appropriate structured and recreational activities for children through static and mobile CFSs. Plan will engage PSS volunteers (60 men and 60 women) to conduct activities in the mobile and static spaces to reach 7500 boys and 7500 girls. Plan will group children according to their ages (5-8), (9-12), (13-17) and by age. Plan will conduct board games, football, skipping ropes, building blocks etc6. Conduct positive parenting lessons for caregivers of vulnerable children using Plan's Parenting Skills manual (4500 women, 1500 men) to enable them gain skills to manage stress, adopt positive parenting methods and support their children and adolescents. The sessions will be facilitated by trained case workers and CP officers.7. Referral of vulnerable children to access inter-sectorial and other specialized services. Plan will update the existing referral pathways in the target locations (at least 10 referral pathways will be updated) and disseminate them. Case workers will refer vulnerable children to Education, Health, Food assistance and Nutrition sectors within Plan and to other agencies for multi-sectorial support.8. Training of Community based child protection committee members, foster caregivers, local authorities, Government officials and Plan staff on child protection, rights, identification and referrals. Based on the Yobe Joint MSNA (June 2021) 49% of respondents felt confident to report protection concerns to the community structures, hence the need to build their capacity and work with them.9. Plan will also conduct key child protection awareness in the target communities on various topics such as prevention of separation, dangers of child labour and gender based violence. The awareness will be conducted by community volunteers and community based child protection networks members.

Plan will also sensitize the community on the GBV toll free help lines that are currently operated by the GBV sub-sector and Ministry of Women and Social Welfare so that they can report using this channel. Additionally, child help desks will be set up in schools.
