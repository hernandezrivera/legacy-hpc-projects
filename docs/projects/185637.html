Project Summary

PLAN International Bangladesh will focus on vulnerable and at-risk children, survivors of violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation, adolescents and youth through three interlinking components – quality case management, community-based child protection and CP system Strengthening. The proposed project will include adolescent life skills, community engagement in everyday peace and social cohesion building. The services will ensure boys, girls and adolescents in camp and host communities are safe and protected from violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation with special consideration on gender and disability. These activities will be aligned with Self-Reliance and Resilience.
Age appropriate, gender responsive and timely individual Case Management Services will be provided for vulnerable CAYs targeting unaccompanied and separated children, child-headed households, children in psychosocial distress, children with specific needs, and child survivors of GBV.
CBCP activities will be structured through feedback from community consultation and participation to ensure inclusion and ownership by both the host and the refugee community. The interventions will ensure inclusion of CAYs, persons with disability and the youths. Community-level interventions will include promoting social cohesion and strengthening self-protection of children. Addressing harmful practices and attitudes, Plan will invest in active community participation and positive parenting.
Keeping AAP in account PIB will ensure that child friendly feedback mechanisms are in place with strong emphasis on PSEA and Child Safeguarding through direct consultation with children and community people. The contact number of PSEA and safeguarding will be well informed to wider community. PIB will also ensure its partners have PSEA policies and the same is implemented.
PIB is committed to coordinating the response through active participation in sectors such as Protection, Education, GBV and Child Protection Sub-Sector and other working groups like Strategic Working Group (SAG), CMTWG to ensure meaningful access, prioritization of safety and integration of services. The services offered will be in line with CPMS, interagency tools and SOPs, CPSS strategy and will be ensured through structured training enhancing competencies in providing quality gender-responsive and timely services. This project will also include a contingency plan for natural disaster with giving importance on environment.
PLAN will also keep close connection with Government officials including capacity strengthening initiatives at different stage of project that will bring positive outcomes on the lives of the children.
This project will carry out A participatory research on social cohesion, facilitate and support dialogue and trust-building activities in pilot locations of host and camp. Workshop with duty bearers (government officials and local authorities), community leaders, parents, teachers and religious leaders in both community groups on developing a basic understanding of everyday peace, social cohesion, social trust and public accountability will also take place. Undertaking capacity-strengthening and training activities for national and local institutions, actors and partners on conflict sensitivity, everyday peace, social cohesion and peace-building will be the important aspect of this project.
Plan will undertake several assessments such as baseline, midterm, impact assessment and will collect disaggregated data including people with disability.
The project will priorities resourcing and capacity building of local partner for quality implementation in both camps and host community.
The project falls within Plan International’s development and humanitarian framework for Bangladesh in which sit several tools, such as The Plan International Bangladesh Country Strategic Plan, The Cox’s Bazar JRP 2021; CPSS strategy 2021, J-MSNA Refugee and host communities – Protection (Findings, 08 November 2021) etc

1 - Identified Needs

As of 30 September 2021, approximately 896,879 Rohingya refugees are residing in 34 highly congested camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas of Cox’s Bazar District of Bangladesh.Among them, 51% are children (UNHCR population factsheet, 30 September 2021). On the other hand, 541,021 people are living in host community of the adjacent camps where around 52% are children. Out of these total number of populations a total of 850,099 have been identified as people in need by the sector (Child <= 17: Refugee 464722; HC 114398 And Adult > 18: Refugee 194471; HC 7650).In camp setting unsafe living conditions, over dependency on external aid, and limited access to education for COVID-19, livelihood opportunities have exacerbated existing health and protection risks, especially for women, children, and persons with disabilities. The Protective environment for Rohingya boys and girls remains a serious concern during this COVID 19 pandemic. Rohingya children already faced risks and threats to their wellbeing. Both refugee and host community children are struggling to cope in the current climate, facing decrease access to services, stressors in the home caused by loss of income, and increase anxiety. Since the start of COVID-19 an increase in child labor, Mental health and psychosocial distress, Violence in the home, child marriage, Child labour and trafficking risks have been idenfied in CPSS Strategy 2021. Child labor reportedly increased with boys working in construction, driving tom-toms (three-wheeler moto vehicle), working at tea stalls, vegetable shops (PWG, 2020).In CPSS strategy(draft,2021) five priority areas have been identified by CPSS to guide it in the coming years, these are community level child protection, Community, System strengthening, justice for children, case management, working across sector According to JMSNA 2021,65% of households having reported community members not having been able to access protection services, reported unavailability of service/staff due to COVID-19 as the reason65% of households having reported community members not having accessed protection services, reporting reasons.56%of households reported needing protection services or support.As per JMSNA Type of support needed, overall and by households having and not having reported access to protection services among their top 3 priority needs for 2022 are (i) Improved safety and security in general, (ii)Improved safety and security for women and girls, Mental health and psychosocial support, Access to justice and mediation. In host community 51% of households reported perceived needs of children in their community not to have been adequately met at the time of data collection. Moreover 33% of households having reported community members who wanted to report an incident or access protection services for any other reason reported that community members were not able to report the incident or access the service they needed+52%of households having reported community members needing or accessing protection services reported barriers. Girls have increasingly resorted to staying at home for fear of sexual harassment (REACH, 2020). Violence against children is a significant issue with findings from the Child Protection Assessment Report (May 2020) showing that many parents and caregivers are especially concerned about the safety of their children. Primary concerns include physical violence (53%), child marriage (47%), kidnapping and/or trafficking (77%), exploitative work, and child labor (49%). Caregivers are also worried about the health of children (91%), sexual harassment (46%), the effects of natural hazards (46%), and wildlife attacks (21%). Findings show that adolescent boys and girls have similar concerns.In recent findings of a joint program of UNHCR and PLAN International Child marriage is found as a significant protection concern. CPIMS+ also identifies child marriage as a significant CP concern

practices that promote children’s protection and mobilize the community around child protection issues, Develop Community Action Plan (CAP)Activity 2: Capacity strengthening for community-level child protection actors such as traditional and religious leaders, youth groups (boys and girls), women’s rights organizations, child clubs on CP risks, gender equality and harmful social norms and practices, existing referral and reporting mechanisms.Activity 3: Support community-level child protection actors through financial and material support such as IEC materials including COVID 19 awareness to develop action plans to raise community awareness on CP risks (including trafficking, child marriage, child labor), Community Based Complaint Mechanism will also be considered as an important factorActivity 4: Provide child and adolescent-friendly awareness raising through activities such as campaigns, workshops, events, radio, and social media on child protection and SGBV risks, information on consequences of child marriage, available multi-sectoral services, and reporting mechanismsActivity 5: Organize age and gender-sensitive life skills sessions for children and adolescent boys and girls, including adolescent parents and married girls and other vulnerable groups, on topics such as CP risks, SRHR, Menstrual hygiene management and support trained children and adolescents to be role models and sensitize their peers,Covid measure will be followed at every stageActivity 6: Establish/Refurbish and equip safe spaces where children and adolescents can access structured and non -structured PSS, PFA from trained facilitators and seek CP servicesActivity 7: Update and strengthen local child protection referral pathways including multi-sectoral services for child survivors, Orientation for ensuring Accountability to Affected Populations will take place so that they feel comfortable and safe providing feedbackActivity 8: Case management services to at-risk children and children who have experienced protection incidents, child laborers, child survivors of SGBV, unaccompanied and separated children, and conduct referrals to multi-sectoral services. Where relevant, BIP and BID will be carried out in collaboration with local authoritiesActivity 9: Provide Cash Voucher Assistance and NFI support (including volunteer remuneration) to selected families whose child are included in case management as well as other vulnerable households with children and adolescents to meet their basic needs, access services both regular and crisis period created by natural disaster and prevent negative coping mechanisms such as child marriageActivity 10: Provide structured PSS and PFA to children and adolescents through home-based support and community-based activities including in safe spacesActivity 11: SGBV awareness session for adolescent girls focusing on crisis period created by natural disaster/DRR, further emergency like fireActivity 12: Provide positive parenting sessions to female and male caregivers of vulnerable Children and Youth including foster parents and parents of children at risk of child marriageActivity 13: Capacity strengthening initiatives for case management staffs, CBCP staff and community volunteer.Activity 14: Conduct A participatory research on social cohesion, Trust-building activities, Workshop with duty bearers (govt.officials, local authority), community leaders, parents, teachers and religious leaders in both community on developing a basic understanding of everyday peace, social cohesion.Activity 15: Establish, train,support women and girls led organizations in host and camps to raise awareness on social cohesion, Gender equality, inclusion of Persons with disability and peace building
