Project Summary

Humanitarian needs within proposed project locations of Masyaf and Salamieh Districts of Hama governorate continue to be high and complex. A key driver of vulnerability for both displaced and resident households, as well as returnees, is limited and stalled income levels leading to negative coping mechanisms and tenuous food security levels. These economic impacts exacerbate hardships and increase humanitarian needs in the food sector; thousands of new families have been rendered vulnerable and need aid. Loss of hope, dignity, and social cohesion are emerging risks that threaten any future stability. Youths, women and children face acute vulnerability due to a reduction in basic social services, and negative coping strategies include: reducing spending on NFIs to meet food needs, taking-on debts/loans, skipping payments of debts/rent to support other needs expenditures and selling productive assets.
Humanitarian programming needs to actively balance between meeting immediate humanitarian needs, while simultaneously contributing to local resilience and transitioning off exclusively humanitarian assistance in order to obtain a decent standard of living. Helping community members earn a living is therefore a key pillar of any intervention in the current Syrian context.
Community-based Cash for Work (CB-CfW) programming represents an innovative modality for cash programming inside Syria. First piloted by AKF in 2017, CB-CfW is a joined-up approach which places community organizations at the center of design and implementation of humanitarian programming while at the same time providing temporary livelihood opportunities to vulnerable HHs able to work.
AKF has been implementing Community-Based Cash for Work (CB-C4W) projects for six years. Through this project, AKF’s extensive experience with various CB-C4W projects will be expanded to address required social services in Masyaf and Salamieh districts. The main objective is to sustain and recover economic livelihoods of crisis-affected people especially women and youth through:
- Providing emergency employment through CB-C4W activities for 2000 individuals, of which 60%, 80% and 4% will be females, youths and PwDs, respectively.
- Implementing 10 CB-C4W projects which aim at improving basic social services delivered to the population within Masyaf and Salamieh Districts.
- Strengthening the civic capacity in the delivery of public services by involving local communities and Local Non-Governmental Organizations (LNGOs) in planning, implementing and monitoring the CB-C4W projects.
Each direct beneficiary household will receive monthly cash estimated at 60,000 SYP. Cash transfers will be provided through AKF’s cash transfer platform via the First Micro-Finance Institution (FMFI). CB-C4W projects and lists of beneficiaries will be informed by the Project Steering Committee (PSC) and community organizations (e.g. LNGOs).

4 - Needs

9.8 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance with at least 69% of people living below $1.90 a day as per the Food Security and Agriculture Sector HNO 2020. An estimated 7.90 million people in Syria are food insecure and a further 1.9 million are at risk of food insecurity. The main drivers to food insecurity continue to be conflict and protracted displacement, loss or lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity and economic sanctions on the country. This has resulted in limited physical and financial access to food, soaring prices and inflation, contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous livelihood depletion for the most vulnerable (HNO 2020).The HNO 2020 indicates that around 70% of the assessed households reported that food accounted for more than 65% of their expenditure, over 37% of food insecure households reported having outstanding debt and 57% of food insecure people had adopted negative coping strategies. Based on AKF’s internal study, in Salamieh district alone, 80% of HHs minimized expenditure on non-food items; 51% skipped payments for rent or debts; and 48% took out more loans and fell further into debt to afford food and medical care. In addition, 28% of HHs took-on jobs that are considered dangerous e.g. harvesting in areas known to have mines to support their households. The cost of living has significantly increased over the last year as the prices of commodities and services have more than doubled, while the average income remained the same, making it even more difficult for vulnerable families to afford basic needs. Female headed households are mostly vulnerable due to female high unemployment rate, and high dependency on social welfare. HHs exhausted economy is further complicating elderly and PwD health situation, as they cannot afford medicine.While there is a lack of in-depth information on Masyaf district, the 2020 HNO indicates that negative coping strategies is a major problem across the country

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will provide wage-earning employment over the course of three months to 2,000 vulnerable individuals, of which 60% of females, 80% of youth, 4% of PWDs, and 30% of IDPs and returnees will be targeted. Each beneficiary/laborer will receive a monthly cash/wage estimated at 60,000 SYP that will be transferred to the beneficiaries’ bank account opened at AKF’s financial service provider the First MicroFinance Institution (FMFI). The project will be implemented primarily by LNGOs and CBOs who will be responsible of assessing communities’ needs, designing corresponding CB-C4W activities and identifying target groups, and implementing CB-C4W projects. AKF will provide intensive training to the LNGOs and CBOs on identifying community needs, designing quick impact project, gender equality, and management of CB-C4W projects. In addition, AKF will provide management and oversight during implementation of CB-C4W projects in the target districts. Direct beneficiaries will be carefully identified in close coordination with LNGOs/CBOs who will generate the list of beneficiaries to be verified by an elected Project Steering Committee (PSC) based on clear criteria with a focus on women, youth, PwDs and IDPs and returnees. Process and outcome monitoring activities for the project will be implemented by AKF directly and include baseline and end-line assessments, labour market price analysis, ongoing feedback with project beneficiaries, post-project monitoring and Focus Group Discussions with project stakeholders. The project adopts a gender- and protection-sensitive approach based on AKF’s Do No Harm and safeguarding policies. In addition, AKF’s local, community-based approach, integrates conflict sensitive programming to actively recognize and mitigate the unintended consequences of targeting strategies, sectarian fragilities, promote transparency and ensure wide inclusive community participation to include the vulnerable groups of women, youth, PwD, and elderly.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A: Workers may be subject to discrimination by residents of neighborhoods targeted by CB-CFW due to negative cultural perception associated with some types of work such as garbage collectors. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Low. To mitigate such risk, Community organizations will expand awareness of work type among targeted communities to identify vulnerable individuals who are willing to undertake such type of work. Regular and confidential monitoring and compliant reporting system will help to identify and investigate any cases of exploitations and take actions against it. B: Gender related cultural and traditional norms and roles may limit ability of women to participate in some types of work such as in solid waste collection projects. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Female trainees which are selected will be asked preference of specific work/task in order to ensure their comfort. AKF will maintain regular monitoring of women contribution along with active complaint reporting system, and facilitate gender equality awareness raising to facilitate positive interaction and appreciation of female participants. Teams will also be established with mixed genders, with female leaders for each team. C: Increased risk of inter-community tension where CBOs members might be criticized or accused of bias in selecting the beneficiaries, Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium. AKF will communicate the project objectives and targeting approach and criteria, oversee the selection process and ensure a precise implementation based on the selection criteria. D: GBV and job associated hazards. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Workers will be provided with protective equipment. Women were assigned to work in locations that are close to their place of residence, which would enable them to be familiar with the surrounding environment, and ensure easy access; consequently, minimizes the risk of gender-based violence, reduces time burdens, and facilitates family care responsibilities.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: The deterioration of the security situation limiting movement. Mitigation: In program areas is deemed a high risk, AKF will delay implementation of activities until the situation is able to be reassessed and determined safe for travel. Risk2: Limited availability of quality goods, services, equipment and difficulties experienced in procurement. AKF has extensive experience undertaking programmes in extremely difficult places. AKF will seek to source from local suppliers and when required expand procurement to within the Governorates. Risk3: Exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitor exchange rate gains and losses to consider the impact of fluctuations on the program and communicate with donor as needed. Risk4: The transfer of funds to implementing unit delayed due to modification in banking regulations. Mitigation: AKF in collaboration with AKF’s head office in Geneva regularly monitor transfers. The impact of prolonged transfers will be mitigated by using bridge financial resources to ensure minimal disruption to program implementation. Risk5: Syrian Pound (SYP) has experiencing high rates of inflation. Mitigation: Inflation risk is mitigated by managing the amount of project liquid cash residing in the country. AKF manage reserve amounts by taking into consideration future cash flow needs to ensure cash on hand in the field is minimally exposed to inflation. Risk6: Significant influx of IDPs and/or the vulnerability of resident populations increasing AKF’s Humanitarian programme continually adjusts its delivery plan to cope with the ebbs and flows of IDP movements, and when the circumstances of particular villages change. WFP supplements food baskets when IDP flows are large, to ensure a social safety net for IDPs and host families.
