Project Summary

Global Communities with its partner have conducted a remote rapid assessment to better understand the needs and wants of the communities that will beneﬁt from the suggested WASH interventions. The project activities and the needs were discussed closely and agreed with the partner (Al Quds Society for Developing Al Mawasi) following their rapid needs assessment that was carried out with the targeted municipalities, local committees, and other CBOs/professionals/service providers in the governorate as pertaining to the affected private/ domestic wells, their distribution, and numbers including critical needs. The Gaza Strip is battling an epidemic with almost no resources. For the ﬁrst time, Covid-19 cases have now been detected in the community. Gaza’s existing deep and protracted humanitarian crisis now threatens to intensify rapidly, jeopardizing the health and safety of two million people. According to the Ministry of Health ﬁgures, 697 cases have been identiﬁed, over 500 in the last week alone and four Gazans have already died.
According to the state of emergency declared by the PWA/ Palestinian Authority after conﬁrmed cases of Coronavirus in the Gaza Strip, a set of measures have been recommended, mainly the adoption of appropriate hygiene practices to combat the outbreak of the disease and to ensure access to safe and good quality water following the interruption of services and power cut off. In such a situation, the project will focus on providing access to water by the provision of chlorination systems will also be supporting the most vulnerable and who are in need of hygiene kits at the household level, construction, and rehabilitation of WASH facilities in Schools and HCFs (handwashing, latrines, sewer system) and lastly we will pilot new techniques at the targeted communities to improve water access by installing ﬁlters and ﬁlling points that will facilitate safe access to water produced at the station in the selected ﬁve localities in Khan-Younis.

1 - Needs

The rise of WASH sector challenges and limitations in managing the scarcity of quality water coupled with the easy access, Covid-19 cases in the general population of the Gaza Strip comes alongside the shutdown of Gaza's sole power plant on 18 August 2020 to put further limitations and pressure on the local communities across the Gaza Strip throughout protracted access to water limitations and the water quality control. Accordingly, one of the most prominent needs was the provision of quality water to various marginalized and amongst the most vulnerable communities with potable water that ensures cleanse as well as safe accesses, especially for women, children, and PwD. Moreover, it was highly emphasized by various stakeholders, which conforms with the recent WASH sector survey/needs assessments for the rehabilitation and upgrading of the health WASH facilities at schools and Health Care Facilities (HCFs). This shall guarantee the supply of clean handwashing taps, useable latrines, and an intact sewer system. This was flagged with emphasis to rehabilitate and adapt the access to those facilities, which provide safer and easy access for the PwD. Following the most recent escalation in hostilities between Gaza and Israel, Israeli authorities suspended fuel supplies to Gaza and closed the Kerem Shalom goods crossing, the Strip’s essential ‘lifeline’. According to the Gaza Energy Authority, the recent power plant shutdown increased the Strip’s electricity deﬁcit to 75 percent, left Gazans sweltering in the summer heat, at home in lock-down. Due to COVID 19 outbreak mitigation measures; around 60% of WASH service providers were not able to collect their service fees; therefore, their capacities in maintaining WASH services for their beneﬁciaries decreased dramatically; moreover, most of these service providers exhausted their capacities in responding to the COVID 19 outbreak. Furthermore, the virus is creating additional challenges the vulnerable WASH service providers are facing; according to PWA updates, many of these service providers do not have access to suﬃcient disinfection materials and PPE tools. In regard to determining the locations and the interventions, Global Communities has worked closely with the WASH cluster members to ensure no overlapping with other interventions and that the suggested locations are considered a high priority for water interventions, ﬁt with their objectives and strategies. The WASH cluster objectives prioritize interventions focused on the Red Zone areas in Gaza. The strategic focus is to Support the local institutions, vulnerable communities’ response measures against COVID 19 outbreak, to decrease the morbidity and mortality associated with the pandemic. Hence, it has been agreed that Global Communities in partnership with Al Quds Society for Developing Al Mawasi to carry out the suggested interventions in the communities presented in the project description and below. It should be noted that the presented activities have been reviewed in detail with the WASH cluster coordinators, the partner, the CMWU, and the PWA to ensure that the suggested interventions are considered a priority and that they do not overlap with other organizations. Based on their feedback, there have not been any objections to the targeted communities, the activities, or the beneficiaries. Once the funding is secured, Global Communities and its partner will commence with the implementation and mobilize their networks to ensure full coordination between the involved institutions.

2 - Activities

For the activities, Global Communities with its partner will work together on designing and implementing the presented interventions. Our focus is to provide access to water, governance of the WASH sector, access to sanitation and hygiene, and access to WASH services in institutions (Schools & HCFs) in Khan-Younis and North Gaza localities. In regard to access to water, GC and its partner proposed the provision of chlorination systems in Khan-Yonis localities (Albatn Alsamin, Qiza nan Najjar, Al-Salam, Al-Shaykh Nasir and Qa’al Qurein). GC will work on the installation of a sterilizing system of a complete chlorine dozing unit - tank and pump for the targeted wells. For access to sanitation and hygiene, GC will work on the provision of household hygiene kits for the most vulnerable in the targeted communities. The number of beneﬁciaries has been calculated based on the average family size (5.3) and represents a third of the total population in each locality. These kits will improve the health standards of different age and gender groups; in addition, there will be a positive impact on public health status and the environment. Men will be encouraged to take on roles in safe water handling and protecting the hygiene of their community in order to reduce the burden on women and girls. Targeted localities for these activities in Khan-Yonis are (Al-Salam, Albatn Alsamin, Al-Shayk Nasir, Alstar, Qizan an Najjar, Qa’al Qurein and Maan). We will be engaging youth volunteers to support us in disseminating the kits which will minimize the required budget and will support better local engagement. Concerning the access to WASH services in institutions, we will be focusing on supporting schools and HCFs in North Gaza and Khan-Younis. We will be working on the construction/ rehabilitation of WAHS facilities (handwashing, latrines, sewer system) in schools in North Gaza (ابو عبيدة بن الجراح, حليمة السعدية, مهدية الشوا الثانوية للبنين, زينب الوزير, عوني الحرثاني الثانوية, انس بن مالك, الشيماء الاساسية), and in Khan-Younis (الخنساء الاساسية للبنات, المتنبي الثانوية, المعري ,القرارة). In regard to HCFs; GC and its partner will work on the construction/ rehabilitation of WASH facilities (handwashing, latrines, sewer system) in Khan-Younis (Albatn Alsamin, Al-Shayk Nasir, Alstar, Qizan an Najjar and Maan). Our focus is to upgrade, rehabilitate, and/or construct targeted schools WASH facilities. In order to do this, GC will undertake in coordination with the local partner a thorough technical evaluation and examination of the facilities' internal and external water network and facilities, with a focus on handwashing, latrines, and sewer system functionalities and needs. This will allow us to identify the type of intervention for each school and HCF. Accordingly, GC will rehabilitate and or construct the facilities for each school and HCF as per the evaluation findings, priority, and urgent needs. This intervention aims at ensuring the provision of high quality of sanitation water, improving the hygiene of the facilities in proportion to the number of students and patients at each school and HCF, respectively. Moreover, the facilities will also be adapted to suit the needs of people with disabilities (PwDs), especially those with physical disabilities. Safety access for all age and sex to the WASH facilities will also be assured. Furthermore, GC will work on the creation - if technically possible - of a system for water washing water reuse for irrigation. This will supply the schools and HCFS garden near the facilities –if available- with usable irrigation water in order to provide a green, clean and comfortable environment for the students and patients as well as to minimize the waste of water Lastly, GC and its partner will be piloting a new technique to improve access to WASH services in Gaza as part of the governance of the WASH sector. Our intervention will focus on the installation of filters and filling points as an innovative solution (2000+5000). GC will establish filling points at locations close to the population centers, which will provide easier access to potable water and primarily serving and facilitating the life of women and young children as they commuting water to their houses. Doing so would also ensure safety to women and minimize possible harassment while filling water at the crowded main stations. The filling points will be linked with the established water desalination stations developed by CMWU. The filling points will facilitate safer access to water produced at the stations and reduces the overcrowding around the desalination plants. Moreover, the construction of those community potable water filling points would ensure safer access within a reasonable walking distance for the women and children. Doing so, besides the safety and access privilege to potable water would minimize the cost of procuring water and hence save hundreds of HH the money for other life basic use. For this pilot, we will be focusing on Khan-Younis locality (Albatn Alsamin, Al’amal, Alstar, Jawrat Alluwt, and Maan).

3 - Indicators

Global Communities has extensive experience in monitoring WASH programming activities. The Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) Plan outlines the anticipated results and the tools and mechanisms through which Global Communities and implementing partners will monitor and evaluate progress over the life of the program. This illustrative plan will provide information about the overall approach to performance monitoring, a description of the proposed methods for collecting, analyzing, and reporting high-quality program data. Global Communities’ proposed plan will ensure that gender and youth are fully integrated into the MEAL system. Further, an illustrative learning and adaptive management plan will be put in place to promote stakeholder collaboration and capitalize on best practices and lessons learned. Monthly written reports will be submitted by the partner to the Global Communities will be involved in developing both the interim and ﬁnal reports to the donor. In order to assess the appropriateness and effectiveness of interventions in line with the overarching goal, Global Communities has designed a holistic MEAL approach that employs rigorous quantitative and qualitative methods to inform an array of performance indicators that include: index/composite indicators, indirect/proxy indicators, complexity-aware monitoring, standard foreign assistance indicators, as well as several custom indicators to monitor the program’s success in designing and implementing an inclusive approach to achieving sustainable WASH outcomes. Regular ﬁeld visits and on-going monitoring will be conducted during the implementation of activities and will be veriﬁed through team progress reports, in addition to partner's daily and weekly reports. In regards to the supplies, they will be purchased directly by Global Communities based on the list reviewed and shared by WASH Cluster. The installation work will be carried out by contractors that will be supervised by Global Communities and its partner site engineers on a daily basis in the ﬁeld. Further, Global Communities will monitor the project activities on a regular basis to ensure that the needed equipment and materials are delivered as planned. In addition. Global Communities will closely coordinate with the Wash Cluster and other stakeholders to ensure no overlapping occurs and enhance coordination with other relevant agencies working on similar interventions. Global Communities implements projects with strict social accountability measures in involving beneficiaries in needs assessment; announcing the project using website and workshops, grievance/ complaint system, pre, and post-evaluation, monitoring and evaluation, and focus group discussions during implementation to discuss possible deficiencies/gaps in the quality of services and challenges faced, as well as opportunities for improvement. Global Communities engages, as part of the accountability process, stakeholders including women and heads of household beneficiaries in transparent dialogues about the project’s results and impact on their lives from health, economic, and social perspectives. Also, GC adopts four principles of social accountability including transparency, feedback, participation, and monitoring and effectiveness. That said, a strict complaint mechanism s put in place allows beneficiaries to directly and through partner organization to file complaints and express concern regarding the provided services and the level of satisfaction along with any allegation pertaining to the various aspect of the project implementation.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project will ensure the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries by ensuring the availability of a safe water supply and creating a clean living environment in the targeted locations. The project services will be allocated based on the number of beneficiaries. Target beneficiaries will be informed of the project objectives and its impact on the allocated quantities of water to be supplied to their respective areas Inaddition, areas supplied by the selected wells are known to be highly vulnerable and groups like people with disabilities, the elderly and women with young children will highly benefit due to minimizing the burden of buying and attaining un-piped water. Global Communities has a specific security manual that includes safety and security guidance for working in Palestine and details the processes, procedures, and risk mitigation measures staff are required to take to ensure their and beneficiaries’ safety and security. All staff expected to work in or travel to the targeted locations will receivesecurity training from Global Community Operations staff. We have established coordination and security protocols working closely with Global Communities DCbased international security team and other organizations, such as UNRWA, on an ongoing basis to understand changes to the security and operational context in West- Bank and advise staff accordingly, including in real-time as needed. In the event of a security incident that affects the project, Global Communities will work closely with Wash Cluster, international and local entities such as the UN, OCHA, AIDA, and local partners to respond accordingly.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Global Communities understands the interlinkage in human rights and that social inclusion stretches beyond focusing on the direct needs of marginalized groups. Promoting the rights to access safe drinking water and sanitation, directly impact the rights of education, health, women‘s right, child rights, and the rights of People with Disabilities (PwDs). The unserved and underserved communities in Palestine fall under three main social inclusion barriers; Attitudinal: stigmatization andmisinformation including the different roles between men and women, which negatively impacts the decision-making process. Environmental: issues in accessing infrastructures and facilities and lack of communications. Institutional/ organizational: lack of policies, strategies, skills, and information to support social inclusion. While designing the technical intervention, GC was committed to using aparticipatory approach and non-discrimination principles to empower the marginalized by addressing WASH barriers. To ensure successful intervention, cultural sensitivity matters were taken into consideration and were incorporated in the monitoring and evolution plan so that unforeseen obstacles can be dealt with in an effective and efficient manner. Participation of stakeholders at the appropriate time during the program cycle is imminent; it enhances ownership, power relationships, accessibility, accountability, and sustainability. GC ensured that identified WASH stakeholders in general and beneficiaries in specific are fully engaged in the program design. Further, GC is committed to disseminating information to all stakeholders throughout the program to ensure informed decisions are adopted. Root causes of GBV are directly related to conflict with Israel and internal division. Generally speaking, the available services and capacity of service providers remain limited in all domains, including water as a vital and significant sector to supply potable water with quality that ensures safe access to it. A small percentage of women and children GBV survivors sought systematic support in Gaza. This disturbing ratio is attributed to survivors’ limited knowledge of available services, limited access to confidential and compassionate services including water. Additionally, humanitarian activities and services focused mainly on managing stress and life skills. Few addressed GBV case management, access and right of basic services including shelter/ food/WASH services, and social, environmental, and economic enablement. The project will announce the startup of the activities /kick off through a community meeting with all targeted localities by providing a full explanation about the project's activities. Conduct a participatory assessment that considers the various stages of project implementation and ensures that communities are well engaged and are participating at all stages through clear stakeholder analysis, enlisting the necessary support for project implementation, identifying other needs, and linking them to project activities. The project team will carry out a series of community engagement activities that include but is not limited to: •Providing citizens, especially women and boys with balanced information that help them understand the project intervention vis a vis problem, alternatives, and solutions•Collecting feedback from stakeholders / local community about the project and its objectives and analyzing the problem/problems, developments, and achievement in different project implementation stages with the close support of the local partner.• Work with the community directly and on an ongoing basis, ensuring that their concerns are understood, taken into account, and addressed•Partnership with stakeholders / local community in the aspects of the decision, including the development of alternatives/options, priorities, and preferred solutionsEnsuring that decisions and implementation are based on the local contribution & presented feedback.
