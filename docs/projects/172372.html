Project Summary

The project will be implemented under the Food Security Sector in liaison and coordination with the protection sector, especially when identifying the most vulnerable households for proper targeting to avoid doing harm to beneficiaries (such as Gender Based Violence). It will also be implemented in close collaboration with the coordination and common services sector for proper targeting, coverage of needs and transportation of agricultural inputs to target locations.

1 - Needs

Continuous fighting and instability across the country, along with the COVID-19 restrictions to curb the spread of the virus – such as closing air, land and sea borders, imposing curfews and social distancing measures – have had a significant negative impact on all components of food security. COVID-19 and insecurity has affected the availability of food, as well as its cost, with many basic food items doubling in price since the onset of the pandemic. This has been compounded by increased unemployment and underemployment, including for those engaged in the agricultural sector, especially in the southern and western regions. Nearly twice as many households engaged in food production have abandoned these activities due to the deteriorating situation COVID-19 that has also disrupted livelihoods and led to food shortages for many households, resulting in families adopting negative coping mechanisms. Although agriculture contributed less than 3 percent to GDP in 2011 (last information available), over one-fifth of the population is engaged in agricultural activities, often producing crops only for household consumption. Those households engaged in food production and the agriculture sector increasingly abandoned agricultural activities due to the deteriorating situation. Agricultural productivity in the last 12 months has been affected by insecurity, scarcity of water and fuel, as well as higher-priced agricultural inputs and constraints on labour due to COVID-19. Among households that were engaged in the sector, around 45,000 households have abandoned agricultural activity in the last 12 months, compared to 15,000 households that were estimated to have abandoned agricultural activities in 2019. This also impacts on food production in Libya, particularly in the southern region, which remains extremely important for the country’s agricultural production and, due to its geographical position, hosts many migrants and displaced people, many of whom are employed in this sector. In addition to supporting household food security and protecting their livelihoods, restoring agricultural productivity is crucial to longer term food security and sustainability and building communal and national resilience against future shocks.

a. Rapid needs assessments b. Distribution of cereals (wheat and sorghum) seed kits (improved seeds, farming tools and training on Good Agriculture Practices “GAP”) c. Distribution of vegetables kits (improved seeds, tools and training on GAP) d.Training in crop production improved methodologies/technologies e.Training & Training of trainers to personnel from the Ministry of Agriculture on pest management and control of plant diseases (ToTs and Workshops).
