Project Summary

The situation in most of Somalia is highly variable. Women and young girls are still exposed to continue protection risks or other forms of exploitation and abuse, due to several social and cultural factors exacerbated by displacement and lack of resources generalized insecurity and limited access to support networks, in particular, lack of specialized service providers addressing HLP issues and lack of information regarding available services prevent even the most basic support for vulnerable IDPs and the host population. Moreover, there are still gaps in preventing and responding to HLP issues along with the limited availability of national skilled human resources with the required knowledge and background. Access to areas where populations are known to be in severe distress is highly restricted. In this scenario, the role of national NGOs like NoFYL, who has the broadest and deepest reach and can access difficult areas, is crucial.
Protection, monitoring and response mechanisms with regards to land, housing and property is lacking in most of the protection programming. Most often legal Aid services, land, houses and property rights are not prioritized hence leaving big gaps in the protection response mechanism. There is poor coordination among protection and response service providers, especially on land, housing, and property rights. Furthermore, the protection response mechanisms have limited or no provision of free legal aid services to IDPs, survivors of GBV and HLP.
Therefore, this project seeks to improve and strengthen coordination among service providers and to enhance protection and response mechanism for IDPs on land, housing and property rights. These will be achieved through eviction monitoring, prevention and response services, improved referral pathways, on spot monitoring and response to assist IDPs to reclaim housing, land and properties, awareness-raising, free legal aid, and innovative and sustainable alternative dispute resolution mechanisms. The project will ensure individuals affected by displacement receive support assistance to improve their tenure security situation and address post-eviction complications including enhanced capacity to Prevention and response to evictions.
During the needs assessment phase, data will be disaggregated by gender, and by age. NoFYL will collect gender disaggregated data by sex and age of relevant data related to the project activities and ensure that all segments of the affected population including children and adolescents at risk, female HHs, elderly, disabled, have equal access to project services and that targeted support to advance gender equality is based on a gender analysis. The programme will meet the distinct needs of women, men, girls and boys. NoFYL shall apply gender segregated community based activities at the safe spaces enabling women and girls to address their specific concerns, priorities their needs and appropriately inform the development of proposed activities.
Cognizant to promote equity, NoFYL shall endeavor to provide equal chance, inclusiveness and participation of minority members of the community as well as disabled groups in the locations and entrench transparency in recruitment of beneficiaries, capacity building, empowerment and resource sharing to achieve a comprehensive community engagement. Active participation of individuals with special needs will be pursued to identify, support and strengthen the coping mechanisms that beneficiaries often create to protect themselves against threats to their safety and dignity, and also foster beneficiaries’ self-esteem and development of skills. Case workers selection, orientations and will also consider gender sensitivity including both men and women. All this will ensure gender issues and equity are addressed as well as offer services as per the community acceptable practices.

Project Activities

Activity 1.1.1: Advocacy and awareness campaigns on HLP issues in target locations to mitigate eviction risks through information sharing targeting 200,000 individuals. Provision of information services will be designed to assist eviction victims access services, navigate complex procedures, and utilize existing remedies in areas of displacement to enhance their own protection. People who lack an understanding of property rights and the legal framework are more vulnerable to the impact of the eviction and to the possibility of losing access to land. Pre-eviction campaigns that provide information on land rights to all members of the community can help reduce this vulnerability. People need to understand the risks of evictions, what the government will do to help reduce these risks, how land disputes will be resolved, and what the landholder can do to maintain rights to land. Activity 1.1.2: Establishment of mobile legal aid clinics for counseling and legal assistant services: Through Lawyers and paralegals NoFYL will establish one (1) legal aid clinic in each of the project locations for the provision of legal advice, counseling, and litigation for cases related to tenure insecurity: The paralegals will facilitate negotiations on tenure security since evictions are highly due to tenancy agreements are either not in place or are not properly negotiated. Counseling will entail the provision of one-to-one advice to displacement affected persons in target locations on how to overcome obstacles to durable solutions, and local remedies that are available to assist them address protection concerns with which they are faced, including forced eviction and other tenure insecurity issues. Legal assistance will be centered on community-based dispute resolution, collaborative dispute resolution, and legal action through the formal court process. NoFYL will assist and provide legal services to about 2500 IDPs and other affected persons through mobile legal clinics. Activity 1.1.3: HLP Prevention and Response Services through protection of IDPs against forced eviction with a focus on sites at high risk of evictions in target sites and beyond. In target locations, small-scale evictions occur on a daily basis and IDPs constitute the largest and most vulnerable of those affected. These evictions are often carried out by means of intimidation, force and violence, including physical assault and destruction of HLP assets, and without an adequate notice period that would have prevented destruction of assets and allowed the victims to relocate with dignity. NoFYL will intervene to prevent eviction of IDPs from their sites. NoFYL will intervene upon alerts received of imminent eviction threats against IDP sites, or in sites assessed to be at high risk of eviction. Prevention interventions will include: negotiating with landowners, gatekeepers or local authorities for the continuation of tenure by the IDPs, with the support of District Commissioners, negotiation of extended notice, consultation on alternatives, alternative accommodations and non-discrimination. Activity 1.1.4: Assessment, monitoring, documentation and reporting threats of eviction and forced evictions by landlords, authorities and settlement managers, the assessment will also cover land tenure use, the status of rental agreements, and forced evictions, assessment to be conducted by trained HLP monitors. This activity involves undertaking a rapid assessment of the current ownership and tenure rights of IDP communities in target locations, and a detailed study of property state, legal documentation, and tenure rights in the case and/or to motivate a return to areas of origin once the conflict has ceased.

Mitigation Measures

1- Preventing risks of beneficiaries not being informed. Develop and widely disseminate the referral networks and ensuring service mapping are regularly conducted and referral networks updated. Enhanced community outreach and awareness campaigns around access and availability of services. Continuously assess and closely monitor the evolvement of the security and accessibility situation in the target areas, and have the flexibility to quickly adapt the implementation methodology as needed in order to guarantee the achievement of the project objective and the most effective impact. In this regard, NoFYL will maintain regular information flow and constantly consult with the cluster leads and donor. On the staff skills continuously train, coach and follow up the frontline workers to ensure timely and quality services is provided to beneficiaries. 2- Preventing risks of exclusion based on disability, gender and age. The proposed intervention has been designed in a way to enable beneficiaries to fully access and enjoy their rights, safety and dignity, while at the same time avoiding harm. All project activities will be carried out in accordance with AGDM principles: equal participation of women, girls, boys and men will be integral part of the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the overall project. NoFYL will ensure that key cross-cutting issues are mainstreamed into the proposed intervention including age, gender, diversity, environment and human rights. Gender will remain a key focus, guaranteeing consultation with female beneficiaries when undertaking protection assessments through NoFYL female staff accessing them and identifying their needs. 3- Preventing risks of community not using available complaints and feedback mechanisms. Ensure all beneficiaries are informed of the available complaints and feedback channels. Ensure CFM mechanisms are easy to use and accessible. Ensure complaints and feedback provided are acted upon and feedback provided to the community.

Please provide further explanation

Sustainability of the project will be ensured through community ownership of the project. For all activities reaching the communities, IDP community leaders and host community leaders, including women and youth leaders will be consulted in order to adapt the activity to the context and ensured community participation, Where possible community members will be encourage to volunteer and participate in the project. Collaboration with both host community and IDPs will ensure the project adhere to do no harm principles. Using community participation leverage will ensure a longer term impact of the project. Establishment of community structures and its capacity building aims at ensuring the community focal persons have capacity to continue support referrals from the communities to other service providers beyond the project durations, established service mapping will also provide a long term network between the communities and service providers thus ensuring sustainability.
