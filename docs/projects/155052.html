Project Summary

Across 2019, this project will address the educational needs of Rohingya Refugees in Camps 1E, 1W, 2E, 2W, 3, 4, 5 and 17. Current figures show that cross these camps, over 65,000 children aged 3-14 are out of education. Furthermore, there is a substantial shortage of appropriate learning facilities across these camps with 781 learning facilities needing to be established.
Mukti Cox's Bazar will address these critical gaps in education in refugee camps by constructing safe and equipped learning centres with gender-sensitive WASH facilities and training teachers, community social workers and community advisors - allowing children with the opportunities to receive basic education and life skills training and youths to receive life skills and vocational training. Home-based training will be delivered for youths with restricted mobility (those with disabilities and caregiving obligations). This intervention will improve learning outcomes and enhance the life opportunities of a total of refugee children and youths in camps.
Furthermore, refugee parents and caregivers will receive basic literacy, numeracy and parenting training in learning centres and through home-based learning for groups with restricted mobility, enhancing their capacity to support their children’s education.

Needs

Latest figures on education gaps in refugee camps show that over 65,000 children aged 3-14 in Camps 1E, 1W, 2E, 2W, 3, 4, 5 and 17 are out of education, with insufficient learning facilities to attend and receive basic education and life skills by a combined shortage of 781 learning facilities. Mukti will address these critical gaps in education in refugee camps by constructing safe and equipped learning centres with gender-sensitive WASH facilities and training teachers, community social workers and community advisors - allowing children with the opportunities to receive basic education and life skills training and youths to receive life skills and vocational training. Home-based training will be delivered for youths with restricted mobility (those with disabilities and care giving obligations). This intervention will improve learning outcomes and enhance the life opportunities of a total of refugee children and youths in camps. Furthermore, refugee parents and caregivers will receive basic literacy, numeracy and parenting training in learning centres and through home-based learning for groups with restricted mobility, enhancing their capacity to support their children’s education. This programme will address the needs of children, youth, and parents/caregivers in camps by incorporating participatory approaches, beneficiary feedback mechanisms, and alternative delivery methods tailored towards persons with specific needs into programme design and implementation (as per Inter-Agency Network for Education in Emergencies (INEE) standards and Street Child's Accountability to Affected Populations Policy), ensuring a gender-sensitive and inclusive approach. An additional needs assessment will establish the exact needs of the different communities. This will be followed by regular assessments that assess whether the programme is meeting the population’s needs and promoting accountability to the affected populations. This will be complemented with regular monitoring of enrollment rates, retention, teacher and community social worker and advisor attendance and improvements in learning outcomes. The proposed programme prioritizes gender responsiveness in participant assessment and selection and programme activities. Participant assessment and selection will (i) dis-aggregate data according to age and gender (ii) identify gender discrimination or gendered barriers and gaps that place restrictions on movement and mobility, and integrate such findings into planning and implementation. Project implementation will (i) prioritize girls and young women assessed as at the greatest risk of remaining out of learning due to gendered barriers; and (ii) differentiate and adapt activities to address gendered barriers or gaps. The proposed programme prioritizes disability responsiveness by integrating the Washington Group Short Set Questionnaire into assessment and selection criteria, designed to addressed under-reporting of disabilities. Implementation will adopt alternative delivery methods tailored towards those with disabilities through home-based learning. Over the past 23 years Mukti has worked as a locally-rooted non-governmental organisation with strong community links in Cox’s Bazar. During the Rohingya crisis Mukti has partnered with UNICEF, VSO and Children on the Edge to build a proven track-record as a project deliverer. Mukti will receive technical support from Children on the Edge and Street Child. Children on the Edge (CotE) is an INGO experienced in working in post-conflict situations, providing basic needs and education for children. CotE has supported Mukti in building 75 Learning Centres. Street Child is an INGO that works in emergency contexts globally. Street Child has partnered with Mukti following requests from the Global Education Cluster to provide technical support in Cox’s Bazar. Mukti will benefit from additional technical support, with up to 6 staff embedded and seconded to Mukti from Street Child.

Activities or outputs

Output 1 - Increased provision of safe, protective and equipped Learning Centres and WASH facilities in refugee camps. Mukti will align with cross-sector standards in the construction of learning centres, ensuring infrastructure is accomodative to persons with disabilities and constructing gender-segregated WASH facilities to increase accessibility and enhance protection. In addition to hosting educational services learning spaces will serve a community function; host to a range of awareness raising and cultural events focussing on areas such as promoting social cohesion, and environmental and child protection awareness. Mukti will undertake regular community advocacy sessions in order to raise awareness about protection issues and the referral pathways that exist in order to address these issues. Output 2 - Teachers, community social workers and community advisors trained on basic education, life-skills, vocational training, and protection issues, to deliver comprehensive and inclusive learning for refugee children and youth. Half of teachers trained will be from host communities for the purpose of co-teaching in centres to promote social cohesion and cross-community dialogue. Participatory techniques (as per Inter-Agency Network for Education in Emergencies (INEE) toolkit) including focussed discussions and 'study circles' with adolescents and parents/caregivers in camps will be adopted to inform the development of teaching and learning materials to ensure context-appropriate vocational training and a cultural, gender and conflict-sensitive adolescent life skills curriculum and parenting course designed to strengthen gender equity, protection and environmental awareness as per cross-sectoral standards. During this training, teachers, community social workers and community advisors will be taught how to identify any child protection issues, how to respond to them and how to refer the issues to the appropriate body. Output 3 - Children enrolled in basic education and life skills training classes accessing life-saving, life-sustaining learning. A current imbalance in gender parity in enrolment will be addressed by a gender balance prioritizing a higher rate of female enrolment in order to achieve gender parity in learning. Output 4 - Youth enrolled in life skills training and vocational training- gaining knowledge, skills and income earning capacity. Community social workers will deliver the life skills training and the community advisor will deliver the vocational training, both in the Learning Centres. For those who are less able to travel to Learning Centres, community social workers and community advisors will work to deliver training home-based learning. Output 5 - Refugee parents and caregivers enrolled in basic literacy, numeracy, and parenting classes delivered by trained teachers and community social workers respectively, enabling parents to engage in their children’s learning. Community social workers will deliver the parenting classes in the Learning Centres - aimed at increasing knowledge of children's rights and gender-related issues within households and supporting parenting skills, and teachers will deliver the basic literacy and numeracy. For those who are less able to travel to Learning Centres, community social workers and community advisors will work to deliver training through home-based learning.

Key project/performance indicators

1 - 66 safe, protective Learning Centres constructed and equipped with sector level-specific teaching and learning materials and gender-sensitive WASH facilities constructed in accordance with sector standards. 2 - 132 teachers, 24 community social workers, and 24 community advisors trained (disaggregated by sex) to deliver the Learning Competency Framework & Approach (LCFA) basic education and life skills curricula, and tailored vocational training curriculum 3 - 5,280 refugee children (disaggregated by age and sex) completing basic education and life skills training (2,867 female 6-14 years of age; 2,413 male 6-14 years of age) 4 - 1,728 refugee youth (disaggregated by age and sex) completing vocational and life skills training in Learning Centres and through home-based learning (384 female 15-18 years of age, 384 male 15-18 years of age, 480 female 15-18 years of age, 480 male 15-18 years of age) 5 - 2,880 refugee parents and caregivers (disaggregated by sex) completing basic literacy, numeracy and parenting classes in Learning Centres and through home-based learning
