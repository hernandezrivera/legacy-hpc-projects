Project Summary

The Upper Nile and Jonglei states in South Sudan face severe challenges due to flash floods, communal conflicts, and the Sudan wars, which have displaced nearly one million people. Refugees and returnees have overwhelmed existing educational facilities, which were already strained by overcrowding and flooding in 2023 and 2024. Over 60% of schools are public, and 20% are community-run, but many have been submerged, destroyed, or rendered inaccessible.
Key impacts include:
Upper Nile State: Refugees and returnees have overcrowded learning spaces, with over 60% of schools affected. The most affected counties include Renk, Malakal, Fashoda and Nasir. Communal conflicts and flooding have displaced communities, leaving children without adequate learning facilities.
Jonglei State: Flooding in 2024 submerged dozens of schools, affecting tens of thousands of learners, especially in Fangak, Canal/Pigi, Twic East and Pibor. Communal conflicts in Pibor have further displaced communities, disrupting education.
The situation has led to significant educational challenges, especially for IDP and returnees’ children, including children with disabilities and their vulnerable families, who face disproportionate inaccessibility to safe education facilities. The country faces low teacher to pupil ratio of 93:1, affecting access and quality of learning. Besides, there is limited access to learning materials with textbook ratio of 8:1. Children with disabilities, girls and out of school children lack gender sensitive and inclusive opportunities to learn.
Community in Need Aid (CINA) proposes a response plan to address these issues by:
The project will benefit 61,956 (37,281 boys, 23,929 girls, 458 male adults, and 288 female adults). Establishing 12 temporary learning structures.
Training 615 (381M 234F) teachers and providing incentives to volunteers.
Supplying learning materials for 61,210 children (37,281 boys and 23,929 girls).
Installing 12 gender-responsive WASH facilities.
Offering psychosocial support and early childhood development (ECD) services for 9,606 (5763boys 3843girls) children aged 3-6.
Engaging 131 (77Male 54Female) School Management Committees to create supportive learning environments.
The project aims to ensure access to safe, inclusive and quality education in a conducive environment. CINA currently implements GESS program in Upper Nile (all counties) Jonglei and GPAA (all counties), a UNICEF - funded EiE project for returnees and refugees affected by Sudan Crises as well as Education Localization (funded by Save the Children coordinated through Education Cluster) project in Renk, Malakal and Fashoda Counties. This HNRP project will ensure that these education efforts are sustained and learning is not disrupted in 2025. CINA will support the OCHA flagship initiative, through a well-coordinated Area Based Leadership, ensuring that communities and local authorities are in the center of humanitarian response, especially in the pilot areas of Jonglei and Upper Nile (Nasir, Pibor, and other locations) where CINA currently implements SSHF funded projects (SA1 2024). The project will ensure that complementarities between humanitarian, development and peace building efforts are well aligned to promote lifesaving, resilience and development, harnessing hope and future for South Sudanese children.

1. Needs

According to Annual Education census conducted annually, 60% of the schools are public owned and 20% are community schools, with the impacts of flooding in Jonglei and Upper State, these pubic and community schools lack the support as majority of the populations are hosting displaced and returnee/refugees children from Sudan and Ethiopia negatively impacting on South Sudan only main source of income, “the oil sector” leading to many government responsibilities dwindled to either privates and humanitarians partners with the privates schools becoming expensive given the depreciation of the South Sudanese pound against the dollar that contributed in hyperinflation. The communal conflicts have taken toll in displacing communities such as the conflict in Nasir between the youth groups and SSPDF resulting to damages of communities’ livelihood and educational facilities; severely impacting learning. In Jonglei, the flash flood has displaced many communities more than any other year since the story of flooding in Fangak, IRNA conducted in Aug. 2024, reported that 25 villages and 30 schools hosting about 50,000 learners have been submerged by floods this year, leaving learners without learning facilities and with devastated livelihoods. In Canal/Pigi, similar report found that 10 Schools have been submerged; affecting learning of 15,000 returnees and host children learners. Twic East which is at the borderline with GPAA, has faced numerous communal conflicts including cattle raiding and child abduction. Many communities at the edge have been displaced to the interior villages due to fear of child abduction and cattle raiding leaving behind school facilities causing congesting in the classes receiving IDPs. The IRNA assessment reports recommended the need for provision of temporary learning spaces, distribution of learning and teaching materials, supporting teacher with incentives, providing psychosocial support and life skills for the youth and adolescents. Similarly, the Annual Education census report 2023 confirmed that 46% of the teachers only possessed secondary certificates and 36% have no certificates at all. This present a serious challenge to improve the quality of education. In Upper Nile State, Renk, Malakal and Fashoda have witnessed increased number of refugees/returnees and IDPs due to the conflict in Sudan and flooding respectively. Transit Centers (TCs) have been set up in Renk and Malakal to host returnees/refugees proceeding to their final locations. Families are expected to spend between one to two weeks in these centers but reports suggest that most families have stayed at the TCs for more than six months and yet there are not formal learning programs in these TCs. In Hai Makoon and Hai Salaam of Malakal Municipality, and IRNA conducted in Oct 2024 estimated that a population of 2,835 households displaced by flood from neighboring Counties and IDPs displaced from the Malakal PoC fighting are camped at the fuel station within Malakal Town. Nasir County has witnessed fierce fighting between armed youth and SSPDF numerous times this year leaving score of people death and causing massive displacement of people from Nasir town to the interior villages of Koat, Pandanyang, Kuetrengke, Nyetot, Dhuoreding, Wandow, Homkhor, Rok-Rok, Dhek-Dhek, Wech-deng, Chanchow,Ngueny, Luakwien and Ketbek seeking for their safety according to IRNA report conducted in Sep 2024. The IDP population that has been displaced to villages outside of Nasir town are temporarily camped in schools, churches, and open spaces under trees. Some are sharing temporary shelters provided by their hosts. This is a serious gap as most of the public amenities like schools are used as shelters driving the school children out of the classrooms which may lead to dropping out of schools by learners especially for girls. This project will address these education needs to ensure that learners access safe and inclusive learning and their future is protected.

2. Project activities

In this project, Community In Need Aid (CINA) seeks to address the gaps seen in the communities where the project will implement the follows activities:a) Provision of Temporary Learning Spaces (TLSs). CINA will use the resources under this project to improve access to learning facilities by establishing/renovating 12 TLSs for the most affected counties such as Canal/Pigi, Twic East in Jonglei State and Renk, Nasir, Fashoda and Malakal in Upper Nile State b) Establishing/renovating 12 inclusive gender disaggregated WASH facilities, using the INEE standards. c) Training of Teachers. The organization will train 615 (381Male 234 female) volunteers’ teachers on basic EiE methodology, mental health and psychosocial support, and disaster preparedness and response. d) Distribution of teaching and learning materials. CINA will secure learning and teaching materials from the pipeline and distribute to the school to benefit 61,210 (37,281 boys/ 23,929 girls) learners. e) Creation of awareness. The organization will engage the communities through back to learning campaign to ensure both displaced children, returnee/refugees and host community children have access to education. This exercise will ensure teachers, PTAs, education officials and other community stakeholders are engaged to support the initiatives. The campaign will be conducted 3 times in the project period every opening of the school's terms in each county. f) Provision of Teacher's Incentive. CINA will support 615 (381Male 234 Female) volunteer teachers with incentives using the agreed Education Cluster rates. the incentive will be used to boost the volunteer teachers' morals as they offer their services in teaching in the supported schools. g) provision of Psychosocial support activities. The Project will provide psychosocial support to children and youth impacted by the conflict and flooding in order strengthen protective factors for the children, including their ability to identify dangerous and risky situations. Child help desks will be established in each of the schools to identify and refer children who needed further support and management. h) Gender responsive and special needs services, CINA will ensure there is strong coordination and support to schoolgirls by ensuring this segment of learners are provided with dignity kits and collaborate with other partners to either refer children with special need or provide the needs. i) Training of School Management committee/parent teacher’s association. The project will ensure 131 (77 Male 54 female) SMC/PTA members are engaged and trained on school management, resource mobilization, school development plans and budget taking the experience from GESS program where school managers are exposed to planning and resource generation for school effectiveness and service delivery. j) Establish peer to peer learning and learning cycles for returnee/refugee children in the transit centre. The peer to peer learning will ensure learners are paired to support each other in formal learning, psychosocial support and mentoring. In the learning cycle, returnee/refugee learners will be engaged in learning activities under tree in the TC to to learn twice or three times a week in order to keep them appraise with learning and prevent dropping out.
