Project Summary

Alignment with the Protection Sector Objective: 2 & 3, the project will promote the meaningful engagement of persons/children with & without disabilities, older people in the humanitarian response mechanism by ensuring their participation. On the other hand, the project will work for protection system strengthening by developing capacities on inclusive protection of different stakeholders including government authorities.
The project ensures age-specific support for persons with disabilities, focusing on children, youth, and older people. Children will receive rehabilitation, inclusive sports opportunities to promote integration. Youth & Women with disabilities will be empowered through leadership training and skill development and Older people will benefit from rehabilitation, and awareness campaigns to reduce barriers.
Under the Community-Based Protection component of the project, CBMG & CDD will provide home-based rehabilitation services to all aged, diverse persons with disabilities, and individuals at risk, aligning with the Community-Based Rehabilitation (CBR) approach.. In order to respect the community’s choice, women staff will be recruited to make the team diverse. Moreover, during community sessions, apart from gender-mixed sessions, specific awareness sessions will be conducted for women’s groups. Additionally, women-led SHGs will be preferred wherever available.
After the rehabilitation services, children with disabilities will be enrolled in learning centers where they will learn inclusive way. Periodically, inclusive sports sessions will be organized. The project will support the persons with disabilities initiatives for promoting their rights. As a part of Disability Inclusion, the project will work with previously formed 5 Self Help Groups and 10 Disability Support Committees (in camp (8W, 8E,11, 13, 18 & 19) to empower them. SHG is the primary stage of OPD. CBMG & CDD will enhance their capacity on different topics which will help in advocacy on disability mainstreaming & ensure rights of persons with disabilities. . Moreover, CBMG & CDD will support them in organizing their regular monthly meetings and mentor them by regular onsite support and exposure visits. CDD will advocate for inclusive practices, claiming their rights to meaningful access to humanitarian services including protection services. These groups will provide a platform for persons with disabilities to advocate for accessing essential services in an inclusive manner. Additionally, these groups will work to raise awareness, reduce barriers, and build resilience within communities.
The project will conduct community awareness sessions on disability rights, etiquette, GBV, and general protection through yard meetings and day celebrations, using inclusive materials. These initiatives will empower persons with disabilities, reduce stigma, and promote inclusivity, promoting safer communities. By raising awareness and strengthening resource access, the project enhances protection and inclusion for vulnerable groups.
Under this project CBMG & CDD will develop the capacity of Protection Actors including field-level staff and Authorities which include training on accessibility and accessibility audit, removing barriers, ensuring meaningful participation of persons with disabilities, ensuring sex, age, disability, and diversity data (SADD), disability-inclusive GBV programming, Gender and Disability intersections, and Disability Inclusive Project Cycle Management.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

In the Rohingya refugee camps in Bangladesh, individuals with disabilities encounter persistent barriers to essential services due to limited infrastructure, scarce resources, and societal attitudes. Although awareness around disability rights has improved, there remain significant gaps in access to healthcare, education, and livelihood opportunities. The restricted availability of rehabilitation services further exacerbates the dependency of people with disabilities on family members, curtailing their autonomy and active participation in camp activities. The 2024 Inter-Sector Needs Analysis (ISNA), aligning with findings from the Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment (ADINA) conducted by REACH, reports that 12% of the Rohingya camp population has at least one disability. This statistic is corroborated by the Washington Group Questionnaire (short set) data, indicating the extensive need for disability-inclusive planning. Additionally, a rapid assistive technology assessment (rATA) revealed that only 1% of people with disabilities have access to the assistive devices they need, while 51% face unmet needs, and 48% lack any assistive products. Gender considerations further deepen these challenges, as highlighted in the Joint Response Plan (JRP) 2024. Women and girls with disabilities face heightened risks due to intersecting vulnerabilities, including limited access to sexual and reproductive health services, exposure to gender-based violence, and restricted involvement in community decision-making. These gender-specific barriers exacerbate exclusion and isolation, particularly for women and girls with disabilities, who remain underserved in many services and opportunities. Addressing these gender-based disparities is essential for creating a more inclusive and protective environment across both the camps and host communities. Host communities in Bangladesh are similarly strained by the ongoing crisis. Competition for limited resources, environmental impacts, and economic challenges compound the difficulties for people with disabilities in these areas. Unlike the refugee camps, which receive greater organizational support, host communities have significantly fewer resources, with only a few organizations like the Centre for Disability in Development (CDD) and CBMG actively involved in community-based rehabilitation (CBR) and disability inclusion. The scarcity of rehabilitation services highlights an urgent need for expanded support to address the challenges faced by both Rohingya and host community members with disabilities.

2.3 - Age & Disability

At least 33% of the project’s participant target will be people with disabilities. CBMG-CDD conducted prevalence surveys using the WG Short Set on Functioning – Enhanced (WG-SS Enhanced) for 18 plus people and UNICEF Child Functioning Module (CFM) for 2 to 4 years & 5 to 17 years age group. The surveys recorded prevalence rates in their programs of between 6% and 13%. These results led the partners to decide on a conservative target for 2024, hoping that this target can be exceeded by leveraging existing, improving data collection using the correct tools, and proactively engaging persons with disabilities to create a snowball effect. A comprehensive GEDSI action plan will be developed in the inception phase. Across all outcomes, WG-SS Enhanced and CFM will be used to identify persons with disabilities. The Self-Help Groups (SHGs) in the host communities and Disability Support Committees (DSCs) in the camps, developed, will be strengthened; SHG/DSC members will be direct beneficiaries and will contain at least 50% persons with disabilities per group as per the Convention of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) requirements. These groups/committees will be enabled through regular meetings, training, and linkage building with GoB departments and with local Organizations of Persons with Disabilities (OPDs), supported by CBMG/CDD. Implementing partners will receive training in disability inclusion, ensuring they can support and empower persons with disabilities to understand their rights and effectively participate in all project activities by making activities accessible and providing reasonable accommodations. All facilities will be modified/retrofitted to ensure the service is inclusive and accessible. Based on specific needs, persons with disabilities will be referred to CDD for rehabilitation and Assistive Technology, if required.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

CBMG & CDD will ensure accountability to affected populations by using already functional inclusive complaints, feedback, and response mechanisms that proactively capture systematic feedback from beneficiaries with disabilities and set strategies to remove barriers accordingly. The iFRM will be made accessible to persons with disabilities through consultations with disability groups, including persons with different types of disabilities. These consultations will gather feedback on preferred communication methods, accessibility needs, and barriers to reporting. Outcomes will directly inform the design of accessible formats and channels for feedback, ensuring inclusive participation in the mechanism.The iFRM will follow the following five steps. Step 1: Embed inclusive feedback & and reporting (iFRM) mechanism in all new projects.Step 2: Analyze the community profile and local context.Step 3: Consult with communities.Step 4: Decide on the most appropriate feedback and reporting channels.Step 5: Develop a plan of action for handling feedback and training staff. CBMG-CDD will ensure that women, men, girls, and boys receiving humanitarian assistance are the primary stakeholders of the project and that they have a basic right to participate in the decisions that affect their lives, receive the information they need to make informed decisions, and to complain if they feel the help they receive is not adequate or has unwelcomed consequences.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

CBM Global staff, representatives, programme participants, partners and community members can report, in writing or verbally, suspected or confirmed cases of safeguarding and abuse through any one of the following:• Notify, in writing or verbally (face-to-face or by telephone), your country Safeguarding Focal Point• Notify CBM Global’s Global Safeguarding Manager: safeguarding@cbm-global.org• Report using our Safeguarding FormCBM Global recognizes that sometimes persons who wish to report a safeguarding concern may feel more comfortable doing so outside these identified pathways. For instance, a staff member may make a report to a trusted colleague and not a safeguarding focal point. The trusted colleague has an obligation to escalate the incident using any of the reporting pathways listed above. All reported cases, regardless of how they are reported, will be treated seriously and will be handled by professionals who are trained in the matter and will treat them with the necessary sensitivity. As part PSEA, CBMG and CDD will take the specific requirements and vulnerabilities of children with and without disabilities and adult-at-risk into consideration.• Carry out regular refresher training for all staff on CDD’s and CBMG’s Safeguarding Policies and ensure codes of conduct are signed.• Ensure staff recruitment and selection processes are in place to deter, identify, and reject individuals who are deemed to be at risk of causing harm to children and vulnerable adults.• Frontline staff, safeguarding focal points, and others who receive complaints will be trained and supported to carry out their duties in a professional, non-judgmental, and culturally sensitive manner, with due regard for confidentiality and data protection requirements.• Ensure that people and organizations do not use photographs and related information beyond the agreed purposes and consent by beneficiaries.• Information on safeguarding will be made available from the CBMG & CDD office.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the project incorporates Climate Action by addressing Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Disaster Risk Management (DRM), and anticipatory actions. It strengthens Disability Support Committees (DSCs) and Self-Help Groups (SHGs) to promote resilience among persons with disabilities. Key actions include: Inclusive DRR and DRM training for DSC and SHG members, with accessible emergency preparedness plans.Anticipatory actions such as early warning systems accessible to persons with disabilities.Climate adaptation will be achieved through sustainable practices that reduce vulnerabilities and build community resilience, including the use of eco-friendly materials for project activities. Additionally, CDD will raise awareness within the community about the benefits of using these materials, ensuring safety and reducing vulnerabilities for persons with disabilities.These actions ensure that climate response efforts are inclusive and protective of persons with disabilities.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project includes Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures. It incorporates:• Emergency Response Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) tailored to ensure inclusive response actions, with specific protocols for assisting people with disabilities.• Contingency Funds are allocated for rapid mobilization of resources to meet urgent needs.These measures enhance the project’s ability to respond quickly and effectively to emergencies, ensuring the protection and inclusion of vulnerable community members.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project will actively engage youth by establishing school brigades made up of school-going students. CBMG and CDD will work together to develop these student groups, enhancing their knowledge and skills on disability inclusion and general protection issues. Through targeted training and capacity-building sessions, the students will become advocates for inclusive practices. Once trained, these youth brigades will raise awareness among their peers and community members, promoting understanding and support for persons with disabilities. Their activities will include organizing awareness campaigns, peer discussions, and community outreach efforts, ultimately fostering a more inclusive and protective environment for all.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees by empowering Disability Support Committees (DSCs) and creating skill-building opportunities for people with disabilities through the referral linkage. 4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project activities in the host community align with development priorities in Cox’s Bazar district. By strengthening Self-Help Groups (SHGs) for persons with disabilities, the project supports the district’s focus on inclusive development and social cohesion. It contributes to priorities outlined in the District Development and Growth Priorities of Cox's Bazar (Dec 2022) by promoting disability inclusion, community-based protection, and resilience-building initiatives. Additionally, the project cooperates with local stakeholders to ensure that its interventions complement district-level planning instruments, promotion sustainable development and improving the quality of life for vulnerable populations in the host community.
