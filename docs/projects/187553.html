Project Summary

DRC intervention will particularly be focused on providing access to basic, safe, and dignified Shelter solutions, and having access to sufficient and safe water for domestic use which will assist in enabling access of the most vulnerable population groups to safe and dignified shelter solutions which contributes to the 2021 Shelter HRP Objective SO1)Provide life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people with an emphasis on those in areas with high severity of needs; SO2)Enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks and respond to protection needs through supporting the protective environment in Syria, by promoting international law, IHL, IHRL and though quality, principled assistance;
The conflict-affected population is assisted in compliance with the Sector and MoLAE standards. DRC seeks to engage with other DRC sectors to complement DRCs work this coordination and complementarity mechanism needs to be expressed accordingly between the shelter activities and achieving the principles of protection DRC will achieve this through the following outcomes: The access to basic, safe, and dignified shelter solutions is improved for conflict affected populations in Rural Damascus, Dara'a, Hama, and Aleppo.
DRC aims to provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter support through addressing overburdened communities with a high ratio of returnees and, members of host communities and IDPs in the rehabilitation of partially damaged apartments, DRC is aiming for a multi-sectoral integrated intervention of Shelter, Education and WASH sectors to increase access to life-saving emergency and life-sustaining assistance while also transitioning to more resilience/early recovery-oriented activities in selected locations of intervention to maximize the impact of humanitarian actions, and enabling access of the conflict-affected population to safe water for drinking and domestic use and safe excreta. A total of 23250 beneficiaries (22550 in 2022 and 700 in 2023) will be reached out in different locations with a 3 and above severity scale.
DRC’s programme strategy is realized through two programme objectives which are responding to emergencies and addressing solutions to protracted displacement. When combined, these can provide a basis for a comprehensive response in all phases of the displacement cycle transitioning.
DRC will respond to emergencies where sudden population movements cause conflict-affected populations to be living in conditions posing immediate life-threatening risks as well as address needs arising due to the covid-19 pandemic.
IDPs assistance following SHELTER Sector Standards will be provided in collective shelters, and private unfinished/demolished buildings in both, urban and rural areas. Newly displaced populations get prioritized as an emergency response including COVID 19 response, according to the Sector’s directive. The intervention will concentrate on areas where DRC has a current presence.
Selection of targeted beneficiaries will be done by DRC in close coordination with MoLA and SARC with verification of needs and beneficiaries by DRC. DRC will liaise with the Mukhtar, with any humanitarian actors present in the area, and with local residents to identify HHs, ensure coordination and avoid any overlap. DRC staff will have free access to move around the target areas scoping houses and approach HHs directly to explain the scope of the intervention and offer to conduct an initial technical assessment. Initial technical assessments will provide an understanding of each family’s situation/vulnerability and determine the feasibility of works.

4 - Needs

Entering the second decade, Syria remains one of the world’s worst humanitarian crises with over 90% of people living in poverty and 14.6 million need humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. The scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain overwhelming. According to GHO 2022, A decade into the crisis in Syria basic service delivery continues to be vastly inadequate and hampered by damaged infrastructure, lack of critical supplies, and, increasingly, financial unaffordability. The continuous economic deterioration has been the main factor to forces displaced families to return to their areas of origin residing in substandard shelter types. Unaffordability of repairing the damaged houses resulting from insufficient income has left the returnees in dire need of shelter support, as most of the interviewed returnees and displaced households reported inadequate access to basic services including the absence of or damaged or non-functioning public infrastructure. Such conditions, when combined with overcrowding in the host communities, significantly increase the possibility of exposure to protection risks and promote the spread of respiratory and epidemic-prone diseases, including COVID-19.DRC conducted a shelter need assessment in November 2021 in the planned areas of interventions. The findings refer to severe needs for rehabilitation of partially damaged apartments to maintain dignified living space and secure safe access.

5 - Implementation of activities

DRC will provide safe and dignified shelter solutions by repairing partially damaged houses/apartments for 490 HH and will pilot a shelter package for 20 HH, electrical repairs, windows and doors, accessories for WASH facilities. DRC will prioritize apartments hosting IDP families alongside members of the host community and returnees for repair. DRC will rehabilitate community infrastructure rehabilitation for 10 projects targeting 20000 benefs.DRC will target the old cities, Rural areas. Rehabilitation works will be based on the Shelter Sector minimum requirements and the MoLAE standards for Syria and are expected to take between one and three months depending on the nature of the project. On average, one PDA requires two months of implementation. Additionally, activities will include, debris removal, the installation of public lighting/solar system panels. DRC will focus on the installation of a public solar lighting system to promote the safety and access of beneficiaries in areas of intervention under UNHCR. Once works are finalized, they would be handed over to DoLAE/municipality. The maintenance of the solar panels will be done by the technical team in the municipality in the future. The evaluation of the outcome of the final executed work is taken over by the DRC internal MEAL department in conducting a satisfactory survey and other monitoring processes involving different stakeholders interventions will be in close coordination with the Sector, SARC, MoLA, and MoWR.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Increased risk of inter or intra-community tension due to varied assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria, or poorly timed distributions leading to a feeling of discrimination. Tension can also arise between the host community and IDPs, due to a lack of assistance from the previous. LH: HIGH, IMPACT: MIDUM, Mitigating Measure: Standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Ensure beneficiary selection criteria are uniform/agreed upon among sector members and with the government and are clearly communicated widely within a community and amongst different groups. Engage with community leaders, to raise awareness in the community. Establish a complaint mechanism at the community level. 2. Selection criteria are influenced by local authorities may contribute to nepotism, the tension in the community, and the risk of the most vulnerable being left out. LH: HIGH, IMPACT: MIDUM, Mitigating Measure: Standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Engage with community leaders, to raise awareness in the community.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Partially damaged apartments may affect women, girls (exposure to GBV risk) and children, elderly, and people with disabilities: LH: high, Impact: high, mitigation measures: Ensuring agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consulting separately girls, boys, women, and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design implementation phases; Establish complaint & feedback mechanism; Monitor safe and equitable access and use of water distribution points.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Due to international bank sanctions, the cash flow from the HQ office to the country mission’s bank account might be affected resulting in a shortage of funds for operations and covering fixed costs. Mitigation measure: exploring several cash flow routes: from HQ to other banks in the neighboring country to DRC Syria bank account 2. Local authorities are not providing required approvals for the intervention and/or for the beneficiaries selected. Mitigation measure: maintaining a good relationship with local authorities, advocation towards the authorities for the benefit of the addressed population, active participation in the Sector Cluster.3. Further fluctuation of the Syrian pound against foreign currencies. Likelihood: high, Impact: High. Mitigation: DRC will implement strong financial management tools to monitor over/underspend on project budgets. 4. Fuel Shortage that affects that may affect the implementation of activities. Likelihood: high, Impact: high. Mitigation: Hiring service providers from the same local communities who can move easily and not be affected by the fuel shortage.
