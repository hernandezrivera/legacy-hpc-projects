Project Summary

The project will increase food security for communities in the eastern and northern areas of the Gaza strip through provision of e-vouchers for food and non-food assistance. Applying a cash-based modality will empower food insecure people to prioritize and meet their own needs while enhancing their access to sufficient food intake.

Needs

The ongoing Israeli blockade, the deteriorating economic situation and the recurrent conflict events have resulted in high rates of poverty (53%), highest ever-recorded unemployment rate (53.7%) and chronic food insecurity in the Gaza strip. Youth and female unemployment is even higher at 70% and 78% respectively. Household incomes are low, costs of living are high and livelihood options are becoming more and more scarce. As part of a current livelihood project, SCI has conducted a socio-economic assessment in North Gaza and eastern Khanyounis on 1,292 poor households (HHs). The assessment has shown that the average household income is NIS 1,000 per month in North Gaza and only NIS 552 per month in Eastern Khanyounis. According to UNRWA figures, approximately 80% of the population in Gaza is dependent on external assistance, primarily food assistance. In light of the declining donor support and complex security situation, the level of aid reliance and extensive needs are particularly concerning. Gaza is not an agricultural economy, and the capacity to produce food is further limited as access to land and the sea is restricted by the Israel in the ARAs. The prevailing poverty, poor access to livelihood opportunities and basic services is increasing health risks and level of malnutrition. Women face particular burden as caregivers. The 2014 conflict continues to negatively impact the productive capacity and income stability for nearly 30,000 farmers, herders, breeders and fisher folk. The restoration of land, irrigation systems, greenhouse emissions, soil fertility and fruit orchards has been going slowly and result in dependency on short-term assistance. The lack of alternative renewable energy sources are a crucial gap in particular in the context of the worsening energy crisis and the increase in the cost of irrigation water. The importation of pipes, timber and fertilizers face limitations and delays. High level of water salinity poses a further impediment to investing in sectors such as guava, palm and citrus cultivation. The red palm weevil infestation continues to damage date production. Generally, farmers lack alternative solutions to restore a more sustainable production environment. An estimated 40% of all Gaza households are considered severely or moderately food insecure. The findings of the Household Expenditure and Consumption Survey, released by the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) in May, show a significant increase in poverty rates in the Gaza Strip: from 38.8 per cent in 2011 (the previous time poverty was measured) to 53 per cent by the end of 2017, which is the equivalent to around 1.01 million people, including over 400,000 children. Food insecure families spend approximately half of their income on food. Other essentials are deprioritized, and a further deterioration of food security and livelihood status evolves, especially in female-headed households. The public sector salary crisis has affected a great volume of households further weakening household income stability and purchasing power which in turn has affected the local market and small businesses such as grocery owners, farmers, drivers, among others.

Activities

The project will directly support the FSS objective 2 through enabling HHs suffering from limited of access to food and income opportunities to meet their basic food needs. As a result, targeted HHs will be better able to cover their essential food and non-food needs, improve their food consumption, food diversity, and reduce negative coping strategies. The intervention will target 4,000 vulnerable HHs (approx. 22,400 people) with distribution of e-vouchers. SCI will implement the project based on the initial SMEB set at US$190 per month ($60 for basic food and 130$ for fresh fruit, fresh vegetables, fresh chicken and fresh beef meat) for an avg. family size of 5.6. SC`s in-depth research conducted from Jun to Sept 2018 in close coordination with the in-country FS sector (incl. presentation of results to sector members) has found that the required amount per HH of average HH size to adequately meet their food needs corresponds to the above target amount. To ensure complementarity and coordination among stakeholders, SC will target exceptionally vulnerable HHs (such as HHs with members with disability, pregnant or lactating women, infants, elderly, child/female-headed HHs, HHs of extended size) only. HHs that are not being served by any actors will receive the full voucher value of US$190. HHs that are already being provided with assistance by others (in the value of US$60) will receive the supplementary support (in the value of US$130). This methodology has been designed to ensure HHs` food security needs are met to the extent that the risks of malnutrition and insufficient level of food intake are as much mitigated as possible. The E-voucher value is expected to cover 100% of HHs` food needs factoring in coping strategy practices that they may use to complete their needs such as but not limited to: rely on less preferred and less expensive foods; borrowings and assistance from relatives or host families; and use of available savings. Food requirement has been calculated based on the FS sector standard of 1,800 kcal/person/day for 30 days. Food items will be based on the list of food items in the basket with the addition of fresh fruits and vegetables, fresh meat and eggs. SC will coordinate with other actors through FSS/Cash working group to ensure integration, reaching to the most vulnerable HHs and to complement or support other actors` interventions (provision of the value of fresh fruit, vegetables and meat for the most vulnerable and big families which are receiving basic food vouchers from other actors.) Specific project activities will include:1 - Rapid assessment of needs, gaps, and market functionality 2 - Kick off and preparation of partners3 - Communication with stakeholders4 - Conduct of poverty assessment to identify and select beneficiaries 5 - Distribution preparation (contracting with merchants and Palpay financial service provider)6 - Orientation to beneficiaries on the use of e-vouchers7 - Distribution of e-vouchers and redemption8 - Monitoring of e-voucher use (Post-Distribution Monitoring) To increase the impact of the project, SCI with implementing partners will conduct 200 sensitization sessions for target HHs and key community members to explain the planned intervention and discuss the pre-selected food and non-food items. The project steering committee will include local partners and key community members, and it will conduct regular meetings to manage potential challenges and social tensions during implementation. Project activities will be implemented in coordination with 3 local partners (Biet Lahia development association in North Gaza, Save Youth Future in Gaza city and the middle area and Bayader society in the South). Implementing partners will be responsible for the selection of beneficiaries, conduct of sensitization sessions, follow-up on the distribution of e-vouchers and vendor compliance. SC will select and sign the contracts with vendors and the e-voucher service provider.

Indicators

Average Coping Strategies Index / % households protected from the use of harmful coping strategies throughout the project period (6 months) (75% of targeted HHs )% of households with enhanced food consumption score (85% of targeted HHs)% of HH with enhanced Diet Diversity HDDS/IDDS (85% of targeted HHs) # of HHs utilizing E-vouchers to obtain nutritious foods (Disaggregated by boys, girls, men, women) (4,000) Project beneficiaries will be selected based on social and vulnerability criteria, in collaboration with the local community and the poorest families. The response will prioritize households with pregnant and lactating women, children under 5, people with disabilities, elderly, child/female-headed households and households of extended size. SC will develop a beneficiary application form requesting financial and social data about the beneficiaries. The beneficiaries will be selected by Save the Children and the local partners based on the level of needs to assistance and the poverty assessment using a scoring system. Home visits will be conducted for verification of data given by the applicants on the form. Beneficiary selection will be coordinated with the FS sector and the MoSD. M&E:Save the Children`s Field Officer will supervise the implementation in cooperation with the local partners. Partners will submit monthly progress report. Save the Children will keep close communications with other organizations working in the same area through the cluster. SCI will also coordinate with the FSS and WASH clusters to identify the preselected items and provide FSS with regular update on the progress of the project as well as share the list of potential beneficiaries for cross-checking. SC has an established accountability mechanism including complaints and suggestion boxes placed at the CBO offices as well as a Hotline. SC staff and partners will make sure that targeted communities are informed on the types of feedback mechanisms available through publicizing materials and information-sharing sessions. Any recommendations, suggestions and concerns of the beneficiaries, along with the mitigation measures will be discussed with partners to be properly addressed. SC has been delivering Food Security activities including through cash-based modalities (cash grants and cash for work) in the target locations since 2000 and has secured access for the implementation of the project. The cash-transfer operational platform is established and a market assessment and functionality has been conducted for the effective delivery of the project.
