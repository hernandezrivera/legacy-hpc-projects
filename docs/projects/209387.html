Project Summary

For Integrated Protection in Gedaref and Blue Nile, specialized Women’s Protection and Empowerment (WPE) prevention and response services will be delivered to support women and girls at risk, such as elderly women, pregnant women, breastfeeding women, women and girls with disabilities, and female headed households (HHs). The project will support the integration of reproductive health and WPE services so that gender-based violence (GBV) survivors can access safe and timely clinical care for the harmful health consequences - both physical and emotional. The project will also deliver Child Protection (CP) services to ensure that children are safe in their homes when they experience harm. The IRC will provide access to quality services and a robust CP response inclusive of case management and Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS) where the IRC's Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) curriculum will be implemented. The IRC will also engage community members to further mitigate potential risks for children and adolescents. Additionally, the IRC will introduce activities to assist in reducing the risk of water-borne diseases and vector control within the catchment areas of the supported Health Facilities through risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) activities by involving IRC Integrated protection. The IRC will target the refugees in both Blue Nile and Gedaref states, affected by WASH-related diseases and outbreaks as well as flooding and malnutrition, to address their dire needs in terms of WASH support. The IRC prioritizes gender and protection as cross-cutting themes across all areas of intervention to ensure that services are equally accessible and safe for clients and that the unique needs of individuals are addressed. especially children under 5 and pregnant, lactating women (PLW), and people with disabilities. WASH activities will be bolstered by life-saving, integrated health and nutrition services to reduce excess morbidity and mortality for the refugee population. This will be done through primary health care facilities and referral of complicated cases to secondary facilities, as well mobile medical teams to offer flexible delivery mechanism for health and nutrition services. The health package will include reproductive, maternal and child health services, treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases, mental health and psychosocial support, emergency preparedness and response including response to outbreaks of diseases including cholera, dengue fever, measles and spikes in malaria transmission. The IRC will provide life-saving treatment for severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), referrals to stabilization centers and counseling of pregnant and lactating women (PLW) on infant and young child feeding (IYCF). The IRC will work directly in the camps to provide services, and through established Ministry of Health (MoH) facilities to scale up services to refugees in urban areas including capacity building to healthcare providers and basic health infrastructure improvements to deliver quality health and nutrition services for the refugees. Refugee HHs in Tuneydbah camp will also receive one round of multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) to meet their basic needs. The transfer value will be distributed as per the guidelines of cash working group. The IRC will use the strategy of blanket coverage and cover around 24,973 refugees in Tuneydbah camp in Gadaref state.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In designing its programs, protection makes every effort to address protection mainstreaming in the technical design. In all aspects of the project, from design to implementation and monitoring, the core protection principles of ‘Do No Harm’, meaningful access, safety and dignity, and participation will be mainstreamed. Community outreach will include messaging on prevention and mitigation of gender-based violence of child protection risks. All staff working on the project will be trained/provided with refresher training on core protection concepts, gender mainstreaming, and referral procedures. The IRC will integrate activities within its referral system, allowing the team to refer potential cases to the health team. All field-based staff will receive information about available protection and response services to make safe and ethical referrals upon beneficiary request as well as receive service mapping from the WPE and CP team, to ensure the relay of information on access to services if/when asked during HH visits.Under Food Security activities, a comprehensive community engagement will be conducted to understand the implementation barriers and seek improved ways to reach the most vulnerable HHs. During community engagement sessions, the IRC will ensure the participation of women and encourage women leaders to take part in the working committees to advocate the needs of women at grassroot level. A specific selection criterion will be included for disabled people and unaccompanied children to be included in the MPCA. At the end of each project, the Lesson Learned discussions will be conducted to capture the local community's views and align program strategy with learnings. The WASH activities will be inclusive of feedback and participation from target beneficiaries, particularly women, including through focus group discussions and by ensuring their inclusion within the WUCs and as hand pump mechanics. The IRC will increase the registration and identification of persons with disabilities and the elderly to ensure that unique needs are addressed, while also strengthening existing community support structures for these groups, such as surrogate water collectors. Hygiene promotion activities will also include information on services available for survivors of abuse and sexual violence. Beneficiaries will also be made aware of the IRC’s Code of Conduct and opportunities for reporting to prevent and reduce the risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.. During project start-up, the IRC will conduct a needs assessment to further identify households that have people living with disabilities and elderly people to ensure the successful delivery of interventions, particularly regarding water collection and hygiene awareness. Through house-to-house visits, CHVs will also identify people with disabilities or special needs within their respective communities, conduct follow-ups, and mutually agree on how to ensure that their unique needs are continuously met.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The targetted communities will be involved in the decision-making regarding the location of safe access to services. Client satisfaction surveys will also be conducted with clientsreceiving services at all health facilities. Throughout implementation, the IRC will leverage its MEAL and feedback systems to ensure community involvement in the design and re-design of the action.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

The IRC adopts a client-centered approach that puts the target population at the heart of the project. All sectors will conduct regular feedback sessions with caregivers, women, and girls within the safe spaces to ensure activities and services adequately respond to their needs. Women, girls, and parents will be involved in the decision-making regarding the location of safe spaces, restrooms, water points, distribution points, etc. Client satisfaction surveys will also be conducted with survivors of GBV voluntarily. The IRC team will engage with women, girls, children, and caregivers from crisis-affected communities in planning and implementing strategies to prevent, mitigate, and address the consequences of CP risks and GBV. The IRC will conduct outreach and awareness to ensure that beneficiaries know the IRC Code of Conduct, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies, child safeguarding policies, the process for providing feedback, and the principles of beneficiary feedback (including confidentiality, non-retaliation, and accountability). Across all sectors, the IRC will provide multiple feedback channels to beneficiaries, including focus group discussions (FGDs), KIIs, feedback boxes, and a hotline, which will inform project design and implementation. The IRC also has a neutral department called the Ethics & Compliance Unit - ECU, which is responsible for all significant complaints, including SEA and abuse complaints.
