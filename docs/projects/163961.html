Project Summary

In line with the priorities of the Early Recovery and Livelihoods Sector (ERL) in HRP 2020 and the ERL Objective 1, the project aims at improving livelihood opportunities of vulnerable unskilled workers in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor Governorates, such as women heads of households (HH) and young adults between 18 and 35 years old breadwinners of the family.
The intervention will be implemented in areas of Aleppo – both city and rural - (Jebel Saman, Maskana, Al Khafsa, Menbij, and Hadher sub-districts) and Deir-Ez-Zor, ranked 4 and 5 in the ERL severity scale. These are considered extremely poor and economically affected areas, in high need of support. The action is designed to train people providing them with knowledge and tools; they will be eventually re-inserted in the work market, boosting the local economy and assuring the sustainability of the intervention.
Beneficiaries will be identified through a comprehensive and participatory approach, which involves also the analysis of data collected by GVC in the same areas in the past years through the conduction of non-formal education activities. The typology of the trainings, with 3 to 6-months length, will be decided through an initial market study to identify the most successful topics, with the involvement of local/national authorities and specific actors. Trainees will be provided with individual professional kits allowing them to self-create their job. Specifically, the intervention is composed of two phases: (i) plan, design, and conduction of vocational trainings to identified 1000 beneficiaries, and (ii) facilitate their regular employment access through linkages with workplaces and/or distribution of individual professional kits.
The target of the intervention are adults breadwinner of the families, identified as the most vulnerable and in need of such support. The identification phase will rely also -but not only- on the analysis of data collected through previous GVC implemented projects about families with out-of-school children. Specifically, the intervention will privilege households with out-of-school children due to economic reasons, and/or with children at risk of dropping out, aiming not only to create a job opportunity, but also to facilitate the children’s school re-insertion and/or school retention.
The initiative will have an impact that will not only directly benefit the head of households, but dramatically improve the economic condition of their entire families, with positive impacts in close relatives and neighbourhoods.

4 - Needs

Securing livelihood is one of the most critical defies faced in Syria. Estimated unemployment rate is 53%, reaching 75% among youth (15-24 years). According to 2015 estimates, 83% of Syrians lived below the poverty line and recent indications suggest it has worsened (HNO, 2019). People are adopting harmful coping strategies (reduced food consumption; reduced hygiene practices; spending of savings and accumulation of debt) due to loss or lack of livelihoods. Adolescents are exposed to exploitation and abuse, with girls also forced to early marriage. Divorced women and widows are at risk of stigmatization, discrimination, and forced marriages. Pressure and stress to provide for their families can lead to increased risks of violence against them within the family and at the workplace. Aleppo was considered the primary industrial zone in Syria with textile, engineering, chemical and food industries. Since mid-2017, 400 companies re-opened, while in 2018 150 new enterprises started their business in Jebel Saman sub-district (GVC Need Assessment, May 2018 and January 2019). Thanks to back-to-learning campaigns at HH level (door-to-door visits) run by GVC in 2018-2019 within education projects, data were collected on out-of-school children in the targeted areas. In Deir-Ez-Zor, results showed that 34% of children (57% boys; 43% girls) are out of school due to economic reasons, 54% for recent displacement, confirming families’ weak economic conditions. Livelihood in the city has been significantly impacted by the protracted siege, electricity outage, shortage of fuel and water and significant destruction of existing infrastructure. Unemployment rate was reported to be high and remaining income sources were salaries for civil servants (less than 20% of the current population) and casual labor. Due to low wages and high needs, child labor was observed to be rife. In rural areas mines make farming land inaccessible. HHs therefore depend on the remaining livestock to survive.

5 - Implementation of activities

A1)Vocational and skills training for vulnerable unskilled workers in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor (target: 1000 people). 1.1-Market study to identify and map the most needed vocational trainings, in order to succeed in labour market. 1.2-Identification of potential beneficiaries. The database developed by GVC with data on out-of-school children will be analysed to identify the most vulnerable families, as it contains information on over 3500 out-of-school children in Aleppo and 2000 in Deir-Ez-Zor, such as number per district, age, gender, reason for not attending school, presence/kind of disabilities, etc. Potential beneficiaries will be also informed through specific advertising in crucial places. The program will address vulnerable people, preferring women heads of households 18-35 years old breadwinners of the family, as well as families of out-of-school children and/or with high probability of dropping out, to facilitate school retention and/or re-insertion. 1.3-Conduction of vocational trainings in cooperation with the Directorate of Education Dept for Vocational Training, for 3-6 months depending on the topic. Training subjects will be identified during the first phase of the project with involved actors and through a market survey. However, first findings show that most successful trainings topics are informatics (maintenance of PCs, mobiles), electricity works, tailoring and sewing, shaving and make up, welding/metal forming, carpentry, farming. A2)Regular employment accessed for 800 workers in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor (target: 800 people), either through job placement of self-job creation. 2.1-Identification of companies in need of workforce: definition of job offers, required profiles and number of workers. 2.2-Support to access job market. Trainees will be put in contact with relevant companies through official contracts/internships; or, trainees will be supported in self-job creation with the distribution of 1000 professional kits in line with acquired skills.
