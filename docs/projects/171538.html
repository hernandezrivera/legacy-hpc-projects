Project Summary

According to the FSNAU and FEWSNET 2020 post Gu Technical release, up to 2.1 million people across Somalia face acute food insecurity crisis (IPC Phase 3). This is due to the combined effects of wide spread and severe flooding, desert locust infestation, impacts of COVID-19 and the impacts of previous shocks. This can have worse outcomes through December 2020 in the absence of humanitarian assistance. Also, 849,900 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through August 2021.The report continued to show how some poor agro pastoral households will face moderate to large food consumption gaps through the end of 2020. In riverine livelihoods of southern Somalia, devastating floods since April 2020 destroyed farmland and crops and caused population displacement, leading to significant crop losses and the loss of income from agricultural employment. Consequently, a significant proportion of poor households in riverine livelihoods will face moderate to large food consumption gaps through the end of 2020. Internally displaced persons (IDPs), a majority of whom are poor and live in urban areas in desperate conditions, have limited livelihood assets and coping options and rely more heavily on external humanitarian assistance. As a result, a significant proportion of IDPs continue to face moderate to large food consumption gaps. Some of the urban poor across Somalia who struggle to make ends meet also continue to face moderate to large food consumption gaps. For both groups (i.e. IDPs and urban poor), the socioeconomic impact of COVID-19 was a factor, associated with a decline in remittances, increased food prices, and a decline in employment and other income-earning opportunities in urban areas.
The project aims at preventing further deterioration of the affected population in central and southern regions of Somalia and offer timely and appropriate assistance to households that lack food and other household needs. Through this project we will target areas in Lower Shabelle, as well as IDP camps in Baidoa and Mogadishu. AWI will offer humanitarian interventions that meet the immediate needs as per Food security cluster objective 1 by starting with providing food Aid (Rice, Sugar, oil and Pulses) through vouchers in IDP camps for two months to 1100 households(6600 beneficiaries). We will also seek to restore acceptable household food consumption and also complement the household income of the affected households. Unconditional cash as well will be given to 600 extremely vulnerable households that are not able to work (women headed households, elderly, sick, disabled) of 120 USD a month for two months. As per Food security cluster objective 2 AWI will also provide households with agricultural inputs such as crop and vegetable seeds and land preparation to households before Deyr and GU rains and train farmers on improved farming techniques to give them knowledge and skills on how to improve crop production. The target beneficiaries are drawn from across different livelihoods who have been affected by the different shocks that have hit Somalia and are in need of assistance to prevent further deterioration of their livelihood situation.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

For AWI the key component of interventions is to do no harm. This accounts for both the beneficiaries as well as personnel of AWI. One of the key advice is to reduce contact between personnel of AWI and beneficiaries as well as reducing contact between beneficiaries in general especially in this time of Pandemic. Through Cash and voucher system it can reduce gatherings and other inconveniences. Also, the cash can be transferred directly through mobile without any need for people to have close contact. This way we comply with government regulations and are at the same time keeping people away from large gatherings where the virus has a chance of spreading. Also through this method it’s more effective and efficient in reaching all who need support

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project design will ensure that market assessments are done before the interventions, as well as monitor market prices in case of any changes. The areas that the interventions are taking place have efficient markets for commodities as well as cash transfer systems.

Mitigation Measures

There are many causes that cause displacements among communities, it might be conflicts, violence, violations of human rights or even disaster. In all these causes, one of the affected things in their lives is lack of necessities such as food, shelter and water and most lose their normal livelihoods and sources of income. Displacements affect individuals in different ways and most of them are marginalized within communities and are often overlooked when assessing needs of the community or even in programme planning. In ensuring these marginalized groups are included AWI will ensure that we work with all the relevant stakeholders to prevent excluding affected groups. In assessing the needs and programme planning AWI will ensure the most affected in the community that’s is IDPS, Returnees and even the vulnerable host communities are targeted as they are most affected and discriminated. AWI will first help them with the basic needs by providing food aid to the most affected communities and then further help the rest of the communities targeted in restoring their sources of livelihoods, this will be done in provisions of seeds and land preparation. Also training them on modern farming techniques and how to store their produce. Conflicts and displacements affect individuals in different ways depending on their gender and age. Children and older persons are often marginalized and excluded from the formal decision-making processes. Also, during the displacements many women and children are left as the heads of households and this forces them to assume different roles and responsibilities. These groups enjoy less social, economic and political power due to the inequalities within the communities and as a result their particular needs are often ignored. Children, in particular when separated from their families, are often at risk of neglect, abuse or exploitation, forced recruitment into armed forces or armed groups, child labor, deprivation of access to basic needs and other violations of their rights. Since some of them have the responsibilities of taking for their young siblings its quite hard for them as they lack the resources and support of managing a household. Sexual violence and Exploitation among women and children is always a common feature as a result of conflicts and displacements too. To counteract these issues AWI will ensure when collecting our data, we ensure inclusivity of all in the community and even disaggregate by age and sex. AWI will employ participatory approach in all the programme stages to incorporate the capacities of the different age groups and gender. During community engagements and even forming committees different age groups and gender will participate equally.Armed conflicts and violence have quite devastating effects on the lives of people as well as access to essential basic services is disrupted. In our programming and delivering our services to the communities we will ensure adequate representation of the marginalized groups in the community. AWI will also ensure that the interventions carried out will be addressing the needs of the different groups in the target area. So, in all our interventions we will be keen to ensure that they promote and respect human rights and that they will not result to any discrimination, abuse, neglect or violence. To avoid major security problems, it is in our plan to work with the local administrations, leaders from the local community and security agencies.

Please provide further explanation

For agriculture and pastoral households that face recurring food shortages in lean seasons, assets (livestock, land and grain storages) play vital role on their income and livelihoods. Households that are supported through this objectives in building up their assets over time through savings and other means of which AWI is aiming at will effectively improving their future prospects while increasing their resilience to weather related and other shocks. To build resilience and ensure sustainability of food insecurity in the target areas, the project intervention is designed to address the immediate needs of the communities targeted and work towards the long-term goal in improving their assets, capacity and reducing future risks/shocks. AWI will collaborate with the host communities and the IDPs to identify the needs in terms of resilience with are directly related to food security and livelihoods. AWI will have capacity building to the target groups in good farming practices and income generation, both which will support resilience in the community. Further AWI will work with the local authorities and community leaders to find durable solutions in capacity building, and community assets and even hand over of projects at the end.
