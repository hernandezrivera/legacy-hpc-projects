Project Summary

In this project, Polish Humanitarian Action (PAH) will provide life-saving emergency shelter materials and life-sustaining non-food items to the most vulnerable populations, focusing on internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees in Nyirol, Pibor and Uror counties in the former Jonglei State; Morobo and Yei Counties in former Central Equatoria State and Guit, Koch, Mayendit, Panyijar Counties in former Unity State through mobile response modality which will contribute to the reduction of protection risks and exposure to diseases attributed with poor shelter conditions and lack of non-food items. Most importantly, the project will support the existing coping mechanisms of the local community and will build resilience towards other shocks that have indirect effect (such as the worsening economic situation) through a component of a cash-based intervention in Bor South County where PAH currently implements FSL and WASH projects.
PAH will provide emergency shelter and non-food items to the most vulnerable groups in order to alleviate human suffering in a safe and dignified manner. PAH will focus on targeting female headed households, unaccompanied elderly, people with no links to the community, separated children, women at risk (pregnant and lactating mothers), child-headed households, people living with disability and returnees whose properties were damaged and looted during the conflict.
The mobile response teams will comprise of experienced ES/NFI officers and protection personnel (50 percent women and 50 percent men). One protection officer will participate in 90 percent of interventions with close coordination and collaboration with the Protection Cluster. The team will implement the interventions jointly with other partners’ especially local partners on ground, more specifically ES/NFI, WASH, FSL and Protection actors and in close coordination with the respective clusters. SFPs and cluster coordinators will be involved in the planning, coordination and reporting of the interventions.
PAH will conduct in-depth need assessment with the involvement of protection staff and with the participation of the community among the target populations with consultative community meetings, key informant interviews, focus group discussions with men, women, boys and girls, household level and individual interviews, and observations will be conducted by the assessment teams at household level and at markets. PAH will encourage support from volunteers from the community in discussions with community leaders and beneficiaries during verification and registration. Mobile data collection tools will be strongly encouraged during the project for needs assessment, verification and monitoring
As mobile emergency responses will still be a major modality of the intervention, PAH will also focus largely on increasing cash based projects based feasibility assessed by market research.
Post-distribution monitoring (PDM) exercise will be conducted 1 month after the intervention (where possible) by the M&E team to measure the impact of the projects and to determine if the assistance reached the most vulnerable groups. During and after the intervention, a complaint mechanism will be in place. PAH will use the PDM report for better programming since we will take into account the lessons learnt, complaints raised and recommendations by the beneficiaries. Rapid monitoring activities will be conducted during every distribution by the M&E team, A M&E officer will be part of the distribution team and all interventions conducting rapid monitoring activities. Security and logistical constraints have made it difficult in the recent past for monitoring teams to go back and conduct PDM therefore rapid monitoring will be conducted during the distribution and few days after the distribution. This will help to measure the quality of the program and know the beneficiaries’ satisfaction.

Needs

Thousands of displaced people are still hiding in the bushes without access to humanitarian assistance and at the same time they are cut-off from social services. Western Equatoria State have experienced a significant segment of the effects of both July 2016 fighting and current armed groups’ activities. Western Equatoria hosts a substantial number of internally displaced people primarily in Mundri, Ezo, Nzara, Yambio, Tambura and Nagero Payams’ IDP settlements (formal and informal camps). In Central Equatoria, a high number of returnees are coming back to their original land in Morobo, Yei, Mugo and Kajo-Keji following a more stable security situation in the county. Families, who were hosted in refugee camps in Uganda and the DRC, are returning to their abandoned homes, however, most of their shelters are burnt down and their household items were looted after they escaped. In addition, the agricultural land and farming areas were affected by the conflict. Jonglei State and Greater Unity have been among the locations where frequent conflicts and mass displacements took place. As a result, millions of conflict-affected people are trapped in the bushes with little capacity to cope with their new living situation and they encounter several risks when collecting firewood, shelter materials and water for their daily activities. Estimates indicate that up to 100,000 people were affected and displaced in Northern Jonglei, many of whom had previously been displaced during the fighting in February 2018, from Motot, Pulchuol, Uror, Waat, Lankien, and Akobo, some moving to Ethiopia (OCHA Humanitarian Bulletin, April 2018). Host communities in Waat, Pathai, Yuai, Khorphulus, Motot and Pieri were forced to leave their homes to protect themselves (Inter-Agency Need Assessment February 2018). The large number of IDPs from Waat and Uror had moved to Akobo (Motot, PAH need assessment, April 2018). PAH interventions will be based on rapid multisector needs assessments preceding any particular response. This will allow PAH responding to the specific needs of the given displaced community, being fully accountable for the identification of the beneficiaries and of their needs according to the cluster strategy. PAH ERT has an extensive experience in conducting EP&R activities in Unity, Jonglei and the Greater Equatorial States. The majority of initial assessments was followed by interventions conducted by PAH Emergency Response Teams or jointly with S/NFI cluster partners. During all interventions, PAH ERT and MEAL team on the ground collects information from the beneficiaries regarding their needs to be used in the ongoing response, as well as for future reference. The data gathered from several sources i.e. REACH surveys, IOM DTM, IPC data, NAWG, IRNAs, partners on ground and local communities confirms that there are significant Emergency Shelter and NFI needs within the affected communities in this locations. Due to sustained difficult situation within the country, numerous factors force communities to flee, leaving behind all of their belongings. When finding new areas to settle communities lack basic non-food items and shelter materials for their families. Provision of items such as: plastic sheet, mosquito nets or cooking set enables the communities to create temporary shelter and conditions to survive in the new environment without exposing themselves to diseases and protection risks. Based on the research done CSRF (Conflict Sensitivity Resource Facility in February-March 2018) trade in South Sudan is growing in importance and most families depend on markets for most of their food. The markets are highly affected by conflict, weather conditions, limited infrastructures, however, the platform for cash based projects is growing, which brings interest of humanitarian agencies, traders and beneficiaries, and likely will affect the changes in response modality.

Activities

1. PAH will use its two response modalities to respond to emergency shelter and NFI needs for the vulnerable population across South Sudan in correlation with protection team:a) CBI interventions will be based on functional and accessible markets through conducting rapid market assessments, needs analysis among the beneficiaries and provision of conditional or unconditional cash supportb) In-kind response will be conducted through mobile teams based in Juba in close coordination with cluster partners in conducting needs analysis in areas hard to reach across South Sudan.2. Conducting in-depth multisector needs assessment (focusing on S/NFI, WASH, FSL, Protection, Education, Nutrition) and analysis for emergency shelter materials and NFIs for displaced population, returnees and vulnerable host communities. PAH mobile team will carry out at least ten coordinated needs assessments for in-kind response and at least six coordinated need assessment and analysis focusing more on the protection risk, gender issues and people with disabilities in precedence locations where emergency is reported.3. Conducting beneficiaries' identification, verification and registration based on the most critical needs. PAH emergency response team will collaborate with the host community leaders, local authorities and beneficiary’s leaders to identify and register the most vulnerable individuals. Clear targeting criteria will be communicated to the leaders and PAH mobile team will disaggregate the population by gender, age and vulnerability. In CBI interventions, there will be stakeholders, like local authority, partners and community sensitisation and awareness on Housing, Land and Property (HLP) through community meetings with large focus on protection component.4. Emergency shelter NFIs distribution will be conducted to the identified most vulnerable population affected by crisis across South Sudan. All distributions will be coordinated and conducted in line with Cluster’s approach. PAH mobile teams will distribute emergency shelter materials, NFIs from the cluster pipeline.5. PAH will largely focus on CBI projects where there will be voucher distribution of unconditional but restricted commodity vouchers for locally available shelter, NFI materials. PAH CBI project staffs will encourage the usage of local materials for shelter construction and repair in traditional manner to increase resilience and coping mechanism of displaced population through setting standards for shelter materials and shelter related NFIs. PAH emergency response team will form distribution committee comprising of 50%/50% ratio of men to women for effective complain/feedback mechanism in both mobile response team and CBI project. The team will develop more effective messaging around the associated potential impacts of responses on protection, environment and usage of the distributed materials, translate all IEC materials to local languages commonly spoken in the prioritized locations of interventions.6. PAH S/NFI projects will be integrated with other sectors when applicable. PAH S/NFI mobile team will work together with WASH and Protection mobile team, joining interventions where needed. In addition, integrated approach will be encouraged with FSL, WASH, Protection, Nutrition and Education. This will be encouraged both in mobile responses and static, where focus on CBI will be enhanced.7. Post-distribution monitoring: Post-distribution monitoring (PDM)s for up to 50% of our distribution, as long as the location are still accessible and secure at least a month after our NFIs distributions. PDMs gives us clear view of whether our beneficiaries have profited from the NFIs and whether they are satisfied with the quality and quantity of the items distributed. PDM reports will be used in subsequent NFI responses in order to improve the quality of programming.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Meeting with authorities and community members at early stage of project design and implementation to discuss project scope. Focus group discussions with various groups to collect feedback on the needs which is related to providing information on activities carried by PAH. Complaint/feedback system allows beneficiaries to approach PAH team member in case clarification or follow up is needed. This includes establishing help desk, supported by both men and women responsible for providing information to the community members. Interaction sessions during distributions, demonstrations and training. Using IEC materials for the promotion of key messages.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Some messages are general for all population, depends on items provided. Specific sessions for women, girls, people with disabilities, will be taking place to ensure safety and security during distributions and on the way to and from distribution site. Needs analysis will focus on ensuring dignity of the beneficiaries through provision of appropriate items.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

PAH has internal regulations on PSEA, with all employees with signed commitment. PAH is also a member of the protection cluster and understands the modalities to ensure protection is mainstreamed within our activities. Key staff employed on field level (enumerators, crowd controllers, other dealing directly with beneficiaries) needs to sign the safety code of conduct. Training is provided with clarification on beneficiary abuse, reporting abuses, confidentiality and risk mitigation.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Focus group discussions with women groups, HH visits and consultations on specific needs related to gender, age group, disability, also within various sectors. Individual interviews with community members, institutional users (Health facilities, Nutrition Centers, Schools, etc.) on needs, protection concerns, other. Community needs to be inclusive of a wide representation of people in an intervention. Equitable Representation must include: Men, Women, Elderly, Youth, Disabled, Ethnic minorities, the most Economically and/or Politically Marginalised people, Boys and Girls. In practice this is challenging with, for example, women only discussions monitored by community leaders. Ensuring that community gatekeepers are managed and allow fair representation is carefully considered by PAH implementing team. Protection issues that may arise are reviewed with protection team supporting project implementation at all levels.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

All PAH’s stakeholders can give feedback and make complaints, including direct and indirect beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries, partners, local leaders, group of persons, organizations, project staff or any person can provide feedback about the PAH program, activities, staff, implementing partners and project suppliers. Complaints are collected through paper based system on the field level, phones calls, emails, complaint boxes, during monitoring visits, established help desk, coordination/review meetings, recipient/complaint committees. Possible courses of action for resolving disputes and complaints include: Apologize and accept the complaints when genuine and consider all relevant information and arguments; Refer/forward to alternative agencies if the complaint/issue raised is not within PAH mandate and is unable to assist with services; Explain the complaint process and the steps that will be followed after complaint submission, if possible, resolve the complaint immediately or commit to a time frame for investigating the complaint further; Refer if the complaint requires additional information and further investigation/analysis by management for decision making; Referral to government body if the complaint requires government intervention.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Men and women are systematically asked to share information about needs and cultural preferences. PAH tries to adjust the intervention accordingly. M&E team is monitoring the community engagement through questionnaire designed to monitor safety of beneficiaries during assistance delivery and AAP. Shelter NFI cluster’s AAP checklist is used for the improvement of community engagement activities.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project measure access of beneficiaries to assistance provided through complaint mechanism, tailored focus group discussions with women groups, youth groups, people with disability, eldery and other community members. Protection specific data collection tools are used by the monitoring team to gather valid information for lessons learnt and future recommendations.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project measures the safety of beneficiaries by establishing a complaint mechanism, consultative problem identification and solving through dialogue. Monitoring team has protection related questionnaires, developed by protection partners, approved by protection cluster. The tools gather key feedback from beneficiaries on safety related to assistance delivery.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Dignity of beneficiaries is measured through focus group discussions with the beneficiaries in which the beneficiaries will express their satisfaction or dissatisfaction of the process. Complain desks will be established during activities of distribution, registration and verification to address complains in a dignified way. Meanwhile throughout the project period beneficiary feedback, complaints and means of addressing will be documented in a well-designed form and analysed by the independent monitoring and evaluation team.
