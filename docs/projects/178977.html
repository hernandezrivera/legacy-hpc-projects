Project Summary

We will continue to deliver comprehensive, service-based protection programs prioritizing the needs of the most at-risk and vulnerable individuals including IDPs, children, adolescent girls, and persons with disabilities in line with specific objective 2.1 of the HNO (Specific protection needs are mitigated through the provision of quality and integrated protection services). We will expand our case management program and referral system to ensure at-risk individuals are provided with holistic inter-sectoral support. For complex protection cases requiring specialist protection services, we refer cases to other agencies within NES in line with interagency guidelines on referral pathways. Likewise, other humanitarian actors in NES refer cases to the protection team. Finally, the protection team receive referrals from other sectoral programs within the organisation. We will ensure meaningful access through our outreach Program and mobile case management team. We will expand support to isolated communities in the western countryside of Deir-ez-Zor which are currently underserved by humanitarian actors while also expanding our existing outreach and case management capacity in Manbij, Kobani, Raqqa and Tabqa where we are one of the few NGOs providing these services.
We will continue to support some of the most isolated IDP settlements in NES by operating a minimum of 12 child-friendly spaces (CFS) and adolescent friendly spaces with an increased focus on Deir-ez-Zor. These spaces provide structured and systemic PSS activities to children and adolescents and educate parents on parenting skills and protection-related topics to build resilience through learning positive coping mechanisms. These spaces also help us develop community acceptance in newly accessible areas, in turn strengthening the outreach program by providing us with sustained and extensive access throughout the community.

4 - Needs

Ten years of violent conflict have led to a severe protection crisis in Syria with children and women in particular exposed to serious protection risks. Inadequate living conditions in makeshift settlements or overcrowded sites, particularly in NES, as well as the loss of livelihoods, property and asset compound protection risks, including risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and child protection concerns. Moreover, the COVID-19 pandemic and deteriorating economy have had serious consequences on the safety and wellbeing of vulnerable groups. In a WoS overview of the protection sector which gathered information from protection partners across the country, 76% of respondents reported that psychological trauma, stress and anxiety is one of the main protection issues. 56% reported that gender-based violence against women and girls has increased as a consequence of the pandemic. Other issues that were mentioned by partners in the report include: limitation of movement (40%), separation/isolation from family and household members (36%), increased negative coping mechanisms (30%) and forced labor or economic exploitation (23%). It is evident that there is a need for comprehensive, service-based protection programs to respond to these protection risks as both the pandemic and economic crisis show no signs of abating. According to the HNO, Children comprise almost half of the affected population. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined. There remains therefore a significant need for comprehensive child protection programmes across NES.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will work with 2100 Children through 12 Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) across NES. Trained Facilitators will implement structured and unstructured PSS activities at each site based on the organization’s agreed curriculum. These spaces offer children protection, empower them, so that they become more resilient, and build their own positive coping mechanisms. We will prioritize underserved areas, including informal settlements where there is no access to formal education or community-based PSS support and children score low on protection indicators such as sense of safety and wellbeing and Child and Youth Resilience. To reinforce child protection and well-being, all children enrolled in the CFS will participate in age-appropriate sessions to build their life skills. Adolescent-specific programing will be provided for youth aged 11-17 as adolescence is a tipping point for exposure to a greater range of protection threats (HNO,7). We will also provide dignity kits to 500 adolescent girls in recognition of the HNO’s finding that this remains a lifesaving and underfunded service. Awareness-raising sessions will also be conducted with 600 caregivers. Caregiver sessions are focused on protection issues and children’s rights to minimize exploitation risks, GBV, raising awareness on the consequences of child labour and the importance of education. The expansion of case management services will ensure the most at-risk children and their families receive individualised support in an appropriate, systematic and timely manner, through direct support and/or referrals. We will coordinate with other protection actors to reinforce referral mechanisms in line with IASC guidelines.
