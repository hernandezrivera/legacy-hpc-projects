Project Summary

Needs:
-According to Syrian HNO 2024," After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023. Many families lost their main breadwinner due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, increasing the vulnerability of millions of people previously unable to meet their basic needs."
- Based on the preliminary information gathered by the Saed team during the implementation of some activities in the sites of earthquake-affected displaced people in the Jindires sub-district, we summarize some of the results:
There are 15 camps and sites for displaced people recently established in response to the earthquake-affected population in the Jindires area suffering from a food gap and not receiving any assistance.
-The population of these camps is 10,206 individuals (2,037 men, 2,375 women, 3,193 boys, 2,915 girls), distributed among 2,083 hhs, including 425 HHs headed by women, and 99 HHs with disabled members. Additionally, there are 227 individuals with disabilities, 408 lactating and pregnant women, and 256 elderly .
* Intervention,
SAED has developed interventions in the food security sector aimed at providing life-saving food assistance to the most vulnerable individuals affected by the February 6, 2023 earthquake residing in camps established in response to the earthquake-affected population in Aleppo Governorate, Afrin District , Jindires subdstrict, in line with the first strategic objective of the Humanitarian Response Plan and the goals and recommendations of the Food Security Sector.
This proposal aims to provide 70% of the food needs (1,500 Kcal) for 2,083 families, Those suffering from severe food insecurity, that include 10,602 individuals (2,037 men, 2,375 women, 3,193 boys, 2,915 girls) who suffer from food insecurity in 15 camps in the Jindires district (actual numbers),
To avoid any overlap, SAED will share project information and activities with the Food Security Sector Working Group and humanitarian partners operating in the area. In the event of actual overlap in one or more camps, SAED will replace these camps with others experiencing food gaps.
The planned objectives will be achieved through two main interventions:
1. Distribution of regular food baskets to 2083 households over 12 rounds (8 months in 2024 and 4 months in 2025) for IDPs to meet 70% of the food needs. Food baskets are designed in accordance with food sector recommendations.
Food basket components ( Rice, Pasta, Bulgur, Beans, Chickpeas, Fish, Tuna, Lentils ,Oil, Salt, Sugar, Tomato Paste)
The cost of the activity in 2024 = 2083 FB * 8 rounds * 58$ =966512UDS
The cost of the activity in 2025 = 2083 FB * 4 rounds * 58$ =483256 UDS
Food baskets will be procured from within Syria, taking into consideration measures to mitigate potential operational risks.
2. Distribution of bread to 2083 HHs for 12 months (8 months in 2024 and 4 months in 2025), with 1 kilogram of bread per HH of fewer than five members and 2 kilograms of bread for families of five or more members.
The cost of the activity in 2024 = 2083 FB *240 Days * 1 $=499.940 UDS
The cost of the activity in 2025 = 2083 FB * 120 Days * 1$ =249960 UDS
Bread will be purchased from bakeries within Syria, taking into consideration the quality control of the bread. This includes: appointing a permanent inspector from Saed during bread baking in the contracted bakeries to ensure quality and cleanliness, as well as adhering to a strict quality control plan for both the quality and quantity.
Beneficiaries will be selected based on clear and transparent criteria, including:
- Families headed by a woman
- Families with two or more children under the age of five
- The family in which one or more of its members suffers from special conditions (disabled, elderly, breastfeeding, pregnant....)
- Families that do not have a fixed income
