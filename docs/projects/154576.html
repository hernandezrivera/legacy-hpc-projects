Project Summary

In 2019 REACH will continue to provide Information Management support to the CCCM cluster as well as its collaboration with other relevant CCCM stakeholders.
With the objectives to 1) address information gaps on IDPs living in formal camps and informal sites, 2) improve planning and monitoring of humanitarian assistance and 3) facilitate risk reduction and maintenance, in and out of camp settings, REACH will conduct: Camp profiling, Risk Assessment Site Priority (RASP), and Intentions Surveys in formal camps and informal sites, Area Based Assessment (ABA) in key areas of return across Iraq.
Under these activities, the followings outputs may be developed, depending on need:
- IDP Camp Directory;
- IDP Camp Profiling Comparative report;
- Rapid Assessment Site Profile Subnational snapshots;
- Intensions Key findings snapshots (English and Arabic) – for informal and formal sites;
- Intensions Comparative report/storyboard;
- ABA Area profile;
- ABA Maps;
- Support to the CCCM Cluster in the form of a part time dedicated REACH Assessment Officer;
- Support to the CRC Steering Committee and CCCM Cluster in the form of a dedicated staff member, who will assist in developing the connection between the data collected through the ABAs, the 5ws and how to create a localised, granular area-based approach in the CRCs.
It is expected that this programming will target around 52,218 IDPs living in formal sites (17,863 persons) and in camps (29,281) as well as host communities, returnees, and those never displaced all living within the catchment areas of the CRCs (5,074). As per the gender disaggregation, the project will aim at targeting equally women and men where possible. Due to the nature of REACH activities which are focused on informing the national humanitarian community and coordination structures it is expected that the intervention will directly benefit the humanitarian community at large, thanks to the production of outputs which will be available to all clusters and partners. Internally displaced persons living in informal sites will indirectly benefit from improved informal site monitoring and referral system while IDPs living in in-camp locations will indirectly benefit from improved camp life cycle management. Moreover, those living within the catchment areas of the CRCs will indirectly benefit from programming planned against the area-based assessments.

Needs

2019 will see the humanitarian context shifting from previous years, as active conflict against ISIL has largely ended, however, an estimated 6.5 million are still in need of assistance. Among these, 500,000 internally displaced people remain living in IDP camps and around 1,500,000 IDPs are living in informal sites across Iraq.IDPs living in and out of camp in protracted displacement face a variety of barriers to return, which result in low percentages of IDPs intending to return in the next 3 or 12 months as evidenced by the REACH initiative and CCCM intentions surveys. Significantly, for the informal settlements, 92 % of IDP households reported intentions to remain in current location. Only 1 % of IDP households reported to have intention to return to their Area of Origin. Recent months have seen the returns to areas of origins slow down, with the barriers to return not easily fixable, including (in different weighing depending on areas of origin and in/out of camp displacement): 1) security related concerns (lack of security forces, fear of discrimination), 2) damaged or destroyed homes, 3) lack of livelihood opportunities and in particular in the Mosul Response camps 4) lack of finance. Other factors include not sufficient basic services in areas of origin, the presence of explosive hazards and challenges related to the social cohesion. Moreover, as the VII Round (August 2018) of the Rapid Assessment of Site Profiling (RASP) in informal IDP sites conducted by REACH and CCCM partners indicates, the basic infrastructure and services such as access to electricity, primary source of drinking water, primary healthcare services as well as shelter needs were to most frequently cited challenges/needs by respondents. As such, further comprehensive assessment is needed to fully understand the complex needs of vulnerable IDP populations in critical shelters and informal sites, especially that such needs are likely to increase with colder weather in the coming winter months.For both IDPs living in formal and informal settlements as well as those returning to their areas of origin, those in protracted displacement, highly vulnerable residents in host communities, and non-displaced families in newly accessible areas, lack of basic services, high unemployment rates in addition to damages to infrastructure and personal residences will continue to pose the biggest challenges in 2019. As such, analysis country wide and over time is required to better understand the needs of different crisis-affected population groups to support a principled, effective and inclusive strategic coordination and evidence-based humanitarian action at the national, regional, governorate, and local levels. Additionally, as the CCCM Cluster anticipates, in 2019 2 million IDPs will reside in formal and informal settlements which is an increase of around 500,000 from 2018 estimates. As some of the camps were constructed quickly, and with the backdrop of a rapidly expanding IDP population, conditions of infrastructure and services vary from one camp to another, and most are likely to deteriorate due to prolonged use and lack of maintenance. In light of the complexity of the scenario described above and in order to support strategic and evidence-based programming in 2019, REACH will continue providing IM products, mostly dedicated to the comprehensive analysis of forecasted displacement patterns the upcoming months, to enable specialized assistance to reach the most vulnerable displaced individuals across Iraq supporting safe and voluntary returns, while contributing to the progressive identification of durable solutions to displacement. Additionally, REACH will support the harmonization and operationalization of information coverage across Iraq, through regular sharing of information via multi-platform information materials, to support coordination efforts, assist gap identification, and improve assistance of IDPs living in informal settlements.

Activities

In line with the REACH’s 2019 CCCM strategy to provide a comprehensive picture of IDP displacement patterns and conflict-affected populations’ priority needs in accessible and hard-to-reach areas as well as informing humanitarian planning, prioritization, and delivery of assistance, REACH will contribute to objectives 1 and 3 of CCCM Cluster in 2019. As such, throughout 2019 REACH will conduct regular nationwide assessments of IDP households residing in formal camps and informal sites through three existing activities: Camp profiling, Risk Assessment Site Priority (RASP), and Intentions Surveys. Additionally, REACH will conduct location-specific assessments; the Area Based Assessment (ABA) in key areas of return across Iraq to inform the Cluster’s support of the CRC initiative and durable returns more broadly. In addition to the specific outputs per activity below, REACH will provide clean, anonymized household and Key Interviews (KI) datasets, and will deliver key findings presentations. REACH will also provide additional support to the CCCM Cluster in the form of a part time dedicated Assessment Officer position, who can work on ad hoc requests from the Cluster on additional analysis on REACH data and products specific to the needs of the Cluster, and will sit up at UNAMI with the Cluster 1 day a week. 1: IDP camp profiling REACH will conduct two rounds of profiling of all accessible IDP Camps in Iraq with 100 or more households, through a mixed data collection methodology. Under this activity following outputs will be developed:a) IDP Camp Directory;b) Comparative report.2: Rapid Assessment Site ProfileREACH will conduct one baseline RASP in informal sites and collective centres across Iraq, to support the 2019 humanitarian community objectives. Site coverage will be determined based on criteria agreed upon with the Cluster and drawing from the 2018 methodology. The data collection methodology and tool will remain consistent with past rounds to enable some comparability of data, but will be revised based on lessons learned from 2018. REACH will provide support in the form of training partners on the RASP methodology and tool, in order to support the development of their independent RASP assessments in support of the Cluster.Under this activity following outputs will be developed:a) Subnational snapshots. 3: Intentions surveyIn Formal camps REACH will conduct an intentions survey in tandem with each round of camp profiling (2 total). As such, the sampling methodology will mirror that of the camp profiling household survey. In informal sites the survey will be conducted in tandem with the RASP baseline (1 in total). However, as RASP is a qualitative assessment, the informal sites intentions survey will use the following methodology: 1. Identification of sites using RASP data and selecting the largest in terms of household population, of approx. 100 HH or more (approx. 5-15% of all sites). 2. Target sample size will be calculated based on a 95%/10% using systematic random sampling method.Under this activity following outputs will be developed: a. Key findings snapshots (English and Arabic) for both formal and informal sites’ Intension Survey;b. Comparative report/storyboard. 4: Area Based Assessment (ABA) REACH will conduct 2 to 4 ABAs to provide baseline data for CRC programming, prioritization, strategy, and advocacy purposes. The locations of these ABAs will be determined according to where new CRCs are established. These assessment exercises will build off of the ABA framework that was validated and endorsed by the CRC Steering Committee and the JCMC in 2018. The methodology combines secondary and primary data collection to produce a multi-layered understanding of areas of return: household needs and vulnerabilities, the state of public services and infrastructure, and security and access concerns. It is designed to be the same in each CRC, to allow comparability between the CRCs, as well as to be aggregated to National level. Methodology for each of the assessments will be adjusted to fit the specific needs of the CRC.As endorsed by the CRC Steering Committee, the REACH ABA methodology will also be used by partners in their CRCs, should they have the resources and expertise to do so. In this instance, REACH will provide the tools, training and guidance in conducting their own ABA.Under this activity following outputs will be developed:a) Area Profiles;b) Maps.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation Measure 1: It is assumed that REACH will be able to access each location. Authorization from local authorities and local leaders will be sought for each location prior to data collection. In case the access challenges occur in certain parts of the country, for example Anbar, the data collection will include combination of direct, in-person interviews and remote, phone-based interviews.Mitigation Measure 2: Should security deteriorate, project areas would be switched to those still accessible and/or trainees would be encouraged and subsidized to travel to accessible areas, if feasible. ACTED security protocol shall be applied.Mitigation Measure 3: Regular security context follow-up to accurately assess any changes to the risk level in areas of operation. Close collaboration with relevant stakeholders to ensure access to targeted sites.Mitigation Measure 4: ACTED and REACH enforces the use of track 24 with speed limits and daily incident logs, combined with driver training to mitigate this risk. Pre-planning of missions, including security clearance for movements of the program teams, take into consideration the types and quality of the roads.Mitigation Measure 5: Regular security context follow-up to accurately assess any changes to the risk level in areas of operation. Security / evacuation plans established.Mitigation Measure 6: For locations where the risk of security incidents is high, ACTED establishes movements in convoy with multiple communication mechanisms, tracking capabilities based on GPS and Thuraya Satellite phone systems as redundant communications, as well as hourly communication checks in order to allow an on-time monitoring of the teams. ACTED has developed the Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) which are the individual safety and security rules to be followed when a specific incident occurs.Mitigation Measure 7: All staff are trained on Mine Risk Education and will closely follow on reports of UXO incidents to get better understanding which areas are mostly contaminated. Same knowledge will be obtained from host communities who usually have good understanding which areas are safe and which are not.Mitigation Measure 8: Regular security context follow-up to accurately assess any changes to the risk level in areas of operation.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Ensuring accountability to affected populations is at the core of REACH’s programming. All activities proposed are beneficiary-facing exercises which ensure that the needs of affected women, men, boys and girls in formal and informal sites and areas of return are involved in the different stages of partner programmes. Their core purpose is to create site and sector specific understanding of the situation as reported by the affected population in order to better inform the humanitarian community and enable effective prioritization of assistance. All assessment activities are designed with consideration for protection concerns and sensitivity towards the population of interest. Indicators and questions will be designed with these factors in mind, including ensuring minimal collection of personal identifiers and the removal of any such data prior to dissemination of datasets. With respect to qualitative data collection, focus group discussions will be sex segregated in accordance with cultural norms and to ensure female participants are able to vocalize their opinions comfortably. Personal identifiable information will not be publicly disseminated in order to minimise/eliminate protection concerns for the assessed population. All data will be aggregated to a location and therefore no household identifiers will be publicly visible from the reports and maps. Where relevant, sensitive protection information will be passed on to the protection Cluster, in order to support interventions and planning. If agencies request the raw data containing household identifiers, then the sensitive name/contact details will be removed and replaced with a unique key so that the identifier information can be reconnected at a later date, based on the respect of protection standards. If there is a further request for the names, then REACH will contact OCHA as the coordination body to examine whether the requesting agency can receive the identifier data or not and under which conditions. REACH collects personally identifiable data only for specific purposes. REACH will include a request for consent within assessments to share personally identifiable information collected, which will only occur in the following circumstances and with the following parties: a) For REACH internal use for the purposes of data management (i.e. avoiding duplicate data collection); b) With donor / partner organisation where data-sharing agreement exists, and only for humanitarian purposes; c) With partner organisation and/ or agency where specific and urgent need occurs, for example urgent medical needs; d) With the owner (i.e. the assessed party) of the information if requested. REACH has designed and will implement the current proposal using an approach which contributes significantly to gender equality in partner programming. The research activity specifically allows the disaggregation of all data by gender and vulnerable sub-sets in order to inform programming that addresses the needs of unaccompanied women and households with particularly vulnerable individuals such as young girls, pregnant or lactating women, and elderly and disabled persons. Based on the data provided by REACH, partners will be able to tailor gender specific activities to the specific needs of these groups by providing such services as: gender-segregated information sessions, gender-segregated distribution lines, and gender-segregated toilet and shower facilities. Due to the limits of characters more information on the accountability to affected population can be received upon request.
