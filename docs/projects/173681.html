Project Summary

As part of this intervention, AAH will provide technical and organizational support to the health establishments of the two health districts regularly affected by shocks in order to better equip them to respond to health / nutrition emergencies and to address psychological distress. Health workers and community volunteers will participate in trainings on health / nutrition / mental health and care practices so they can better address the different needs and facilitate referral to activities provided by AAH and other actors in the region. AAH will set up mobile clinics to provide emergency assistance in nutrition / health / mental health for affected and isolated vulnerable populations in the Mayo Sava & Mayo Tsanaga divisions). The package of services and care that will be delivered free of charge will primarily target children under five, pregnant and lactating women, but also disabled people and the elderly among the newly displaced. With regard to the MHCP (mental health and care practices) component, the activities will target children over six and adults with a specific focus on pregnant and lactating women to ensure they follow appropriate care practices and to limit the impact of traumatic stress on pregnancies and infants.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In the Far North region in Cameroun, according to the Food and Nutrition Security Surveillance and Early Warning Bulletin, 16,400 households in the region are in need of emergency food assistance and nearly 31.6% of the population saw their sources of income impacted by the barrier measures against COVID-19. The conclusions of the Harmonized Framework for Food Security revealed that the whole region is under food stress with 12% of the population in a crisis. This proportion will increase if no support is provided during the lean period and depending on the impact of COVID-19 on the projected phase (June -Dec.). 21,000 Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) cases have been admitted between January and June 2020. A recent localized screening carried out by AAH in 3 health areas of the health district of Mora, shows a proportion of SAM is established at 6% with a proportion of GAM around 20%. Since the end of AAH's support for the SAM treatment integration in Mora health district in December 2019, SAM cases management activities, were practically abandoned in the health centers (no supervision by the health district chief, almost non-existence of screening at community level, rupture of RUTF ect.), as no other nutrition partners are present. Regarding maternal and child health, indicators of the use of health services also show poor access to care for these targeted groups. According to data from our program (February-December 2019) in Mora health district, ANC 1 rate was 26% even if that of 1st contact was almost 100%. The rate of assisted delivery in health center was barely 40% with, however, a high rate of home delivery of 55%. The rate of attendance of the curative consultation by U 5 was established at 68% (0.6 contact / child / year). In Koza health district, an attendance rate among children under 5 of 66% was assessed with around 15% of health workers trained on the IMCI approach. The assisted delivery rate was very low (11.24%) in these health areas A gender-sensitive approach will be integrated in the recruitment of community volunteers. The relays and mobilizers recruited will then be introduced to the ACF code of conduct and the PSEA policy.The mapping work and referral mechanisms with specialized actors will be improved to cover specific needs.
