Project Summary

The purpose of this project, in line with the Ethiopia Education Cluster Strategy (2021), and with the INEE Minimum Standards for Education in Emergency, is to provide an education in emergencies response for a total of 12,373 Internally Displaced People (IDP) children (5,108 girls/7,265 boys). Of these 9,358 children are living in host communities of 3 woredas of Benishangul-Gumuz (BG) Region, and 3,015 in 3 affected woredas of Afar Region (1,361 girls/1,654 boys). These regions are two of the nine priority regions identified by the Education Cluster for humanitarian response, and education is integral to this.
The target population of the project are IDP children who are living with host communities in both regions, as well as the host-community children whose education and family life is likely to have been impacted by the influx of people from other areas. An initial rapid assessment will help to further identify specific needs for response, particularly those of the most vulnerable children, including children with disability, orphaned children, and children separated from their parents or who are unaccompanied due to the crisis.
Link will coordinate with the Education Cluster at national level, the Education Cluster at BG and Afar regional level, the BG and Afar Regional Education Bureaus, the identified zonal and woreda education bureaus, other INGO partners, and implement in tandem with a national NGO partner in each region.
Link Education Ethiopia--which is part of Link Education International (LEI), a family of not for profit organizations working together to transform education for children and communities across impoverished areas of Ethiopia, Malawi, Rwanda and Uganda-- already works in both regions, providing technical assistance to the Regional Education Bureaus through the TARGET project which supports GEQIP-E, and focuses on school leadership. Building on these existing relationships, Link will provide additional emergency support to a total of 60 schools (30 in each region), and 12,373 children with focus on: construction/rehabilitation of existing school structures/child-friendly temporary learning spaces; distribution of teaching and learning materials; provision of Social Emotional Learning (SEL) to students, teaching staff and family; provision of training for teachers, school leaders, woreda experts and cluster supervisors on disasters, crisis-sensitive education, safeguarding and risk management related issues (including GIRP/literacy, numeracy).

1. - Projects activities

Project activities will start and be further informed by an initial education rapid needs-assessment in the selected woredas of both regions to ascertain more detailed information on needs from affected populations. Activities will be aligned with HRP education objectives and indicators, meet conflict-sensitivity standards, as well as the INEE Minimum Standards for Education in Emergency: Preparedness, Response and Recovery. The education rapid needs-assessment will be conducted following the training of key education stakeholders on education in emergencies response and conflict-sensitive data collection to strengthen the capacity of education institutions and personnel to gather data and provide crisis-sensitive education to affected girls, boys and children with disabilities.Link will work through a national NGO partner in each region to deliver activities to ensure that the most vulnerable are reached, and that the project is implemented with a strong understanding of context and the impact of the conflict on children and their communities.The project will work to ensure that there are temporary, safe learning spaces in place for IPD and host-community children. Depending on information collected at assessment, this may mean: constructing classrooms made from local materials and providing furniture, or making use of spaces which are safe to use within schools that are closed or damaged. It may also mean doing some rehabilitation of a school structure to make it safe. COVID-19 prevention will be considered in and around these spaces. Teaching and learning materials will be provided to ensure that learning continues, and to ensure that adolescent girls do not miss any school days, sanitary items will be also provided.Training will be provided for teachers, school leaders and school supervisors on disaster and risk management, as well as pedagogy which is responsive to gender, inclusion and safeguarding in the context of internal displacement and conflict. Content will include understanding and responding to children’s needs, and building resilience in challenging circumstances.Psycho-social support will be extended to both teachers and students, and as part of the teacher training, teachers and school leaders will learn how to identify children who need this kind of support, to provide it, or as relevant, refer to others (parents, community bodies/services) through Social Emotional Learning (SEL).The project will provide school readiness assistance through accelerated school readiness (ASR) for children and their communities to minimize loss of learning due to the emergency.Community engagement will be cross-cutting at every stage of the project, and training will be provided to community-school structures (SIC, SMC, PTA, KETB) on crisis sensitive education, risk management, conflict-resolution, safety, safeguarding and child protection, and the provision of life-saving messages to children and the wider community.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

One of the strong characteristics of Link’s work is working in partnership with local, regional and national actors, and with communities/civil society in design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of projects. Link works closely with government partners at all levels in the FCDO-funded Supporting Transition of Adolescent Girls through Enhanced Systems (STAGES) project in SNNPR region, as well as in the FCDO-funded Technical Assistance to Reinforce GEQIP-E (TARGET) project in Afar, Benishangul-Gumuz, Gambella and Somali Regions. The project will work closely with the Regional, Zonal (as relevant), and Woreda Education Bureaus in project design and initial rapid assessment, building on and establishing partnership as well as developing institutional capacity to provide crisis-sensitive education for girls, boys and children affected by disability. With an adaptive management approach, joint monitoring of project activities will be set up with key stakeholders on a regular basis, followed by review and planning meetings to agree how to address challenges arising, and agree adaptations. The project will document the findings of project monitoring to help identify what works well in the context for the children affected by the crisis, and what does not. Evidence and learning will be shared within and across the 2 regions, with other HRP partners, and through our 2 national NGO partners, as well as with the umbrella CSOs Network--Basic Education Network - Ethiopia-- which advocates on issues affecting education in Ethiopia. Feedback mechanisms to affected communities will be integrated.As already stated above, the project intends to work through a national NGO partner in each region to deliver the project's activities and increase outreach, particularly to those most marginalized by the crisis, and to benefit from strong local contextual knowledge and expertise.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

The initial education rapid needs-assessment will establish a methodology which ensures that all respondents, including the most vulnerable community members, can safely participate. Care will be taken that the data collection and analysis process does not exacerbate or create conflict as per the INEE minimum standards for education in emergencies, and ethical considerations will include the basic principles of respect and non-discrimination. Those collecting information will receive training on their responsibility to protect participants, informing them of the purpose of data collection, the right not to participate, the right to withdraw at any point without negative effects, and the right to confidentiality and anonymity. The rapid assessment will utilize a range of crisis-sensitive tools and methodologies to safely hear from girls, boys, parents, community leaders and members, teachers and school leaders, including focus group discussion and key informant interviews. A ‘do no harm’ approach will be central to safely ensuring that feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members.As a valuable past experience, Link worked with partners in SNNPR on the STAGES project to develop and implement a rapid assessment to safely ascertain the education and wellbeing status of girls during school closures due to COVID-19 in 2020. The assessment was developed to be sensitive to gender, age and other characteristics, to be safe and to be confidential as was relevant.
