Project Summary

HOPE is planning to work in improving access to PHC and WASH services for the Vulnerable population in Tendalti Locality of White Nile State that will contribute to the three HRP Health sector objectives and 2nd, 3rd & 5th HRP WASH sector objectives for 2020.
In this project HOPE will (1) Provide essential primary and secondary health care services, including EmONC and referrals for 20,000 individuals disaggregated to 41% (8200) male, 59% (11800) female, 8% (1600) elderly, 63% (12600) children and 29% (5800) adults through conduction of inpatient & outpatient consultations, mental health consultations, antenatal care sessions, postnatal sessions and referral for specialized treatment that are not available in PHC facilities. (2) Increase access to health services by supporting referral pathways by supporting mobile medical units. (3) Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services through (a) conduction trainings for health staff on different health topics and, (b) conduction trainings for community health workers/ promoters on different health topics. (4) Increase access to health services by rehabilitating and refurbishing health facilities with provision of medical equipment the rehabilitation of health facility with emergency operation and support. (5) Provide and enable crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to limited sanitation services. (6) Provide and enable crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to hygiene services. (7) Provide outbreaks ‘at-risk’ population access to minimum WASH for Health package.
HOPE will focus on Tendalti Locality of White Nile State based on poor indicators with regard to PHC, WASH, RH service provision, patient/service provider ratio. The BIN/ targets in Umm Tendalti locality are 22,593 in needs of health services including 904 Pregnant, 3,841 U5, and 452 Elderly people (PIN & Severity 2020 HPC).
HOPE will consider accountability to affected people through fully community participation, strengthen the current community mechanism/ committee as well as do follow up for all benefits targeted separately to assess the project, identify challenges and provide give recommendations to improve the project outcomes.

1 - Needs

HOPE suggested this multi-sectoral intervention due to the high rate of contamination and vectors including of flies, and mosquito (Aedes Aegypti) that are living at the locality, so Tendalti becomes at risk to expand the current outbreak of Cholera, Dengue Fever, Yellow Fever, and Chikungunya as well as high Malaria cases reported in this locality due to lack of vector control.During the last flood and rains, Inter-Agency Assessment Mission was held in Sep 2019, findings and recommendations for Health and WASH are: - The sanitation is a big issue; no latrines in most villages; open defecation is widely practiced increasing of Malaria cases,- Total affected population in the northern corridor are 198,000 people covered by only one hospital in Wad Belail is not well equipped; all water sources North of Tendalti were affected by floods; drinking water are expected to be contaminated, - In Tendalti there are 16 private hospitals; - In Almiliah village there is the increasing number of cases of snakes and scorpion bites. - They recommended the need for mobile clinics to cover the needs in the remotes villages. - Different gaps were identified in Tendalti hospital including lack of staff, rehabilitation of the hospital's building and patients’ words; and lack of PHC supplies.-There is only one midwife in Kitra village; no trained midwives in other villages.- Malaria and respiratory infections are the most reported cases in Tendalti hospital,- No water sources in Kitra village; drinking water is suspected to be contaminated. - No latrines in Kitra and El Mailiah; open defecation is widely practiced. - No vector control was conducted; increasing of flies and mosquitos was reported in all villages visited. - No health promotion was functioning as well as Jerry cans and soap distribution is highly recommended.The SMoH reported that, the coverage of health services only for 49% of People with only 34 Gov Health Facilities out of Tendalti, 1 Gov hospital and 23 Private hospital located in Tendalti city, only 3 General Medical Doctor, 27 Medical Assistant, 97% of Health facilities with no delivery room, 97% with no birth registry.HOPE will focus on Tendalti based on poor indicators with regard to PHC, WASH, RH service provision, patient/service provider ratio by current service providers. Inadequate provision of and access to PHC services especially for women/ girls and children across Tendalti remains the main constraint as these same areas have high mortality rates for infants and mothers, the reasons for this deterioration of health services are lack of equitable access to life-saving services. The main communicable diseases and outbreaks in the target locality are Malaria, AWD, Respiratory Diseases, Schistosomiasis, and Typhoid as well as high risk to expand the last outbreaks of Chikungunya, and Dengue Fever that is happening in Sudan due to victor is living in the locality.There is an increase in other forms of GBV such as domestic violence and early marriage of young girls. The natural disaster and poverty have also increased the number of women who are the sole providers for their families. Sudan also faces issues with more chronic levels of malnutrition with one in every three children under the age of five years affected. These children will suffer from more long term developmental and health impact resulting in both a shorter stature than non-affected peers, educational outcomes as well as poorer health in the immediate and longer-term future. As a result, these children will have lower lifetime earnings, and lower productivity as adults, influencing the economic growth and potential of the entire country.HOPE, through MoU that is signed with SMoH in White Nile, has access to health facilities that are not fully functional in Tendalti as well as HOPE will fully coordinate with WHO and UNICEF of Health and WASH partners in Tendalti.

Provide outbreaks ‘at-risk’ population access to minimum WASH for Health package through access to safe drinking/ chlorinated water by water treatment and Vector control.

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

During implementation, HOPE will support the capacities of committee members and assist to know their rights to health and WASH. All essential drugs at the pharmacy will be handled with the utmost care and storage - warehouses will be clean to avoid contamination. This project will include psycho-social stimulation activities for infants and young children will also be provided with psychosocial support to help mothers deal with their own distress and improve care for their children. Child protection messages on prevention and response as well as referral mechanisms will be included in activities related to health and WASH outreach and awareness-raising. All health staff will be trained on referral procedures and mechanisms linking patients directly to experts from other agencies and government is already supporting the health sector at nearby facility and other secondary facilities. Adequate shelter and waiting areas of all the health centers will be provided with all basic furniture and clean water to be accessed by all seeking services. Children and women will be given priorities. Privacy during medical history taking and examination, keeping medical data without unnecessary disclosure. Awareness-raising during community mobilization on health and WASH, education a key component that HOPE has put in place for better understanding among the beneficiaries for self-protection on various programmatic matters. HOPE also will develop a system to collect feedback from beneficiaries to measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.
