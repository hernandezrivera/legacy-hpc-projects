Project Summary

WASH Interventions will aim at ensuring that populations affected by displacement have reduced risk of exposure to water and sanitation related diseases. NRC will provide access to safe water and sanitation as well as promotion of better hygiene practices that lead to behaviour change.
Primarily target areas are White Nile State, Area East in Gedaref and South Kordofan States through stand-alone WASH interventions, whilst there will be complementary emergency WASH interventions in Al-Jazeera, Gedaref, West and North Darfur states through Education (WASH in Schools), Shelter, MBA, ICLA and Camp Management integrated programming.
• WASH interventions in the South Kordofan Area Office are stronger and more durable in nature. Pastoralists and herders frequently pass through these areas (sometimes causing rifts between clans) but very much in sync with ancient seasonal migration patterns. In the South, the focus of WASH activities will be in the harvesting and better utilisation of rain through construction of dams with improved offtake structures and facilities i.e. infiltration gallery, (slow sand filter where feasible), solar pumping, elevated tank and distribution network – as part of an Integrated Water Resource Management Plan for different villages. Capacity building will be an integral part of the project for the village development committee, local NGO and local government technical workers and groundwater sources with excess yields being used for irrigation.
• The While Nile state area office will prioritise on protracted South Sudanese refugees living in temporary settlement (widely referred to as Open Areas). These refugees suffer lack of tenure on property and land, as such WASH infrastructure is poorly built, inadequately maintained, and prone to destruction and failure. Water supplies are minimal and often contaminated. In White Nile State, current WASH interventions are nested under the umbrella of Education under WASH in schools and NRC is gradually looking into a more stand-alone WASH interventions in the wider community, especially in terms of basic WASH provisions starting with drinking water supplies and sanitation infrastructures.
• The East Area Office was established primarily to serve an influx of Tigrayan refugees fleeing the recent conflict in Ethiopia. Their needs will be continuously assessed and planned to be met through the provision of integrated, basic WASH services which in turn are currently integrated with shelter provision as well as WASH in schools. Standalone WASH projects are not prioritised in the upcoming year due to other humanitarian actors in the area with similar capacity to serve the currently 50,000+ Ethiopian refugees in Um Rakuba and Tunayadbah camps while supporting other WASH actors like CARE international.
The West Area Office continues to service the needs of internally displaced Sudanese settled into ‘gathering sites’ in Ag Geneina town, West Darfur. NRC also recently opened up an office in El Fasher, North Darfur. Once again a euphemism has been used to describe undocumented settlement in small areas where people have no rights and are being discouraged to stay. Services to these groups are minimal and temporary in nature. NRC WASH services will be limited to support interventions in schools.

1 - Needs

Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Majority of the project location has the majority of the displaced population source their water from unprotected/open water sources which are prone to contamination. Poor water quality at water sources is due to multiple factors including open defection around the water points and limited community management of existing water points, leaving them prone to algae contaminations and pollution from waste debris in unprotected water sources. It is not uncommon for women and girls to walk more than 500m to collect water in many of the camps and settlements we serve. Hafir ponds, sand dams, and even hand pumps from which water is collected can be potentially very dangerous for women to visit, especially before and after dark. Secondly, we see clearly within our target populations that current volumes of water supplied to beneficiaries is not far above ‘survival levels’ as defined in the Sphere Standards. Whereas rainfall data, perennial flooding and static rivers levels would indicate that far larger quantities of water are available for use, not just in drinking water but for washing cleaning and cultivation . Bringing safe clean water closer to the point of use can have profound implications for women and girls in many ways and their safety and dignity can be better assured.Existing sanitation facilities are either full and overflowing, some latrines are without privacy and withoutdoors, abandoned or in disrepair, and often there is a high user to latrine facility ratio especially as a result of new influx brought about by the needs displacement from the crisis. Drawing upon numerous internal assessments , frequent updates on the emergency WASH needs from the recent displacement, meetings held with partners in the field and through consultation at country level, certain recurrent themes quickly become apparent. These themes are then echoed by government and UN agencies. The first of these themes spotlights the level of open defaecation prevalent in Sudan and just how far behind the country is lagging. The eradication of this practice has been prioritized by many in the sector . A quick survey of schools across Sudan and in particular those visited by NRC in its areas of operation usually indicate inadequate levels of hygiene awareness amongst pupils. This reflects what is happening in the adult population and why open defaecation is so prevalent. Consequently, inside and outside of schools, NRC needs to be more inclusive and participatory in its approach to hygiene promotion. To this end, the WASH strategy prioritises the introduction of Community-Led Total Sanitation, the London School of Tropical Hygiene and Medicine’s - WASH ‘Em Approach and Children’s Hygiene and Sanitation Training - CHAST . In consideration of the volatile context, NRC needs to retain enough capacity to respond to new emergencies and to work innovatively with the WASH team to respond quickly and effectively. Each of the abovementioned interventions in the response plan address the necessary transition from emergency to durable solutions. This approach to hygiene awareness raising amongst beneficiaries adds weight to the message. Then, by increasing quantities of available water, NRC’s overall approach is linked to livelihoods and food security, allowing people to cultivate their own kitchen gardens or larger areas of land. And finally, through capacity building at all levels and within NRC we draw on the experience of the whole WASH team to deliver significant benefits to all.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The response strategy plans to facilitate and increase the access of WASH facilities to affected people, including those with special needs and the elderly. WASH response will be done in full package as Water Supply, Sanitation and Hygiene.Provision of safe drinking water: Sustainable and equitable access to drinking water will be ensured through the construction of dignified and secure hydraulic WASH infrastructures. NRC will construct new water points and rehabilitate dilapidated water points to address the gaps associated with access to adequate water supply. The water points in this case include shallow wells, deep wells, water yards, hafirs, sand dams and infiltration galleries in the South Kordofan State alongside improvements of surface water supplies, tap stands and shallow wells in the West Darfur Darfur State, Gedaref and AljAzeera State. Additional water supply works will include rehabilitation/construction of new water points in form of tapstands in the White Nile State. Water quality, delivery and monitoring: NRC will also conduct periodic water quality analysis on existing water sources points for bacteriological, physical and chemical parameters from a competent or certified laboratory. The water quality tests will also be conducted before handing over of any new or rehabilitated water point. NRC will continuously sensitize the community on other traditional water quality management options notably boiling of drinking water to ensure that the community is able to continue with water quality management at household level even in the absence of aqua tabs. Provision of appropriate sanitation and handwashing facilities: In order to ensure that interventions are gender responsive, latrines will be located in safe locations within reach of the households, while ensuring that adequate privacy is provided. NRC will consult with women and girls before agreeing on appropriate latrine locations. NRC will also guide on the design, building materials, size, while also ensuring that the target beneficiaries will feel safe while using those sanitation facilities. In addition, NRC will promote construction of family/household latrines as opposed to communal latrines that have proven difficult to clean and maintain from the lessons learnt on previous assessments especially in the White Nile camps. Menstrual hygiene management: NRC will combine hardware interventions designed to address material deprivations such as the provision of absorbents (menstrual hygiene kits), and ensuring provision of improved Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) facilities. The software interventions will address deficits in knowledge of menstruation and management by providing education. Other interventions will include: • Focused group discussion and gender analysis to collect feedback on appropriate and preferred culturally sensitive approaches on safe disposal and management of menstrual waste to avert environmental health risks. • Partnership with local organizations demonstrating Menstrual Hygiene Management capacities will be sought and considered. Solid waste management and vector control: Solid waste management systems shall be introduced gradually and supported through awareness-raising to promote community-led waste management using simple waste collection pits, soak away pits for wastewater and water collection point for re-use. Dump sites will be constructed in schools in line with SPHERE standards and in consultation with local solid waste service providers and ministry of helath departments aligned to solid waste management. Markets based programming (MBA) – Markets: NRC will deliver on this through the use of cash & vouchers and use of the service providers notably contractors, subject to proper markets assessments. The use of vouchers will be preferred over in-kind as it gives the beneficiaries a variety of choices to purchase the actual items needed and in the desired brands.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project is expected to have moderate environmental impact based on the type of activities and activities targeting the beneficiaries.The project is expected to have moderate environmental impact based on the type of activities will be directed to the beneficiaries. Their potential adverse environmental effects and mitigation measures are outlined below:• Adverse effect 1: while displaced populations are currently moving from place to place to find safety and security for their family, it is expected that most of them could find a more permanent settlement in the course of this year. This will increase the density of populations in safe areas with potentially negative impact on social services such as waste management or sanitation infrastructure, which will in turn increase risk of disease outbreaks (cholera, etc.).Mitigation measure 1: as part of the hygiene promotion component of the project, NRC will ensure that populations are aware of the sanitation and hygiene risks they face and how to mitigate them by avoiding open air defecation. The latrines constructed will offer alternative solutions reducing the occurrence of open-air defecation. With funding from other donors, NRC would also endeavor to find durable solutions in case of longer-term displacement, including through more permanent housing and livelihoods in secure areas. • Adverse effect 2: the latrines that will be constructed could lead to pollution of water sources if not located at an adequate distance from water points.Mitigation measures 2: NRC will ensure that these latrines are located more than 30 meters away from any open water sources and will sensitize communities on how to avoid future borehole construction close to these latrines during and after the project. • Long-term cumulative effect: the increase of the population density will come with an increase of environment degradation (increased risk of open air defecation, increased quantity of waste when there is lack of infrastructure to manage them, and risk of water source pollution). Management: Through the hygiene and sanitation component, NRC will work with households and communities to protect their environment through community hygiene committees that will be put in place in each beneficiary community. NRC will train those committees on how to protect and manage their environment. Information about these communities will be shared with all WASH actors in that geographic area to avoid duplication or multiplication of committees within the same communities.• Positive environmental effects and measures to enhance them: the project will contribute to strengthening the capacities of affected communities to manage and better protect their environment. They will be better organized through the set up and capacity building of community committees, the availability of latrines and provision of faecal potties for children under 5 years will in turn contribute to reduced open defecation.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Within this project, men, women, girls and boys in displaced and host communities will be involved in the project, especially women who are excluded from decision-making circles, while ensuring it will not cause any harm. NRC will sensitize men during project activities, on the importance of women’s participation and explain the rationale of NRC’s focusing on female and children headed households. Females will participate in the same way as males in the identification of beneficiaries. Girls and women will be consulted about the location of latrines to ensure their protection and privacy. Men will also be sensitized on the importance of the participation of women, girls and boys in decision making in IDP settlements and host communities. NRC will conduct prior security checks and organize distributions of assistance in safe places where beneficiaries can access without fear. NRC will also continue to work on community acceptance making a clear difference between NRC’s assistance and the government’s one. The Do No Harm principle will guide all activities to ensure safe and effective participation of women, men, girls and boys.The selection of beneficiaries will be based on well-defined vulnerability criteria, exposure to protection risks, people at risk of eviction and people with less access to humanitarian assistance. NRC will target the areas most affected by displacement in accordance with the HRP and data collected from local NGOs, local and religious leaders. NRC will carry out this project regardless of status and will provide assistance to all categories of vulnerable and at-risk people affected by the crisis.
