Project Summary

44 million people live in Sudan and 33 million of them use mobile internet on the daily basis (ac-cording to the international statistics from 2020 on https://www.statista.com/). Instagram and Tiktok are in top-10 most frequently used applications (3d and 5th place on https://datareportal.com/ from world digital statistics) by young people of Sudan We are planning to organise the biggest collabora-tion of Sudanese influencers who use the plat-forms of Instagram and Tiktok.
We understand that in order to effectively level the existing problem, it is necessary not only to de-velop feedback and response mechanisms, but also to explain the problematics, first of all, to young people, changing their attitude to what is happening and explaining that the situation in which they can getting caught is not the norm. We plan to identify and unite the blogging community in Sudan through a project to educate their audi-ences about sexual exploitation and abuse by hu-manitarian workers.
All of the posts/videos will have same hashtags and contacts of where victims can go to receive help. We expect the project to resonate in Sudan society as such thing has never been done before and all of the participants that we contacted have 12 thou-sand-1 million subscribers, the greatest advantage of the project is the fact that it will continue raising awareness and informing young people even after it is officially finished as the posts and videos will stay on the social networks with the opportunity to like, share and comment. The algorithm of Tiktok shows its users videos that are popular in their countries or that were posted by the person they follow.
We contacted the most-followed Tiktok and Insta-gram users of Sudan which gives us a strong chance that those posts will be seen by thousands of peo-ple. We want to contribute to raising a strong socie-ty of people who are completely aware of what SEA is and how to fight it.
We want to shape zero tolerance to Sexual Exploi-tation and Abuse through opinion and support of role-models that young people look up to. Young generation is the future of this country, yet there is such little effort put at educating them on what SEA is and what they can do to help build a strong society. Persons with disabilities and from distant areas (and they also prove to be active users of the Internet) will also benefit from this project as all major activities will be held online.

1 - Needs

In confidential interviews with HADC staff in Khartoum and Darfur, women and girls report-ed that they often encounter issues of sexual exploitation when trying to find work or obtain food and medicine. Incl. 60% of the respondents expressed distrust to the workers of humanitar-ian organizations. About 56% explained that, first of all, mistrust arises towards local workers who can use their official position to receive compensation in the form of sex for the human-itarian assistance provided. HADC conducted a rather limited survey (30 ppl in Darfur and 78 in Khartoum), we understand that this issue is widespread in the whole country. Only 4% an-swered that they understand how to act in such situations.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Our target audience is younger generation of Sudan aged 12-30 which is subscribed to famous influencers. We have contacted 25 influencers from different states of Sudan that produce different types of content in different genres (comedy, cooking, beauty and lifestyle, news and politics, art, gaming etc.) and have 12 thousand – 1 million subscribers from all over Sudan which will allow us to reach diverse groups of young people of different interests and social background.We will conduct a series of online trainings for bloggers involved in the project on what SEA is; what the motives are for humanitarian workers who decide to take such a step; how to understand that you are being manipulated and these actions/words are abusive; why you shouldn't be afraid and it is necessary to report such cases, how to behave in such situations. By agreement with the donor, hotline contacts will be added to the information videos, where people can go in case of such situations. All of the posts/videos will have same hashtags and contacts of where victims can go to receive help.We have held initial negotiations with influencers to find out the number of their active subscribers, their age range and location to understand how many young people we can reach. We are planning to conduct online organizational meeting with all of the influencers involved in the project to see their vision of the best ways to reach their audience and for us to understand the best way to convey information correctly and what key messages to use and then to conduct a training-session to help influencers understand the situation deeper.Project activities:- Collecting data- Negotiating with influencers- Writing plans of organisational meetings and training-session with participants, appointing the coaches for training session- Purchasing the items needed for a project- Conducting a training session for influencers- Organising the organisationl meeting with participants, thinking through a strategy, discussing the appropriate type of content individually, hashtags- Influencers from Instagram post “stories” and photos to their accounts- Influencers from Tiktok post short videos to their account- Influencers collect data about the number of views and reposts, location and age of their followers, We change the strategy if needed- Influencers of Instargram and Tiktok start flashmob asking their followers to share and repost the information- Preparation of the final descriptive and financial report for the donor on the implementation of the projectAll of the participants will be asked to monthly share the statistics of activities in the frame-work of the project (this includes the number of views, reposts, likes and comments and the age range of users)We plan to start several flashmobs for young-sters to share their experience (influencers will suggest their subscribers to share their story or thought and to post it in their profile under common hashtag) and hashtags to help people find the information even after the project is finished.Before the start of the project, HADC plans to hold working meetings with SEA, PWG, RWG to discuss key messages and choose the most appropriate ways to convey information.Also, throughout the project, there will be close coordination with the SEA Working Group, as well as the PWG and RWG at the state level. With them, HADC will share requests that come to the project's hotline and take into account the feedback of these groups to shift / strengthen the emphasis of bloggers' rhetoric, etc. All HADC monitoring for the project will also be provided by SEA, PWG, RWG, so that they can use them in their work and share with other humanitarian actors.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” understands that communities living in countries affected by conflict are more vulnerable to the impacts of climate change. Armed conflict destroys infrastructure, weakens institutions, impedes access to basic services and erodes social cohesion - all critical to human resilience and adaptation to climate change. Communities are on a survival rope in the face of these complex crises. Given that the protection of civilians remains the highest priority in the implementation of this project, it also takes into account environmental lenses that will contribute to the effective management of natural resources, which, among other things, contribute to the restoration of basic livelihoods. In the implementation of this project, the Foundation operates with one of the humanitarian principles – "Do no harm", which is part of the concept of reducing the impact on the environment. In the context of climate change, the carbon cost of international deployment will also be taken into account when defining the workforce. The project will employ mainly local staff, with a limited presence of international staff. Local staff will be trained in the principles of environmental protection. Also during the project, the staff of the Foundation will conduct a systematic analysis of the cost-effectiveness of the impact of this humanitarian operation on the environment.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

People affected by conflicts, riots and natural disasters are almost always exposed to high risks of violence (physical, psychological and sexual), rights violations, coercion and exploitation. Individual and community protection capacities, as well as national protection mechanisms, are often compromised or insufficient to cope with the protection risks posed by ever-emerging threats. Thus, the situation in the field of protection will be taken into account separately, including when interacting with stakeholders and government officials. When developing and implementing the project, the location of the facilities where the project is being implemented will be taken into account, the distance of routes from real or potential threats, such as: violence; especially the risk or threat of gender-based violence and attacks by armed groups.Protection risks and violations will be determined by characteristics such as minority status, gender, sexual orientation, age and other diversity factors. Thus, the central role of protection in this project is, together with the communities, to identify groups at high risk in the field of protection, to find out the types and causes of such risk, and also to analyze these features, taking into account external factors that affect the protection mechanism itself. In addition, this approach involves identifying specific weaknesses that give rise to such risks, taking into account the unique experiences of men, women, girls and boys, such groups as the elderly, people with disabilities, LGBT and other groups.A wide range of representatives of the local community will have the opportunity to participate in the decision-making process in an inclusive and non-discriminatory manner that has a real impact on the provision of a humanitarian response. The CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” in this direction will use communicative approaches, assuming the presence of clear and direct feedback between the staff who develop and control the program, and the communities themselves. The project also plans to improve protection by differentiating the risks (needs) of the local community, which will allow for more targeted assistance. This will help, among other things, identify the underlying drivers of conflict and thereby improve the protection of the local community, while avoiding the harm that humanitarian action could otherwise do.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

During the implementation of the project, all activities will be carried out in compliance with the basic humanitarian standards of quality and accountability to the affected population. Actions and approaches will enable local communities to participate in key decision-making at all stages of program implementation: evaluation, analysis, planning, implementation, monitoring and revision. For this, communicative approaches will be used, involving transparent and constant two-way communication, through which information about the risks, needs and opportunities of communities will be collected.To submit complaints, the CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” has developed a mechanism for feedback and complaints. During the project, beneficiaries and stakeholders will have the opportunity to contact the management of the Foundation by telephone, e-mail, or through social networks. Also, at the locations of the project implementation, "feedback" boxes where you can leave requests will be installed. All complaints are brought to the attention of the management of the Foundation, and those who applied are provided with a response (if they have left their contact details). If this is a complaint, then it is communicated what measures were taken. Information about the start, implementation and completion of the project will be published on the organization's website, pages in social networks, and also posted in local media.
