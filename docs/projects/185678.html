Project Summary

Considering the situation of the COVID-19 pandemic and WASH in the ground level, ACF will continue to support critical WASH facilities to ensure the equitable access of the WASH facilities by operation and maintenance and/or construction/ upgrading of tube wells, water network, latrines, hand washing areas, washrooms, garbage bins for waste management, drainage, etc. along with the hygiene promotional sessions with necessary NFI kit distribution to improve the most critical personal hygiene practice. WASH initiatives will tackle GBV by targeting WASH related GBV risk areas in the camp environment (e.g. latrines, water collection points, distance to WASH facilities) and some facilities will be accessible to Person with special requirement. Beyond this, ACF will engage local partners (like HYSAWA), who will manage two blocks directly. ACF will coach, capacity build especially for the water quality test, and provide oversight to partner/s WASH team members with the ultimate goal of handing over operation & maintenance of WASH activities in future. 1 - Identified Needs

"Improved Water Services:There is a total of 10 water distribution zone (WDZ) in camps 1E and 2E (06 in 1E and 04 in 2E) serving 87% of the population, although equitable access has not been assessed and assured. Water quality surveillance shows that 99% of water networks tested for Free Residual Chlorine (FRC) met the minimum sector standard although 71% were above the maximum recommended 0.5 mg/l (O&M issues at remain). At household water quality surveillance, 30% were found to be below sector standards (23% of the 30% having zero FRC).Beneficiaries living in areas not yet covered by piped water networks continue to access water from tube wells fitted with hand pumps with quality surveillance showing that 90% of hand pumps had zero coliforms. Although between 45% and 77% of the population reported accessing the piped networks, 19% reported having used negative coping mechanisms for water needs, with fetching water from a further source sighted as the main issue. Hand pumps are still a major source of water for domestic use and bathing. Access to Sanitation:Latrine coverage at a camp level provides access at 1:20 in line with sphere standards, but block-level data shows that the current access ratio is 1:30 in the 04 blocks of ACF intervention, People are unable to facilitate the necessary minimum ratio due to the high population density with a camp/block wide estimated at 739 latrines and needs more around 1092 latrine cubicles. Current functionality has an average of 92% and is a continual process to keep it functional and also for the improvement of the facilities. Despite additional latrine installation during 2021, improvements in existing facilities with inclusion and privacy features and regular facility monitoring by the Area Focal Agencies and partners show number of latrines has stayed constant with 2020 figures, highlighting the continued lack of operational space for WASH infrastructure. Although the gap in bathing spaces has reduced, there has been a significant increase in private spaces, with 86% of households reporting private bathing space inside or attached to their shelter for but those bathing places are sometimes associated with laundry spaces (79%) and dishwashing places (77%), very often those spaces are unhygienic and poorly maintained or used as alternatives for urination and defecation, with 11% not connected to any form of drainage. Women and girls face issues of overcrowding and lack of privacy when using latrines and bathing spaces during the day, while at night they risk harassment and violence with 95% preferring private bathing spaces. In this response, some pilot programs have been instigated to install women friendly spaces and WASH blocks but are challenged by space and the lack of direct water supplies to each facility. A continual concern regarding sanitation is the quality of the fecal sludge treatment as facilities require upgrades, improvements or scale-up. Solid Waste produce daily in the targeted area, SMW remains one of the main challenges as only 51 percent of the refugee households are disposing of waste in the designated areas and 16% reported disposing of waste direct to open space. On the other hand, 74.5% of households are satisfied with the household level segregation and waste collection practices. ACF will continue the initiative to scale up this in the designed programme.Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement:Hygiene knowledge and access to soap remain high although behavior change remains an issue that requires further research to assess the impact of knowledge on hygiene habits. Access to MHM remains a barrier for many females through stigma, taboo and restrictive practices but also from a lack of MHM kit individuality due to common practices of one size fits all or variations of inter-sector kit and insufficient coordination. It also requires a wide scale of Hygienic Practice monitoring, staff capacity building, and meaningful community engagement."
