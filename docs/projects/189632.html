Project Summary

South Sudan’s health system relies heavily on international humanitarian assistance and is unable to cope and respond to humanitarian emergencies and provide basic essential life-saving health services. Limited health facility functionality and capacity, and poor coverage, have made it difficult to meet additional health needs posed by frequent conflicts. Women, children, the elderly and persons with disabilities are particularly vulnerable and most frequently face limited access to healthcare. Government contributions to health services was just 1.9 per cent of 2021 national budget allocations, with the addition of an underperforming economy also contributing to poor health outcomes.
Maternal mortality in the country is one of the highest in the world, with 789 per 100,000 live births139. Only 39 per cent women are availing recommended four Antenatal Care Services during pregnancy and institutionalized delivery as well as skilled birth attendance are low at 12.3 per cent and 14.7 per cent respectively. Access to BEmONC is very low and contributes to pregnancy related and new-born morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable populations. Teenage pregnancy is estimated at 30% among girls 15-19 years. Malaria is the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounting for 52.6 per cent of all morbidities and 58.2 per cent of all mortalities in 2021. While no new cholera outbreak has been declared since the end of 2017, the number of reported AWD cases is increasing and 20 counties are now classified as cholera hotspots with a high risk of new outbreaks. Further, Hepatitis E cases significantly rose to over 1,800 cases with 11 deaths and still remained above epidemic thresholds in 2021. At least 119,000 people were displaced both within and to neighboring counties. Western Equatoria is also the third highest recipient of returnees according to UNHCR, and most of these are settled in the main towns, namely Yambio, Ezo, Nzra and Mvolo towns. Mvolo has also been prone to border conflicts with Lakes State, and has seen multiple displacements due to communal violence, creating continue insecurity.
Project Objective: CMMB aims at addressing the immediate needs of essential life-saving health care services including maternal, child, adolescent health provision and treatment of the most common illness (Malaria, Diarrhea & Pneumonia) and others in Tambura and Ezo Counties, through static health facilities and outreach clinics linked to Boma health workers (BHW). In addition to maintaining the core pipeline for essential medicines and supplies to respond to emergency health needs, the project will also conduct disease outbreak surveillance for early intervention, and address nutrition needs through CMMB’s integrated Nutrition project.
The project target is 80,000 vulnerable IDP women, men, children, people with disabilities and the elderly in five counties of Western Equatoria State. The planned clinical services will be implemented through twenty three health facilities in five Counties (Yambio, Nzara, Tambura, Ezo and Mvolo), where CMMB currnetly has presence doing MNCH, Nutrition and GBV programming.
Catholic Medical Mission Board (CMMB) is a faith-based organization with a focus on medical interventions in vulnerable and hard to reach communities. CMMB has been operating in WES since 2009 and is well established in the State, working closely with the SMOH. CMMB’s signature program is Maternal and Child Health with services in four counties (Yambio, Nzara, Ezo and Mvolo) and 22 facilities. CMMB has acquired 3 new Ambulance to facilitate referrals. CMMB has been investing in health infrastructure in the state and has recently established 24 hours maternity units in Yambio PHCC (just completed), Nzara Hospital (now a referral unit) and Ezo County Hospital. CMMB has a well established medical donation program.

1 - Needs

During 2022, the health needs are likely to remain high due to disease outbreaks, low immunization coverage, Epidemic diseases, the most common illness (Malaria, Diarrhea & Pneumonia) might continue to rise which will continue to impact vulnerable people due to the lack of healthcare capacity and gaps in contributing sectors such as for adequate shelter, water, hygiene and sanitation. The porous border and limited capacity to curtail COVID-19 spread will increase the risk of transmission, exacerbated by the low vaccination coverage. The recurrent Ebola outbreak in neighboring countries continues to pose a significant public health risk in South Sudan. For children under the age of five years, and women who are pregnant or lactating, undernutrition driven by illness, poor hygiene, and unclean water, limits their ability to thrive. It is likely that the interconnected nature of drivers of people’s health outcomes will continue to drive humanitarian need, with increased levels of morbidity and mortality. The project will focus on the most health intervention gaps such as: low immunization coverage, low capacity of the health personnel, limited essential medication, weak maternal and child health and low awareness on how to prevent, control diseases and early treatment seeking at community level

2 - Project activities

Community health service provision linking with facility-based services through SMOH seconded staff and MOH initiatives such as Boma Health Initiatives. Provision of life-saving essential healthcare services. including maternal and child health services, management of non-communicable diseases (AWD and Malaria) and child immunizations through static health facilities and mobile healthcare services. Procurement and prepositioning of essential medical supplies. Training of health workers including Boma Health Workers. Provide technical and operational assistance to health facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and accountability to the affected population. Undertake Covid-19 prevention response, Covid-19 vaccination and related surveillance. Conduct disease outbreak surveillance for early intervention, working closely with WHO. Provide comprehensive management of rape (CMR) in health facilities and strengthen referral pathway. Establish health committees and conduct health and hygiene awareness among the IDP communities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CMMB will review complaints from the feedback mechanism on a regular basis, and use the information to inform activities and approaches, as well as strengthen protection of the most vulnerable in identified areas. The project will also use the feedback to inform protection mainstreaming approach, so as to reduce the possibility of PSEA or child abuse.basis to support real-time response to beneficiaries needs. 7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will form Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) committees who will be collecting complaints and providing feedback to the beneficiaries in the project sites. The project will establish a complaint desk center for reporting beneficiary concerns during the project implementation.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The AAP committees and project staff will raise awareness of the project beneficiaries on the danger of PSEA. Each project sites will have PSEA focal Person to report any suspected PSEA case in the project site.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) committees will Established and trained in their roles. All project feedback to the Community will be channeled through AAP committees. Key project Message can be communicated through Community meetings sessions.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

PSEA Focal Points will be identified and trained across project sites in the health centres . They will conduct awareness-raising sessions with women, girls, boys, and men of communities of concern ensuring that they are aware of their rights and entitlements through posters, videos, community meetings, focus group discussions and information pamphlets and complain and feedback box in the health centres. Assessment and mapping out potential risk areas for children and recommending protective actions to combat SEA as well as having appropriate feedback mechanisms are key. All such complaints should be immediately reported to the Country Director by beneﬁciaries/survivors/PSEA focal points. Community feedback mechanisms will be enhanced through volunteers, meetings with children and community, focus group discussions, displaying available complaint options/suggestion boxes placed in strategic locations at the health centres.
