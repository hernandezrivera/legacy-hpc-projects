Project Summary

Project goal: To increase access to child protection and interventions in South Kordofan.
Project objective: To protect children in war-affected areas from abuse, exploitation, neglect and receive response services when they experience harm.
Gap Analysis:
Child Protection (CP): Due to outbreak of war in 2011 in Kadugli and persistence escalating tribal conflicts had left huge gaps among children and Internal displace persons in South Kordofan as the situation for children continued to worsen. Approximately 69% of the affected populations in South Kordofan- are children, of which 51151 boys and girls facing severe protection risks since 2011 more children had been affected by psychosocial distress. The UASC children under 18 and IDPs community residing in South Kordofan need of protection services including family tracing and reunification, case management and PSS. According to South Kordofan Gender & Social Welfare (GSW) said children in the community are exposed to different forms of abuse such as neglect, exploitation, sexual and physical violence. Adolescents lack basic life skills that could have helped them to establish healthy relationships with adults and receive support when they experience harm. However, community lack capacity to protect children from abuse, exploitation, neglect, violence and respond to identified cases.
Profile of the Target Population: The population of Kadugli estimated to be 3,537,331 .The CP interventions will focus solely on the children and parents in the South Kordofan in 10 locaties such as Kadugli, Al Reif Ash Shargi, Dilling, Delami, Al-Quoz, Habila, Rashad, Abbassiya and Abujebaha, Talodi localities.
Project Description: The proposed project will address the critical child protection gaps in South Kordofan, focusing on four components of CP which include: comprehensive case management/Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR) services; Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS) and Safer Adolescents and Families in Emergencies (AFE); parenting skills intervention for biological, customary and foster parents; and strengthening community-based CP mechanisms. SUDO will provide case management services for 2000 vulnerable children and adolescents, including UASC, children at risk, and children who have experienced abuse, neglect or exploitation in the SK and IDPs community. SUDO will use the customized national interagency case management forms to manage individual cases, conduct child and family assessments, work with caregivers and children to establish care and safety goals, create plans to achieve those goals, provide direct and indirect services to meet needs identified in assessments, monitor progress toward achievement of those goals, and close cases when goals have been achieved. SUDO will utilize the updated interagency case management national standard operating procedures (SOPs) developed by UNICEF and the Local authorities to guide the case work, and ensure strong coordination and appropriate referral mechanisms. The SUDO Case Workers and government social workers will receive case management and Psychosocial First Aid (PFA) training based on training manual for Sudan.
On how to identify and register children with different child protection concerns from referral points including a) child protection helpdesks established at schools b) SHLS/SAFE, where children and adolescents will access structured and non-structured social and emotional learning PSS activities, c) Child Protection community-based structures, d) other humanitarian organizations, e) GSW, f) self-referral, g) case workers, conducting home visits on case and provide one-on-one feedback in order to improve their work with children and families. The main goal is to ensure the child’s needs assessment corresponds to the actions established in the case plan in all targeted localities; FTR services for UASC will focus on reunifying children based on best interest determination with the support of CP network.
