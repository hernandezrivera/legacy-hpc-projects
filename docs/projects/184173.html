Project Summary


In Somalia, nearly 3.5 million people are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of livelihood assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through the end of the year 2021, in the absence of humanitarian assistance (FSNAU/ FEWSNET, Sept. 2021). Furthermore, approximately 1.2 million children under the age of five are likely to be malnourished.
From October to December 2021, food insecurity is expected to further deteriorate among poor and displaced populations due to the impacts of anticipated, below-average 2021 Deyr (October-December) season rainfall, continued insecurity and other food security related risk factors, including rising food prices and declining availability of milk, and a likely reduction in agricultural employment opportunities. Without sustained humanitarian food assistance, 3.5 million people across Somalia are expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes between October and December 2021 and probably into the following year 2022. Out the 3.5 million people in need of urgent food assistance, 640,000 people face significant food consumption gaps and engaging in negative coping strategies to meet their food and non-food needs.
According to FSNAU (October – December 2021) projections, out of a total population of 464,487 in Sool, 119,340 will be in IPC 2, 81,690 in IPC 3 and 22,260 in IPC 4. In Sanaag, out of 362,723 people, 84,270 will be in IPC 2, 94,640 in IPC 3 and 22,570 in IPC 4. Candlelight assessments and consultations from other cluster partners further confirm the severity of food insecurity in the 2 proposed target districts of LaasCanod and Laasqoray. These require interventions aimed at reducing food consumption gaps, eradicating acute malnutrition, saving lives, and protecting and saving livelihoods.
Response
In respect of the above, the proposed project seeks to contribute to Food Security Cluster Objectives 1 and 2; which focus on improving immediate access to food and protecting livelihoods and related food sources respectively. The project will have positive impacts on food security, agricultural productivity, household incomes and resilience of smallholders particularly women in Sool and Sanag. The above will be achieved through 3 components highlighted hereafter.
Component 1: Cash transfers: Vulnerable families will receive and utilize unconditional and conditional cash transfers to improve their access to food and to stabilize their family’s food security. 200 households (HH) will receive UCTs, while 247 households will receive conditional cash.
Component 2: Support to small holder pastoral and agro pastoral households’: This will support the rehabilitation of communal infrastructure to ensure pastoral and agro pastoral households have access to water resources, pasture and other livelihood assets and earn income. Also, the project will support timely provision of seasonal agricultural inputs and trainings to help 60 farmers re-engage in their livelihoods and revitalize their value chains.
Component 3: Cross cutting Monitoring Evaluation Accountability and Learning (MEAL): This will support to track project progress against set indicators and ensure accountability to the beneficiaries.
Target communities and beneficiaries
The project targets vulnerable IDPs and host communities in Laasqoray district of Sanag and Laas Caanood district of Sool regions. Specifically, the project will directly target and support approximately 3,549 people (55% female and 45% male). The vulnerable households will be identified in consultation with the communities using a mutually agreed upon criteria. The categories will mainly include IDPs, women or child headed households, elderly (65 years above), PwDs, chronically sick and severely food insecure families among the host community. The project will address the needs of these vulnerable groups who are at high risk or even mostly affected.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The cash transfers empower people with choice to address their essential and individual needs in local markets. It’s also anticipated that if proper market monitoring is done, the cash transfers will have a multiplier effect on the local commodity and labor markets. Another reason for Candlelight to apply cash transfers are reduced facilitation costs, as mobile transfers do not require any transportation or insurance costs. Furthermore, vulnerable households benefiting from cash transfers are empowered to decide about their lives and make choices that improve their food security and wellbeing sustainably. This contributes to one of the key humanitarian principles; humanity. The use of cash as opposed to in-kind food support enhances dietary diversity for the benefiting households as they will be able to buy a variety of foods.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The proposed project considers market functionality. The cash values to be transferred on a monthly basis are provided by the Somalia Cash Working Group (CWG). On quarterly basis, the CWG analyses the minimum expenditure basket (MEB) as per region across Somalia. The MEB takes into consideration a minimum culturally adjusted mix of basic food items and non-food items (NFI) a Somali family requires per month and the prices of the different items are verified in the local markets. The latest (September 2021 by WFP) cash transfer values for food items for families are USD 60 in Sanag and USD 93 for Sool. At the time of project implementation, these will be revised to suit the prevailing rates. Additionally, on a continuous basis and using standardized tools, Candlelight MEAL team will monitor markets especially in the target regions to assess how well the market is functioning, track whether existing responses positively or negatively distort local markets, assess the continued appropriateness of the chosen cash delivery modality and also determine whether the value and level of assistance remain adequate. This information will be used to inform corrective actions regarding the intervention.
