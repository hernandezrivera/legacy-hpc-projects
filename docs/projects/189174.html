Project Summary

SSGID is requesting to be part of HRP to respond to the high nutrition needs of the most vulnerable population especially children <5 years and PLWs in hard to reach areas of Tonj East and Twic East counties.
SSGID will implement the proposed emergency response for twelve months through complementing the existing nutrition services in the static health facilities in Tonj East and by establishing 6 mobile OTP/TSFP outreach sites in hard-to-reach areas of Twic East County while coordinating with existing partners IRC Twic East to improve the nutrition response coverage though rapid response and referral mechanisms. Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) will be engaged to conduct routine active case finding, rapid assessments, and continue with surveillance of the nutrition situation to ensure that the targeted population has access to timely, appropriate and care on a needs basis. SSGID’s nutrition team will provide technical support to CNVs to conduct community mobilizations and sensitization, carryout mass screening by use of MUAC approach to ensure timely detection, identification and referral of Severe Acute malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute malnutrition (MAM) cases among children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs). Additionally SSGID will train 64 Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) on Community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN), provide training on GBV, PSEA and child protection for all nutrition staffs. SSGID will recruit and train 36 nutrition assistants to ensure provision of quality nutrition services are provided to all children 6-59 months and PLWs who are acutely malnourished. To improve the quality of services and ensure sustainability of the program, SSGID will provide refresher trainings for 64CNVs and 36 Nutrition assistants on CMAM and Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN). These will be recruited locally in those Payams where static and mobile sites have been established.
SSGID will establish a total of 6 OTP/TSFP sites in Twic East while continuing with the 10 OTP/TSFP sites in Tonj East respectively. This will be in response to low service coverage of nutrition services as a result of inaccessibility for people living in hard to reach areas of Twic East. Further, this will enhance focused rapid response mechanisms through provision of lifesaving nutritional services. SSGID’s mobile teams will provide gender-sensitive MIYCN counselling and nutrition education sessions for mothers and fathers, with key focus on breastfeeding; nutritious complementary foods for infants and young children; care and feeding of malnourished children; referral to health services for care and growth monitoring; and information on safe storage and treatment of water, proper hand washing, and sanitation.
SSGID will form 60 Mother Support Groups (MSG) for promotion of optimal MIYCN practices. SSGID’s nutrition program will focus on disseminating nutrition preventive messages to the targeted community in Tonj East, and Twic East. SSGID will print and distribute to all nutrition staffs guidelines, reference tables, and look up tables with relevant key nutrition messages and cut offs, while providing regular supportive supervision to the nutrition staffs to ensure that the nutrition services are provided in accordance with national and international standards. SSGID will rely on UNICEF, WFP and partners like IRC to provide nutrition supplies for treatment of SAM and MAM beneficiaries respectively, whilst ensuring proper coordination.
All children with MUAC <11.5 cm or WFH z-score <-3 and/or edema will be classified as having SAM. The identified SAM cases without medical complication will be admitted to our static and mobile OTPs and provided with appropriate therapeutic care as per the national CMAM guidelines

1 - Needs

South Sudanese women and children continue to suffer the greatest proportion of effects with high levels of acute food insecurity (SS IPC, September 2019). The IPC (2019) projections estimates 4.54 million people (39% of the population) likely to face crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food from September to December 2019 and a further likely deterioration from January to April 2020 affecting about 5.5 million people (47% of the total population) who are likely to face crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. The nutrition situation in South Sudan remains critical with overall GAM rate at 11.6% and SAM rate at 2.3% with contributing factors of poor harvest, dry spells and past conflict (FSNMS round 23 August, 2020). According to the IPC, September, 2020 report for Acute Malnutrition (IPC AMN), Tonj East County in Warrap state was classified as IPC AMN Phase 5 with the highest GAM rate of 32.1%. Further, the national stunting level indicate that 17.9% of children under age five are stunted, and 5.2% of children are severely stunted. In Tonj East, and Twic East, a GAM rate of 15.5% and SAM 1.3% (IPC, August 2018), 16.3% and SAM 3.0% (IRC, 2020) and 21.0% SAM 4.9% (ACF, June 2020). According to the (SS IPC, August 2020), the three counties are classified as IPC AMN phase 4, respectively. According to FSNMS round 23 (August, 2019) indicates a severe food and nutrition insecurity status among counties of Tonj East, and Twic East due to continuous poor harvests, long and heavy rain season that spoiled the crops. Findings of FSNMS report further indicated that children 6-59months and PLWs are further at recurring risks of acute malnutrition. The poor nutrition situation has been aggravated by poor infant and young child feeding. In partnership with WFP and UNICEF, SSGID is implementing nutrition interventions in Tonj East County within ten static OTP/TSFP sites. Therefore, SSGID is well vast with experience in implementation of nutrition services and has operational presence in all the 2 counties where it is proposing to implement this project. In Twic East, SSGID is proposing to establish 8 mobile OTP/TSFO sites in two counties to complement nutrition services offered by IRC in Twic East and ACF in Aweil east counties within static health facilities so as to reach the most vulnerable children in hard to reach locations and reducing vulnerability of women and girls to GBV and PSEA whenever they travel long distances to access nutrition and health services at the health facilities. Secondly the proposed interventions will complement UNICEF and WFP interventions to adequately provide support to the program in Tonj East County. SSGID will collaborate and coordinate with these partners who are operating in static health facilities. With this, SSGID is applying for nutrition fund in order to provide integrated nutrition activities, establish and start 6 outreach/mobile nutrition specific and sensitive interventions in Twic East County.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

SSGID interventions are right based and informed by need to ensure dignity, privacy and comfort of people in need when accessing humanitarian assistance. As such SSGID will embed accessible, effective, and safe feedback and complaint mechanism throughout the implementation process. During registration and selection of beneficiaries, SSGID will avail feedback and complaint desk in every beneficiaries’ selection and distribution sites. SSGID will also provide lockable suggestion boxes where complaints can be submitted.Desks and suggestion desks will be manned by a trained enumerator who will ensure all complaints received are submitted to SSGID protection and Safeguarding officer, who in turn will feed the complaints to a complaint and feedback file for redress. Also, SSGID will provide and display toll free phone numbers which beneficiaries can use to submit their complains and receive feedback. All received complaints must be addressed within two weeks with feedback provided in safe and protective way to ensure security and privacy of complainants.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The intervention will be implemented through the involvement of community members and other key stakeholders, to ensure synergy and coordination with other organizations working in the impact area. SSGID will ensure beneficiary participation at all stages of the project is adhered to and vulnerable groups participate in the implementation process. Before the start of the project, Focused Group Discussions (FGDs) will be conducted with the beneficiary groups at each project site. Needs assessments will jointly be reviewed with the key community representatives. A good working relationship with the community will be developed and SSGID will involve community leaders in deciding date and venue of activity review meetings. SSGID has an open-door policy where community members are welcomed to give feedback; and will create platform for community members to give their feed-backs anytime. A suitable information sharing environment will be created. Beneficiaries will be informed of the contact details/telephone numbers to call or focal persons to report complains to. SSGID will also work together with the GBV Sub-cluster to make sure there is adequate information sharing at cluster partner level. A real- time or post-emergency evaluation will be undertaken by SSGID to assess for impact, deliverables, appropriateness, timeliness and quality of this project component. Post-completion lessons learned meeting will be held to brainstorm lesions learned. Activity plans will be made together with the representatives of the line ministry in accordance to the submitted master plan. GBV Coordinator will take up the responsibilities for implementation and generate the weekly, monthly, quarterly and final reports.Final and narrative reports will be submitted to the donor (SSHF) according tothe reporting deadlines.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SSGID will deploy three gender protection officers who will be responsible for gender mainstreaming during implementation. The gender officers have received training on PSEA and are well versed with GBV sub-cluster standard operation procedures while dealing with GBV cases. The GBV officers will be responsible for conducting GBV awareness and communication during project activities. They will also conduct training to project implementation committee and locally hired enumerators on PSEA mitigation, prevention, and reporting. SSGID is a member of GBV referral and will be able to refer any cases of PSEA to relevant referral pathway in order for survivors to access services. All new SSGID staffs, locally hired enumerators and project implementation committee members will receive training on organisation’s PSEA policy. They will then be required to sign the policy prior to be included as part of implementation team. SSGID PSEA policy clearly stipulates that the organisation has zero tolerance to PSEA. The policy is clear on steps to be taken if any staff or individuals working for and with SSGID is found to have contravened the policy.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information on feedback and complaint mechanism will be provided during inception meetings with community leaders, beneficiary’s representatives, local authorities, and other relevant stakeholders. All trainees including PIC and local enumerators will also be provided with information on CFM. Further, posters bearing a toll-free number and banners will be displayed prominently at registration and selection of beneficiaries’ sites, distribution points, during meetings and training. Further, public announcement will be made by gender protection Officers and project staff on avenues beneficiaries and non- beneficiaries can use to submit complaints and receive feedback.
