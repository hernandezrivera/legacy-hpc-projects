Project Summary

For many years now, Sudan has been under a protracted crisis driven by conflict, natural disasters, poor governance and insecurity. As a result a considerable proportion of the population in Darfur, South Kordofan and Blue Nile were displaced. The total number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in Sudan is estimated at 4.8 million including 2.3 million IDPs in conflict affected areas . In addition to that, an estimated 795,353 (421,537F, 373,816M) South Sudanese refugees live in Sudan; 46,430 (24,608F, 21,822M) of them live in South Kordofan, 51,847 (27,479F, 24,368 M) in South Darfur and 169,043 (89593F, 79,450M) in White Nile states. In 2017, about 192,404 (101,974F, 90,430 M) South Sudanese refuges crossed the border to Sudan; 60,429 (32,027F, 28,402M), 33,581(17,798F, 15,783M) and 26,810 (14,209F, 12,601M) of them arrived in White Nile, South Darfur and South Kordofan states respectively .
With reports of an upsurge in armed conflict and emergency levels of food insecurity in areas of South Sudan near the Sudan border, the influx of refugees will likely increase in the coming months. An estimated 64% live in out-of-camp settings, either collectively or dispersed, and an estimated 65% of them are children. As humanitarian actors have concentrated their limited efforts to refugee camps, very little attention has been given to those refugees who live scattered amongst host communities, making use of their limited resources that have been stretched already even before the arrival of refugees. The increasing refugee influx coupled with the ongoing conflict in Sudan has created a significant pressure on the already stretched infrastructure and basic services in different affected regions such as Darfur, South and North Kordofan and Blue Nile states. South Sudanese refugees particularly live in dire situation. They suffer from malnutrition, lack access to clean water, and health services. According to WFP 2017 report 78% of South Sudanese refugees surveyed in Darfur are food insecure.
In two years, the project aims to strengthen community structures to improve basic services in WASH, Health and DRR thereby contributing towards improvement in resilience of the communities.

Needs

South Kordofan state currently hosts over 40,000 South Sudanese refugees, all of them living in out-of-camp settings in the remotest areas of the Eastern province of the state. In Abu Jubeiha locality, there are 10,878 (5765F, 5113 M) registered refugees in Sirajiya 5,124(2716F, 2408 M) in Gedied; 6,507(3449F, 3058 M) in Qurayd; and 2,415(1280F, 1135M) in Abu Jubeiha town. Water is very scarce in all localities, and their hygiene and sanitation needs are largely unattended. According to the Government authorities in Abu Jubeiha, the two areas of Gedeid and Qurayd are in urgent need of a water intervention. In El Radoum where about 16,000(8480 F, 7520M) South Sudanese refuges arrived in 2016 and 2017, NCA's most recent inter-sectorial assessment in August 2017, revealed that a further 95% of the water facilities in ElRadoum locality, in South Darfur, are not functioning. The refugee population rely on water being tracked daily by UNHCR for water consumption, which still is not enough and sustainable to cover the needs. A total of 33,000 South Sudanese refugees live in El Radoum locality 300 Km South of Nyala the capital city of South Darfur state. The influx of refugees increase pressure on existing basic service. There is only one health facility operated by the national social insurance that serves the whole population in Al-Radom locality including refugees. On average 250 patients are seen by one medical staff per day at the health facility way higher than the recommended 50 patients per medical staff per day. 1o staff work at the health facility including 1 medical doctor, 1 medical assistant 2 nurses 2 midwives and 1 lab technician. The health facility is run on support from UNHCR which covers the cost of consultation, laboratory diagnosis and medication for refugees. With support from the Norwegian Ministry of Foreign, NCA is in the process of setting up a primary health care unit (PHC) that serves South Sudanese refugees and neighboring host communities. Diarrhea, Malaria, Eye infection and skin diseases are the most common illnesses in the area. However, shortage of medicine is a major challenge particularly during the rainy season. South Sudanese refugees children lack access to nutrition service as outpatient therapeutic program (OTP) and supplementary feeding centers in El Radoum are not fully functional. Refugees in El Radoum depended on water trucked with support from UNHCR. Currently 9 water tanks with 1000 litter capacity are used for storage and distribution of water. The average consumption per day per person is less than 2 liters which is far below sphere standard. NCA again with support from Norway MFA is constructing two hand pumps and one mini-water yard that will serve about 3500 people. Water trucking is expensive, time consuming and unsustainable increase possibility of contamination. Inadequate access to water combined with lack of sanitation facilities and poor hygiene increase the risk of water borne disease.

Activities

South Darfur: El RadoumThis project aims to provide safe access to timely, equitable, and sufficient quantity of water of appropriate quality for drinking, cooking, personal and domestic hygiene to refugees in South Darfur located in El Radoum camp. The project will aim to contribute towards addressing the specific WASH needs of women, men and children as target groups. This will be achieved by doing an analysis of how each intervention will affect women, men and children differently. For example, in design and locating of water sources, more consideration will be given ensure safe and easy access to the water points by women and girls, as they are in most cases the ones tasked with fetching water. Such considerations will include: distance to water points, time for water distribution, waiting time at water points, and safety and protection of users. Latrines will be built near households to ensure safe access and privacy. The project will continue to strengthen community water management communities to manage the operation of water systems through trainings and provision of essential tools for maintenance of water facilities. Water supply: 16,000 people (8480F, 7520M) will be direct beneficiaries through the operation and maintenance of one hybrid solar systems and 2 hand pumps. Under the project, 2 boreholes will be drilled and equipped with hand pumps. In addition, two installation of hybrid solar system will be installed aiming to increase the water coverage from 2 Liters person per day (lppd) to 15 Liters per person per day .The project will continue daily operation and maintenance for the current system and hand pumps which include fuel, supply spare parts for the generators, engaging oil and fittings, aiming the increase the water coverage. The Non- functional boreholes (Hand pumps) that located in area adjacent to the camp will be rehabilitated. Moreover, Water quality monitoring including daily chlorination and bacteriological test will be conducted.
