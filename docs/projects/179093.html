Project Summary

The overall goal of the Project is to reduce the threat from explosive ordnance (EO) to IDPs, returnees, and host communities, enabling safe returns, increased access to villages, land, and infrastructure as well as safe livelihood and stabilization activities. Further, MAG aims to contribute to building a substantial national demining capacity as well as laying the foundations for future training facilities that can be utilized by local authorities and other HMA NGOs.
MAG’s strategy in NES aims at developing an integrated response focusing on Humanitarian Mine Action which has been designed to incorporate the necessary skills and technical capacity to address the contamination threat in North East Syria (NES). This approach is an essential pre requisite for early recovery, stabilization, and safely consolidating humanitarian efforts across NES. MAG will conduct non-technical and technical surveys as well as clearance of land contaminated with explosive ordnance in NES, and delivery of Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) messaging directly as well as by training Community Focal Points.

In addition, as existing national mine action capacity remains extremely limited, and the scale of the contamination is very extensive, training of new national clearance teams together with advanced training in management for existing national demining staff members will be a key component throughout the project. Furthermore, MAG will continue to work with national partner organisations, to enhance additional efforts of national capacity building through delivery of Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) and contamination mapping. The main capacity of the project will be focused and deployed across Hasakeh and Raqqa governorates, with additional resources deployed in Deir Ez Zor to support the overall protection and stabilisation response in NES.

4 - Needs

IDPs, community members and returnees have been placed at risk of death or injury from explosive ordnance (EO) in the form of improvised landmines, cluster munitions, booby traps, and other explosive remnants of war. 6.1 million (M) people remain internally displaced within Syria and 1.8 M population movements inside Syria were reported in 2019. These EO are particularly dangerous when transiting or returning to areas which have witnessed high levels of violence and conflict. The 2020 Multi Sector Need Assessment (MSNA) estimates that at least 1/3 communities in Syria are potentially impacted by EO contamination. Such findings correspond to MAG’s database which contains 285 Hazardous Areas (HAs) totaling over 13km² of contaminated land in the targeted governorates, of which 71 HAs were identified in just the first 3 months of 2021. Although MAG has already cleared over 6km² of land, large portions of the targeted areas require further survey and clearance. In 2020 alone (01 Jan–03 Nov) over 25,000 EO incidents were recorded, an average of 81 incidents per day, with the real number expected to be even higher. Further, 61% of key informants surveyed indicated that they know someone who has been injured or killed by EO. On average, 25 people are killed or injured each week by EO, with approximately 1 in 3 survivors suffering at least one limb amputation. Victim data collected by MAG shows that 80% of victims are male, 43% adults and 25% between the ages of 11-17. The MSNA also reports that over 85% of respondents have not attended an EORE session over the past three months. Further, UNMAS reported (2020) that between Nov '13 to Feb '20, 97% of the victims recorded reported not to have received EORE prior to the accident
