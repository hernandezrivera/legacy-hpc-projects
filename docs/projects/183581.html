Project Summary

MAP is planning to purchase a list of essential drugs and disposables to be delivered to the Central Drug Store and also to be prepositioned at MAP’s stores in order to ensure the access of trauma and NCDs patients to the treatment and medical care they need as and when needed. The lists will cover emergency and ICU items as well cardiac, oncology, and hemodialysis patients. This approach will ensure that all vulnerable people have access to essential health services without neglection or fear of diversion of funds. The project is aiming to help mitigate avoidable mortality and morbidity among the trauma and non-trauma patients. Moreover, the project will be contributing to the current emergency response projects that MAP is implementing in coordination with the health cluster and stakeholders and will be in line with the strategic objective 2 and strategic objective 3; and health cluster strategic objective #1 and #2.
MAP will make sure that all the items are provided to all patients attending MOH facilities without any discrimination. Also, MAP is going to ensure proper safeguarding and PSEA through all of its projects. More details in the following sections.

1 - Needs

For more than 14 years now, the health system in Gaza has been suffering from the direct effects of the blockade which has been described by the UN as an “illegal blockade”. The consequences of this have been felt heavily by the patients and year by year, the numbers of people in need are increasing. Specifically, this year with the aftermath of the May 11 days of assault on Gaza and the ongoing health crisis of COVID-19. As per the HNO for 2022, there are: • 104,000 acute emergency cases in need of immediate medical interventions• 7,560 NCD patients including hemodialysis patients• 9,600 cancer patients• 2,500 patients in need of cardiac servicesAll the above groups of beneficiaries have common needs and on the top of the list of needs is the provision of essential needed drugs, and disposables related to their treatment.To dig deeper into the analysis, in September 2021, according to the latest MoH’s CDS report, 39% out of the essential drugs list, were reported at zero-stock levels, and these include 48% shortages in immunity drugs that are useful for oncology patients, 41% shortages in hematology drugs, and 19% shortages in emergency and ICU drugs. Additionally, 21% out of the essential medical disposables were reported at zero-stock levels including: open heart and catheterization with 45%, hemodialysis disposables at 24%, and 7% shortages in emergency, surgery and ICU related disposables. In addition, a tiny group of children is in danger and are always at need of support and these are the 260 children of PKU in Gaza. According to MAP’s own expertise and based on the many assessment interviews we have held in 2021 with health officials and also with patients in various fields related to our interventions, lack of drugs and disposables are crucial factors in increasing the mortality, morbidity and the long term disability of patients in Gaza. The lack of such resources if key to the referral of patients outside of Gaza. This rhymes with the WHO study for top reasons of referral where the number one reason for referring patients outside of MoH facilities was lack of resources, specifically the drugs and disposables. To give a clear example, in 2021, MAP conducted an assessment of cardio-vascular services in Gaza and we found out that in average, 83% of the patients were referred from MoH facilities to other facilities inside of Gaza mainly due to the lack of the needed supplies to conduct the services inside MoH facilities. In addition, in 2021, MAP received an urgent request from MoH to support hemodialysis patients amid the COVID-19 pandemic with essential life saving disposables and this category of patients is always neglected on the account that funds get diverted easily for on-set disasters such as the emergency situation resulting from the recent assault on Gaza. This request was repeated at least two times more during 2021. The case for oncology patients in not different with them having the most expensive sets of drugs needed to save their lives. MAP did an assessment of breast cancer, and colorectal cancer services and we also found out that the lack of drugs for oncology patients was a major factor in causing mortalities. To sum up, MoH’s ICD have recently (8 October 2021) published that Gaza had a reduction of 53% in referrals, in 2020 compared to 2019, due to restrictions. However, the MoH went to elaborate further that lack of supplies coupled with less referrals was a factor in increasing deaths for cardiac and oncology patients (half of the patients die from a heart disease in Gaza, with a rate of 49% mortalities).Please note that in addition to the above, the MoH cannot have a strategic stock of prepositioned drugs, disposables and other medical supplies due to the chronic emergency situation and the aforementioned levels of shortages. The HNO of 2022 calls for prepositioning supplies outside of MoH facilities in order to be able to help the MoH during the golden hours of an emergency. This was evident during the 11 days of assault when MAP was able to release drugs and disposables that were prepositioned to the MoH and how critical that assistance was according to the MoH officials. This calls for INGOs and all relevant stakeholders in the health system to help in strengthening the capacity of the health system via increasing the preparedness to emergencies via the stockpiling of medical supplies. MAP has been one of the very few organizations in Gaza with prepositioned stock of drugs and disposables ready for release any minute now. These lists, like any other lists we procure for the MoH, are coordinated with the CDS, ICD and the health cluster to ensure that MAP is filling critical gaps in the health system.It should also be noted that MAP has a programme assistant now tasked with updating the Partner Reporting Activity Platform on the Health Cluster website and we will be able to update all the lists according to the actual procurement process to inform other partners of the ongoing activities and to avoid any duplications.Subsequently, MAP is seeking to procure more items of essential drugs and disposables to continue supporting MOH and to ensure the availability of drugs and medical supplies for non-trauma and trauma cases regardless of their gender or age. Striking a balance of supporting NCD patients and trauma ones is crucial at this stage not to further exacerbate the suffering of any patient.

2 - Activities

Please describe the activities that make up your project- To procure a list of essential drugs items used for NCD and trauma cases- To procure a list of disposables used for NCD and trauma cases- To procure and preposition drugs and disposables items in coordination with MOH to be released during crisis times. Please note that MAP has a very adaptive and flexible procurement system that is intended to facilitate work and speed up the delivery of these urgently needed items. We take into consideration, when awarding, the speedy delivery, quality of the items, brands suggested, and prices that are fluctuating day-by-day. We have an extensive experience in procuring emergency and non-emergency related items in a speedy fashion we are a trusted partner for MoH, the oPt HF and many other donors in relation to medical procurements.This project was coordinated with the health cluster, MoH, and WHO and there will be no duplication of efforts. We made sure to explain the categories targeted to the MoH, WHO and health cluster and all are in agreement that the interventions will be complementary.Gender: MAP has designed the project activities to meet the different needs of women, men, boys and girls. For example, there will be a component of the project focusing on children with PKU while at the same time needs for women and men in this project will be addressed via procuring medical supplies that are needed for both. Accountability:In terms of accountability to beneficiaries, please note that MAP has a dedicated team combined of in-country and over-seas staff dedicated to Beneficiary Feedback Mechanism and Accountability. We hold weekly meetings and discuss the BFM in all the projects implemented with community partners and the MoH. We have recently held meetings with MoH staff and agreed that it would be very important to have BFM tools in projects where we have direct interaction with beneficiaries. The tools that we agreed on are a mix of WhatsApp number, FGDs, handing mini leaflets with data about BFM and giving the beneficiaries a mini scoring tool to measure satisfaction of services provided. The tools were discussed and chosen carefully to make sure that we reach as many people as possible regardless of the literacy level and also to make sure that there are secure ways of reporting without the feel of being exposed. MAP has also a page on our website dedicated to feedback and complaints in both Arabic and English. The algorithm at the organisation has a triage system that makes sure that complaints are all handled in complete confidentiality but also ensuring that serious complaints would reach the highest levels of management in the organisation to examine and investigate.As for the MoH, as a partner and beneficiary, we make sure that they are involved with us in all aspects of the project from the selection of the lists to the technical committees and the examination of items once being received. We also make sure that we inform them of the donors (if any) and how to provide feedback to us or our donors at the start of each project.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Gender: MAP has designed the project activities to meet the different needs of women, men, boys and girls. For example, there will be a component of the project focusing on children with PKU while at the same time needs for women and men in this project will be addressed via procuring medical supplies that are needed for both.MAP applies strict rules to ensure that the items procured are what is needed by MoH. We provide testing as part of the contracts, and we reject any items that do not match the technical criteria ensuring that the medical items are of the most benefit to the beneficiaries.The provision of the items will make sure that availability (free donated items with no charge) and accessibility are guaranteed for the targeted population. In addition, we make sure that the quality of the items procured is top notch as we have many clauses in our contracts defending this right. As for acceptability, MAP always seeks feedback from the beneficiaries and the partner and will make sure that the intervention is tailored to the need and fills a critical gap. MAP will also make sure to coordinate the response with the MoH, health cluster, other stakeholders in order to avoid duplication of services which in a way is considered as a harm being brought on the health system in general and the beneficiaries in specific.Safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults. MAP has developed an organisational algorithm for responding to Safeguarding concerns and region-specific algorithms for the initial investigation of such concerns. These documents were developed in conjunction with safeguarding experts and are considered fit for purpose and consistent with current sector guidance and the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS). They contain clear lines of responsibility and reporting and, where appropriate, confirm the timelines to be followed when responding to safeguarding concerns. All these documents are reviewed at least every two years. An incident report template is used to report any suspected breaches in MAP’s Code of Conduct or Safeguarding policy to MAP’s Safeguarding focal point.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

MAP is neutral and does not lean toward any group over the other in the context we live in. MAP is impartial, and we provide aid to those who need it regardless of any other consideration such as gender, age, religion, etc. MAP is an independent organisation and is not affected by commercial powers. MAP is humanitarian and provides aid to those vulnerable and suffering all the time. We deliver directly to the CDS which in turn distributes to all MoH facilities in need ensuring access to all services by all vulnerable groups regardless of their age or gender. MAP monitors the delivery at the facility level to ensure that items are made available for the beneficiaries. In addition, the BFM in place will make sure that any breaches to the above principles are to be addressed immediately by the organisation in order to avoid any partial and un-equal access to the services.
