Project Summary

Following IOM’s manual of community-based mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) in emergencies and displacement, the Organization will target conflict-affected populations in the North-West, South-West as well as the Far-North regions of Cameroon to address their many related needs. IOM will first conduct an MHPSS assessment to identify the available, resources and key stakeholders, then implement community-based mental health and psychosocial support activities (art-based, sports and play, socio-cultural) in targeted communities, with inter-disciplinary teams, to address needs and strengthen community networks, as well as to aid victims of gender-based violence. Additional support will be given to strengthen local capacities to carry out community-based MHPSS activities ensuring sustainability for the future.
By incorporating a client-centred approach and delivering culturally sensitive services, which are also tailored to the age and gender of beneficiaries, IOM contributes to reducing psychosocial vulnerabilities and promoting community resilience and ownership. Through additional capacity building,, IOM also contributes to ensuring that key MHPSS providers including existing local government and civil society structures provide support to survivors of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and other types of victimization affecting the regions.
Continuing from its technical support to the government in the framework of the COVID-19 pandemic, IOM plans in 2021 to pursue using its specialized health capacity to respond to the COVID-19 pandemic at points of entry in Cameroon, as well as to strengthen public health preparedness in preparation for future health crises. This will be done building on existing strong partnerships with WHO and the Ministry of Health, continuing to provide operational and technical assistance through the strengthening of both technical and material capacities at priority PoEs in Cameroon, with the promotion of cross-border coordination and collaboration for standardised bilateral and regional public health frameworks, and the continued technical and coordination support for national pandemic emergency preparedness planning.
Continuing IOM’s co-lead role with the government for the Point of Entry working group for COVID-19 preparedness and response in Cameroon, IOM aims to expand its public health interventions at priority PoEs and PoIs along mobility corridors to curb the spread of the virus in Cameroon and to neighbouring countries, as well boost preparedness for future health crisis with a body of information and publications on mobility trends and practices.
In addition, a further capacity building campaign led by the Government of Cameroon with IOM and WHO in support will be conducted based on the new release of the outcoming SOPs, in preparation for the effective and streamlined screening of suspect COVID-19 cases as well as future health crises.
The strengthening of government capacities in both material and non-material ways will also be implemented at specific priority PoEs to effectively detect and respond to suspected COVID-19 cases, and to mitigate the risks of COVID-19 via large scale public communication, community engagement and community support including psychosocial support at key PoEs and PoIs.
Proposed activities in Cameroon will target a strategic selection of additional prioritized sights identified by further participatory mapping processes and previous mapping exercises and research. Priority sights for further public health interventions along mobility corridors and at specific PoEs will be selected based on a number of criteria, including mobility size, mobility type, proximity to large urban centres, proximity to large areas of disease infection, capacity gaps of the national, regional and local level, and any further selection criteria decided upon within the Cameroon Working Group on Points of Entry or in partnership with the Government of Cameroon, IOM and WHO.

1 - Besoins / Needs

MHPSSThe 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Cameroon views addressing the needs related to displacement as a priority alongside food security and health. The Lake Chad Basin Crisis, an insurgency by Non-State Armed Groups in areas surrounding Lake Chad, began in 2009 in Nigeria’s North East and spread to Cameroon’s Far North in 2014, leading to what is one of the most severe and complex humanitarian emergencies and displacing hundreds of thousands of individuals. Armed conflict in Cameroon causes a wide range of psychosocial problems such as the dislocation of the family structure and support system, the destruction of community structures, the loss of resources, the increase of all forms of violence and abuse, and the subsequent psychological disstress. There is a consensus among humanitarian agencies involved in health and protection clusters that mental health and psychological support is a high priority Humanitarian need among displaced populations . Not all sites have so far been reached with humanitarian assistance due to security issues and related access problems, in both North-West, South-West and Far-North regions of Cameroon, making the provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) so far limited.IOM Cameroon suggests the implementation of MHPSS actions with participatory approaches for affected populations by providing client-centered and community and culturally sensitive services contributing to reducing psychosocial vulnerabilities and promoting community resilience An initial needs assessment in the North-West, South West (NWSW) will be conducted to identify needs and coordinate future actions, followed by the capacity building of local actors in NWSW and Far-North, the building of community safe spaces and child friendly spaces, and the operationalization of mobile psychosocial teams. Expected results following this project are that displaced populations, returnees and host communities' in the North-West, South-West and Far-North regions benefit from improved mental health and psychosocial wellbeing. This will be in line with Cameroon Health cluster guidelines, with an integrated and holistic MHPSS response to the most common types of victimization, and prevention models and risk mitigation actions based on knowledge management and capacity building of actors.Pandemic Preparedness and COVID-19 ResponseThe Government of Cameroon is yet to officially reopen all land PoEs to full operationalisation that was present before the arrival of COVID-19. As the eventual reopening of PoEs in Cameroon is expected, partners are looking at ways to support the Government in this eventual reopening, to ensure that best practices are guaranteed for the limiting of the further spread of the pandemic. The planned approach will strengthen capacity to prevent, control and respond to COVID-19 and other public health hazards in Cameroon in line with Health cluster priorities in Cameroon. A focus will be kept on COVID-19, through enhanced public health measures at priority sights and capacity building on the new SOPs on border health protocols at Cameroon’s official points of entry. The project’s approach is anchored in IOM's Health, Border and Mobility Management (HBMM) Framework. The framework links an understanding of population mobility with disease surveillance and provides a platform to develop country-specific and multi-country interventions, emphasizing health system strengthening along mobility corridors.The proposed activities are to be implemented over a period of 12 months and will provide Cameroon border and local authorities with the tools, knowledge, and capacities to more appropriately manage incoming flows of migrants at high-flux borders and mobility corridors, in line with IHR and national COVID-19 guidelines and SOPs.
