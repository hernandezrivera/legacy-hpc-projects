Project Summary

The project targets 8,000 individuals in Pibor County (GPAA), with an integrated WASH/nutrition/GBV approach, using in-kind distribution modalities, hygiene promotion and construction/rehabilitation of water points and sanitation facilities, including a Cash-For-Work component (250 HHs). YEDA has static presence in Pibor, with a fully functional operational office and dedicated staff.
The project targets 8,000 individuals in Pibor County (GPAA), with an integrated WASH/nutrition/GBV approach, using in-kind distribution modalities, hygiene promotion and construction/rehabilitation of water points and sanitation facilities, including a Cash-For-Work component (110 HHs). YEDA has static presence in Pibor, with a fully functional operational office and dedicated staff.
YEDA will provide hygiene kits to 110 SAM child-caretaker units at nutrition facility/household level, sensitizing the caretakers about items’ use and disposal, including demonstration of the use of chemical products for the treatment of the water inside houses and clearly explain how/when to use the different items. This will be coupled with promotion of basic hygiene practices at household level, including hand-washing at critical times, hygiene and household water treatment, food preparation and safe storage, and household waste disposal. The content of a standard kit includes soap, sanitary pads, toothbrushes, buckets with lids, and feeding utensils for both the caregiver and child, water purification tablets, and mosquito nets to ensure cleanliness, infection control, and protection from waterborne diseases.
Concurrently, access to safe water will be improved in payams/communities with high GAM prevalence related to WASH, by rehabilitating 2 water points at nutrition/health facility level, facilitating nutrition programming and reducing the risk of Hospital Acquired Infections (HAIs) transmission. Other 3 water points and 3 latrine stances will be rehabilitated at community level, using a flood-resilient design. Additionally, safety audits will be conducted to grant improved access to water and/or sanitation for 248 women and girls.
Quality testing and treatment using Del Agua water kits will be conducted on regular basis. After the initial tests, bacteriological testing will be done on a monthly basis, which will determine the water treatment according to the results (e.g. chlorination where bacteriological contamination). The treated water sources will be monitored daily or on a need-basis, measuring that the Free Residual Chlorine (FRC) at point of use is within recommended standards (0.2–0.5 mg/l).
The project aims at reaching 1,954 people with increased access to safe water source i.e. handpummp, solarized water pump, water yard etc, and 2,480 people with access to sanitation facilities.
More so, 859 vulnerable people (143 HHs) will be t reached through distribution of WASH kit during emergency paired with hygiene promotion (community sessions) using the PHAST/CHAST approach to improve hygiene behaviours, to reduce diarrheal diseases and encourage effective management of WASH services. Additionally, 15 trained Community Hygiene Promoters will conduct house-to-house visits and hygiene awareness sessions on hand washing, MHM, infant and young child feeding (IYCF), water treatment, food storage and waste disposal, reaching 1,954 beneficiaries. Of those, 667 women and girls will be reached with distribution of Menstrual Health Management (MHM) kits.
Key messaging including HIV/AIDS, EVD and cholera prevention will be passed on during distribution and trainings, as an EPR mainstreaming. Additionally, GBV mainstreaming will be applied throughout the project, ensuring sensitization on prevention, activation of existing mechanisms of self-protection at community level, and referral pathways for safe and timely access to quality case management and psychosocial support.

1. Needs

According to the South Sudan projections for 2025, 6.5 million people will be in need of WASH assistance. (OCHA, Nov 2024). This is mainly caused by multiple shocks related to conflict, natural disaster (floods) and diseases, coupled with high influx of returnees from Sudan. As of October 2024, flooding has affected over 890,000 people across South Sudan, with about 226,000 displaced. Additionally, since the beginning of the conflict in Sudan in mid-April 2023, 56% of South Sudanese returnees received assistance to reach their final destinations, mostly in Upper Nile, Unity, Jonglei/Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) and Central Equatoria. At their final destinations, the conditions of the returnees remain precarious, while arrivals put pressure on already constrained resources. These are compounded by already existing vulnerabilities in host communities, induced by floods and intercommunal violence (OCHA, Humanitarian Update, Feb 2024).The WASH needs of people in Pibor remain acute, largely due to conflict, displacement, and environmental challenges. Pibor, located in the Jonglei State, is one of the most vulnerable areas in South Sudan, with significant humanitarian needs in terms of water, sanitation, and hygiene. Approximately 50% to 55% of the population in GPAA lack access to improved water sources (e.g., boreholes, protected wells, and hand pumps), based on the most recent assessments. Majority of the population relies on surface water or unprotected wells that are often contaminated. This contributes to high rates of waterborne diseases in the area. (according to the WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme, 2024).Poor access to WASH services and goods, combined with high levels of food insecurity, increases the likelihood of water-borne diseases, malnutrition and school dropout (HRP 2024). The most recent IPC analysis for the Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) in South Sudan places large portions of the population in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) and IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) (IPC Analysis report, Sept 2024). The prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in Pibor County, South Sudan, is reported to be above 15%, which is considered critical by international standards.Access to improved sanitation is estimated below 10% of the population, far below the minimum standards set by WHO and UNICEF. Recurrent flooding regularly damages the existing sanitation infrastructures and with frequent displacement, there is great hesitation by the communities to invest in, reconstruct or repair sanitation facilities (HRP, 2024). This increases the risk of cholera and other diarrheal or waterborne diseases.WASH-related GBV remains a reality for majority of the population, with 20% of both rural and urban households reporting that they felt unsafe while collecting water at the water points. Women and girls frequently face physical and sexual assault/harassment at water collection points, bathing areas and latrines, and 58.5% of households surveyed expressed sexual violence as the main security reason for movement restriction (2024 ISNA Household survey).Finally, hygiene practices, and especially hand-washing at critical moments are reportedly low across GPAA, due to high concentration of the population in specific areas, increasing risk of epidemic outbreaks. The combination of the critical WASH and nutrition situation will lead to high levels of morbidity and mortality if not faced rapidly. In fact, inadequate WASH services contribute not only to disease outbreak, but also to increased SAM cases; lack of improved sanitation and limited knowledge of hygiene practices contributes to poor feeding habits of children leading to malnutrition. Data from IOM, OCHA and WASH partners revealed that displaced persons in the targeted areas have little access to clean water or improved sanitation, as well as little opportunity to practice proper hygiene in what turns out to be flooded areas for most of the year.

2. Project activities

The project activities will include:(1) Assessment, Analysis and Planning: (a) identify needs and gaps, and market functionality; (b) and investigate community norms and practices related to WASH that may increase the risk of GBV; (c) assess the level of participation and leadership of women, adolescent girls and other at-risk groups in WASH, (d) analyze physical safety of and access to WASH facilities to identify associated risks of GBV, (e) assess awareness of WASH staff on basic issues related to GBV, (f) review existing/proposed community outreach material related to WASH to ensure it includes basic information about GBV risk reduction. (2) Resource mobilization: (a1) Mobilizing items from the WASH cluster core pipeline/field warehouses for in-kind response or (a2) make arrangements through MoUs with local suppliers to ensure availability of stocks and fixed prices for Cash Voucher Assistance; (b) training WASH staff and Project Management Committees/hand-pump mechanics on the safe design and construction of WASH facilities that mitigate GBV risks, (c) targeting women for job skills training on operation and maintenance of water supply and sanitation.(3) Programming: (a) activity planning involving women and other at-risk groups in the siting, design, construction and maintenance of WASH facilities and in hygiene promotion activities; (b)Training of hand-pump mechanics, Water Management Committees (WMCs-60% female) in water resource management/maintenance and HHWT use, and Community Hygiene Promoters (CHP); (c) Hygiene kits’ distribution at nutrition/health facility level and household level for units “caretaker/SAM child”; (d) Hygiene promotion campaigns (house-to-house, community sessions) and distribution of soap; (e) construction/rehabilitation of water points and sanitation facilities using a flood-resistant design, including a Cash-For-Work component; (f) water quality testing and treatment using Del Agua water kits on regular basis; (g) regular community consultations, improving their mechanisms of self-protection to increase the availability and accessibility of water for women, girls and other at-risk groups and strategies to maximizes the safety, privacy and dignity of WASH facilities; (4) Communication and Information Sharing: (a) Consultation with GBV specialists to identify safe, confidential and appropriate systems of care (i.e. referral pathways) for survivors, (b) GBV messages (including where to report risk and how to access care) incorporated into hygiene promotion and other WASH related community outreach activities using multiple formats to ensure accessibility.(5) M&E: (a) Monitoring of WASH facility construction/rehabilitation, and monitoring of hygiene promotion activities (house-to-house, community sessions); (b) M&E visits to the field by the WASH and M&E team, jointly with community leaders and partners for quality, measuring results against KAP baseline; (c) PDM 4-6 weeks after distribution; (d) end line evaluation with external consultant to measure impact of the project, documenting best practices while providing evidence-based recommendations for improvement; (e) documentation and reporting to the cluster and UN OCHA.NOTE: At all stages, the CHP, WMC members, volunteers and YEDA staff will be trained on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies and will sign a Code of Conduct. IEC materials in local languages will be used for sensitization on PSEA, WASH-related GBV and health risks in all areas. Gathering of people will be limited to the essential and any activity that involves that will be conducted following the WHO prevention guidelines for Covid-19, as well as hand-washing stations. Each activity will incorporate beneficiary sensitization on disaster risk reduction, PSEA and health risk preventions in collaboration with GBV/protection and health partners in the project locations.
