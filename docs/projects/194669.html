Project Summary

The project aims to address the following needs under sectoral Objective 2; Life Saving in disaster contexts through immediate restoration of access to basic WASH services. The targeted areas are prone to floods, water scarcity due to extended dryness, disease outbreak, and displacement of people due to violence. 3 of the targeted Counties (Pariang in Unity State, Bor South in Jonglei and Aweil East in Northern Bahr El Ghazal) fall under WASH Severity Classification 4 and Priority 3 in the WASH Cluster. While Ezo, Nzara and Yambio fall under priority 4 with 20% of the people in need of WASH services (HNO, 2022).
The intervention contributes to WASH Cluster sectoral objective 2.1 Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties which ensures beneficiaries have safe access to quality basic, gender responsive services including safe water and sanitation facilities. This will be achieved through household water treatment, provision of safe water for drinking, conducting hygiene promotion campaigns/distribution of soap and construction of sanitation facilities. The intervention contributes to WASH Cluster sectoral objective 2.2 looking at WASH specific activities for GBV (or mitigating WASH GBV). This fits into strategic objective 2 concerned about Vulnerable people who are exposed to fewer protection threats and incidents, and those who are exposed have access to safe, tailored, timely and dignified access to appropriate services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action. The intervention contributes to WASH Cluster sectoral objective 3, which reduces community members’ exposure to environmental hazards through the construction of disaster resistant structures and infrastructure. Under this, water points and latrine stances will be constructed with flood resistant designs, where the borehole platforms and latrine stances foundations will be elevated. This will build resilience and provide sustainable WASH services.
An estimated 6 million people’s living standards and their wellbeing will be impacted in 2022 as a result of prolonged conflict and limited/inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. Displacement, insecurity and the economic downturn have compounded the impact of historically low coverage of water infrastructure. The economic downturn has exacerbated the disruption of water services, limiting resources available for the maintenance of wells and boreholes. In areas hosting displaced people, above-capacity demand results in water points being unable to supply enough drinking water to the people, with the situation worsening when additional people are displaced due to flooding or insecurity. Recurring multi-faceted socio-economic impact on people, including severe economic contractions, soaring in prices of basic commodities, loss of livelihoods particularly in urban areas, increased protection risks, and disrupted access to basic services. These driving factors will impact 6 million people in 2022 (HNO, 2022).
The project targets the host communities, IDPs and Returnees. The total beneficiary figure is 104,316 people. The targeted recipients of this response are disaggregated as follows: 21,176 Women, 32,338 Girls, 20,550 Men and 30,252 Boys. The beneficiary figures are inclusive of the persons with disabilities in the communities.

1 - Needs

Only 40% of people in South Sudan have access to safe water (UNICEF WASH Briefing, July-Sept 2021). People’s access to safe and improved water and sanitation is low. In Yambio 21-40% HHs have access to Safe Water, Ezo has >20%, Bor South has 61-81%. Across South Sudan, 17% of people are unable to collect enough water for drinking (SSD_WASH_PIN_2021_Severity_booklet) and only 35% of households have access to an improved water source in less than 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns (FSNMS Round 26). Commonly reported issues in accessing WASH facilities include long lines and congestion at water collection points, leading to disputes and tensions. Severe flooding in 2021 resulted in the destruction and damage of water facilities, reduced access to sanitation and loss of hygiene items which resulted in the urgent need for water, sanitation and hygiene services. The impact of floods, linked to the effects of climate change, and high levels of food insecurity has increased water and sanitation needs (UNICEF, WASH Briefing Note, HUMANITARIAN NEEDS OVERVIEW 2022 120 April - June 2021, Available at https://www.unicef.org/southsudan/ media/8326/file/WASH%20briefing%20note%202021%20Q2.pdf). This intervention will use climate resilient methods like elevation of borehole platforms and Institutional latrine to avoid damage and inaccessibility from floods.Lack of latrines and/or overfull latrines lead to open defecation resulting in waterborne diseases. Approximately 10% of the population in South Sudan have access to improved sanitation while 61% practice open defecation (UNICEF, WASH Briefing note, October – December 2021). Northern Bahr el Ghazal had 9% HH with access to sanitation while Jonglei had 10%. Limited access to appropriate and dignified sanitation and hand washing facilities, in particular in urban and peri-urban settings, increases the risk of diarrheal or vector-borne diseases. 39% of people reported having insufficient access to water for hand washing Proportion of HHs without access to Hand washing and soap are as follows Bor South 21-30%, Aweil East 6-10%, Yambio 11-20%, and Ezo 11-20% (SSD_WASH_PIN_2021_Severity_booklet). Most communities lack hygiene and sanitation facilities like latrines, hand washing facilities, bathing shelters and rubbish pits. They lack knowledge on good practices of both household and personal hygiene practices. This project will promote hygiene and sanitation through Radio talkshows and CLTS Triggering. At national level, 18 % of households reported feeling unsafe while collecting water (FSNMS, 2021). Of those, 12% of women and girls reported avoiding water points due to GBV concerns (FSNMS, 2021). The findings from the 2021 FSNMS + analysis indicate that access to WASH facilities by women and girls, boys, persons with disabilities and elderly and services remains a challenge. Women and girls who are traditionally responsible for water collection face an increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water far from their homes. Ezo neighboring Tambura has very high concern for water related protection concern at 46.3% of HH that reported concerns while Bor South reported between 11-20% of HH having protection concerns (SSD_WASH_PIN_2021_Severity_booklet). This is an indicator that GBV and Protection concerns are present when accessing WASH facilities and services across all counties of South Sudan. 2 - Project activities

GENDER ANALYSIS IN THE 6 SELECTED COUNTIES OF PROJECT IMPLEMENTATION: This will help to analyze how WASH crisis affects beneficiaries differently (women, girls, boys and men) for appropriate action. This will help in identification of barriers to women participation in WASH and ways of closing the gap. WASH Cluster check list for AAP will be used for better understanding of and incorporation of needs from various beneficiary groups.SAFETY AUDITS: Analysis of physical safety of and access to each WASH facilities will be conducted to identify associated risks of GBV, analysis of community norms and practices related to WASH that may increase the risk of GBV, assess the level of participation and leadership of women, adolescent girls and other at-risk groups in WASH, assess awareness of WASH staff on basic issues related to GBV whether they are able to provide basic support/directions to GBV survivors, review existing/proposed community outreach material related to WASH to ensure it includes basic information about GBV risk reduction. This will identify gaps in service delivery, gaps in security, access, distance covered, privacy, lighting, segregation, help desk, special needs, and first aid services. The analysis checks for inclusion of Gender related issues in WASH Programming, throughout the Program Cycle (Abuse of power for sex, monitoring tools, reporting mechanisms, linking services to beneficiaries and how women are involved). Findings of GBV Analysis will help decision makers in risk mitigation/ averting risks related to GBV and promote gender equity..REHABILITATION OF BOREHOLES (180 BOREHOLES): This involves assessment of the broken down borehole to determine the spares required and local materials needed. Major repairs involve complete change of the borehole parts including construction of a new platform whereas minor repairs involve change of damaged parts. The platforms will be elevated to become climate/flood resilient.REHABILITATION OF MINI WATER YARDS (6 MINI WATER YARDS): This involves assessment and replacement of damaged parts such as solar panels, control boxes, submersible pumps, tower, cables, water tanks, water collection points, and fence and drainage channel.PROTECTION AND ELEVATION OF 12 SHALLOW WELLS INTO HANDPUMPS: Protective rings will be molded for each shallow well, and constructed to prevent filtration of surface contaminants into ground water and turning the well into a handpump. This reduces the risk of contamination and water borne diseases.CONSTRUCTION OF 6 NEW MINI WATER YARDS: This will be done in Institutions like Schools, Health centers, Nutrition Centers and densely populated areas like market centers.PROMOTION HOUSEHOLD WATER TREATMENT: Households will be trained in household water treatment methods and provided with reagents like Pur and Water Guard or HTH (Hypochlorite)/Chlorine to treat drinking water in cases of emergency due to flooding.DISTRIBUTION OF SOAP: Soap will be distributed in 30 villages targeting 2500 households for 3 months as period of vulnerability/immediately after shock. 400mgs/person/month, 400mgx6person in HHX3 months=7,200mg/hh =9bars/hh,=9barsx2500hhs=22,500 bars in 3 months= 900 cartons of soapWATER QUALITY ANALYSIS FOR 204: Water Quality Analysis is conducted after completion of the water points to pass the water fit for human consumption. CONSTRUCTION OF CLIMATE RESILIENT SEX DISAGGREGATED AND DISABILITY FRIENDLY VIP INSTITUTIONAL LATRINES: The latrines will be constructed to withstand floods by elevating the platforms above the flood level, and lining the latrine pits to avoid collapse.INSTALLATION OF HANDWASHING FACILITIES: These will be installed at the latrines and some at the market places to increase good hygiene practices of washing hands.COMMUNITY SANITATION AND HYGIENE PROMOTION: 36 Radiotalkshows will be conducted to promote hygiene and sanitation. Different hygiene topics will be discussed on 3 radio stations/month (1 for each State)

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

• IAS has got a Project Communication Method (PCM). The project will ensure broad and inclusive consultations with individuals living with disabilities, illiterate, women and girls.• The project will make consultations directly with the different groups of vulnerable members and their representatives (associations) where necessary. This will ensure feedback is captured from the most vulnerable that have been neglected.• The project aspects will be shared with all parties, so that they understand and give their personal views on how, where, what form they want the services.• Various communication channels depending on the vulnerability will be given to them as options, these will include door to door visits, committee trainings where these groups are represented in every committee, mobile phone communication, help desks and radios. These channels will ensure vulnerable members have easy options to provide feedback.• The project will identify and address environmental/institutional attitudes, barriers and enablers for people with disabilities to participate. This will enable free flow of feedback from the most vulnerable members.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

• Information about the project will be shared through different media which caters for both the educated and illiterate these include radio programs, text messages/phones, newspapers, leaflets/flyers, social media, notice boards, community workshops, meetings, project launch workshops depending on the appropriate method for specific groups. This will enable wide range of beneficiaries receive and understand the project. Then will be able to give their opinions, perspectives and ideas.• IAS has a Complaints and Feedback mechanism tool which captures different categories of complaints ranging from issues dealing with project design, transparency, accountability, M and E, Sexual exploitation, violation of human rights and early warning. These complaints are captured in data base and responses registered. The IAS designated team processes the complaints/ideas for address by the relevant committee on complaints.. • The project is designed to collect information from beneficiary communities through various media such as hotlines which are made available to them, text messages/phones, suggestion boxes, help desks and community meetings. • During the mid and end term project evaluations, data is shared with all the relevant stakeholders. This helps the beneficiaries to track progress of the project and eventually own the project. • IAS has got Project Communication Method document and Feedback/Response Mechanism Communication Cycle documents that guide our operations in community engagement.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

• International Aid Services adheres to the UN policy of zero tolerance to Sexual Exploitation and abuse. • IAS has a PSEA policy which can be found on the site, www.ias-intl.org or https://www.lakarmissionen.se • In community meetings, IAS provides communities with multiple ways of passing information depending on the privacy needed. These include help desks, complaints and feedback template to be dropped at the suggestion box, phone calls where contact information of the senior management members and GBV focal person are shared with all the beneficiaries. • International Aid Services also conducts training and awareness about Prevention of Sexual Exploitation such that all stakeholders are aware that IAS has zero tolerance for Sexual Exploitation and Abuse policy.• The project is designed to conduct awareness workshops on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. This topic will be included for committees such as Water Management Committees and village chiefs who are normally influential people to advocate against SEA.• The project will also use findings of Safety audits to address SEA, in participation with women and girls find mitigation measures to reduce risks to sexual abuse.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback will be processed by the Programme and Project Team to accomplish the following according to the need;• To change or re-design project, • Change implementation strategy, • Change of human resource, • Become inclusive, • Avoid conflicts; • Early warning from beneficiaries may help in re-scheduling of activities. • Ensure transparency and accountability to the beneficiaries about the project. • Removes aspects of the project that may violate human rights and may not be culturally acceptable amongst the beneficiary community. • Maximize profits and minimize losses• Promotes innovations, accountability and learning. The new information will help in adaptive management of the project, where responses will be incorporated due to new knowledge and practices.
