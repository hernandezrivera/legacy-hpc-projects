Project Summary

This project aims to continue the delivery of CMAM services for SAM and MAM cases within the existing health system in Khartoum (Umbada, and Jabal Awlia localities), South Kordofan (Abujubiha, Liri, Talodi and Rashad localities) /West Kordofan (Lagawa), East Darfur (Assalaya, B.Elarab, Eldeein and Abu-Karinka localities), through the provision of full package of treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition among (boys & girls) U5 and PLW. The project targets acutely malnourished children (SAM with or without complication and MAM) either through treatment or rehabilitating services. Improve the capacity of MOH staff to provide quality services and community volunteers ,CBOs to mitigate the risk towards food insecurity and reduction of malnutrition, the project enhancing the need analysis of the nutrition situation through conducting rapid nutrition surveys involving the target communities in identifying the needs , planning and in implementation as well as monitoring of project on regular basis. Moderate Acute malnutrition and the underlying causes of malnutrition will be managed through participatory nutrition education activities using the Nutrition Impact & Positive Practice approach (NIPP) through targeting predominantly mothers and fathers of moderately malnourished children. The NIPP circles emphasize behavior change, communication & counseling (for improved awareness), livelihood (for improved food/nutrition security) and cooking demonstration using locally available foods (for improved feeding and care practices). Community screening of cases will equally target boys and girls & depending on their nutritional status they will be referred to the OTP or NIPP circles based on the admission criteria.
Mother to mother groups where a group of mothers with malnourished children, grandparents group) to help empowering Mothers to solve issues related to the nutrition with focus on IYCF practices and knowledge and coordination with relevant sector (for multi-pectoral nutrition activities). Mainstreaming child protection through reflecting in the assessment, design, monitoring and evaluation of the project. Girls and boys of all ages and their caregivers, especially pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls, have access to awareness about women and child rights for the prevention of malnutrition Gender, COVID19, HIV/AIDS and the environment are mainstreamed throughout the project activities.
The ultimate goal of the project “save the lives” of acute malnourished children U5 in Refugees, IDP and host communities.
In addition the project is seeking to provide the medical treatment and referral for the vulnerable populations in the targeted states, through targeting the health system in the stated localities in coordination with the Health sector and Ministry of Health and other service providers to fill-in the existing gab through ensuring the implementation of SO1 (Increase equitable access to humanitarian lifesaving and life-sustaining basic health services for those residing in high risk areas and those most vulnerable and in need) & SO2 (Strengthen health sector capacity to prepare and deliver timely response to affected population by conflict, natural emergencies, and disease outbreaks). This will be achieved through construction or rehabilitation of existing health facilities, well trained staff, purchase of medical equipment and referral of cases to the health facilities in the areas, as well as taking necessity precautions health procedures to ensure safety of staff, volunteers and community from the most communicable diseases such as COVID19.

1 - Needs

Despite the democratic efforts made by the Transitional Government to advance Sudan’s economy, stability,and peace,humanitarian needs continue to grow.Some12.7millionpeople(28% of the population) including 7.4 million women and girls, will need humanitarian assistance in 2021. A decline in the economy is driving food insecurity, further disrupting health services, including the availability of medicines with only 15% of essential drugs available in the country.Additionally,the country continues to face the health and humanitarian consequencesof COVID-19, with over 18,254 people contracting the virus, including 1,265 deaths as of Dec 3rd 2020. Sudan remains prone to various hazards, including localized conflict, floods,and cyclical disease outbreaks,which drive humanitarian needs and affect thousands each year. In 2020,almost 900,000 people were affected by floods. More than 79,442 houses and 7,708 acre of agricultural land were destroyed in what was one of the worst flooding in the last 100 years.The continuing economic crisis, exacerbated by COVID-19, high inflation and erosion of many families’purchasing power has resulted in high levels of food insecurity and severely curtailed livelihoods.The gradual reduction of fuel and food subsidies planned by the Government is likely to fuel the inflation further and disproportionately affect vulnerable residents, IDPs and refugees, as well as costs of humanitarian assistance.While the economic situation does not stabilize the international support will be critical to secure humanitarian support to millions of people in dire need of assistance. Sudan’s humanitarian crisis impacts the already fragile health systems’ capacity to provide basic health services.The health system struggles to handle the multiple emergencies affecting the country. Health needs are exacerbated by the deteriorating economy,conflict,civil unrest,floods, and disease outbreaks.In September,WHO classified Sudan as grade 2 acute health emergency. Patients pay 70% of their medical costs and in 2020,these costs increased by 90%. At the same time, the availability of emergency medicines declined steadily. As of Sep 2020, only about half the medicines required were available.In addition, malnutrition in all its forms is a serious public health challenge in Sudan.It is associated with physical and mental well-being because when nutritional needs are not met it is impossible for an individual to achieve optimal physical and mental health.This creates limitations on educational and employment opportunities. The rate of malnutrition in Sudan has changed little in the last three decades, with an estimated 52 per cent of cases living in the nine non-conflict states.Children U5 and pregnant and lactating women are the main groups of concern as their nutritional needs are relatively high and deficiencies readily manifest when nutritional needs are not met.In 2020,15 out of the 18 states had high global acute malnutrition rates. Around 95 localities reported extremely high levels of malnutrition. Around three million U5 suffer from acute malnutrition out of which an estimated 570,000 have severe acute malnutrition. (Sudan HNO 2021). Reference to the S3MII2018 results the GAM rates for the targeted states in this project are as follows: KRT(Umbadda 5.06%, Jabel Awlia 6.44%), SK (Rashad 5.81%, Liri 7.75%, Talodi 8.48% &Abu-Jubiha 8.84%),WK(Lagawa) is 8.99%, and ED(Eddien 13.29%,Abu-Karinka 14.29%,Assalaya 26.98% & BahrElarab 14.92%) while the SAM rate is 1.66% in KRT,2.57% in SK, 2.36% in WK and3.80%inED.Additionally,the maternal nutrition services remains a concern with approximately 647,623 PLW in of need Moderate Acute Malnutrition management services to attain adequate diet.The GAM rate for the targeted localities are as follows:KRT(Umbadda 5.4%, Jabel Awlia 610.3%), SK (Rashad 20.8%, Liri 16.2%, Talodi 12.5% &Abu-Jubiha 28.8%), WK(Lagawa) is 16.4%, and ED(Eddien 21.7%,Abu-Karinka 9.2%,Assalaya 11.4% & BahrElarab 14.8%).

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated through the entire project management cycle, according to Almanar 2020 strategy. Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated in the activities to ensure that environmental harm is reduced or eliminated and environmental benefits are maximized. Key environmental concerns will be built into the project in order to reduce deforestation in Sudan. Almanar will attempt to switch away from fired bricks to alternative construction methods such as stabilized soil blocks (SSB) or concrete blocks (CSB) depending on the prerequisites of the region. Environmental mainstreaming will be included during monitoring and evaluation of the project outcomes of the response to ensure that the effects sought was indeed delivered, and to help identify possible negative environmental effects in time in order to adjust activities accordingly. It is a necessary part of ensuring that the principle of do no harm is fulfilled. Furthermore, to help identify best practices and lessons learnt that offer valuable insight for making improvements in future aid efforts. Almanar will work with local NGOs, CSOs and communities including working with the national and state governmental authorities, since Community consultation and participation is a key part of environmental management. Environment is taken into consideration as a specific activity to be implemented and across all health and nutrition activities, as environmental sanitation and hygiene cannot be separated from healthy practices at the household and community levels, and thus is often correlated with high rates of malnutrition. Throughout the project cycle gender will be considered to access to, and management of natural resources to receive consultation from both men and women. The combined information will give more accurate information on the total need for the community and ensure equally assessing.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Almanar through coordination with the local government authorities and humanitarian partners will incorporate protection principles with DO NO HARM and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity in the implementation of this project by avoiding causing harm preventing and minimizing as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the project activities which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psycho social risks, ensuring equality by arranging access to impartial assistance andservices without discrimination and paying special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services through the vulnerability assessment and identification vulnerability criteria. Through the CBPCs and consultation sessions Almanar will ensure accountability to the beneficiaries, where they can address their concerns and complaints. Through the awareness session and training workshops, networking and forums Almanar will support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights and empowerment.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

In the all stages of the project the communities are kept engaged and involved through community dialogue meetings, project dissemination. Accountability will be ensured across the activities in this project through establishment of complaints and response mechanisms at project level aimed at ensuring that the voices of the affected populations will be heard. The existing/ newly community committees and community leaders will be actively engaged at all levels of the project including management, monitoring and evaluation. Complaints handling mechanisms will be established to ensure that complaints are comprehensively analyzed and responded to with the beneficiaries getting feedback on a monthly basis. These committees are expected to be gender and ability sensitive with the inclusion of both males, females, elderly and people with disabilities to ensure that all voices, including those of the marginalized are heard, equality and equity are realized with regards to access to services that will be provided by the project. Project staff will ensure adequate consultation with the communities to ensure that existing complaints handling mechanisms are incorporated into the complaints and response mechanisms to be established by the project.
