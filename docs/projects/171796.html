Project Summary

The project goal is to provide community-based and protection-oriented assistance to internally displaced persons (IDPs) and conflict-affected population, including returnees and persons at risk of arbitrary displacement living in the areas along or in close proximity to Line of Contact (LoC) in the Government Controlled Areas (GCA) of Ukraine.
The project goal is to provide community-based and protection-oriented assistance to internally displaced persons (IDPs) and conflict-affected population, including returnees and persons at risk of arbitrary displacement living in the areas along or in close proximity to Line of Contact (LoC) in the Government Controlled Areas (GCA) of Ukraine.
The project will be implemented through seven offices at Svitlodarsk, Toretsk, Chasiv Yar, Avdiivka, Mariinka, Maiorsk and Volnovakha in Donetska oblast and three offices in Luhanska oblast established in Shchastia, Stanytsia Luhanska and Zolote. Interventions will include:
● advocacy for access to rights at regional level and to remove any discrimination;
● protection monitoring including individual case management, transportation support, protection counselling, information sharing, referrals, and assistance in identification for implementation of UNHCR cash programme;
● mobilization and empowerment of local communities through community support initiatives;
● promotion of peaceful coexistence by small-scale infrastructure support for communities with the procurement of certain services, materials, and other supplies;
● psychological support and counselling for residents of contact line settlements;
● support with permanent housing of particularly vulnerable PoCs;
● emergency response to the population(s) affected by the constant military activities along the contact line;
● activities in response to COVID-related situation along the contact line;
● mine victims assistance (rehabilitation, social and psychological support, assistive devices provision);
● restoration and/or rehabilitation of disrupted health services and infrastructure (due to the conflict and/or lack of maintenance);
● provision of health facilities with necessary medical equipment and supplies.

1 - Needs

On regional level, there are often discrepancies in implementation of regulations affecting IDPs in different regions. Capacity of regional and local administration is often limited and the level of services provided to communities living close to the contact line is not sufficient. In some of line of contact settlements Ukrainian government does not provide its services, so the need of social help is high.Forced to leave their homes or living along the line of contact, IDPs and conflict-affected people in Ukraine face protection risks. Main concerns are denial of registration, denial of payments, limited access to services, arbitrary deprivation of land, homes and other property, and direct exposure to the effects of armed conflict. Elderly persons are especially affected by those risks, and affected population in some 70% are women and nearly half of them are older women.The armed conflict has generated significant humanitarian needs among displaced and non-displaced conflict-affected communities. On many locations along the line of contact families are still suffering from regular shelling and are in need of urgent shelter assistance or relocation to safe areas. Localised flare-ups in the conflict, or other related emergencies, incidents damage housing and infrastructure cause large scale shelter and basic needs, especially for most vulnerable and persons with specific needs. As consequences of conflict and severe economic crises, many IDPs in Ukraine could not count on existing traditional support mechanisms such as extended families, friends, neighbours and other networks. This has added to other life struggles they have experienced and contributed to reducing their sense of control over the lives. Communities living close to the line of contact are in very specific situation, marked with security concerns, isolation, lack of the access to basic services, including health services, and under constant threat of displacement. As a demographic consequence of the conflict most of community members are women (some 70%), elderly (some 40%) and people with disability (some 10%). Their capacities to organize themselves and to use their own strengths are significantly weakened.Many of community infrastructures in the areas where Proliska is operating are in bad condition. Having 5 years of experience of health service access analysis, Proliska ascertains that health services and infrastructure are disrupted, there is no necessary medical equipment and supplies. There was no opportunity to the authorities to rebuild the infrastructure due to frequent conflict escalations. Those living in remote locations with imposed restriction of movement are not properly covered by the government provided services. Due to the protracted nature of the crisis, people’s ability to cope decreased drastically. The ability to access services deteriorated and livelihood opportunities become more limited, which most of all affects elderly people and single parenting women. Thus, humanitarian actors are still the main source of the protection and assistance provided to conflict-affected population residing in areas close to the contact line. Settlements in this zone are often composed of people of different background: while many are long-term residence, certain number of IDPs and returnees also live there. In some cases, host communities have raised concerns that IDP presence is making additional pressure on already limited community resources, services and infrastructure.As long as the problem of mine contamination is relevant, the civilian population will be exposed to systematic risks to life and health. The state lacks programmes to help civilians affected by ERW.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Advocating for the rights of PoCs, efforts will be made to influence regional and local administration, whose concrete actions in provisions of services are necessary for maintenance of a normal life of communities living close to the contact line and prevention of further displacements. Expected impact: Ukrainian policy, practice and law will reflect the current needs of IDPs and people at risk of displacement.Proliska's staff conducts individual case management for vulnerable men and women with protection risks and provides assistance for displaced and other conflict-affected people of concern with the emphasis on people with specific needs. Proliska will carry out the protection monitoring, including monitoring of access to health services, and will collect, verify, and analyse information obtained through various sources in order to identify human rights violations, protection risks of PoCs. Immediate response will include individual protection counselling, sharing of information, as well as referrals to other agencies. Expected impact: Proliska will identify and measure trends and changes in the protection situation over time, and will have the information necessary to inform specific protection interventions. IDP access to appropriate services will be increased. In the cases of emergency, Proliska will assist affected populations with the UNHCR provided non-food items (plastic bags, bed linen sets, blankets, thermo blankets, towels, kitchen sets, towels, jerry cans, backets) – in case of direct damages of houses caused by shelling, forced displacement, military or armed violence, fires and other disasters, people stranded at the EECPs, etc., and shelter materials for acute emergency repair (plastic sheets, tarpaulin, timber batten, nails, OSB, insulation foam) – in case of direct damages of houses caused by shelling, fires and other disasters, etc.. In addition, purchasing houses in safe locations will assist vulnerable persons to voluntarily relocate from isolated villages close to the contact line. Expected impact: The number of households that will be able to repair their dwellings to the adequate standards of living and to meet their basic needs will increase, the number of families who will get access to adequate housing and whose right to life and security be ensured will increase.Through implementation of peaceful co-existence projects in selected communities, where community support initiatives are already established, Proliska aims to improve community life. Expected Impact: The communities’ self-esteem, dignity, sense of responsibility and ownership will increase, community members will get new skills and expertise, and community systems will become more effective. The quality and availability of local social/ community infrastructure will be improved.Having 5 years of experience in restoring access to health services through social accompaniment, cash assistance, PPE for medical facilities purchase and provision, repair and equipment of FAPs in cooperation with local communities, authorities and international donors, accompanied by constant monitoring of access and satisfaction level, Proliska will support the health facilities and improve the access of PoCs to medical services through restoration/rehabilitation of disrupted health facilities and provision of them with necessary medical equipment and supplies. 14 facilities in Stanychno-Luhanskyi and Volnovaskyi raions will be restored and provided with equipment aimed to expand the range of health services that should be available in the state medical facilities but are not. Also, Proliska has MHPSS expertise (including PFA & mhGAP trainers/supervisors) and is ready to collaborate with organizations with health expertise to provide constant quality monitoring of the activities carried out. Promotion of access to medical, physical and psychological rehabilitation services will improve it for persons affected by mines and ERW hazards after trauma.
