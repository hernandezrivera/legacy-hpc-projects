Project Summary

The project aims to assist /6000/ of the affected people, including (1480: men, 1660: women, 1380: boys, and 1480: girls) in NW Syria as follows:
1. Provision /282960/ liters of heating fuel (diesel) for /786/ households (4000) individuals including 960men, 1080 women, 960 boys, and 1000 girls), where each household will get 360 liters of diesel during the winter season.
2. Rehabilitation /358/ shelters for /358/ households (2000 individuals including 520 men, 580 women, 420 boys, and 480 girls) depending on (HLP) and Shelter guidelines.
This project contributes to life-sustaining assistance to the most vulnerable groups in Northwest Syria; Aleppo governorate; sub-districts of Afrin, Sharan, and Mabatli, through the following two interventions designed based on the attached Need Assessment (Attachment.1):
Firstly, provision of house repair for /358/ of unfinished buildings for the IDPs communities. HLP due diligence will be included in implementation to achieve as much legal certainty about tenure as possible to ensure the security of tenure by verifying the ownership of the land or shelter and to prevent the exploitation and eviction of the IDPs. The contractor- modality will be adopted for preparing houses; this modality supports an accountable, timely, and efficient use of resources while protecting the rights of the beneficiaries.
HLP due diligence will be included in implementation to achieve as much legal certainty about tenure as possible to ensure the security of tenure by verifying the ownership of the land or shelter and to prevent the exploitation and eviction of the IDPs.
Secondly, provision of winterization assistance (diesel stoves with accessories, diesel fuel) for the most vulnerable people(4000) for the IDPs who live in the camps or (outside camps) in the makeshift shelter or damaged/unfinished buildings, and they do not receive winter assistance in the past. All the beneficiaries will be selected based on the vulnerability criteria so that blanket distributions will be avoided. The in-kind- modality will be adopted to distribute the fuel and stoves. Diesel fuel was chosen for heating because it is the most common and available helpful targeted areas; it is the most efficient and relatively safe, so vulnerable HHs can install the stoves and use them easily. Also, it will be useful in cooking food in addition to heating. SRD will purchase them from local markets for revitalization and will be responsible for quality control.
SRD will utilize a detailed Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability & Learning (MEAL) framework to carry out this project; MEAL Processes will include quantitative and qualitative data analysis. MEAL activities will involve interviews with beneficiaries to understand their views on SNFI's service quality. In addition, the M&E plan would develop outlined data collection methods (interviews, Focus Groups Discussion, observation, data sources, timeframe, responsibilities, and dissemination procedure).
During the life of the project, SRD outreach workers will ensure that all vulnerable groups are involved in project activities and will accompany the shelter teams during the door-to-door verification to detect vulnerability cases in the beneficiaries' houses and refer them to nearby SRD community centers to respond (Attachment.2). In addition, they will ensure that technical assessments conducted by shelter teams meet the demand of persons with disabilities and the elderly. Also, they will deliver awareness messages for beneficiaries who receive winterization assistance to protect from COVID-19 and ensure that the distribution process is in line with protection principles.

3 - Needs

According to HNAP-SHELTER SITUATION IN NORTH-WEST SYRIA,16% of shelters inhabited by HHS in NWS are damaged, and more than 1 in 4 households live in moderately or severely damaged shelters. 25% of HHs were unable to afford repairs to their shelters. More than 1 in 3 female-headed households in NWS cannot afford repairs to their shelter, compared to 1 in 4 male-headed households. 70% of HHs in the NWS report facing an issue in their current shelter. The first issue was the Cold and damp conditions, the second was the Lack of space at 27%, and the third was the Lack of heating at 26%. Based on HNO 2022, It is estimated that approximately 5.7 million people in Syria will require shelter support in 2023. In accordance with the MSNA 2022 findings, 34 percent of the overall population live in ‘sub-standard’ shelters. Lack of access to electricity/lighting, insulation from heat and cold, deterioration of shelter integrity, and lack of privacy are the top shelter adequacy issues identified. Based on HNO 2022, Improvements in NFI access were short-lived as nearly one million people were displaced in North-west Syria, accompanied by the impact of COVID-19 and the deteriorating economic conditions. Access to NFIs is seen as lifesaving, especially during winter when vulnerable families cannot access basic items to keep them warm, such as heating fuel, blankets, and winter clothes.

Impact: High Mitigating Measure: SRD ensure that their staff has read and signed their Code of Conduct. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All contractors and SRD staff will sign on the PSEA and Safe Guarding Code of Conduct with their contracts. The awareness session will be conducted by the outreach team to understand all issues related to protection from sexual exploitation and sexual abuse, GBV prevention, and mitigation guidelines, humanitarian principles, and they do not harm principle. All proposed activities will be delivered in adherence to the principles of humanitarian neutrality and ensure equal access to all beneficiaries in need without discrimination. Practical measures have been implemented, such as training staff on protection core principles and appointing focal points for at-risk women and children in addition to signing a code of conduct (CoC) and training on PSEA. SRD enhanced these procedures and created awareness training for all staff regarding CoCs, PSEA, and Child Protection. SRD improves overall PSEA & CS, ensures that all of HQs and field staff have fully understood PSEA and child guarding policy and signed Code of Conduct. SRD will continue to enhance PSEA reporting channels and respond to the complaints related to the SEA in a structured manner


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Provision heating fuel-diesel: Risks and Assumptions: 1.1 Diesel fuel available in local markets is of poor quality, and its combustion, when used in heating, causes the emission of toxic and harmful gases. Mitigating Measures: • Contracting with reputable, well-known suppliers in local markets. • Our field team will conduct periodic and surprise tests prior to purchase. 1.2 The indirect purchase of conflict-associated resources such as diesel fuel might contribute to conflict. Mitigating Measures: • Conducting (Anti-Terrorism) check for suppliers before contracting them. • Monitor the lines of supply of the fuel track the source down and ensure fuel is not procured from parties involved in the conflict SRD staff should understand the risks of COVID-19 introduction and propagation in the site, be trained and monitored on self-protection measures. Avoid direct physical contact and shaking hands with others. • SRD outreach team will deliver awareness messages about protection from COVID-19.
