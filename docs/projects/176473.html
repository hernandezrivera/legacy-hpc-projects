Project Summary

ACTED (REACH) proposes interventions under the Coordination and Common Services (CCS) cluster in-line with the 2020 HRP Sectoral Objective 1; “Strengthen joint needs analysis and strategic response planning for effective and well-coordinated humanitarian action”. The proposed intervention is multi-sector needs assessment (FSNMS+), to inform the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), building on the successes of the 2020 FSNMS+ collaboration, as well as a set of rapid needs assessments following the identification of areas of acute humanitarian need by using the MSNA, REACH Areas of Knowledge (AoK) data collection, and our Integrated Needs Tracking (INT) system and the Shocks Monitoring Index (SMI). Rapid Needs Assessments will be conducted in these areas, feeding into the wider response through the NAWG, ICCG and NGO forum, strengthening the relationship between reliable data and humanitarian intervention.

1 - Needs

Activity 1: Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) Humanitarian needs continued to increase in South Sudan in 2020, and the convergence of multiple shocks in already vulnerable areas have led to severe intersectoral outcomes. Shocks included COVID-19, flooding, desert locusts, continuation of armed conflict, displacement and mobility restrictions, increase in market prices, and disruption in aid delivery. Each of these shocks may not apply to all locations in the same way, which means that county-level monitoring and analysis is particularly important to better identify needs and hotspots. REACH, in partnership with OCHA, and in coordination with WFP, FAO, and UNICEF proposes to carry out a multi-sector needs assessment (FSNMS+), to inform the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). By providing a comprehensive, country-wide, and representative overview of humanitarian needs, FSNMS+ seeks to ensure that the humanitarian community is better able to utilise methodologically rigorous and reliable data for evidence-based prioritisation and decision-making. The multi-sector needs assessment will be fully aligned with the Humanitarian Program Cycle (HPC) and will contribute to the calculation of inter-sectoral severity scores and proportions of People in Need (PiN) for the 2022 HNO. By providing a common framework for joint analysis, it will provide reliable, quality data, which can be used to accurately calculate levels of need, and increase understanding of cross-sectoral vulnerability at the household level. Activity 2: Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA) REACH, an initiative of ACTED and UNOSAT, aims to improve the identification and prioritisation of areas of acute humanitarian need by using the MSNA, AoK, INT and the SMI to track areas of concern through time, with the objective of flagging up rapidly deteriorating locations for further investigation. Rapid Needs Assessments will be conducted in these areas, generating a response stream fed primarily by REACH data, and findings will be fed into the wider response through the NAWG, ICCG and NGO forum, strengthening the relationship between reliable data and humanitarian intervention. In the context of COVID-19, REACH has the capacity to engage with a widespread and established network of partners to monitor situational developments and provide technical support so that partners on the ground in priority locations can collect high quality, granular multi-sectoral data to inform the humanitarian response. The lifting of COVID-19 travel restrictions allows REACH to directly assess hard-to-reach areas of concern, which will be facilitated by the existing REACH Area of Knowledge (AoK) field structure (16 field bases across the country), and the expertise of REACH field based analysts. REACH will continue to work closely with a geographically diverse network of partners on the ground to collect data, using standardized tools, to improve information flows and strengthen the relationship between data and humanitarian intervention.

2 - Project activities

Activity 1: Multi-sector needs assessment (MSNA) • Input and review of survey questions, in partnership with relevant sectors;• Sampling, including proposing sampling options and carrying out the sample draw;• Tool testing and piloting;• Developing training guidelines for enumerators;• Training of enumerators in coordination with other data collection partners;• Developing and carrying out data cleaning and following up with both REACH and partner field teams;• Data collection in coordination with other data collection partners;• Production of anonymised cleaned, final dataset;• Initial sectoral and inter-sectoral descriptive analysis;• Joint analysis workshop with all key stakeholders; • Provision of support to sectors in the calculation of their severity scores and PiN;• Provision of support in the calculation of the inter-sectoral severity score and PiN;• Production of sector factsheets based on the findings;• Production of inter-sectoral MSNA report based on the findings. Activity 2: Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA) • Creation of a multi-sectoral quantitative toolkit for rapid assessments;• Consolidation of qualitative FGD tools designed specifically for use in acutely food insecure contexts; • Training of ERRM partners in rapid assessment research design;• Working with ERRM partners to redesign Alerts and assessment Triggers, to prioritise assessments according to clearly defined thresholds of need;• Training of enumerators in coordination with other data collection partners;• Training of translators in coordination with other data collection partners;• Developing and carrying out data cleaning and following up with both REACH and partner field teams;• Data collection in coordination with other data collection partners;• Production of anonymised cleaned, final dataset when quantitative data is collected;• Production of data saturation grid and published FGD transcripts;• Initial sectoral and inter-sectoral descriptive analysis based on context and shock(s) experienced;• Production of external reports based on assessment findings, where appropriate;• Production of internal briefs for advocacy, where appropriate.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The protection cluster will be consulted to support with training of enumerators to ensure that the core protection principles are followed during data collection to prioritize the safety and dignity of all community members who are engaging with FSNMS+ and the Rapid Needs Assessments. PSEA dedicated questions will be included in the questionnaire in order to support the National PSEA Task Force. The data will allow the task force to have for the first time at South Sudan level, a comprehensive understanding of the perception of beneficiaries on the reporting mechanism and its functioning and to take further actions to adjust the response to meet beneficiaries needs and preferences. The questionnaires will be developed to ensure safety and security of beneficiaries and enumerators.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Given that this will be a randomly sampled assessment, all population groups, including vulnerable groups, such as persons with disabilities, have an equal chance of being selected for participation in the assessment, so enumerators will also be trained on how to include and conduct a survey with potentially vulnerable groups. Data collection teams will also be trained on PSEA as well as given guidance by the protection cluster on appropriate processes for providing protection referrals.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Accountability to Affected Populations will be a core component of the FSNMS+, including incorporation of AAP focused questions within the survey tool with the intention of collecting the first ever representative country-wide AAP data across all of South Sudan. Additionally, AAP feedback loops with communities will be designed and incorporated to ensure that REACH promotes “closing the feedback loop” throughout and following the assessment.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

REACH will include questions for additional comments and feedback from key informants at the end of surveys/FGDs, in the FSNMS+ as well as the rapid needs assessments, and will be informing communities of findings wherever possible, through other work streams (AoK assessments conducted in 16 bases). Similarly, Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) will be a core component of the FSNMS+, including incorporation of AAP focused questions within the survey tool with the intention of collecting the first ever representative country-wide AAP data across all of South Sudan.
