Project Summary

The project is aimed at providing food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations in IPC 5, 4 and some in 3 target Counties.
The purpose of the project is to save lives through food assistance (in-kind and cash-based transfers) in the locations that have households (HHs) categorized in IPC 4 and IPC 5. We will use IRRM (WFP) for crisis event response.
The project targets vulnerable households and populations including women, girls, men and boys, the elderly, people with special needs, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), children under five years, people living with HIV/AIDS and chronically sick individuals in the community. We will focus on famine prevention and response through multi-sector integrated actions (e.g. project integration with the prevention of Gender-Based Violence (GBV), Nutrition and Health programming.
We will ensure Accountability to the beneficiaries with the complaint and feedback mechanisms in place to ensure the inclusion of the vulnerable target groups, and that the beneficiary feedbacks are taken into consideration before, after and during the project implementation.
The project focuses on seasonal food assistance with other multi-sector integrated actions (e.g. with Protection, Nutrition, WASH and Health actors)
The needs, voice, and rights of women will be put at the center stage of project implementation. The assistance will be informed through robust assessment and post-distribution monitoring that will ensure targeting the severely food insecure households. We will also incorporate flexible contingency plans to adapt to the changing context and responsive to priorities.
We plan to achieve gender mainstreaming in the project through integrating Prevention of Sexual Exploitation (PSEA) and Child Safeguarding, Gender-Based Violence (GBV), linking People Living with HIV/AIDS to the service providers, focusing on the People with Special Needs and the elderly in the communities.

1 - Needs

According to the August 2019 IPC Analysis, an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). Compared to the same period in 2018, there is a slight reduction in the proportion of people facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity by an estimated 5%. However, high levels of acute food insecurity persist in the country. In both periods, these estimates are in the presence of humanitarian food assistance. In the projection period of September to December 2019, the food security situation is expected to improve as seasonal harvests become available. During this period, an estimated 4.54 million people (39% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food. In the post-harvest period of January to April 2020, the food security situation will deteriorate as household food stocks start depleting and an estimated 5.5 million people (47% of the total population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. The projection analyses have both factored in the presence of likely humanitarian food assistance. In Eastern Equatoria State, about 52.6% of the population, mainly from the counties of Torit, Kapoeta South, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta East, Magwi, Budi, Lafon, and Ikotos are expected to be in crisis phase (moderately and severely food insecure) in the first quarter of 2020 (IPC, August 2019). Insecurity still contributes to the underlying causes of low agricultural production in the state. The drought in the lean season from September 2019 to January 2020 will lead to low livestock production. There have also been some cases of intercommunity and clan-based conflict due to cattle rustling. There is also an influx of returnees from Kenya leading to more stress on the limited resources in the State. The insecurity incidents cause displacements, destructions to properties, deaths, and disruption of livelihood activities. These continue to hinder efforts to early recovery process and resilience building among communities. In most of the areas, communities are also faced with crop failures due to limited and erratic rainfalls, fall armyworm infestation, limited access to food and basic services with escalating (more than tripled) food and fuel prices. Consequently, there is increased inaccessibility to some areas and farming lands/gardens, poor or low food production, limited health services, poor water sources, and dysfunctional markets. The combination of the above factors resulted in high percentages of food insecure populations that critically require both food assistance and early recovery form of food assistance to not only enhance their recovery from the stresses and future shocks but also build their resilience. About 150,000 of the people in Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South, Budi, and Kapoeta East are in IPC 4 and 5. They become the main target of the project. Food utilization is a significant problem in the four targeted counties because of the chronic nature of waterborne diseases, low use of latrines, poor personal hygiene, and living environments, and limited access to soap for handwashing. Access to health services is also poor and this leads to high incidences of diseases that not only affect the health of the population but also negatively affect the availability of labor and lead to reduced income at the household level. WASH needs for the country are high and require significant investment to address them. The dry season associated with the harvest and post-harvest periods records a reduction in the incidences of water-borne diseases, but the risk still exists.

2 - Activities

The project will have various activities including:1.Community-Based Participation and Planning (CBPP). Consultation with the community through community leaders (local authorities) will be conducted as the first step meant to create ownership and ensure the sustainability of implemented measures. We will assign food monitors in each of the distribution sites to facilitate the active involvement of the community members to select planning teams. The CBPP exercise will focus on the full participation of selected community planning teams, who, in participatory ways, will identify community needs, suggest some recommendations and draw the final community’s plan. A range of participatory methods will be used to guide the planning process. This exercise will be conducted at the Boma level. Each of the Bomas will have a CBPP report after the planning exercise. The communities will also use this planning forum to select project management committees at the Boma level. 2. Community Mobilization and Sensitization.This will involve organizing community meetings to inform them of the planned project activities and their roles in the project. 3. Identification and Registration of the Beneficiaries.Households with extreme and severe food-insecure individuals will be targeted including women, girls, men, boys, the elderly, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), people living HIV/AIDS, people with Special Needs and Children under five years. 4. In-kind food assistance: General Food Distribution (GFD): Both Blanket and Seasonal targeted food distribution.This modality will involve the distribution of cereals, pulses, oil, and salt per individual in severe food-insecure households. Each beneficiary will get 385g of the assorted food per day. The indicator is the number of beneficiaries receiving food and the quantity of food assistance distributed(tonnage). 5. Cash and voucher-based transfers to save lives. Each beneficiary will be 0.450USD per day. The indicator for this modality will be the number of cash or voucher received by beneficiaries. 6. Post-Distribution Monitoring.This will be done after every distribution to cross-check whether the targeted beneficiaries have been reached and monitor the progress of the other project activities. 7. Project EvaluationWe plan to do a Mid-term evaluation to check the outputs and another end-term evaluation to confirm the impact of the project activities. 8. ReportingThere will be a series of reports to be submitted including bi-weekly, monthly distribution reports, final narrative and financial report to be submitted to UN OCHA.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The project is likely to have the above-selected risks and we plan to mitigate them in the following ways:1. Women and girls will be consulted and involved right from Community-Based Participatory Planning, Identification and Registration of beneficiaries, food distribution, project monitoring, and evaluation. For the risk of community members moving from their habitual places of residence for the purpose of humanitarian assistance, our field officers will ensure that we set the distribution sites in locations convenient to them. The pastoralists move with their livestock in search of water and pasture, therefore we will ensure that we follow them to their locations of places close to their temporary residents for cash and food distribution. The risk of women not being involved in cash-based transfer activities will be mitigated by targeting at least 50% of the women-led households with cash-based transfer activities. This will help to increase the participation of women in cash-based transfers. Women and girls will be targeted for capacity building trainings on GBV risk reduction and community M & E. Men and boys will be engaged in collective action to change negative gender norms and to mitigate and prevent gender based violence at home and at the community level. Persons living with disability, caregivers of unaccompanied and separated children, persons living with HIV and AIDS as well as the elderly will equally participate in project activities from inception to project closure. Also, individuals within this category who will apply for jobs through the project will be given priority as long as they will exhibit ability and the skills to productively participate in direct implementation of the project activities. Finally, all project activities will be conducted near settlement areas to ensure ease of access to training and workshop venues by those living with disability, the elderly and lactating mothers.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will ensure beneficiary participation at all stages. A needs assessment has been done jointly with local authorities representing beneficiary members whereby gaps have been identified jointly. Community members are aware of needs INTENT intends to address, however, there will be joint needs review during the implementation stage with different beneficiary groups to ensure services provided by the project are efficient to different beneficiary groups. Before the start of the project, Focused Group Discussions (FGDs) will be conducted to identify the specific needs of women, girls, and special groups at each project site. INTENT will maintain a good working relationship with the community leaders and the Department of Health of the targeted Payams, who will always be notified to involve community members at all stages of project; and also to participate in joint monitoring of activities so as to give feedback on whether project activities meet previously discussed expectations/needs identified.
