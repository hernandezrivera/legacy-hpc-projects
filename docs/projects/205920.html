Project Summary

The Rescue Initiative-South Sudan (TRI-SS) seeks to fortify the health system, prevent and address health concerns, and offer crisis-affected populations high-quality medical care. The aim is to achieve the Humanitarian Response Plan's (HRP) 2024 goals of improving coping and recovery, lowering health risks, and saving lives. The plan's main objective is to provide kits and life-saving medical care in accordance with humanitarian standards. Demands related to trauma, seasonality, and disease epidemiology determine the response. TRI-SS will collaborate with various partners in an effort to deliver better coordinated and effective emergency health solutions to host communities, internally displaced persons and returnees in five counties of Juba, Kajo Keji, Morobo, Pibor and Tambura.
This project will benefit 28,490 host communities, internally displaced persons, and returnees in Juba County, 5,020 in Kajo Keji County, 5,500 in Morobo County, 9,896 in Pibor County (Boma) and 5,649 in Tambura County. The beneficiaries will included children under five years of age, children aged 5 -17 years, adults aged 18-59 years and the elderly people 60 years above. People impacted by health events will receive high-quality healthcare services as part of the response, including those related to GBV (gender-based violence), common and chronic diseases, neglected tropical diseases, maternal, child, and adolescent health, and sexual and reproductive health. The response will also enhance family planning, PMTCT (prevention of mother-to-child transmission), clinical management of rape, and supplies for vulnerable women and girls, as well as sexual and reproductive health services provided by EmONC (emergency newborn and obstetric care).
To increase population coverage, a combination of mobile and static teams will be used in the proposed implementation methodology. These will consist of six mobile teams, five PHCUs, and two PHCCs. The method for referral will be used in the response to improve care quality, efficiency, and integration. The approach will prioritize prevention, readiness, response, and mitigation for vulnerable populations while adhering to the principles of universal health coverage. As South Sudan faces the threat of EVD and other viral hemorrhagic fevers, the response will also continue efforts to avoid, prepare for, and be ready for EVD.

1. Needs

Juba, Kajo Keji, and Morobo counties are located in Central Equatoria State. In the year 2022, the population projections by UNOCHA showed that Juba County has a total population of 528,872; Kajo Keji; 232,657, Morobo; 116,016, Tambura; 119,107, and Pibor; 227, 855 people. The 2013 and 2016 civil unrest devastated the populations of these five counties and severely affected the functioning of the health system. The COVID-19 pandemic, ongoing insecurity, cyclical inter-communal violence, droughts, flooding and the high inflation rate have aggravated the situation in counties where over 78% of the population lives below $ 1.90 (World Bank 2020). For example in Pibor County, nomads migration patterns have altered, and entire households have moved to ensure access to food products from cattle, such as milk. The inter-ethnic conflict in Tambura County has displaced around 80,000 people, with 45,000 in Ezo and 8,500 in Yambio counties. Many have moved to Nagero, Mutina, and Nzara counties, with others at Hayat construction camp, UNIMISS, Saint Mary Catholic church compound, Ministerial Complex, and sheltering at Nbanga.Mortality rates remain unacceptable in these counties. In 2022, the maternal mortality ratio for Juba County was reported to be 137 deaths per 100,000 live births; neonatal mortality, 1.4 deaths per 1000 live births and under-five mortality rate, 2 deaths per 1000 live births. For Kajo Keji County maternal mortality ratio was zero deaths per 100,000 live births; the neonatal mortality rate was zero deaths per 1000 live births; and the under-five mortality rate was 18 deaths per 1000 live births (MOH 2022). For Morobo County, the maternal mortality ratio was 230 deaths per 100,000 live births; the neonatal mortality rate was six deaths per 1000 live births and the under-five mortality rate was 14 deaths per 1000 live births. For Tambura County, the maternal mortality ratio was 51 deaths per 100,000 live births; the neonatal mortality rate was 0.2 deaths per 1000 live births; the under-five mortality rate was two deaths per 1000 live births. For Pibor County, the maternal mortality ratio was the highest i.e. 1525 per 100,000 live births; the neonatal mortality rate was three deaths per 1000 live births and the under-five mortality rate was 71 deaths per 1000 live births (MOH 2022). In all these counties, the leading causes of maternal deaths were postpartum haemorrhage, antepartum haemorrhage, puerperal sepsis, and obstructed labour (MOH 2022). On the other hand, the leading causes of morbidity and mortality among children under five years of age were malaria, acute respiratory tract infections, diarrheal diseases, birth asphyxia, severe acute malnutrition, congenital disorders, neonatal tetanus, and newborn sepsis. Disease outbreaks such as cholera, measles, and hepatitis C are common. The coverage and utilization of maternal, newborn and child health services remain poor. In 2022, the proportion of women who attended antenatal care at least four times was 38% for Juba County, 214% for Kajo Keji, 113% for Morobo, 8.9% for Pibor and 16.2% for Tambura. The proportion of women whose deliveries were attended by skilled birth attendants was 102.6% for Juba, 95.5% for Kajo Keji, 98% for Morobo, 8.9% for Pibor, and 51.5% for Tambura County. For children under five years of age, BCG coverages were; 146%, 12%, 27.5%, 49.6%, and 73.5% for Juba, Kajo Keji, Morobo, Pibor and Tambura respectively. Penta 3 coverage was;125%, 20.8%,19.1%, 38.7%, and 69.4% for Juba, Kajo Keji, Morobo, Pibor and Tambura respectively and Measles coverage; 109%, 1.8%, 12.9%, 51.7%, 38.3%, for Juba, Kajo Keji, Morobo, Pibor and Tambura respectively.The county health systems are weak because of limited financing from the government, a limited number of qualified health workers who are demotivated because of poor remuneration, inadequate drugs and medical supplies, poor health infrastructure, poor access to services and weak leadership (WHO 2022).

2. Project activities

The main activities selected for this response and aligned with the specific objectives for HRP 2024 include: 1) OPD & Inpatient Care: case management for common diseases, maternal, newborn and child care services:basic emergency obstetric and newborn care (BEmONC) and Emergency Obstetric and newborn Care (EmONC), antenatal care, postnatal care, clinical management of rape and abortion care and integrated management of childhood illnesses. 2) Sexual and reproductive health and adolescent health services: provide family planning services and adolescent friendly reproductive health services targeting women of reproductive age, adolescent girls and boys and key populations. 3) Immunization; vaccination of children against vaccine-preventable diseases according to south sudan guidelines; 4) Psychosocial health and mental health services and GBV; provide psychosocial support to victims of gender-based violence, intimate partner violence, and HIV positive people, identify and refer cases of mental illnesses for definitive treatment etc. 5) Health education, and promotion: provide health education to women, men, adolescents, young women and men to positively influence health behaviours of individuals and communities with the integration of peace through community consultative and dialogue meetings. 6: Infection control, and prevention, emergency preparedness and response and disease surveillance: COVID-19 and EVD surveillance, other epidemic prone disease surveillance, implementing effective infection control and prevention activities at health facilities to protect both health workers and patients. 6) Capacity building: Build the capacity of health workers at health facilities, and county health department to ensure sustainability of interventions. Train and mentor health facility staff, train and mentor county health department staff, train and mentor boma health workers.
