Project Summary

HDPO started its interventions in North Darfur, mainly in Leil (Umbrau locality), where community-managed education infrastructures were implemented; however, HDP also intervened in COVID19 response Khartoum state and Elfashir as part of saving return approaches. In North Darfur, HDPO signed an MoU with the Ministry of education to continue the education state's active support in e current selected localities were identified jointly with the education sector. HDPO will work closely with the education sector in 2021 to sustain the interventions and better coordination, including the gap analysis and supporting the sector in reporting sector meetings' activities and participation.
This project is mainly aims to ensure increased access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education for the children boys and girls affected by emergencies and improving the quality of education to ensure continuity of learning during and after emergencies for the most vulnerable school-aged children, both boys and girls in the targeted localities (Tina, Kornoy and Umbarrow localities) of North Darfur state which are one of the most affected areas by the crisis and has a big number of internally displaced people (IDPs). Humanity for Development & Prosperity Org (HDPO) proposes to support the affected communities to provide programming for those IDPs and host community with more focus to serve the most vulnerable groups of children in the targeted localities through the provision of the following qualitative and protective minimum standard for education services, the most affected children boys and girls will be more effectively protected from violence, risks, and exploitations. They will be able to mitigate external shocks and ensure continuity of learning. This will be achieved through the following emergency learning materials; psychosocial supports, lifesaving skills/messages, and emergency learning materials (exercise books, bags, pens, blackboards, rulers, etc.). Besides, the proposed education project will help to ensure schools remain open and operational by providing training for 120 teachers, rehabilitation of 20 classrooms in 3 villages which are crowded with displaced children, distributing school kits, materials, furniture, and supplies to targeted schools and COVID 19 hygienic kits besides, this project will help to restore damaged and destroyed school WASH facilities( hands washing stations ) in North Darfur State (Tina, Kornoy, and Umbarrow localities ) targeting 16 schools agreed upon with authorities & community leaders and school with child protection referral pathway established. The proposed project will target 11,540 persons (4937 boys, 6283 girls, 120 men, and 80 women). The project will reduce child labor risk, recruitment into combat, gender-based violence prevention, and joining criminal groups. It will reduce the risk of girls dropping out of school, reducing early marriage and domestic violence. Further, long travel distances to schools discourage families from enrolling their children (both boys and girls). The 3 areas` percentage during the last basic school exam was under 50% who were succeeded in the exam; this project will increase the school-aged children enrolment especially girls, children with disabilities, new returnees children, orphans and teachers will return to schools after the improvement of the school environment. This as well will increase children`s success rate at basic education exam for the upcoming years.

1 - Needs

During 2020, there has a significant focus on villages development from various communities in Kernui, Fourawia, At Tina and Umbaru localities where school construction was one of the focus areas that communities contributed through cost-sharing approach; HDPO worked closely with many of the village development committees through guiding and provision of the technical assistant at different stages ( assessment, target setting and creating systems of cost-sharing) and HDPO supported and funded two full school infrastructure construction in both Urshi and Leil in Umbaru locality and supported he teaching staff beside provision of water supply for schools. HDPO has conducted a rapid assessment mission between 30 Sept up to 06 Oct 2020 and done the community consultations on how to improve further the education in these lactates; later, HDPO coordinated with the Ministry of Education at the state level in North Darfur state for 2021 plans and how to improve the education environment in these areas. HDPO’s team met with village committees in Umbaru, Kornoy, and Tina Localities.Umbarrow ,Kornoy, and El Tina Localities that are located about 350 km northwest of Elfashir town with a total population estimated to be about 443,582 individuals; the teams had visited 10 administration units: Tina locality 4 administration units (Tondabaia , Garadaya, Basaow, and Tina), Kornoy locality 3 administration units (Abugamra, Forawia, and Kornoy) and also Um Barrow locality 3 administration units (Orchi, Muzbat, and Um Barrow). During the assessment, the teams used forms for collecting data from field visits, used data from the Education database, and captured, analyzed with close coordination with the locality authorities, local communities from different villages, and Education partners working in these areas. The assessment team visited several areas in the targeted localities and administration units and divided them into three groups separated by 3 localities. The assessment mainly focused on gathering qualitative and quantitative descriptive data on the educational needs of Umbarrow, Kornoy, and Tina, as well as a shortage of teachers and education deterioration in the areas. Although the community level efforts were more than the expected level, the sector still needs to deploy resources to meet the urgent needs and make the community contribution to education stronger and sustainable. School feeding is also one of the key important interventions that fit the context, which will help in school enrollment, especially for poor facilities. It will help support the protection of the young students. This includes gathering education information for local communities, schools, and other educational components that will directly shape program planning in these locations. The main objective stated is to assess the status of education facilities at villages, schools, school health facilities and to enhance sustainable proper education for all children and to facilitate an understanding of existing capacities and activities in the areas related to education issues in schools and to identify priorities for education intervention in the areas to address the shortage and to improve access to safe and protected school for all children. As the peace agreement has been signed, it is expected the school enrollment in these areas remain hight and will increase even in coming months; hence it is also crucial to expand and add more school infrastructures and teaching staff as well with a focus on the school environment improvement, especially during the COVID19 period.HDPO will continue playing the role of supporting the communities in these areas through leading the sector coordination and keep an eye on the upcoming expecting wave of return. Hence the education sector is requested to advocate with the plan urgent resources budget under this project to meet the needs.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The schools in the targeted location that already desert and semi-desert area with lack of green coverage; hence school infrastructures mainly from the local materials such as woods and other trees- based materials then the limited tree in the area significantly affected by unregulated tree cutting and wood collection but also to humanitarian-aid related activities, especially construction, can lead to soil deterioration, desertification, loss of livelihoods and conflict (as a result of competition over land and resources), especially in this part of North Darfur where Issues around forest management, household energy, poverty alleviation, livelihoods and conflict mitigation need to be addressed together. It is essential to ensure that water outtake does not exceed the replenishment of the water source. The targeted localities are already facing depletion of groundwater; hence, having the school children and their families gathered in the area affects groundwater extraction. They also use it to supply water for their animals. Groundwater monitoring is essential, and in case the water demand exceeds available resources, there may be a need to cap wells and develop alternative sources.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

This project's gender marker is 2a because it is designed to contribute significantly to gender equity by serving men, women, boys and girls. However, lack of access to schools due to a long distance from their home harms children’s education, particularly for girls, and it cased the girls drop out the schools. A lack of access to drinking water and toilets during the school day affects both students' and teachers' lea. Conflicts and natural disasters affected women, girls, boys and men differently; they face different risks and are victimized in different ways. In crisis, educational needs change as does the ability of girls and boys to attend school. It is important to ensure that male and female learners of all ages have equal access to quality, relevant and protective educational opportunities. However, providing educational facilities alone will not guarantee their optimal use or a positive impact on individual learners and the affected communities; only a gender-sensitive, participatory approach at all stages of the project cycle can help ensure an adequate and efficient response is provided. For an education project to positively impact women, girls, boys and men must be involved equally in the process. HDPO will systematically integrate gender mainstreaming approaches into programming; through gender analysis throughout the project life cycle and through gender-sensitive monitoring and evaluation tools. Humanity for Development and Prosperity Organization (HDPO) is planning to involve all community groups at all stages of the project planning, implementation and monitoring. HDPO will ensure that the community-based activities consider the cultural difference in the roles of men, women, girls and boys. HDPO aims to uphold the rights to equal access to services for women, girls, boys, men, older people, minorities, PWSNs and persons with disabilities, which will be established through vulnerability targeting and identifying and removing barriers to access.The project will make sure that girls and boys of all age groups can access education services equally. Continuous monitoring of enrolment, retention, drop-out and completion rates, disaggregated by sex and age, and who takes part in decision forums will help ensure all are accessing services. To allow optimal access, attention should be paid to the mitigation of sexual violence risks on the way to/from or using educational facilities. Besides, the project will ensure women, girls, boys, and men participate equally in the design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of education projects, programs, and strategies, and that women are in decision-making positions, also will ensure that women and men benefit equally from training or other capacity-building initiatives offered by the project. Ensure certain that women and men have equal opportunities for work or employment

Humanity for Development and Prosperity Org(HDPO) program activities follow the standards of: Commitment to Humanitarian Standards and Rights; Setting standards and building capacity; Communication; Participation in programs; monitoring and reporting on compliance; addressing complaints and development of strategy to improve the quality of partnership on Principles of Accountability. Through previous implemented activities, HDPO assures that community were engaged and involved at various stages of implementation where key information was shared and collected. This was done through regular and scheduled community dialogues meetings targeting the community leadership, youths as well as local leadership. At the start of the project, community dialogues meetings will be held to gauge acceptance, needs as well as ensure that the project is well understood. HDPO mainstreaming approach in 2021 will focus on assessing and addressing the needs of all within communities, including men, women, boys, girls, those living with disabilities among other vulnerable groups. During implementation ad hoc committees will be created to organize and mobilize beneficiaries. Information collected and analysed will be discussed with key leaderships as some of the foreseen challenges will be overcome by the community itself. The field staff will be responsible for reflecting any complains to the attention of the partners or Khartoum Country support team accordingly .The action is part of the wider intervention by the Education members to ensure transparency and positive messages during implementation of which HDPO is well versed with feedback mechanisms from community are incorporated throughout the project lifecycle. HDPO will organize a training session for staff members on the prevention of sexual exploitation and sexual abuse to ensure having an internal complaint mechanism in place and be bounded by the Community Based Complaint Mechanism (CBCM) and to ensure communities they are serving are aware and have safe and equal access to these mechanisms and can report on any sexual violations in case if anyone is exposed to any type of sexual exploitation and abuse; HDPO as well will organize community based awareness raising sessions for community people benefiting from its supported activities including girls, boys, women and men equally to enable them to avoid the risks of sexual exploitation and harassment , Sexual activity with children (persons under the age of 18) is prohibited regardless of the age of majority or age of consent locally and exchange of money, employment, goods, or services for sex, including sexual favours or other forms of humiliating, degrading or exploitative behaviour is prohibit
