Project Summary

This IOM project contributes to the achievement of all three Protection Cluster Objectives and the core GBV AoR and HLP AoR priorities. IOM’s Protection, Gender Equality and Inclusion Unit will implement protection, disability inclusion and GBV programs across South Sudan to address the needs of South Sudanese individuals (including returnees, IDPs and host communities) who have experienced or are at risk of experiencing protection violations, including GBV. The project seeks to build resilience among the most vulnerable, including women, children, persons with disabilities and the elderly, among others, to reduce their vulnerabilities. IOM will work to achieve these aims by implementing GBV prevention programs, mitigating protection and GBV risks across multisectoral interventions and addressing the violations experienced by vulnerable members of the community through protection and GBV response services. The project will include provision of individual protection cash assistance (IPA) to those facing protection risks or those who have survived protection violations. The project will also continue raising awareness on protection and conducting protection monitoring in hotspots of both conflict and climatic shocks. Building on longstanding relations with South Sudan OPDs, and in line with the IASC Guidelines for Inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action and the Must Do Actions defined therein, IOM will implement robust disability inclusion programming, including OPD capacity building and direct assistance to persons with disabilities. IOM DTM products will improve availability of quantitative data on IDPs and returnees, disaggregated by disability via the Washington Group Questions, while findings from barriers and facilitators assessments and KIIs with persons with disabilities will further inform inclusion efforts. IOM will contribute to strengthening the humanitarian response to GBV through building humanitarian staff capacity to implement GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response programming based on the Interagency GBV minimum standards. IOM will also join interagency service mapping and development of referral pathways. In all, IOM aims to support more than 240,000 individuals (44,000 girls; 44,000 boys; 80,000 women; 76,000 men) with core protection and GBV awareness and support services. These interventions will target IDPs, host communities and returnees, including those who have fled the conflict in Sudan.
Committed to full implementation of the PSEA National Action Plan, IOM will have a range of dedicated PSEA activities, including, inter alia, SEA risk assessments and implementation of mitigation measures, review of high-risk areas and processes, orientation and training of staff, dialogues with staff aimed at addressing attitudes, and community engagement and awareness raising, as well as the operationalization of field-level PSEA taskforces and community-based complaints mechanisms.
The IOM HLP component will implement due diligence processes for any activities that are linked to land and property in order to safeguard the communities’ and the agencies’ rights and mitigate and prevent HLP issues. Since HLP is one of the core grassroots issues in South Sudan and one of the main drivers of conflict country wide, working with the government, traditional authorities and the communities is key. IOM will focus on building the technical capacity of government officials and traditional authorities on HLP best practices and alternative dispute resolution (ADR) mechanisms. IOM will also raise awareness in communities on HLP rights and ADR, in addition to identifying HLP needs and providing needed support services, such as tailored counselling, legal assistance, legal drafting of agreements and accompaniment for concerned entities and other related HLP assistance.

1. Needs

Millions of South Sudanese - including IDPs, returnees, refugees and host community members - face protection risks, such as sexual violence, early/forced marriage, intimate partner violence, forced recruitment into armed groups, killing and injury during localized violence, child labour, abduction, trafficking in persons, gang violence, child abuse and neglect, lack of civil documentation, SEA and inability to access core humanitarian services. Children (especially UASC), women, elderly individuals, and people with disabilities are particularly at risk of protection violations. In the context of the Sudan crisis, many returnees have faced particular protection violations, such as CRSV, physical attacks perpetrated by armed groups, family separation, and trafficking in persons. These individuals must undergo protection vulnerability screening to ensure prioritized and safe access to needed services and support.15 per cent of the world’s population are estimated to be persons with disabilities, with higher prevalence in low-income and conflict-affected countries. In crisis, two to four times as many persons with disabilities die compared to those without disabilities, and people with disabilities face a heightened risk of GBV. Furthermore, people with disabilities are often excluded from humanitarian assistance, consultations, and core services due to mobility challenges and lack of inclusive programming. Women, men and youth with disabilities in South Sudan consistently share that the lack of income generating opportunities is a key barrier for leading independent lives, in addition to access to assistive technologies and devices. Among returnees fleeing Sudan are individuals who already had disabilities prior to the conflict, as well as those with a new disability due to injury during the conflict.Gender inequality and harmful gender norms drive GBV in South Sudan, with a large proportion of women and girls experiencing various forms of GBV. Due to dowry traditions, women and girls are generally seen as property and a source of wealth. Women generally hold a subordinate position in the family and community, and the use of GBV and discrimination are seen acceptable tools of authority and control, preventing women from fully realizing their rights or having equal access to decision-making in the household and broader community, as well as placing women and girls at risk of the serious and myriad negative consequences of GBV. Efforts to tackle gender inequality and harmful gender norms are therefore critical for the prevention of GBV and empowerment of women. Additionally, there is high risk of humanitarian assistance increasing risks of GBV and other protection incidents, as well as sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA).Amid high rates of GBV across South Sudan, survivors need comprehensive, quality response services that address the health, psychological, safety, legal and other needs they have. GBV service provision continues to be limited across the country, with very few safe shelters and a limited number of women and girls’ friendly spaces for survivors to access GBV case management and other services, such as income generating activities and psychosocial support. This is especially true in some areas, such as Aweil, where returnees are arriving from Sudan, including many survivors of CRSV. There are a variety of HLP needs related to lack of security of tenure. These include the need to enhance land governance, as there is a lack of existing legislative framework and unified customary laws; the need to support the government technically in enforcing existing HLP policies, as there is a lack in the implementation of the existing HLP policies, which is linked to lack of technical capacity in government and traditional authorities in addition to lack of human and financial resources; and a lack of awareness within the communities about HLP rights and access to them, as well as safeguarding mechanisms.

2. Project activities

IOM will undertake protection monitoring to determine protection risks, including through KIIs, and identify individuals in need of referrals to multi-sectoral and specialized protection services, while providing cash-based support to vulnerable persons in need of time-sensitive individual protection assistance. IOM will also provide sustained cash-based support for longer-term protection outcomes and durable solutions for individuals receiving protection case management by partners or IOM. IOM Protection staff will mainstream protection throughout IOM’s multi-sectoral response, informed by IOM or Protection Cluster/GBV AoR-led safety audits and protection assessments, and will ensure safe and dignified access to assistance by identifying vulnerable individuals for fast tracking and prioritization in line with the do-no-harm principle. At distribution points in host communities, hard-to-reach locations and camp-like settings (IDP camps, transit centres), IOM will manage information, complaints and feedback desks for information provision, referrals and PSEA and general protection messaging. Protection Outreach Workers will hold awareness sessions on protection, PSEA, GBV and access to services, including for returnees from Sudan. In border areas where returnees/refugees are crossing from Sudan, IOM will coordinate with HDC to monitor and screen vulnerable individuals for fast tracking and prioritization for assistance and protection referrals. IOM will also train humanitarian actors on protection and gender mainstreaming, GBV risk mitigation, disability inclusion, and PSEA. IOM will support participation and empowerment of persons with disabilities and address social and infrastructural/physical barriers in humanitarian response, host communities and transit points. IOM will train OPDs on the rights-based model of inclusion; diversity & inclusion of OPD membership and leadership structures; advocacy & communication; and project development & management. Collective and individual community-led projects that facilitate self-reliance of persons with disabilities will be supported. People with physical disabilities will receive rehabilitation, individual assessments and assistive devices, while visually impaired individuals will be given white canes alongside mobility, orientation, and independence training. IOM will support OPDs and psychosocial support teams to address social barriers, community stigmatization and marginalization. Led by a staff member who is a certified SASA! Facilitator, IOM will implement gender transformative approaches primarily targeting men and adolescent boys, including the Engaging Men in Accountable Practice (EMAP) and SASA!, and IOM’s gender transformative programme, adapted to various South Sudan contexts, as well as lead women’s and girls’ empowerment and leadership initiatives. IOM will also support management of WGFS, including GBV case management, psychosocial support, community outreach and awareness raising sessions. IOM and partner staff will be trained on responding to GBV disclosures and safe and ethical referrals, and health professionals will be trained on clinical management of rape in coordination with IOM Health Unit. IOM will focus on HLP governance activities, such building capacity building of government and traditional authorities on HLP, dispute resolution mechanisms, case management and referral mechanisms. IOM will also be working with the government and traditional authorities to provide technical capacity and support in implementing HLP policies and facilitating discussions to review existing policies with gaps, unify customary law approaches on HLP and advocate for the development of further policies. Furthermore, IOM HLP staff will lead awareness raising and community engagement activities on HLP and dispute resolution mechanisms. Finally, IOM will provide assistance on HLP related disputes, and resolution mechanisms, through legal assistance in all its forms.
