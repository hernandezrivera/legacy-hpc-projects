Project Summary

Through this project, ADRA aims to improve access of population affected by the conflict in Eastern Ukraine to protection and psychosocial services.
This will be achieved by increasing their access to essential and life-saving services through establishing community centers and CFSs along the contact line , strengthening the existing referral/case management mechanism in coordination with duty bearers in the social sector and the Ministry of Social Policy in local and Oblast level, delivering educational sessions in schools, kindergartens and community centres, as well as reaching remote locations through mobile teams. ADRA will also strengthen the capacity of protection actors through training on the delivery of gender-responsive PSS services and sexual gender-based violence (SGBV) prevention and response - targeting community centre facilitators, case management workers, social workers and social service providers (police, medical, etc.) . Also this will be achieved by providing free transportation means to isolated settlements so that residents can have access to essential services in nearby large cities.
The project will be implemented in GCA Donetsk Oblast with main focus on settlements near the contact line (0-20 km), and will be targeting vulnerable groups with focus on elderly people, persons with disabilities or injury, persons with chronic illness, and women & girls. Activities will be coordinated with local authorities, local and international organizations operating in the same areas and sectors, and the relevant cluster (health and protection/child-protection) plans and strategies.
The project proposal is prepared based on Humanitarian Response Plan 2019 (HRP). The project will contribute to the implementation of HRP Strategic Objective 2 (SO2): Provide emergency assistance and ensure non-discriminatory access to quality essential services for populations in need. The project will also contribute to the Protection Cluster Objectives 1 and 2: Protection Objective 1. Strengthen protection, including prevention and mitigation of rights’ violations, for conflict-affected people, including IDPs. Protection Objective 2. Ensure conflict-affected people, including IDPs, benefit from full and non-discriminatory access to quality essential services and enjoyment of their rights, with particular attention to people with specific needs .
ADRA will ensure that gender considerations are integrated in all phases of the project cycle. Girls, boys, women and men will be consulted throughout the project about their concerns, specific needs, protection risks, opinions, feedback and solutions to key issues as ADRA values community participation and accountability in all aspects of programming. All data collected for assessments, monitoring, analysis and reporting will be disaggregated by gender and age. Parity in recruitment will be carefully considered and staff will be sensitized on gender.
ADRA will apply a range of mechanisms to ensure accountability to the affected population. Information about ADRA and the planned activities will be made available to target communities through appropriate media (verbally and written in local languages). ADRA will also create opportunities for communities - including children - to participate in programming decisions and provide with feedback throughout the project cycle, including complaints. A feedback hotline is already in operation and receiving calls on a daily basis from all areas of eastern Ukraine. Programme staff and partners will be orientated on their responsibilities in collecting and reporting community feedback. Specific complaints and response mechanism (CRM) provisions will also be made, such as placing suggestion boxes in places visible and accessible to children, adults, partner staff and community volunteers.

1 - Needs

An estimated 5.2M people are affected by the active conflict in eastern Ukraine,3.5M of whom need humanitarian assistance &protection due to widespread mine contamination,escalating psychological trauma & lack of access to basic services.70% of them are children, women, elderly, including 200,000 in <5 km zone &400 000 in zone 5-10 km from CL .The most vulnerable groups are elderly,1-person low-income HHs,people with disabilities &children.Older people make up 30 % of the needy,while HHs run by people aged 40-60 are more vulnerable than before due to widespread unemployment.Older people with disabilities in conflict-affected areas are often excluded from humanitarian assistance. The conflict has revealed particular vulnerabilities amongst affected communities,with clear gender underpinnings.With a large number of men gone,there has been an increase in FHH,placing them at a higher economic vulnerability.Limited work opportunities are exacerbated by limited availability of childcare services in conflict-affected areas,further restricting women’s ability to engage in employment &leaving them economically dependent on childcare allowances &social benefits.Conflict has also led to heightened levels of domestic violence due to increased tolerance of violence in society,easier access to weapons & PTSD among former combatants.For men,there is pressure & a prevalent gender stereotype that men should be fighting &protecting their families.The conflict has undermined ability of males to respond to social expectations,which led to increase in harmful behaviours such as alcohol consumption,drug addiction &high-risk sexual activities,resulting in increasing reports of domestic violence .Several assessments on the issue of gender based violence in the context of the conflict in Eastern Ukraine point to the existence of SGBV, in particular affecting women &girls .Child protection remains a major concern in the conflict area.The conflict also continues to endanger the physical &psychological wellbeing of children.More than 242,000 children &teachers in schools located along the CL regularly experience shelling &face extreme levels of mine contamination.Explosive hazards endanger millions of people in eastern Ukraine.Around 2M people are affected by landmines &ERW in GCA, while almost 1/3 HHs along the CL perceived these hazards.Conflict has had a gendered impact on children’s physical safety &psychological well-being.Mines & ERW are the leading cause of children casualties.The rates of child injury &death as a result of ERW & mines is disproportionately higher for boys(140 child casualties were reported since 2014). Despite these critical threats to children, access to MRE is low,with 75% of schools surveyed through the Education Cluster expressing the need for MRE in 2019 .In terms of psychological distress,conflict-affected adolescent girls are more likely to internalize problems related to the conflict (anxiety, depression, self-harm) than boys who will externalize problems (delinquency, substance abuse, bullying, risky sexual behaviours &aggression).These findings reflect the reinforcement of traditional gender social norms as a result of the conflict,which can in turn reinforce gender inequalities between men, women, girls &boys. Although all residents of the conflict-affected (particularly those within 20 km of the contact line) area face increased risks,there are additional concerns relating to populations living in those settlements that have become isolated as a result of the disruption in transport networks &infrastructure caused by the conflict.These are settlements where access roads have deteriorated or become inaccessible, local authorities cannot extend their services, and/or checkpoints control access.Within this context of an already highly vulnerable population, those isolated settlements experience greater restrictions on movement, significant security risks &unemployment, compounding needs of the resident population

2 - Activities & outcomes

Overall Objective: To provide emergency assistance and ensure non-discriminatory access to quality essential services for populations in need• Result 1: Specific services for persons of concern with psychosocial needs provided  Activity 1.1: 8700 beneficiaries, including those living along the line of contact, will receive individual and group support in psychosocial care through establishing three community centers with mobile teams and child-friendly spaces (CFS). Activity1.2.: 600 individuals who find themselves in a difficult life situation will be supported with the case management approach (with a component of cash support for the most vulnerable cases). This will be achieved through strengthening 1 existing Case Management unit of oblast level. Activity1.3: 150 beneficiaries of the most critical needs referred from the case management will receive cash assistance  Activity1.4: 150 beneficiaries of the most critical needs referred from the case management receive legal advice and counselling. Activity1.5: 10 facilitators from community centers and mobile teams will receive a training of trainers from certified MRE trainers, so they can also roll it out in community centers and their mobile teams reaching isolated settlements along the contact line. Activity1.6: 7500 beneficiaries will receive Gender and age-responsive mine risk education sessions in schools and kindergartens by mobile teams and in community centers.ADRA will ensure that all planned MRE activities will comply with IMAS standards and UNICEF & GICHD’s Best Practice Guidebooks . Boys and girls will be consulted to understand the gendered differences in risky behaviors in relation to mines/ERW. The findings will be documented in an internal report to inform a training of trainers led by a recognized international trainer (mainly FSD :Foundation Suisse de Déminage ) , targeting facilitators for MRE & community centers. • Result 2: Improving the capacity and technical expertise of caregivers and local professionals to meet the urgent need of psycho-social support for vulnerable families and their children.  Activity 2.1.: 200 psychologists and social workers are trained in practical methods and trauma management techniques mainly in Humanitarian principles, CPMS and CPiE, MHPSS recommendations, PFA, Case Management approach, Child Participation, etc. Activity 2.2.: 250 teachers in 25 schools along the contact line receive therapeutic support in dealing with traumatized children and in the prevention of secondary/ indirect traumatization Activity 2.3.: 750 care givers (males and females) will attend sessions to increase their knowledge, form practical skills how to support children experienced crisis situations and traumatic events. • Result 3: Full and non-discriminatory access to quality essential services and enjoyment of their rights for people of concern in hard-to-reach areas along the contact line have been provided Activity 3.1.: 6,000 beneficiaries benefited from free social transportation services for emergency relief measures
