Project Summary

UNRWA proposed intervention aims at ensuring continuity of learning for UNRWA students in the West Bank in the context of COVID-19 pandemic. The project will provide UNRWA schools with cleaning and hygiene materials in order to guarantee the safety of students and teachers.
Through the provision of IT equipment and offline printed material to the most vulnerable students, UNRWA will ensure their access to education in the context of COVID-19 pandemic through its self-learning programme. Simultaneously, the project aims at providing emergency child protection response through psycho-social support in schools.

1 - Needs

The 2020 COVID-19 emergency has brought major consequences on the education system and UNRWA is working within this challenging context to continue providing quality, inclusive, and equitable education to Palestine refugees. Following the school closure in March 2020, UNRWA reacted immediately to ensure continuity of education services. As part of the Education in Emergency response, UNRWA has been implementing its Self-Learning Programme, through the regular dissemination of self-learning material to its 46,050 students. After six months of closure, in line with the Palestinian Ministry of Education, schools gradually reopened in September 2020. As part of the COVID-19 response, UNRWA developed its back to learning plan, so as to ensure a safe access to schools, while maintaining social distancing and hygiene standards, within a merged education framework. For UNRWA school reopening implied a revision of the class formation that accommodates social distancing and hygiene norms and standards, which require additional resources. The school year is characterized by a blended learning approach, combining face-to-face education and self-learning. Part of the curriculum was converted to self-learning to decrease students’ presence in schools, and minimize the risk of COVID-19 infection. All students have 60% face-to-face education and 40 per cent self-learning programme (from home). According to a survey conducted in March 2020, around 70% of students accessed self-learning material and online resources during the period of March to May 2020, and self-learning materials will continue to be needed to ensure the quality of students’ learning. However, 11,500 UNRWA students in the West Bank do not have access to the internet at home or adequate equipment to access the self-learning material. Finally, children, youth and education staff are all likely to face additional stress during this period. UNRWA has previously emphasized face-to-face support and social interaction with peers as key dimensions of its psycho-social support but this is not feasible in the context of COVID-19. School counselors are however finding new ways to continue to support the psycho-social needs of students through alternative means of communication.

2 - Activities

As a response to COVID-19, UNRWA schools are following strict protocols to limit the risk of further contamination among students and teachers. All schools facilities must be disinfected and cleaned prior to receiving children. The project will provide cleaning supplies and hygiene material to be able to conduct regular cleaning in line with standards and WHO guidelines. To guarantee that students learn in a healthy and hygienic environment, students will receive reusable cloth masks, which they will be able to use daily. The 40,000 masks will be made by young trainees from UNRWA vocational training centre in Ramallah in accordance with Palestinian/international specifications for masks. Furthermore, all school staff will receive adequate PPE to conduct their classes in safety. Awareness raising activities about topics such as hand washing habits, communicable diseases and COVID-19 prevention measures will be carried out in all the schools. As part of the school curriculum was converted into a Self-Learning Programme to ensure continuity of learning in the context of COVID-19, UNRWA will equip the most vulnerable students with self-learning material. 7,500 vulnerable students will receive tablets containing electronic versions of the self-learning material, in addition to electronic copies of the textbooks and will be provided with printed material to access the self-learning Programme. This is particularly important as students might be prevented from coming to schools due to infection, isolation or quarantine at home even though schools remain open. Furthermore, 900 teachers from the most vulnerable schools will be equipped with laptops to enable them to plan and conduct remote learning and create self-learning material for students though the recording of videos and texts.Finally, UNRWA is strengthening the provision of MHPSS activities to boys and girls studying at UNRWA schools through supporting the school counselors' capacity in all schools. As part of their role, UNRWA school counselors form the backbone of MHPSS support in the Education Programme and provide both individual and group counselling activities and identify, support and refer children-at-risk. To do so, UNRWA will recruit 19 additional school counselors, for the most in-need schools, to support addressing the increased psycho-social needs during the COVID-19 pandemic through virtual or phone consultation.

UNRWA is committed to strengthening existing standards and timeframes for the conduct of investigations as they relate to cases involving children, including feedback mechanisms and communication with complainants. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In UNRWA Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritization of safety and dignity is explicitly addressed as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneficiaries, communities or staff. Further, UNRWA Medium-Term Strategy 2016-2021 explicitly prioritizes the "do-no-harm" principles by stating that UNRWA will address protection challenges that arise from its programme service delivery in education. This will include focusing on instances when UNRWA, through its actions or policies, unintentionally undermines the realization of specific rights such as the right to education. By addressing such concerns through "do-no-harm" and other approaches, UNRWA service delivery promotes and respects the rights of beneficiaries and ensures the security and dignity of beneficiaries. Protection needs are addressed in all aspects of regular UNRWA programme and project design, policies, protocols and procedures, as well asin staff training. In all contexts, UNRWA will work to ensure that its assistance and service delivery is carried out in accordance with internationally agreed standards and its own minimum protection standards. UNRWA will regularly measure its level of compliance with these minimum standards and take necessary corrective action. The best interests of the child will be the main focus of this project throughout the activities’ implementation, including through guaranteeing the confidentiality, and safe data storage of, information disclosed by the children.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA welcomes all Palestine refugee children from grade 1 to 9 in its 96 schools across the West Bank. Educational staff, who are regularly targeted by capacity building activities including on protection mainstreaming and gender, are able to refer specific protection cases to MHPSS counselors for follow up. Towards greater student inclusion, UNRWA continues to strengthen support for students with additional needs or those who risk losing education opportunities due to heightened conflict. The percentage of students with disabilities provided with support according to their needs increased from 37.71 per cent in 2018 to 71.74 per cent in 2019. The Education in Emergency programme continued to enable increased psycho-social support, parental engagement and safety and security efforts to facilitate access to education in times of crisis. In relation to gender equity, the majority of gains in key education indicators are reflected among both male and female students.The work of the Education programme to enhance systems for gathering, monitoring and engaging actively with relevant data to improve quality, equity and inclusion at multiple levels continues. The Agency also continued to strengthen the implementation of its School Quality Assurance (SQA) process. The SQA process is one of rigorous review of a school with resultant clear linked recommendations and subsequent tailored support.
