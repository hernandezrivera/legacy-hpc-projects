Project Summary


The proposed project is designed in response to the Humanitarian response plan for 2024 and seeks to enhance access to quality and inclusive education through integration of local innovation in Mubi north and Michika LGAs of Adamawa State.
The HRP and HNO 2023 indicated that out of the 8.3m persons in need, (4.9m are children aged between 0-17 years and 53% of them are female), 1.39m persons requiring education and vocational services.The GBV AoR and Education sector further illuminated the specific needs, with 1.8 million persons requiring service. It further showed that host community members and returnees face severe needs, with 24.1% respectively, experiencing extreme severity levels in Adamawa state. The plight of returnees in the State, where 42% of this vulnerable population seeks lifesaving assistance, adds another layer of complexity. The gender dynamics of returnees, with 53% being female and 60% children, place women and girls at the forefront of the crisis. In Mubi North and Michika LGAs of Adamawa state, normalcy has been achieved but there are still remains insufficient classrooms and lack of basic teaching supplies. There is need for training of teachers to support children cope with stress and provide psycho-social support to the children.
Following the needs identified at Mubi North and Michika LGA of Adamawa state, J-HF proposes to implement activities that will ensure conflict affected children and youths have access to quality and inclusive education in safe learning environments. Furthermore, J-HF will provide accelerated basic education for the children who have never been in schools or dropped out at early grades, provide learning materials and train teachers.
This project also seeks to Support State and local authorities/structures to build resilience for crisis-affected communities. Therefore, youth groups and other community structures such as School Based Management Committee (SBMC) will be strengthened to perform their functionality. More so small grant will be provided to support management of education, monitoring, maintenance, resource mobilization, and safety for quality education.
The proposed project will construct and rehabilitate existing classroom and temporary learning centers across the communities of intervention. Furthermore, to improve quality education, J-HF will strengthen the capacity of teachers to adopt Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) and the use of experiential learning which is a great teaching method because it encourages creativity, helps children learn from mistakes, fosters reflective thinking, and prepares pupils for future experiences. J-HF will also provide tablets,smart boards and other modern teaching and learning materials to boost children’s engagement in the classroom which allows them to be more alert and participate in active classroom learning.
To enhance access to education for children and youth, J-HF will form and strengthen Girls 4 Girls Clubs and He 4 She Clubs (safe spaces) within the temporary centers; this will provide a platform to address barriers to education and gender inequality..
The project will improve access to quality education in safe learning environments where children and youth feel safe while communities build resilience to live peacefully and sustain programs. This intervention will also adopt a community-inclusive, culturally sensitive approach involving community institutions and structures.
The project will include persons living with disability (PLWD) across all age and gender groups. Persons living with disability will be supported to benefit from the intervention through a tailored support in line with the type of disability, in Mubi North and Michika LGAs of Adamawa state.

1 - Needs

The HRP and HNO 2023 indicated out of the 8.3m persons in need, 1.39m persons requires education and vocational services, while 4.57m persons require protection services. Out of the total population of 8.3m, 4.9m are children 0-17 years and 53% of them are female. The GBV AoR and Education sector further illuminated the specific needs, with 1.8 million persons requiring services. The HRP and HNO 2023 emphasizes the impact on people with disabilities, who are virtually invisible in existing services and infrastructure. The MSNA 2023 underscores the vulnerability of female-headed households, with higher risks of hunger, lower income, and negative coping strategies for their children. It further showed that host community members and returnees face severe needs, with 24.1% respectively, experiencing extreme severity levels in Adamawa State. The plight of returnees in the State, where 42% of this vulnerable population seeks lifesaving assistance, adds another layer of complexity. The gender dynamics of returnees, with 53% being female and 60% children, place women and girls at the forefront of the crisis. As the impact of the conflict.At Michika and Mubi North LGAs of Adamawa state, normalcy has been achieved with insufficient classrooms and lack of basic teaching supplies. There is need for training of teachers to support children cope with stress and provide psychosocial support to the children. It is also worthy to note that children with other protection concerns are also identified in those locations.Bulletin published by the NE Nigeria Education in Emergency working group in collaboration with the states Ministries of Education in November 2023 has reported that there is an urgent need to enroll many out-of-school children into formal schools, because some parents still send their children and wards to attend informal education system. [Nigeria - Sector Status: Education | Digital Situation Reports (unocha.org)]. Also, the report has indicated that there is a need to support learners who completed non-formal education programs and vocational training to mainstream into formal education and training opportunities. Another education report has indicated that despite the importance of education, there is continued unimpressive registration of students' enrolments in Michika and Mubi into basic education classes. Other independent surveys have also indicated that the perpetration of abuse, assault and violence against boys and girls and women is largely triggered by them having little access to formal education, leading to early and forced marriages among girls in their basic education stage. Following the needs identified by J-HF at Gombi, Hong and Michika LGA of Adamawa state, J-HF proposes to implement activities that will ensure conflict affected children and youths have access to quality and inclusive education in safe learning environments. J-HF is currently implementing an education project in Mubi North LGA on girl child education and will want to leverage on the lessons learnt for replication in other schools.

2 - Activities

ActivitiesActivity 1.1.1.Inception workshop/Advocacy/community engagementActivity 1.1.2Learning outcome assessment for learners A baseline assessment will be carried out at the beginning of the project to determine the knowledge and grade placement of learners. While the end line will be carried out towards the end of the project. Then, an evaluation will be conducted to determine the impact of the project on the knowledge gained by the learners. Activity 1.1.3 J-HF will Collaborate with the Local Education Authority to assess the levels of damage for classrooms across project locations . Activity 1.1.4Provision of learning spaces through the construction and rehabilitation of dilapidated classrooms and learning space for more children to access education. Activity 1.1.5 Formation of Girl 4 Girls Clubs and He 4 She Clubs The G4G and H4S Clubs is a peer learning clubs (Age 6-17)with a membership 25 members per group. J-HF will form and strengthen the Clubs on right to education, the importance of Education, gender equality, GBV, good hygiene practices, leadership, and life skills; this is to bring a behavioral change of their mindset, conceive core value of education, determining and willing to adapt on situation that surrounds the flow of the system to retain, complete and transit from level to levels. Activity 1.2.1 Engagement of Community Volunteer Teachers (CVTs) to provide Accelerated Education Program (AEP) Qualified CVTs will be engaged and provided with monthly incentives for a period of 9 months. The CVTs will be recruited from the community following a recruitment process. Subsequently, successful candidates will be chosen to provide lessons at the alternative learning centers. Activity 1.2.2Training of CVTs on the delivery of Teaching at the Right Level (how to ensure pupils read and write with the use of a simple testing tool to assess and group children according to their learning level rather than their age or grade)and the AEP curriculum . Activity 1.2.3Provision of 3000 teaching and learning materials The project will provide teaching and learning materials including those of Teaching at the Right Level to teachers and learners, respectively, in order to improve teachers' pedagogical skills and increase student participation in the classroom. Learners will be provided with numeracy and literacy workbooks, school bags, and writing materials (including exercise books, pencils, erasers, and sharpeners). While teaching materials will consist of facilitators manuals, chalk, tablets, virtual boards and classroom registers.Activity 1.2.4Conduct supervision and monitoring visits for schools and other educational facilities. J-HF will organize a joint monitoring visit to schools and educational facilities. A monitoring checklist will be developed by the M&E department to ensure focused and effective monitoring and supervision during school visits. A joint monitoring team will be formed with individuals from SUBEB, the Ministry of Education (MoEs), LGEAs, and J-HF staff. Regular monitoring and supervision of schools and project sites will serve as quality assurance for effective teaching and learning.Actitivity 1.2.5Mapping and Capacity Building for SBMCs J-HF will use the OCA tool to assess the needs of identified SBMCs in order to determine their capacity. The tool will provide them with a set of criteria to assess their capacity to carry out their mandate. A capacity-building improvement plan will be developed in collaboration with the Committees based on the needs assessment findings. J-HF will train SBMC members and other community-based groups on how to effectively carry out monitoring of education services, budgeting, advocacy, and how to efficiently manage data and information through the development of tools and processes. Activity 1.2.6SBMC will be supported with a small grant to enable them execute their functions.Activity 1.2.7Project close out workshop.
