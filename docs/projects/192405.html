Project Summary

Save the Children’s Child Focused Social Assistance program for Vulnerable Families, also known as Child Wellbeing Assistance, is an updated approach to providing multi-purpose cash plus programming for families, bearing in mind that Lebanon has entered a chronic economic emergency.2 The focus, aligned with the ERP, is for vulnerable Lebanese and migrant families to meet their basic needs including food needs. The value of the cash transfer will ensure families not only meet the survival needs of their children, but can also afford other basic services to ensure the wellbeing of their children. Childhood is a uniquely vulnerable period, from birth, through 0-5, through schooling, and then later as an adolescent and youth. Families can struggle to adequately meet the healthcare, nutrition, protection, and education needs of children, resulting in multiple, cumulative deprivations that can have long-term, irreversible negative impacts on a child’s life as well as on society as a whole.
SC’s cash “plus” programming combines counselling for families and caregivers to ensure that children’s needs are covered and spending choices are made in the best interest of the child, to support their wellbeing, and to prevent harmful coping strategies.. This is approach is based on SCI’s evidence that protecting children from the impact of poverty is one of the most economically and socially sound investments that Lebanon can make to support the development and economic stability of the country.
Targeting
Child Wellbeing Assistance is targeting children in the family and their caregivers. As such, only families with children will be eligible to receive the support. A “child” for the purpose of this program is between the ages 0-18. This can include families with pregnant women in their third trimester. The updated child vulnerability assessment tool takes a more comprehensive approach to the vulnerability of children in the household, combining risks from many sectors to establish a multidimensional assessment of child vulnerability, and therefore eligibility for Child Wellbeing Assistance.
Specific vulnerabilities including children or caregivers with disabilities, female-headed households, and other characteristics will be included and scored in the selection tool. Outreach to these specifically vulnerable families is prioritized to ensure programming reaches the most underserved, and this is supported by SC’s Gender & disability Specialist.
Transfer value and timeframe of assistance
The calculation methodology of transfer value at the time of writing will remain in line with the SMEB values used by the national inter-agency Basic Assistance Working Group. Under the Emergency Response Plan it is expected SC will support families to meet their basic needs for between 6 to 12 months.
Transfer modality
At the time of writing SC’s basic assistance programmes disperses funds in LBP at the Sayrafa rate. However, with recent declaration of the hybrid, two phased approach, SC will be moving toward dollarizing cash assistance in line with the Food Security & Basic Assistance groups

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Under this activity SC will identify vulnerable families with children, assessed through the child vulnerability assessment tool, and provided with monthly cash over a period of 6 months, to support them to meet their basic needs including food needs. The focus will be on ensuring child survival and wellbeing, for the most vulnerable Lebanese, PRL and migrant families. Integrated within this activity will be essential messaging and referrals. Amounts are coordinated through Basic Assistance group

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

SC undertakes a risk assessment, including a focus on ensuring the protection and safeguarding of children and adults. All staff are trained on PSEA and safeguarding and sign up to SC’s code of conduct & safeguarding policies, with regular refreshers on reporting procedures. SC is committed to mainstreaming diversity and inclusion across all its projects and programs. Gender Equality Markers are completed for all project and use of GBV Minimum Actions at the design stage with a close monitoring and on-the-job coaching at the field level. SC works towards creating gender-sensitive to transformative and inclusive/accessible programming with strong focus on women, girls, and diverse groups' empowerment. SCI's approach includes conducting regular gender and disability analysis, framing the analysis recommendations and outcomes into practical action planning, setting gender equality and social inclusion indicators in the MEAL framework.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Accountability to affected populations is central to SC’s work and ensured through feedback and reporting mechanisms, regular engagement and communication with stakeholders including children. Focus group discussions are held with adults and children to ensure full understanding of the project, as well as to hear any feedback. In particular for this project, communications to cash recipients will ensure clarity in the purpose and application of the cash assistance. Families should be made aware of GRM faculties and field teams will always distribute hotline number cards during cash distributions. A Q&A document will be circulated with the field staff (across sectors) and the call center staff to support them in answering frequently asked questions by the community that are related to our cash programs. The Q&A should be continuously updated to reflect any trends in the field, or any recurrent questions received by the staff that are not included in the document.
