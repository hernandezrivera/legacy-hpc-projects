Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen protection and resilience of Syrian IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities through advocacy, coordination and provision of community-based protection services.
UNHCR Syria will continue to lead the Protection Sector/Cluster to ensure coordinated protection interventions programming for safe refugees return.
UNHCR’s protection interventions include legal counselling and assistance, protection monitoring, referrals and information sharing, social protection through livelihoods support to most vulnerable people, GBV prevention and response, child protection, psychosocial support and in-kind assistance for persons with specific needs, non-formal education, and awareness activities on various subjects targeting women, men, boys and girls. In addition to over 100 community centres providing integrated protection services, outreach to communities will be convened through over 2,800 outreach volunteers.
Accountability to affected people will be realized through meaningful participation of communities, structured focus group discussions and enhancement of community-based complaint mechanisms. UNHCR will continue its capacity building efforts to ensure services are delivered according to the standards and that accountability to affected population and do no harm principles are respected.
UNHCR Syria will continue to lead Protection Sector in coordination of response plans of IDPs and affected people as well it will execute leadership roles in refugee return programmes in collaboration with the Government. In North West Syria, it will lead Protection Monitoring Task Force (PMTF).

4 - Needs

Protracted displacement, lack of services and deterioration of living conditions exacerbated by the COVID-19 have affected all population groups and in particular IDPs. Focus group discussions, surveys and interviews conducted by UNHCR and its partners indicated that the needs have increased, community resources are overstretched while populations’ resilience capacity has been severely eroded. This has further undermined communities’ ability to address complex and multi-linked protection issues and may lead to additional harmful coping mechanisms and increase in social tensions. Returnees experienced additional challenges in 2020 due to socio-economic consequences of COVID-19 and deteriorating economic situation, resulted in increased reliance on humanitarian support. The protracted the crisis combined with traditional patriarchal structures in Syrian society, have caused GBV to be normalised. Combined with economic hardship, the situation has forced many families to resort to negative coping mechanisms such as child marriage. Additionally, changing roles within family structures have resulted in increased domestic tensions and partner violence, causing psychosocial & physical injury for survivors and children witnessing such violence. Syrian children face heightened risks as a result of the crisis. Continuous displacement, exposure to violence, deepening poverty and persistent lack of access to services have had a profoundly negative impact on children’s well-being. The situation has exposed them to a wide range of protection risks including family separation, school drop-out, child marriage, child labour, exploitation, physical/emotional abuse

5 - Implementation of activities

In line with the identified needs, the project will be implemented through integrated protection services with a focus on community-based approaches, including 1) targeted support to persons with specific protection needs and the elderly; 2) legal counselling and legal presentation on civil statues documentation; 3) advocacy and support to government on HLP related matters, 4) provision of quality and life-saving response services to GBV and child protection; 5) psychosocial support; 7) community based child protection and women’s committees; 8) PSEA awareness raising and response to incidence, and 9) community empowerment through community led initiatives and proactive engagement of community based grassroot organizations to build resilience and solidary.UNHCR will strengthen its outreach to people in need in all governorates through capitalization of community outreach volunteers, community and satellite centers and mobile units. Prevention campaigns and awareness sessions will be undertaken to reduce harmful behaviors and promote gender equality. Response services to GBV and child protection cases primarily will include individual case management and referrals to other services. Through the GBV sub-sector, UNHCR will be rolling out the global SGBV mainstreaming programme throughout 2021 to ensure SGBV is mainstreamed in all other sectors and SGBV survivors are considered when planning for legal or livelihood activities. Prevention of child protection issues will be also carried out through community-based structures /child welfare committees and children clubs. Likewise, women committees will be expanded to assist in GBV prevention and response activities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when delivering assistance and service. Mitigating measures: Raise awareness among staff of UNHCR and its partners, as well as communities about SEA and that humanitarian aid and services are rights and provided free. Apply mandatory SEA screening when recruiting staff members. Design specific complaint mechanisms and ensure adequate staff to follow up on complaints, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality. Risk 2: Increased community tension due to the assistance, and/or unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to perceived discrimination. Mitigating measures: Provision of standardized assistance packages with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Harmonized and transparent selection criteria used by all partners. Awareness raising conducted with communities. Regular monitoring, consultation, post distribution monitoring and adjustments to the program to prevent and manage any tensions which may arise. Risk 3. Lack of involvement and technical capacity of national stakeholders in areas where the response to needs require their involvement, e.g. as regard to access to civil documentation and HLP documents, specific child protection interventions (family reunification, alternative care arrangements). Mitigating measures: Proactive coordination with relevant authorities. Technical and capacity support to strengthen national institutions in service delivery.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria. UNHCR will work closely with other stakeholders, including the GBV Sub-working Group and PSEA network to ensure there is an adequate and multifaceted response in place with due regard to the “do no harm” principle. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR. In addition to mandatory trainings to its own staff, since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment has become mandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partners who failed to meet the standard of the PSEA checklist will not be able to continue its partnership with UNHCR in the following year.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Fluid context, shifting frontlines and unstable political situation in North Syria. Mitigating measures: Proactively coordinate with UNDSS and sister agencies in terms of security and access issues. Advocate at senior levels of all stakeholders to identify political solutions to ensure safe and continued access. Risk 2: Limited ability to conduct monitoring of delivery of assistance results in potential loss of resources. Mitigating measures: Advocacy with authorities for access to beneficiary data. Strengthen risk based monitoring tools and establishing remote/third party monitoring arrangements where UNHCR does not have direct access. Support partners to establish harmonized monitoring systems and provide training to partner staff on monitoring. Strengthen communication with communities and feedback mechanisms.
