Project Summary

AAH has started a 5-month project in Benghazi funded by SDC, which aims at reducing the impact of Covid-19 and its psychological and psychosocial consequences, through strengthening the capacities of community members and frontline workers to face the psychological and psychosocial distress related to COVID-19 risk and spread, and to adopt appropriate behaviours to avoid contamination from COVID-19. Through this project, AAH is targeting 3000 individuals, including 50 Health workers/CBO workers and 2000 vulnerable people in need of psychosocial and psychological services. At this early stage of its mission in Libya AAH has opted for a mental health project integrated with RCCE and IPC activities, not yet including a purely health approach. This first project will allow AAH to better understand and analyse the health context for a better future response.

1 - Needs

The total number of people displaced across the country is around 390,000, remains 30% higher today compared to the same time in 2019. Around 490,000 people returned to their place of origin. . In 2020, the Libyan humanitarian situation has been further compounded by the continuing spread of COVID-19, which is causing a public health emergency. From a population of 6.7 million, it was estimated that over one in four people (1.8 million people) were affected by the crisis and more than one in ten (900,000 people) were in need of humanitarian assistance . Given these crises, in 2021 the humanitarian needs are anticipated to be significantly higher. Since the first case of COVID-19 was reported in Libya on March 2020, a total of 57 975 people have been infected with the virus. Of this number, 812 people have died. The national case fatality rate (CFR) is 1.40%. Libya has witnessed a 22% increase in the number of cases of COVID-19 over the first two weeks of November 2020. Libya remains classified as “community transmission”. This armed conflict, have been reducing peoples’ access to health services, with the WHO estimating that more than 25% of public health facilities in Libya are closed and are facing a lack of health workers, of medicines and Personnal Protective Equipment (PPE), a low level of basic service provision, especially in Primary Health Care (PHC) facilities.
