Project Summary

(Planned – SO2 )
First: Provide 1000 livestock breeders with Veterinary Assistance ( Veterinary vaccines, veterinary consultations, distribution of veterinary supplies, vitamins, and salt blocks) in the North West of Syria. Three main diseases spreading widely among livestock in North West of Syria, including the following:
1. Foot and Mouth Disease
2. Sheep-pox and cowpox
3. Enterotoxemia
- Misk seeks to implement a comprehensive project to vaccinate sheep, goats, and cows(Three types of vaccines against the aforementioned three epidemic diseases in three rounds ) - They will conduct onsite consultations to raise veterinary awareness among livestock breeders in the region as well as provide technical consultations on causes, treatment, and prevention of livestock diseases Sheep, goats, and cows.
Second: - In Tell Abiad will the distribution of agricultural livelihood assistance ( seasonal agricultural kit) through cultivating small homesteads this intervention will benefit 600 households.
- Distribution of sheep & fodder for 600 households.
- The project will incorporate support through the project life agricultural extension support.
This will yield higher household-level income and ultimately improve food security. The integration of these activities, in a phased and comprehensive approach, is essential for ensuring a sustainable impact on the productive capacity and long-term resilience to shocks for communities in northeast Syria.

4 - Needs

Referring to HNO 2021, • Between 2020 and 2021, the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in northwest Syria has increased from 2.8 to 3.4 million people. The overall humanitarian situation remains dire for 4.2 million people living in Idleb and Afrin as well as A’zaz to Jarablus areas in northwest Syria. Humanitarian needs in northwest Syria continue to be driven by the impacts of over a decade of hostilities exacerbated by economic deterioration and multiple displacements.• Meanwhile, according to the Humanitarian Needs Assessment Programme (HNAP), 242,468 individuals have returned to their place of origin in northwest Syria in 2020. Between January and February 2021, close to 7,400 people are estimated to have returned to southern Idleb and western Aleppo governorates despite difficult conditions for return, including contamination of explosive hazards, and limited access to basic service and livelihood opportunities. The crisis has also led to an increase in serious bacterial and viral diseases affecting livestock, especially sheep, cows, and goats as their owners face difficulties and financial burdens dealing with these diseases. After nine years of crisis, the people of Syria, including those residing in camps, continue to face great challenges, many struggle to feed and educate their children. The situation in Tell Abiad is characterized by progressive stabilization following the change of control in northeast Syria, The main trigger of food insecurity is uncertainty, depleted livelihood opportunities, poor access to food, decreased crop production, and livestock decline. Areas with a higher concentration of IDPs have higher rates of food insecurity, reaching up to 54% of the population.
