Project Summary

The intervention will contribute to the empowerment, basic rights and well-being of girls, boys and adolescents and their caregivers in Rohingya camps (17, 8E and 14) and host communities in Cox's Bazar. Considering this goal, the ISCG and education sectors the project will target 6,005 Children, adolescents and youth, men and women by the humanitarian crisis. The specific objective is to create access to quality gender-sensitive education for children and their caregivers facing life-threatening risks of abuse, neglect, exploitation, gender-based violence and severe distress in three camps and associated host communities. The project will: 1. improve Safe, inclusive and equitable access to learning opportunities and protective environment, 2. Provide quality and inclusive in-formal, non-formal and formal Myanmar curriculum education through Accelerated learning programme (ALP) 3. Enhance community engagement and system through strengthen of Educations partners and relevant stakeholders to ensure quality and accountable implementation of Education service 4. provide gender-sensitive education and training for children and women at risk of abuse, exploitation, gender-based violence and disasters,
The to address the serious needs for education regarding school enrollment and attendance, Understanding Child learning challenges, safety and environment and Children in Early Education according to J-MSNA 19 October 2023. Educo will be reach 450 (15 ECD*30) children (3-5 yrs.) have access to pre-primary education through ECD interventions.1500 (50CBLF*30) girls, boys and adolescents (13-18 yrs. old, 70% girls and including PWD) have access to education through CBLFs.The target group of children and adolescents will be chosen based on their risk of exposure to exploitation and all forms of violence, mobility limitations, limited access to basic services (protection, education), and due considerations for children and adolescents with specific needs. The project will train 125 teacher on basic padagogy 5 for host teacher and train 3900 parents/caregiver on positive parenting.
In parallel this project will enhance the protection to ensure a safe, gender responsive and protective environment and beside that provide inclusive quality education for gender mainstreaming emphasize on female volunteers and teachers along with crosscutting issues. The project will be monitoring the activities for ensure accountability to affected population (AAP) to strengthening the community-based education system is the key approach to the action that includes mobilizing caregivers, child protection actors (social workers, community volunteers, government officials) community leaders (Majhis), and non-child protection actors. The project will engage and equip communities to prevent and respond to educations in appropriate and approved channels, be able to identify them, and adopt the necessary mitigation measures. Simultaneously, the intervention will ensure that children feel safe in their communities and are able to cope with external shocks, and parents receive positive parenting training to improve their relationships with children. In addition to this, this project will help enhancing understanding of child Learning challenges among children, adolescents, parents and caregivers.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The results of the Joint-Multisectoral Needs Assessment Report, October 19, 2023 (J-MSNA) Educo has designed the project based on Education sectors PiN and the target of JRR 2024. Despite advancements in education provision, concerns within the Rohingya community regarding girls' enrollment and attendance surpass those in the host community. According to the key findings of the J-MSNA in October 2019, both boys and girls initially exhibited similar attendance rates at 86% and 89%, respectively, with a slight advantage for girls. However, attendance sharply declined to 34% for girls compared to 54% for boys by the age of 14. Notably, the drop worsened to 2% for girls and 13% for boys. For boys, a significant concern centered around the relevance of their learning materials was expressed by 52% of them. Conversely, girls faced entrenched cultural norms and family dynamics, impacting 65% of them. These challenges included fears of bringing shame to their families, worries about marriage prospects, skepticism about the value of educating girls, and experiences of harassment. Child marriage emerged as a direct obstacle for 24% of girls, restricting their educational opportunities and perpetuating gender inequality. The latest J-MSNA in 2023 comes back to these concerns, revealing that only 21% of refugee girls attended learning facilities compared to 51% of refugee boys. Reasons mentioned by 21% of girls included the belief that education is not a priority, while 34% of boys preferred attending Madrasa over learning facilities. In summary, addressing these gender-specific barriers requires not only enhancing educational quality but also challenging and reshaping deeply ingrained gender norms to create a more equitable educational environment for children, irrespective of gender. In terms of safety and environmental conditions, much of both the host community (97%) and refugee households (98%) reported safe travel and learning conditions for their children. However, 2% of refugee children face challenges in acceptable learning conditions due to factors such as long travel distances, security concerns, recruitment risks, unsafe infrastructure, verbal bullying, a lack of qualified staff, curriculum inadequacies, insufficient materials, and teacher shortages. During the 2022-2023 school year, 54% of early education-aged children (aged 3–4 for refugees and 4 for the host community) were attending early education programs, while 46% were not attending. The host community reported that the obstacle is the inability to enroll children, while for refugees, the main factor is the child being considered too young. Significant challenges also exist for children with disabilities contributed by insufficient skill and training of educators, poor awareness, negative attitudes, and stigma related to CwD. LF also commonly lack ramps and accessible seating arrangements, toilets, and water facilities, combined with surrounding terrain that is often steep and enough and difficult to navigate and there is poor availability of assistive devices. To bridge the learning condition gaps for both the host community and refugee children, a comprehensive approach is proposed. This includes investing in infrastructure enhancements and exploring solutions to minimize lengthy travel distances. Enhanced security measures, in collaboration with local authorities, aim to address safety concerns during travel. Strategies to counter recruitment by armed groups involve community collaboration and awareness campaigns. The recruitment and training of qualified teaching staff, contribute to creating a supportive learning environment. Adapting the Myanmar curriculum, ensuring material availability, and engaging communities with cultural sensitivity further enhance educational access. Collaboration with NGOs, regular monitoring, and advocacy for to foster inclusivity and security in learning environments for both communities.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This project will enhance the protection to ensure that children and adolescents have access to improved child protection services in the camps and adjacent host communities. Multi-purpose learning centers will function as child-friendly spaces where they can access life skills training, participate in activities that build their resilience, and receive gender-sensitive mental health and psychosocial support, case management and referral pathway assistance, structured PSS interventions, awareness sessions on child protection, GBV, needs-based literacy and numeracy training, and be reached through home visitation programs. Strengthening the community-based child protection system is the key approach to the action, which includes mobilizing caregivers, child protection actors (social workers, community volunteers, and government officials), community leaders (Majhis), and non-child protection actors into community-based child protection committees (CBCPC). When building knowledge and changing the attitudes, behaviors, and/or practices of target groups, the project will focus on the prevention of and protection from physical and sexual violence as well as related major child protection risks. The project will engage and equip communities to prevent and respond to child protection risks through appropriate and approved channels, identify them, and adopt the necessary mitigation measures. Simultaneously, the intervention will ensure that children feel safe in their communities and are able to cope with external shocks, and parents will receive positive parenting training to improve their relationships with children. In addition to this, this project will help enhance understanding of child protection issues among children, adolescents, parents, and caregivers in order to create efficient community-based child protection mechanisms and to identify and refer cases of abuse, neglect, violence, and exploitation to the appropriate child protection service providers.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Educo has been mandated by its core values, policy and procedures to be transparently accountable to children and other stakeholders for our work and its consequences. In line with this, in this project we will apply a set of accountability tools and techniques to those we will work with and for (children, parents and other stakeholders). A space will be created for them to express their opinion on the project, its activities and its impact, and for Educo to use the feedback to continuously improve. The national Suggestions, Complaints and Compliments (SCC) committee in the country office will develop and introduce the SCC mechanism to receive and respond to SCCs and adjust feedback regularly. After consultation with the participants, a proper feedback mechanism will be installed in the CPMs, relevant spaces and partner offices. Educo and project partners will have a common agenda at project meetings, training/workshops and other events, to receive questions regarding SCCs and respond accordingly.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Educo has a PESHA policy, which is mandatory for all employees, volunteers, partner staff, and vendors. To ensure staff uses their power responsibly and upholds the principles set by codes of conduct when engaging the affected communities, in particular any forms of sexual abuse and exploitation (SEA), Educo has established the Suggestions Complaint and Commendation (SCC) system to receive feedback and ensure all PSEA cases are based on the policy. A PSEA focal point will be appointed and trained to effectively manage, refer, and report any cases related to SEA in the camps. Educo is an active member of the PESA network. And for sustainability, Educo will empower the community groups, with a focus on adolescents' girls and youths, to empower and enhance their capabilities in managing protection to have access to positive alternatives for coping and reduce negative coping mechanisms in both the host and camp communities. Educo adheres to a zero-tolerance policy on sexual harassment, gender-based violence and abuse of authority within the organization.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Mainstreaming disaster risk reduction Educo and partners should continue to undertake concrete and innovative protections and sensitive preparedness interventions to mitigate the risk of landslides, cyclones, and floods, particularly in areas where refugees are unable to evacuate to safe areas. Contingency planning is essential in advance of seasonal events such as monsoon preparedness, cyclones, and potential outbreaks of diseases. Both Educo and partners need to establish stand-by/roster systems for key staff during the weekend and extended holyday periods, during the cyclone and monsoon seasons, as well as any kind of emergency. In addition, the project will have close collaboration with disaster management committees both at camps and host. The project will obey the instructions of those DMCs. Besides, project will increase awareness among the target groups regarding human or climate-related protection issues. It will also help School Management Committees (SMCc) and CBPCs incorporate these factors into their plans of action.
