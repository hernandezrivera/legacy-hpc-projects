Project Summary

The project seeks to provide immediate life saving food support and agricultural inputs that will enable vulnerable households affected by the humanitarian crisis to withstand the effects of the food security and livelihood shocks as well as put them back on the road to recovery. Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), returnees, and host community members will be targeted to complement World Vision's relief and recovery activities in South and East Darfur, Blue Nile, and South Kordofan states. Emergency food support will be provided in the form of food basket vouchers. Voucher programming is preferred where applicable to reduce logistical processes and to give the beneficiaries the ability to choose food commodities of their own choice with a basket designed to meet the nutritional and energy requirements of households.

1 - Needs

Food security continues to decline as a result of economic challenges and humanitarian partners estimate that about 12.7 million people – 28 per cent of the population – will need humanitarian assistance throughout 2021. This is 3.4 million people more compared to 2020 or an increase of 37 per cent in the year. Of the 12.7 million people in need, 2.3 million are boys, 3.2 million girls, 3 million men, and 4.2 million women. 2.5 million people in need are IDPs, 688.5 thousand returnees, 1.2 refugees, and 8.4 vulnerable residents. People with disabilities constitute 1.9 million of the total number of the people in need.Overall, this is the highest number of people in need in Sudan over the past decade. This situation is driven by an economic crisis, exacerbated by COVID-19 containment measures, protracted internal displacement that is yet to find durable solutions, unprecedented flooding in 2020, disease outbreaks, and more than 1.1 million refugees and asylum seekers hosted by Sudan. Of the 12.7 million people in need, about 8.7 million need emergency assistance for life-threatening needs related to critical physical and mental well-being issues. The Food Security and Livelihoods sector has 7.6 million people in need. According to the 2020 HNO, South Darfur state has some of the highest numbers of people facing critical issues related to living standards, based on indicators including food security, as well as access to basic services . The Integrated Phase Classification of 2020 estimates that between June and September 2020, 9.6 million people (21% of the total population) are experienced Crisis or worse levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and above) and are in need of urgent action. 2.2 million people are facing an Emergency acute food insecurity situation (IPC Phase 4) and around 7.4 million people are facing Crisis acute food insecurity(IPC Phase 3), while nearly 15.9 million people are estimated to be in Stress (IPC Phase 2). This dire situation is compounded by massive price increases in the prices of food commodities that make up the standard food basket in the country given that as much as 70% of households in South Darfur rely on purchases for their food needs at some point during the year . The WFP market monitor for August 2020 indicates that the price of the local food basket increased by 17.3% compared to the previous month. These increases are eroding the purchasing power of households and are a major contributor to low food accessibility and high levels of food insecurity. The situation is made worse by the disruption in the food supply chain both nationally and locally as a result of the necessary measures, including the lockdown, adopted by the nation to reduce the spread of COVID-19.There are 2.5 million internally displaced people (IDPs) in Sudan. Most of them are in Darfur, South Kordofan, and Blue Nile, which were the epicenters of conflict over the past 17 years. These are also areas where about 52 percent of people in need are concentrated. This means that almost half of the people in need are in non-conflict central and eastern parts of the country, whereas in 2018, 74 per cent of people in need were in Darfur, South Kordofan and Blue Nile. Over the past three years, the economic crisis and other exacerbating factors have made about 4.5 million people in central and eastern Sudan vulnerable and in need of humanitarian assistance.Of the 1.1 million refugees in Sudan, about 75 per cent (814,000 people) are from South Sudan. Khartoum and White Nile states host two-thirds of all South Sudanese refugees in the country, with Khartoum having the highest number amongst all states.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project is going to address the environmental issues by considering the local environmental context of the targeted area, and a continuous assessment for potential environmental impacts will be carried throughout the project life. The project is also going to do mitigation measures by compensating for negative impacts (if any), and enhance environmental benefits in the project by practicing some measurements, including replacement of fired bricks with Soil-Stabilized-Blocks (SSB) or Cement-Stabilized-Blocks (CSB). • Locally available material is recommended provided that it is not impacting negatively on the environment. • Include environmental awareness sessions in projects, and support solid waste management schemes • Reduce packaging material and discourage use of plastic bags to reduce the amount of waste generated. • Promote alternatives such as Fuel-Efficient Stoves to reduce use of wood, solar panels for lighting and pumps. Include fuel efficient cooking techniques (e.g. pre-soaking beans, sheltering cooking fires, etc.) in training. • Raise awareness on environmental protection and conservation • Reduce wood consumption by supplying iron pipes and using environmentally friendly material for constructions and rehabilitation; and promoting alternatives such as Fuel Efficient Stoves, and fuel efficient cooking techniques. Consider also alternative energy sources such as solar panels for lighting and pumps. • Enhance environmental benefits in the project by tree planting.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will both be mainstreamed into FSL activities. The project will ensure that each activity does not expose the most vulnerable groups including women and children to protection risks. Community consultation will be conducted throughout all the project cycle. Community feedback and complaint mechanism will be set up. The feedback mechanism will ensure anonymity and confidentiality to protect the reporting person. In cases of GBV or child abuse cases, support including referral to other service providers will be provided and the survivor will be ensured protection to prevent exposure from further harm. To the extent possible, community discussions will be held separately between women and men to create safe space for everybody to participate equally. Discussions for children will always be held separately from the ones for adults. Project will ensure inclusion of most vulnerable groups within the target communities in close coordination and collaboration with key stakeholders. The safety of beneficiaries, other project stakeholders and WV staffs is not negotiable and the project will ensure implementation activities will not put beneficiaries, WV staffs or project stakeholders on security risks. Activities for children will be done during the day with the present of caregivers or other responsible adults from the communities. While project will prioritize female and child-headed families to participate in project activities or benefit from the assistance, it will also ensure such inclusion does not create another type of exclusion. Discussion will be held to inform communities on beneficiaries’ selection criteria. The discussion will involve different groups within the communities including women and children. To adhere to Humanitarian Accountability Standard, feedback mechanism will be set up using suggestion boxes or other means preferred by the communities. Program staff will use their current safeguarding and accountability standards and policies. Orientation to new staffs will include Code of Conduct and safeguarding principle to prevent exploitation of beneficiaries.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

All project staff have built and maintain channels for constant communication with affected communities. The project will pay special attention to the needs of vulnerable populations and take into account the different layers of vulnerabilities and coping strategies within a community and within specific vulnerable groups (men, women, age, education, socio-economic status, disabilities, etc.) For distribution of food and inputs, the project will ensure that information regarding the distribution is available in public places, distribution points are not too far away or difficult to reach, and that beneficiaries receive information related to the distribution with enough time to prepare for it. Necessary information includes (where distribution will take place, when distribution will happen, what will be distributed, who is entitled to receive the inputs, etc.). The project has a robust complaints and feedback mechanism in place that ensures that feedback and complaints received from beneficiaries are dealt with and that a timely response is given to the complainant.
