Project Summary

The protracted conflict in the east of Ukraine continues to affect the standard of living of citizens. The shelling of settlements located near the contact line led to the closure of enterprises, social institutions and medical centers. People in rural areas have limited access to various services. The closure or absence of medical centers in re-mote and sparsely populated rural locations forces people to postpone visits to primary health care. Poor transport infrastructure is a significant barrier to health care. Residents of villages and towns located near the demarcation line are forced to rent private vehicles (both shared and unshared) in or-der to cope with the lack of public transport and be able to visit the nearest medical facility. The out-flow of specialists provoked the absence of narrowly specialized doctors even in hospitals of regional and city significance, and the cost of specific diagnostic services, such as ultrasound, MRI, colposcopy, mammography, laboratory diagnostics, etc., makes them unavailable for most residents of remote areas. One of the alternative solutions to this problem is the launch of mobile diagnostic medical centers that help solve many health problems associated with improving the availability and quality of medical care for rural residents. As part of the proposed project, it is planned to purchase and launch a mobile medical center for the most remote and sparsely populated locations without FAP. The medical complex will be equipped with gynecological, diagnostic and laboratory equipment. Since a visit to a gynecologist is often associated with various psychological barriers for women, reinforced by the transport inaccessibility of a specialist, the presence of a gynecologist in a mobile center will help to maintain the reproductive health of rural residents. The accessibility of various types of diagnostics will allow early detection and prevention of various diseases, which will also positively affect the results of treatment. It is foreseen that the medical center will operate for 10 months, 5 days a week, receiving at least 40 patients daily. The estimated minimum target result of the project is 7800 people who have gained access to medical services. First of all, assistance will be directed to beneficiaries of advanced age who, due to their limited mobility and need for financial resources, are forced to refuse medical examinations and ad-equate treatment. Second priority target group - women of reproductive age and teenage girls

1 - Needs

The outflow of medical specialists during the military conflict provoked a deterioration in access to medical care. The problem is particularly acute in remote locations. For example, pos. Sedovo in Novoazovskiy district, the main place of summer recreation for people living in NGCA, is characterized by the absence of a medical worker. The same situation has developed in Kominternovo (Novoazovskiy district), Spartak, Vesele and Kruta Balka (Yasinovatsky district), Novaya Laspa (Telmanovskiy district), Signalnoe (Marinskyi district) and many other sparsely populated areas. The ongoing hostilities and mine pollution have led to the isolation of thousands of people. In many villages along the contact line, isolated men, women, and children have enormous difficulties accessing timely medical care, transportation, employment, and psychological assistance. Often these services are intermittent, and sometimes completely unavailable. The conflict in eastern Ukraine has a special impact on older people. According to the Population estimation for 2020 HRP Planning, out of 1564.9 thousand people in need of assistance living in the 20-km zone from the contact line in Donetsk oblast (NGCA), 413.3 thousand (26%) are elderly. These beneficiaries face serious difficulties accessing basic services due to their limited mobility. They are also more vulnerable to abuse and lack of custody, have special medical and nutritional needs, and are most economically vulnerable. Lack of livelihoods and income is another cause of concern for people of working age who have been affected by the conflict, as they often have to make an impossible choice between buying food and paying for medical services. Thus, joint humanitarian actions, primarily vital medical assistance should be provided to those who needed it most.Gender and age analysis indicates that the needs for this type of assistance for both gender and age groups are almost the same. However, the intervention will take into account a greater number of women, as one of the goals of the project is to maintain the reproductive health of women, adolescent girls and elderly due to their low mobility and special needs for medical care.Gender analysis will be an integral part of project activities during all stages of project development and implementation. For this, sex- and age-disaggregated data (SADD) and vulnerability criteria will be taken into account. All events will adhere to the principles of equal access and offer appropriate solutions to people with special needs. To measure the affectiveness and appropriateness of the assistance provided, all project activities will be regularly monitored and evaluated. The inclusion of the local population in the project desin will significantly strengthen gender and age equality in supported communities. A survey of beneficiaries will be used to analyze the relevance of humanitarian assistance provided. Gender and age considerations will also be taken into account in the monitoring process to provide equal opportunities for women and men and different age groups.In addition, the Foundation will apply the practice of interaction through social media. Persons affected by the conflict (men, women, boys and girls) will have equal access to feedback mechanisms and will have an opportunity provide feedback on the assistance received. All activities will be carried out in compliance with the basic humanitarian standards of quality and accountability to the affected population. The above measures and approaches will allow local communities to participate in key decisions at all stages of the program: evaluation, analysis, planning, implementation, monitoring and review. To this end, communicative approaches will be used, including the existence of a transparent and constant two-way communication, through which information on the community risks, needs and opportunities will be collected.
