Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) aims to continue access for children affected by the displacement crisis to safe, protective, inclusive and age appropriate learning and development opportunities in the camps and host communities under Ukhia and Teknaf of Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh through maintaining and upgrading Learning Centers (LCs), Community Based Learning Facilities (known as home based learning spaces) and Cross Sectoral Facilities (known as Girl Friendly Spaces) for early learning and basic education, enhancing knowledge of parents and caregivers on how to support their children’s learning, development, and well-being, and improving the quality of teaching through continuous teaching-related support. Inclusive aspects of programming including: gender-sensitive, child-sensitive, psychosocial support, disability-sensitive as well as integration between sectors including child protection will be mainstreamed in all activities to ensure that different needs of girls and boys will be responded to, and they will equitably benefit from the interventions. Special attention will be paid in capacity building of staff on key teaching and Social Emotional Learning (SEL) issues. All the learning facilities will provide with gender segregated WASH points while physical infrastructure, materials and methodologies will be strengthened to ensure access and equity for children living with disabilities. Being a sector co-lead, SC will continue to play a key role in advocacy, increased quality and sustainability of education services for the displaced Rohingya in Bangladesh. In adapting to COVID-19, SC plans to continue the caregiver-led education modalities, explore alternative pathways to accelerated learning and remedial learning and advocate for inclusion of education as an essential service and the permission of internet data in the camps so that the ICT/remote learning activities can be introduced.

1 - Identified Needs

49% of individuals aged 3-24 were reported to have attended a learning center (LCs) run by an NGO or the government for at least 4 days a week in the 30 days before LCs closed due to the COVID-19 outbreak. 27% of households reported loss or diminished access to education as an impact of the COVID-19 outbreak. Pre-existing gender bias for boys’ education is likely to be exacerbating with new schooling arrangements. Lack of learning materials and guidance from teachers are appeared as challenges to continue the learning (J-MSNA 2020, Refugees). Save the Children’s Rapid Needs Assessment (10-17 May 2020) states that there should have alternative mechanisms to continue educational activities for the children of the Rohingya community that can be the peer education and also distance education by using community Radio. The project identifies the needs of education for adolescent boys and girls, and aims to continue the community-based learning facilities and peer education programme in line with the data reflected in PIN/Gap Analysis from Education Sector. There is need to maintain current infrastructures and ensure current programming continues and expands with the scale up of Myanmar Curriculum. Existing LCs are at risk of landslides and flooding caused by the annual monsoon and cyclone season. Due to restriction of permanent or semi-permanent structures, temporary constructions are made with bamboos and tarpaulins, most LCs are already in need of renovation and this is predicted to incur significantly during the recurrent monsoon and cyclone season (April-October). Land scarcity is a key issue severely hampering the response, and requires innovative approaches like double storied facilities, home based facilities and peer education programmes to meet the vast education needs. Adolescent girls are much more disadvantaged in their access to education due to social norms, which restrict their mobility after puberty, safety concerns and widespread occurrence of child marriage and teenage pregnancies. Access to education is only meaningful if the programmes offer quality teaching and learning. Delivering quality education for Rohingya children is hugely challenging due to a number of constraints of technical, contextual, COVID-19 and political nature. The recruitment of sufficiently educated Rohingya learning facilitators remains a challenge. The situation is further complicated by the low levels of teacher competencies with the majority either under-qualified or unqualified, with insufficient ability in basic child-centered methodologies as well as poor subject knowledge, leading to weak learning outcomes. Given these challenges, learning outcomes are unlikely to improve without provision of continuous quality Teacher Professional Development (TPD) in order to support the teaching and learning process. In order to ensure that children's learning continues through alternative education modalities, there is a critical need for technology-based solutions, COVID-19 health and preventive messages, as well as mental health and psychosocial support activities. There is a need to convert or construct two-storied LCs as per government approvals to accommodate more children, considering the ones promoted to the upper grades. There is a need to strengthen engagement with parents, caregivers and communities in order to achieve high enrollment and attendance rates in learning facilities. There are limited capacities in local education authorities and educators in crisis-sensitive planning, monitoring and management of the education system. The current context of Cox’s Bazar requires strengthening of system for information that produce more useful, practical, timely and high level evidence about educational processes and outcomes.

2 - Activities or outputs

(a) Access to Education: Repair, maintain and upgrade 100 learning centers, 126 community-based learning facilities (CBLF) and 9 cross sectoral facilities, including gender segregated Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) facilities; Construct two-storied learning centers reach more children in needs; Support 15,498 girls and boys with learning services; Distribute learning, hygiene (considering COVID-19) and relevant kits to 15,498 students; Create provision of reading materials in the learning hubs through small libraries; Ensure continuity of learning during COVID-19 restrictions (such as, caregiver-led approach, digital contents for remote learning etc.); Transition from the existing Learning Competency Framework Approach (LCFA) to the Myanmar Curriculum with necessary remedial supports aligned with Education Sector’s guidelines; Provide necessary remedial and accelerated learning programme support(b) Quality of Education: Conduct perception survey and end-line assessments to measure children's learning progress and socio-emotional learning outcomes; Provide incentives to Rohingya and Bangladeshi teachers/facilitators; Train teachers/facilitators on psychosocial support, psychological first aid, socio-emotional learning, child safeguarding, and child protection, and establish referral mechanisms; Organize COVID-19 specific training for teachers (e.g. COVID-19-related health and hygiene messages); Train teachers/facilitators on learner-centered pedagogy and content for ECCD and basic education; Train teachers on Myanmar Curriculum with necessary remedial support aligning to Sector’s plans and guidelines; Distribute teaching aids/materials; Conduct teacher observation/support visits to each learning facility and provide individualized feedback and support; Facilitate teachers’ learning circles to promote peer learning, sharing and support; Conduct monthly meeting with implementing partner to support coaches’ ongoing reflection, learning and development; Advocate for internet access in the camps through coordinating with government and Education Sector to ensure equivalency between materials taught in Cox’s Bazar and Rakhine and support accreditation(c) Community Engagement and Participation: Strengthen Community Education Committees (CECs); Train CEC-members on access to education for all and how to encourage and support students’ enrollment and participation in the learning facilities; Conduct orientation sessions for parents and caregivers to raise awareness about issues relating to health, hygiene, nutrition, positive discipline, the benefits of girls’ education and the importance of education for children with disabilities; Conduct training sessions to increase awareness among parents and caregivers on the importance of self-care and well-being, and how to best support children as they learn to read and foster their love of reading; Facilitate school development plans and follow up, build capacity of school management committees (SMCs) and school teachers, provide financial supports to para teachers, and develop and distribute teaching materials in government primary schools from host communities(d) System Strengthening: Develop existing e-primary, e-monitoring system, training manual/guidelines with primary education authorities; Develop capacity of sector members, education officials, teachers and other stakeholders; Pilot low cost EdTech solutions to improve the overall education response
