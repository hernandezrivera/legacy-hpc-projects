Project Summary

The proposed project will increase child protection activities that will work to consolidate efforts that ensure services reach the most deserving and vulnerable children’s groups. This project is aligned with the UNICEF child Protection strategy note and will contribute to the overall goal of “2020, children are protected from all forms of abuse, neglect and exploitation. It targets the most vulnerable in society including UASC and at-risk ones and the strategies proposed will not only mitigate risks, but will also work towards resilience building, and sustainability through investing in community structures CBCPCs knowledge building. ARC has expertise in protection and currently leads the Protection Sub Regional Cluster in Sool and Burao of Somaliland with close coordination of UNHCR and NDRA and chairs the GBV Working Group Sub Cluster in Kismayo and co-chair the regional protection cluster in Jubbaland in collaboration with MOWGFA AND UNHCR in hosting all protection meetings with the Ministry in Jubbaland. ARC have strong programmes, community acceptance and accessibility to the proposed locations. The intervention will also include activities for adolescents between the age of 14 and 17 years old in order to address the unique needs of this group. Despite adolescence being the most important stage for children in terms of making critical choices in life, interventions available for this group remain limited across Somalia. As a result, early parenthood, dropping out from school, recruitment into armed groups and making un-informed choices in life are predominant challenges. 1,200 IDP children (650 girls; 550 boys) drawn from the above IDP camps will benefit from psychosocial and life skill development activities at safe spaces. 360 children will benefit from case management; 500 adolescents (300 females; 200 male) identified from the above IDP camps will benefit from mentorship programs. 10,000 IDP children (6,400 girls; 3,600 boys) from the above IDP camps will benefit from improved child protection environment as a result of improved awareness on child protection, monitoring, reporting and referrals among the wider community in their camps. 15,000 adult IDPs will benefit from awareness campaigns and sensitization initiatives on child protection. child protection networks created/enhanced across the locations of intervention. 20 child protection service providers including INGOs, local NGOs, formal schools, Qur’anic centers and Maternal and Child Health Clinics workers will be trained.

External stakeholder interference in beneﬁciary selection: Protection concerns stemming from displacement a. Under this proposed project, disability inclusion will be fostered under all activities, 15% of the target project beneficiaries will be people with limited mobility including children. during assessments people with limited mobility will be included and visited into their residentials to acquire the accurate need and priories for children and people with limited mobilities. The community based child protection networks and volunteers selection will be included disables to factor in inclusion and proactive participation. ARC will train community leaders, opinion leaders, parents and influencers on rights of children with limited mobility and provide advocacy iniatives for inclusion at all levels of activities. ARC will build contractive feedback machanism for people with limited movement to raise their complains, challenges and needs and a train focal point will handle the hotline and address matters urgently. ARC have Kuja Kuja feedback machanism that is suitable for every individual to participate project custonmers feedback to strengthen the implementation and service delivery.ARC will select beneficiaries based on their needs and will consider disability to all activities and 15% of the targeted population will be people with limited mobility, a defined selection criterion with disability inclusion will be developed in consultation with community-based targeting committees and leaders. While need influences the decision to select beneficiaries, ARC will give consideration to groups, who remain highly vulnerable in crisis situations, such as female headed households, who have no extended social support, child-headed households, persons with disabilities, elderly and marginalized and minority groups who have no major income source or at risk to violations. Disability beneficiary involvement will be enhanced through meaningful disability inclusion and engagement process that allows participation of the different disable groups and the committees will be included formal and informal local authorities who are responsible of the people with limited mobility in the selection of beneficiaries. The process of the target beneficiary’s selection will give priority to communities to identify their most vulnerable households through locally established community-based targeting committees who work hand in hand with well-respected clan elders and influential leaders to enhance local ownership and accountability practices at local level. Due to the increased concern of inclusion and exclusion errors, the ARC will carry out joint verification with Community based committees to affirm the beneficiaries selected indeed meet the agreed criteria and the agenda of disability inclusion.
