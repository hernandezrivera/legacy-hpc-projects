Needs

Since 25 August 2017, targeted violence and human rights violations against the Rohingya population in Rakhine State, Myanmar have forced 655,500 people to seek refuge in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh. Currently, the fastest growing refugee crisis in the world, the daily environment for recent arrivals, long-term refugees and host communities is beset with risk. The refugee populations are living in unsanitary overcrowded conditions and are facing a number of unique and challenging risks including accessing food and income. According to the WFP REVA Assessment (Dec 2017), it is estimated that ""80 percent of the overall refugee population are highly to entirely relying on life- saving assistance."" The clear majority of new arrivals have no source of income and have spent all their savings traveling across the border and establishing a shelter. Most of the new arrivals do not have cooking utensils, money, access to firewood or space to cook – this after access to cash was the most frequently reported problem (RI-IRC Assessment report Oct 2017). In addition, Oxfam's Rapid Protection, Food Security and Market Assessment (Nov 2017) found a high demand amongst refugees to earn income, yet restrictions for Rohingya populations, particularly women and girls (due to lack of clothing and need to uphold the purdah) to do so in displacement. Up to 90% of new arrivals are only eating one meal a day, and do not have a sufficiently diverse food intake and are primarily eating rice with salt. This lack of dietary diversity significantly increases the risk for malnutrition and anemia. The RI-IRC report also corroborates stating that over ¾ of the population relies on coping strategies with 90% reporting less nutritious food, 69% less food and 68% adults eating less. While there is no gender segregated data, women and girls are more likely to consume less food in scarcity conditions due to prevalent gender norms, the Protection, Food Security and Market Assessment also confirmed that women are reducing their food intake in order to reduce the number of times using the latrines. Market access restrictions due to a lack of information, fear of getting lost in camps and upholding of purdah for women has lead to poor food consumption. The protracted displacement is leading to Rohingya populations who are unable to make income for themselves, depleting assets and resources and adopting increasing dangerous and risky coping strategies to meet critical needs. With the Rohingya population now reaching one third of the total population (ACAPS Host Communities Review - Thematic Report Jan 2018) in Teknaf and Ukhia Upazilas, the Bangladeshi host community is feeling the economic strain on already limited resources created by the rapid increase in population. On average, for host communities, one in three people live below the poverty line and 17 per cent live below the extreme poverty line. According to the REVA, 38% of host community households in Ukhia and Teknaf are food insecure with female headed host community households more vulnerable than male headed households. Only one in three women in the host community have access to a minimum diversified diet (REVA Dec 207) and in relation to cultural dynamics have fewer economic opportunities or access to income generating activities. In order to respond to high malnutrition rates and a desire amongst affected populations to choose more diversified foods and suppot local markets, Oxfam will continue to implement an electronic voucher system to allow extremely vulnerable food insecure households to access fresh food on a monthly basis through local vendors in Pan Bazar,Thenkhali.

Activities or outputs

• Through continuing it’s provision of Fresh food assistance via electronic vouchers, Oxfam will continue to complement ongoing WFP and Partners General Food Distribution. In order to respond to persistently high malnutrition rates amongst and a desire amongst affected populations to choose more diversified foods, Oxfam proposes to continue to implement a voucher system to allow extremely vulnerable food insecure households to access fresh food on a monthly basis through local vendors in Ukhia Upazila. Through engaging local vendors in a paper based voucher system, Oxfam proposes to increase household food security and dietary diversity thfrough offering beneficiaries a choice of fresh food items to purchase on a monthly basis, complimenting ongoing activities by the WFP. The project aims to cover 25,000 households across Camps 3,4, 4 extension, 12, 18, 19. Oxfam has coordinated with the WFP and FSS throughout inception and start-up to ensure prices and items are in line with the WFP e-voucher program. The project will only target areas already receiving the General food distribution (GFD) basket of rice, cereals, oil and salt. Monitoring data and lessons learned will be shared with the WFP-led Food Security Sector. Oxfam will coordinate a phase out and transition onto SCOPE as this system comes in, in all camps. The project started in January 2018 and transitioned from paper to electronic vouchers in December 2018. • Working with local partners, Oxfam will continue to reach vulnerable lower socio-economic communities and refugee settlements through Cash for Work activities tied to longer-term disaster resilient assets and structures to help mitigate future disasters. Oxfam will also work with local partners and vocational training centres to deliver Cash for Training (CfT) modules to teach technical and vocational skills related to Cash for Work DRR activities, plus topics on women’s empowerment, gender equality and governance. CfT will also be utilised to teach communities ways to mitigate seasonal hazards and how to physically build community assets prioritised under the Cash for Work schemes. • Oxfam is also supporting the formation of youth and women-led groups at the community level and planning inhouse consultation with government and donors for working with host community for support and service provision. Early assessment findings indicate a need for income generating activities specific to female headed households with an interest in enterprise. Oxfam will promote female entrepreneurship as a way to build resilience and address long term livelihood needs. Oxfam will work with local CBOs to provide vocational training packages, facilitate access to financial institutions and link the local businesses to markets. A cash grant, furthermore will recognise care work as work under a Cash for Care Work scheme.• Oxfam will continue to work with local partners to identify female-headed households in target communities and help them to start-up their own businesses through income-generating activities. These activities will also provide linkages between women in the camp and their host community. • Based on the findings of its Sustainable Value Chain Assessment (Teknaf, 2018), Oxfam will explore opportunities to support specific value chains to build greater market linkages and better business practices for small holder producers.• The project will link vulnerable women and men from the refugee and host community populations to understand the Governance structures in relation to DRR and economic programmes and services. • • To prepare for and respond to seasonal hazards such as flooding, cyclones, winterization or monsoon rains which may impact refugee and Host communities ability to access to markets and cause damage to homes or food stocks, Oxfam will continue to have standing emergency response and preparedness initiatives in place including the provision of emergency food vouchers of ready to eat food
