Project Summary

This project proposals targets 4000 children of age 2 - 14 including boys and girls for quality literacy, numeracy, environmental sciences, social sciences, health and hygiene, safety/security and protection. It lays serious emphasis on 1000 adolescents and young adults boys and girls of 14-24 years training in vocational training to generate income as quick impact projects. Serious considerations is given to about 300 teenage mothers as victims of the crisis where they will be trained and empowered. These will by creating 25 Child Protection and Social Learning Centers and 12 Vocational Centers in 9 villages accommodated in community spaces and churches. 150 Volunteers teachers and trainers will be recruited and trained in child protection tips and on EiE principles. 9 Community Education Advisory Committees in emergency situations will be created in the various villages. These committees will be trained on community emergency response, child protection, safety and security tips. They will also help to formulate cultural child protection policies in their various villages.
The project seeks to create a Special Education In Emergency Board (SEEB) to design a quality learning programme for the child at various levels, produce manuals and will train the volunteers on the usage. The proposal also intend to distribute didactic and learning materials to the centers and the children. Volunteers trainer will be motivated and children will be motivated for 2 months with one school refreshing meal to boost enrolment.
Giving the vulnerability rate of children especially the young girls and boys in Njikwa, this proposal will be putting deep concern on GBV and SEA education, WASH related lessons, civic responsibility and the fear of God. Special centers will be created for the Muslim Mborolo children to meet their specificity in 4 central zones and their needs supplied accordingly.
Given that education in emergency is at the heart of humanitarian action as a basic need of children caught in emergencies, it remains the fundamental right of children. Therefore, this project will be of great relief for the communities in Njikwa for the various reasons;
First, education provides immediate physical, psychosocial and cognitive protection in the course of a protracted crises. Out-of-school children are more vulnerable and exposed to protection risks, eg. about 300 teenage mothers in 3 years, GBV, child labour and there estimated 200 children connected to NSAG in Njikwa.
Second, education is a major contributor to individual, community and societal resilience. The only industry in Njikwa is Education as their backbone. Therefore access to safe, quality education combined with the provision of lifesaving skills, social and emotional learning, and psychosocial support, can help children and adolescents cope with, and recover from crisis. The skills and knowledge learned in education enhances access to opportunities for the next generation, breaking cycles of vulnerability to shocks.
In our preliminary need assessment, education was prioritized by communities, parents and children themselves to build their future, even in the most challenging humanitarian situations. That is why Community education in NW during this crisis started in Njikwa. In line with the principle of accountability, humanitarian responders should listen to the self-identified needs of the affected by crisis and respond accordingly.
Humanitarian responders are uniquely placed to meet education needs in crisis situations, with the agility and capacity to reach children that development actors cannot provide, especially as the crisis is protracting children have missed three education cycle. Therefore a consideration to this project as their top need will very much appreciated.
The last reason is that much ground work has been done in the communities for the acceptability of the project by IVSPD.

Besoins / Needs

The protracted crisis in NW-SW Regions of Cameroon has plagued the country with thousands of school dropout and the rural areas are the most affected as no formation education is going on. Schools institutions and premises are either destroyed or deserted, and some hosting armed groups. Teachers and children in formal learning centres are under serious threats of life or ransoms for daring to go to school. As a consequence, children in rural areas have missed three school cycles with devastating impact on the children especially adolescents and young adults in local communities. Njikwa Sub Division is highly hit especially Ngwo and Oshie Villages where most public and social services have been shut down. Njikwa had been calm and at peace until the killing of the Police Commander at Police Post in Oshie on a matter that was totally unconnected with the crisis (eye witness confession) which provoked the burning down of Oshie Market and other private home on the May 4, 2018. Now in Njikwa there are no schools, no healthcare services, no administrative services, no market, churches occasionally shut down. The main and biggest market in the sub division which serves not just the sub division but also parts of Ngie, Widikum-Menka, and Akwaya sub divisions was burnt down. The biggest district hospital in Momo Division which is in Ngwo - Njikwa, the Health Centre in Oshie and other health posts in the various villages were shut down and all the doctors and nurses fled. All the schools in Njikwa - 7 secondary/technical/SAR-SM and 28 primary are closed and teachers fled affecting over 7 000 children who dropped out of school and over 3000 deprived from the opportunity to start schooling. 2 secondary schools burnt down and one badly damaged and looted. 68 homes were burnt down affecting 80 families directly who lost basic needs - shelters, clothing and bedding. 51 shops were burnt affecting 51 direct families as the only source of supply of their needs. About 1000 houses have been damaged and looted causing over 3000 people to desert them. The central administrative area is abandoned to the military with no other inhabitant. From our preliminary inquiries, about 300 school dropped out girls have been impregnated as teenage mothers excluding the over 60 cases of abortions. Some are now unwillingly married while a good number remain single teenage mothers. Unofficial verbal report from the nurses in Njikwa says HIV/AIDS is most prevalent in Njikwa than any other sub division in Momo. Now, promiscuity is at its peak especially situation of GBV and SEA. Njikwa has no electricity, no radio or TV signals that come from Cameroon, the MTN telephone network that was newly installed was damaged and looted in course of the war and so no telephone network, and broken water pipelines with no repairs since all technicians fled and other water points left in thick bushes. The common playing grounds for children had been the many large school fields which are all deserted and no go zones. There are no community recreational or friendly spaces and no social services for the children. The Mborolo community in Njikwa is highly affected and threatened for their purported involvement in the crisis supporting the government that caused much pains and loses for the people. As a consequence trust was lost and the Mborolos are being chased away and have suffered a great losses of their livelihood - cattle and sheep and some have been killed in the course of the crisis. Their children are unable to attend the few learning centres we established. The need to create theirs is an urgent necessity. From our findings, apart from our organisation that is doing some humanitarian work in Njikwa, no other organisation has visited the communities for any assistance. Given these varying problems in the sub division, we hope that this project proposal will bring some relief to the traumatizing population especially the children.
