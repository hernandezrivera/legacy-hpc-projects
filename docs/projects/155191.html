Project Summary

To ensure continuous improvement of the quality of services and camp coordination in a cyclone and flood-prone area of Bangladesh, in 2019 there remains a need to implement site improvement activities, construct mitigation measures and build the resilience of both camp and host communities. This means that CAID will focus on approaches which embraces resilience building aspects, mainstream protection and incorporate a robust accountability system towards all stakeholders. Also, to increase social cohesion with the host community’s preparedness activities are included creating greater awareness towards potential hazardous events. CAID as site management agency continues to work with the GoB through the designated Camp in charge (CiC) to offer overall administrative services in camps 14 and 15 and will conduct regular assessment of services, and with UNO with regards to cyclone preparedness and DRR awareness-raising campaigns in host communities. In host communities, the aim is to provide training, conduct community awareness-raising campaigns and mock drills, as most community shelters are in place or will be retrofitted with WB or IOM funds. The proposed activities complement those efforts with additional local capacity building with regards to cyclones and floods. Furthermore, there is still a need to enhance access to information on available essential services; incorporating referral pathways for protection with a focus Child Protection, GBV and people living with the disability. Besides that CAID acknowledges that the season at higher risk of cyclone is expected to increase GBV risks for refugees.
Safe and dignified living remains a priority and this also includes regular infrastructure maintenance and equitable access to essential services for refugees which will be featured with complementary IOM funding. Strengthened community engagement and consultation practice is another focus for CAID; next to provision of opportunities to get local community actively involved in decision making which is linked with the block development committees that comprises of a 50% male 50% female ratio and are an important platform to advocate and discuss changing needs of communities. This is an essential part of programme planning, because as the response progresses, the needs and priorities of the target population differ.
In order to support site improvements efforts the following small-scale community-based mitigation measures are proposed; upgrading and fortification of community spaces in case of emergencies such as monsoon, flooding to be converted into temporary shelters, slope protection and small scale retention walls to reduce the impact of environmental degradation and mudslides during monsoon season, improvement of staircases, pathways, bamboo bridges and clearance of drainage to ensure that camps remain accessible by foot also by the most vulnerable groups
In terms of Site Management activities; the focus will be on awareness raising activities for camp setting and host communities- these are supplemented by IOM SM activities which are complementary and enhancement of site information management services, improvement of accountability and referral systems, coordination meetings. This comprises of various activities such as service monitoring to generate referral reports, updating camp-level contact/3W list, Conduct FGD and physical site verification to produce assessment reports and maps, train Safety Volunteers on Fire Safety Plan and emergency response. Lastly, regular follow up and improvement of the accountability system in camp 14 and camp 15 will continue to be linked to protection cases. The existing complaints and feedback system will be strengthened to facilitate identification and tracking of protection cases, ensuring that the Site Management Protection team addresses the issues adhering to Protection Sector standards and guidelines.
In total 82,665 people will be reached in camp 14, camp 15 and host communities.

Needs

As the response progresses, there is need for effective camp management and camp coordination to ensure improvement in the quality of the services provided by the different agencies and to embed activities that foster social cohesion to support the negatively affected households near the camps. Government services for host communities are stretched very thin due to added pressure from the camps, leading to dissatisfaction inside the host community, therefore reducing tensions and striving for social cohesion programming is necessary. The site management agency continues to work with the GoB through the designated Camp in charge (CiC) to offer overall administrative services in camps 14 and 15 and regular assessment of services, and with UNO with regards to cyclone preparedness and DRR awareness raising campaigns in host communities. There is still the need to enhance access to information on available essential services; incorporating referral pathways for protection with a focus Child Protection, GBV and people living with disability. Furthermore, the season at higher risk of cyclone is expected to increase GBV risks for refugees. As some of the GBV service points, community centers might be affected by the flood and landslides. The project will address the need for continued coordination efforts to ensure the protection actors and the different sectors provide information on people living with disability to the different sectors and design services in a way that ensure equitable access to services for all. In host communities that are affected the focus will be on training, community awareness raising campaigns and mock drills, most structures are in place or will be retrofitted with WB or IOM funds. This complements those efforts with additional local capacity building with regards to natural hazards.Safe and dignified living remains a priority and this also includes regular infrastructure maintenance and equitable access to essential services for refugees. Strengthened community engagement and consultation practice; involvement of the local community in decision making. This is an essential part of programme planning, because as the response progresses, the needs and priorities of the target population change, and regular monitoring and assessment helps to identify new gaps as well as how these can be addressed.Additionally, Adolescent girls’ and boys’ participation at the child-friendly spaces is limited, because some are prevented from participating by their guardians. Hence, continuous community engagement and focus group discussions with adolescents and parents are ongoing to address the issues. Cox's Bazar and the nearby area fall in the High-Risk Zone for tropical cyclone. On average the impact of cyclone means that 70% shelter in refugee camps is damaged, 20% destroyed; approximately 523388 people will be affected, approximately 11,300 households shelters destroyed or damaged, 221,000 people in need (shelters destroyed or severely damaged), estimated 24,000 newly arrived pregnant women half will require emergency medical care for difficult pregnancies. Up to 70% of latrines and other WASH facilities will be destroyed or damaged and desludging sites will overflow, creating a toxic mix of water and sewage over most of the camp. In camp 14 and camp 15 due to the hilly environment and overcrowding access to health centre points are disrupted, debris will cut off inside camps. There is a need for increasing and activating more CPPs to reach the target of 100 per camp instead of 60 to cover all blocks and be equipped with medical evacuation tools, search and rescue, first aid and enabled with support of the SMEP clear debris and restore communication lines.
