Project Summary

The project’s main goal is to scale up safe and equitable access to education for crisis affected children and youth, through the implementation of multiple activities that improve both the soft and hard components of the educational system in conflict affected areas, including rehabilitation of schools, provision of materials and supplies, conducting awareness campaigns and enhancing the capacity of teachers and education personnel.

Needs

The protracted crisis in Syria, now in its eighth year, has forced approximately 2.1 million children to leave school and has put a further 1.3 million students at risk of dropping out. Over 5.8 million school-aged children (including more than 100,000 Palestine refugee children) and over 120,000 teachers are in need of education assistance inside Syria, 61 per cent of them are in acute and immediate need.Hostilities have resulted in over one in three schools being damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for shelter and other purposes, the education system in Syria is overburdened and overstretched. This situation has negatively affected the quality of education for all children, and children in different areas across the country are constantly facing challenges in accessing education.Volatile circumstances, safety and security issues and limited access for humanitarian partners has prevented them from providing educational support to the people in need, which leaves the community with extremely limited access to quality educational services.Public services are unable to support the children and adolescents in urgent need of education, school infrastructure is severely damaged, the number of returnee teachers and educational staff lack experience in teaching the affected students. The parents are afraid to send children back to schools due to the fear of explosive hazards and unsafe routes to schools. Barriers between humanitarian aid and development and the security situation in the area fail to respond to the challenges in education presented by the protracted crisis.Considering, that education is a vital investment in the future of children, the rehabilitation and improvement of schooling services in conflict-affected areas in Syria is essential to the country’s recovery.However, Governorates in the northeast like Deir-ez-Zor and southwest like Daraa are facing multiple challenges, in particular in Education.Schools are destroyed and this destruction has resulted in the extreme decrease in the number of adequate schools and created a real crisis in the classrooms’ accommodation capacity. Where functioning educational facilities do exist, the most commonly mentioned barriers to accessing education are poor conditions of buildings, customs relating to early marriage and children labor. The large majority of children are at risk of dropping out of school due to besiegement in Deir-ez-Zor, and multiple displacements. Most of the children in Deir-ez-Zor governorate haven’t received education in schools for almost 5 years. 25% of children and adolescents aren’t attending classes due to the poor condition of educational facilities and percentages vary between 29% and 50% for early marriage situations in Deir-ez-Zor.Estimated attendance rates were significantly lower for IDP children than for host community children across all areas. The need for children to work and help their family was the top barrier facing out-of-school children in Dar'a governorate. Early marriage was also cited as a common barrier to attendance for older children. The most urgent needs reported in Dar'a governorate were ensuring safety and security for chil¬dren, teachers, in addition to providing an adequate space for children to receive their education.The displacement and flight of educational staff has led to severe lack of qualified educational personnel capable of teaching the affected children in both governorates.The project will support the cooperation between humanitarian actors, donors and local authorities to act in favor of the affected children guarantee their basic right of continuous access to education and reduce the risk of children’s exposure to gender-based violence and sexual exploitation.Rebuild Syria Reconstruction Program will ensure that educational services won’t be suspended when the humanitarian response is finalized but improved access to education services allows a healthy development of the students.

Implementation of activities

Despite the de-escalation of hostilities in areas across the country, the needs remain massive and humanitarian partners must increase and harmonize efforts in supporting the children and adolescents to receive the education they deserve.RSRP's planned activities for implementation are in line with the education sector’s response strategy which adheres to the needs based approach, identification and prioritization of the people in need within the framework of the HNO, down to sub-district level and the following strategic objectives which have been set in consultation and coordination with the Syrian national and local authorities as well as the communities and education cluster:1- Scaling up access for education to the crisis affected children.2- Enhancing the quality of formal and non-formal education within a protective environment3- Strengthening capacity of the education system to deliver a timely, coordinated and evidence- based education response.In order to achieve these strategic objective RSRP will be focusing on:1- Conducting a back to learning campaign, to raise awareness in communities about the necessity of education, this campaign includes: conducting Communication for Development (C4D) recreational activities, conducting focus group discussions, distributing flyers, banners and poster and disseminating mobile text messages.2- Improving education for children who have missed years of formal education, dropped-out or have no access to educational facilities through Remedial Classes (A support program targeting children enrolled in formal schools at risk of dropping out or falling behind, also referred to as “Child Club” in public schools).3- Capacity building for teachers through trainings and orientation sessions (Basic Psycho-social support Active learning, Life skills, ICDL,…) 4- Improving the capacity of local staff involved in educational projects through trainings and orientation sessions for example: Basic Psycho-social support.5- 6- Distributing educational and recreational supplies and materials such as (recreational kits, school in a box, math teaching kits, science teaching kits, school bags, and other kits that could improve awareness among children and adolescents…).7- Minor rehabilitation of damaged schools (Infrastructure as a word is not inclusive to WASH facilities, infrastructure can include damaged and visible structural elements, damaged architectural elements in classroom and halls, in addition to WASH facilities), to allow the students to return. All that is mentioned above will be translated in assessments, surveys, monitoring and evaluation reports and photos with results shared with donors, partners and relevant clusters to participate in the humanitarian coordination process.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: Lack of safety and security (Attacks on schools, attacks on children, attacks on educational personnel).Likelihood: LowMitigating measures: - Plan and design projects in consultation with national and local authorities as well as the communities.- Ensure relevant authorities are aware of/ engaged with the project and commit to ensuring basic safety/access for its implementation.- Ensure all staff and educational personnel working in insecure locations are trained in safety and security.- Inform and update parents and children of the risks.- Take into consideration parents’ concerns in Back to Learning Campaigns.Monitoring and Evaluation:- Frequent updates on the safety and security situation in the area of intervention.- A strong reporting mechanism that enables staff to communicate problems happening on site, this is reflected through periodic progress reports, internal reports, issue reports, photo documentation and active engagement of all field staff in RSRP reporting mechanism ,which a is transferred and reviewed at higher levels to be solvedResources required:- Experienced field staff, experienced monitoring, evaluation and reporting staff, strong strategic planning and a robust reporting system.Protection Risk 2: Discrimination leading to certain minority groups or communities to be excluded from the education opportunities.Likelihood: MediumMitigating measures: - Focus Group Discussions held with different community groups and leaders to assess their needs and vulnerabilities.- A fair and just selection criteria when targeting communities and schools.Monitoring and Evaluation:- Ensure that clear transparent beneficiary selection criteria is set to targets diverse communities and include minorities.RSRP plans to identify the most vulnerable groups in need of educational services through beneficiary selection criteria, based on the identified vulnerable groups in the 2019 HRP. We take into consideration gender issues, displacement status, health issues and other aspects that respect the affected people’s cultural background in the location of intervention.For education projects in particular, our main goal and aim is to target children and adolescents with proper educational services. However, we understand that in order to achieve this, educational personnel, parents and all people involved in a child’s education are entitled to receive support.For that, age and gender diversity is always included within the beneficiary selection criteria also (Children, adolescents and adults). - Establish a complaints mechanism that enables people to communicate openly about project related problems. RSRP has established many channels of communication to allow people and staff to give feedback, suggestions or complaints regarding the implementation of projects and issues in the field in a way they feel is most suitable and comfortable.We currently receive feedback about our projects either from a dedicated mobile number for this matter, or through a complaints box set in the site where the project is taking place.Staff are also trained prior any fieldwork on how to resolve urgent problems immediately and how to correctly report it.In addition to all the aforementioned feedback methods, we ensure that focus group discussions and interviews with key personnel in the targeted communities are arranged and conducted by field staff to stay updated regarding the educational needs in the area.Required resources: Monitoring and Evaluation team, strong program team.Protection Risk 3: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers.Likelihood: LowMitigating measures: Inform and train humanitarian workers on all aspects related to protection.Obligate humanitarian workers to adhere to a code of conduct.Establish an efficient and effective complaints mechanism.M/E: Conduct satisfaction surveys to ensure beneficiaries are content with the interventionRequired Resources: M/E team.
