Project Summary

NP aims through this project to enhance the protection of civilians and resilience of communities in South Sudan. Civilians throughout the country have been severely affected by the existing humanitarian crisis resulting from recurrent violence, insecurity, and natural disasters as well as the influx of refugees and returnees from Sudan as a result of the Sudan conflict. NP will directly implement activities utilizing a participatory model that seeks to further build on local protection capacities, social cohesion, and resilience as well as prevent and respond to pertinent protection concerns so as to alleviate suffering and increase the protection and security of affected and vulnerable communities. All project activities will be designed to best respond to the immediate security needs of beneficiaries and support the resilience, adaptive capacity, and recovery of communities from violent conflict, insecurity, and environmental shocks utilizing NP’s Unarmed Civilian Protection (UCP) methodology. The project’s three programming components centers upon: 1) General Protection 2) GBV and 3) Child protection.
Through this project, NP will utilize a community-based participatory approach in order to prevent and respond to protection needs, including gender-based violence (GBV) and Child Protection (CP) concerns. All project activities will be designed based on NP’s methodology of Unarmed Civilian Protection (UCP) to respond to the immediate protection needs of affected populations as well as support the resilience, adaptive capacity and recovery of communities from violent conflict and insecurity. Project activities are implemented in both static locations, as well as through mobile response missions. NP also recognizes the specific and unique needs of women, men, boys, girls, elderly and Persons with Disability (PwD). As such, NP has made use of community feedback/information in the design of the proposed project. During project
implementation, concerns of all community members will be captured and disaggregated by age, sex and PwD, and will be constantly reviewed to adapt the project.

1. Needs

The massive influx of returnees and refugees from Sudan to South Sudan since April 2023 as result of the conflict in Sudan in addition to cumulative and compounding effects of years of conflict, subnational violence, food insecurity, the climate crisis and public health challenges in South Sudan, all continue to exacerbate a worsening humanitarian situation in South Sudan. In 2023 alone, an estimated 9.4 million people in South Sudan — including 2.2 million women, 4.9 million children and 337,000 refugees — are projected to need humanitarian assistance and protection services, reflecting 76 per cent of the population. Two thirds of the population are affected by the precarious food security situation, making South Sudan one of the world’s worst food insecurity crises. This is with exclusion of the returnees and refugees from Sudan. Covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks as well as other household-level stressors, like gender-based violence (GBV) and poor WASH services, continue to be among the key drivers of humanitarian needs in South Sudan. In addition, the cumulative impact of recurrent flooding has contributed to the destruction of, and damage to water facilities, increasing the likelihood of water-borne diseases and intensifying the existing vulnerabilities of the affected people, which include high poverty rates, widespread displacement and limited access to basic services such as health, nutrition and education.Women and girls continue to be exposed to risks when carrying out their routine activities to access their basic needs, which are further exacerbated by flooding that limits livelihood opportunities (e.g., collection of firewood and water). Increased cases of sexual violence against, and harassment of, women and girls were also reported, along with the risks of abduction, killings, revenge killings and threats from armed forces. Most verified conflict-related sexual violence cases occurred in Southern Unity State in 2022. Children continue to be at risk of recruitment into local defense groups and other forms of abuse, including abduction and possible trafficking.These and other protection concerns, particularly against conflict and flood-affected populations, children, women, women- and child-headed households, elderly, and people with disabilities, continue to exacerbate the protection needs for South Sudanese people. In areas where flood-displaced populations are not able to return to their areas of origin due to ongoing conflict in their communities, civilians face exacerbated vulnerability to protection concerns. In addition, perceived disenfranchisement of youth in humanitarian assistance compounded by high levels of unemployment and lack of livelihood opportunities, has spurred violent strikes/protests by youth against humanitarians, hindering access and indicating the risk for further violence perpetrated by youth in the absence of holistic and inclusive approaches to safety, security, peace, and development. Gender inequalities in South Sudan contributes to the lack of participation of women and girls and exacerbates vulnerabilities. At the household and community levels, inequalities have a mutually reinforcing relationship to levels of structural violence, preventing females from accessing opportunities and exposing them to violence. Inequalities also assist in the normalization of violence against women/girls. Further, gender inequalities contribute to women/girls being used as commodities, with CEFM being used a coping mechanism to multidimensional poverty. GBV is also perpetrated against males with militarization of masculine behaviors and rigid norms on men’s roles reinforcing hegemonic masculinities that contribute to normalization and cycles of violence. In the context of an increment in localized violence in the country, women and girls continued to be deliberately targeted. Gender inequalities sit within the wider divisive social structure exacerbated social and cultural norms.

2. Project activities

NP’s activities will focus on the efforts to enhance the protection and resilience of the communities in South Sudan. Under PC 2 – which is focusing on ensuring that human rights are upheld and promoted for the persons of concern by strengthening coordination, advancing centrality of protection among humanitarian actors to reduce protection risks, and addressing coordination gaps/issues through close dialogue with partners and effective protection monitoring to inform programming, advocacy, and response, NP will: 1) conduct protection mainstreaming activities reaching humanitarian partners and local community leaders and workers who serve the local communities in different ways to ensure that protection principles and standards are observed across all the activities; 2) facilitate awareness raising activities on GBV risks, available response services, and timely reporting for women, men, girls, and boys; 3) Facilitate and support the implementation of GBV prevention initiatives in communities; and 4) protection monitoring activities through community FGDs and KIIs in order to inform the humanitarian intervention. Under PC1 which is focusing on ensuring mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability, NP will t: 1) provide PSS first aid, recreational, developmental, and education support through child safe spaces to girls and boys, with a specific focus on the inclusion of children with disabilities; 3) conduct protection needs analysis and intervention activities to ensure that immediate needs are responded to and that needs are identified timely and shared with humanitarian partners to ensure context specific humanitarian intervention; 4) conduct awareness raising sessions in child protection for children and caregivers; and 5) identify and strengthen referral pathways as well as work with partners to establish new referral pathways were they are not existing. Under PC 3 focusing on promoting nexus to development and solutions as the transition between the delivery of emergency humanitarian assistance, systems building and the provision of long-term development assistance to meet peoples’ needs, mitigate risks and vulnerabilities, exacerbated by systemic and socially reinforced exclusion of women and girls and persons with disabilities, NP will : 1) facilitate general protection awareness raising activities to women, men, boys, and girls, with a focus on DRR, EWER, and community resilience; 2) facilitate awareness raising on child protection with a focus on reducing the suffering of children at risk or experiencing violence, abuse, exploitation, and neglect; 3) conduct activities establishing and strengthening community-based protection structures; 4) conduct training activities to strengthen the capacities of the local community structures in ensuring the protection of their communities; 5) Provide trainings on GBV guiding principles and response services to NGO staff and frontline workers to enable effective provision of immediate response support to survivors through a survivor-centered approach; 6) facilitate and support the implementation of GBV prevention initiatives in communities; Provide trainings, workshops, and mentoring for community mobilizers on GBV mitigation, preparedness and early warning; and 7) Refer women, men, girls, and boys to GBV response services. For protection and child protection activities, NP calculated its target number of beneficiaries with the assumption that average budget per beneficiary is 30 USD. This results in 12,309 people targeted under GP; 24,508 people under GBV; and 13,122 people targeted under CP. These targets are considered realistic based on NP's experience, and will be met by NP's 14 static teams and 2 mobile teams each having an adequate number of program and operational staff. Juba's office will provide guidance and support also.
