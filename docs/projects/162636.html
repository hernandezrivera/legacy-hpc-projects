Project Summary

Sustainable improvement of food security, nutrition and livelihoods through increased production, resilience and health services as an approach of integrated development in Central Darfur, Sudan” is a three-year project funded by the German Federal Ministry of Economic Cooperation and Development (BMZ) and Caritas Germany. The working title of this project is “Resilience Approaches to Integrated Development in Darfur” (RAID). Its overall goal is to sustainably increase food security and nutrition of 20,212 rural returnees and host households (HHs) in 15 selected villages of Mukjar and Um Dukhun localities of Central Darfur in line with Sustainable Development Goals 1, 2, 3, 5, 11 and 16. Catholic Relief Services (CRS) and the Trust Rehabilitation and Development Organization (TDO) partner on the RAID project across its strategic objectives and its cross-cutting issue to contribute to resilience among Central Darfurian households. The project focuses on the sectors of agriculture, microfinance, health and nutrition, and capacity strengthening.

1 - Needs

The communities targeted by the RAID project are primarily mixed returnee and host communities, with livelihood activities mainly based on mixed agro-pastoralism, winter cropping, and rearing animals. These livelihood activities have been disrupted due to long-term conflict between farmers and nomadic pastoralists around inequitable access to the shared resources - livestock migration routes, grazing land/pastures and scarce water resources – further reducing the capacity of communities to sustainably protect natural resources. Equally disrupted were access to basic social services, like local medical coverage, and the communities often have to travel long distances to receive quality care.

2 - Activities / Outputs

o The communities targeted by the RAID project are primarily mixed returnee and host communities, with livelihood activities mainly based on mixed agro-pastoralism, winter cropping, and rearing animals. These livelihood activities have been disrupted due to long-term conflict between farmers and nomadic pastoralists around inequitable access to the shared resources - livestock migration routes, grazing land/pastures and scarce water resources – further reducing the capacity of communities to sustainably protect natural resources. Among the major interventions of RAID under its SO1 is to support water infrastructure projects in the targeted areas. RAID will also provide key agricultural livelihoods inputs (seeds, tools, supplementary animal feeding kits) to support the reestablishment of normal production patterns, in addition to providing capacity strengthening for these producer groups to improve practices and techniques. Finally, the project will enhance access to community savings and credit practices, thereby generating start-up capital for community contributions to income generating activities. Under the second SO, the project identified the high level of moderate and acute malnutrition among pregnant and lactating women as well as children under 5. Especially in the targeted locations where access to regular medical services is low, extension activities under the community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) is vital. Among this approach, the practices of building capacity for community nutrition volunteers, state Ministry of Health staff, and local lead mothers will lead to the screening and active case referral to nutrition centers. In the meantime, the project will support the rehabilitation of nutrition centers at select health facilities in each of the two localities

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Due to the nature of its resilience programming, RAID is heavily focused on the environmental impact of its interventions. A core activity of the project is the promotion of reforestation, the creation of natural barriers, the provision of community shading, and the proliferation of community fruit trees through supports and inputs for community-based nurseries. To date the project has distributed for planting over 17,000 seedlings. Additionally, the project is currently in the early stage of organizing an environmental impact assessment as a precursor to the rehabilitation of community water infrastructures, with the intention of supporting 4 of these infrastructures to improve the quantity and use of water.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

o CRS maintains Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning Policies and Procedures to ensure that protection is mainstreamed through the project life. Data management is a core component of these policies, including the safe collection, keeping, and evaluation of data for our project participants. In addition, the project maintains a community feedback and response mechanism to ensure that the information flows both from and to project participants and targeted communities. The project includes perspectives during all evaluations respecting the sex, age, and other identities of project participants. RAID also partners with local authorities and community leaders for all activities, ensuring that community voices are represented in each of our activities.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

o The RAID project, like all of CRS programs, utilizes a list of MEAL Policies and Procedures to ensure compliance with CRS polices regarding the Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning core functions of implementing our programs. Among these procedures is the establishment of a community feedback and response mechanism. Communities are introduced to various channels through which they can provide complaints, feedback, suggestions, and positive acknowledgements to the project. The Senior Project Officer is the mechanism focal point, and this position has been oriented on the different categories of complaints, as well as how to treat and respond to the low-sensitivity information. For high-sensitivity information, the communication is elevated directly to the Country Representative. Responses are provided to the source of the feedback in a timely manner and catalogued in a confidential manner.
