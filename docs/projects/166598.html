Project Summary

The proposed action aims at mitigating the impact of COVID-19 on vulnerable Zimbabweans livelihoods and capacity to meet their basic food and nutrition requirements.
The action will follow a two-pronged approach to impact both sides of the food system. On the consumption side, the project will support both urban and rural acutely food insecure households through Cash and Voucher-based transfers. On the production side, smallholder farmers whose livelihoods have been affected by COVID-19 will be supported to restore their production through distribution of agricultural inputs (seeds and tools for both crop season and off-season gardening) and E-Extension services. In addition, Food Security and livelihoods intervention will be complemented by sensitiazation to good nutritional practices and caregiver-led screening of acute malnutrition.
This project aims at adapting ACF's current HRP action "Multisector emergency intervention for vulnerable Zimbabweans affected by the protracted drought and socioeconomic crisis" to the context of COVID-19. In particular, the following modifications are proposed:
- Ensuring all COVID-19 prevention measures as recommended by WHO, WFP and the FSL cluster are implemented across all interventions. This includes, but is not limited to, staggering distributions to abide by the 50 person gathering limits and the adaptation of distribution sites to enable physical distanciation.
- Procuring PPEs required for staff and beneficiaries, in line with WHO recommendations (in particular hand sanitizer, chlorine, non-surgical cloth masks.
- Switching, wherever possible, to remote implementation modalities: Interactive Voice Recording (IVR)-based monitoring and sensitisation activities, mobile money and/or e-voucher distribution, E-Extension through interactive radio and digital extension application, etc.
The action will contribute to both sector objectives:
• SO1: Saving lives through support to food access for acutely food insecure population, aimed at ensuring they are able to meet their basic food and nutrition requirements during severe seasonal shocks or other disruptions.requirements during severe seasonal shocks or other disruptions.
• SO2: Prevent further deterioration of living standards for acutely food insecure population, by providing emergency agriculture support aimed at ensuring they can achieve food security and resilience to repeated exposure to multiple shocks and stressors.

Plan fields

1 - Activities

For the urban areas : - UCT: to complement the WFP response where needs are uncovered. A feasibility study has been conducted and identified possible modalities in the fast evolving situation, considering the lack of liquidity, the limited capacity of the e-money service providers. The modalities being explored are e-mobile services, direct cash distribution or/and vouchers. The value will be based on the MFB updated in May.2020: 18.00 USD/individual - MPCT: For the most vulnerable HH with limited access to the land, MPCT is planned to help them to cover their basic needs (including non-food) beyond the lean season, since the country will still rely on grains import and the HHs would not have recovered their livelihood. The MPCT will help the targeted HHs to pay for school fees, medical care or treatment and food depending on their priorities. MPCT will be based on the 62% of MEB updated in May.2020 (18 USD/Individual) - Agricultural support: to help the HHs to cope with low crop season and to prepare themselves to next crop season through IK distribution or voucher system or fairs to access agricultural inputs for off-season agriculture (vegetable) and for the next season (maize, millet, sorghum). Voucher systems and fairs would have the advantage to link small farmers with suppliers (strengthening the market linkage). In addition, e-extension services will be provided. For both urban and rural areas: - Awareness raising for healthy behaviors (nutrition practices, food diversity, hygiene, etc.): diet diversity is still an issue in urban areas, level of stunting is close or beyond the high WHO threshold in some areas (Manicaland urban: 27.8% rural 34.8% - ZimVac ULA& RLA 2019), feeding practices for mothers and children are key for the future cognitive development of the children. Hygiene also is an issue especially in poor areas, which have high risk of waterborne diseases’ outbreaks. Through behavioral change strategy, awareness sessions (mass campaign, care groups, etc.) are to be implemented with gender and age-specific groups (men, women and youth) to overcome the barriers to healthy practices. - Mass screening of U5 children: although the level of GAM is not high, the prevalence between 2018 and 2019 increased by 40% between 2018 (2.5%) and 2019 (3.8%) According to UNICEF, 98,112 U5 children are suffering from acute malnutrition. In addition, health services are not properly functioning. Thus, the invention will conduct mass caregiver-led screening activities among UCT beneficiaries. - Protection and feedback mechanism: Mapping of protection actors for the referral system. The selection process of beneficiaries will be explained to non-eligible persons through community leaders. ACF and its partners will pay special attention to PSEA through the setup of measures protecting women and girls from risks of survival sex when accessing services. To ensure people’s participation and for accountability and learning purposes, ACF and its partners will set up ways to collect feedback from beneficiaries (via VC, a hotline) in order to mitigate risks of violence/women and child abuses.
