Project Summary

According to the Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) 2022, in northeast Nigeria, 5.0million people, including13% women and 70% children require life-saving and essential mental health services. This funding shall be deployed to provide emergency integrated information service with the aim of reducing health related mortality and morbidity. The overall objective of the project is to increase awareness on SRH care, communication and service uptake to conflict affected individuals in Kala/ Balge LGA of Borno State. Supporting conflict-affected individuals in accessing improved outpatient primary healthcare and comprehensive sexual and reproductive healthcare services. we hope to see an increased rate of awareness and service uptake amongst participants and communities we would engage. In northeast Nigeria, 5.0 million people including 0.65 million women and 3.5 million children are in need of lifesaving and essential health services, amongst which 2 million are women and girls of reproductive age. Borno State accounts for more than 76% of displacement over Northeast Nigeria, with more than 1.8 million internally displaced people (IDPs) as of July 2022 (IOM Mobility Tracking IDP and Returnee Atlas, July 2022). More than 4.2 million people in Borno State need assistance, 80% of them being women and children (Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview, 2022). The Health Resources and Service Availability Monitoring (He RAMs) assessment of 809 health facilities across 27LGAs in Borno, indicates that only 41% are fully functional, while up to 48% of the facilities are not functioning at all. Some 11% are partially functioning. In addition, 64% of the assessed facilities were either completely (31%) or partially (33%) damaged. Damage to infrastructure (60%) and lack of access due to security constraints (32%) were the main reasons for health services being out of action. Of the fully and partially functioning facilities, just over 25% have operational in-patient services, 20% have trauma services, and only 25% have essential medicines and other vital supplies. Access to health services is also hampered by high medical costs as reported by IDPs (75%), returnees (61%) and non-displaced households (80%). Although this information does not show specifically the disaggregated expenditures and funding gaps for SRH and Mental health services. This leaves little or no opportunities to exercise people’s sexual and reproductive health and rights or care for women’s reproductive health needs. While maternal mortality and morbidity is a common cause of death among women of reproductive age living in resource poor setting, having the extra stress of living outside their usual stable environment into desperate conditions camps makes delivering a child more difficult and potentially life threatening.

2 - Activities

The intervention proposed will be carried out jointly between two partners, which are CRUDAN/SKF. This intervention fits, in general, the HRP SO1 of 2022 which is “Affected people receive life-saving assistance to remedy and avert the most severe threats to life and health, in order to mitigate excess mortality and morbidity”. CRUDAN/SKF will advocate 50 key stakeholders (Religious leaders, traditional leaders, youth leaders, women leaders, government representatives, local security groups, and national security personnel) on SRH, cholera and mental health services, sensitize adolescent boys and girls of reproductive age and women including people living with disability (PWD). The following activities will be done.1.1 Consult with and agree with a partner on the use of a facility in Kala-Balge for counseling1.2 Consult with community stakeholders, parents, and caregivers and notify them of the availability of service in Kala-Balge1.3 Sensitize community stakeholders, parents, youth leaders, caregivers, heads of households, and adolescents on reporting ofmental health cases in Kala-balge.1.4 Conduct training for mental health for selected community leaders, partners, and community health monitors deployed to Kala-Balge LGAs1.5 Carry out Counseling and psychological evaluation for boys and girls reported or identified to have been unstable in the healthfacility1.6 Consult with partners and MHPSS WG for developed mental health key messages that will promote well-being1.7 Print developed mental health key messageActivity 2.2.1 Consult with the Risk Communication Pillar, the Health Sector, and the Borno State Ministry of Health and Nigeria on developedCholera prevention and control messages2.2 Consult with community, religious and traditional leaders on Cholera and SRH key messages developed2.3 Print Cholera prevention and control messages in collaboration with the Risk Communication Pillar, Borno State Ministry ofHealth, and community leaders.2.4 Engage SKF Risk Communication team in camps and host communities in Kala-Balge, for weekly and megaphone awarenessraising on the prevention and control of Cholera2.5 Distribute printed IEC materials (English, Hausa &amp; Kanuri) to target individuals in camps and host communities reached withawareness-raising outreaches in Kala-Balge, LGAs

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SKF and its partners will work to respect all protection principles through the adopted safeguarding policy and code of conduct that is linked to the survival centre for the implementation of this project. SKF and partners through their existing Monitoring Evaluation Accountability and Learning framework will ensure that there is progressive tracking, learning, collaboration, and adaptation through the synthesis of best practices. Mental health data will be collected weekly, compiled, and shared with the project team to provide a clear vision and visibility of the project indicators’ performance. Regarding the project’s performance, the project manager will compile the project’s performance indicators in SKF and partners project monitoring tool (PMT) on a monthly basis, with data that has been jointly validated with the MEAL team at the field locations (Base). These processes will generate feedback at all levels with regards to beneficiaries reached, thus providing the project manager with guidance on adapting activities, if need be, for the overall project’s quality and effectiveness improvements. Before the project starts, a project kick-off meeting will take place to analyse the areas of adjustment and discussion on the activity planning. The project kick-off meeting will provide an opportunity for the project team and the technical coordinators to review the data collection tools. Also, before activities will commence, a comprehensive security risk assessment will be done and this will be updated semi-annually to ensure the identification of new evolving threats and risks. SKF and its partner have established their operational base in Kala-bulge (Rann), SKF have been able to observe that there was strong community acceptance through the need analysis conducted and the sensitization and awareness carried in our previous self-support project. Also, SKF and partners will provide safe and equal access to inclusive and non-discriminatory services.
