Project Summary

"Gonoshasthaya Kendra, a national NGO specializing in comprehensive health services, has been actively involved in the response to the Rohingya crisis since 2017, supported by the GFFO through Malteser International. Following the mass influx of Rohingya refugees from Myanmar in 2017, the crisis has evolved from an emergency into a protracted situation with no clear timeline for repatriation. The Rohingya population continues to face numerous challenges, natural hazards, and daily health risks within overcrowded camps. Currently, over 960,000 refugees rely heavily on external aid, which strains resources and intensifies vulnerabilities for both refugees and host communities in Bangladesh. The proposed project aims to enhance the health and well-being of vulnerable populations within the Rohingya camps and host communities in Cox's Bazar. It will focus on sustaining two health posts in Camps 1E (UID-007) and 11 (UID-112), aligning with the Joint Response Plan Objective 1 to ensure equitable access to primary and secondary healthcare services. Additionally, the project supports JRP Objective 3 by promoting individual and community well-being through outreach activities conducted by community health workers, and Objective 2 by incorporating emergency preparedness and response measures.
The project is set to directly benefit 55,943 individuals, including 44,754 refugees, with 11,189 members of the host community gaining access to the health services provided by the two health posts UID-007 and UID-112 in Ukhiya.
Outcomes
1. Preventive Health Measures: Focused on reducing the incidence of communicable and non-communicable diseases through proactive health initiatives.
2. Curative Health Measures: Providing comprehensive primary healthcare services to enhance well-being and address health concerns
3. Emergency Response: Strengthening readiness and response capacity
Activities:
1. SRH Services: Experienced midwives will deliver SRH services, including antenatal and postnatal care, in line with the essential health service package.
2. Health and Hygiene Promotion: Over 46 community health workers will conduct health, hygiene, and nutrition education sessions through home visits and group activities in the community.
3. Adolescent SRHR: Adolescents, groups in the camps, will receive targeted sessions to improve their understanding of SRH rights, enabling them to make informed decisions.
4. Primary Healthcare Services: The project will sustain operations at the two health posts, ensuring the delivery of essential health services six days a week. Patients will receive consultations from qualified medical professionals, undergo necessary diagnostic tests, and access prescribed medications free of charge.
5. NCD: Establishing dedicated non-communicable disease (NCD) corners within the health facilities to provide screening and counseling services.
6. MHPSS: Offering medical and psychosocial support, including referral pathways for individuals facing protection issues, domestic violence, and PSEA concerns. Psychologists will provide individual and group counseling, with severe cases referred to secondary facilities.
7. Response to Domestic Violence: Recognizing the high rates of intimate partner violence, the health posts will provide medical and mental health support to survivors. This will include establishing referral pathways with GBV/protection partners to ensure comprehensive care.
8. EPR and Contingency Planning: The project will maintain stockpiles of essential relief items and conduct regular training and simulation exercises to enhance staff capacity for timely humanitarian response.
The project will emphasize gender-sensitive programming, ensuring that the specific needs of women and children, person with disability and elderly are prioritized in service delivery and outreach."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

"Nearly a million Rohingya refugees are living in 33 makeshift camps in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh, in conditions marked by extreme uncertainty and vulnerability. They face significant challenges in access to basic needs, including health, protection, while the crisis has also placed immense pressure on the host community in Cox's Bazar. Both refugees and host populations are susceptible to seasonal cyclones, monsoon floods, landslides, and recurring fires, adding to their vulnerabilities. Of the displaced population, 51% are female, 52% are children under 18, 4% are elderly, and 12% are persons with disabilities.Barriers to healthcare access in the camps still remarkable particularly for the most venerable groups. Women face numerous obstacles in obtaining SRH services, leading to poor reproductive health outcomes, high maternal mortality, and widespread misinformation around sexual and reproductive health. Adolescent girls are highly vulnerable to child marriage and poor mental health, while adolescent boys face risks of drug abuse, violence, and educational disruption. Elderly and persons with disabilities encounter significant challenges in obtaining regular medical care and rehabilitation services, with many unaware of NCD risks, leading to low engagement with preventive services.A recent Public Health Needs Assessment (PHNA) for 2024-25 highlighted the main health concerns in the camps, underscoring gaps in service accessibility and quality. It identified the top health issues affecting refugees as acute respiratory infections, skin diseases, diarrhea, hepatitis B & C, and gastroenteric conditions. Further findings revealed that 29% of pregnant women lacked prenatal care, 6% experienced complications during childbirth, and 19% of children were not fully vaccinated. In addition, 32% of respondents reported experiencing trauma or emotional distress. The PHNA report emphasized that urgent improvements are needed in NCD management, a key area given the high demand for NCD medications among camp residents. Persons with disabilities, the elderly, and pregnant and lactating women are the most underserved, with limited access to health facilities. Only 4% of respondents felt healthcare providers were adequately sensitive to gender-specific issues, and a significant portion of medical staff reported challenges in providing gender-sensitive care. Approximately 3% of households refrained from seeking healthcare due to gender-related concerns, highlighting a critical gap in service delivery for gender-sensitive health needs.The proposed project seeks to address these critical health needs through a comprehensive Primary Healthcare (PHC) approach. Key interventions should include SRH services for women, and adolescent girls; NCD management for the elderly and vulnerable groups; preventive and curative care for communicable diseases; and enhanced gender-sensitive programming to improve service access. In addition, outreach activities and health awareness sessions will target misconceptions and barriers to accessing healthcare, while collaboration with other humanitarian actors will strengthen the response to emerging health risks. This needs assessment underscores the pressing requirement for expanded primary healthcare services in the camps and host communities to address persistent and evolving health challenges. The project aligns with these needs by fostering resilience and improving health outcomes for refugees and host populations alike, ultimately contributing to a more sustainable and effective healthcare response in Cox's Bazar."

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

"Gonoshasthaya Kendra (GK) integrates protection mainstreaming into all its humanitarian activities to ensure safety, dignity, and equitable access for people at risk. This involves identifying who is vulnerable, understanding potential risks, and assessing the impact of GK’s interventions on these populations. To address specific protection needs, GK provides referral pathways to specialized services, helping individuals access support to prevent or recover from exploitation, abuse, and neglect.Key protection principles guide GK’s approach:1. Safety, Dignity, and Avoidance of Harm: GK prioritizes minimizing unintended negative effects of its interventions, thus reducing physical and psychosocial risks to affected populations.2. Meaningful Access: GK ensures assistance and services are accessible without barriers, paying special attention to those who may face greater challenges in accessing support. 3. Accountability: GK establishes mechanisms that allow affected communities to provide feedback, raise concerns, and make complaints, reinforcing transparency and trust.4. Participation and Empowerment: GK builds individual and community capacities, helping people to understand and assert their rights, especially in areas like health and personal well-being.5. By embedding these protection principles, GK’s programs not only target those most in need but also enhance human dignity and safety. GK’s commitment to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) includes active membership in the Cox’s Bazar PSEA network and a comprehensive PSEA policy. GK staff are trained to address PSEA concerns, reinforcing a safe, respectful, and inclusive environment within its operations"

2.3 - Age & Disability

"The project will ensure that at least 3-5% of the beneficiaries are persons with disabilities, in line with the commitment to inclusive health services. Age and disability considerations are integrated throughout all phases of the project cycle, from survey to evaluation, as reflected in the Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) plan. Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and checklists are in place to identify barriers and facilitators to accessing services. Our services are designed to support all target groups—regardless of ethnicity, status, disability, age, or gender—upholding the principle of equal treatment. Privacy during medical examinations is maintained in accordance with cultural norms. To reach individuals with limited mobility, the project includes mobile clinics, and emergency referrals are supported by a liaison to facilitate administrative access.Each of the three health facilities will be equipped with assistive devices and wheelchair ramps to improve access for persons with disabilities and the elderly. Trained staff will provide support to ensure that individuals with disabilities receive priority access to services.The community outreach component promotes equity and access through home visits, with staff sensitized to identify and prioritize persons with disabilities and the elderly. A mobile medical team will provide home-based care, and if a caregiver is unavailable to collect medicines from the health facility, volunteers will deliver them directly to the beneficiaries."

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"The project engages the community from design through implementation, incorporating feedback to refine strategies and crisis response. Regular consultations with community representatives, particularly leaders, are held at health posts to gather input that informs project actions. GK ensures transparency and accountability by providing clear information on roles, responsibilities, targeting criteria, and complaint processes.The community’s views on service quality and appropriateness are prioritized, with ongoing consultations to capture and respond to beneficiaries’ voices. Since 2017, GK has provided trusted health services in Cox's Bazar Rohingya camps, earning a positive reputation. Lessons from previous projects, community feedback, and local consultations will guide current efforts to meet community expectations.Community outreach is central, with mechanisms to continuously gather and address feedback from both the target and host communities. Quarterly meetings allow for open communication, and health centers will display service details and clinic manager contact numbers for emergencies or concerns."

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

"Gonoshasthaya Kendra (GK) is a committed member of the PSEA network, upholding a strict zero-tolerance policy toward sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). GK prohibits any form of SEA by its staff, associates, partners, or representatives involved in its operations. The organization is dedicated to safeguarding all communities it serves from SEA in all circumstances.GK’s PSEA policy defines prohibited behaviors and clarifies the roles and responsibilities of all staff, associates, and partners in preventing SEA. To ensure accountability, GK has established clear reporting and investigation processes, promoting a safe and responsive environment. Additionally, GK actively assesses PSEA risks and vulnerabilities within its programs and implements measures to mitigate these risks. Community awareness is a priority, with regular meetings and the distribution of Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials to inform communities about PSEA standards and reporting mechanisms. GK has a designated PSEA focal person who maintains ongoing contact with the PSEA network, ensuring alignment with sector standards and effective information sharing. A code of conduct is strictly enforced for all staff and partners, reinforcing GK’s commitment to preventing SEA and protecting community members’ rights and dignity."

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

"The project locations are frequently impacted by natural disasters such as cyclones, floods, fires, epidemics, and occasionally, violent conflicts. GK will coordinate closely with other humanitarian organizations through clusters and ad hoc working groups to enhance emergency response and prevent duplication of efforts. GK’s approach is adaptable, enabling flexible responses through various modalities, such as in-kind support or scaled-up service delivery, based on immediate needs and available resources in the local market.To ensure rapid and effective response, GK will maintain a contingency plan and standard operating procedures (SOPs), alongside specialized training for all staff to equip them with essential skills for emergency preparedness and response. This preparedness will support GK in reaching affected populations within 72 hours. Additionally, GK will pre-position critical relief items, including non-food items and hygiene kits, to ensure immediate availability and response capacity. Currently, GK is an active member of the Emergency Medical Team (EMT) across several camps and will continue this commitment in future projects, enhancing coordination and readiness for rapid deployment."

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

"Yes. The project will continue the Youth and Adolescent program which includes the youth aged 15 to 24 years, Adolescents are among the most vulnerable groups in the Rohingya camps, lacking adequate provision and access to relevant education. Adolescent girls face heightened risks of gender-based violence, including early marriage, abuse, and other forms of violence.Efforts will be made to provide assistance to youth adolescent boys and girls, aiming to enhance their awareness of issues related to sexual and reproductive health rights that significantly impact their lives. These efforts will involve educational sessions covering health and hygiene management, including menstrual hygiene, pubertal changes, and various other health and nutritional issues. Initially, we will establish groups in each camp and in Kutubdia, with 10-15 members in each group. Considering the cultural and social values within the Rohingya community, separate groups will be formed for boys and girls."
