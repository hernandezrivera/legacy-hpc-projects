Project Summary


Due to flooding and chronic lack of investment in WASH infrastructure, these areas continue to suffer from AWD -acute watery diarrhoea /Cholera and the risk factors for an epidemic persist in particularly critical areas such as Bay and Gedo. Between January and December 2019, 6,709 cases of AWD/Cholera were reported across the country, with Gedo and Bay reporting 1,012 and 453 cases respectively, of which at least one third were children under 5 years of age. In May 2020, the Ministry of Health of the South West State (MoH) reported 121 cases of AWD and 4 deaths in Bay Haw and Bay Regional hospitals. Three of the four water samples collected by the Ministry of Health, the Ministry of Energy and Water and the WASH Cluster of four sections of the city of Baidoa were found to be contaminated (UNOCHA Situation Report, June 2020).
According to Multi-sector joint assessment on Berdale IDP Influx Berdale, Bay Region, and South West State September 2020, the main water points in the area are shallow wells and boreholes according to the respondents. Most respondents (95 per cent) stated that they feel safe to collect water from these points and (95 per cent) stated that displaced people have access to these water points. Only 35 per cent of people said that they have containers for storing and fetching water. Previously, 74 per cent had confirmed having jerry cans but the families do not have storage materials. With regard to sanitation, 51 per cent families stated that there are latrines in the village but only 12 per cent of displaced people said that they have access to the latrines.
Faced with these high levels of vulnerability, responses remain limited and weak in terms of provision of funding due to focus on COVID-19 outbreak. It is therefore necessary to focus on immediate emergency planning for the most vulnerable people living in affected areas through life saving WASH interventions to ensure an effective response to the most vulnerable population groups. COOPI proposes to provide emergency water through vouchers in Baidoa and Dollow, desilt and ensure proper protection of hand dug shallow wells as well as establish and build capacity of community water management through O&M training with basic tools provided. COOPI will further construct communal latrines and conduct hygiene promotion campaigns in Dolow, Luuq, Baidoa, Hargeisa, Odweyne and Burco. COOPI will also ensure strong protection applied to reduce risk of violence against women and children, and men or other vulnerable groups (ageing and disability) during distribution of water storage containers and install solar lighting and locks in already existing gender sensitive latrines.
Due to ongoing-armed conflicts in Somalia estimates of People with Disability (PWD) is much higher that the global average of 15% which a significant part of the beneficiaries. COOPI will incorporate disability specific actions in the programming as follows;
1. COOPI will include people with disability during identification of beneficiaries for water vouchers and storage jerricans distribution as well as ensure accessibility to the commodities by providing extra support to their caregivers. In addition, awareness will be elevated amongst WASH staff on the need for and approaches to data collection on children with disabilities.
2. All WASH infrastructure will be designed and build to ensure is accessibility for instance; all communal latrines will include ramps and a dedicated cubicle with holding rails. Children with disability will be provided with cleanable chairs in the latrines.
3. The Water Management committees will include at least one member with disability to ensure consideration during decision making ragging operation and maintenance of infrastructure.
4. During Hygiene Promotion, WASH campaigns will be planned to ensure that out of school children, adolescents with disabilities have access to WASH information (including menstrual hygiene management for girls with disabilities)

Mitigation Measures

A. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services 1.COOPI WASH team will ensure that the hygiene kit size is appropriate for the beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them2.No facilities or distribution points will be placed near military installations or threats such as attacks stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Weapon bearers will not be present inside distribution points.3.COOPI will Identify central locations for distribution points - beneficiaries will not traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points.4.Distributions will take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. B. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities 1. Consultation will be done with women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific WASH requirements and preferences for location and design of WASH facilities2.Considering that most often women and children collect water, COOPI staff will talk to them directly about safety of the location of and routes to the water collection point3.Community and committee representatives will be engaged to play an active role in identifying solutions and the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response.4.Coordination will be done with civil society specialising in working with persons with impaired mobility or disabilities to help identify such individuals and use them as a resource to improve service delivery, train staff and for the referral of cases.4. COOPI WASH staff and committees will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) and all staff and members should be given protection mainstreaming training.
