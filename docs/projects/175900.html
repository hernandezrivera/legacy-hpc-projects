Project Summary

The project will be aligned to Health Cluster objectives, namely 1. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities; 2. Improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems; 3. Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly. It will complement the ongoing intervention in selected priority Counties, narrowing the health care provision gaps in the most remote sites and for the most neglected groups, proposing a range of different services provision modalities (static and mobile), expected to be able to overcome the accessibility issues affecting current health care coverage at all level of the health system and to respond to the essential needs of the target population, in particular of those people whose peculiar conditions (disabilities, mental health disorders, exposure to SGBV) makes them particularly vulnerable and their access to services more challenging. For this purpose, health related activities will be integrated with those pertaining to the protection sector, through the opening and sustain of reciprocal referral channels for people to be offered both clinical care and psychosocial support. In general, project impact will be maximized through a multisectoral approach. Nutrition and WASH will be given high attention, with the result of services being highly comprehensive, as addressing both health and nutrition conditions and the inter- related complications, and offering quality care, being informed by hygiene promotion and IPC principles, becoming the first setting for an effective prevention of the main infectious diseases. At this regard, the project will guarantee timely and effective prevention, containment and management of outbreakes (COVID19 but also EVD) as well as the main health related consequences of other emergencies, as flooding. Surveillance and early warning will be strenghten, with the engagement and coordination of various stakeholders. In general, the project will promote the proactive involvement of local communities and health authorities and this will facilitate the selection and implementation of the most responsive, feasible and acceptable intervention options, focusing on the most serious and urgent gaps, promoting better outcomes and ensuring the due accountability to beneficiaries. Local actors technical assistance and operational support will be one of the core strategies informing the proposed action, contributing to its effectiveness and to the quality of supported services and activities.

1 - Needs

LAKES STATE can be considered priority humanitarian setting. It has been heavily affected by flooding, 5 over 8 Counties have been targeted by emergency interventions under the related SSHF Reserve Allocation. The adverse environment has exacerbated its already poor situation, due to years of wide spread insecurity affecting essential services provision/access and resulting in high IDPs burden over hosting families (5.1% of these latter are in these conditions, versus 3.9% national average), whose resources are limited, because of raids/lootings and environmental factors. This is even more concerning when considering that, due to the continuous fightings, 72% households are headed by a woman, more exposed to violence when dealing with domestic tasks and likely to be food insecure. Lakes State is amongst those with the worst Food Security level; more than 50% of its population in IPC Phase 3 or above. About half of U5 children thought to be acute malnourished, as well as almost the 40% of women in child bearing age. They are highly vulnerable to diseases (54% morbidity amongst U5), but inadequate HFs number, distribution, conditions (staff number and skills, equipment, supplies), with insufficient funding for sustaining routine services, fail in offering the due preventive and curative functions. Lakes health indicators are low. ANC4 30% (ANC1 85%), institutional delivery 27% (63% of which SBA), curative consultation rate 0.75 (1.2 in U5, 2.3 when considering children attended by community based health providers). Malaria/Diarrhea/Pneumonia are U5 main diseases, favored by poor WASH: 96% households practice open defecation, 84% do not have soap. In such conditions and given the low EPI coverage (63% Measles in U1), outbreaks have always been quite common (measles and cholera as the most recent) and some clusters of COVID19 cases have been reported in Lakes as well, calling for State wide improvement of both prevention and surveillance sectors. In spite of what mentioned above, nutrition services integration at HFs is not complete yet; ie. growth monitoring is not part of routine OPD package, resulting in missed opportunities for SAM/MAM identification, positive health/nutrition practices are poorly adopted (exclusive breastfeeding). GREATER MUNDRI (Western Equatoria) HFs have been reactivated after long internal conflict, but their full functionality is penalized by the impact looting and damages had on their infrastructures and staff reluctance. Security situation improved, but mobile services resumption has been affected by flooding. Services demand is acceptable (87% ANC1, 2.4 U5 OPD UR), but utilization still low (42% ANC4), as well as institutional deliveries coverage (42%), with 78% of them concentrated in Hospital/PHCCs (little assistance in the periphery). Community based health system has been recently launched, too early to confirm its effectiveness in boosting HFs coverage and in enhancing County health response. Food security is at crisis stage. Fragmented vertical nutrition interventions hamper malnutrition response (alert level). In general, health system appears as not really effective, not even in preventing/containing outbreaks. COVID19/EVD preparedness and response measures in place missed the due continuity. In ALL SITES, crises lead new needs health system has to deal with: mental disorders and disabilities. The HMIS does not capture all their data, understanding the size of the problem site by site and tailor the intervention to account for their necessities as well requires complementary data collection and monitoring activities. In Western Equatoria 18.6% households have at least one member belonging to this group. Few information on SGBV, mainly affecting women (97% of cases); although related data are now captured in the HMIS, victims do not speak up. OCHA estimated 50% women in each household undergo a violence episode within a year. [2020DHIS2, IPCSouthSudanDecember2020, FSNMRRound25,HNO 2021 OCHA]

2 - Project activities

Activities align to Health Cluster orientations, functional to achieve its sectorial objectives. They will be implemented in targeted Counties’ selected PHC Facilities/Hospital and villages, agreed with Health Cluster, after needs assessment and discussion with beneficiaries. Priority will be where existing services are not sufficient to cope with care demand, as neglected by common funding mechanisms or overloaded because of IDPs/outbreak/flooding/violence. To “reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities” (SO1), focus will be on outbreak prevention, investigation/detection, effective/integrated response, with a multidisciplinary supervision/coordination mechanism for stakeholders’ concerted actions. EPI coverage will be boosted, with static and outreach routine immunization and mass campaigns. UNICEF/WHO will be advocated for regular antigens provision and cold chain maintenance. Link with WASH Cluster will promote IPC at community/facility level; HFs will establish IPC committees and use score cards for regular monitoring, people will be sensitized on environmental health. Authorities’ surveillance tasks’ continuity/consistency will be sustained (wide distribution of related GL/tools, surveillance officers training/mentoring), strenghtening the existing IDSR system. In specific hot spot and particular circumstances (EVD or COVID19 alert), suspected/confirmed cases tracking and first line management will be done, as per National Task Force orientations. To “improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems” (SO2), the essential preventive/curative actions as per Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services will be provided at selected PHCU/PHCCs/Hospitals and through mobile health team and community/boma health workers. Mobile/community based services will complement/replace static ones when HFs functioning/accessibility will be compromised by insecurity/adverse environment and to respond to outbreak/displacements. The project will link with the existing Boma Health Workers network, expanding its geographic scope and sustaining its service delivery performance. County wide free of charge ambulance service for obstetric/pediatric emergencies will be guarantee. PHCCs/Hospitals will be supported to provide BEmONC/CEmONC. All this will be realized by: hiring/rewarding/training/supervising health staff and BHWs/CHWs, with emphasis on prevention, diagnosis, treatment of the most common diseases (as Malaria) and on safe motherhood, including skilled attendance to delivery; procuring/distributing essential drugs and supplies buffer stock, complementing what provided in kind by other actors (as LLTNs, to be integrated into ANC/EPI and mass distributed at hot spots); replacing old/ looted equipment and repairing damaged infrastructures to restore enabling conditions for services provision; ensuring transport means availability/running. CHD will conduct regular supportive supervisions to all targeted sites. To “increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly” (SO3), protection (age/gender sensitive) approach will be streamed, ensuring due attention to these vulnerable groups, respecting their dignity and exigences (with special care for adolescents). They will be promptly identified and carefully handled, given adequate clinical attendance and referred to specialized centers for psychosocial/legal support and follow up. Network with these facilities will be strengthen. The quality of all implemented actions will be guarantee by CUAMM technical assistance and operational support on health services delivery/management to CHD, while services responsiveness and acceptability will be promoted engaging beneficiaries in all project phases, including M&E through community feedback mechanism.

3 - Project indicators

The project will be monitored through the indicators proposed by the Health Cluster. The related data will be collected as much as possible through the existing Health Management Information System (DHIS2) and through already adopted integrative tools (as the Washington Group Short Set of Questions (WGSS)), avoiding creating parallel channels and increasing health staff workload but contributing to health system own monitoring and evaluation enhancement. At this regard, CUAMM Technical Assistance to targeted Counties authorities, staff and stakeholders will concern data collection/reporting/analysis as well, to promote prompt evidence based decision making process. Supportive supervisions will be functional also to verify the completeness and reliability of available data. For those indicators not found in the DHIS2, CUAMM and Cluster will agree on the specific tools to be used, choosing an user friendly design to facilitate field team and health staff work. When possible, CUAMM will encourage data disaggregation by age and gender, for being able indentifying eventual cases of unbalanced access and utilization of supported services. The proposed activities are expected to contribute to data production for each of the suggested indicators. Further, some of them implies the set up or strenghten of process for the collection of complementary information, both quantitative and qualitative, as those related to community feedback mechanism (interviews, questionnaire…)

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CUAMM has recently approved and endorsed its own PSEA policy. The related communication strategy consists in integrating PSEA information dissemination into the ongoing activities aimed to sensitize people on Gender related matters, including Gender Equality, Gender Empowerment, GBV. These activities includes health staff and authorities training, community meetings, IEC materials production and events…activities which are part of CUAMM on going intervention and which will be included in the project as well, to reach a wider audience. Integrating PSEA information in this framework is expected to facilitate its understanding and to promote its acceptability. CUAMM PSEA policy is completed by a toolkit aimed to assist the field team in this process

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CUAMM and CHD are already working to ensure the above mentioned Health Committees gender balanced and inclusive composition and attendance, making them as participatory spaces women and other vulnerable individuals can be part of, an empowerment driver, offering public engagement opportunity, usually very limited for certain groups, and making these latter aware of their own value, to be enhanced through a virtuous circle of increasing participation. Similarly, Review Meetings and community based activities will be open to CSO representing women, PWD, and other vulnerable groups. Boma Health Workers or other community based health providers will bring services as much close as possible to beneficiaries, minimizing access constraints faced by who is not in condition to freely move because of physica, social and economic dependance, as women and PWD and offering these people the opportunity to provide their feedback and inputs. The anonimous Clients Satisfaction Survey has been designed and it is administered in a way that makes it accessible to all kind of patients, indipendently on their education background (usually poor for the most vulnerable and neglected as women and PWD) and without requiring time wasting and further movements

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information about the project will be driven through existing community consultation mechanism, to avoid overlapping and confusion and to contribute to strenghten them even further. The same mechanism will be used to get feedback and complaints, to make easier for the beneficiaries, already used to them, to give their contribute. CUAMM is used to maintain a constant dialogue with communities representatives, from those belonging to the Health Facilities related Health Committees to those from community based associations, which are invited to take part to the Quarterly Review Meetings held at County level and which ensure a wider perspective on community needs and expectations and can report on services responsiveness and acceptability. The recently introduced Client Satisfaction Survey offers the opportunity to get feedback on provided services by the direct beneficiaries. It is administered at service delivery site, meaning it is easily accessible, it is filled by literate volunteers so that it can be administered to any patient, without discrimination based on education level (which would have left behind the most vulnerable) and it has been piloted to confirm all questions being easy to understand and to be answered by people with different background. Finally, CUAMM is supporting local health authorities in managing County wide network of community based health providers (Boma Health Workers), operating at grossroots level, directly in contact with beneficiaries and therefore in the position to inform them about the project and to get their feedback.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries feedback will be collected through the above described community consultation mechanism, already existing and regularly used to get population perspective and perceptions on services responsiveness and acceptability. This consultation mechanism has been designed to get continuous inputs on the situation on ground and to be enabled doing timely adaptations of the proposed activities/supported services. As already used to do, CUAMM field team will assist the local health authorities in collecting, consolidating and analyzing the various feedback and to translate them into action points to be integrated into proposed project work plan and overarching strategy, making them more tailored and focused to respond to the needs and to address the issues beneficiaries perceived as priorities in that specific moment. This is also functional to ensure project constant update and alignment to the reality, narrowing the gap created by the time passed from project writing and project implementation.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The project will support essential preventive and curative health services, promoting them as entry point for protection ones, especially for women and children and people with disabilities. The project will enhance health services accessibility, by providing them as much close as possible to beneficiaries, overcoming those geographical/economical/social barriers which are more penalizing for vulnerable people and groups; in this way, health providers will be in the best position to capture these latter and to facilitate their entrance in the essensial services net. Health staff will be trained to promptly identify patients with physical or intellectual impairment and/or who experienced GBV and provide them with the most suitable first aid, while arranging for their referral or for more structured treatment, according to facility/health provider capacities and competencies. The staff will also learn how and will be given the means to make health facilities as well as mobile health stations as much accessible and acceptable as possible, starting from infrastructural barriers reduction up to developing a right based approach in receiving and treating patients, creating an enabling and comfortable environment for those who are used or fear to be discriminated/stigmatized because of their conditions. CUAMM and health authorities will ensure the due coordination and cooperation with protection sector actors and stakeholders, creating structured and effective referral pathways for people reaching health facilities/being reached by health providers to have prompt access to all other services they are in need of for a comprehensive management of their conditions

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The project aims to benefit both host and displaced population and it will complement existing resources to strenghten and expand existing services, making these latter specifically responsive to the most vulnerable (women, children, PWD, elderlies) needs, usually neglected or not adequately addressed, especially during emergencies. For instance, one of the problems it will address is the inadequacy of routine health services to respond to an increasing population, under IDPs influx, especially when IDPs prefer to locate in remote sites, not served by any health facilities or to reach those who cannot easily move to the few/dispersed ones. The adoption of mobile service delivery modalities is functional to bring the needed assistance at the closest possible level to beneficiaries, reducing both the distance they would have covered to reach static providers and the risk they would have been exposed to while travelling. Health staff as well as community based providers will be trained and mentored to be able identifiying and handling patients with particular vulnerabilities, considering both clinical and psychosocial aspects, through the effective linkage and referral to specialized entities. CUAMM will work with local authorities to ensure health services data being collected as per HMIS required gender/age/disabilities disaggregation, in order to get evidence for informed decision on more responsive system design/planning

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project contribution to global acute malnutrition reduction will be direct and indirect at the same time. On one side, the project will support the provision of essential care as per Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services, therefore integrating, through staff training and mentorship and equipment/supplies provision, nutrition component main functions into the health offer, as growth monitoring and/or MUAC screening for children accessing OPD and EPI and for PLW at ANC/PNC and consequent referral to SAM/MAM treatment program. In this way, health services will become the entry point to nutrition services for malnourished children, who will be timely identified and transfered to receive the due care. Further, health providers will include nutrition related topics into the health education sessions program, contributing to malnutrition prevention. Finally, health authorities will get a good amount of data on patients nutrition status to take informed decision on how to improve it.On the other side, the project will guarantee children overall health improvement, promoting their access and utilization to quality preventive and curative health services and therefore it will make them less vulnerable to malnutrition. The already mentioned health education session will transmit and spread good behaviours and practices as those related to wash and hygiene, which are functional to protect from both water borne diseases and malnutrition.
