Project Summary

RI has been implementing Cash For Education (CFE) programs in Bekaa since 2016 and in the North since 2022 through the provision of cash assistance to parents/caregivers of children in schools. The project supports the retention of students in public schools through the provision of homework support groups in order to enhance their educational capacities and performance in school and increase access to education through a focused intervention across multiple targeted locations supporting children and caregivers of vulnerable Lebanese and migrant communities across the targeted locations in North and Bekaa regions of Lebanon. Through the project, RI will improve education through back-to-school (BTS) campaigns, including awareness sessions about the importance of education, psychosocial support (PSS) activities, capacity building trainings for teachers in public schools, and cash support to encourage participation and ongoing learning. Due to Lebanon's continually worsening economic situation, coupled with limited financial capabilities, households are potentially forced to drop education as a necessary and life-saving aspect of their children’s lives due to the need to save money and/or resort to negative coping mechanisms. The prolonged lockdown caused severe effects on the quality of education provided and also slowed down the planned activities and reforms, such as early childhood education, addressing learning outcomes, new curriculum, rehabilitation works to improve efficiency, national scale-up of teachers’ performance standards, etc., hence the de-prioritization of education. With the continued rise in unemployment, many families are subjected to extremely stressful conditions and more children are now reduced to eating one to two meals per day. In addition, the COVID-19 pandemic resulted in the deterioration of hygiene, and increase in transmissible diseases has pushed families to extreme poverty, leading to PSS disorders and child protection risks.
Therefore, RI’s integrated education intervention targets the out of school and out of learning (OOS and OOL) children through a team of supervisors and Education Outreach Workers to supplement HH’s education needs in alignment with the Ministry of Education and Higher Education (MEHE) and education cluster strategic objectives.
Moreover, RI will address the education needs of 2,500 children aged between 12 and below in the North through the CFE assistance (which provides $20 per child per month during 2022-2023 scholastic year (October 2022 – May 2023). The cash assistance is provided through Online Money Transfer (OMT) financial service provider. The program will also strengthen the capacity of teachers in public schools through the provision of several professional development training courses that include child-centered methodologies, learning strategies, child safeguarding, creating a holistic vision of child development, integrated approach to language teaching, building critical thinking of the child, and engaging parents in their child’s education. RI targets 100 teachers in public schools (50 teachers in Bekaa, 50 teachers in North) who will receive the full package, aimed to impact positive behavior and virtues.
Through mental health (MH) mainstreaming and integration, RI will provide PSS to the 2,500 targeted children in the North selected for the CFE interventions. Four Social Workers will conduct daily PSS sessions at the targeted public schools, contributing to improvements in overall adjustment, which will result into more positive behaviors inside and outside the classroom, including improved academic performance. Overall, the project will increase access to education, promote learning, and also provide essential mental health support through the provision of PSS to children, while contributing to educational support for vulnerable communities and helping decrease dropout, child protection risks, and negative coping mechanisms amidst the economic crisis.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Activity 1.1: RI will recruit a team of supervisors and Education Outreach Workers will conduct the BTS campaign in selected areas in the North and Bekaa to inform the parents about the registration dates of the public schools and any other information that will benefit during the registration phase. Activity 1.2: In close coordination with the Education Sector in Lebanon and the MEHE, RI will target public schools in Tripoli to identify 2,500 vulnerable children (from KGs to Grade Six) who are enrolled in public schools as an effort to retain them in the formal education system through the provision of CFE assistance. Activity 1.6: RI’s Education Technical Lead will provide training and coaching on the education awareness sessions that will then be facilitated by the EOWs to the parents/caregivers of the children in Bekaa and North areas. Activity 2.1: To strengthen teachers’ capacity in the public schools, RI’s Education Technical Lead conducted several professional development training courses throughout the education projects.Activity 2.3: In order to tackle the exponentially increasing mental health concerns among both refugees and host communities, RI’s Social Workers will develop and conduct three PSS sessions (I.e. stress management and anger management group support sessions) for the children enrolled in the selected schools in North.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Protecting participants from harm will be the center of RI’s interventions. RI will offer services to eligible groups regardless of their nationality and gender following clear selection criteria and outreach components to prevent exclusion of any vulnerable group from obtaining assistance. The feedback, complaints, and response mechanism (FCRM) will ensure and reinforce protection during the proposed activities as it will be emphasized to the targeted communities for reporting any complaints, including safeguarding concerns. Moreover, staff will be hired taking into consideration gender balance and will be trained on gender-based violence (GBV) safeguarding, and accountability to affected population (AAP). Such training will not be limited to only RI staff and will also be provided to schools considered under this proposed intervention. 3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

RI adheres to the Interagency Standing Committee’s (IASC) five commitments to AAP focusing on: 1) Participation and Inclusion through the education outreach workers (EOWs) selected from the targeted community, outreach activities, and surveys while focusing on regular 2) Information Sharing and Transparency in disseminating relevant and timely information on activities, selection criteria, and available services at RI and other agencies-level through its referrals mechanism. The 3) FCRM is set with clear pathways relevant to complaints and feedback received through accountability channels, collected and secured by the accountability team, and shared on a need-to-know basis in relevance to feedback, complaint sensitivity levels. Findings will serve in 4) Program Adaptation through data collected under FCRM and MEAL activities reflecting participants' feedback on services provided and shared with the program team during regular meetings and lessons learned events to address needs.
