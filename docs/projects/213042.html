Project Summary

This project focuses on the economic empowerment of young individuals through a multifaceted approach centered on small business grants and freelancing training. Our comprehensive financial program seeks to promote inclusion, enhance economic resilience, and provide tangible opportunities for growth.
The core activities of this project entail intensive training programs in both technical and soft employment skills required for successful freelancing careers.
In addition to freelancing, we understand the importance of nurturing entrepreneurship and small businesses as key drivers of economic development. Therefore, we will offer comprehensive small business management and entrepreneurship training to equip young participants with the knowledge and skills needed to establish and grow their ventures.
To further bolster economic empowerment, Sanad will provide small business grants, enabling aspiring entrepreneurs to invest in essential market assets and resources. Through these initiatives, our project aims to not only create high-income employment opportunities but also to lay the foundation for sustainable economic growth, ensuring that young individuals are well-prepared to navigate and thrive in the face of economic challenges.
At the local level, the concept of Freelancing is relatively new. Between 2017 and 2019, four Freelancing events were organized in Damascus, Hama, Latakia, and Aleppo. Sanad Foundation played a role in implementing two of these events and provided support for others. Additionally, insights were gathered through interviews with event organizers and participants, as well as reports and questionnaires. These findings underscore the need for a supportive environment in this field.
The limited number of events, activities, projects, and entities dedicated to supporting freelancing has hindered its growth. A lack of awareness and a deficiency in the regulatory framework have also contributed to these challenges. Recognizing the importance of Freelancing in expanding opportunities for youth, supporting economic recovery, and achieving the Sustainable Development Goals for 2030, the Freelancing Project aims to equip young individuals with the necessary skills, both technical and managerial, to boost their economic potential.
Our objective is to provide them with essential tools, foster an enabling environment, and facilitate connections with the private sector, including banks, market-established companies, advertising and marketing agencies, and programming firms. This initiative aims to empower young people to pursue freelancing as a viable path to enter the job market, enabling them to generate income effectively and sustainably.
We acknowledge the potential risks associated with our interventions, such as the potential for exploitation or abuse. To mitigate these risks, we will implement strict monitoring and evaluation mechanisms, provide participants with channels to report any issues, and ensure that all activities are conducted in a safe and respectful environment.
Our project is committed to creating a safe and respectful environment for all participants. We will implement a zero-tolerance policy towards any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. This includes providing training to all staff and participants on appropriate conduct, establishing clear reporting mechanisms for any incidents, and taking swift action against any violations.
We recognize that there are inherent risks and assumptions in our project. These include the assumption that participants will be able to successfully apply the skills they learn, and the risk that external factors, such as economic or political instability, could impact the project's success. To mitigate these risks, we will provide ongoing support to participants, regularly review and adjust our strategies as needed, and work closely with local communities and stakeholders to ensure our interventions are responsive to changing conditions.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Here are potential risks for the project and their corresponding mitigation measures: Risk of Inadequate or Insufficient Training: This could lead to a lack of necessary skills among the beneficiaries, limiting their ability to successfully freelance or run their businesses. Mitigation measures include: regular assessment of training quality, continuous improvement of training content and methods, and provision of additional support and resources for beneficiaries.Risk of Low Adoption or Resistance to Economic Empowerment Strategies: This could result in low participation or ineffective implementation of the project’s strategies. Mitigation measures include: community and leaders’ engagement at all phases of the project cycle, adapting strategies and messages to the specific context, and hiring staff who are familiar with the local culture and context.Risk of Inadequate Support for Freelancers and Entrepreneurs: This could hinder the growth and success of freelancing careers and small businesses. Mitigation measures include: establishment of a robust support system, including mentoring and coaching, access to resources and networks, and continuous monitoring and evaluation of the support provided.Risk Related to the Location of Training and Support Services: This includes safety of movements for beneficiaries, contamination from remnants of war, proximity with sensitive buildings. Mitigation measures include: choice of locations done in close consultation and agreement with the community, conducting safety audits, and ensuring safe and secure access to services.Risk of Digital Divide: This could limit the beneficiaries’ access to digital resources and online opportunities. Mitigation measures include: provision of digital literacy training, ensuring access to digital devices and internet connectivity, and promoting digital inclusion in all aspects of the project.These risks and mitigation measures are aligned with the project’s focus on economic empowerment through freelancing training and small business grants, and they take into account the specific context and challenges of the project’s location.The grants will be in the form of tools and services to support entrepreneurial projects and not direct cash grants
