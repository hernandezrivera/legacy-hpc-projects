Project Summary

A) Project Rationale:
Northeast Nigeria faces one of the world’s largest protection crises in the world, violence perpetuated by Boko Haram (BH) and the Islamic State’s West Africa Province (ISWAP) has displaced nearly 2.5 million people, hindered livelihoods, and restricted affected populations from accessing basic services and humanitarian assistance. Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe are among the most affected states, with an estimated 1.7 million internally displaced people (IDPs), many living in camp settings.
Moreover, the protracted nature of the conflict reinforces pre-existing gender inequalities across the Northeastern states. An estimated 700,000 women and adolescent girls are at risk of sexual violence and exploitation, with 54% of IDPs being female and half of them of reproductive age; about 260,000 of these women are currently pregnant. Over 50,000 births will face complications with a high-risk of maternal mortality due to lack of appropriate SRHR services and insufficient referrals for secondary care and SRHR-trained medical staff.
Poor security conditions in local government areas (LGAs) in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe prevent IDPs from returning to their homes, while IDP camps frequently face attacks from BH and ISWAP. The main protection threats in camps include physical assaults and GBV, notably women forced into transactional sex. According to HRP for 2019-2021, individuals who are most at risk of GBV and insurgency-related violence include female and child-headed households, unaccompanied children, and adolescents. While armed groups often forcibly recruit, abduct, arbitrarily arrest, and detain men and adolescent boys, many female IDPs in camps have been forced or coerced into marriage and sexual exploitation. These women and girls suffer from severe stigmatization by fellow IDPs and the society, as well as insufficient support to promote their health and reintegration.
Emergency response must address the mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) needs of these vulnerable affected populations.
B) Proposed Response
HAI proposes “Access to specialized MHPSS case management and reintegration of IDPs, survivors of abductions, GBV and SEA in Northeast Nigeria,” in alignment with the Nigeria HRP priorities for the country. Over 12 months, Access will provide quality MHPSS and SRHR services and will enhance the reintegration of IDPs into their communities, including people affected by armed groups and survivors abductions, GBV and SEA.
HAI will improve the mental and physical health of IDPs (SO1) and support the urgent reintegration of people impacted by armed groups into their communities (SO2). Utilizing a community-based approach, HAI will provide quality emergency MHPSS services and specialized case management to IDPs through trained community outreach workers (CWs) and psychologists. Access’ CWs will provide psychoeducation awareness sessions to IDPs and will teach coping skills to community members through a gender-sensitive, peer-support group model. For individuals presenting complex mental health needs, Access’ roving psychologists will provide individuals with specialized and gender-sensitive mental health care.
Access will also support IDPs to access SRHR services through mapping existing SRHR and HIV/AIDS services that are suitable to the needs of IDPs women and girls affected by GBV and armed groups’ violence. Roving nurses will provide SRH services to IDPs including accurate SRHR information and individual counseling, prevention and treatment of sexually transmitted infections (STIs), including HIV/AIDS, and accompaniment to relevant services for emergency cases. Under SO2, HAI will support people impacted by armed groups to have access to income generating activities and supplies through vocational training targeting female heads of households and provide microloans to individuals to leverage the received training. Livelihood activities will be culturally and geographically appropriate

1 - Needs

Needs OverviewNortheast Nigeria is home to one of the world’s largest protection crises in the world, due to violence perpetrated by Boko Haram (BH) and the Islamic State’s West Africa Province (ISWAP) that has displaced nearly 2.5 million people, hindered livelihoods, and restricted affected populations from accessing basic services and humanitarian assistance. The protracted crisis in Northeast Nigeria results in complex and destructive consequences for women, men, girls and boys who are abducted and exploited. The ongoing conflict is characterized by deliberate and widespread attacks on civilian populations including sexual violence, abduction (especially of girls and women), forced disappearances, child and forced recruitment, forced displacement, and the use of suicide bombers. Female and child-headed households, unaccompanied and separated children, and adolescent boys and girls are particularly at risk of gender-based violence (GBV), forced recruitment, and human trafficking, while adolescent and adult males face threats of arbitrary arrests, prolonged detention, and often torture and other abuse during detention. According to the GBV Sub Sector annual report for 2018, of all the GBV incidents occurring in the context of sexual slavery and sexual violence reported, at least 36% were perpetrated by non-state armed groups (NSAG) and occurred to individuals held against her/his will, abducted or kidnapped. Of the women and girls who were forced and abducted from their homes by NSAGs, many have been raped, forced into marriage and labour, abused physically, sexually and/or emotionally, exposed to sexually transmitted diseases, and often impregnated by their captors. Violence against women and girls, including sexual violence, and exposure to trafficking and abduction is widespread but grossly underreported.The confict affected population is facing additional stressors created by the displacement itself, such as family separation, difficulties and sometimes denial of access to services, limited opportunities to livelihood, breakdown of family and community support mechanisms, potential stigmatization, secondary and repeated displacement. These factors can contribute to increasing risks of exposure to GBV. Exposure to GBV risks also includes exposure to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Boys and men are at risk of forced recruitments by armed actors and at risk of arbitrary detention and exposure to different forms of violence, yet the life-saving services are not readily available in some areas. Increased access to empowerment and livelihood will mitigate the occurrence of survival sex. Thus, need for provision of continuous psychosocial support, engagement of women and girls in livelihood activities, will encourage women participation in decision making, and ensure we mitigate all risks that might predispose women and girls to GBV.

2 - Activities

IR 1.1: IDP have increased access to quality emergency MHPSS services.Activity 1.1.1: Train Community Outreach Workers (COWs) (at least 50% females) to provide basic emotional and psychosocial support (PSS), to survivors of abductions, GBV and SEA. Activity 1.1.2: Trained COWs provide psychoeducation awareness sessions to IDPs, survivors of abductions, GBV and SEA on conflict and displacement-related stressors. Activity 1.1.3: Trained COWs teach coping skills to community members through a Community-Based, gender sensitive, Peer Support group model, with a particular focus on the needs of IDP. Activity 1.1.4: Roving psychologists provide individual, specialized mental health care when appropriate (for referred critical cases). Activity 1.1.5: COWs and psychologists receive consistent clinical supervision. Activity 1.1.6: Project Coordinator provides staff care sessions to prevent burnout and secondary trauma. IR 1.2: IDP have improved access to sexual and reproductive healthcare (SRHR) with a specific focus on women.Activity 1.2.1: Project coordinator maps existing SRH/HIV services and identifies the most sustainable sources for SRH services and products that are appropriate to the cultural context. Activity 1.2.2: Train COWs on SRHR outreach and on SRHR referrals.Training will include culturally appropriate information considerations around participants’ age, gender and sexual orientation and gender identity/expression (SOGIE). Activity 1.2.3: COWs conduct outreach at camps and host communities to sensitize IDPs and host communities on SRHR services. Activity 1.2.4: In partnership with the Ministry of Health (MoH) and the United Nations Population fund (UNFPA), COWs and roving nurses provide SRHR services to IDPs, such as:- Accurate sexual and reproductive health information (by COWs);- Specialized and culturally-appropriate individual SRHR counseling (by roving project nurses);- Prevention and management of STIs, including HIV testing and counseling (by roving project nurses);- Accompaniment to relevant services for emergency cases (by COWs).MoH will primarily support with products and HAI will provide the specialized services and individual counseling. SO2: People impacted by armed groups are better integrated into their communities.IR 2.1: People impacted by armed groups have improved access to income generating activities and supplies.Activity 2.1.1: Train staff on gender norms/how to leverage opportunities to tackle power imbalance and stereotypes throughout the project activities targeted at improving livelihoods. Activity 2.1.2: Provide vocational training to female heads of household.(Based on geographic location and culture, people from different LGAs specialize in different crafts, labor.) Activity 2.1.3: Administer microloans (not to exceed $50 per individual). IR 2.2: People impacted by armed groups and host communities are able to resolve disputes and better understand each other.Activity 2.2.1: Awareness raising with stakeholders, including religious and tribal leaders, around issues that people impacted by armed groups might experience. Activity 2.2.2: Lead community mediation sessions with affected populations and community members discussing issues that people impacted by armed groups face.Community mediation sessions might take different forms not limited to meetings but also including dance activities, storytelling, community theater and performance and others. Activity 2.2.3: Creation of alternative dispute resolution fora to support people and communities impacted by armed groups. Activity 2.2.4: COWs support individuals in obtaining civil documents, including IDs, marriage, birth, death certificates.
