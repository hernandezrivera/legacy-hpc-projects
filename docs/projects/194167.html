Project Summary

This project is built on the recent Needs assessments and evaluating the impact of previous humanitarian interventions in Syria within the context of sustainably improving the living conditions of those who still living in the country,
The project prioritizes providing lifesaving assistance to the most vulnerable people in order to bridge some of the identified critical gaps in response and prevent a larger and more protracted crisis, this project puts more focus on the shift in response nature from emergency towards the restoration of basic and essential services through which affected individuals can support themselves more sustainably (resilience programming), targeting : 10,848,814 with high severity scale (over three) in addition to ( Al-Ramal Ganobi in Lattakia
This project supports:
• Water quality monitoring and surveillance, by equipping MOWR laboratories and providing portable bacteriological water testing kits in Cholera hot spots
• Improving access to education and health services reaching 36,300 students as the following disaggregation ( Girls: 18,900, Boys : 17400 )
• Promoting awareness by conducting campaigns targeting vulnerable communities in the whole of Syria, on water-borne diseases, Covid-19, climate change, and distributing materials and kits in parallel
• Increasing access to sanitation services
• Providing sustained access to drinking water by rehabilitation of the main water sources and optimization of Syria’s largest drinking water systems targeting: 7,985,000
• ensuring a sustainable impact by increasing dependency on solar energy for operation (boreholes and schools)
• Provide sustained water sources for irrigation by establishing sewage water treatment plants (pilot project in Al Hassakeh), rehabilitating solar boreholes, and supporting water harvesting

3 - Needs

The impact of eleven years of conflict and displacement combined with unpreceded inflation in addition, shortage of water supplies, electricity cuts for long periods, and limited to access safe drinking water. Also the protraction of the crisis resulted in multiple displacements, Ongoing population movements, especially in north and western Syria, and more than 2 million people living in informal settlements and planned camps. The displacement of Syrian families has resulted in one of the largest refugee crises in the world. Moreover, Access to WASH services is compromised, as damage over years of conflict, limited maintenance, climate change effects, electricity shortages, and economic downturns, are putting 13.6 m people in need of WASH assistance and 52% of the vulnerable population depending on unsafe water sources.The SARC Water-Rehab department has always been the main contributor in humanitarian interventions. SARC conducts field visits to export severity scale maps for each Syrian governorate, divided into sub-district reflecting the water status of each sub-district according to the following criteria: natural water sources, availability of water infrastructure, and population. These maps help identify the most vulnerable areas and build the prioritization of our interventions._________________________statistics are according to Syrian humanitarian needs(HNO 2023)

4 - Implementation of activities

SARC Water-Rehab department depends on a specialized workforce of over 700 trained civil, mechanical and electrical engineers, architects, doctors, and pharmacists working with our partners towards restoring the basic services in the fields of water, sanitation, and shelterduring the last decade and until this moment SARC Water-Rehab teams have been providing shelter and services, implementing projects targeting providing access to safe drinking water, sanitation, and executing rehabilitation projects increasing access to health, nutrition, and education. Based on its accessibility and the local community trust, the Water-Rehab Department in 2023 plans to continue in•support water quality monitoring by equipping MOWR laboratories (Dara, DerAzor)•support water testing in Cholera hot spots•Rehabilitate damaged schools (Soueida,Dara, Aleppo, DerAzor,Raqqa) and health care facilities (Dara, Homs,Latakia,Aleppo,Hassakeh,Qamishly)•conduct awareness campaigns targeting vulnerable communities in whole Syria.•rehabilitate sewage and water networks•provide sustained access to drinking/ irrigation water by rehabilitating solar boreholes.•rehabilitation and optimization of water systems(AlQsair, Fijeh, Alashari, Alsen, Khafseh)•provide power supply on household level In terms of monitoring and evaluation, the WASH teams will conduct a baseline and end line surveys quarterly report to measure the impact of these interventions and will share the reports with SARC HQ and partners.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Health risk associated with construction works and exposure to chemicals.level of risk: high impact: medium Mitigation: Ensure staff/partners/contractors involved in construction works or working with chemicals wear appropriate PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) and follow established safety regulations; Conduct/repeat safety training for staff/partners/contractors.2- Water distribution is not safe to access or not accessible, especially for women, girls (exposed to GBV risk) and children elderly, and people with disabilities, inequality in the distributionlevel of risk: high impact: high mitigation: Establish complaint & feedback mechanism and FU; Monitor safe and equitable access and use of water distribution points. 3- Quality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards level of risk: highimpact :high Mitigation: Monitor water quality and quantity on a daily basis, implement the corrective action4- Improper storage of hazardous supplies (chlorine and other) materials) - Possibility of explosion, human casualty and asset destruction.level of risk: mediumimpact: high mitigation: Ensure proper storage of supplies with clear provision of care and safety consideration. Make sure that clear instruction for use is available for chlorine products primarily for household use.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: the accessibility of SARC to the affected areasmitigation:Continued coordination and collaboration with local actors to maintain access and acceptance. We can mitigate it through community engagement and accountability activities through proper communication and guarantee integrated CEA policy and feedback mechanism. Risk: Delays in the procurement of required goods and equipment for the response due to lack of availability of certain items or long delivery times by suppliers which affect the timely replenishment of the kits.mitigation measure: A rapid check on the availability of items among suppliers and market price will be undertaken at the beginning of the procurement process.Risk: Shortage of electricity, fuel, and transport/trucking prevents humanitarian assistance and volunteers/staff from reaching the people in need with the required assistance. The unavailability of electricity hampers communication between SARC HQ and the branch teams including sharing information and providing operational updates.mitigation measure: available fuel will be prioritized for the delivery of humanitarian Internal reaching the people in need with the required assistance. The unavailability of electricity hampers communication between SARC HQ and the branch teams. SARC has a framework agreement for trucking service that is used when its capacity is exceeded. Volunteers engaged in the response are from the governorate. The operational budget includes the procurement of fuel
