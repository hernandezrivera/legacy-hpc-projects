Project Summary

AMAN (Association for Mass Advancement Network) has developed the project with objectives to ensure and sustain the timely provision of life-saving nutrition-sensitive food assistance for Rohingya Refugee; Support food security resiliency of Rohingya refugee / Host communities through climate-sensitive food production in a stressed environment with early warning and early actions; Strengthen household food security of the host and local communities through climate-smart agricultural production, agro-processing, market linkages, resilient income opportunities and disaster response preparedness with early actions.
The project proposed locations are Camp 15, 16, 17 & 19 and Palongkhali where the activities will be food assistance, awareness raising and cash for work. Total 10 Community Promoters & stuffs and 5 Facilitators will be assigned with the project including cross-cutting activities along with Health, Wash, SNFI, GBV, LSD and Education project. AMAN’s estimated budget for the project is $154,475 to reach 25,370 individuals (Rohingya Refugees and Host Communities) segregated by all age and gender groups with priority on PWDs during 2023 aiming to multi-year planning till 2025. The proposed project will be implemented by coordination with FSS and its partner agencies, CiC, protection sector, LSDS, camp focal and other stakeholders where further coordination with the Age and Disability Working Group (ADWG) and Energy & Environment Technical Working Group (EETWG) will also be maintained.
Rapid and Ad-hoc Food Assistance support; Awareness Training; and Cash for Work will be provided for disaster preparedness and disaster risk reduction to Rohingya Refugees (camp 16, 17, 19) and Host communities (Palongkhali). Lunch facility (Hot Meal) under school feeding will be provided among the students of AMAN operated learning facility located at camp 15 during learning session where it will ensure proper coordination with WFP to avoid overlapping. Awareness raising activities will be provided on food utilization, social cohesion and disaster preparedness. AMAN will deliver need based supports to the targeted Refugees and host community linked with market-based approach in light of safeguarding policy and PSEA maintaining necessary coordination.
AMAN has skilled manpower to run the proposed project. AMAN has necessary policy and guidelines those will be followed for implementation of the project. AMAN always emphasizes on capacity building training and its concerned staff has already attended in different training by the initiatives of concerned sectors and stakeholders. Moreover, AMAN is an active member of PSEA network and its staff will be trained further to ensure proper protection mainstreaming.
AMAN always conducts baseline survey, FGD, KII, etc. while collects, analyzes and uses disaggregated data. Analysis on gender, age and diversity and equally consultation with women, girls and marginalized groups during assessments and in the overall response monitoring will strengthen the project with quality implementation.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Rohingya refugees have been receiving food assistance since their arrival in 2017. Initially, some of the Rohingya used their assets to meet food needs, but as they depleted these assets, the refugees became fully dependent on assistance. In reff. of Thematic report, ACAPS - NPM ANALYSIS HUB, 30 August 2022, The food consumption situation was stable for the two years before COVID-19, with 56% (2018) and 58% (2019) of the camp population having an acceptable FCS. Humanitarian food support continued during the pandemic, but containment measures, market closures, price increases, and restrictions on economic activity drove a slight deterioration of the FCS in the camps. There are indications that the FCS began to recover in 2021, although 45% of the population still had an unacceptable FCS. Humanitarian assistance has likely been benefiting the food consumption situation of the host community since 2018. There appeared to be a higher prevalence of unacceptable diets in FHHs in the host community than in Rohingya refugee camps. Overall, the FCS of the host community declined during the pandemic. It has yet to recover. The provision of targeted humanitarian assistance had a positive impact on the FCS of the host community, but measures put in place to address COVID-19 also affected their food consumption. have access to fewer income-generating opportunities and self-reliance activities, and the increased vulnerability is likely linked to the challenges in acquiring and transporting assistance without the help of an adult man. As the frequency of humanitarian distributions decreased, the consequent increase in the size and weight of distributed packages resulted in many recipients selling a proportion to pay porterage (ACAPS/IOM 28/04/2021). The problem for some households, notably those comprising an elderly person and only young female members, was they were not listed as extremely vulnerable given the presence of a younger member at working age (IOM 17/06/2021; ACAPS/IOM 28/04/2021).According to the survey of Resilience Index Measurement and Analysis (RIMA) Round 1 Report | FAO, August 2022, around 33 percent of respondents from the host community received cash transfers in the last 12 months, with the average amount being BDT 3,258. (BDT 4,269 from the treatment group and 639 from the control group). None of the households of the FDMN community receive cash transfers. In contrast, almost all of the FDMN community households received in-kind transfers such as food, shelter, medical facilities, agricultural supplies, LPG, and so on in the last 12 months, with an average value of BDT 78,520 (82,621 from the control and 74,419 from the intervention) per household. Food assistance accounted for most of the in-kind transfer (74 percent), followed by LPG gas (12 percent), and shelter (4 percent). In contrast, the host community only received a small number of in-kind transfers—on average, BDT 8,637—in comparison to the FDMN. Based on the above information AMAN will conduct the project title “Provision of life-saving food assistance and strengthen Food Security for Rohingya Refugees and Host Community” where estimated budget for the project is $154,475 to reach 25,370 individuals segregated by all age and gender groups giving priority on PWDs in 2023.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

AMAN has designed this project based on the Principles of Rationalization for the Rohingya Refugee response in Bangladesh, prepared by the ISCG to adopt an integrated approach aims to improve intersectoral camp-based coordination, service delivery to refugees and host communities, and relation with the CiC, sector and other stake holder. AMAN will ensure stability in the refugee operation, and improve relations with refugees, maji, the Government, sector and other partners in the camps. Necessary activities and strategies have been adopted ensuring that all targeted groups have equitable access to all basic services in a predictable, efficient, and timely manner, and that the humanitarian community is more transparent and accountable. Food security is an outcome of the food system leading to human well-being, which is also indirectly linked with climate and ecosystems through the socio-economic system. Following Adaptation measures by adding, deleting, editing, narrating, condensing, integrating, and altering will help to reduce negative impacts of climate change on the food system and ecosystems. AMAN will provide food assistance which are available and produced in the local market, lunch (hot food) facilities for the learners in LF and conduct awareness raising activities and cash for work among the Rohingya Refugees and Host Communities segregated by all age and gender groups giving priority on EVHHs and PWDs. AMAN will deliver need based supports to the targeted groups in light of safeguarding policy and PSEA maintaining necessary coordination with sectors and CiCs and above all, following the sector prescribed guideline and principles. AMAN will also provide monthly 5w reports and other necessary reports as required by the sector. AMAN has experienced manpower who have received different training and workshop by Protection sector and will be involved in further capacity building initiatives by the sector to ensure a minimum standard of service delivery.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

AMAN will ensure that the planned activities target the most vulnerable, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the rights of refugees without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation in light of Safeguarding and PSEA policy for effective implementation of the project. There will be SOPs or guidance on the establishment and operation of feedback, complaints, and response mechanisms. It will link with an appropriate referral system for protection and PSEA issues. In addition, collaboration with other stakeholders, such as peer agencies, sectors, partners, local authorities, and involvement of camps leaders’ and SMC groups to fulfil this commitment will also strengthen the program.

3.2 - Gender

AMAN collect, analyze and use disaggregated data and analysis on gender, age and diversity and equally consult with all age and gender groups with PWDs, including during assessments and in the overall response monitoring. AMAN will apply clearly defined risk management processes and ensure that project design and implementation are aligned with, and clearly mainstream, age, gender and diversity, and to ensure that all refugee groups are taken into account and that protection concerns of persons with specific needs are duly addressed with PSEA Policy. The activities will be performed through involvement of site management team, proper communication with CiC, sector focal and other stake holder who are related to implement the project. It will link with an appropriate referral system for protection and PSEA issues.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

AMAN will implement the project, with or come into contact with children by following its safeguarding policies and procedures to ensure that every child, regardless of their age, disability, gender reassignment, race, religion or belief, sex, or sexual orientation, has a right to equal protection from harm. Setting up and following safeguarding and PSEA policies and procedures by smooth cooperation with site management, CiC, sector focal and other stake holder for children are safe from adults and other children who might pose a risk. This includes voluntary and community organizations, faith groups, private sector providers, as well as schools and hospitals. AMAN Safeguarding and PSEA policy is the action that is taken to promote the welfare of children and protect them from harm.

3.4 - Diversity

Diversity program will have followed by AMAN during project implementation period which involves three major steps: (1) Building a corporate culture that values diversity. (2) Changing structures, policies, and systems to support diversity. (3) Providing diversity awareness training to fulfill the project needs. AMAN will follow transparent, fair and equitable hiring practices, establish pay equity, provide support that helps each team member succeed and Give all team members a voice. AMAN will follow true diversity—be it of gender, age, race, experience, sexual orientation, thought, nationality or culture. AMAN will focus cultural diversity, racial diversity, gender diversity, physical Disabilities and diversity in interest during implementation of project. The activities will be performed through involvement of site management team, proper communication with CiC, sector focal and other stakeholders who are related to implement the project.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) is an active commitment by AMAN to use power responsibly by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the people they seek to assist. AMAN will consider following suggestions while designing humanitarian projects for JRP 2023 and beyond: Information, Communication, and Transparency; Participation, Engagement, and Inclusion; Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanism; Learning and Adaptation. Information should be routinely provided to the community by both the organization and its partners on the organization’s vision, mission, mandate, accountability commitments, code of conduct, feedback, complaints procedure, and relevant contact details. Community participation should take place during all phases- needs assessment and program planning, program implementation, distribution and service delivery, monitoring and evaluation. The Do No Harm framework or equivalent approach will be employed with the participation.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) Programme will assists in developing and delivering services that are directed at protecting lives, livelihoods and property from natural hazard in a cost-effective, systematic, and sustainable manner in Refugee and host community. AMAN aiming the DRR plan by support preparedness through early warning systems; provide hazard information for risk assessments, prevention, response and recovery, and risk transfer across sectors; mitigate existing risks and prevent the creation of new risks; respond to user requirements; cooperate and engage in disaster risk governance structures at all levels by providing food assistant as well as other necessary elements as needed.
