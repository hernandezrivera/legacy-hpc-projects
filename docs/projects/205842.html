Project Summary

The proposed intervention will contribute to increased and equitable access to quality education with improved education outcomes targeting children of school-going age in selected counties in Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, Central Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. The project will increase the enrollment of out-of-school children; ensure the availability of protective and safe learning spaces for school children through the construction and/or rehabilitation of semi-permanent learning spaces (SLS), Temporary Learning Species (where appropriate) and Gender sensitive latrines; hygiene and sanitation education; provision of school equipment and learning materials; training of School Management Committee (SMCs) members, Parents Teachers Association (PTA) members and other school stakeholders on effective school management and equip them to mobilize school enrollment for out-of-school children between the ages of 3 to17 years. Inclusive education of children with disabilities will be prioritized. The project will target community members with messaging and social mobilization awareness-raising on the importance of sending children to school. In total, the project will seek to directly reach 5000 children (2,500 boys and 2,500 girls) and 210 adults (107 women and 103 men) in selected payams across the 5 states. The project will deliberately target communities with a high percentage of out-of-school girls and boys to increase overall school enrolment and pupil retention rates. Areas consisting of large Internally displaced persons (IDP) or returnee populations will be assessed and mapped to ensure equitable inclusion of school children from this target population. The project will establish gender and disabled-friendly water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) infrastructure at the established or rehabilitated learning spaces in the area. All new or rehabilitated learning structures will be semi-permanent in nature, fenced, and have four secured classrooms that can accommodate approximately 50 learners. An office space with furniture for teachers and facilitators, three disabled-friendly latrines (1 male and 2 female) with handwashing devices. Women and girls will also have privacy provision for menstrual hygiene management and a water access point. Community members will be mobilized for construction, while materials will be sourced locally to the best of ForAfrika's ability. To increase learner enrolment and retention, Forafrika will support teachers, facilitators, and community structures (PTAs and SMCs) to conduct quarterly enrolment drives within the 5 km radius of the temporary learning spaces centers. Girls, IDPs, and returnees will be prioritized during door-to-door campaign sessions. To promote girls' education, Forafrika will identify role model women with post-secondary education attainment to engage directly with parents on the importance of education for girls. The project will also incentivize girls' education through the provision of dignity kits for adolescent girls in schools. The project will map targeted counties to identify facilities that require school learners to travel more than 5 kilometers to access schools, schools with inadequate, absent, and dilapidated structures including appropriate WASH facilities. This will allow Forafrika to corroborate existing data with active consultation and feedback from the government, community leaders, and education stakeholders. To achieve the project outcomes. ForAfrika will leverage expertise, experience, local presence, and community acceptance. To ensure effective multi-sectoral coordination, ForAfrika will work closely with humanitarian, government, parents, community leaders, and all the education.

1. Needs

The 2023 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) focuses on the humanitarian needs of internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and vulnerable host communities/non-displaced people in South Sudan. The current analysis also includes vulnerable sub-groups, including female-headed households and people with disabilities (PWD), whose vulnerabilities have been aggravated by the ongoing conflict, flooding, and economic depreciation.The projected 9.4 million people in need for 2023 include 1.9 million IDPs, 1.4 million returnees, 5.8 million host community/non-displaced people and 337,000 refugees. Among them, there are 2.2 million women and 4.9 million children, including 2.4 million girls and 2.5 million boys. Nearly 15% of the total people in need are PWDThere are over 392,000 returnees and 1.7 million IDPs across the country, with both groups facing severe constraints in accessing education (UNHCR 2021). Insecurity remains one of the single biggest threats to access to education in South Sudan. Active conflict and recurring communal violence throughout 2022 led to school closures in some locations. Jonglei, the GPAA, Lakes, Upper Nile, Warrap, Northern Bahar El Gazal, Unity, and Western Equatoria are some of the worst affected. Safety and security incidents in and around education facilities, as well as destruction or occupation form barriers to accessing education facilities. Learners in 2021 ENA cited insecurity in and around schools as a major reason for learner absenteeism and school drop-out. Extreme weather conditions of drought and flooding severely impact educational attainment for children. Across the country, 30% of existing schools are estimated to be damaged, destroyed, or closed (MoGEI &, UNICEF Dec.2020). It is also estimated that by October 2022, at least 887 schools in several states were closed or damaged due to floods while several others were occupied by IDPs seeking shelter on higher ground (Education Cluster, Oct.22). Socio-cultural influences also negatively affect children's education. In Pibor County, only 56% of households reported at least one school-aged child attends formal schooling regularly (REACH 2020). A significant number of classes are conducted outside (under trees) leaving children without the protective environment of a physical classroom and often lacking access to basic classroom necessities like blackboard, desks, tables, and chairs. Long walking distance to schools, lack of access to basic teaching and learning materials, and a poor teacher incentive system further compromise the delivery of quality education. Barriers and negative impacts are multiplied for girls, young women and children with disabilities (CWD) due to negative cultural traditions. In South Sudan, 52% of girls are married before the age of 18 (UNICEF 2021). Child marriage, early pregnancy, and viewing girls as valuable for bride price (UNICEF August 2017) form key barriers. As a result, school dropout is common and decreases girls' chances of attaining an education further reducing their opportunity to break the cycle of poverty. Access is even more constrained for children with disabilities evidenced by the lack of mobility or assistive devices in a significant proportion of education facilities. Against the above background, ForAfrika will ensure the availability of an enabling learning environment as well as embark on efforts to increase school enrollment and retention rates and improve the quality of gender and special needs education.

2. Project activities

ForAfrika will build 57 semi-permanent classrooms in primary schools and ALP centers with dimensions of 16.6m x 7.4m, following the approved designs of the Ministry of Education in South Sudan. To support these structures, we will use 100mm x 100mm x 3mm steel, and cover the roofs with either corrugated iron or aluminum sheets. Due to the poor soil conditions, we will cast the concrete works on site and raise them above ground level to prevent flooding. Additionally, we will use 400mm height of clay bricks as a superstructure to further prevent flooding, which will be rendered with mortar to prevent spalling. We will coordinate closely with the state-level Ministry of Education to prevent duplication and ensure that the neediest schools are targeted. The ForAfrika infrastructure team will supervise the project and ensure quality assurance and control.Water, sanitation, and hygiene interventions will be promoted to tackle the identified WASH challenges. These interventions include water supply, sanitation/waste management, and hygiene promotion in the targeted schools. To promote child hygiene and sanitation approaches in schools, particularly for children who are pastoral or nomadic, knowledge building and improvement of personal hygiene and sanitation practices are essential. This will be achieved through awareness campaigns and encouraging peer-to-peer learning. It is envisaged that children's social behavior in society as well as a healthier and safer learning environment will be improved. This project will address all WASH challenges through the construction of 103 gender-segregated latrines in formal Primary Schools and Accelerated Learning Programme (ALP) centers.One thousand twelve teachers (1012) will be trained on EiE, Subject Matter, Pedagogical skills, and on the South Sudan new curriculum. The training will also focus on the provision of educational skills to pave the way for structural and participatory teaching approaches, integrating classroom management, mental health, and psychosocial support (MHPSS), peace-building, gender, gender-based violence, and human rights. MHPSS will target boys, girls, and teachers to address the psychological distress caused by COVID-19 and climate change.The project will capacitate ECD and Primary school teachers to promote multiple flexible pathways to get out-of-school children into learning. Also, ECD learners and CWD will be deliberately targeted and supported. A girls’ mentorship program will be established in the targeted schools. These interventions shall enhance the quality of teaching and the learning processes and improve the performance of learners.School Management Committees (SMCs) and Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) play a key role in maintaining a functional and quality environment for school leaders, teachers, and students. To help them carry out their roles effectively, the project will strengthen the capacities of 222 SMCs/PTAs in targeted locations. That will help the PTA/SMC members in the efficient implementation of school development plans, improved learner and teacher attendance monitoring, retention of learners, and management of other school resources. For instance, The PTAs will be capacitated in conducting awareness campaigns for children/adolescents and youth (3-17 years old) to be enrolled in schools in affected areas while the SMCs will support in follow-up of out-of-school children to either enroll or return to school. The project will also provide members of the various community structures (Local Chiefs, Religious Leaders, Youth and women Leaders, etc) with critical information and skills to facilitate social mobilization back-to-learning campaigns, inclusive and protective education, proper nutrition, basic health information, and social cohesion.ForAfrika will provide learning and recreational materials distributed to children and youth (6-17 years) and ECD kits distributed to children (3-5 years) to the constructed schools.
