Project Summary

The aim of this intervention is: conflict-affected communities in MMC and Jere LGAs are sustainably protected from, and treated for, the main causes of morbidity and mortality; supported to meet immediate and long-term Shelter and Settlement, WASH and Protection needs, while being protected and provided with the legal assistance they need. To achieve this, the proposed project will be implemented by a consortium led by the International Rescue Committee (IRC), in partnership with the Babatunde Ojei / Babatunde.Ojei@rescue.org / 234(0) 9097579611
Helina Belay / helina.belay@rescue.org / +234 (0)90 731 549 69
Dr. Olusegun Afolabi / Olusegun.Afolabi@rescue.org / 08051300504he expertise of each partner to deliver a multi-sectoral, area-based response, providing integrated services across the following sectors: Health, WASH, Nutrition, Food Assistance, Multi-purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA), Nutrition, Economic Recovery and Market Systems (ERMS), Protection and Shelter and Settlements. The consortium aims to meet the most immediate and life-saving needs of IDPs, host communities and returnees in MMC and Jere, which will translate into reduced mortality and improved living conditions. The consortium will also address the ongoing, protracted needs, and supporting early recovery through capacity-building, the strengthening of existing health systems and community-based structures, and increasing the ownership of life-saving, life-sustaining, and life-building services by local authorities, service providers, and local communities to ensure sustainability. In order to build the resilience of individuals and communities, the unique humanitarian and development challenges in MMC and Jere LGAs (presence of service delivery actors juxtaposed against an ongoing flux of IDPs in and out of camps and host communities throughout the city) must be addressed simultaneously by increasing access to quality, sustainable basic services, building sustainable livelihoods, and strengthening local systems and structures.

1 - Needs

Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC) LGA continues to host the highest number of IDPs in Borno (19%), closely followed by Jere LGA (297,610 IDPs, 18% of all IDPs in Borno). In addition, 5.8 million people, including an estimated 4 million women and girls, continue to require life-saving and essential health services across the region. Frequent AOG attacks have had a severe impact on the health system, damaging two-thirds of all health facilities across the BAY States. Indeed, out of 2,631 health facilities, 23% are either damaged or non-functional, while 11.4% are only partially functional. Lack of access to safe, drinkable water and sanitation infrastructure, and the consequences of protracted displacement (overcrowded and under-serviced camps and settlements, underlying poverty, etc.) have resulted in the high prevalence of malaria, measles, diarrhea and acute respiratory diseases. Key child health indicators are low across all three BAY States, and communicable diseases are highly prevalent. Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) services remain a considerable need for women and girls; however, limited access to skilled birth attendants, and strong sociocultural beliefs, often prevent them from accessing the services they need. Moreover, the World Health Organization (WHO) Early Warning, Alert and Response System (EWARS) and Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) estimate that more than half of recorded deaths in the BAY states in 2020 were due to malaria, combined with malnutrition – more than all other causes of death combined, including cholera, measles, hepatitis, and key non-communicable diseases. The ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, finally, has exacerbated all existing operational challenges, leading to economic deterioration, strained public services and an increase in humanitarian needs and compound operational challenges

2 - Activities

The IRC will provide Health services via mobile clinics managed by a multi-purpose outreach response team who will provide integrated basic essential health care services, with built-in capacity to scale up in response to new displacements and disease outbreaks. The IRC will also support selected government facilities to provide a comprehensive suite of health services and contribute towards early recovery by strengthening health information management, logistics systems and pharmaceutical support. To further improve demand for available services and encourage community ownership of the targeted health facilities, the proposed action will also strengthen community-based structures and community volunteers.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The IRC will prioritize the needs of women and girls to narrow the gender gap. Women will be encouraged to participate in mother to mother support groups (MtMSGs), which are groups of mothers who receive training on best mothering practices and use their knowledge to educate the community. They will also be encouraged to become CHVs. Their ability to visit homes more easily than men will be particularly useful for followup of family planning clients, home deliveries, malnourished children and defaulter tracing. At the same time, the IRC will explore ways to also reach men without reinforcing gender norms. For instance, men’s participation in health awareness-raising sessions and men-to-men support groups will be encouraged. The IRC will integrate the four principles of protection mainstreaming in their workings in all of its health facilities which will be accessible to the bene􀂦ciaries. The IRC will ensure that all vulnerable groups safely access all facilities; where necessary, the IRC’s Health and PRoL teams will collaborate to ensure that basic structures like ramps are constructed and all pathways made comfortable for persons with limited mobility. Health facilities will have private consultation rooms for privacy and con􀂦dentiality purposes, and screens/curtains provided for privacy. The health team will continue to engage members from both host communities and IDPs as CHVs while adhering to the age, gender & diversity mainstreaming principles to ensure that issues affecting the different groups are well articulated. The health team and the protection teams will jointly conduct community dialogue sessions with Bulamas, community structures, community/opinion leaders and community members to discuss uptake of project services, key challenges, and gaps including issues on protection. Through FGDs and key informant interviews (KIIs), the IRC will collect information from different groups (women, men, girls and boys). The IRC will also gather information through existing structures (i.e. community leaders, Protection Action Groups (PAGs), MtMSGs, health and nutrition data). This will help the IRC assess the speci􀂦cs that exist among the needs of the population, depending on age and sex. IRC conducts regular protection risk analysis will enable the IRC to identify protection risks speci􀂦c to women, men, girls or boys, Protection responses will be speci􀂦cally tailored to reduce the vulnerabilities of the speci􀂦c segments of the population, while simultaneously increasing their resilience

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The IRC has established Accountability Feedback Mechanism (AFM) in all its operational areas. This AFM will be maintained to receive feedback and complaints and provide responses on actions taken or to be taken will be provided to community members. In addition, the IRC will also receive feedback from the established IDP camp compliant mechanism, and action plans to be taken and responses will be provided to community members. In addition, other approaches the IRC uses are through dialogue meetings with the community leaders. The project will integrate client responsive feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, and people with disabilities. These will include regular focus group discussions (FGDs) and listening sessions that include sessions speci􀂦cally with women, girls, and persons with disabilities, the elderly, and other vulnerable groups. It will additionally make use of the existing suggestion boxes and community discussion forums to collect bene􀂦ciary feedback on quality emergency response actions. The IRC also has a toll-free hotline with dedicated hotline handlers who record and refer complaints to the appropriate staff. The number for the hotline is advertised on IRC banners at program implementation sites. The IRC’s hotline, suggestion boxes and community engagement meetings/focus group discussions will ensure that community voices are inclusive of the implementation process and furthermore serve as reporting mechanisms in case of maltreatment or abuse. Client exit interviews will be conducted to receive feedback directly from beneficiaries attending clinics, which will allow the IRC to make informed adjustments in project implementation.
