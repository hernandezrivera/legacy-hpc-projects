Project Summary

SHAFAK organization will respond to the child protection concerns (death and Injuries due to the ERWs, Child Labor, Child Recruitment and Use by Parties to the Conflict, sexual violence, Child marriage, child separation…etc.) through center-based activities in Child-Friendly Spaces (CFS) and community outreach teams (mobile teams) deployed in Idleb and Azaz district. SHAFAK will deliver case management services for vulnerable children and for their caregivers by providing direct assistances and refer them to a proper care arrangement using the service map for both girls and boys under the 18 years and will deliver the services to the disable children and help them to reintegrate with their communities, and deliver raising awareness sessions/campaigns to the people in the communities about child rights and child protection and risk education. Furthermore, SHAFKAK will organize recreational events in the CFSs and care center to highlight the child concerns in the target communities.
Also SHAFAK will strengthen the capacity of the local communities about the child concerns. Firstly, by establishing the Child Protection Networks (CPNs) in the targeted communities and train the key persons in these communities and the workers in care centers, schools, hospitals and all persons who are in direct touch with children about the Child Protection Minimum Standers (CPMS) and Psychosocial First Aid (PFA), Safe Identification and referral (SIR) taking into consideration the recommendations received from the local councils, children, caregivers to enhance the quality of provided services. SHAFAK will use the monthly reports of the Protection Monitoring task force on the latest developments that threaten child protection and will provide services according to the need there.

Needs

According to the latest statistics, the number of children in Syria is in need of humanitarian assistance is 5.3 million. The child protection needs regularly increased due to the absence of the proper care arrangement for children. Shafak finds from the published data that the needs of community-based Child protection(CP) service such as; CP case management, awareness session about CP concerns, enhance the care arrangement within the families still considered as critical needs in Syria. Shafak reviewed these concerns and highlighted the following concerns in this project are:1-Child labour: According to the monthly reports for the child protection monitoring task force, the results clearly show the increase in the percentage of the child labor in 2018 and that was directly reflected on the key findings of the HNO 2019 that considered the first threaten for the child protection. The monthly reports mentioned to about 72% of children who are suffering from child labour don't go to schools, and 62 % of those children are heading households. Those children are subjected to abuse, exploitation and harassment. The reason for this issue is dying the caregivers (one or both) and the need to support the families to enhance their incomes.2- Child Recruitment and Use by Parties to the Conflict: the available data according to the AN OVERVIEW OF CHILDREN’S PROTECTION NEEDS IN SYRIA report. They could indicate the percentage of occurrence of Child Recruitment which was 47 % of assessed communities indicated that child recruitment was occurring, with reports of the issue being either common or very common in 18 % of communities. Adolescent boys 12-17 years were considered most affected (47 % of assessed communities),3- sexual violence and gender-based violence and Child marriage: the results showed the issue of GBV become very common in communities. due to lack of awareness between the parents, and spread of the child early marriage. the motivation for these practices were spread ignorance and lack of awareness about children's rights, economic motives that prevent families from securing the needs of their children.4-Death and Injuries: 3.3 million children remain exposed to the risk of explosive hazards in their communities which may cause injury or death for children while playing as a result of explosive hazard. The use of explosive weapons in some inhabited areas by the fighting groups prevented access for the basic services such as healthcare and education for children. These remnants caused injury to children and raised the proportion of children with special needs due to losing some parts or hurting them. Those children are living in marginalization and disempowerment, as many live isolated lives and struggle against stigma, discrimination and an environment that does not accommodate their needs, and excludes them from social participation. In emergency situations, such as happening in Syria, the suffering of these children is on the increase, and they may face additional challenges: losing assistive devices and/or caregiver and thus become extremely vulnerable to physical violence, and to sexual, emotional and verbal abuse and their access to humanitarian assistance and basic services such as education may be hampered among many other concerns. There is no accurate data available on children who have permanent disability from conflict-related injuries in Syria, nor is there accurate pre-crisis data. However, the 2018 HNO estimates 2.9 million people living with a disability and are in need. of humanitarian assistance, which means approximately 1.2 million children in Syria are living with a disability.

Implementation of activities

Establish CPNs: SHAFAK will establish three CPNs in Azaz District and 5 CPNs in Idleb governorate with logistic services and train members about the Child Protection topics. The CPNs will responsible for monitoring the child protection concerns in their communities and work closely with CP staff through holding the monthly meetings and report any concerns related to the child protection.Train the staff: SHFAKF will delivered trainings for CPNs’ members, education staff, care centers staff and other key persons about the CPMS, CPiE, PFA, SIR. These trainings will be conducted by Child Protection field staff. Conduct awareness sessions about the child protection concerns:SHAFAK through the outreach team and CFSs will conduct awareness sessions about the specific topics about the child protection concerns that mentioned in the Child Protection Monitoring Task Force monthly report which are; child right, early marriage, separation preventions, alternative care and others.CP staff will be trained on case management and dealing with children with special needs. Conduct Structured PSS in CFSs for children and their caregivers: SHAFAK will establish 5 CFSs in Idleb governorate and establish three in Azaz district and furnish them with all required equipment: safety and security and health equipment. These CFSs will be equipped to host children with special needs. SHAFKA will deliver structured PSS activities through the CFSs for vulnerable children who are suffering from lack of protection and parenting skills for the caregivers (host communities and IDPs); the PSS activities will be selected from I-Deal and Big Deal curriculum; the child will attend about 18-22 sessions. The children will be divided into groups that include 18-20 children, age and gender disaggregated. For the parenting skills, there will be a group of Men and Women for ten weeks on parenting skills sessions issued by the International Rescue Committee (IRC) for north Syria Raising the awareness of the communities about child protection concerns: SHAFKA outreach team will organize events in schools, care centers, camps, CFSs and public places in coordination with local councils, education directorates and other key stakeholders. During these session SHAFAK will distribute the flayers, key massages and refreshments. Deliver specialised services to high-risk cases through case management SHAFAK through the caseworkers will document children with vulnerabilities (Abuse, Neglect, Early Marriage, Exploitation, Children Associated in Armed Forces and Armed Groups (CAAFAG), Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC), Gender-Based Violence(GBV)), and provide essential case management service for those cases and refer them to education, health, protection services (internally or externally). SHAFAK will provide special case management services for the high risk vulnerabilities. Targeting the survival for GBV by providing individual psychosocial support. For the whole project, SHAFAK will hire 5 GBV case workers to provide consultation services and follow up with survival and link them with proper services. Regarding the UASC that will be recorded in Idleb governorate, case workers will refer these cases internally to FTR program that will receive the cases and provide the special case management service – SHAFAK has already an FTR program funded by Save the Children International-SHAFAK will work closely with local communities and CPNs to identify the vulnerable cases and reach them to deliver the proper support. Moreover, SHAFAK will use the CP and GBV sub-cluster SOPs and referral pathways for case management.MEAL plan: Shafak will monitor the activities and prepare a visits to implementation locations- apply the complaints mechanism which includes (complaints boxes, brochures)-pre-post test after delivering the CP awareness sessions to measue the benefit.-pre-post test after delivering PSS sessions for children to measure the changs in atitude.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk (1): Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), including harassment, against women and girls by distributors and male beneficiaries while queuing and before/after distributionsRisk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure:- recruit male and female volunteers who are respected in the community and independent of armed groups. No children to be employed (under 18 years old)- make sure staff are already trained on Do No Harm, protection from SEA (PSEA), and child safeguarding; and sign and adhere to code of conduct that respects and uphold the rights of beneficiaries and PSEA- set up communication and complaints mechanisms or desks that are clearly marked; face interviews with beneficiaries, including women and children Risk (2):Tension between the host community and IDPs (who were evacuated)Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure:- clearly communicate selection criteria to the community, community leaders and beneficiaries prior to the announcement of the activity.- harmonize the assistance delivered as per Cluster instructions and guidelines.- coordinate with the local council in the targeted area as well as being active in field coordination Risk (3): Difficult access for women, unaccompanied children, elderly, people with disabilities (PWD)Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure:- Register all adult women, unaccompanied children, elderly, people with disabilities as main recipients of Kits distributed- During post -distribution follow-up, understand how women, children, and other groups accessed the distributed goods and their feedback on the process and materialsihood program)

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:1 - Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served. 2 -Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality 3 -Impact of activity is minimal on children's protection 4 -Challenges in measuring the impact of capacity development; compromised the quality of training due to contextual constraints; the high turnover of staff5- Difficulty in referring some cases to other services (such as livelihood program) Mitigation measures:1- ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remote managed context2- Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules.3- include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision.4- Service mappings should only include services that have been determined to provide safe and quality services.5- Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place.6- Child Protection messaging to non-Protection actors7- Link with other capacity building initiatives to ensure synergy 8- Support development of a cohort of core child protection trainers in Syria 9- Encourage follow-up, mentoring and coaching initiatives 10- Self-care strategies as part of programmes and organizational HR policies11- Coordinate with other sectors to develop the service maps regularly
