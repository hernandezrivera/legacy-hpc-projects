Project Summary

Medair worked in Sudan from 2000 to 2012, and retuned in 2020, receiving its registration in March 2020. Current programming consists of COVID and Flooding response in Khartoum state, Mayo locality. We are expanding in 2021 to include sites in Gedaref and Blue Nile (refugee response) with the main objective of decreasing preventable morbidity and mortality due to critical gaps in basic health, nutrition, WASH, shelter and NFIs services. Medair will be: supporting health and nutrition needs thought PHC-based facilities to meet the acute health and nutrition needs, WASH needs though improving access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene promotion activities, and Shelter and NFI needs through distributions of shelter and NFI materials. These response goals will serve repeatedly displaced and newly arrived refugees as well as host communities.
Medair’s main activity in all the sites will be to implement a needs assessment and to design appropriate responses to those recently displaced such as refugees (including recently arrived Ethiopian Refugees). Coordination with communities, Government, UN organizations and other NGOs will be essential to ensure needs are covered and prevent duplication of services. The target for 2021 will be to serve 10,000ind. with Health/Nutrition services, 44,219ind. through WASH services and 2,500 HH (resulting in an estimated 12,500 ind.) with Shelter and NFI programming. These numbers include both refugee and host communities targeted.
In Khatoume, Medair has been supporting HHs (refugees and IDPs) affected by COVID and floods thought support for local PHC-facilities, hygiene promotion and through the provision of basic household items. Many HHs who have been displaced repeatedly are now further impacted by the historic flooding seen in 2020 and these households who have lost their shelters due to the flooding have often combined with others, increasing their risk of exposure to COVID. Further assistance and support are needed to continue to address these needs.
Medair has conducted an assessment in Blue Nile identifying refugees in need of assistance in communities depleted of resources following years of conflict and underdevelopment. Further shocks, including internal displacement, arrival of additional refugees and large scale retunes could quickly overwhelm coping capacities within affected communities.
Ethiopian refugees began arriving in November 2020 as a result of active conflict within the Tegry region of Ethiopia. So far 45,689 Ethiopian refugees have arrived in Sudan (as of 30th November 2020), predominantly in Gedaref and Kassala states. Medair is actively planning to respond in Gedaref and is coordinating with UNHCR and NGOs on the ground to understand the needs and respond in a coordinated and appropriate way to meet critical needs.
In all these projects Medair will coordinate with internal and external multi-sectoral programming to ensure cross cutting issues, needs and concerns are reported and addressed as needed.

1 - Needs

In Khatoume, Medair has been supporting HHs (refugees and IDPs) affected by COVID and floods thought support for local PHC-facilities, hygiene promotion and through the provision of basic household items. Many HHs who have been displaced repeatedly are now further impacted by the historic flooding seen in 2020, the flooding within Khartoum state impacted a reported 102,000 people (20,055 households) in seven localities. This includes 30,064 IDPs (6,232 households). Some 12,370 households have been destroyed and 8,145 damaged. Those households who have lost their shelters due to the flooding have often combined with others, increasing their risk of exposure to COVID. Further assistance and support are needed to continue to address these needs.Medair has conducted an assessment in Blue Nile identifying refugees, recently arrived from Ethiopia, in need of assistance in communities depleted of resources following years of conflict and underdevelopment. Further shocks, including internal displacement, arrival of additional refugees and large scale retunes could quickly overwhelm coping capacities within affected communities. Ethiopian refugees began arriving in November 2020 as a result of active conflict within the Tegry region of Ethiopia. So far 45,689 Ethiopian refugees have arrived in Sudan (as of 30th November 2020), predominantly in Gedaref and Kassala states. Medair is actively planning to respond in Gedaref and is coordinating with UNHCR and NGOs on the ground to understand the needs and respond in a coordinated and appropriate way to meet critical needs. There are currently two MOH-support facilities serving the growing refugee populations between Kassala and Gadaref. However, gaps in staffing shortages, essential services, and medicines continue. Lack of beds and ambulances is hindering referrals to specialized care. The overcrowded setting is ripe for a possible COVID-19 outbreak. Significant needs in food and nutrition remain high. With the establishment of new camps and relocation of refugees, expansion of health services is urgently needed.Provision of WASH services for newly arriving refugees will be critical in ensuring basic services are accessed by the new arrivals. Water treatment and testing quality is of utmost concern during the onset of this phase as the community relies heavily on water trucking stored in temporary bladders for the community to access through limited water points that have been installed. Drainage of wastewater at the water collection points has been observed as a great concern as stagnating water precipitates mosquito breading grounds. Overcrowding at water points is a growing concern as numbers for new arrivals soar. Safe management of human waste has been observed as an upcoming concern with few constructed communal latrines shared by all the community members and not segregated. A lack of bathing/washing basins has resulted in communities fabricating the provided jerrycans (cut into two) previously distributed to be used for washing purposes limiting water collection & storage containers available at households. Limited bathing shelters for the refugees has hindered personal hygiene depriving human dignity. Hygiene promotion is a challenge at the onset with fewer available hygiene kits, improper practices amid the COVID-19 pandemic must be urgently addressed to avert a large disease outbreak related to poor or insufficient WASH services.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Multisectoral interventions for Health, Nutrition, WASH, Shelter and NFI interventions would include the following activities:Assessment – prior to any response a thorough assessment of needs in consultation with the affected population, host and local government, and in coordination with other actors and sectors. Tailoring a response to clearly identified needs, considering gender, age, vulnerabilities, protection and conflict sensitivity throughout the planning and implementation of the project. Health & Nutrition – Medair anticipates supporting a PHC-based health facility in Gadaref that will provide integrated health and nutrition services to a catchment population of 10,000. Services will also include a portion of psychosocial support. Due to current staff shortages, the project is expected to be directly implemented. Medair will support the rehabilitation or reconstruction of a facility with latrines. Medical supplies will be supported as well as essential medicines and nutrition supplies through the WHO and WFP pipelines. The facility will also be supported with extra staff as the project will be directly implemented. There will also be staff providing psychosocial services and support. Staff will provide primary care consultations and will refer those in need of more specialized care. Additionally, Medair will also carry out community-based health programming tailored to the local language and culture of the refugees.As a result of ongoing relocation from the border entry points to the demarcated camps in Kassala and Gedaref States, Medair intends to meet the immediate need of the community by undertaking the following activities in Um Raquba refugee camps or other camp location as directed by needs and gaps in service provision. Water Supply – Provision of water treatment tabs (aqua tabs) for household water treatment targeting 500 households for 3 months while ensuring routine Free Residual Chlorine between (0.2 to 1.0mg/l) monitoring to be conducted. This will ensure consumption of safe water at the household level to avert any cases of WASH related illness. In addition, bacteriological water testing will be carried out on sampled households with low or no FRC levels, to determine if the water consumed is free from faecal coliforms. Appropriate mitigation steps will be taken in the event of a failed test. Support the construction of 100 emergency communal latrines of 4-5 stances for the affected population Shelter & NFI – Registration will be conducted with close community involvement, using clearly identified and communicated registration criteria agreed with community leaders. Verification of the list will be done to consider full inclusion and to guard against fraud or duplication of qualified beneficiaries. Distribution – Distribution would be carefully planned and conducted with community participation and would include protection considerations for timing, layout and functional aspects to ensure all beneficiaries can safely access the distribution site. Distributions would ensure fair and equal access to all registered individuals, clear communication before, during and after distribution on the registration criteria, process to receive items, items received and how to report questions or concerns. A complaints mechanism will be clearly set up and available at the distribution site; beneficiaries will be made aware of the complaints mechanism and encouraged to share their complaints.Training will be given at the distribution to explain and demonstrate use and care of the distributed items. Additional training would be given based on items distributed and community needs. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) – A PDM and/or follow up assessment will be conducted to ensure complete and thorough response to the identified needs and to identify gaps, newly emerging needs or concerns within the community in relation to shelter and NFI involving feedback from households and community members.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The environment is considered through the project planning and implementation process. Shelter and NFI has two primary impacts on the environment, these include littering (of items and packaging for distributed) and deforestation (as a result of shelter construction). These impacts are mitigated though intentional assessment, cleanup and planning. In this case a careful cleanup of distribution sites will be done to ensure no litter remains following the distribution and that any packaging is reused or disposed of responsibly. Deforestation will be prevented and/or mitigated though the selection of shelter type, in this case tents have been selected by UNHCR to prevent the depletion of natural resources in the area. Medair would implement shelter programing in line with recommendations of the cluster and UNHCR site leadership.Through the immediate WASH response planned, targeted population will be encouraged to ensure sustainable use of water to avoid wastages at the household, water collection points and reusing waste water for other purposes namely kitchen gardening and cleaning of sanitation facilities. Additionally, through sanitation and hygiene promotion, waste management at household level will be prioritized to ensure that little waste generated is properly managed.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Specific considerations will be made at all stages of the project (assessment, planning, registration, verification, distribution and post distribution monitoring) to ensure fair, safe and equal access for all. This will ensure inclusion of men, women, child headed households, PLWs, PLWDs and the elderly within the consultation of needs, planning and follow up. Considerations will be made, in coordination with community members and relevant organizations to ensure fair, safe and equal access to registration and distribution activities. This will include risk assessments to establish mitigating measures such as timing, location, layout, function and support given at the distribution and or service locations. Follow up after the programing, to assess strength and effectiveness of these measures will be included in the PDM process. Medair will ensure that protection mainstreaming is included throughout all phases of the project. Medair will engage and coordinate with the Camp Management partner and Protection partners in Health, Nutrition, WASH, Shelter and NFI components. Medair will ensure that the needs of vulnerable groups are prioritized and addressed during the interventions where key beneficiaries will be consulted and encouraged to actively participate.
