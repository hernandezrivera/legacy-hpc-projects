Project Summary

This project targets 3,000 children and adolescents over the period January – December 2022, of which 3,000 (55%) are female. H4BF seeks funding to initiate the implementation of the project by focusing on reaching girls and boys in MIFI and Noun in the west region and, Mezam and Donga Mantung in North West region Cameroon. In total, over 3000 girls and boys will access learning opportunities, including some 500 girls and boys in early learning programmes, 1000 at primary level and some 1500 at the secondary level, in both formal and non-formal education settings.
• 3,000 girls and boys will access learning opportunities, including
• 500 girls and boys in early learning programmes,
• 1000 at primary level and
• 1500 at the secondary level
These children will be reached through both formal and non-formal education settings.
H4BF will focus on over 60% of the children, adolescents targeted, and the programme will ensure at least 10% of beneficiaries are children and adolescents with disabilities. 10% support Early Childhood Education for the youngest learners. Over the three-year period, implementation will contribute to achievement the overall EiE Strategic Plan.
This will be achieved through following outcome interventions;
Outcome 1 Increased access to quality, inclusive, holistic education for crisis-affected girls and boys (including adolescents)
Outcome 2-Increased continuity of education for crisis -affected girls and boys (including adolescents) is through improved transitions to formal education from alternative provision
Outcome 3 Existing learning environments are child-friendly, gender-responsive, equitable, disability-friendly and inclusive
Outcome 4 Improved quality of learning for crisis-affected girls and boys, including adolescents
Outcome 5 Safe and protective learning environments for crisis-affected girls and boys are established
Outcome 6 Improved coordination mechanisms to respond to education in emergencies
Outcome 7 Improved rates of enrolment and learning outcomes for girls, including adolescent girls
Outcome 8 Sufficient resources are available to implement all H4BF strategies and enable the handover of the beneficiary caseload to development and government partners
H4BF will complement its interventions and resources to achieve overall goal of H4BF through following key initiatives;
• Continued delivery of strong education in emergencies programming;
• Mainstreaming of learners into formal education;
• Addressing key crosscutting issues, with a special focus on gender, disability and mental health and psychosocial support;
• Strengthening educator and school leader capacities and motivation:
• Strengthening local leadership to take full ownership of delivery and transitions to formal education:

1 - Besoins / Needs

Very little support this year, has been pledged or provided to meet the significant humanitarian needs in Cameroon. A total of 4.4 million people in Cameroon need humanitarian assistance because of on-going and protracted crises rooted in conflict, displacement, and periodic disease outbreaks. The conflict in the NWSW has had repercussions for the education. The whole system has failed in providing access to inclusive learning. An estimated 2,288,000 school aged children have been affected by the crisis. While girls and women are particularly vulnerable due to the combined effect of gender discrimination and socio-economic vulnerability caused by the crisis, leading to specific threats against them (in particular gender-based violence), boys and men are more exposed to arbitrary arrests, forced recruitment and physical violence. Both genders therefore live with psychosocial trauma related to violence, including sexual violence and rape. These multiple crises have a common denominator: children are in distress and teachers lack knowledge and competence to deal with crises affected students. Children are highly affected by conflict, exposed to violence, abuse and exploitation, family separation issues and have significant psychosocial needs. As a result these children easily drop out of school, face early and forced marriage, engage in petty commerce, child labour and other negative coping mechanisms for survival. With the COVID-19 crisis, teachers are being forces to adapt IT skills and learn to work in a remote way, while children continue with home schooling, which adds additional burden on the education system in these areas where structural challenges were already inherent, particularly in the Far North . Conclusively, the Far North continues to face educationally challenges that include i) Access to the Protective Learning Environment, ii)Quality and relevant education services provided by teachers and education personnel with the knowledge and skills to deal with formal and non-formal approached to learning iii) Provision of Teaching and learning materials and iv) Capacity building of teachers on Pedagogy, EiE, PSS and Protection. The percentage of women over 25 with some secondary education is 32.5% compared to 39.2% for men.• Young girls in university studies focus primarily on humanities, social studies, law and social and economic management studies. They represent only 32.3% of the students in sciences, school streams that are more promising in terms of employment• For more than three years, tens of thousands of children in the North-West and SouthWest of Cameroon have not been able to return to school.• In areas still offering schooling possibilities, boys are in some cases privileged when school fees cannot be paid for all: While they go to class, girls are responsible for domestic chores and small trade to support their families. This tendency takes place in a national environment already favoring, before the crisis, the boys ‘education: In elementary school, 110% of girls against 125% of boys are enrolled (gross rate of schooling). In secondary school, the gap widens, with 53% of girls enrolled compared to 65% of boys• Beyond the costs associated with schooling, adolescent girls face additional barriers, particularly in secondary school, mainly because of the burden of household chores. The safety conditions and distances to go to school are also important barriers to education.
