Project Summary

Chronic WASH needs have a significant impact on living standards amongst communities, thereby contributing to several vulnerabilities. Different gender groups are affected uniquely by stressing factors. Low levels of WASH adversely affect coping mechanisms of already vulnerable rural populations. Correlations are evident amongst low WASH and high malnutrition, GBV and food insecurity data sets from the states of Jonglei and Upper Nile.
Christian Mission for Development (CMD) works in Greater Upper Nile and Equatoria providing much needed support to vulnerable communities. The project will be implemented in the counties of Ayod, Duk, Nyirol, Twic East, Bor South and Fangak (Jonglei) and Renk, Nasir, Maiwut and Longechuk (Upper Nile State). CMD envisages an opportunity to provide much needed WASH services through an integrated approach aimed at reducing mortality and morbidity amongst women, girls, boys, men, the elderly and persons with special needs. The project will seek to enhance supply of sustainable access to safe and clean water (15l/p/d), dignified, inclusive and adequate access to sanitation services and opportunities aimed influencing social behavior and norms in relation to personal, household, communal and institutional hygiene.
Community led durable solutions in the provision of services will be sought through the establishment and upgrade of flood resilient WASH infrastructure, the roll out of sustainable initiatives to access to water and sanitation.
A total of 22,222 persons will be reached (58% of these female) in the counties of Ayod, Duk, Nyirol, Twic East, Bor South and Fangak (Jonglei) and Renk, Nasir, Maiwut and Longechuk (Upper Nile State)

1. Needs

Covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks as well as other household-level stressors, like gender-based violence (GBV) and poor WASH services, are some of the key drivers of humanitarian needs in Greater Upper Nile. The states of Jonglei and Upper Nile have some of the lowest standards in WASH, Nutrition, Health, Protection and Livelihoods. The most recent IPC report indicates declining trends in food security and nutrition. The South Sudan FSNMS latest report indicates that the cumulative impact of recurrent flooding have contributed to the destruction of, and damage to water facilities, increasing the likelihood of water-borne diseases and intensifying the existing vulnerabilities of the affected people, which include high poverty rates, widespread displacement and limited access to basic services such as health, nutrition and education. In Upper Nile and Jonglei, access to clean and safe water is below 28%, with over 72% of the water points requiring at least 30 minutes of walking for an average family per the cluster led WASH severity mapping. This not only raises the likelihood of protection related issues for women and girls, but further encourages populations to rely on nearby open surface water points that are unsafe. Settlement patterns further affect water point access equity and equality, since they are mainly determined by flood proneness of the location rather than vicinity to basic services and security. There’s a direct correlation between low WASH standards and high levels of malnutrition amongst U5s, PLW, PLHIV and the elderly. Poor WASH standards also affect school retention of adolescent girls, and contribute to GBV, SEA and food insecurity. Open defecation is widely practiced in both semi-urban and rural populations in Jonglei and Upper Nile; with less than 23% of the households having a standard latrine. This is mainly attributed to costs attributed to setting up flood resistant pit latrines, constant displacements as well as attitudes and knowledge pertaining to hygiene practices. KAP surveys conducted indicate low levels of knowledge related to handwashing, disposal of human and animal waste, personal hygiene, water treatment and management, vector control as well as disease prevention. GAM rates in the locations targeted exceed the global threshold of 15%, and are mainly attributed to low WASH standards and a high disease burden. Water related diseases are seasonally sound during the peak of the rainy season, due to contamination of water points with fecal materials improperly disposed. Locations in parts of Greater Upper Nile lie along the flood plains (Sobat, Nile and Eastern) witness a complete destruction of locally made pit latrines necessitating sustainable end environmentally friendly solutions to waste disposal. Women and girls continue to be exposed to risks when carrying out their routine activities to access their basic needs, which are further exacerbated by flooding that limits livelihood opportunities (e.g., collection of firewood and water). Increased cases of sexual violence against, and harassment of, women and girls were also reported, along with the risks of abduction, killings, revenge killings and threats from armed forces.(UNFPA, 2023)

2. Project activities

The project seeks to provide much needed sustainable, inclusive, gender sensitive WASH services to vulnerable communities in the counties of Ayod, Duk, Nyirol, Twic East, Bor South and Fangak (Jonglei) and Renk, Nasir, Maiwut and Longechuk (Upper Nile State). CMD will ensure provision of adequate and sustainable quantities of water are available for vulnerable groups of persons at communal, household and institutional level through the construction and rehabilitation of flood resistant water points suitable for all gender groups and the provision of household water treatment solutions. The project will work towards the availability of sufficient, accessible and dignified sanitation facilities for vulnerable groups at household, communal and institutional level through the construction and rehabilitation of gender inclusive, dignified and flood resistant sanitation facilities, provision of WASH NFIs to vulnerable groups and communal mobilization for vector control and management. Hygiene awareness modules will be designed and enhanced to ensure increased knowledge and practice in proper hygiene practices and norms through sustainable community approaches to WASH challenges through inclusive and gender sensitive hygiene promotion, distribution and provision of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM), Hygiene and Dignity kits and Enhanced coordination of WASH activities in the counties of Ayod, Duk, Nyirol, Twic East, Bor South and Fangak (Jonglei) and Renk, Nasir, Maiwut and Longechuk (Upper Nile State).
