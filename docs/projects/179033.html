Project Summary

This project will contribute to prevent and treat malnutrition by providing infant and young
child feeding interventions, Micro-nutrient deficiency prevention, control and improve
equitable access to high quality, lifesaving, curative nutrition services through early
identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished children and PLW. The project will be implemented by nutrition Rapid Response Teams (RRTs). This distinctive
RRTs will conduct HH visits and orientation sessions in areas populated with IDPs and
affected people in both Idleb and Aleppo governorates. These RRTs will screen, refer and follow up children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women who are malnourished and provide emergency IYCF counselling for mothers whose children are
moderately malnourished. Moreover, RRTs will distribute nutrition supplements and micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children, pregnant and lactating
women. Also, they will screen the children regarding general danger signs and educate
their mothers about them. They will do hygiene promotion through their activities and will
distribute IEC materials for targeted PLW, girls and boys. There will be 11 nutrition RRTS
teams distributed in various locations in Idleb and Aleppo. Each team will provide basic
nutrition services (MUAC screening, distribution micro-nutrient, and high-energy biscuits, and case referral for treatment). The project duration is 12 months and the target
beneficiaries are 99,500 including boys and girls <5 years, pregnant and lactating women from IDPs and Host communities,
On the other hand, this project is an important foundation for integrated primary health and nutrition services and community-based and outreach activities, it will include a network of RRT, to
provide integrated health and nutrition services focusing on IYCF counseling and
screening of children under 5 and referral for treatment of malnutrition at a community
level. These RRT will be linked with 8 health facilities. This project will also contribute to
treating malnutrition cases in 8 facilities in Idlib and Aleppo.
The project will also focus on building the capacity of the health and nutrition staff on
IYCF counseling to provide integrated health and nutrition services. The health facility
staff and RRT will be trained in aspects related to child health care and nutritional screening and referral, they will also be provided with sufficient education to help to raise
the awareness of the related communities. RRT will cover a big gap resulting from poor coverage of nutritional services in health facilities. QC will provide training on IYCF principles for all staff in RRT and health facilities, and this training will be conducted for 3 times, each time will include 25 people and it will be organized in Syria.

4 - Needs

In 2021, the nutrition sector aims to address urgent and life-saving nutrition needs for 4.9 Million mothers and children in Syria which corresponds to a 4 percent increase in people in need compared to 2021. Most of the nutrition needs lies in high severity areas. Nutrition vulnerabilities in Syria during 2021 are marked with 0.6 Million children who might lose their future learning and developmental potentials because of chronic malnutrition, over one million mothers who are anemic and/or malnourished. Based on the previously mentioned vulnerabilities as well as the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic and the devaluation of local and household economy, QC has conducted the nutrition Needs Assessment in order to capture the needs on the ground. The data was collected through Surveys, Key Informant Interviews, in Idlib and Aleppo districts.Needs assessment targeted the whole community, with a special focusing on vulnerable groups including children under 5 in addition to pregnant and lactating women.With regard to Nutrition, the study which screened the health and nutrition services available in the targeted communities showed that Nutrition services (infant formula, IYCF counselling, MUAC screening, etc.) 52% and micronutrient distribution for (children under five, and PLWs) 62% were between the main types of not available services. The study also revealed that Malnutrition 38%, Child health 37%, Maternal / newborn diseases 23%, Pregnancy complications were between the main health issues faced by the families in the targeted communities.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented by supporting 11 nutrition Rapid Response Teams "RRTs" in high severity communities and IDPs hosting areas. Each RRT will consist of "nutritionist, 2 CHWs, Data entry, nurse and midwife. The teams will provide basic services such as screening of children, pregnant and lactating women for malnutrition signs and referring them to the mobile clinic or OTP or SC, this in addition to PSS cases.Moreover, the RRTs will disseminate IYCF-E messages among mothers of children under five through individual counseling and group sessions, facilitating IYCF-E campaigns and integrate it to the Child Health Days, registering all screened cases and reporting to thenutritionists and data entry team member. In addition, the RRTs will distribute micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children and pregnant and lactating women, monitoring distribution and use of breast milk substitutes and distributenutrition supplements. The RRTs will disseminate messages (IEC/BCC)on IYCF-E recommended feeding practices, support hygiene promotion through raising WASH awareness by providing key messages such as Hand Washing, Food Processing, etc.,identifying the abused cases among boys and girls 6-59months, pregnant and lactating women and refer them to child protection GBV partners for appropriate action. QC ensures programs and projects are monitored, controlled and evaluated with a recommendation toimprove the services delivered in the targeted areas. The MEAL structure at present in QC consists of; MEAL Coordinator, and MEAL Field Assistants. The Field assistants work inside Syria, make visits to all projects' locations and report the work progress.They are responsible for data collection

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Gender-Based Violence (GBV) affects accessing nutrition services. MitigatingMeasures: Provide nutrition services in accessible easy to reach areas. Training ofnutrition RRTs on Prevention and management of sexual violence. The presence of femalestaff in the nutrition teams’ increases access of women and girls to the nutrition services.Risk: Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) of women: Mitigating measures: Establisheffective community-based complaints mechanisms, awareness-raising on SEA amongstnutrition team members, with continuous training on the topic. Performance managementand monitoring of all team members. Risk: Lack of Duty of care for Nutrition RRTs.Mitigating measures: DOC policy in place and applied for all staff, measures ensuringregular and consistent delivery of salaries, bearing in mind the ethical and logisticalimperative to go further than basic labor rights. Risk: Inequitable access to nutritionservices by Vulnerable Groups. Mitigating measures: Implementation of standardessential services package across the sector interventions, including improved qualityreporting and robust monitoring through standardized SOPs. Addressing the specificneeds of vulnerable groups through supporting dedicated nutrition subgroups groups(IYCF, CMAM, Capacity Building...etc.)

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Through all project locations, environmental risks (foods, earthquakes, etc.) may have an effect on the implementation of the activities in the case that a major disaster happens in one of the areas of implementation. The likelihood of this risk to happen is low. While the impact on project activities will be very high. QC through its staff will have a preparedness plan and mitigation mechanisms in all the activities and this to be shared with all staff and schools’ principals. In this intervention, QC assumes that the implementation of the activities will go smoothly in all targeted locations. In regard to this, some risks might be beyond QC control. 1) The reinforcement of health facilities does not provide sufficient protection for patients, exposing them to physical harm (some nutrition activities are provided within health facilities). * These can be mitigated by putting in place taskshifting guidelines as part of care protocols - providing Training and adequate follow- up of competency - decentralizing services, clear messaging to communities around risks associated with health care facilities and underground care 2) Increased malnutrition rate due to increasing the opportunity of diseases and medical conditions that needs professional intervention. * Mitigating measure: - Hire a qualified medical staff - Distribute nutritional supplies and rations. After the Sushi treaty between Russia and Turkey, QC assumes that areas out of Syrian government control in northwest Syria will be stable for the whole of next year. The risk of increasing pressure on health services influx of IDPs is very high. Program activities will be negatively impacted when the number of IDPs in some areas suddenly increases
