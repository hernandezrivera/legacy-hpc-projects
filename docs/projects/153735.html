Project Summary

CHADO will Conduct 6 community dialogue and awareness meetings project locations to introduce project activities, outputs, stakeholder’s roles and responsibilities as an AAP strategy. CHADO proposes to build the capacity of 120 (60 female) community social services provider including local authorities and community leaders (Teachers, social workers, clergy, CPCs, etc.) and strengthen their ability to protect, prevent and respond to the core rights violations of children especially those affected by the conflict and support their participation in peace building activities and community-based referral pathways. About (6) CFS's will be established and equipped with child learning and recreational materials.
CHADO will provide comprehensive case management for 250 most vulnerable children
CHADO resolves to conduct family tracing and reunification of 250 UASC. During the implementation of the project, CHADO propose to train caregivers (750 female and 250 male) on proper child care.
Through coordination CHADO will establish and strengthen referrals of children and also provide direct psychosocial support to 8,400 children (including disabled children) affected by conflict.
In a bid to strengthen the reporting mechanism, CHADO will Conduct 6 CP monitoring and contribute information to CPiE and share reports with relevant clusters
Through awareness raising sessions, CHADO will carry out 6 CP campaigns to support girls and boys in vulnerable situations through Case management and available specialized referral services, in addition 6 other awareness dialogue sessions on prevention of recruitment of children into the armed forces and groups targeting children, their families and communities through displacement villages, churches and schools will also be conducted.
Following the release of 707 children formerly associated with armed forces and group and other vulnerable children below 18 years 250 will receive school kits to go back to school to protect and reintegrate them into formal learning. Another 300 children formerly associated with armed forces and group and other vulnerable children will be provided with life skills and resilience training and comprehensive sexuality education (UNESCO guidelines). In addition livelihood programs, will be provided to sustain the youths in day to day life.
To create resilience, CHADO will Mainstream Child Protection and psychosocial support services in schools through 8 capacity building events that will enhance knowledge and skills of teachers on how to support and encourage children through provision of life skills.

Needs

In 2018, the conflict continued to force people to remain on the move and undermine their access to assistance. Almost 4.5 million people have been displaced, including approximately 2.5 million in neighbouring countries and nearly 2 million internally. Approximately 61% of the affected population are children, of which 1.9 million boys and girls will face acute and severe protection risks in 2019. Boys and girls continue to be exposed to threats of recruitment, psychosocial distress, family separation, abuse, neglect, exploitation, and sexual and physical violence. Since 2013, over 100,000 children in South Sudan have been affected by 3,500 verified Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism incidents, of which killing and maiming accounted for 12%; denial of humanitarian access 10 ten per cent; rape and other grave sexual violence accounted for 8 per cent; abduction 8 per cent; and attacks on schools and hospitals 7%. Over 19,000 children are estimated to have been recruited to armed forces and armed groups (South Sudan Draft HNO 2019).Most parts of Western Equatoria State, have experienced a series of population displacement since mid-2015 when fighting broke out between government forces and armed rebel groups. The wave of ¬fighting and general insecurity spread from Yambio to Nagero and the rest of the State. Yambio town has been hosting many displaced communities around the State in different periods despite being directly affected by sporadic fighting too (DTM- South Sudan Assessment Report 7th–12th April 2018). More so, Nagero and Yambio have had continuous cases of child recruitment in armed forces. In the process of recruitment and their involvement in the military ranks, these children have witnessed horror and killings; have been exposed to protection risks including injuries wounds, violence, abuse, exploitation, neglect and discrimination.The communities in Yambio, Western Equatoria state where children have been recruited, live in prolonged conflict that results in an overwhelming wide spread displacement, collapse of livelihoods, poverty and breakdown of family, community structures and absence of law and order. As a result most of these children have not had opportunities to go to formal school and most of them are not able to read and write. Some of them had worked exploitative jobs before they joined the militant which furthers their grievances, others have not known to their biological families and lived with relatives.From CHADO protection data, children remain at risk of recruitment by armed forces and groups and, with traditional social structures damaged, they are also increasingly vulnerable to violence, sexual abuse and exploitation. The recruitment and use of children as soldiers is a clear violation against International legal instruments such: Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC), the Optional Protocol of the CRC on the recruitment and use of children, Geneva Protocols, IHL , Paris Principles and Guidelines on children associated with armed forces or armed groups and the Paris Commitments to protect children from unlawful recruitment . The rehabilitation and reintegration of these children is an integral component of stabilizing South Sudan and implementing the peace agreement. CHADO envisages that the process of reintegration presents opportunities for these children to attain normality in their lives, and establish systems of protection and support without which they will be at further risk of being re-victimized through re-recruitment and other protection formsIn Central Equatoria conflict affected and displaced children have often been exposed to physical threats. Without protection and support their physical survival has threatened, and their status puts them at high risk of abuse, exploitation, forced labour, abduction, or recruitment into armed forces.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

A broad spectrum of the community will participate in decision-making in a way that is inclusive, non-discriminatory and provides direct influence in the humanitarian response.This influence will be achieved through commitment of CHADO in communication approaches that include clear and direct feedback loops between programme design and management and the communities themselves. Protection of the most vulnerable population will further be enhanced because the disaggregated risks/needs of the diversity of the community will be recognized; this will enable a more targeted response. The drivers of the conflict will then be mitigated, thereby reducing the risk.CHADO will institutionalize feedback mechanism to allow beneficiaries, girls and boys, women and men including persons with disabilities to voice their successes, as well as complaints and concerns to stakeholders on quality and impact of the project as a means to measure accountability to affected population. The action will launch regular, structured dialogues to create platforms for target groups to engage with local government officials, service providers and CHADO partners. This will foster a sense of mutual trust and encourage teamwork from planning, implementation and project review. Context specific and culturally sensitive feedback plan or social accountability guidelines will comprise a number of sub-activities such as beneficiary scorecards about the services, complaint collection boxes; displaying activities and budget of this action in visible places, periodic joint review sessions of beneficiaries and the local authorities. Other feedback plans will include: Joint stakeholder field monitoring visits, freedom of information petitions, community monitoring of service delivery and participatory budgeting.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All gender and age groups will receive appropriate contextual messages of their age group and gender which are also culturally appropriate. The messages will be tailored to foster understanding of the project by all groups of beneficiaries. At such they will be able to make informed choices or decisions on how they will want CHADO to implement the project. Historical, political and social dynamics within and between gender and age-groups, impact on the crisis and protection risks; Therefore CHADO through a sustained approach will ensure that there is improved interaction/communication by different age groups and gender to reduce protection and tailored messages using proven community approaches such as FGD's and open community forums or meetings.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

CHADO has ratified and endorsed the UN PSEA policy and uses it as a code of conduct document signed by all employees, volunteers and contractors. CHADO has it own child safe guiding policy adhered strictly by all its staff and volunteers.Staff members will promote the human rights of all people and may not discriminate on the basis of an individual’s race, color, ethnicity, national origin, religion, age, political affiliation, gender, sexual orientation, marital status, pregnancy, or disability.Staff members will be accountable to local, national, and international laws and are aware of the consequences for violating these laws.Sexual exploitation, sexual abuse, corruption, trafficking of adults or children, forced early marriage and other abuse by staff members constitute acts of gross misconduct and will result in disciplinary action, up to and including immediate termination of employment.In accordance with international law, sexual activity with a child (a person under age 18) is prohibited regardless of the age of majority or age of consent locally. Mistaken belief in the age of a person is not a defense. Staff members are prohibited from having sexual relationships with beneficiaries. These relationships are often based on unequal power dynamics and may undermine the credibility and integrity of humanitarian work.This includes the exchange of money, employment, goods, or services for sex, including sexual favors or other forms of humiliating, degrading or exploitative behavior, for selection as a beneficiary or for assistance as a beneficiary.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All activities are designed to eliminate all forms of discrimination against the beneficiaries. CHADO child protection programme is designed to reach girls and boys who are most at risk of, or who have experienced violence, abuse, exploitation, or neglect. Beneficiaries will include approximately 55 per cent girls and 45 per cent boys, and programme activities are specifically designed to assess and meet the unique needs of both girls and boys and their caregivers to ensure the most appropriate care and child friendly services. While girls and boys may share some negative consequences of separation, there are also specific gender-related risks. For example, girls are more likely to face forced marriage, sexual violence, exploitation, and abuse, whilst boys often face coerced or forced recruitment into armed forces and groups, child rights violations when in contact or in conflict with the law, arbitrary detention, and difficulties in foster care placement due to societal perceptions about male youth. Thus, CHADO will utilizes local and national advocacy, parental support for temporary caregivers, routine monitoring visits, and other community based mechanisms to prevent abuse and neglect and intervene to reduce girls’ and boys’ vulnerability; and, through onsite coaching and mentoring, seeks to ensure that communities and children are aware of the gender specific risks to the children. CHADO will also collects and analyzes data disaggregated by sex and age to track trends and modify programming as often as necessary
