Project Summary

Strengthened response for and support to sexual, physical, and emotional violence survivors through provision of integrated services.
In addition to the above, violation of the most basic human rights continues to occur across the North East of Nigeria as armed conflict persists. In the last few years, with escalation of violence, negative coping practices have increased, exposing groups at risk to further abuses, including gender-based violence. Vulnerabilities are further endured by limited access to protective shelters and NFIs, limited or non-existent access to food or livelihood opportunities and the population’s increased fear of being targeted by surrounding violence. The GBV needs and risk assessment intended to gain an overview of humanitarian needs, and GBV in particular, in hard to reach areas where the humanitarian presence is currently not addressing extensive gaps. The assessment sought to measure the level of understanding of GBV and protection related principles among the population in those areas, as well as the frequency and primary causes for incidents of GBV.
The greatest protection challenge for the communities assessed remains urgent access to safety which is not guaranteed at this time namely due to continued sporadic attacks by armed actors on the displaced community and perpetration of violence against vulnerable people, specifically sexual violence and exploitation by a variety of actors. According to the HRP policy for 2019-2021, the situation facing women and girls is particularly dire. Violence against women and girls, including sexual violence, and exposure to trafficking and abduction is widespread but underreported. Some 99% of reported GBV incidents in the first half of 2018 were made by women and girls
INTEROS will ensure protection mainstreaming and will address cross-cutting protection needs, whilst delivering essential services to the most vulnerable households in target communities in health, nutrition and WaSH. At every stage of the project, Women and Girls will be consulted separately through FGDs conducted by women, to freely express their needs. Participatory sessions will be used to access and incorporate gender equality and age perspectives in all aspects of the project cycle, with a specific focus on gender during the design phases, with community discussion and assessments mandatorily conducted with groups of men and groups of women. As well, protection mainstreaming and referral pathways among the partners will be ensured throughout the intervention.

1 - Needs

Due to overcrowding and stresses of living in shared basic conditions, IDP sites and host communities often experience higher levels of tension and violence. A recent assessment conducted by REACH found that populations in Northern Borno had the highest proportion of households with unmet protection needs (80%) . Such a phenomenon has led to a deteriorating security situation for the most vulnerable members of each community particularly women and girls among others. Within these risks abound internally due to the merging of disparate communities, the transient nature of the populations and the breakdown of traditional social protection mechanisms. Such phenomena and risks require a direct protection response.Moreover, during INTERSOS needs assessment, the people interviewed said that the main safety concerns for women and girls are no safe place in the community (22%), sexual violence/abuse (27%), violence in the home (by another family member) (14%), trafficking (2%). Safety concern for Women and Girls is largely linked to the risk of travelling outside the community at an average of 36% across all 5 LGAs. No safe place in the community is especially a concern for women and girls in Ngala (42%) and Magumeri (38%), while in Bama and Dikwa the major source of concern is sexual violence/abuse at a proportion of 61% and 48% respectively. Also, from this survey respondents said 36.3% of survivors of sexual abuse seek help while 46.3% do not seek help. This survey indicates that other than sexual violence/abuse types the most common is Physical violence at an average of 60%. When participants were asked where they would usually seek support 36% will seek support and 46% will not seek support. 29.6% of respondents will seek support from traditional leaders while 7.4% go to the police and only 3.7% refer to an NGO.Overall, during the assessment, field observations highlighted the lack of coherent support to GBV survivors and women in general in the targeted areas. Insufficient GBV related psychosocial support services was observed as well as poor judiciary system to prosecute perpetrators of violence, which diminished keenness of survivors to report instances of violence.

2 - Activities

The protection intervention aims at both providing the targeted communities with quality service to respond to current protection needs but also contributing to strengthen their capacity for self-protection reinforcing positive coping mechanisms to stressful events. INTERSOS will ensure that protection is mainstreamed throughout all phases of the implementation in each sector of intervention, ensuring that the affected population has safe and dignified access to services provided in an impartial way and that those are provided based on the population real needs through strictly following confidentiality, respect, do no harm and accountability principles. Sector-level Coordination: INTERSOS will coordinate all crisis response activities with the local authorities including the LGA authorities and community protection management committees. In addition, INTERSOS will coordinate with appropriate pillars in sector coordination. INTERSOS will continue to liaise with other international actors regarding planned activities to avoid duplication of services. INTERSOS will ensure coordination with the Protection Sector WG, the CP, GBV, MHPSS Sub-sectors WG for sharing of information (through among others GBVIMS reports), trends analysis and gaps on a monthly basis through active participation into monthly meetings as well as ad-hoc meetings. This will support informed intervention, tracking and analysis of gaps, and improve service provision to the population. INTERSOS will coordinate with UNFPA and GBV Case Management Technical WG, the GBV WG and other partners to receive proper training for all staff to ensure quality in the treatment facilities. INTERSOS main goal is to provide protection to vulnerable women and girls who are at-risk or GBV survivors. Beneficiaries in need of GBV prevention or/and response services will have access to INTERSOS GBV activities by disseminating information about the services through work of community sensitization, Women and Girls Friendly Space activities. New GBV cases will also be facilitated through referrals from other humanitarian actors operating in the same target areas. Individuals including vulnerable women, girls and boys will be ensured with improved access to GBV services and referral mechanisms in a safe and confidential environment. Protection team will provide technical support to health, nutrition, and WaSH staff to incorporate protection mainstreaming into their operations and response plans. Moreover, direct awareness and sensitisation over GBV principles, behavioral acceptance of good practices and relevant rights will be provided to community leaders through specific workshops, while at the same time to additional key community members through large scale events organised by project’s beneficiaries.INTERSOS intends to provide safe and confidential disclosure, survivor-centered case management services for GBV cases including for women at risk.
