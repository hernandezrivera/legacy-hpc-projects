Project Summary

Ethiopia continues to experience critical humanitarian needs and displacement, exacerbated by recurrent crises caused by environmental and conflict drivers. The multiple crises in Ethiopia have affected the ability of humanitarian organizations to respond quickly and in a meaningful manner, as well as the capacity of affected communities to deal with multiple shocks and secondary displacement. In addition, the conflict in northern Ethiopia has led to a significant increase in internal displacement, as well as spillover effects creating increased tensions in Amhara, Afar, and Somali regions, and heightening the targeting of ethnic groups in different parts of the country.
IDPs and IDP returnees face substantial protection risks, including threats of human rights violations before and during displacement, lack of meaningful access to essential services, loss of HLP rights, including civil documentation, and protracted displacement with high risks of secondary displacement or lack of durable solutions. Protracted displacement also serves as a conflict driver, due to increased tensions with host communities around resource scarcity, local integration, and inequalities in service provision.
As such, DRC proposes a comprehensive protection response to support populations affected by crises, inclusive of those affected by conflict, violence, disasters, or climate change, and those at risk of displacement; this includes host communities, IDPs, returnees, and refugees in and out of camp settings. DRC's programming will focus on supporting displacement-affected populations in Tigray, Somali, and Gambella region, as well as refugee and returnee response in Addis Ababa; further, DRC will provide an integrated protection and emergency response to affected communities in Afar or elsewhere through a mobile response capacity.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

This project complements ongoing DRC protection programming, as well as proposed WASH activities which will target the same populations, ensuring that both interventions are designed to be conflict sensitive and meet the priority needs of the populations. The proposed intervention has been coordinated and reviewed with the relevant stakeholders in the field and at national level. DRC actively engages in coordination with all key actors and fora at the national and subnational levels; to strengthen coordination and response in relation to the IDP response DRC hosts the HINGO Forum and the National Protection Cluster co-coordinator, as well as serving as a member of the Protection Cluster SAG. At the subnational level, DRC is an active participant of the Somali Protection Cluster, including sharing of protection monitoring findings and working to support advocacy efforts to build the protective environment.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

The project ensures active engagement of beneficiaries from project design, implementation through monitoring and evaluation. It also integrates lessons learned from past programs and solicits feedback and participation from target communities to ensure acceptance and community buy-in. DRC is committed to ensuring accountability to affected populations and follows the 2010 Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) guidelines for accountability to affected populations. Compliance with Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) help to ensure greater beneficiary representation in all stages of a project and improve access to feedback and complaints mechanisms. The project will conduct a context assessment in the project sites to design a contextually appropriate information sharing, feedback and complaints mechanism. As per commitment 4 of the CHS “communities and people affected by crisis know their rights and entitlements, have access to information and participate in decisions that affect them,” the project requires that information is shared with beneficiaries through a combination of site-level kick-off meetings and awareness raising. In addition to general project information, DRC will share information on beneficiary entitlements, organizations’ Code of Conduct (CoC) and how to use the organizations’ feedback response mechanism. Secondly, as per commitment 5 of the CHS, “communities and people affected by crisis have access to safe and responsive mechanisms to handle complaints,” the project will use multiple platforms through which beneficiaries can provide feedback including direct feedback collection by trained staff, through periodic help desk sessions and through feedback boxes. All feedback collected through these platforms will be channeled to field-based MEAL Officer and Area Manager as well as the Addis Ababa-based Accountability Officer. All feedback received will be analyzed, responded to in the most appropriate manner and recorded in a database held at the country office. Responses and corrective actions are equally recorded. A complaint handling committee will be established, strengthened and supported in refugee camps, IDP sites and host communities. Staff will be oriented on complaints handling and the community will be informed on ways of handling various types of complaints and how to make complaints safely/anonymously without having to fear any repercussions. DRC will provide accessible and timely information to affected populations on DRC procedures, structures, and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices. DRC will establish feedback and complaint that actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate, and robust enough to deal with complaints about breaches in policy. DRC will ensure the community plays an active role in the decision-making process through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized (women and children) and affected are represented and have influence. Prompt response to complaints will be made. DRC will organize awareness raising sessions so that people know how it works and will consider a joint complaints mechanism with other sectors (e.g. Protection) to minimize confusion. AAP will be jointly managed by the protection and MEAL teams of DRC in Somali.
