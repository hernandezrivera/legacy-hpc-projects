Project Summary

The proposed intervention will conduct protection monitoring, referral and provide assistance to the 60% most vulnerable IDPs, 20% returnees (both IDP and refugee returnees,) and 20%Host Community in Kapoeta East County in Eastern Equitoria state. The project will build on the protection work ADRA previously implemented in Kapoeta East focusing on ensuring that conflict-affected persons have increased access to rights and entitlements. Through the project, ADRA South Sudan will facilitate protection monitoring to determine the threats, needs and concerns of the target population to inform individualized support which will include and not limited to identification and assistance to PSNs, case management, cash based support to PSNS e.g survivors of SGBV, children at risk and referral to the appropriate services. It will be noted SGBV remains one of the critical threats to the protection and wellbeing of women and children with unclear magnitude because these acts are not culturally recognized as crime and therefore often ignored and not reported by both the community members and the local authorities. This intervention will therefore focus on monitoring these human rights violations, reporting and advocating for mechanisms to mitigate their occurrence. The intervention will also facilitate dialogues on peaceful coexistence since violence is one of the drivers to threats faced by the communities which largely compromise the protective environment of the affected populations. ADRA will facilitate training sessions on human rights, protection and protection mainstreaming targeting the duty bearers such as local leaders, youth, Faith based organizations, teachers and staff of humanitarian partners . The intervention will be implemented to deliver activities under integrated components of protection, GBV and child protection. The integrated protection interventions will seek to address the vulnerability, limited access to services, psychosocial distress, peace building and weak protection structures that continue to characterize the IDPs, returnee’s and host community context in Kapoeta East county especially among persons of concern including the elderly, persons with disability, pregnant women and lactating mothers, Unaccompanied and separated children, former child soldiers, child-headed households and girls at risk of forced and early marriage. ADRA and partner led assessments reveal that women and child headed household are at risk of exploitation as they try to cope with the effects of protracted crisis hence they will be a primary target by the proposed intervention. The UNHCR vulnerability criteria will be used during identification of the beneficiaries as it clearly indicates the categories of individuals to benefit from the proposed intervention. For instance the The proposed project will focus on the persons with specific needs in which category the elderly fall. Households where the elderly are caretakers will be targeted for assistance as well as families that are fostering the elderly, orphans and accompanied/separated children. Additionally, the persons living with disabilities (PLWD) will be included for assistance. During any activities, deliberate action will be taken to ensure participation of PLWD. The youth who are more often being violet activities like cattle raiding and intercommunal fighting including and not limited to rape, will be targeted with peace conferences, dialogues and quick impact projects like sports for peace. They will be linked to livelihood services that disrupts their minds from criminal activities. The children will not only linked to schools but also engaged through recreational and psychosocial activities to deal with the trauma suffered through witnessing violence in homes and community. Particular focus will be paid to orphans separated and unaccompanied children who will be monitored and provided assistance which will include family tracing, cash based support, PSS and referral

1 - Needs

According to OCHA’s (2019) Humanitarian Needs Overview for 2020, over 112,000 people in the county have significant humanitarian needs, which is approximately 74% of the estimated population for Kapoeta East County. The County is one of the biggest in Eastern Equatoria State with 7 payams of Narus, Natinga, Katodori, Kauto, Lotimor, Mogos and Jie.It is mainly inhabited by Toposa people who are mainly pastoralist who get into conflict internally and with their neighbors in other counties and neighboring countries like Kenya over water and pastures for their animals. The insecurity resulting from the conflict compromises the protective environment leading to displacements, loss of lives and loss of property. This increases vulnerability of the communities. According to needs assessments conducted by ADRA and other partners in the targeted location, the communities needs are recorded as follows: protection from armed conflict and GBV, lack of S/NFIs, inadequate food and lack of livelihood opportunities, inadequate access to education for the children as well as inadequate access to health and WASH services. For instance, in April 2019, a team from WHO and the Ministry of Health went to investigate 6 unexplained deaths in the county. The investigation revealed that the only health facility in the area had not been operating for two years, leaving residents with few options when faced with even common health risks such as malaria and malnutrition. Assessment conducted by ADRA also revealed Kapoeta East County as having the highest cases and prevalence GBV in the payams of Narus, Katodori, Kauto, and Mogos. The rigid cultural norms, power imbalance and the patriarchal dominance of in households or family, resources and property like land ownership and inheritance continue to expose women and girls to abuse. It is common practice for girls to be forced into marriage without being given an opportunity to choose partner. For instance, there are 65 young girls taking refuge at St. Bakhita Day and Boarding Girls primary schools for safety having ran from forced marriages perpetuated by their parents and male siblings. Domestic violence is also very prevalence as well as rape incidences with survivors being forced to marry their perpetrators. This therefore requires monitoring and advocacy for enforcement of laws that protect women and girls. Child neglect is very common in Kapoeta East county. The communities are mainly pastoralist hence they hardly have time to provide quality parenting to the children. During conflict, boys as young as 10-14 years are likely to be recruited in armed conflict. The needs of children are enormous, while some are orphaned due to war and diseases, others lack basic needs to survive into adulthood, others are separated and unaccompanied lacking parental guidance and more often than not they engage in criminal activities for survival. Such children are also used by traders for cheap labor. In such an environment, the rights of children are abused and go unreported. Other needs include poor judicial systems that are biased to female gender. Children who face abuse including rape and abduction lack legal systems that could protect them. Lack of livelihood opportunities and idleness among the youth is a driver to conflict in the county. These youths need to be engaged in peaceful coexistence as well as livelihood activities to engage in productive activities that would turn their attention from criminal activities. Poor communication network, low capacity and inadequate logistical support for leaders to respond and prevent human right violations has further lead to increased abuse of human rights which need to be monitored and report. The local leaders need to be trained on human rights, protection and protection monitoring. CP and SGBV concept. The impact of COVID 19 and climate change have also continued to put communities at increased vulnerabilities.

2 - Project activities

Protection Monitoring-The proposed intervention will monitor through 120 Key informant interviews and biweekly monitoring missions at the community and household to track threats, risks and needs of the vulnerable 1000 individuals from IDPs and Returnees. Material support to PSNs-Through the project,300 persons with specific needs will be targeted and provided cash based and material support based on the identified needs. ADRA will strengthen 1 referral pathway to increase access to services in the project locations. Capacity building -60 local and community leaders and 100 youth 20 per payam to be trained on peace building and conflict resolution and will act as peace agents in their communities. Case management ADRA will provide case management to 100 boys and girls 200 women and girls to determine specialized support. To address the rampant SGBV needs of the community, ADRA will establish 1 Women and girls friendly centres and support 400 women to access PSS activities and support 300 women and girls with livelihood .The action will refer 25 women and girls to access GBV services.ADRA will also procure and distribute reusable hygiene kits to 500 women and girls. At all times, the vulnerable persons will be linked to the available services utilizing the referral pathways. To mitigate the risk of exposing the communities to the risk of SEA and GBV, the intervention will target 20 staff of organizations with capacity building activitiesIn order to promote protection of children in Kapoeta East county, ADRA will identify and train 60 women and men and engage them in parenting sessions. ADRA will establish 1 child friendly space where children will conduct psychosocial and recreation activities. ADRA will identify and reunify 10 unaccompanied children during the project period. To identify and respond to the PSS activities, ADRA will recruit the social workers to conduct case management and refer the children who require specialized support to the counselors. At the CFS, the recreational activities will be conducted by the volunteers.For protection, GBV and CP activities, ADRA will work closely with other implementing partners to refer cases and ensure holistic support to beneficiaries. The proposed intervention will be implemented through lenses of age, gender and disability mainstreaming. Every effort will be made to ensure persons living with disability will be included in every aspect of the intervention to participate in the activities. Awareness raising will be conducted to ensure the 3,400 targeted population (1000 under the GP, 1400 under GBV and 1000 CP) has the right information to access services and report violations when they do happen.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will be collect feedback form the community taking into consideration the most disadvantaged members of the community such as the elderly, persons living with disability, women and girls. For instance during community dialogue sessions and FGD sessions to collect feedback, every effort will be made to include these marginalized groups. The community will be sensitized on the vulnerability criteria and informed the percentage of representation for the most vulnerable groups. Women and girls will form 60% of the organized community dialogues to collect feedback. Also ADRA will mobilize homogeneous groups sessions to ensure women can have a free environment to speak up and voice their feedback whenever it may seem they cannot speak freely in a mixed group.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

At the inception of the project, ADRA will conduct start up work shop and inception meetings with stakeholders to create awareness on the new project. In order to reach a wider audience, ADRA will make announcements through the radio to create awareness of the project and the activities to be implemented focusing on the locations, vulnerability criteria and provide contacts of the persons and the channels that beneficiaries could use to register their complaints. The project will also conduct meetings with the local leaders at the county and payam level as well as awareness sessions with the targeted communities to inform them about the project and feedback/complaint mechanisms while seeking their feedback on the preferred mode of reporting complaints to be incorporated. Through focus group discussions, the project will collect views of the community on the preferred methods of communicating complaints and receiving feedback. The project will also use informal community dialogue sessions to listen to the community complaints and provide feedback for the complaints that are addressed. In the project locations, complaint/feedback committees will be established and trained to receive complaints from the community and be another medium to communicate feedback to the affected community. Age gender and diversity will be considered while collecting complaints and providing feedback. Hence, all the proposed protection related activities will be implemented in a participatory manner using the community-based protection approach. For instance, during the monitoring and awareness activities, groups of men, women, boys and girls will be targeted through the lenses of inclusivity. ADRA will engage the communities in data collection to ensure the interventions are designed to respond to their needs that they identify during focus group discussions (FGD), Key informant interviews (KII) and door to door visits. The leaders who are trained in human rights will be supported to implement work plans to share messages with other community members. The project team will strengthen the capacity of community-based protection networks in identification and addressing their protection issues, as well as providing assistance on those issues where such capacity in communities is lacking. During the protection monitoring, the protection will interact with persons of concern to find out the protection issues in the community, collect data using protection-monitoring tools, conduct focus group discussions with protection stakeholders and identify protection or human rights violations and protection gaps. All activities will also be implemented while paying attention to safety and dignity of the beneficiaries recognizing and addressing barriers to meaningful access to beneficiaries especially for the most at risk groups such as women and girls, elderly, persons living with disabilities, child headed households, orphans and unaccompanied children. The project will make every effort to involve the beneficiaries through out the project cycle. The reports generated from the project activities will be shared in a timely manner with the relevant clusters at the county, state and national level ensuring compliance to reporting and data protection procedures.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Information about PSEA will be provided during community awareness sessions and use of pictorial IEC materials that that will be developed and posted in publics locations. The duty bearers, such as members of community networks, community leaders, and local leaders, Teachers, heads of clubs in schools, staff of organizations PTAs/SMCs and members of the FBOs will be trained on PSEA and tasked to mainstream the information in their activities at the community. Public gatherings and any partner distribution will be used as avenues to share SEA related messages with the community

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Through out the project cycle, ADRA will seek to get feedback from the beneficiaries about the satisfaction of the project and collect feedback on their needs and how best they can be met.From the consultative discussions, ADRA will use the information to inform the relevant interventions including and not limited to choosing when to use cash and/or in-kind assistance. Locations for any distributions will be selected after taking into consideration the feedback provided by the members of the community regarding their safety.
