Project Summary

In response to the growing humanitarian crisis in Borno state, Mercy Corps (MC) is proposing gender-based violence (GBV) response and prevention interventions to expand lifesaving GBV, and protection services to vulnerable women and girls in Borno state, Nigeria. Based on the complexity of the operating environment, MC understands that any GBV and protection interventions in the locations would require a multifaceted approach that is effective, age appropriate, and synergetic in nature to comprehensively address risks of GBV to women and girls. The proposed project will aim to strengthen the prevention of, and response to, gender-based violence (GBV) for 8600 women and girls individuals from vulnerable, displaced, returnee, and host communities through coordinated, principled humanitarian support in Bama and Dikwa local government areas (LGAS). This intervention will address immediate needs for GBV response, strengthening community mechanisms, and improving the dignity and wellbeing of women and girls in the proposed LGAS.
The proposed activities will include;
- Increasing women and adolescent girls’ access to life-saving GBV case management, psychosocial support (PSS) services, and protection to reduce risks of GBV. Mercy Corps will also ensure 2500 women and girls have access to menstrual hygiene services and dignity kits in Borno to empower women and girls. To support adolescent girls, Mercy Corps will operate community-based non-static healing safe spaces for adolescents. This will facilitate their recovery, restore sense of normalcy and promote engagement with peer support networks and referral to Mercy Corps livelihoods and vocational skills programming as an entry point to develop future intervention.
- Strengthening local-level GBV coordination and capacity for GBV actors and services, to reduce the barriers in accessing GBV response services, and to mitigate GBV impacts among women and girls in Bama and Dikwa. MC collaborates with local-actors to strengthen referral mechanisms, and to improve coordination and capacity building for GBV focal points in Bama and Dikwa.
- Promoting GBV awareness and working to prevent GBV, including domestic violence. MC will work to increase men and boys’ participation in GBV campaigns and prevention efforts in Bama and Dikwa. By creating gender champions, MC will collaborate with men and boys as key actors in addressing GBV including domestic violence to create awareness in communities including with religious and traditional leaders.
- Integrating protection monitoring and mainstreaming into the GBV intervention to improve the overall context analysis and integration of protection principles in humanitarian response in Borno

1 - Needs

Violence against women and girls has been one of the most visible faces of the current crisis. Findings from MC’s gender and risk assessment in Borno (MCs Gender Risks and Vulnerability Assessments, November 2019), indicate that women and girls have seen increased risk of violence due to the increased presence of military actors and non-state actors, mass displacement, and limited and shrinking humanitarian response. Rape, sexual exploitation, abduction, and intimate partner violence are widespread and GBV services are very limited. The continued attacks on civilian targets escalate food insecurity and increase risks of GBV, as women and girls have to walk longer distances in search of food or are forced into exploitative situations to survive. MC reviewed the protection sector and GBV sub-sector (SS) situational reports for 2019 and 2020 to identify how this proposed project can best help spur access to protection and GBV services for women and girl. MC has previously used sub GBV SS reports to identify the needs for younger women are a hard to reach population in Damboa, Ngala, and Dikwa. In addition, MC has also reviewed existing GBV SS findings including the report “SGBV in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states January –June 2018.” This report revealed high rates of sexual and gender based violence in Bama and Dikwa, with Bama coming just second after Pulka in the ranking. The availability of trained GBV personnel in host communities in Bama to provide basic PSS continues to be insufficient. Few women and girls have access to lifesaving GBV services and in some locations, women are unable to access post rape care due to a lack of trained staff or confidential referrals. Both LGAs have been affected by prolonged conflict. According to MC gender risk and vulnerability assessment, IDPs and returnees have suffered from repeated displacement due to shifting conflict lines and the search for basic commodities. Services in the two LGAs are basic, and individuals are at risk of relying on negative coping mechanisms. According to the FAO NE Nigeria protection analysis report January 2019, adolescent girls and women continue to face heightened risks of GBV, including domestic violence, forced/early marriage, and exploitation and abduction. MC Programme implementation also documented majority of adolescent girls are out of school, experience-growing levels of sexual violence, abductions, sexual harassment, early and forced marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Most women and girls who face multiple forms of violence have limited support systems and access to information. The Protection Cluster highlighted concerns of high numbers of pregnant adolescent girls in Bama IDP populations in the humanitarian situation report of December 2018. The conflict disproportionately affects girls and women. There is heightened violence that cascades to the household level, with increased rates of domestic violence reported by community members and aid workers. This is linked to people’s own experiences of loss and trauma, as well existing cultural norms. Of particular concern is a culture of silence and stigmatization over reporting of violence. This has led to acceptance of violence especially within family structures. MC Assessment found that safe menstrual practices are an issue for women and adolescent girls in the two locations. Due to displacement of families to locations without sufficient WASH facilities and services (provision of reusable sanitary pads and information). The lack of information or safe space to discuss issues of menstrual health management (MHM) also increases potential risks of teenage pregnancy and sexual harassment. While the humanitarian response has primarily focused on IDP camps in Bama, the number of vulnerable women and girls in the host communities continues

2 - Activities

Establishment of 75 safe spaces for adolescent girls in Bama Damboa Dikwa Gwoza and Ngala : Adolescent girls and boys are often neglected participants to humanitarian interventions. Mercy Corps will reach 1875 adolescent grils through safe spaces .- Engage 1500 adolescent boys and young men in accountable behaviours towards adolescent girls and women: Adolescent boys continue to face heightened risk of forced labor, joining armed groups and suffering from trauma as a consequence of ongoing violence. In an already patriarchal enabling environment, this means that adolescent girls and women face greater risks from young men in a context of relative hopelessness and lack of opportunities among this target group. - Establish and operate one in Listening Center for women and girls in Damboa: In response to increasing GBV cases in host communities in Damboa, Mercy Corps will establish and operate one Listening Center (women and grils Friendly space) for women and girls. Around 1500 women and girls will be targeted by this activity. -Provide individual case management and gender sensitive individual psycho-social support for 600 women and girls and GBV survivors: As part of the response to GBV, Mercy Corps will provide case management services to adolescent survivors of GBV in Southern Borno and Kaduna. - Working with the WASH Teams Mercy Corps will provide an immediate in-kind material support to survivors according their specific needs, include dignity kit for 2500 women and girls and for survivors of sexual violence which comprises of: cloths, bucket, soap, underwear’s, sanitary pads, torch and carry bag. - Conduct structured dialogue session’s for1500 men and boys in accountable behaviors towards women and girls: Mercy Corps will also engage men, boys, and community leaders to address negative masculinities and engage in activities focused on community participation and awareness creation on GBV risks

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

MC will conduct participatory assessments at project inception to identify locations for interventions, and to assess the GBV and protection concerns faced by men, women, girls, and boys, in particular by vulnerable/marginalized groups within the society, and patterns and risks factors for violence and abuse. MC will carry out service mapping of existing GBV services in the two locations, and of other actors including WASH, Shelter, and Food actors, to assess capacity and identify areas for technical support and training. As part of the startup in all locations, MC will also conduct a mapping for community and religious leaders and their level of influence in the community. To identify community coordinators, MC will work with community stakeholders in identifying and vetting community volunteers to support programming and activities MC will continue to work closely with relevant partners to strengthen the effectiveness of responses, harmonize coordination and ensure protection and GBV mitigation is integrated across responses. This will enable key stakeholder to inform programming and contribute to protection responses. Regular meetings will be held with community leaders, government officials, local and international partners. All MC protection and GBV programming is designed to adhere to and uphold the humanitarian principles of Do No Harm. MC has an existing code conduct which all staff, contractors, and volunteers must sign upon taking a contract with MC. All new staff are taken through the code of conduct by our Human resources including the responsibility to Mandatory Reporting of cases of SEA and fraud. Under MC’ Accountability to Affected Populations Framework, MC has developed our PSEA policy. Staff training is always conducted on PSEA and humanitarian code of conduct when new staff are hired. Project staff will be retrained on PSEA and Do No Harm while all new staff will undergo an on-boarding session on the Code of Conduct and the MC PSEA policy. All

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

MC uses a community acceptance strategy to both ensure the safety of MC staff and to mitigate the risk of diversion of aid intended for beneficiaries. MC is transparent with the communities where it works, sharing its operational modalities and humanitarian principles with community members. MC also ensures the recruitment of local team members, while maintaining ethnic and gender diversity, which promotes acceptance and representation of the communities where we operate, and establishes good linkages and closer ties to the communities to build trust and alleviate any perceptions of bias. The selection of sites involves communities and leaders, taking into consideration displacement, vulnerability, and demographics, so that communities of all ethnic groups who are included. Community Accountability & Reporting Mechanisms (CARM) - MC is committed to preventing all forms of exploitation and abuse. All programs are required to provide accessible and effective reporting mechanisms so that our participants and the communities are able to report and provide feedback concerning the actions of our staff, partners, as well as any unlawful situations and behaviors. CARM is an essential part of MC’s commitment to excellence and integrity. Risk Analysis - MC’s PM@MC minimum standards require that a risk assessment be conducted during the program design phase, and be continuously updated throughout the program life cycle. The risk assessment is considered a living document, and at minimum, is updated on an annual basis
