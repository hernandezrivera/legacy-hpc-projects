Project Summary

This is a twelve-month project to be implemented in Pibor, Awerial, Kajokeji, Lainya and Yei counties and designed to provide quality education to children especially adolescents girls and young women including those with disabilities, affected by flood, food insecurity and conflict. The intervention targets 14170 as direct beneficiaries (555F, 615M and 7000G, 6000B). Adult men and women targeted by this project are within the age range of 18-60 years while the school age going learners targeted are those between 3-17 years and out of this target, at least 15% of adults, youth and children will be People Living With Disabilities (based on the cluster recommendation), Young mothers, pregnant girls, orphaned children etc.
The intervention will mainly focus on ECD, Primary and Alternative Education Program levels of learning in all targeted locations. In addition, the project is designed to create access of learners to schools, safe quality and inclusive learning centres while ensuring that barriers like poor access to schools, lack of scholastic materials, school retention for girls, WASH facilities etc. to children's education are addressed through community mobilization and sensitization as well as Back to learning campaigns. Also, the project will plan to improve the quality of education by building the capacity of teachers through continuous professional development tailored to improve their teaching skills. The project will further strengthen the capacity of local education authorities such as County and Payam Education Supervisors and Inspectors by training them on better skills of school supervision, report writings and how to develop strategic plans. This will improve the already ravaged systems in the counties and Payams to deliver quality education services to schools. Also the project shall distribute teaching and age-appropriate learning materials to both teachers and learners targeted since a lack of school supplies have been identified as one of the factors causing poor attendance and learning outcomes in schools. Moreover the project will create better learning environments in schools through construction of inclusive classes to cater for all learners including CWDs. Yet the program plans to renovate those schools that have been destroyed by conflict and floods to enable learners access education in targeted locations and also ensure that gender segregated latrines and WASH facilities are established in schools lacking such facilities to address issues of girls hygiene while attending classes. But also this project will plan to equip targeted schools with C19 materials to prevent further spread of the pandemic among school going age children in addition to distribution of sanitary materials to adolescent girls alongside training them on MHM and SRHR issues to increase their school attendance. And finally, the project will form Roll Models in the community who shall engage community members on importance of education of children especially the girl child. This will be done in close coordination with the PTAs/SMC who will be trained on school development as well as community outreach activities with parents and guardians of school children and youth

1 - Needs

The education HRP 2022 estimated that over 3.6 million (51%) school-aged children lack inadequate access to quality education services. Children and youth age 3-17 yrs represent 44% of the population but only 6 out of every 10 school-aged child attend school and yet 45% are girls. The current trend in girls’ enrolment in schools is alarming. Girls are more likely to be excluded from receiving education as parents prefer to send boys to school which increases the risk of girls ending up in early and forced marriage. There is lack of awareness on the importance of sending children to schools amongst parents and guardians especially ECD. In addition, ECD centres have limited learning kits, learning spaces as well as facilitators posing a challenge in accessing quality education. The closure of all learning institutions due to C19 in 2020 had devastating effects on the education of children and jeopardized critical learning gains made over the years. The South Sudan CO 19 Preparedness and Response Plan 2020 highlighted that the school closure affected over 4 million children from accessing learning institutions adding more challenges to the already fragile education system in the country and yet the capacity of the Ministry of General Education and Instruction (MoGEI) to respond to the needs of children remains limited. The pandemic also affected learners especially adolescent girls to different forms of gender-based violence including forced marriage and teenage pregnancies. The IRNA reports of 2020-2021 highlighted that floods have significantly impacted education infrastructure in Awerial and Pibor damaging about 430 schools. Of these schools, 185 are occupied by over 50000 people displaced due to floods and violence; lack of alternative options has forced IDPs use school buildings as temporary shelters. As a result, school structures, WASH facilities, furniture, and educational supplies have been damaged and destroyed. IRNA report of Sept 2020 recommended the provision of school supplies including furniture, teaching and learning materials, rehabilitation and reconstruction of already ravaged TLS and WASH facilities and the provision of PSS as key priorities for education support in the two counties. In addition, a multi sectoral needs assessment conducted by Plan International in Dec 2021 reveals that Yei, Kajokeji and Lainya counties have experienced continued conflict which has affected children to access education services yet more than 15909 number of returnees of which over 50% are school going age children and women are returning from neighbouring countries of Uganda and Congo increasing the need for education services. The assessment stipulated that over 40% of the schools are not functional and close to 10 schools occupied by armed groups limiting children access to education facilities. Moreover girls education in the counties of Yei, Lainya, Kajokeji, Awerial and Pibor is comprised due to poverty levels and traditional beliefs where school girls age 13 are married off to husbands in exchange of dowry for family riches. The greater Yei Humanitarian Monitoring Report Aug 2021 states that CWDs are not prioritized for education and regarded as curse in families Education quality is affected by in adequate and unskilled teachers to meet learners needs in all the five counties and yet teachers are poorly remunerated by the government demotivating them further. Also in these counties, MOGEI has limited supervision mechanisms in place to support teachers because of inadequate capacity (UNESCO Institute of Statistics report 2021). In addition, in greater Yei, Pibor & Awerial, 31% of the schools do not have functional sex segregated latrines which pauses huge challenges to adolescent girls as changing WASH rooms during menstruation discourages their regular school attendance. Moreover, the few WASH facilities in schools are reportedly not user friendly for CWDs making these category of learners fail to access schools.

3 - Project indicators

The plans for monitoring and reporting activities are catered for in this project. During project implementation, activities and budget monitoring will be undertaken at all levels of intervention. Education Project Officers will fully be accountable for daily management and running of the project in close coordination with the M&E personnel, Also the Program Implementation Area Managers and Education coordinators and Managers at Field locations and Country office will support the intervention. The team will use reporting tools such as 5 W standard templates in addition to donor reporting guidelines and frameworks. PI will further select major indicators and monitor the progress throughout the intervention of the project using IPTT. Further, reports including weekly, monthly and quarterly will be shared by each program location which will be compiled by education coordinator and manager at county office. The project will consider continuous analysis of all reports collected specially through M&E department, also community feedback and improvement mechanisms will be taken into serious consideration particularly feedback from children got from review meetings and suggestion boxes in schools will be valued. But also stakeholders including PTA, Local Authorities and teachers will be given a chance during activity implementation and program meetings to identify areas of concern. The project will use PI project performance trackers on monthly basis which will be compiled by the M&E department and shared to education program staff to inform the performance of the project. Meanwhile quarterly review meetings will be conducted with all stakeholder to mainly highlight achievements, challenges and good practices. Also the project staff will compile quarterly, midterm and final reports which will be submitted to donors based on agreed guidelines and frameworks. The project team will further work with PI country office to ensure close coordination with education cluster both at field and national levels to give top project monthly highlights and reports but also provide updates any time required. In addition, FO will conduct joint monitoring and supervision of project activities with authorities and likeminded partners implementing similar programs in target locations. But also PI will invite the same partners and stakeholder to attend project start up workshop, review meetings and close up workshops all held at field level and to achieve the above plans, the following key indicators will be used in this project • Number of children (3-5 yrs) benefitting from Early Childhood Development (ECD) kits• Number of children and youth benefited from teaching-learning, and recreational materials.• Number of teachers receiving teaching and learning materials to improve lesion preparation in schools• Number of learners receiving go back to school services such as awareness and mobilization campaigns• Number of youth and adolescents (14-17 years) benefit from life-skills training• Number of community education committee members and Local Education officials trained in school management• Number of local Education Authorities receiving training on better school inspection methods, report writing and management of school development• Number of temporary learning spaces constructed• Number of children with access to gender sensitive WASH facilities (in line with INEE and sphere standards)• Number of teachers received/ trained on psychosocial support and basic teaching pedagogy.• Number of temporary learning spaces renovated• Number of teacher receiving teaching incentives • Number of schools receiving COVID 19 protective materials for teachers and learners• Number of adolescent girls receiving sanitary materials

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

People Living with disabilities will be treated with major concern in this project. They will be consulted from time to time before and after provision of services. In addition, they shall be involved in delivery of services for instance, teachers with disabilities will be engaged to teach in schools and will be used to get feedback from learners especially those with disabilities on quality of education services provided.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project staff will provide suggestion boxes and hotlines in built in the community for beneficiaries to provide feedback on the quality of the project. Beneficiaries will be trained on the use of the suggestion boxes especially installed in schools so that any complaints or feedback can be gotten through the suggestion box. The box will be lockable and opened on agreed dates with the community to retrieve the feedback. Also, the hotlines and telephones will be distributed to all schools and community centres for any beneficiary to call in case of any feedback or complaints

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PI takes protection, safeguarding and PSEA as a serious concern in all programming work. As such, all project staff and associates will be trained on protection, safeguarding and PSEA and thereafter, they will sign the three policies. Any bridge of the policies will lead to dismissal of the staff or associate once evidence proves him/her guilty. In addition, beneficiaries and volunteers will be trained on reporting procedures for protection, safeguarding and PSEA. Further to that, safe call numbers will be distribute to schools and centres such that beneficiaries can directly call in case of any protection, safeguarding and PSEA concerns committed to them.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The beneficiary feedback will be treated very important to provide project assistance. All feedbacks will be collected and opened or analysed by a team including M&E department to ensure what has not gone well is corrected and change the quality of services provided. Also more focus will be given to the suggestion of the feedback especially if it requires changing approach and services that shall fit the needs of the beneficiaries.
