Project Summary

The project will support 42,310 vulnerable conflict-affected children in northeast Nigeria, together with their caregivers and community members, through a holistic identification - prevention - mitigation - response framework that aims to strengthen the overall protective environment of communities in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States.
The project will prioritise the most at-risk children including Children Associated with Armed Forces and Groups (CAAFAG), Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC), Children with Disabilities (CwD), and survivors of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) - particularly children who fall under multiple categories of risk. Caregivers of these children will also receive support.
Street Child will provide comprehensive case management, psychosocial support services, strengthening of community structures, and targeted socioeconomic reintegration under this project.
Street Child aims to operate in the following LGAs under the 2023 Humanitarian Response Plan:
Borno: Bama, Damboa, Dikwa, Gwoza, Jere, Mobbar, Monguno, Ngala
Adamawa: Hong, Madagali, Michika, Mubi N, Mubi S
Yobe: Gujba, Yusufari

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2023 identifies 1.5 million people in need of child protective services to be targeted for support in northeast Nigeria(1,050,000 children, 53% girls and 450,000 caregivers, 53% women). Child protection needs have increased since the 2022 HRP due to macroeconomic factors and continued insurgency/counterinsurgency operations in the northeast. In particular, the government-sponsored relocation of displaced people from camps to their LGA of origin has strained services and disrupted fragile coping mechanisms. The large numbers of surrendered or demobilised CAAFAG children are at very high risk of protection issues, particularly when informally or incompletely reintegrated with their communities. Caregivers require psychosocial services to support their own mental health and positive parenting practices, which are jeopardised by chronic psychosocial distress. High inflation, particularly fuel and food, has driven more households into poverty and the associated heightened protection risks such as child labour and marriage, neglect, and abuse, and threatening the sustainability of recently reintegrated families.National organisations within the Child Protection Area of Responsibility (CPAoR) also demonstrate needs in additional capacity strengthening and support linked to the projected beneficiary needs and activities in 2023. Strengthening and support to national organisations is a key requirement under the localisation agenda.

2 - Activities

Street Child will continue its engagement and strengthening of community structures like Child Protection Committees (CBCPC) to improve these mechanisms' ability to identify, prevent, mitigate, and respond to child protection and safeguarding issues within the community, through comprehensive referral mapping and coordination with multisectoral service providers. The team will engage CBCPC members and community leaders in discussions and dialogues on acceptance and facilitation of socioeconomic reintegration for children, such as CAAFAG and UASC, re-entering the community to strengthen resilience and cohesion.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The prioritisation of protection, safety, dignity, accountability and minimising unintended negative impacts ensure that the communities where Street Child works can access meaningful, dignified support. Street Child is committed to centring and mainstreaming protection throughout its activities and projects, and has pre-emptively identified potential protection risks within the project as well as taking active steps to integrate mitigation and management strategies. Protection reporting, response and prevention mechanisms are integrated into the design, and protection mainstreaming indicators are included within the monitoring and evaluation framework. At the beneficiary selection stage, Street Child will use tested vulnerability assessment tools and clear selection process to ensure that beneficiaries are selected based on need and through predetermined and clearly communicated criteria of vulnerability. In order to ensure participation and minimise inter-group tensions, targets have been set to include different communities (IDP, host community and returnee) as well as both men and women. Community leaders and members will be informed of the selection process and criteria during the inception stages, and continuous sensitisation of the selection criteria will be used to ensure that possible intra- and inter-group tensions are minimised. Sensitisation on these topics through regular community visits and feedback sessions has proven successful during past projects, where dissatisfaction about not being included in the project has been reduced by clearly explaining the aims of the project and how beneficiaries were selected. As part of our commitment to accountability and protection, Street Child commits to work towards the elimination of exploitation and abuse in the communities where we work and in our engagement with vulnerable populations. Street Child has a clear safeguarding policy in place which applies to all personnel and affiliates, including implementing partners and community members who are affiliated to our work. All Street Child staff, partner staff, and project-affiliated community members such as volunteers and committee members receive comprehensive training of the Street Child safeguarding policy. This comprehensive approach, which is also intended to be rolled out to vendors, ensures that all actors within SC projects are operating under the same rules and procedures and creates a harmonised safeguarding environment. In addition to training key community members on the Street Child safeguarding policy, wider community outreach and sensitisation is carried out via hosting of community forums and meetings, or use of visual aids including posters and murals which offer accessible and culturally relevant and appropriate key messages to empower community members on understanding and reporting concerns. Finally, Street Child is an active participant of the PSEA working group in Borno, as an extra level of accountability, and follows the best practices and lessons learned shared through this working group.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback mechanisms will be contextualised to the community in question. Street Child has a dedicated CRM Officer with the responsibility of implementing and managing accessible and relevant feedback mechanisms, based on community information. Previous interventions have utilised toll free lines, complaint desks, anonymous boxes, and similar modalities. Safeguarding and complaint mechanisms can be contextualised through altering images and languages to suit the community, both for audio and print material. Project staff will also attend regular coordination meetings with community structures. M&E teams will conduct baseline, endline, and regular monitoring, including FGDs/KIIs with community members, to gauge satisfaction with the implementation. All feedback received will be fed into review sessions and management decisions, allowing activities to be adapted to respond where appropriate in line with Street Child's commitment to accountability and evidence-based learning.
