Project Summary

iMMAP sponsors the NES HMAC that acts as the HMA Sector Lead within the Protection Working Group and NES Forum. Acting as an independent focal point for all Humanitarian Mine Action activities. The HMAC Role is also responsible for the collection, collation and sharing of HMA Data in line with International Mine Action Standards.
The HMAC coordinates with all HMA actors that are active in NES in clearance activities, non-technical surveys (NTS), EO Risk Education (EORE) and Victim Assistance. The HMAC represents the HMA sector with the local authorities and supports both the partners and sector and the newly established NES Mine Action Center (NESMAC) has ambitions to act as the Mine Action authority, which is a crucial role, especially when it comes to tasking and prioritization. The HMAC also provides a bridge and services and HMA Coordination across the Inter Sector Working Group (ISWG) and the Whole of Syria HMA and Protection Working Groups.
The HMAC is supported by the Information Management team that collects data with the aim of ensuring a living archive and creating a transparent picture of EO contamination in NES. This information is publicly available that supports local communities, authorities and humanitarian/stabilization actors and donors in conducting safe agricultural activities and (re-)construction of infrastructure and developing strategic planning that supports such as activities as the HNO, HRP and Donor decision making.

4 - Needs

EO contamination claims lives on a weekly basis and blocks rehabilitation efforts of vital areas such as agriculture, education and water infrastructure. It is found in NES is found in areas where hostilities between ISIS and SDF occurred and it follows the Kurdish offensive from 2016 to 2019. Additionally, conflict around OPS lines continues, with frequent shelling. Delays in 2019 and 2020 because of the OPS offensive and the COVID-19 pandemic created gaps in clearance activities. Consequently, many rehabilitation works are hindered or delayed by EO contamination. Contamination data is still incomplete. Surveys are limited to areas where HMA actors are active. Only a handful of contamination impact surveys have been conducted in communities potentially at risk. In NES, INGO fulfill coordination roles traditionally played by UN agencies and local authorities. The HMAC is part of the NGO forum, because of the need to represent the HMA partners and coordinate with them a consolidated response viewed as essential to set the tone of the long-term relationships. The nature of HMAC is changing because of the transition from an emergency setting into an early recovery phase. This requires a more structured way of coordination and a mechanism of prioritization. Because of different perceptions on how data is collected, there is a need to standardize reporting. Available HMA data needs to be better shared with the wider humanitarian and stabilization community.

5 - Implementation of activities

iMMAP has provided HMAC since 2017, in the absence of the UN cluster system, but in close communication with UNMAS in Damascus. Through the HMAC, the various partners come together and discuss their activities and issues such as data collection. The HMAC represents the HMA sector with the local authorities, other coordination bodies in NES and Damascus. The HMAC has a large role to play to support the NESMAC in developing prioritization mechanisms. iMMAP collects HMA data from the active HMA actors, analyzes it and disseminates it to the wider humanitarian and rehabilitation sectors, in order to facilitate their efforts in reconstruction components such as agriculture, education and water infrastructure. This is possible thanks to a team of Information Management Officers with specific expertise in mine action data. In this regard, mine action data management is essential to improve coordination and the efforts in improving the quality for data collection among HMA actors is also one of the priorities of iMMAP. To deal with the challenge of data collection, IMMAP is working with NESMAC and HMA actors to standardize data. This will enable iMMAP to improve the analysis and do more with the available data, such as supporting the NESMAC in prioritizing, coordination and monitoring and evaluation of HMA activities. iMMAP is developing a risk assessment tool to mainstream HMA thinking into inter-sector risk management practices. Finally, the HMAC coordinates EORE activities.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The risk of sexual exploitation and abuse is internally only, as iMMAP does not directly implement humanitarian activities. This risk is mitigated with an internal Global PSEA policy dating from December 2020:a) iMMAP commits to ensure the respect among its personnel and its programs of IASC Six Core Principles relating to SEA and to the Secretary General’s Bulletin on Special Measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuses (ST/SGB/2003/13).b) iMMAP staff, implementing partners/sub-contractors as well as beneficiaries/hosting communities have the right and obligation to report any claims and suspicions related to sexual misconduct by iMMAP personnel or sub-contractors/implementing partners/staff through the iMMAP Whistle Blower and Complaint Mechanism Policy.c) In both HQ and Field, iMMAP management is responsible for creating and maintaining an environment that prevents sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse. Management shall take appropriate measures for this purpose, including awareness training regarding iMMAP’s Safeguarding and Anti-Discrimination Policy package.d) The violation of Policy principles is considered as gross misconduct and is ground for disciplinary measures, including the possibility of termination of contract.e) iMMAP commits to ensure necessary dissemination of this policy takes place in all field missions and take all necessary measures should any breach of this policy be reported.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In the context in NES there is always a risk of a sudden change in the humanitarian and security situation. The main risk is a continuation of EO contamination claiming lives, also because over 40% of the population has not received any risk education. This risk is amplified by a decreasing availability of funding for HMA. Related to this is the risk that the Self Administration and NESMAC will continue to struggle to deliver in their role as Mine Action authorities. This in turn creates a risk that a lack of proper prioritization limits effective clearing efforts. Additionally, without proper coordination, the risk of information gaps immediately resurfaces. Finally, there is always a risk of recontamination, especially around frontlines close to the OPS areas. The project maintains the digitized archive of all HMA activities including geo referenced hazards and clearance and activities. During previous insecurity incidents NGO's lost large amounts of data or the ability to collect and manage data. The HMAC's data management system through iMMAP's data management protocols ensures the security and availability of data using online cloud based servers ensuring the safe storage of data in the event of complete close down.
