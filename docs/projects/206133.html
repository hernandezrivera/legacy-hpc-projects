Project Summary

DRC will deploy 2 highly Mobile Multi-Task Teams (MTTs) to respond to urgent threats to physical safety, dignity, freedom of movement and access to humanitarian assistance. Alongside this, DRC will deploy 6 (Explosive Ordnance Risk Education) EORE teams to provide Explosive Risk Ordnance Education to conflict or displacement affected communities to mitigate risks of explosive hazards and this will include new arrival of some South Sudanese returnees from Sudan. DRC will work with other sector responses, peacebuilding and development actors to ensure maximum socio- economic benefit of cleared land, and to strengthen the protective environment for affected populations. DRC will support both static and emergency responses with EORE, NTS and clearance activities.
The proposed project aims at:
• Providing immediate lifesaving assistance to address the urgent protection needs of the most vulnerable (including women, children, elderly, disabled and survivors of ERW in conflict) in hard-to-reach areas.
• Enhancing prevention and preparedness programming to mitigate risks and strengthen resilience
• Enabling durable solutions for IDPs and other affected populations

Tasks that will support the humanitarian response in the country will be prioritized through the following key activities:
Non-Technical Survey (NTS) and Technical Survey (TS) – NTS and will be conducted to target and prioritize clearance according to humanitarian need. Surveys will be conducted using a mixture of key informant interviews and household questionnaires held with the local population and authorities. NTS will determine the likely locations and density of ERW contamination in target locations, whilst assessing the impact of these hazards on vulnerable populations in any given location. TS (using detectors) will be used upon discovery of an area with a high concentration of explosive hazards.
Manual Mine Clearance (MMC) / Battle Area Clearance (BAC) - the MTTs will be able to act as a MMC / BAC capacity, performing static clearance activities across larger, clearly defined areas (identified through the above data collection activities and technical survey as appropriate) or already recorded open Hazardous Areas in Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) data.
Explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) – once NTS has determined the nature of the threat, technical capacities within the teams will be deployed to remove and destroy identified hazards.
Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) – all teams will contain a capacity to deliver risk messaging, to raise awareness about how to recognize dangerous items. The risk education will emphasize safe practices that should be followed, and high-risk behavior that should be avoided.
Integration: DRC will work with economic recovery, HLP and other relevant responses to support individuals use land for socio- economic purposes. This involves stakeholder mapping, and where DRC has other activities, coordination on the integration of beneficiaries into existing programming.

1. Needs

Over a decade after South Sudan gained independence, about 2.2 million South Sudanese remain internally displaced and more than 2.3 million South Sudanese are hosted in neighboring countries. The economic development of civilians has greatly been threatened by conflicts, the presence and illicit flow of small weapons, disasters and climate change.As of May 2023, 16 million sqm of South Sudan are confirmed to remain heavily contaminated with explosive ordnances (including land mines, cluster munitions and other UXO), with over 2.5 million people in need according to the most recent Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), with an anticipated arrival of 420,000 additional people moving from Sudan into South Sudan. Contamination levels are the highest in the Central and Eastern Equatoria as well as Upper Nile, Jonglei and Unity States. There is also over 3 million sqm of suspected contamination along mined roads across South Sudan. There are high levels of displacement internally with South Sudan as a result of flooding and conflict, but also returnees from Sudan, Uganda, Kenya and Ethiopia. In addition, South Sudan hosts over 160,000 refugees in Maban. These contaminated hazards block access to agricultural land, critical infrastructure, including schools, hospitals and routes, threatening the lives of communities, and vulnerable IDPs, refugees, and returnees, and inhibiting the safe delivery of humanitarian aid as well as economic recovery.Frequently used routes, including around Fashoda, Malakal, Maban, Magwi and Akobo counties are contaminated, exposing users to risks of accidents. exposing them to risks of accidents. To support development of national mine clearance, EORE and NTS expertise, DRC is working with a national partner to support skills transfer and capacity development. Magwi county, Eastern Equatoria State is ranked at the highest severity level for the mine action sector under this year’s HNO. This means that 1,666 million sqm are known to be contaminated. Maban county, Upper Nile State is ranked as extreme in terms of severity for the mine action sector with 189,800 sqm of known contamination and is seeing significant number of new arrivals of refugees escaping conflict in Sudan. Akobo county, Jonglei State is ranked as extreme in terms of severity for the mine action sector with 322,877 sqm of known contamination and is also seeing high population movement owing to conflict and challenges in Gambella and Sudan. Potential emergency locations: Given the above-mentioned severity levels in terms of contamination as well as existing DRC triggers for mobile response missions (including on conflict and flooding), DRC will deploy to locations referenced above.

2. Project activities

Respond to and address notifications of hazardous areas through survey and clearance. Release cleared land back to communities for productive and social use, supporting integration of project beneficiaries with other services such as livelihoods, or House, Land and Property (HLP) assistance. Increase awareness of Unexploded Ordnances through EORE activities. By doing so, DRC seeks to prevent and mitigate the physical risks posed by land mines and ERW and enhance preparedness and resilience through EORE messaging. This directly supports the Protection Cluster Objective 1 by providing assistance to vulnerable populations that directly contributes to their safety, Protection Cluster Objective 2, by building national capacity on humanitarian mine action, and supporting coordination of mine action activities, Protection Cluster Objective 3, by leveraging clearance activities to support longer term recovery, without safe access to land, agricultural development, social spaces, living areas and other livelihood activities cannot be safely conducted.
