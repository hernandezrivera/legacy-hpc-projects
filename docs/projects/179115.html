Project Summary

This project will be implemented in Governorates of Al-Hasakeh, Deir Elzour, and Al-Raqa of NES. It will target a total of 6000 women and men and their families, of which 60% are female, through an FSL intervention aimed at integrating resilience in the relief and recovery
actions. Blumont FSL activities aim to promote the resilience of the most vulnerable communities in NES); the intervention is designed to improve food security and protect and restoring livelihoods. Farmers, both men, and women in the targeted locations will receive extension services. Farmers will also receive appropriate skills training.
Selection of beneficiaries will be made based on the criteria of vulnerability and capacity. Village Savings and Loans Associations (VSLA) expert will provide VSLA training after establishing the groups. The project's first component will target women, especially mothers and caregivers of under 5years old children. They will receive immediate support in agricultural inputs or basic training, which will protect and restore their essential livelihood assets. This is expected to lead to increased production of food, increased food access, and dietary diversity at the household level. Blumont will establish twenty working groups of active women to form women to women Support
groups. These groups, a total of 200 women, will be trained on kitchen gardening, and the training will focus on transforming these leading mothers into examples and agents of change. These groups will also be able to sell any surplus vegetables/food produced within the program and use the collective income for any other activities of the group's choice. Groups will a good performance working together, and that is generating income will receive training in VSLA methodology. The revenue generated from the gardens will go into personal savings for these women. The project will also work with men in the community by way of
engaging farmers in restoring community production and equally promoting the establishment of VSLA schemes among the male farmers. This will create more value for the communities and ensure the do-no-harm principle (avoid that women are perceived as having support or assets and therefore exposed to GBV / control of assets by the family's male members). Finally, the vouchers for the asset component will directly contribute to improving the food security of vulnerable
households in the project area and enhancing rural development through the creation of productive community-based assets.

4 - Needs

Blumont conducted a Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) in January – February 2021 to understand current conditions and the needs of the population living in NES. One-third of the surveyed people had access to land (32.53%) and, of those with access, 87.70% reported owning the land. Most respondents (89.49%) stated that they used their land to cultivate cereal crops. Other responses included cultivating cash crops (14.77%), cultivating vegetables (14.54%), and using for pasture (9.84%). Of these host community households, over 60% reported planting all the land they owned during the current season. When discussing crop yields, the majority (55.26%) reported decreased yields. When asked what assistance would help restore agricultural production, 61.71% of respondents stated inputs and training, 36.02% said inputs and less than 3% stated solely training. Specific assistance cited as most needed included seeds (64.88%), fertilizer (60.63%), fuel for irrigation (25.06%), pesticides (21.70%), and efficient irrigation systems (15.21%). Agricultural inputs and assistance reported by host community households were limited. “Other types of assistance” was the highest reported category across the three governorates at 7.79%. The only other assistance above 1% of the surveyed households was received agricultural production tools (2.26%) and seeds (1.67%). In terms of cited irrigation methods, most respondents reported rainfed irrigation (55.03%), followed by the water pump (21.48%), and borehole and irrigational channels each were reported by 11.19% of the households.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will work in NES with 5000 beneficiaries (1000 HH). Women targeted for the project will receive extension services support and VSLA training. Groups will work for 12 months and cash out their savings at the end. The project will identify and register group members (women to women Support Groups and Farmer Groups), verify arable plots, and HH garden space that will be cultivated. Blumont will procure vegetable seeds and proceed to train the groups and commence the cultivation practices. A committee from each location will select farmers farming sites. Assets will be identified through Community Based Participatory Planning sessions. Lead women will act as‘ demonstration plot’ owners and thereby influence others within support groups. The next step will be to introduce women groups’ ideas and initiate self-organization into cooperatives, based on women’s interests, availability, and cultural aspects to ensure the do-no-harm principle. Training on vegetable marketing skills and record-keeping will accompany the cultivation work. The formation of VSLA groups will follow this initial stage. We will include intercropping techniques, climate-sensitive cultivation practices, and disaster risk reduction planning based on the priority risks. Farmers will work on adaptation through resilient production approaches, learning about water and soil management. The program will consider offering smallholders cash protection.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Blumont integrated Do-No-Harm principles in the program’s design, selection, and implementation strategy and was informed by comprehensive consultations with the FSWG, LAs, and members from target communities. Blumont’s eligibility criteria for beneficiary selection will continue to be based on need, not sociopolitical identity. Before implementing any activity, Blumont’s field staff will consult with local authorities and community leaders (including sharing beneficiary criteria) to communicate program activities, targeting logic, and conflict resolution. Vulnerable women, children, PwD, disabled persons, and elderly-headed HHs will receive selection priority. The activities involve providing in-kind inputs specifically to reduce protection risks or mobility challenges for such HHs and promote livelihoods for women and other vulnerable groups. Training for women and farming will be gender-segregated as needed, and typically female staff will lead or support assessments for female-headed HHs. During the training, Blumont will ensure all Covid 19 prevention measures are followed. Finally, Blumont will distribute the farmer's protection tools.
