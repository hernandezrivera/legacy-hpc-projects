Project Summary

UNICEF is both a service provider under the partner presence rationalization in the sector and the provider of last resort (POLR). The largest response is in NE Nigeria (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states) where protracted armed conflict has continued to force displacement of population into camps and camp-like settlements for more than a decade. In other regions, the need for response a rise from sporadic conflicts, natural disasters like floods or risks of outbreaks/spread of infectious diseases such as cholera, which is endemic in Nigeria, and the recent covid-19 or other hazards. Gaps also arise as a results of new population displacements or diminished capacity/departure of other humanitarian WASH partners. As a service provider, UNICEF is optimizing the water production and distribution, increasing attainment of the required minimum standards. UNICEF is rolling out the approach of urban type network water distribution in deep field LGAs with high yielding boreholes and high-density population of displaced persons or returnees and host communities. This approach will support decongestion in camps and expansion of WASH services to schools and health/nutrition facilities. UNICEF will also expand cash in wash programming for hygiene kits and other WASH NFIs. Through this project, UNICEF will have specific target populations in needs in IDP camps and host communities in Borno State, specifically in Maiduguri Metropolitan City and 10 LGAs (Bama, Dikwa, Gwoza, Jere, Kala Balge, Konduga, Mafa, Mobbar, Monguno, and Ngala), whilst in Adamawa and Yobe, and possibly other states, the target population will be determined by locations where needs will arise. UNICEF will adhere to the SPHERE standards and scaleup the sustenance of the services as needed.
To meet the demands of the POLR responsibility, UNICEF may need to mobilize additional resources. Through this project UNICEF will provide the initial lifesaving response to fill the gaps, prioritizing areas receiving newly displaced people or under an outbreak of cholera, hepatitis or other WASH-related diseases including covid-19. UNICEF will strive to sustain the basic initial WASH services until further response becomes available from other sector partners. The project is also aimed at sustaining critical humanitarian water, sanitation and hygiene services to displaced population affected by protracted armed conflict in NE Nigeria (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States: target 1,512,786 people) and emergency affected population in three states including other locations.

1 - Needs

According to the MSNA data, 2022. 1,969,439 million people, comprising of 1,153,450 children (58%) and 432,305 women (21%) lack sufficient access to safe water, while 2,788,704 million children and 1,065,042 women have no access to sanitation and 2,638,945 million children and 993,284 women have no access to a hand washing facilities with running water and soap. Area-specific data from camps and settlements highlight the specific needs relative to the sector standards. These include a) Water Supply to ensure 15ltrs/person/day: humanitarian support is required for the management of operations, maintenance and repairs on handpumps, motorized systems (hybrid and solar) and efficient restocking of consumables for water treatment and safe collection, transportation and storage to meet needs for sustained access to chlorinated water in sufficient quantities. Community ownership, recently introduced, require strengthening to forestall vandalism and theft of submersible pumps in locations with high water stress issues. In most IDP camps, access to safe water remains a challenge due to low ground water capacity hindering construction of new boreholes, long queuing time of over 30 minutes, including high rate of new arrivals. IDPs living in camps in MMC, Jere, Dikwa, Gwoza, Konduga, Monguno, Ngala and Damboa have reported acute water needs with some camps receiving as low as 3liters/person/day, resulting in scale up of water trucking. b) Access to Sanitation: more than 20 people share a latrine. Open defecation in camps which has contributed to health concerns including the Cholera outbreak, will likely rise with new displacement/influx and mass movement of people out of MMC camps, unsanitary conditions due to overcrowding, lack of space for construction of new latrines and high desludging needs due high-water table, flooding and temporary nature of the construction coupled with high demand. c) Lifespan of latrine designs: The impact of adverse weather and high dislodging needs accelerate the wear and tear of emergency design of facilities. A prolonged delay of policy shift and investment to improve designs have left thousands of sanitation facilities not functional. More permanent solutions are required given that the residence of displacement persons in camps is protracted, now exceeding a decade since the beginning of the armed conflict. d) The interim solution of regular desludging of latrines is not cost effective in the long term. Major investment is needed for an integral faecal sludge management from containment, collection, transportation and disposal of faecal sludge from camps for displaced persons and host communities. The faecal sludge management is poor, with low infrastructure to support liquid waste from desludging activities, which is considered as one of the reasons for yearly outbreaks of cholera. Additionally, flooding is a major concern as most of the faecal sludge worsens with households facing exposure of untreated waste. Introduction of treatment and possible recycling of the sludge will render the sludge safe and incapable of becoming an environmental hazard. e) Hygiene promotion and environmental awareness: Due to low awareness of personal hygiene, the need to train more hygiene promoters especially due to COVID 19 and outbreak is critical. The WASH sector has also included environmental hygiene clean ups through mass mobilization campaigns. Experience over the years highlights a need of providing cleaning materials to support environmental campaigns, mainly in schools and other social places (like markets) which are hotspots for cholera and AWD outbreaks. f) Supply and replenishment of hygiene kits and other WASH NFI is not keeping up with the needs. Supplies are depleted and replenishments are provided long beyond useful life of the commodities.

2 - Activities

UNICEF will implement seven key activities (outlined below) through various partnerships, Long Term Agreement (LTA) contracts or Project Cooperation Agreements (PCA) with selected partners and State Govt Agencies under the Basic Cooperation Agreement with Federal Government of Nigeria and Annual Work Plans with the state governments in Borno, Adamawa, Yobe, Sokoto, Zamfara, Katsina and Kano. This project will consolidate UNICEF capacity as a sector partner implementing in allocated locations/camps in Maiduguri Municipality and 10 LGAs (Bama, Dikwa, Gwoza, Jere, Kala Balge, Konduga, Mafa, Mobbar, Monguno, and Ngala), where UNICEF will reach SPHERE standards. In Adamawa, 16 LGAs (Demsa, Fufore, Girei, Guyuk, Jada , Lamurde, Madagali, Michika, Mubi North, Mubi South, Numan, Shelleng, Song, Toungo, Yola North and Yola South). In Yobe, 14 LGAs (Bade, Damaturu, Fika, Fune, Geidam, Gujba, Gulani, Jakusko, Nangere, Potiskum Tarmuwa, Karasuwa, Yusufari , Yunusari,) where UNICEF will support AWD/ flood response. Additional resources will be needed for UNICEF to play the second role of provider of last resort (POLR) where there is no other capacity from the sector. The key activities to be implemented under this project are: 1) Water trucking for limited periods to provide temporary access to safe water to newly displaced people or people affected by water, sanitation and hygiene related disease outbreaks. UNICEF will deploy LTA contractors on water trucking to the areas in need. The same population will be provided more permanent water sources. Target: 50,000 people (also part of the target below). 2)Optimizing water production, storage and distribution to provide 15 litres per person per day, increase storage and expand distribution to reduce water collection time to within 30minutes round trip including queuing time. This is based on assessed needs and tested optimal water production of existing water resources. Dysfunctional facilities will be repaired or rehabilitated according to needs. Higher capacity pumps matching the tested safe water pumping will be installed. Storage tank capacities will be increased to cover two days' water demand in case of breakdowns. Priority are large population camps with high-capacity boreholes. This activity will optimize benefit for the affected population and enable possible development of water supply for host communities and other population. Construction of new facilities will be a last resort when there are no pre-existing facilities.Target: 493,716 people. 3) Daily chlorination and management of the operation and maintenance of water supply services to sustain access to safe water in IDP camps and urban areas in Maiduguri and Yola. Implementing partners will support the management of existing water systems in camps allocated to UNICEF: daily chlorination of water using automatic chlorine dossers; monitoring of the free residual chlorine levels and faecal contamination of the water at production point and at the shelters using the water; and organizing orderly queuing at the water collection points and support repairs in case of breakdown. The target is to restore service within 48 hours. Distribution of water treatment chemicals to Borno and Adamawa States for municipal water treatment and distribution in cholera affected LGAs. Target: 1,008,524 people. 4) Increasing access to safe sanitation. UNICEF will construct additional latrines to reach the SPHERE standard. Target 305,366 people. 5) Daily cleaning and disinfection of latrines including desludging latrines in allocated camps and per demand from CCCM for other camps. Target: 872,473 people. 6) Distribution of hygiene kits including cash for kits in Maiduguri. 7) Risk informed hygiene promotion in camps and host communities, and cholera hotspots, using a range of communication channels. Target: 1,512,786 people.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To design the appropriate interventions, UNICEF ensures that all groups including women, children and elderly are engaged in gathering data on their specific WASH needs. Concerns of all affected people, including vulnerable groups are factored into the programme design ensuring that activities proposed are specifically designed to meet the needs of different age and gender groups. Concerns of all affected people, including vulnerable groups are factored into the programme design ensuring that activities proposed are specifically designed to meet the needs of different age and gender groups. The programmes include estimated sex and age disaggregated data for the target groups based on the findings of the need’s assessment. For latrines, gender segregated sanitation location, design, safety and appropriateness and convenience of facilities will be decided in consultation with the users, particularly women, adolescent girls and any persons with disabilities to ensure the protection of affected people. All sanitation facilities, including latrines, will be constructed near residential units to ensure the privacy and security of women and children and easy access for elderly. Project will ensure that separate lockable, well-lit latrines are constructed for women and children and the criteria and details of activities will be explained in the community for better understanding. Project will ensure women's participation while selecting the sites for sanitation facilities, water points and timing of hygiene promotion sessions and WASH committee meetings.All the interventions proposed under the project have a component of building the community resilience for new arrivals, existing IDPs and the host communities. The proposed activities are cost effective and having a long-term solution coupled with immediate lifesaving priorities.The principle of Do no Harm has been considered while designing the WASH activities. The community will be mobilized to take ownership and protect the water and sanitation equipment installed. People from the communities will be involved during installation of WASH facilities to build their capacity and strengthen local ownership. All structures will be provided with doors and basic locks for privacy and protection of women. WASH committees will be trained on operation and maintenance of water and sanitation facilities.
