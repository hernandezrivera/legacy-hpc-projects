Project Summary

The UNICEF Child Protection programme provides support to the extremely vulnerable, conflict-affected girls, boys and women in South Sudan who are exposed to child protection risks and gender-based violence. The programme includes providing case management and family tracing and reunification services (FTR), mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), reintegration interventions for children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG), explosive ordnance risk education (EORE), monitoring of grave child rights violations through the monitoring and reporting mechanism (MRM), gender-based violence interventions, and critical, frontline emergency services, through integrated rapid response mechanisms (IRRMs). Within the programme budget $10.5M is needed for Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE), $6.5M for gender-based violence (GBV) activities and $600,000M for EORE.

1 - Needs

According to the inter-sectoral severity analysis of the 2021 HNO, a total of 72 counties face extreme needs while five are in severe need. Protection concerns remain significant, with affected populations expressing fear over persistent insecurity, protection threats including child protection and gender based violence, human rights violations. According to the IPC report October 2020 -July 2021, the most severe acute food insecurity conditions are in counties where chronic vulnerabilities have been exacerbated by a combination of shocks, including floods, insecurity incidents, the macro-economic crisis, and the effects of COVID-19 measures. According to HNO 2021, 2.3 million children in need are at risk of violence, exploitation and abuse requiring lifesaving child protection services. 15 per cent of the total target represent persons with disabilities. High levels of displacement and family separation affect over one million, causing severe psychological distress and leaving children vulnerable to exploitation, violence abuse and neglect. Displacements separated children from their families, in the first half of 2020, DTM reported on 75 instances of spontaneous population movements comprised of 145,628 IDPs and 21,771 returnees across the seven states and Abyei Region. Communal clashes accounted for 83 percent of the displacements and 11 per cent fled floods while seven per cent fled armed conflict. UNICEF will work in 42 identified counties where IRNAs in 2020 and other assessments at state level have highlighted child protection concerns such as psychosocial distress, family separation, GBV, EORE and recruitment in armed forces; neglect, during intercommunal violence, floods, food and nutrition insecurity and the COVID-19 pandemic. Five of the six identified food and nutrition insecure counties (Phase 5) in collaboration with the nutrition and health sectors will also be targeted. More than 26,000 children have been registered as unaccompanied, separated or missing since December 2013 with over 7,000 children still needing to be reunified with their families. Child protection case management services are not fully integrated into the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare creating a gap in services by government. Since 2013, over 12,900 grave violations against children were documented and verified by the UN. UNICEF will integrate MHPSS into other sectors like education and health. The crises in 2013 and 2016, plus 21 years of civil war, have left all 10 states contaminated with explosive ordnances especially in Central, Eastern Equatoria and Jonglei states with 73 per cent contamination. Capacity building will be provided to strengthen EORE and awareness. The continuous flooding in some areas, inter-tribal conflict and clashes coupled with high levels of food insecurity continues to expose women and girls to GBV including sexual violence and sexual exploitation and abuse. According to the findings in the multisector needs’ assessment, FSL, shelter, and WASH needs were found within the same household, in combination with protection and/or health (65% of households). Vulnerable household members include the presence of an elderly household member, a separated or unaccompanied child, a physical or mental disabled household member, a chronically ill household member, a pregnant or lactating woman. Increased risks, gaps in GBV service provision, and a response constrained by the COVID-19 pandemic necessitates urgent and strengthened GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and response services. Interventions to address GBV will ensure continued delivery of specialized case management for survivors of GBV ( adults, adolescent and children), prevention and awareness raising activities, GBV risk mitigation including safety audits and distribution of dignity kits as well as prevention and social norms transformation through the communities care model.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will integrate PSEA information into the prevention and awareness raising messaging for all activities implemented at the CFS, WGFS, schools etc. The end trainers and volunteers will also receive training on PSEA and referrals to ensure that the information is adequately communicated to beneficiaries.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Affected populations, including boys and girls, persons with disabilities, youth and women will be part of planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project. Feedback from vulnerable members will be sought through programme monitoring visits. UNICEF, in collaboration with partners, will not interview or use any information without consent from any affected population, especially children and vulnerable women.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

This project will use complaint and feedback mechanisms at child-friendly spaces, women- and girl-friendly spaces, referral mechanisms, and structures established in schools and other locations where community members have access. The project involves strengthening community-based child protection and GBV structures and networks, including child protection committees. Through these committees, community members can report concerns. Focus group discussions and monitoring visits will also provide opportunities for people to give feedback.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will work with community-based child protection committees and source information from programme monitoring visits to children and families. Community-based feedback mechanisms will be established. These data will be fed into mid-year and end-year review processes to ensure improved project delivery. Feedback will also develop safer programmes and help adapt or improve reporting mechanisms.
