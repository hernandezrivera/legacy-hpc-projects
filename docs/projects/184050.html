Project Summary

HOPE is planning to work in increasing safe access to education and CP services for children and adolescents in CD, WD and SD States, which will contribute to the HRP education and CP strategic objectives (for 2022). This project is an integrated project, some CP beneficiaries such as out of school's children will be benefits from school activities as well as the education beneficiaries will be benefited from CP activities. In the Education component, HOPE will target 24 basic schools, 6 schools in each locality of Gharb Jabal Marrah (Nertiti) & Shamal Jabal Marrah (Rokero) in CD, Nitega in SD, and Kereneik in WD to deliver: (1) Provision School Kits to 15,000 school children, (2) Conduct 4 EiE Training targeting 80 teachers, (3) Provision Teacher kits to 360 teachers, (4) Provision of 400 Set of School furniture, (5) Provision of 96 Classroom kits, (6) Provision of Recreational kit to 24 schools, (7) Provision of School capitation grant to 24 schools, (8) 8 classrooms constructed, (9) 16 classrooms rehabilitated, (10) Conduct 4 PTAs on School Management targeting 80, (11) 100 Children ALP/catch-ups, (12) 4 Gender-sensitive Latrines Constructed, (13) 4 Permanent/Semi-Permanent Handwashing Construction, (14) Improved Water Source to 2 schools, (15) Provision new water sources to 2 schools, (16) 24 Schools Received Hard Copy Of COVID-19 School Protocols, (17) 8 Schools Provided with WASH minimum package, (18) 80 Schools Staff/ PTA Trained On COVID-19 School Protocols, (19) 20 Teachers/School Staff Trained on PSS / Resilience Building, (20) 24 Teachers / School Management Members Trained on School-Related GBV Prevention, (21) 24 Schools with Child Protection Referral Pathway Established, (22) Establish 2 child clubs focusing on lifesaving psychosocial support, fine arts, drama & games, peace & protection, hygiene promotion, and leadership activities, and (23) Conduct 16 community campaigns for awareness on issues of CRC/CP, GBV, the importance of education, mine risk. In the CP component, HOPE will target 8 communities, 2 in each locality, the main activities are CP Component: (27) Provide Case Management and referral services to 1600 children, (28) Establish or construct of 4 semi-permanent safe spaces/multi-purpose (child and adolescent), (29) Procure and distribute of 4 recreation PSS and dignity kits tents for emergency response, (30) Place and support 80 Unaccompanied and Separated children in Alternative care, (31) Provide Family tracing services and reunification services to 60 UASC, (32) Provision of Mental health and psychosocial support services to 80 children and adolescents, (33) Provide rehabilitation and reintegration activities of 10 children released from armed groups and armed forces , (34) Conduct 32 Community engagement and awareness, (35) Provide legal assistance to 160 children, (36) Provide legal assistance and counselling on civil documentation to 800 children, (37) Conduct of 4 community-based group activities (includes support to child protection teams, (38) Capacity building activities, including mentoring and coaching, for government, NGO staff and community-based child protection structures targeting 80 participants, and (39) Assistance to 40 children with Disability. HOPE will consider accountability to affected people through full community participation, strengthen the current community mechanism/ PTAs and CBCPN as well as do follow up for all benefit targeted separately to assess the project, identify challenges and provide give recommendations to improve the project outcomes.

1 - Needs

After the longtime of conflict and prolonged humanitarian crisis, the emergency situation in Darfur raised intensively, and the conflict between the government and armed groups and then between tribes as well as negatively affected children especially in the targeted localities. The Education and CP severity for targeted localities are 4 or 5, the People In Need (PIN) for Education are 40,425 in Gharb Jabal Marrah (Nertiti) of CD, 86,250 in Shamal Jabal Marrah (Rokero) of CD, 22,657 in Nitega of SD, and 88,207 Kereneik of WD, while PiN for CP are 41,952 in Gharb Jabal Marrah (Nertiti), 67,284 in Shamal Jabal Marrah (Rokero), 27,193 in Nitega, and 95,779 Kereneik. In the targeted localities, and based to HOPE assessments findings (Mar-May 2021) in targeted localities, the access and quality of education are very low, based on the following: - The number of pupils/children per classroom is more than 70-90 pupils, in addition to that, it is not at an adequate level even lack learning environment. - Lack of WASH facilities including latrines, water points, handwashing, and hygiene promotion, even the schools have access to WASH facilities, there are partial facilities of it. - There are more than 40% of school-age children are out of school. - Lack of school children seating. - Lack of textbooks, exercise books, and teaching aids, the ratio of the textbook. Also, based to HOPE assessments findings (Mar-May 2021) in both targets’ localities, a huge gap for child protection services for the below reasons: - Ongoing conflicts have been and newly displaced resulted in severe negative impacts on people in Jebel Mara and WD; casualties, trauma among children, females, and other groups. - Child, women, and young females have been at risk and live within a very destructive environment; do not enjoy their rights, possible incidents of GBV, poor health services, and many other issues affecting their lives. - Lack of protection services especially services to address Sexual & Domestic Violence and Family tracing & reunification, and referral system. - Lack of awareness of parents of Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC). - Poor capacities local mechanisms of Community-based Child Protection Networks for effective protection issues and concerns resolution and absence of follow up for action taken. Women and girls continue to face the risk of GBV including sexual violence when they leave the villages. Fear of reprisals from attackers often prevents rape survivors from seeking medical help. The conflict in Darfur has also increased the number of women who are the sole providers for their families. Sudan also faces issues with more chronic levels of malnutrition with one in every three children under the age of five years affected. As a result, these children will have lower lifetime earnings, and lower productivity as adults, influencing the economic growth and potential of the entire country. The child protection needs in targeting localities are lack of case management and referral services, lack safe spaces in communities, lack of legal assistance including civil documentation, and trauma experiences. The UNs reported that, the sexual violence and other forms of gender-based violence against children including teenage pregnancies and sexual exploitation have recorded to be on the rise in some states include targeting state of South Darfur, and West Darfur registering the highest. Due to the above situation, HOPE looks for improving access to Education and CP services. HOPE, through MoU that is signed with SMoE in Central Darfur and South Darfur, has access to schools with high needs in target localities as well as HOPE will fully coordinate with UNICEF and other Education and CP partners in targeting localities.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To ensure protection mainstreaming, HOPE will use the below actions: - Conduct regular consultations with particularly high-risk groups of the affected population suchas women, girls, and children with disabilities. - Support community engagement and interaction with schools through PTA meetings. - Establishment of complaints and feedback mechanisms. - Foster school-based Disaster Risk Reduction plans/strategies; to support activities that contribute to child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) prevention including psychosocial support, and referral pathways; - Teachers, learners, and parents, will be oriented on topics such as protection, human rights, sexual and gender-based violence, and non-discrimination approaches to access humanitarian aid. - Ensure that all school construction is in line with accessibility standards for persons with a disability, cash-based assistance will be provided to children with disabilities to access tools and materials to enhance their learning. - Pay attention to the choice of locations and targeting of beneficiaries to avoid perceptions that assistance is delivered in a discriminatory or non-equitable manner. - Conducting community safety audits and mapping of exiting services and ensuring that the results of these two exercises inform programming and advocacy. - Maintain transparent, inclusive, and adequate communication with communities on available services; and by consulting communities and duty bearers in the planning, selection of services, feedback after service provision. - Guided on ensuring prioritization of services to groups considered particularly vulnerable, such as young children, children with disability adolescent boys and girls, children without adult care,the elderly, and children and adults with specific needs ensuring that programming istailored to respond to the needs of adolescent boys and girls. - Involve community actors (especially CBCPN, PTAs, school teachers in this project) in the project planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation to promote participation andempowerment. - Involving and consulting CBCPN, and PTAs in planning and conducting a monthly meetings at the community level to avoid causing harm and prioritize safety and dignity.
