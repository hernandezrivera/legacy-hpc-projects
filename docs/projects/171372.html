Project Summary

This proposal presents a humanitarian response project to meet the immediate food needs of identified population in crisis in south-central Somalia to ensure that targeted households have access to safe food of the right nutritional quality and quantity. The project will use a wide variety of tools to improve household access to food, these include: (1). Direct food transfers through Food vouchers which entails the provision of coupons to receive a fixed quantity of specified commodities to households in crisis (2). Unconditional cash grants or provision of cash for the purpose of increasing access to food and (3) livelihoods assistance to protect and restore livelihoods related to food and income sources primarily through provision of agricultural inputs as well as through conditional cash transfer or cash for work for farmers targeting IPC 3 and 4 areas. The food vouchers will be designed to meet the total minimum nutritional requirements i.e. 2,100 kcals per person per day, 10% of total energy provided by protein, 17% of total energy provided by fat, and adequate micronutrient intake. It will include 50kg of Rice, 5kgs of sugar, 3.5kg of oil, and 6kgs of pulses per household per month. Agricultural input provision will help to reestablish a ‘self-help’ mode within communities affected by emergencies: once families have seed and farming tools, they can start the process of producing their own food and/or making money from selling crops, and thereby reducing their dependence on external sources for their livelihoods. Cash for Work interventions will provide employment to unskilled and semi-skilled workers on labor-intensive projects to rehabilitate irrigation systems. it will have dual objectives of Providing income support to poor, vulnerable men and women through short term and unskilled labor so they can meet their essential needs and to rehabilitate community infrastructure that is vital for sustaining livelihoods of the community and restoring agricultural activities. For accountability purposes, WOCCA will Communicate project objectives, our expectations of workers, the payment amount, and process to the community prior to implementation proper. Activities will be gender-sensitive and will promote the participation of women without prejudice to their capacity to perform the work. The wage rate is set to meet the basic needs of the targeted population. The minimum consumption basket(s) - an estimated amount of cash, or the number of basic commodities expressed in cash equivalent, needed by a household to survive for a month (estimated at 2,100 kcal/person/day) - will be used. We will cross-refer information on prevailing wages from Vulnerability Analysis and Mapping Units such as FSNAU/FEWSNET and the cash working group. Disposition of the tools such as a shovel and wheelbarrow will be done at the end of the project preferably to beneficiary groups or to the local government unit with a well-documented handover process. Given that payment is a key feature of CFW programs, various control instruments must be put in place including attendance sheets, payroll sheets, and identification details. We will run unconditional cash transfer schemes alongside CFW programs in order to extend assistance to people affected by a sudden-onset disaster who are physically unable to work. (e.g., PLWHIV, the elderly, the disabled, vulnerable children, and families with high dependency ratios). Targeting will be carried out according to clear and transparent targeting criteria. It will involve the community and local authority and use the triangulation of information to minimize inclusion and exclusion errors. Systematic respect of the «do no harm» principle so as to make sure that a response to one crisis does not increase beneficiaries’ risk-exposure and vulnerability to other crises.

Project Indicators

Context description: Humanitarian food assistance needs are high across Somalia, where Stressed (IPC Phase 2) and Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes remain widespread. An estimated 3.5 million people in rural livelihood zones, urban areas, and IDP settlements need food assistance. The below-average 2020 gu cereal harvest, economic impacts of COVID-19, and forecast of below-average deyr rainfall from October to December 2020 drive a worsening food security outlook through at least early 2021. Furthermore, a long-term decline in rainfall performance and preliminary climatological research suggest an elevated likelihood of below-average rainfall in the March to May 2021 season. Two consecutive poor seasons would likely result in rapidly worsening acute food insecurity in 2021. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

When markets are functioning and accessible, cash based assistance is more timely, effective and appropriate than its in-kind equivalent. Depending on the circumstances, cash transfers offer the following advantages: - (1). Choice: - Cash will give households a greater degree of choice and permit them to spend money according to their own priorities.(2). Cost-effectiveness: - Cash is likely to be cheaper and faster to distribute than alternatives such as restocking, seed distribution, and food distribution. (3). Dignity: - Offering cash maintains people’s dignity, by giving them choice. Delivery mechanisms do not treat them as passive recipients of relief. (4). Economic recovery: - Injections of cash have potential benefits for local markets and trade. (5). Flexibility: - Cash can be spent on both food and non-food items and is easily invested in livelihood security. (6). Empowerment Cash can improve the status of women and marginalized groups.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Besides receiving Somalia WFP Joint Markets and Supply Chain Weekly Updates, Wocca routinely provides market assessment to food security actors in Somalia to determine the functionality of food market systems in middle Shabelle, Johwar district. However due to the volatility of the security situation and adverse effects of climate in Somalia, markets can be unstable. Wocca will continually monitor market to identify whether local markets have the ability to effectively respond to increased consumer demand by verifying adequate food supply sources/levels and that the likelihood that food prices will remain stable in the short and long term.

Mitigation Measures

To ensure that members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are included in consultations and/or are not excluded from humanitarian services/assistance and that Women, girls and boys are included in consultations and decision-making processes, Wocca will employ staff representative of all groups in the community by gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group and disability. Gender balance of distribution teams and implementation committees will be considered to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community. During distributions, we will ensure that the mode and frequency of distribution minimizes safety risk putting in mind that beneficiaries may face theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion whilst taking rations home. Distributions will take place giving sufficient time to travel home before nightfall while also ensuring that the location, distance and routes to the distribution points is accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable. Separate male and female queues will be designated with female staff present at distributions so that they are accessible to women and girls. special needs of pregnant and lactating women, under 5 year olds and older persons will be responded to and the quality of their assistance made equitable to that of other groups. ration cards to female-headed households, child-headed households and unaccompanied children will be issued in their own name. Livelihoods programmes will be developed in a way that protects and supports household caring responsibilities and promotes family unity. They will be developed in a manner that ensures that both men and women have equal and fair access to work opportunities (including cash/food-for-work schemes), micro-credit, cash transfers, agricultural inputs and skills training and that they receive the same benefits for their input and work. Clear information will be provided to the affected population explaining that they do not have to pay or provide favors to anyone in exchange for receiving food assistance or being selected for livelihood projects. Deliberate efforts will be made to ensure that this information is communicated to women and girls. To ensure that delivery of humanitarian assistance does not affect peaceful coexistence and spark intercommunity conflict over access to resources and Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance does not occur Wocca will promote equitable and impartial access to all; we will Consult both displaced and host communities about food security and livelihoods needs and assess whether assistance could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities and make decisions. We will apply a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries to ensure the displaced as well as the mobile populations are not excluded, lack of documentation will not necessarily exclude individuals from accessing services Wocca will employ staff who are representative of all clans and groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) and ensure all staff and members receive protection mainstreaming training, they will be sensitized sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. This will ensure that our staff play a key role in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and be proactive in ensuring the voices of marginalized groups are represented. To further avoid community tensions between displaced and host communities we will provide the local/host community with access to camp services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of food, assets and inputs. We will implement livelihoods components in a way that protects and supports household caring responsibilities

Please provide further explanation

The planned humanitarian interventions are designed to meet not only the immediate needs but also to build resilience: Households will be provided with agricultural inputs before Deyr and GU rains and trained on improved farming techniques for improved production. Household metal silos provision and training on proper food storage will be done before harvesting to reduce post-harvest losses; it is anticipated that the households will not only preserve food for the future consumption but will also preserver seed for planting during successive rain seasons. The area lacks proper infrastructures due to poor maintenance of canals and river embankments. Reflection workshop with the government and irrigation committees will be conducted to work out modalities of restoration and maintenance of these infrastructures. The infrastructures will contribute to provision of water domestic use and irrigation purposes and also facilitate transaction of businesses through feeder roads. There will be Provision of vocational training especially to the unemployed youth and provision of materials to start up a business of their own (market surveys will be conducted to identify the most profitable business ventures and training provided based on these findings). This activity will make the youth self-dependent and less vulnerable for recruitment into militia groups.
