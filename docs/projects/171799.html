Project Summary

The most vulnerable to the challenges faced by IDPs have been identified as women who head 60% of IDP households, households headed by girls, minority and marginalized groups, and People living with disabilities or old people who have no source of income. Factors contributing to their vulnerabilities have been identifies as inadequate access of clean water and sanitations, increased.
The project will target 16,000 beneficiaries( 6,547 women, 4,958 men, 3,373 girls and 1,122 boys) living in Beledweyn, Baidoa and Belet Hawa in Gedo region. Activities will be:
•Rehabilitation of 2 existing boreholes to operationalize and/or optimize their functionality; our past studies have revealed that most boreholes have been vandalized and are not functioning. The rehabilitation will include installation of hybrid pumping equipment for both solar and diesel as alternative sources, construction of additional storage tanks with capacity of 30,000 liters, extension of piping system and construction of additional water kiosks and livestock troughs for sheep, cattle and camel specifically in per urban areas of Belet-Hawa and Beledweyn.
•Training and awareness creation on hygiene, social distancing and other COVID-19 preventions measures and management measures to 16,000 community residents in Beledweyn, Baidoa and Belet Hawa, to reduce the spread of the virus in these regions.
•Installation of 100 handwashing equipment in Beledweyn and Belet Hawa next to the rehabilitated boreholes, to improve sanitation and hygiene and reduce the spread of COVID-19.
•Construction of 200 desludgable latrines to ease congestion and risks associated with accessing latrines that are far especially for women and girls at night. The latrines will be constructed in the IDPs camps and within the poor host communities especially those that are living below the standard and in poor health conditions
•Construction of 50 disludgable latrines in Beledweyn district hospital, Baidoa district hospital and Belet-Hawa district hospital
•Installation of 50 solar streetlights to increase safety of using latrines at night
•Sanitary survey to show the locations of the planned and existing infrastructures with their current functional levels. In all target locations hygiene promotion will be undertaken including at the IDPs camp and the poor host communities.
•Conditional cash transfer system will be adopted through the cleaning campaign and each beneficiary will be given USD100 per month for two months to allow the collection of solid wastes. The cash transfer system will be issued through mobile transfer system and through EVC to each individual sim card. A total of 100 beneficiaries will receive the cash transfer .
•Sanitation tools will be procured and issued to the beneficiaries who are doing the sanitation exercise in the areas, the tools include wheelbarrow, rakes, and spades.
•Production of IEC material; the materials will be cultural center and pictorial showing all best hygiene practices will be done and the messages will be done and convey in the local vernacular language and not only in Somalia but will be contextualized in the area set up depending on the local indigenous communities. The material will also include COVID-19 prevention and management measures.
•Desludging of the existing latrines to ensure there is the sustainability of the existing latrines specially in the institution and at the poor host communities.
•Participation of women as well as people with disability is ensured right from the collection of needs assessment data where 70% of the respondents were women. Data reveals that 7% of the target households have at least one person with disability living with them. Design of infrastructure will take into consideration the needs of special groups.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The Markets in Belet Hawa, Beletweyne, and Baidoa are able to supply the necessary goods and services at appropriate prices and quality and the beneficiaries also have easy access to the markets. In addition, cash transfer was deemed the most effective intervention as the recipients need to be given more choice, supporting their dignity, supporting livelihoods, strengthening local markets, and promoting financial inclusion.The relative cost of providing cash transfer is cheaper than in-kind support to the beneficiaries.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The feasibility of cash transfer rests on the expected future ability of markets to respond to increased demand if cash were provided, and not simply on the state of the market before any intervention. Decision-making included consideration of possible measures that could be taken to address any market constraints (e.g. addressing constraints facing traders, such as capital, credit, storage and transport). Seasonal and regional trends can all influence prices. Possible price rises caused by external factors do not rule out the use of cash transfers if they can be managed through the programme design; although in our case, we do not anticipate abnormal price rises due to any factors - both internal and external. If in-kind assistance is purchased locally or regionally, the ability of traders to ensure timely supplyat an appropriate price and quality may be critical. While the local market is potentially able to supply the needs of the beneficiaries in a timely manner, the program design team also considered the need to give the beneficiaries some freedom on how to use their money and purchase what they want, when they need it, to improve their dignity.

Mitigation Measures

SYPD will generally observe the mainstreaming principally, the location and access routes to WASH facilities and distribution points are safe and accessible to the vulnerable people and IDPs, people with disabilities and do no harm principle is observed. During the distibution of hygiene kits the frequency of distribution minimizes exposure to safety threats and SYPD will ensure that the beneficiaries will not face any theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion whilst taking their hygiene kits home. SYPD will conduct constant community consultations meetings to identify barriers to the participation of women, girls, marginalized and minority groups and youth to ensure that risks regarding exclusion based on gender and age are identified and mitigated in a timely manner. The project implementation will also include the formation of feedback mechanism that involves women, men, and youth to gather information from beneficiaries’ access to assistance and provide timely feedback on the project achievement. SYPD will carry out careful and deliberate inclusion of women, girls, youth and other vulnerable groups in the project design, implementation and monitoring of the project implementation. Project committees will be all inclusive of gender and age. SYPD will train gender balanced committees to strengthen the capacity of vulnerable groups in participating in project implementation. To ensure that women and girls have access to assistance without exposing them to risks such as violence, SYPD will manage assistance provision proximity to the dwelling places and in day light to allow safe travel back home. Distributions will be managed through effective crowd control mechanisms and separate lines for men, women and people with special needs.The beneficiaries, local authorities, camp leaders, village leaders, local government representatives and host communities’ leaders will be factored in during the program implementation. They will oversee the beneficiary selection process throughout the project. To avoid abuses against civilians, including wide spread sexual and gender-based violence (GBV), recruitment and use of children, indiscriminate and disproportionate attacks on civilian areas and infrastructure, explosive hazards and forced displacement while Women, children, youth and marginalized communities, especially the most vulnerable (older people, persons with disabilities, etc.), SYPD will ensure that beneficiaries are not exposed to armed conflicts and violence by nature of assistance provided by undertaking community consultations during the project mobilization, communication and implementation to understand local dynamics and take due care in project design. SYPD will conduct pre-intervention assessment and do evaluation to identify any risks and community dynamics that may lead to violence or armed conflict. In particular, stakeholder consultations and participation will be mainstreamed from the onset of the project to final hand over. Also, SYPD will ensure the participation of all on affected communities, inclusion of all stakeholders in WASH projects and coordination with government authorities.SYPD will also undertake pre-context analysis to identity the existing government institutions like the presence judicial framework available in the area of intervention so that any protection issues arise, the survivor can go and look for solutions through legal process. As one of the humanitarian principles is do no harm, SYPD will avoid overcrowding during the distributions as it may arise protection concerns The entitlements of the project will be done through clearly well label burners and complin mechanisms established at the distribution centersAll date collected on beneficiaries and vendors will be securely kept and managed in a manner that is consistent with the EU GDPR regulations. Date subject consent will be obtained before the data is collected, processed or transferred to third parties.
