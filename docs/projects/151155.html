Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to ensure that children of school going age who have been affected by emergency crises (famine/drought/conflict) in Kismayo and Afmadhow districts of Lower Juba region are able to access immediate learning opportunities. Further, the intervention proposes to support learner retention, improve learning environment and strengthen/improve quality of effective service delivery A keen focus will be placed on system strengthening to ensure sustainability, and enhancing education capacity by particularly strengthening education in emergencies preparedness and response planning of the education system in Jubbaland. The intervention has been designed to meet the needs of vulnerable girls, women, boys and men, IDPs and host communities in targeted districts.

Needs

As a result of the protracted conflict and collapse of education system, a high number of children have been left with no access to education in Somalia and for those who do access it, retention and continuation is a challenge as institutional education capacity remains weak with no trained teachers in service and a severe lack of learning facilities. In Lower Juba region particularly the districts of Afmadhow and Kismayu, the education sector has significantly suffered from these crises leading to a sharp increase in the number of children forced out of school. Needs assessment conducted by ADRA in these two districts indicate that the main barriers to accessing education is lack of learning facilities (only two public primary schools were functional in Afmadow town during the assessment in June 2018 - Afmadow and Waamo Intermediate School), inability of families to meet the cost of education and for girls in particular household chores and lack of gender sensitive facilities in schools. Without learning and the protective environment that schools provide, more boys and girls in Kismayo and Afmadhow are vulnerable to and at risk of child labour and recruitment by armed groups or criminal groups. Girls face a higher risk of dropping out of school and become exposed to early marriage and domestic violence. A significant percentage of out of school children in the targeted districts (70%) consist of IDP children, and there is therefore need of assistance for IDPs with a focus for increased access, retention as well as sustainability. In both districts, IDPs suffer from the greatest educational inequities, in addition to females, nomadic/pastoralist and rural communities. There is therefore a need to support these vulnerable and marginalized IDP girls and boys with education services aimed at ensuring they have access to learning and retention in a safe and protected learning environment.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

In order to enable durable solutions/resilience, displacement affected populations have been put at the centre of all planned activities. The intervention has also been designed within the Somalia National Development Plan, as a way of ensuring that hat government engagement and agreed policies are at the centre of planned actions, both at federal and state levels. Further, the program has been designed to ensure inclusion of participatory approaches that will not discriminate between different people (host, IDPs, returnees), and will continue throughout project implementation. Broadly, the program has been designed to ensure sustainable local integration for IDPs in their present locations in Kismayu and Afmadhow. This action will thus engage local authorities and CECs in the quest to develop the most ideal and sustainable solutions to provide and maintain education service delivery in targeted IDP camps and host communities. Only sustainable solutions will be chosen and implemented. Capacity building will not only emphasize technical operations but simple education management procedures. Local governance structures will be closely involved to ensure support and ownership.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Drought and conflict continue to be the main drivers causing displacement in Somalia. Combined, these two drivers have forced more than 2.1 million to be displaced. Over 80,000 children are also out of school due to drought induced displacement. Young boys and girls/youth/young adolescents make a significant proportion of this displaced population. Given their vulnerable circumstances, this population chapter is often faced with serious violations including forced recruitment into armed groups, engagement in petty theft and organized crimes or sexual exploitation that end up ruining their lives and future. Another driver for conflict over resource and resource distribution (e.g. water points and usage, rangelands and economic opportunities like employment). Efforts will be made to ensure the opinions and viewpoints of minority clans, disadvantaged, less educated, and marginalized groups are taken into consideration in a participatory and inclusive manner, and that the implementation process and benefits of the action and related service delivery are realized in an equitable manner that does not result in any exclusion or deprivation of any of these groups or persons. Careful selection and prioritization of targets will be done to enhance equity. Working in close liaison with Jubbaland authorities also, ADRA will ensure the implementation of special measures to prevent the displacement of persons, especially those with a special dependency on or attachment to their land (vulnerable hosts, minorities, IDPs and pastoralists). Further, the intervention will put into place provisions for the protection of specific groups (e.g. women, children, older persons, and minorities) which will subsequently enhance IDP protection. In order to address the causes of displacement, ADRA will work with local and national authorities to among other issues, put in place measures to stop, prevent or mitigate the causes of flight and displacement.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

ADRA understands that conflict and displacement affect individuals differently, depending on their age, gender, and diversity (AGD). In this regard, the intervention will seek to ensure that all targeted people fully participate in and enjoy their rights on an equal footing with others, regardless of their age or gender. Some of the risks faced by this target group include armed attack and abuse, heightened risk of sexual and gender-based violence and displacement. The program design will be based on assessments, both of protection needs and the capacities of affected and host communities based on gender and age, with targeted actions being identified to address the specific needs of the population of concern. These activities will be implemented with care, making sure that inequalities are not exacerbated and that individuals or groups are not improperly excluded. The proposed action will also ensure equal participation of people of both gender in all aspects of the project. Gender segregated data will be collected for planning and reporting. ADRA will ensure that service delivery considers and addresses the distinct needs of boys, girls, men and women in a systematic manner.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Major risks to armed conflict and violence in Afmadhow and Kismayo districts consist of: armed attack and abuse while fleeing in search of safety; family separation, including an increase in the number of separated and unaccompanied children; heightened risk of sexual and gender-based violence, particularly affecting women and children; arbitrary deprivation of land, homes and other property; and displacement into inhospitable environments, where they suffer stigmas, marginalization, discrimination or harassment. In order to address this, the intervention will implement protection interventions which will ensure safety from violent acts and exploitation. This will include a system for the prevention of and response to gender-based violence (GBV), child protection system as well as a mechanism to deal with the risks faced by persons with specific needs in both districts. Protection activities by the intervention will also include ensuring safety from the effects of armed conflict for targeted population. Further, tested conflict-sensitive approaches effective at addressing risks related to the alienation and exclusion of adolescents and youth will be employed.
