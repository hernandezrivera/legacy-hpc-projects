Project Summary

A protracted humanitarian crisis aggravated by a prolonged dry weather risked escalating Somaliland’s unstable and fragile drought crisis. The humanitarian impact of the prolonged drought has been devastating, with acute malnutrition levels worsening. According to the FSNAU 2021 Post-Gu assessment results indicate that food insecurity is expected to deteriorate among poor rural, urban, and displaced populations. The anticipated causes include below-average 2021 Deyr (October-December) season rainfall, locust infestations, and other food security-related shocks including a likely reduction in agricultural produce during the Deyr season. Moreover, the Post-Gu 2021 projections indicate that the food security situation for the majority of the IDPs and rural population in Somaliland will deteriorate from Crisis (IPC3) to Emergency (IPC4) in the absence of urgent integrated interventions to support recovery and prevent further worsening of nutrition situation.
The project will provide integrated nutrition preventive and treatment services targeting children less than five years (boys and girls) as well as pregnant and lactating women. It will support the identification, referral and treatment of children suffering from severe acute malnutrition including SAM cases with medical complications. In addition, the project will support community mobilization and screening as well as micro-nutrient supplementation for PLW (multiple micro-nutrients and Iron folate) and Vitamin A supplementation for children between 6-59 months as well as home-based food fortification with micronutrient powder targeting children from 6 to 23 months. It will also provide de-worming for children 12-59 months and pregnant women in the 2nd and 3rd trimester as well as counselling and promotion on infant and young child feeding compounded with health and hygiene promotion. Moreover, measles immunization will be provided to infants living within the catchment areas of the target health centers who are not immunized against measles. Systematic defaulter tracing for immunization services will also be conducted at community level. ANPPCANSOM will support 36 fixed OTP sites, 2 stabilization centers and 58 community-based IYCF sites scattered in Oodweine, Sheikh, Berbera, Hargeisa, Gabiley and Borama districts of Somaliland. Every effort will be made to ensure integration of prevention and treatment services.

In order to strengthen the accountability with affected population, we will introduce mother-led MUAC approach to increase detection, identification and early referral of malnourished children. ANPPCANSOM will train mothers to use MUAC tapes to enable mothers screen, detect oedema and self-referral of their children to nutrition sites
ANPPCANSOM will maintain treatment outcomes consistent with Sphere standards using the combined protocol for the management of acute malnutrition in close consultation and coordination with Somaliland Nutrition Working, Nutrition Cluster, Somaliland Nutrition, M0HD, UNICEF, WFP and other nutrition partners. ANPPCANSOM will build on and sustain the delivery of a holistic basic nutrition services package (BNSP) in priority to the target communities including internally displaced people and their host communities and other socially marginalized groups in pastoral and hard to reach in Oodweine, Sheikh, Berbera, Hargeisa, Gabiley and Borama districts of Somaliland while ensuring the inclusion of treatment, promotion and prevention components in this project. Likewise, the project will give particular emphasis on the current COVID-19 pandemic prevention measure to contain the spread of the virus. Gender and protection activities will be effectively.
