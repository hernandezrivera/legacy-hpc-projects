Project Summary

The project aims to improve access to nutrition services for the vulnerable groups of people affected by both man- made and natural disasters in Ulang,Nasir, Magwi and Kapoeta East. The project locations were chosen based on county-specific vulnerability related not limited to flooding and COVID-19 Pandemic that affected several counties, intercommunal fights, cattle raids, loss of property, displacements across the country. The objectives of the project are aligned with the nutrition cluster strategic objectives including; (1)Increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventive nutrition specific service delivery for children and woen in Ulang,Nasir,Magwi,Kapoeta East by the end of 2021. (2)Increase equitable access to live-saving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls, boys and under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in the project locations (3). Enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring analysis and utilization of nutrition information (4) Increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL ,education and protection through enhanced coordination and joint programming, targeting vulnerable groups in Ulang, Nasir, magwi, Kapoeta East Counties respectively. The project will target 139200 beneficiaries and 89,000 vulnerable children under five years of age with acute malnutrition in the counties.
According the REACH report of September 2020, the counties of Gogrial East, Nasir, and Ulang have living standard gaps of >75% for health, FSL, WASH, and Vulnerability for the general population. These counties have suffered the wrath of floods, insecurity, and effect of covid-19 preventive measures that escalated their food insecurity levels.
UNKEA proposes to support the implementation of several nutrition activities as follows; in Nasir, 3 OTP sites that have been established in hard-to-reach locations (i.e. Nasir, Dhekdhek, and Homkor) and establishment of 10 outreach/mobile OTP sites that would cover the whole county especially in Kiechkuon and 2 outreach sites to cover Burebeiy, Makak, and Maker areas which are returnees transit and IDP areas. In Ulang the project will support locations (Duk, Nyarkueth, Wunbut, and Torkiel) that are prone to insecurity, flooding and hosting IDPs with nutrition services and additional 10 outreach sites will be established. In Gogrial East establish 3 integrated mobile nutrition/health sites in 3 PHCUs Amethjak, Panacier and Majak nyuom. These sites are in hard to reach locations and most times accessibility to the nearest OTP/TSFP site is a major challenge to the affected populations. In Magwi and Kapoeta county the implementation of the RTGoNU peace agreement has brought some calm, these has led to the return of refugees who do not have shelter and basic items including food for their children. Due to the sparse nature of the population, NGOs have not been able to reach several areas. There is need to support these areas.
The project will implement the following activities in the selected counties;
Training health/nutrition staff on MIYCN guidelines; Training MSG & CNVs on MIYCN; Establishing baby mother friendly shelter; Training health/Nutrition care providers and CNVs on CMAM guideline; Providing routine care/management of children with SAM in OTP site; Establishment of community feedback mechanism; Training of health/nutrition workers on Malaria testing for children admitted with SAM; Training of health/nutrition workers on GBV/CP referral; GBV risk assessment for all nutrition sites; Provision of vitamin A to children 6-59mths and Albendazole to children 12-59mths;

1 - Needs

In 2020 the emergence of covid-19 pandemic and the eventual prevention measures recommended has increased the effects of various shocks to the livelihoods of general population as there were halts to some aspects of services. Acute food insecurity at the household driven by lack of food; high dependency on expensive food commodities in markets; climatic shocks such as flooding, pests and diseases such as covid-19; low household coping mechanisms; limited livelihood opportunities; the prevailing poor micro/macro-economic conditions in the country has worsened the nutrition situation in the country.According the REACH report of September 2020, the counties of Gogrial East, Nasir, and Ulang have living standard gaps of >75% for health, FSL, WASH, and Vulnerability for the general population. The recently conducted IPC puts Gogrial East, Nasir and Ulang counties in IPC 4 (emergency). These counties have suffered the wrath of floods, insecurity, and effect of covid-19 preventive measures that escalated their food insecurity levels. These coupled with high morbidity, limited access to basic services, food insecurity as well as poor child care and feeding practices which contribute negatively to the prevalence of acute malnutrition. Therefore, continued scale up of treatment of acute malnutrition targeting such populations and expansion of services to previously insecure and inaccessible areas is a priority. In Ulang and Nasir, hosting of returnees and IDPs by the community has led to overstretch of their coping mechanism to shocks. This has been aggravated by the massive floods, intercommunal fights which has caused displacements, asset loss and worse humanitarian situation for people. Nutrition interventions to access-constrained locations is therefore much needed since the existing project doesn’t reach those areas. In Gogrial East county, the 2020 floods created inaccessibility to across river Jur from Lietnhom for the whole year. UNKEA is implementing health in Gogrial East, however the northern part of the county is not supported with the HPF funding. There are 3 PHCUs (Amethnhom, Panacier and Majak nyuom) which are not supported with health and nutrition services in Gogrial East. These locations are bordering Unity state and have cases of acute malnutrition. Mothers travel long distances to Gogrial West county to access nutrition services in Alek, Kuajok and other areas or cross the swamp to Lietnhom. To combat these challenges, UNKEA proposes to support; In Gogrial East establish 3 integrated mobile nutrition/health sites in 3 PHCUs Amethjak, Panacier and Majak nyuom. These sites are in hard to reach locations and most times accessibility to the nearest OTP/TSFP site is a major challenge to the affected populations. In Nasir, 3 OTP sites that have been established in hard-to-reach locations (i.e. Nasir, Dhekdhek, and Homkor) and establishment of 10 outreach OTP sites that would cover the whole county especially in Kiechkuon and 2 outreach sites to cover Burebeiy, Makak, and Maker areas which are returnees transit and IDP areas. In Ulang the project will support locations (Duk, Nyarkueth, Wunbut, and Torkiel) that are prone to insecurity, flooding and hosting IDPs with nutrition services and additional 10 outreach sites will be established.UNKEA has operational presence in Eastern Equatoria with the main coordination office in Torit. Currently UNKEA is implementing FSL activities in Kapoeta and Magwi Counties. Kapoeata East county is in IPC phase 4, the socio-economic and cultural dynamics exacerbates the livelihoods coupled with the nomadic pastoralist nature of the population. In Magwi county the implementation of the RTGoNU peace agreement has brought some calm, these has led to the return of refugees from Uganda who do not have shelter and basic items including food for their children. Due to the sparse nature of the population, NGOs have not been able to reach several areas. There is need to support these areas.

2 - Project activities

UNKEA proposes to support the realization of strategic objectives and sector objectives as follows: Strategic Objective 1: With Specific Objective 1.2: The project will contribute to the cluster objectives 1, 2, and 3. Strategic Objective 2: with Specific Objective 2.1: the project will contribute to cluster objective 4. The project will implement the following activities in the selected counties;- Training health/nutrition staff on MIYCN guidelines. - Training MSG & CNVs on MIYCN. - Establishing baby mother friendly areas/shades for care and key messages education sessions. - Training health/Nutrition care providers and CNVs on CMAM guideline. - Providing routine care/management of children with SAM in OTP site. - Establishment of community feedback mechanism. - Training of health/nutrition workers on Malaria testing for children admitted with SAM. - Procuring and distributing ECD kits to all nutrition sites for stimulation of children admitted with SAM. - Training of health/nutrition workers on GBV/CP referral. - Conducting GBV risk assessment for all nutrition sites.- Prevention of malnutrition through behaviour change communication by provision of MIYCN counselling to caregivers of children 0-23 months and conducting cooking demonstrations at selected nutrition sites and mobile sites targeting caregivers of children 0-23 months. - Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies by provision of vitamin A to children 6-59mths and Albendazole to children 12-59mths every semester.- Early detection and treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition by screening, admitting and provision of care to children with SAM, MAM and PLWs with Acute Malnutrition. - Strengthening of program monitoring by conducting regular monitoring visits to ensure services are provided as per the sphere standards. - Promotion of community participation for a responsive nutrition service delivery by establishing community feedback mechanism at nutrition sites. - Improve facility-based data collection and management by ensuring all nutrition information are entered into the NIS. - Prevention, testing and treating for malaria among children under five years with acute malnutrition by testing all children admitted with SAM and children with MAM who have sign/symptoms of malaria.- Provision of WASH kits to children with SAM in nutrition sites- Establishing or maintaining demonstration kitchen garden at 35 nutrition sites.- Linking children admitted in Nutrition program with Cash and Voucher Assistance program- Integration of child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM by provision of child play materials- Mainstreaming of child protection and GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites by supporting GBV survivors at nutrition sites and referring them to GBV response services, and conducting GBV safety audits in all nutrition sites.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

UNKEA has PSEA policy that is used to guide UNKEA staff and anyone affiliated to the organization. All programs implemented by the organization has PSEA issues addressed from programing, implementation aspects. During community engagements the protection related issues including PSEA will be passed to the community. In this project, information about PSEA shall be passed during events that gather community such as cooking demonstrations, nutrition education sessions, MIYCN counselling sessions and community dialogue events. Such entry points of information passage shall be employed to inform the community about protection against sexual exploitation and abuse.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project implementation process will not be so segregate of the most vulnerable community members when gathering feedback. There will be no distinction between the different groups of community members. Vulnerable community members will be listened to and given the chance to express their opinion in any way possible. For instance, language of implementation of the project will be the local language, therefore even the illiterate will be able to give a feedback since it is in their language. Priority shall be given to women, girls, and disabled persons to give feedback and their feedbacks are taken into considerations.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Before implementation: UNKEA will engage with the community leaders and other interested community members in an orientation meeting (project initiation meeting) in all the counties that the organization will support. This meeting will be used to disseminate information about the project, the selection criteria, the objectives of the project and expected community participation and inputs to the project. During the meeting, minutes will be taken and attendance list generated. The information that will be got during the project initiation meeting will be used to guide the implementation of the project. During implementation: UNKEA will establish site based community feedback committees who will act as a link between UNKEA project team and the community. These feedback committees shall comprise of local authorities, youth leaders, women leaders and any other resource persons. Regular engagements shall be made with the feedback committees during which key community issues shall be identified and addressed. The organization will use the same committees for delivering any community related information. After implementation: UNKEA will, in the event of closure of the project, maintain the already established structures for future use. The Feedback committees who will have been instrumental in the delivery of the project shall be used for future project in their area.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

As much as the project will employ all avenues available and appropriate based on context for purpose of information gathering to guide the implementation of the project, information collected either written or verbal from all sources such as suggestion boxes, anonymous letters, direct engagement with community during meetings, etc. the information shall be used to guide and advice project management. Such information when got, will be shared with the senior management of the project, discussed with the local authorities, and ways forward deviced to address the issues based on the magnitude and dynamics of the feedback, that would suit the project assistance best.
