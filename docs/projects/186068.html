Project Summary

The nutrition response aimed at scaling up nutrition interventions for the management of acute malnutrition in Konye and Kumba Health districts in the South West Regions will support the under-funded nutrition cluster HRP 2021 strategy and response framework hence creating a significant impact in the nutrition response plan for 2022.
Caritas Kumba will undertake both passive and exhaustive screening of children under-5 for acute malnutrition, appropriate treatment of uncomplicated SAM cases and referral of complicated SAM cases. Simplified approaches to management of acute malnutrition to improve coverage to care for children suffering from wasting and edema. Also the provision of cash to cover for the transportation expenses of caregivers seeking “inpatient” treatment of SAM treatment will be provided. Family MUAC/Mother MUAC approach will be scaled up to enhance early identification of SAM and MAM cases. Preventative actions that will include promotion of infant and young child feeding in emergencies (IYCF-E), deworming prophylaxis and micronutrient supplementation - Vitamin A supplementation (VAS) and iron folate supplementation with the aim of addressing micronutrient deficiencies shall be incorporated. For 2022, Caritas shall improve on the equitable access of iron/folic acid supplementation and deworming prophylaxis by pregnant women and breastfeeding mothers - with special attention to pregnant adolescent girls.
To reinforce partners and the community’s capacity to deliver basic nutrition services to affected communities, the need for refresher courses to revisit the IMAM protocol by Trainers of Trainees (ToT) at regional level to enforce their ability to train community health workers will be necessary. Caritas Kumba will continuously liaise with the Regional delegation of Public health in implementing appropriate nutrition interventions without affecting health system. To enhance collaboration, endeavors to participate and support nutrition cluster activities and enhance multisectoral collaboration with other nutrition sensitive clusters (Health, Food Security, WASH and protection) to realize optimal nutritional outcomes shall be emphasised.
The “do no harm” principle shall be implemented through the training of all staff on core humanitarian principles mainstream aspects related to gender, protection centrality, age, gender, disability and Accountability to Affected Population (AAP). Interventions will also be adapted in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic.
The outstanding performance in collaboration with UNICEF over the years has given Caritas KUmba a high level of access and acceptance within Kumba III and Konye due to her over 3 years experience in the implementation of General Food distribution as well as WASH and Child Protection Interventions, within these communities. Intervention will use static health structures for inpatient care, rapid response mechanism (RRM) and comprehensive child response (CCR) approaches to access the affected population both in accessible and those in hard to reach remote areas.
The target groups will include boys and girls (irrespective of their disabilities/abilities) under-five for screening, referral, children with SAM, Vitamin A supplementation and deworming prophylaxis. Adolescent, pregnant and breastfeeding will be targeted for the iron and folic acid and deworming prophylaxis. Health workers (Male and Female) aged over 18 years will be targeted for training on SAM management (mobilize, screen and refer acutely malnourished) and IYCF-E.
Konye and Kumba have recorded very low access to health care services with most caregivers and mothers resorting to the use of traditional methods in managing malnutrition. It is extremely important to manage this diseases at the community level since the hospital of these vulnerable communities are faraway from their homes and the highly impoverish communities lacks the financial capacity to pay hospital bills.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The situation in the North-West and South-West regions (NWSW) which has evolved in to an armed conflict remains worrisome, with women and children most vulnerably exposed inter-alia to violence, abuse and exploitation leading to continuous displacement. According to OCHA, in October 2021, several roadblocks, lockdowns and ghost towns obstructed humanitarian access, preventing the delivery of aid to affected people. Due to security challenges, 1.1 million people are severely food insecure in the Cadre Harmonise report, published in March 2021. Despite the prevalence of food insecurity, only 387 children including 207 girls have been identified with SAM and referred for treatment. This represents just 5.0 per cent of 8,000 estimated SAM cases targeted for 2021. This is largely due to the low coverage with less than 20% funds made available for nutrition activities and poor access to communities in need of nutritional assistance. With the foreseen nutrition situation deterioration due to the ongoing crisis, as anticipated by the nutrition cluster, 189,000 children, boys, girls and women in the NWSW regions will require emergency nutrition services in 2021. This includes an estimated 108,000 children and 81,000 women with an estimated 8,000 children under five will be severely wasted and require life-saving treatment and if left untreated, it can lead to an elevated increased risk of death. Konye and Kumba Health Districts in Meme Division in the South west region of Cameroon is one of the main areas highly hit by the crisis with numerous displacement of inhabitants. The communities however, live under precarious conditions with severe low level of food security and poor access to healthcare facilities. Base on the strength of the forgoing, there is a high risk of acute malnutrition in these communities and hence a probable surge in the rate of morbidity and mortality for infants under 5 years. The following needs shall be addressed through the activities listed below; - Active Screening to Identify Malnourished Children: 20000 beneficiaries to be reached These will be done with the use of Trained Community health Workers (50) and Caritas Nutrition team - Family MUAC Approach: Need: 500 caregivers will be trained Mothers and caregivers will be train to effectively screen for MUAC and oedema and hence widen the scope of the screening. - Sensitisation of IYCF-E: 25000 caregivers shall be sensitized on the importance of Infant and Young Child feeding Practice at emergencies with the use of counseling cards - Treatment and referral of complicated 100 SAM cases in the Areas of intervention with the use of RUTF. Malnourished children without complication shall be treated at the OTP while complicated Children shall be referred to the IPF; St. John Hospital Fiango Kumba.
