Project Summary

This is a year project which will focus on strengthening the capacity of management of severe acute malnutrition across the BAY states. It will strengthen the capacity of existing government health workers and partner organizations in provision of quality treatment to over 25,000 SAM children with medical complications across the accessible LGAs in the BAY states. WHO will also preposition essential medicines for the smooth running of the Stabilization Centres to ensure uninterrupted supply of commodities to the implementing partners. This will be achieved via procurement and distribution of 390 SAM kits to the various SCs and support the establishment of new ones. Also over 350 health care staff will be trained on inpatient management of SAM with complications and supportive supervision and mentoring. The limited capacity of health care workers which is worsened by high staff turnovers (attrition rate) in especially the security compromised areas is an additional justification to sustain these training sessions. The training in 2020 will focus on T.O.T(training of trainers) which will focus more on resident health care professionals to ensure continuity. Some of these professionals will also be rotated to SCs with low capacity for regular on-site sessions. The project will also facilitate access to life-saving nutrition services through early detection and referral of malnourished children especially from Hard-To-Reach areas to nutrition treatment centres for timely intervention. It will complement the humanitarian nutrition response of the sector in general and WHO’s efforts in particular by addressing unmet nutrition and health needs. In addition, this project will build on the current achievements of addressing unmet nutrition needs for complicated SAM cases e.g the planned expansion of SC bed spaces and setting up of new ones. Partners will be supported with the sustained supply of SAM kits throughout the project lifespan. It will also support the State MoHs by providing integrated life-saving nutrition services to the under-5 children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in the priority areas. The services will be provided through 113 mobile health teams positioned in hard-to-Reach communities in the states by enhancing their capacity to strengthen community mobilization activities for timely detection of SAM children through routine screening for malnutrition and subsequent referral to the nearest treatment centres at the same time promoting IYCF practices. In summary, project is to support the following aspects:
• Identification and referral of children with SAM children in HTR areas plus Protection, promotion and support of optimal IYCF practices in these locations.
• Strengthen inpatient care of SAM through full integration of SAM treatment into existing secondary health care centres.
• Procurement of life saving drugs nutrition supplies namely SAM Kits.
• Training of Trainers for Health workers on inpatient management of SAM and Mentoring/Coaching.
and supporting the state structure with joint supportive supervision with on-the-job coaching and mentoring sessions.
• Establishment of nutrition surveillance system on inpatient management of SAM

1 - Needs

The insurgency has caused mass population displacement throughout Northeast Nigeria since 2012. Preliminary results from the Round 7 North East Nutrition & Food Security Surveillance reveals that there is still high global acute malnutrition (GAM) & severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rates in majority of the domains when compared to the previous 6 rounds of October 2016 to Oct 2018, and the problem is worse in Southern part of Yobe state. Weighted analysis reveals that GAM rates are at serious level 13% in Yobe (with Southern Yobe having the highest prevalence of 13.3% but with SAM rate of 1.1%) and 12.5% in Borno(MMC/Jere) based on WHO classification. Humanitarian access to new areas remains very fragile. Whilst government is making efforts to return and resettle the IDPs, there is also increased incidence of attacks and threats in previously safe and accessible areas leading to new displacements e.g Gubio,Magumeri, Nganzai etc thus increasing, revealing significant humanitarian needs. The overall nutrition situation in the North East has remained precarious for the past few years. Decrease in food production and household income as a result of the ongoing conflict is one of the key factors that contributed to the deterioration of nutrition in NE Nigeria. The situation is further aggravated by the increased incidences of Malaria and diarrheal diseases which is exerting more demand on an already stretched health services. Findings from (JANFSA) conducted in October-November 2018 showed that prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) remains at serious levels in several domains, particularly in Borno and Yobe states.Age disaggregated findings show that prevalence of GAM was highest among children in the younger age cohorts (6-11 and 12-23 months) compared to older counterparts under the age of five. Some of the drivers of malnutrition were poor dietary diversity, low rates of exclusive breastfeeding (particularly in Yobe), incidence of common childhood illnesses (fever, diarrhoea, cough etc.) and poor water, sanitation and hygiene practices. Under five mortality rates among children under five exceeded the emergency threshold in Southern Adamawa and Northern Yobe B, but was also high in other domains.The management of acute malnutrition with medical complication in BAY states have been outstretched beyond capacity. Also, the increased incidences of Malaria, AWD and other waterborne diseases exerts more demand on already stretched services hence the increased risk of mortality. According to the recent flood update shared by UNOCHA in august 2019 there is increased number of severely malnourished children with medical complications (due to diarrhea) in all Stabilization Centres resulting in congestion. Over 33% increase of new Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) admissions in July/August compared to previous months, mostly attributed to increased cases of diarrhea. An increase of SAM cases is directly linked to gaps in the response of other sectors, for example, unhygienic conditions, lack of food, and water-borne diseases that increasing the prevalence of malnutrition. Considering the increased influx of new arrivals and returnees with high prevalence of SAM due to the deprivations they suffered while they were inaccessible. Currently, there are 38 functional Stabilization Centres(SCs) in the BAY states but some LGAs do not have any. This is mostly due to insecurity. It is recommended to set up SCs in secondary level health facilities as a measure of preparedness to respond to outbreaks that cause spikes in SAM+MC (e.g., associated with cholera, seasonal malaria and other disease outbreaks.On the other hand, the existing capacity of the health delivery system and the actors in the state is limited. If these triggers continue to go unabated it means 10% of the SAM cases are at risk of dying with no treatment measures put in place. The case fatality rate of complicated SAM cases can be as high as 30-40% without treatment.

2 - Activities

The main thrust of WHO in emergencies is to strengthen, through information dissemination and training; the national, sub-national and international capacities of partners to meet the nutritional needs of populations in emergencies. This includes mainly supporting government and partners in-patient management of SAM with medical complications with supplies and capacity building, provision of effective technical support through the production and dissemination of scientifically validated and up-to-date guidelines, norms, criteria and methodologies relating to nutritional standards. WHO will: • Provide integrated nutrition services (MUAC screening, IYCF ) to children in Hard to reach areas of Borno ,Yobe and Adamawa State during 12 months through routine screening and prompt referral for malnutrition by mobile teams,provision of IYCF counseling to caregivers of children under 2 years. • Provide lifesaving drugs for treatment of SAM children with medical complications: More than 20,000 severely acute malnourished children with medical complications will be provided treatment with lifesaving drugs through the provision of 390 inpatient kits to the stabilization centres across the 3 states. The kits consist of basic, supplementary and malaria drugs as well as include some equipment and supplies for the SC. • WHO will conduct 12 training each six days on inpatient management of SAM according to the national protocol. About 25 SC staff from MoH and partner iNGOs will participate and benefit from each of the training. • WHO will conduct regular supportive supervision field visits to at least bi weekly to nutrition stabilization centres. SC staff will be provided on the job training on gaps identified in their knowledge and skills. WHO will also conduct coaching sessions with SC staff to sit with them in detail and support and demonstrate addressing the gaps identified. WHO will also develop job aids/IEC material for the SCs, some of them will be pasted on the walls as important reference material for the staff. • Nutrition surveillance system will be established strengthen These interventions will improve nutrition status and infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices among most vulnerable population of target areas. It will also reduce morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition in the BAY states. Close collaboration with sector partners including SMoH, Hospital management board, nutrition sector, health sector, and partner organizations will be maintained to review needs, assess progress and devise a strategy for strengthening inpatient management of SAM.
