Project Summary

The project provides support to the most vulnerable, conflict-affected girls, boys and women in South Sudan who are exposed to child protection risks in targeted areas of Raga, Wau, Uror, and Pochalla counties of Western Barl El Ghazal State and Jonglei State respectively.
To address the needs the project will provide frontline services to most affected populations through; the provision of comprehensive case management, provision of family tracing and reunification, provision of psychosocial support services (PSS), conduct child protection assessments and monitoring using the existing and recommended tools (monitoring of grave child rights violations through the monitoring and reporting mechanism (MRM)), establishing and supporting of referral mechanisms, conduct Mine Risk Education (MRE), conduct awareness-raising & prevention messaging and Peacebuilding and peaceful coexistence activities.
To avoid overlapping and duplication of activities in the targeted locations Hold the Child will work closely with other child protection partners in mapping the areas with the close supervision and support from the Child Protection Sub-Cluster at the National level and the Child Protection Working Groups at the State level. As this will enable Hold the Child and other Child partners to have specific areas of interventions as agreed.

Needs

The 2017 fragile States index ranked South Sudan the most fragile state in the world. 5 years down the line the humanitarian crisis in South Sudan has deepened and spread, affecting people in areas previously considered stable and exhausting the coping capacity of those already impacted. Approximately 7.5 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection across the country as a result of armed conflict, inter-communal violence, economic crisis, disease outbreaks, and climatic shocks. Children percent over 61% of the people in need and are at an increased risk and particularly vulnerable to separation from their families, abuse, neglect, recruitment, and psychological distress. Nearly 4.3 million people – one in three people in South Sudan – have been displaced, including more than 1.74 million who are internally displaced and about 2.5 million in neighboring countries. Displaced people are more vulnerable to threats to their safety, health, and livelihoods. The continued armed conflict in South Sudan in 2018 has dire consequences for civilians (children, youths and elderly) resulting in deaths, separation, and conflict-related sexual violence, boys and girls are at high risk of recruitment into armed groups, social structures and family bonds are breaking; limited access to basic services, lack of alternative and post-primary education means that adolescents are more exposed to these risks as they have few other things to do. Boys are also vulnerable to abduction due to their role in cattle herding, while girls are abducted in anticipation of returns on dowry. These issues are compounded by growing food insecurity, limited basic services, widespread health issues, and exacerbated by heavy rains and flooding. Tens of thousands of people are estimated to have been killed since December 2013 the figure includes children. There continue to be reports of sexual violence, including rape and gang rape, committed by all parties to the conflict, where Wau, Raga and Uror counties contribute to this. Over 18,456 (8578 girls, 9878 boys)have been registered as unaccompanied, separated or missing as per recent reports 28th October 2018 out of this a total 7,361 (3388 girls, 3973 boys) active cases where Raga, Wau, Uror and Pochalla counties contribute to entire nationwide caseload. Through recent IRRMs to Raga, Baggari (Wau), Partet, Pieri, Pulchuol (Uror) have indicated that child marriage is increasing due to conflict and economic pressures. An estimated one million children are believed to be in psychological distress More than 1.17 million children aged 3 to 18 years old have lost access to education due to conflict and displacement since December 2013. About 31 percent of schools open has suffered at least one or more attacks by armed actors. Over 17,000 children are estimated to have been recruited by armed actors in South Sudan.

Activities

• Provide family tracing and reunification services, including identification, registration, and documentation, interim care, family tracing, follow up visits and reunification, reintegration follow-ups and case closures for UASC. Target 400 children (150 girls and 150 boys).• Provide comprehensive case management services and referrals to extremely vulnerable children. Target 500 children (250 girls and 250 boys). These will include training and coaching of staffs and volunteers on case management. • Provide community-based psychosocial support activities for girls, boys, adolescents, and caregivers in extremely vulnerable situations and those facing multiple protection risks. Target 10,000 children (5,000 girls and 5,000 boys); 3,000 adults (2,000 females and 1,000 males).• Provide release and reintegration programmes for 200 children (150 boys and 50 other vulnerable girls) previously formally and informally released from armed forces and armed groups, in addition of 50 children to be released in 2019.• Conduct awareness campaigns on the prevention of family separation, prevention of recruitment and re-recruitment of children. Target 7,000 adults (4,000 females and 3,000 males).• Support caregivers and parents to provide appropriate care and good parenting skills for affected children and mitigate risks of psychosocial distress and protection concerns for children in their households, communities, and schools. Target 7,000 adults (4,000 females and 3,000 males).• Conduct Mine Risk Education in schools, child-friendly centers and communities living in high mine risk areas. Target 2,500 children (1,250 girls and 1,250 boys); 2,500 adults (1,500 females and 1,000 males)• Building capacity of CP staffs and community volunteers, community-based child protection structures, including government officers through coaching and monitoring in CPiE.• Conduct monthly mobile and static protection assessments and monitoring for advocacy and inform the cluster on interventions.• Conduct peacebuilding and peaceful coexistence activities among youths and adults. Target 5,000 adults (2,500 females and 2,500 males)

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

There will be a regular provision of information to the beneficiaries through all stages of the program cycle for instance during project planning, consultations, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. With the full participation of the community will lead to project ownership and driven by the community. Strengthening of the existing of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanisms hence flow of information to beneficiaries.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will provide services through messages that are relevant to the context of South Sudan where all activities will focus on structured gender and age-appropriate. The messages must be approved and agreed by the cluster leads with the green light from the beneficiaries and other key stakeholders at the community level. The messages will be inform of IEC materials, through verbal communication (awareness raising campaigns) and other means of communication; however for easy understanding key messages will be translated to local languages of a specific target community and age groups.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Sexual exploitation and abuse are critical issues South Sudan is facing; through this protect specific measures and activities will be tailored. Through our child safeguarding policy and other related policies like PSEA will be maintained and always will be referred to for guidance. All staffs must sign a code of conduct before providing frontline services and engagement with beneficiaries. Through awareness of other services and referrals will all contribute to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. With the support from UNICEF and UNFPA will strengthen capacities of staffs in PSEA

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Regular consultations will be key, throughout the project. The consultations will range from the beneficiaries, community-based child protection committees, local authorities, other agencies, donors, specific technical working groups and clusters.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

A friendly feedback and complaint mechanisms such as suggestion boxes, one on one, a focus group discussions among others will be explored in all our target areas. Through regular field visits will enable to have open meetings with the communities of the affected populations, therefore the beneficiaries will be always be given a chance to express themselves in a safe way. Reports will be collected from the community-based child protection members who are close with the beneficiaries hence strengthening the linkages of feedback and complaints.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will identify and addresses barriers to access. Hold the Child will adapt gender strategies to ensure that most vulnerable girls and women equally participate in interventions. Through the implementation of the project Hold the Child will make sure that all targeted populations, for instance, the unaccompanied and separated children do not at any point face barriers in accessing the frontline services. By closely working with OCHA and other partners will enable to monitor the barriers to access and hence advocacy will be key.On the other hand, Hold the Child will have field focal points who will closely monitor and report on barriers that are related to access.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is designed to make sure that protection threats are minimized throughout the project implementation. The project will provide its frontline services to all targeted beneficiaries with equal opportunities in consideration of sex and age. Safety of beneficiaries will be key, and prevention from further threats by addressing key child protection issues such as abduction, sexual exploitation and abuse, neglect, gender-based violence, child labour, gender inequality and harmful traditional practices (child marriage), recruitment and use of children by armed forces and armed groups and family separation.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Monitoring and evaluation will be through the active involvement of beneficiaries with equal participation at all stages. To understand the progress and improvements of the project and as a measure access Hold the Child will engage beneficiaries and other key stakeholders (community members, community leaders, local authorities) through focus group discussions and individual key informants.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Primary consideration will be given to accountability to affected populations, to identify, understand and support their own protective measures.To assess perceptions of improved safety, Hold the Child will conduct focus group discussions, key informant interviews and direct observation methods with community members to better understand their safety and security and protection risks whether there has been any improvement in all targeted areas during project implementation. The project will focus on physical safety, for instance, the location of CFS will be guided by the existing guidelines, security, and protection of accessing services. To ensure the safety of beneficiaries’ especially with informed consent must be key and confidentiality will be mandatory.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Different segments of affected populations will be meaningfully engaged in all decisions and actions that have a direct impact on their well-being.To assess perceptions of dignity, Hold the Child, will not interview or use any information without the consent of any affected population including children and women themselves. Affected population without forgetting children, youth and women will be part of the planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of the project. Information will be collected on the perception of dignity and whether there have been any improvements in all targeted areas during the intervention.
