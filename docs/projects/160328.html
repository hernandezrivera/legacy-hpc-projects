Project Summary

The proposed project will cement on the work CH is already doing on the ground in some of the selected counties. This emergency Water, Hygiene and sanitation (WASH) project will assist the most vulnerable households from the Returnees, IDPs and host communities to have access to safe drinking water through provision of raft of measures. In order to address WASH related gaps, this project will provide life saving WASH interventions by distributing essential WASH items which include WASH Hygiene kits, buckets, collapsible, AQUATABS, PUR sachets and non-collapsible jerry cans and other items with support from WASH Core pipelines. A total of 5,000 households (30,000 individuals) comprising of women: 12,000 men: 3,000 girls: 10,500 and boys: 4,500 will directly benefit from the project across the 9 counties of Unity State which include; Koch, Leer, Panyinjiar, Mayendit, Guit, Rubkona, Pariang, Abiemhom and Mayom.
Further disaggregation of beneficiaries from each county shall be informed by actual needs during the intervention.
This response is necessary as evident by data from the recent IPC released in September 2019 where most of the households in the locations targeted are in IPC phase 4 and 5. 355,000 people are stressed phase and depend on the humanitarian services. It is estimated that more 475,000 people are in crisis, and 115, 000 people are in emergency and a staggering 55.4% of the estimated 6.21 million above the threshold of phase 3,4 and 5,a humanitarian disaster. Community members will be mobilized through the established community hygiene promoters (CHPs) and encouraged to avoid bad practices such as open defecation. Hygiene promotion activities including hand washing after visiting toilets, before and after eating food will be shared with households to minimize common water related diseases such as typhoid, cholera among others in the IDPs and returnees living in poor hygienic conditions.
The project will address and mitigate risks related to Gender Based Violence (GBV), which has badly affected women and girls. These groups of beneficiaries suffer multiple problems including sexual assaults while accessing this water points, sexual assaults and rapes due to distances travelled as well as intimate partners violence (IPV) where women are subjected to beating for failing to bring enough water home in time and suspicion of having affairs with other men if delayed by the long queues in few boreholes or far away stream or river. Women and girls will get support and access to WASH MHM kits to assist them during menstrual periods.
The WASH project will also continue with current CH efforts in implementing WASH GBV and WASH NUT whereby caregivers of malnourished children at stabilization centers and health centers are provided with messaging through focus group discussions, key informant interviews on practices which encourage healthy living for children admitted for SAM/MAM. Hygiene kits shall also be provided to caregivers to continue with the needed knowledge attitude and practice (KAP) after discharge. Furthermore, the project will rehabilitate or dig pit latrine located at the health centers to improve the hygiene conditions. The project also will distribute hand-washing soaps and train community volunteers. The project will be designed to incorporate knowledge gained during the trainings on the accountability to affected persons (AAP) and widely consult beneficiaries, before coming up with WASH activities. Overall the project is designed in line with the WASH cluster objectives and overall the humanitarian response plan objectives.

1 - Needs

South Sudan in general has been going through multiple crises and the former Unity State in particular. From the devastating civil conflict that started in December 2013 to multiple internal communal conflicts, revenge killings, cattle raiding, serious gender basis violence, high level of diseases outbreak and malnutrition due to broken health system to lack of food and no food markets. Water, hygiene and sanitation (WASH) system is completely broken down resulting in high level of diseases outbreaks and acute malnutrition especially among the children under the age of 5 years. The few water points which were functional prior to conflict have been destroyed and abandoned as areas become insecure, leaving a vast number of the communities relying on the untreated water drawn from the stream or rivers, some which are stagnant and are serious causes of diseases. The general absent of WASH service has also become a serious GBV and protection concerns where women and girls have become subject of sexual assault, harassment and rapes when accessing the water points. Frequent fights which sometimes escalate to community levels and cause killings and displacement start at water points.According to the recent IPC report released in September 2019, more than 55.4 % of the 6.21 million are in needs and fall in phase 4 and 5, which is a worrying situation. This projection is higher than for 2019. OCHA snapshot reports point to the same direction. The information has been corroborated by our internal CH assessment reports and partners in Panyijiar, Leer, Mayendit, Koch and Guit. Furthermore, the REACH reports describe the situation in South Sudan generally and Unity State in particular as a “complex humanitarian crisis, restricting humanitarian access and hindering the flow of information required by aid partners to deliver humanitarian assistance to populations in need”. These reports confirm dire humanitarian needs and urgent support to the most affected population with limited humanitarian assistance especially for those outside the POCs. Some of these locations are not easily accessible further jeopardizing the lives of innocent children and women.Most of the population in the former Unity State are either in Protection of civilian sites (POCS) or internally displaced in hard to reach areas for safety or outside in the neigbouring countries. While the ceasefire that was declared in August 2018 has largely held, there is no guarantee that peace shall prevail with multiple delays in formation of unity government. Either way, even if all things go smoothly in term of peace implementation, local population shall be faced by multiple challenges when returning to their former homesteads and wanted to rebuild their lives. Therefore humanitarian assistance shall remains the only option in 2020.With internal and external insecurity especially in cattle camps, limited road access and close down of most functional markets, these counties remain constrained in terms of access to humanitarian services. As a result, WASH NUT and WASH related GBV risks have been experienced due to inadequate safe water, hygiene and sanitation as well as limited access to nutrition services. This is because of the massive need and gaps identified in various reports such as the OCHA humanitarian snapshot reports, IPC, Reach regular displacement reports, NAWG and HRP2020 documents. In those reports, it is recorded that the locations continue to receive high number of returnees and IDPs either internally within Unity State or from neighbouring states such as Jonglei, Upper Nile and Khartoum where there was political stalemate. This project is therefore, designed to contribute and reduce these huge needs and gaps as identified by many partners during the assessment missions. It is estimated that these figures will be worse in the first quarter of 2020.

Activities and Outcomes.Activity 1.1.1: Distribute 18, 000 Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits and under wears to 6,000 women and 4800 girls in Koch, Leer, Mayendit, Panyinjiar, Guit, Rubkona, Pariang, Abiemhonom and Mayom County. Activity 1.1.2: Distribute 4,320 cartoons of washing soaps to 18,000HHs) for personal hygiene in Koch, Leer, Mayendit, Panyinjiar, Guit, Rubkona, Pariang, Abiemhonom and Mayom County Activity 1.1.3: Distribute of 3,000 latrines digging kits to beneficiaries for 3,000 HHs in Koch, Leer, Mayendit, Panyinjiar, Guit, Rubkona, Pariang, Abiemhonom and Mayom County Activity 1.1.4: Distribute (18,000) households water storage collapsible and non-collapsible jerry cans, buckets to beneficiaries in Koch, Leer, Mayendit, Panyinjiar, Guit, Rubkona, Pariang, Abiemhonom and Mayom County Activity 1.1.5: Distribute to 18000HHs 402 cartons Aqua Tabs and 402 cartons of PUR sachets to water treatment filter clothes in Koch, Leer, Mayendit, Panyinjiar, Guit, Rubkona, Pariang, Abiemhonom and Mayom County.Activity 1.1.6: 540 hygiene messaging sessions on hand washing with soaps, after visiting the latrines and before and after eating conducted 9 counties. Activity 1.1.7: Train 540 women and girls as Community Hygiene Promoters (CHPs) volunteers with integrated component of basic Nutrition to create awareness and sensitization to the community on the WASH NUT and GBV risks related to WASH in Koch, Leer, Mayendit, Panyinjiar, Guit, Rubkona, Pariang, Abiemhonom and Mayom County Activity 1.1.8: Using locally available materials construct or refurbish 18 women and girl friendly toilets with locks in urban/market centers in Koch, Leer, Mayendit, Panyinjiar, Guit, Rubkona, Pariang, Abiemhonom and Mayom County to mitigate GBV related risks and promote hygiene.Activity 1.1.9: Targeting caregivers of malnourished children and admitted for SAM &MAM and discharged including malnourished mothers from functional OPT/SC in Koch County provided with 18000 hygiene kits to 9360 children and women. Activity 2.1.0: Create awareness on hygiene and sanitation to 6000 Households through home visits by CHP volunteers in Koch, Leer, Mayendit, Panyinjiar, Guit, Rubkona, Pariang, Abiemhonom and Mayom County. Activity 2.1.2: Conduct 540 WASH in Nutrition FGDs, KIIs and semi structured interviews and meetings with the Health/Nutrition workers and the couples "caretaker/mother - SAM/malnourished children) at OTP/SC or community level. Activity 2.1.3: Conduct jointly with other partners (GBV, Health and protection) 360 sessions on WASH GBV risk analysis and mitigation using focus groups discussions, KIIs, semi-structured interviews before launching the hardware component.Activity 2.1.4: 6 joint WASH National cluster, sub cluster and partners, conduct monthly to identify gaps and risk related to WASH-GBV, WASH NUT.Activity 2.1.5: support 9360 malnourished children and women admitted for SAM/MAM and discharged from functional OPT/SC.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Coalition for Humanity as a humanitarian organization is cognizant of the rights of our beneficiaries and the environment we operate. We recognize and advocate for the human rights in general and especially for vulnerable women, girls and children. We are guided by DO NOT HARM principle across the board and ensure that our staff, consultants and volunteers are guided by our strict code of conduct which do not condone any abuse of authority and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse.As part of these efforts we ensure that our beneficiaries are brought on board in all intervention that we do by consulting them and taking their views into consideration. By this they all feel valued, right recognized and become part and parcel of everything that will happen within the communities. We advocate on behalf of our beneficiaries with different actors both government and other oppositions to help reach to the vulnerable households for assistance that they greatly need.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project targets internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities without access to basic service. Priority benefits are channeled to the most affected communities with limited access to other support. As per the principle of humanitarian response,no one is left behind when it comes to provision of lifesaving support to all categories of beneficiaries identified.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project has limited linkage to the government systems or programme. We however, usually collaborate and request relevant government authorities in each location to cooperate in terms of providing security for the humanitarian workers, beneficiaries and property/services we give to the vulnerable population. The project is humanitarian service led to ensure interventions respond to the needs of the vulnerable persons in a short term which will eventually propel the modles/systems to the long term process once a total peace is finally guaranteed in all locations in South Sudan.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned with Humanitarian response plan, which is national in nature. However, since this is an emergency response, government participation except in provision of security, coordination and facilitation is quiet limited. The project primary beneficiaries include IDPs, returnees and host communities with limited attention to refugees. The project is aligned with WASH cluster objectives.

WASH kits distribution sites will be decided upon by community and services quality measured through constructive focus group discussions with key people being central in this project.We will adhere to the principle “DO NO HARM”. Practices like MHM kits will be distributed after consulting with women and under reproductive girls to choose when and where to distribute MHM kits. Our staffs were trained AAP sessions by WASH cluster and AAP agents like, REACH, IOM and NRC. This accountability involves areas of PSEA and sexual related abuses with beneficiaries. Our team will be strongly guided by our professional code of conduct to prohibit sexual exploitation.Children and women are top priority in our hygiene kits distribution and other distributions. Durable Solutions:This project will run concurrently with shelter and non-food items, FSL, Health and nutrition, protection and GBV to reach durable solutions. 6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Beneficiaries are involved in the project designed including exit plans. While the project is emergency in nature, beneficiaries including leaders mostly women and girls, the community hygiene promoters (CHPs), pump mechanics, Water management committee (WMC) other community members including leaders are trained to take over the responsibility once the project is wind up. This means there is sustainability/handing over in mind at the inception of it. The project will build on our emergency work in most of the targeted locations where we have in place trained Project management committees (PMC)

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

CH undertakes thorough and careful planning before all its interventions. We work with community members we target in pre project design to identified all the risks and come up with measures to minimize or prevent them altogether. We have lay downprocesses to ensure that all range of risks including exposure of women and girls to sexual violence are identified and addressed before hand. This includes sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. This risk will be mitigated by making massive awareness to the humanitarian workers andcommunity members on the principles of humanitarian response focusing on (AAP). Every body engaged in this project will know accountability to affected persons (AAP) and PSEA principles. More knowledge and kills to women and girls to avoid sexual exploitationwill be given to mitigate any occurrences of GBV exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements. The women and girls living in insecure places like cattle camps will be protected through engagements with the local leaders and family heads to ensure women and girls are protected especially when searching for water and firewood. Other risks exposure such as violence because of distance to and from the locations of humanitarian assistanceand services will look into before any intervention is carried. Women and girls walk long distances to collect food from distribution centers and this is when they are exposed to sexual violence. In-this project, Humanitarian workers will take servicescloser to the community locations to limit women and girls from walking longdistance.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

This project will be more accountable to the affected population through many ways including consultations with stakeholders: people living with disability representatives and caregivers to ensure participation an inclusivity. Consultations with women,men,girls,boys, religious groups, health workers at the hospitals, PHCCs and PHCUs,SC and local authority will be carried out. These consultations with the communities and other stakeholders, will continue throughout project lifecycle to get their views on how the project progress with the view to provide suggestions or changes they feel are necessary. During the Need Assessment missions and WASH kits distribution, communities will decide distribution sites and services quality measured through constructive FGDs and KIIs with beneficiaries such as caregivers at nutrition sites, health centers and stabilization centers. CH strongly adheres to the principle “DO NO HARM”.This project will ensure that, distribution of MHM kits will be conducted after consultations with women and girls under reproductive age to choose when and where they will receive the MHM kits. Our staffs were trained AAP sessions by WASH cluster and others like, Protection cluster, GBV Sub cluster, REACH, IOM and NRC. We will also conduct training on AAP with all incentive staffs both at field and head office levels. Our team will be guided by CH codes of conduct that prohibits sexual relations with our beneficiaries. We will establish complain feedback desks to collect feedback from communities on the services provided. This informs our intervention on how best we can improve for the interest of the beneficiaries. Feedback mechanisms will be monitored every two weeks in a month to allow timely adjustment and respond to the beneficiaries’ complaint in effective manner.Our services to affected communities are done through consultations in all stages right from need assessment, verification and distribution.
