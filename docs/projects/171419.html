Project Summary

The rehabilitation of agriculture productive assets in Beit Lahia is a project
of 6 months targeting vulnerable small-scale farmers either open field or Green Houses
“GHs” comes as an emergency response to those farmers who have already affected by
the outbreak of COVID 19 and the lockdown of the north governorate, the second most
affected governorate by COVID 19 in Gaza Strip. The continuous lockdown for more two
months compounded by the protracted Gaza Strip blockade of about 14 years stripped
out their productive base and exhausted their coping strategies and increase their
vulnerability to a risky level. The project comes in line with HRP strategic objective 3 and
pertaining to the FSS objective 1. Moreover, SIF and its partner BeitLahia Development
Association “BLDA” who has a rich experience in emergency interventions in food
security “FS”, agriculture and livelihoods thematic areas will fill the most urgent gaps in HRP
with focus on agriculture seasonal priorities The project aims at restoring farmers based
livelihoods and improving FS in northern governorate mainly Beit
Lahia locality through supporting the vegetables production cycle to increase the self -
reliance and promoting the resilient of farmers HHs by restoring agricultural
productive assets in response to the increased vulnerability from current chronic
circumstances including consequences of climate changes, protracted blockade and
COVID 19. The project will target 92 vulnerable farmers which are at high risk of losing
their livelihoods means.
The project intends to have a positive impact on hundreds of poor farmers with small-
scale holdings of open field and GHs and laid out 7 km of a communal water carrier line which is serving 266 dunms. The project will focus on the
rehabilitation of 1) 46 dunms GHs pertaining to 46 farmers through replacing the
degraded polyethylene sheets, polyethylene wire mesh and other fixation accessories and
providing UPVC pipes for moving rainfalls water from the rooftops of GHs to water ponds.
2) laid off 7 km of carrier lines of 110 mm with all required fittings for 133 farmers serving 266 dums 3) providing inputs for open field farms including irrigation network for 46
farmers with holding of 1-2 dunms, the inputs will be for one dunm e.g. nylon sheets,
galvanized wires and organic fertilizers.
The project has been designed in accordance with the SIF& BLDA extensive experience in
agricultural livelihoods and in close consultation with beneficiaries and FSS cluster to

Support vulnerable small-scale farmers with the needed urgent inputs for restoration their livelihood activities.The project will target92 vulnerable farmers exposed to extreme shocks in the past due to chronic crises. This urges them to fall into rounds of debts as a result of high inputs cost and marketing prices went down. Such cumulative shocks have been diminishing their agricultural activities and farmers lost significant part of their livelihoods capitals. The intervention will decrease the leakages of irrigation networks either in carrier line or secondary network for 312 dunms for 40% to 45% and this will decrease the inputs cost of irrigation water meanwhile conserving water resources.
The project beneficiaries have been identified by SIF partner BLDA who was conducted recent assessment in BeitLahia through 2 FGDs in August 2020 targeting a sample of 40 farmers,4 PWDs and 8 women Headed HHs among 40 have been participated in the FGDs as SIF policy provide equal opportunity for both men&women
The assessment found a need for urgent rehabilitation and restoration for agricultural productive assets
ahead of the spring season to enable them to restore their livelihoods. The assessment
also revealed that 60 % among the participants are food insecure. The project will
maximize the productivity of the rehabilitated assets as SIF will employ the Building Back
Better“BBB” Approach in a full participatory way with beneficiaries

1 - Needs

The proposed intervention addresses the allocation priorities of the HRP 2021 strategic objectives 3 and SO 1 for FSS for building the capacity of vulnerable vegetable growers to cope with and overcome protracted crises. It will focus on most needy farmers in Beit Lahia area in northern governorate; the second affected governorate in Gaza Strip in terms of of the recent outbreak of COVID 19 according to the Gaza epidemiological map, as well as this intervention meets priority No. “1” according to FSS priorities focus matrix in Gaza for HRP 2021. The proposed intervention will focus on restoring productive agricultural assets for the both GHs and open field for small-scale vulnerable farmers and it was stem out from actual assessment conducted by SIF’s partner BLDA last August. According to HNO 2021, some of 1.57 M who need one or more humanitarian assistance live in Gaza and among 1.4 M who severely impacted by the current crises 77% in Gaza of which 11,898 small-scale farmers, herders and fishermen which have continued to face significant food insecurity and losing their means of livings due to degradation of their productive assets due to prolonged blockade for over than 14 years, consequences of climate changes which hardly hit the small scale farmers in the past years compounded by the recent outbreak of COVID 19 which has severely disrupted agricultural production and markets. While prices has always gone down over the past years as well as the productivity is declining to many reasons as one of them most of Gaza vegetables growers applied to some extent wrong agricultural practices. The situation has become even worse with the outbreak of COVID 19. Many literatures on agricultural sector in Gaza indicated that the total locally grown food supply was significantly reduced of pre-outbreak of COVID 19 level, mainly due to a reduction in the cultivated area as many farmers lost the productive base of their agricultural activities “position paper from PNGO August 2020”. The cumulative crisis have reduced field activities and severely disrupted livelihoods in the agriculture sector. The prolonged blockade resulted in a lack of inputs such as seeds, fertilizer, irrigation systems and storage facilities together with deterioration of electricity service which directly affected farming activities. The frequent and long cuts of electricity as well as the degradation of the agricultural production assets, led to the disruption of locally agricultural production. In August 2020 SIF’ partner BLDA” conducted an assessment for the vulnerable farmers there, shows that more than 92 small scale vegetables farmers are among the most needy for rehabilitation their productive assets either green houses, open fields and replacing degraded water carrier lines which leaks of more than 40-45% of the pumped irrigated water and cost farmers additionally cost in particular in the prevailed chronic electricity cuts. Moreover, the assessment shows that 92 farmers out of 2000 vegetable growers are the most needy small-scale farmers and will be at high risk to lose the remain means of livings and to join the rapidly increase of unemployment in Gaza. The project in this assessment is fully considers the participatory approach through involve the most vulnerable farmers groups women headed HHs and PWDs in the need assessment as more than 50 invitations to farmers women HHs were distributed and only 8 women were participated in the FGDs . Despite the fact of outbreak of COVID 19 BLDA and SIF succeeded to gather the target groups in open space for conducting the needs assessment through two FGDs taking into considerations all safety measures including social distancing . It is worth noting that more than 500 vulnerable farmers either for open field and GHs approaching BLDA seeking for assistance in term of rehabilitation their productive assets. Thus, SIF & BLDA and through its assessment identify a pool of potential beneficiaries and would announce on the project to receive applications and later on to be evaluated against a pre-set of criteria addressing farmers vulnerability and other factors to identify the project beneficiaries. The world bank forecast that by the end of 2020 the poverty rate in Gaza will increase from 53 to 64 percent which will impact the family dynamics and increase the GBV which is considered the key protection concern in Gaza as it will impact the behavioral of men being more frustrated unable to fulfill the expected role at HH level. Thus, this intervention will meet the most vulnerable and food insecure population in northern governorate and mainly in Beit Lahia Area targeting refugees and non-refugees through aiming to assist smallholders in advancing from food insecurity and vulnerability to shocks to increased income and food security through reducing the risk.

2 - Activities

Below is the description of the intended project activities, It is worth noting that the planned activities were coordinated with the MoA and FSS coordinator prior the design of the project activities. Moreover these activities were prioritized as priority one in FSS priorities focus matrix for HRP 2021. The project will be implemented over six months as SIF &BLDA from their previous experience deem that such period is enough for selecting beneficiaries, contracting out suppliers and contractor and completing the entire project activities. SIF and its partner BLDA will utilize all possible announcements tools when announcing on the project activities through placing announcements on mosques, public buildings, using social media channels and SMS. On other hand, SIF &BLDA will consider the precautions and safety measures of COVID 19 for the registration of the applicants in order to accommodate open space at BLDA premises equipped with chairs keeping distancing and hygiene kits and to maintain certain numbers in a waiting area not exceeding 10 in each round of applicants and provide access root for PWDs. These applications will be evaluated later on against a pre-set of criteria addressing applicants “farmers vulnerability” and other factors to identify the project beneficiaries. 1.1 Rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs for farmers: This standard activity is targeting 92 vulnerable small-scale farmers in Beit Lahia area to restore their agricultural productive assets, the sub-activities as following: 1.1.1 Conduct beneficiaries selection through different advertisement tools, BLDA website, Facebook, SMS to farmers in the target areas namely Seyifa, Attatrah, Salateen and Shaima’a localities and placing advertisements in front of mosques &municipality. social distancing will be maintained and applicants will be distributed over four days based on locality and other safety measures e.g. face mask, gloves and sanitizing materials will be available at the registration places. It is also worth to mention that PWDs will be in a separate day with accommodating place for registrations. Moreover, verification visits to applicants will be conducted by the project team taking the COVID 19 safety measures into consideration. The project team will be trained by SIF security and safety officer on these arrangements prior the starting of project. 1.1.2 Laying out water carrier line of length 7 KM to convey irrigation water to 266 dunms of open field and GHs. The work will include all fittings and parts needed for connecting agricultural plots secondary network with the main water carrier. This activity will be implemented through contracting modality taking into consideration do no harm and protection aspects for labors and beneficiaries as well in the site. 1.1.3 Rehabilitation of 46 units of greenhouses through providing polyethylene sheets to replace the deteriorated ones, polyethylene wire mesh, and wooden slides for fixation, nails and nylon wire & UPVC pipes for drainage rainfall water to water pond. This activity will be implemented through selecting vendors based on SIF procurement requirements to supply all specified materials with high quality as SIF cumulated rich and unique experience in the field of provision inputs for small-scale farmers through its own livelihood programs. 1.1.4 Rehabilitation of 46 dunms of open field through providing secondary irrigation networks to replace the deteriorated ones, nylon sheets and wire galvanized steel, in addition to organic fertilizers for each dunm to encourage the best agricultural practices. This activity will be implemented through selecting vendors based on SIF procurement requirements to supply all specified materials with high quality as SIF cumulated rich and unique experience in the field of provision inputs for small-scale farmers through its own livelihood programs. Gender and age distribution analysis will be conducted for each activity. Moreover, the application template will capture all the disaggregated data for the applicants when applying for the project including gender and age distribution as well as in the scoring system that will be developed by MEAL department at SIF for selecting the beneficiaries a considerable criteria weight will be given for women headed HHs and PWDs and SIF has already given an equal opportunity for both men and women in applying to the project and it will be clear statement in the announcement of the project. Since the registration and verification visits for project beneficiaries will be carried out by BLDA in close coordination with SIF, SIF & BLDA will follow contractor modality in the laying out the water carrier line of 7 km, as the contractor will furnish all skilled labors and materials to complete the work. The contractor will take the required safety measures against COVID 19. The rest of work SIF and BLDA will disturbed the inputs” secondary irrigation network, fertilizers and other inputs for renovation of GHs” and farmers will carry out the installment. SIF will carry out all the procurement inputs of the project according to SIF procurement modality in partnership with BLDA, as BLDA will receive all inputs items, distribute it through its team among the beneficiaries and monitor the contractor work that will be contracted for laying out the irrigation water carrier line of length 7 km. In terms of PSEA, SIF will carry out series of activities related to PSEA through the following: SIF will conduct brush up training to Project team, Partner’s team covering an introduction to PSEA including SIF’s Abusive Behavior Procedures (ABP), Child Safeguarding Policy, and Code of Conduct. Moreover, SIF Gender and age distribution analysis will be conducted for each activity. Moreover, the application template will capture all the disaggregated data for the applicants when applying for the project including gender and age distribution as well as in the scoring system.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project will support acutely vulnerable small scale farmers including women headed HHs and PWDs. The project is designed based on a need assessment that has been carried out with participation of the beneficiaries, who will continue to be included in the implementation phase through a representative group to ensure that the project rehabilitation works meet their needs and expectations and that they are able to monitor how the rehabilitation works can address their protection needs. SIF will refer to specialized agencies for certain vulnerable group with specific needs not covered by the project. Also SIF will oversee the entire rehabilitation works to ensure there is no discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, exploitation or any other harm done to beneficiaries, specifically any potential harm related to COVID 19, and SIF and BLDA will emphasize on the contractors and vendors who will carry out the work to be accountable for the entire safety measures. SIF will ensure compliance by all parties to its protection Code of Conduct and protective measures related to COVID 19. In addition Safety and Security Officer at SIF will introduce the recommended safety measures to the contractor, vendors and beneficiaries of the project and will conduct regular visits to the site to ensure the compliance to these measures. The dignity and safety measures will be maintained and ensured through SIF & BLDA team when the applicants are applying to benefit from the project as the BLDA will secure a place wide enough to absorb the applicants meanwhile providing chairs for waiting during registration submitting the applications. Moreover, SIF mainstreams the Abusive Behavioral Procedure among all project stakeholders including the beneficiaries as anyone can submit a real, clear, and accurate description of the facts of abuse or misconduct incident by writing directly to stop@secours-islamigue.org or to the complaint box at SIF Gaza office. The Abuse Behavioral Procedure documents will be an essential part of the contract document with the beneficiaries. At the time of signing the agreement with the beneficiary. SIF will conduct an orientation session for all concerned parties in the project including vendors and contractor and workforce on the code of conduct as well as SIF will emphasis on respecting the culture sensitivity of working with households headed by women and PWDs to maintain the dignity and the rights of those beneficiaries. SIF’s protection unit’s team (Protection focal point and Counselor/Case management) will carefully monitor the protection concern by building the capacity of the livelihood project’s team, targeted beneficiaries, and related vendors, contactors and labors. by enhancing their understanding of protection aspects, and provide an avenue to address any protection concerns such as GBV, child abuse and or child labor through monitoring visits and mentorship. In case any GBV case arises during the project implementation, SIF Counselor/Case management will refer any case(s) through relevant referral services. The project works will take into account any special needs according to Gender -Age and PWDs. It is worth noting that any additional needs beyond the project capacity for PWDs will be addressed and will make referral to more specialized agencies. A specific emphasis on applying protective measures and behaviors related to COVID 19 to ensure the protection and safety of all people involved in the project (project team, beneficiaries and laborer). SIF plans to apply the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) from the beginning of the project design. At the end of the project SIF will complete CHS self-assessment tool and the final score of the assessment will be used to further improve the accountability and protection mainstreaming. SIF will disseminate all lessons learned and good practices in protection mainstreaming within the project among programs in SIF and with FSS cluster.
