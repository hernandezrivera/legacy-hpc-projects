Project Summary

The proposed project aims to combat acute malnutrition and improve nutrition for children and mothers in emergency affected communities of Adamawa and Yobe States. The program includes mass screening for children aged 0-59 months to identify those with severe or moderate malnutrition and refer them for treatment. It strengthens Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTP) for stable children with severe malnutrition and expands Nutrition Stabilization Centers or Inpatient Therapeutic Feeding Centers for more critically malnourished children. Additionally, it promotes optimal Maternal, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) through counseling and community support groups. The program also enhances micronutrient supplementation for pregnant women and provides supplementary nutrition and food demonstrations to improve food security and utilization at the community level. The overall goal is to reduce malnutrition, improve health outcomes, and empower communities to prevent future nutrition crises.

1 - Needs

Nearly 2.55 million children aged 0-59 months in Northeast Nigeria are currently suffering from acute malnutrition, with projections indicating this number will persist through 2025. This includes 1.0 million children with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 1.55 million with Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). Additionally, nearly 309,000 pregnant and lactating women in the region are also facing acute malnutrition, further exacerbating the public health crisis. The situation is particularly dire during the lean season (May – September), where 31 of the 62 Local Government Areas (LGAs) are classified as facing serious or critical levels of malnutrition, and the first projection period (October – December 2024) suggests that 31 LGAs will remain in these high-risk phases. Even during the second projection (January to April 2025), 34 LGAs are expected to remain in these phases. The high prevalence of SAM and MAM among children, alongside the nutritional challenges faced by pregnant and lactating women, reveals deep gaps in healthcare and nutrition services in the region. Contributing factors include ongoing insecurity, limited access to healthcare, and the region's seasonal food insecurity. The weak health infrastructure, compounded by the seasonal spikes in malnutrition and the long-term impacts of insecurity, underscores the urgent need for comprehensive and sustained intervention strategies. To address this, preventive and curative services must be scaled up, and policies to strengthen the health system, promote cost-effective nutrition interventions, and attract government and private sector investment in nutrition must be prioritized. Furthermore, external risks such as flooding, seasonal epidemics, and childhood illnesses, coupled with the insecurity, remain critical factors that require continuous monitoring and adaptive intervention strategies to curb the malnutrition crisis in Northeast Nigeria.

2 - Activities

The proposed project will implement the following key activities;• Conduct mass MUAC screening for all children 0-59 months for identification of children with acute malnutrition (SAM/MAM) and referral to treatment services • Strengthen outpatient therapeutic program (OTP) for the treatment of severely malnourished children. • Strengthen, or scale up nutrition stabilization centers / Inpatient therapeutic feeding centers (where feasible). • Promote optimal Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practices in emergencies though facility counselling and community-based Support Groups.• Improved coverage of micronutrients tablets (IFA/MMS) to pregnant mothers• Support supplementary nutrition assistance (such SQ-LNS) and community-based food demonstrations

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming in this project, several critical modalities will be implemented. First, the project will prioritize Gender-Based Violence (GBV) prevention by providing training for staff to recognize and mitigate GBV risks. Safe spaces will be created for women and children, and referral systems will be established for GBV survivors to access medical, psychological, and legal support. Additionally, community awareness campaigns will educate local populations about GBV and available resources to prevent and address it.The project will also engage with vulnerable groups, including pregnant women, and the elderly, ensuring that they are prioritized and their specific needs addressed. Efforts will be made to include these groups in decision-making processes to ensure their participation and representation in the program. This will help foster an inclusive environment that respects the needs and rights of all members of the community.To safeguard children specifically, the project will ensure that children accessing services are protected from harm. This will include creating child-friendly environments in health centers and ensuring that all staff are trained in child protection procedures. The project will also screen for signs of abuse or neglect and refer cases to appropriate authorities or services.Finally, the project will ensure confidentiality and data protection, particularly regarding sensitive information about GBV or child protection cases. Personal data will be securely handled, ensuring it is used only for program purposes and maintaining the privacy of beneficiaries.By embedding these protection measures throughout the project, it will create a secure and supportive environment for vulnerable populations, allowing them to access nutrition services while ensuring their safety and dignity are upheld.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members, the project will employ inclusive and accessible methods that cater specifically to those at risk of being marginalized. Vulnerable groups, such as pregnant women, children, the elderly, and survivors of Gender-Based Violence (GBV), will be actively involved in the feedback process through several key strategies.First, the project will utilize community engagement methods that prioritize inclusivity, ensuring that the voices of vulnerable populations are heard. This will include conducting regular consultations with women, children, and other at-risk groups in safe and accessible spaces. These sessions will be designed to gather their input on the nutrition services and ensure that their unique needs are being met. Community meetings, focus group discussions, and individual interviews will be conducted in ways that are tailored to the specific circumstances and needs of these groups, ensuring that everyone can participate, including those who may have limited access to formal channels.Furthermore, safe spaces will be established for the collection of feedback, particularly for women and children, where they can express concerns in a secure environment. For example, these spaces will ensure that survivors of GBV feel safe in sharing their experiences without fear of retribution or stigma. Additionally, special efforts will be made to include child-friendly feedback mechanisms to ensure that children’s perspectives on nutrition services are heard. These can include child participatory activities, like drawing or group discussions, that allow them to share their experiences in an appropriate manner.To enhance the accessibility of feedback, the project will use multiple channels to collect input from vulnerable members. In addition to community meetings, channels such as toll free lines, community radio, and even door-to-door visits will be used during the project implementation.
