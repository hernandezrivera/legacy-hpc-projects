Project Summary

The idea of the project is based on providing protection & PSS services for children aged 6-17 years old affected by conflict related violence & violence in households (from both sexes including PWD), the activities will be provided through Al-Awda Community Centers in Jabalia & Beit Hanoun areas Northern Gaza Strip, and for the successful implementation of the project, the local community will be communicated with information about the project’s activities & locations through: (i) Radio spot (ii) Facebook ads (iii) Meetings with the local committees.
In order to ensure the maximum protection for the children while receiving services from the project staff (M&F), the following will be done: (i) Inclusion a clause about PSEA in the work contract (ii) Orientation of the project staff on the Child Protection Policy, PSEA Policy & Code of Conduct adding to signing on these policies by the staff (iii) Building the capacity of the project staff through 3 training courses as follows:
• 1st training in the manuals for working with children (Islamic Relief manual for ages 6-9, War Child’s manuals I DEAL & BIG DEAL for ages 10-13 & 14-17 respectively)
• 2nd training in remote service delivery, CP minimum standards, PSEA, complain channels & Al-Awda’s Feedback, Complain & Response Mechanism (FCRM).
• 3rd training in self-care mechanisms & stress management including 3 recreational days.
To improve the performance of the project staff during working with children, they will receive coaching sessions on the manuals by specialist. And in the context of improving the performance of the case managers & psychologists in the project, they will be supervised by technical specialized supervisors. Moreover, they will also be coached on the caregiver's guide.
The project staff will provide the following services for the targeted children & their caregivers: (i) Implementing structured PSS sessions according to age groups, where the children will be divided into 3 categories that are commensurate with the manuals on which the staff has been trained. (ii) Conducting collective & individual PSS services (iii) Providing case management & referral services, while special needs will be provided to some children, such as medicines, clothes & shoes. (iv) Conducting PSS caregivers sessions in positive parenting, stress management & cyber violence protection.
The project will include community based activities as follows: (i) Conducting training courses for non-protection specialists (whether from the Al-Awda’s workers or CBOs) in the detection & referral, protection mainstream & disability inclusion. (ii) Publishing radio spot & Facebook ads with the aim of expanding awareness of the community in the field of child protection (iii) Distributing leaflets & publishing 2 motion graphic videos (interpreted to sign language to reach to PWD) with awareness messages related to PSEA (iv) Conducting awareness sessions with different groups of the community members, including women, men, boys & girls (PWD), with the aim of raising their awareness about child protection & encouraging them to detect cases & refer them to specialized centers.
In order to achieve the principle of AAP, the beneficiaries will participate in all the project cycle phases & will be informed about Al-Awda’s FCRM through publishing motion graphic video interpreted to sign language to reach to PWD, moreover they have been consulted on these mechanisms. Noting that simulation on submitting feedback & complaints will be carried out with the children in order to encourage them to submit feedback & complaints that will help us to improve the services.
Within the framework of coordination & complementary work & to avoid duplication in the provision of services, coordination was made with the Child Protection Officer & the Ministry of Social Development. Knowing that, during the implementation process coordination will be done with the CP service providers in Northern GS.

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip suffers from a significant deterioration in the humanitarian situation as a result of a series of accumulated crises that affected all aspects of life, including the frequent Israeli attacks, the most recent of which was the May 2021 aggression, in addition to the internal Palestinian division and the unjust siege that extended for 15 years, and the COVID19 pandemic came to make matters worse and increase from the complexities of life in the Gaza Strip. During the last Israeli aggression against the Gaza Strip, the OHCHR report 2021 confirmed that 67 children were martyred, and 685 children were injured, including injuries resulting in permanent or long-term disabilities. The Euro-Mediterranean Human Rights Monitor confirmed in July 2021 that the Israeli attacks on densely populated residential neighborhoods were 75% of children and women, and that approximately 5,400 children lost their homes, either completely destroyed or severely damaged, while the homes of 42,000 children were partially damaged, and the report indicated that 241 children lost one or both of their parents, and 72,000 children suffered from the scourge of displacement to UNRWA schools or relatives' homes. The same report confirmed that about 91.4% of children in the Gaza Strip today suffer from some form of conflict-related trauma. The HNO 2022 indicated that the children in need of child protection, MHPSS support are 678,000, of whom 345,800 are boys and 332,200 are girls, including 122,007 in need of focused non-specialized support or specialized services, of whom more than half (53%) of Children in the Gaza Strip need these services. This is in addition to the fact that 82% of adolescents in the Gaza Strip suffer from a bad psychological situation. The same report (HNO 2022) indicated that child protection caregivers were subjected to enormous psychological pressures that sometimes stopped them from working, especially during the days of the Israeli attacks on the Gaza Strip, where there are 137,000 caregivers in the Gaza Strip, who also need various mental health and psychosocial support services. Including adaptive training to increase resilience to enable them to continue providing their services during crises. The same report also stated that children face high levels of violence in the home, with 90% of them facing some form of violent discipline, representing 92.3% of males and 97.9% of girls, and this includes 20.1% of children who have been subjected to severe physical violence by their caregivers, representing 23.3% are males and 16.9% are females, and the percentage in the Gaza Strip is (28.5%). Therefore, there is an urgent need for society to receive awareness of the mechanisms of positive parenting for children, stress management and to popularize the concept of child protection. With regard to persons with disabilities, the report indicated that 63% of persons with disabilities in the Gaza Strip lack access to mental health and psychosocial support services, which means that they must be targeted according to the disability inclusion approach in the services provided. As a result of the spread of the Al-Awda’s permanent community centers in all governorates of the Gaza Strip and its implementation of a series of child protection projects in addition to the activities implemented by the teams including holding meetings with the local community to determine their most important problems, needs and priorities, where the results of these meetings showed that there is an urgent need to provide protection services for children, including psychosocial support services, especially since the children of the Gaza Strip have been exposed and threatened to be exposed to dangers related to the Israeli attacks, In addition to the dangers of negative education by parents and teachers, besides to the effects of COVID19 pandemic it has had on the lives of people in the Gaza Strip, which have reduced the psychological status of all members of the community, especially children, including PWD.

2 - Activities

The Al-Awda has long experience in the field of child protection & PSS activities, as it is currently implementing 2 projects in the centers of Rafah & Nuseirat. It has previously implemented dozens of projects related to PSS of children. It has recently worked on developing its performance in the field of child protection through its participation in the child protection sub-group & the protection network. In addition to benefiting from the work guides manuals that were prepared by active institutions in the sub-group, & to improve the performance of the work, a number of Al-Awda’s workers were involved in several training courses related to child protection in addition to appointing a technical consultant to supervise the teams in the field of child protection & case management. Regarding this project, its activities are designed in response to the protection needs of children aged 6-17 years (from both sexes including PWD) affected by conflict related violence and violence in households in the northern area of the Gaza Strip (Jabalia and Beit Hanoun) through the Al-Awda’s permanent community centers located in those areas, as follows: 1- Publishing Radio Spot & Facebook Ads about the project's services & Al-Awda (FCRM).including the complaint channels for PSEA for 120,000 (10000 boys, 10000 girls, 50000 men, 50000 women) 2- Implementing 8 meetings with the community committees (20 persons, 10 women, 10 men including 2 PWD) . 3- Implementing a training course for 14 persons ( 4 men, 10 women)) including (10 project team + 4 workers in from Al-Awda’s protection program) for 48 hours (8 days, 6hours/day). About PSS manuals.4- Implementing a training course for 14 persons ( 4 men, 10 women) (10 project work team + 4 workers in Al-Awda’s protection program) for 42 hours (7 days, 6hours/day), provided that the topics include remote service delivery, CP minimum standards, PSEA & Al-Awda Feedback, Complain & Response Mechanism (FCRM) including complaint channels for PSEA. 5- Conducting a self-care & stress management training course to the project staff (10 persons) (2 men, 8 women)) for 18 hours (3 days – 6 hours/day). 6- Organizing 3 self-care days to the project staff (2 men, 8 women) at a rate of 1 day after the end of each work stage. 7- Implementing 47 coaching sessions to the project staff (2 men, 8 women) at a rate of 2 hours each session on the guides for working with children aged 6-17 years, in addition to the guide to working with child caregivers. 8- Implementing 90 technical supervision meetings with the case managers & 90 meetings with the psychologists (8 women) in Jabalia & Beit Hanoun during the project duration at a rate of 10 hours each month for each of them. 9- Providing structured PSS sessions to 432 children (216 boys, 216 girls including 9 PWD) (216 children/center) aged 6-17 years of both sexes (including PWD) (12 children/ group). The 1st age group (6-9 years old), the 2nd group (10-13), & the 3rd group (14-17 years old). It should be noted here that the Islamic Relief manual will be followed with the 1st group, while the I DEAL manual will be followed with the 2nd group & the 3rd group will be based on the BIG DEAL manual in implementing the activities. Each group will receive 20 sessions, including 19 sessions upon the manuals & one session on children's protection of themselves. 10- Conducting 72 collective PSS sessions to the children (60 boys, 60 girls including 2 PWD) who have been detected from the above-mentioned sessions,(12 groups - 10 children/group – 6 sessions/group) with the aim of dealing with some problems such as lack of motivation, social isolation & poor social skills. 11- Providing individual psychosocial sessions for 160 children (80 boys, 80 girls including 3 PWD) (who detected from the collective sessions or who come directly out the children’s groups to receive this services) who have psychological, behavioral & emotional problems (with average 8 sessions/case). 12- Providing case management services for 150 children of both sexes (75 boys, 75 girls including 4 PWD) who are exposed to protection threats, including referral to receive MH, legal services and speech therapy services, in addition to responding to the basic needs of some children such as clothes, shoes and medicines 13- Conducting PSS caregivers sessions on positive parenting, stress management & cyber-violence for 120 persons of both sexes (60 men, 60 women, including 2 PWD) who will be divided into 10 groups (12 participants/group) (7 sessions/group) as follows (4 stress management, 2 positive parenting & 1 cyber-violence protection). 14- Implementing 2 training courses for 24 persons (M & F)(8 men, 16 women including 1 PWD) (12 persons in each course) targeting non-protection specialists in the Al-Awda & in 6 CBOs for 24 hours per course, (4 days, 6 hours/day) the training course will include topics on detecting & referring children exposed to threats & violence, protection mainstreaming & disability inclusion. 15- Publishing Radio Spot & sponsored Facebook Ads with the aim of educating the community (10000 boys, 10000 girls, 50000 women, 50000 men) about child protection issues. 16- Designing, Printing & distributing 2000 leaflets targeting the local community (1000 men, 1000 women including 40 PWD) about PSEA including information on rights & safe complaint channels. 17- Publishing 3 motion graphic videos about PSEA & Al-Awda FCRM interpreted to sign language to reach (50000 women, 50000 men, 50000 boys, 50000 girls). 18- Conducting 100 community awareness sessions targeting 1200 people of both sexes, including women, men, boys & girls (300 men, 300 women, 300 boys, 300 girls including 24 PWD), 25 sessions per group, (12 persons/ session), with the aim of raising community awareness about child protection issues and spreading the concept of child protection.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

The Al-Awda gives great importance to obtaining feedback & complaints from the beneficiaries, as they are considered a key partner, where they are involved in the planning, implementing & evaluating Al-Awda activities. Several mechanisms have been allocated to receive feedback & complaints while ensuring that they can be submitted by all age & gender groups, including PWD (whether hearing, visual or physical disabilities) as the means of submitting the complaint varied in terms of written complaints directly to the complaint box (note that there is a special protocol for opening the box) or voice complaints through the use of the Toll-free No. which is a free No. specified for complaints, this in addition to electronic complaints via a generic e-mail, besides receiving complaints face to face or by filling out questionnaires. The Al-Awda is currently developing a Feedback, Complain and Response Mechanism (FCRM) including SEA complaints channel through the development of an electronic model that includes the following classifications: • Sensitive complaint. • Complaint & dissatisfaction. • Inquire about existing services. • Request for individual support & assistance. • Thanks & appreciation. Where the call center employee, through the electronic form, fills in all the details of the complaint and forwarded it to the MEAL manager, except for sensitive complaints that are forwarded to the Executive Director directly. As for the complaints on the Executive Director himself, they are forwarded to the Chairman. In order to response to gender needs, there will be call center workers of both sexes. It is worth to mention that, the center beneficiaries have been consulted about Al-Awda’s FCRM and will be informed about it through publishing motion graphic video interpreted to sign language to reach to PWD. Noting that simulation on submitting feedback & complaints will be carried out with the children in order to encourage them to submit feedback & complaints.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The idea of the project, its services and implementation mechanisms stem from respecting the protection principles stipulated in the Humanitarian Charter, including the right to live in dignity, the right to assistance and the right to protection. Moreover, these mechanisms are part of the values of the Al-Awda within its strategic plan 2020-2024. Through this project and to avoid exposing the beneficiaries to harm the following pillars will be taken into consideration: 1- Safety:- The Al-Awda will provide the project services through its 2 community centers in Jabalia and Biet Hanoun Northern Gaza Strip. The geographical locations of these facilities are distinguished by their presence in populated places, and the windows of these centers are protected that ensuring safety for the children.- In order to prevent SEA while providing project services, project staff will attend PSEA training. In addition, their contracts will include a special clause on PSEA in accordance with the IASC Principles and Guidelines and they will sign on the Al-Awda child protection policy, code of conduct and PSEA policy.- Leaflets and motion graphic videos about PSEA will be distributed and published including information on rights and safe complaint channels available to beneficiaries and including sign language interpretation in order to be reached by PWD. - In order not to expose the beneficiaries to further harm, they will be educated about PSEA and the Al-Awda FCRM and children will be introduced to this mechanism through simulation sessions.- Providing insurance of work accidents for the project staff. - Providing health insurance for the project staff in the Al-Awda health facilities. 2- Confidentiality and privacy.- Cases consultations and documentation are private and confidential. - Children’s caregivers will be provided with all the necessary information about the intervention plan. 3- Adaptability and acceptability.- Gender needs will be considered by having female staff among the work teams to meet the gender needs of women and girls. 4- COVID-19.- To achieve protection from COVID19 infection while providing services, prevention and safety procedures are followed according to the directions of the MoH and the WHO, that includes providing work teams and beneficiaries with PPE.- As part of building the capacities of the Al-Awda’s staff to confront the COVID19 pandemic, a group of them was trained on ways to prevent the transmission of COVID19 infection.- Avoiding stigmatization in the case of dealing with cases infected with COVID19.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Al-Awda adopts the principle of equality and impartiality in providing its services to beneficiaries, especially marginalized groups who need humanitarian interventions to improve their protection and humanitarian situation. Al-Awda provides its services without any discrimination based on age, gender, religion, race, or political affiliation. Knowing that through this project and to achieve the principle of impartiality in providing services, Al-Awda will provide services to the targeted children on the basis that each case has his/her own individual characteristics and special needs, especially PWD as the most vulnerable in society and who need special care and protection while maintaining dignity, confidentiality and nondiscrimination. The project services will be easily accessible to the target groups, as they will be provided through 2 community centers affiliated to Al-Awda in Jabalia and Bie Hanoun governorates that are well known to the beneficiaries. To ensure that the targeted children have access to the services, they will be provided with transportation fees. It is worth noting that the project staff and other staff from Al-Awda and CBOs, will participate in training about protection mainstreaming and disability inclusion to ensure that the services are provided without compromising the beneficiaries dignity.
