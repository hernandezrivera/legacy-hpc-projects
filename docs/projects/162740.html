Project Summary

The overall intervention aims to support refugees affected by disasters in target areas
to meet their basic food needs during and after crises. Food-insecure people affected by
long-term conflict and/or displacement are able to meet their basic food needs and
increase their self-reliance throughout the year.

1 - Needs

According to Sudan’s 2020 HNO, it is estimated about 9.3M will need humanitarian assistance in 2020. This has been exacerbated by the current macroeconomic situation which has negatively impacting the food and nutrition security of a large share of the population including IDPs, refugees, and resident population. Despite a good harvest mid-year in 2019, the economic crisis has kept food costs high. Inflation, combined with uneven and unpredictable rain, drives food insecurity. In some Darfur areas, prices in July this year were 15 to 25 per cent higher than same time one year ago. Households are unable to purchase a basic daily basket of food. In July, the national average price of a local food basket reached SDG31.5, which is 13.7 per cent higher than the previous month and 40 per cent higher than in December 2018. East Darfur, followed by South Darfur and White Nile recorded the highest prices, with SDG37.5, 36.2, and 33.4 respectively. This is manifested by households already experiencing food insecurity prior to the onset of the crisis sinking to even deeper levels of food insecurity and previously marginally food secure people becoming food insecure.Under normal circumstances, IDP and refugees are already the most vulnerable, given their limited livelihood opportunities and these are most affected by the economic crisis. There are 1,87M IDPs and 1.1M refugee and asylum who seekers continue to need humanitarian assistance and protection in both in camps and communities. As of June-August 2019, the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC), estimated that 5.8 million people (14 per cent of the total population) are experiencing crisis or worse levels of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and above). Around 1 million people are facing emergency levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4) and around 4.8 million people are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). The three states with highest number of people IPC 3 and above are Khartoum (793,000), South Darfur (763,000 people), and South Kordofan (607,000 people), followed by White Nile, Kassala, Red Sea, and North Darfur, with more than 400,000 people in each state in at least IPC 3 levels. Extreme and severe levels of intersectoral needs (severity 3 and 4) add to the already severe situation in Blue Nile, Central Darfur, Kassala, Nile state, North Darfur, South Kordofan, and West Kordofan, with a lesser extent in Al Gezira, Sennar, and White Nile. Women-headed households are more food insecure. 41 per cent of households headed by women were food insecure (moderate and severe). Following the similar trend, the purchasing power of the households headed by women was lower compared to households headed by men. With a commitment to zero hunger by 2030, there is a need to strengthen and continue the humanitarian response within the food security cluster on access and coverage of internal displaced people and residents through a diverse set ofactivities for them to effectively meet their food security needs. With a commitment to zero hunger by 2030, there is a need to strengthen and continue the refugee response within the food security sector on access and coverage of refugees in and out of camp settlements through a diverse set ofactivities for them to effectively meet their food security needs.

2 - Activities / Outputs

Activities: WFP’s response will ensure all refugees deemed in need of food assistance will be supported to mitigate negative coping mechanisms. A diverse approach will be adopted to ensure a food-secure environment through: in-kind assistance (a full ration of four commodities) to enable refugees to meet their daily calorie intake requirement and nutritional support for children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women in the most affected states; cash-based assistance in the form of value voucher will be implemented and, through commitment to accountability to affected populations and protection mainstreaming, a hybrid approach, of both in-kind and cash-based assistance will be distributed to specif camps. The introduction of different modalities of cash-based transfers (cash or voucher) is based on a multi-sectoral capacity assessment (MSCA) which looks into the capacity of: cooperating partners, supply chain at the local markets, financial institutions, information and communication technologies, gender and protection aspects that verify acceptance of cash transfers by intended beneficiaries, and security and safety of staff and WFP assets. Transfer values are established based on a standard local food basket and this in turn is regularly monitored through our market monitoring tools and adjusted on regular basis – at the moment on quarterly basis. Given WFP’s unique capacity to undertake price monitoring on weekly basis across the country, food price inflation and other economic constraints are closely monitored and reflected in our internal transfer value review exercise.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WFP strives ensure that beneficiaries’ safety, dignity and integrity are upheld, and that they are not exposed to any harm or protection risks through WFP programmes. WFP also seeks to enhance the integration of protection by identifying and building on opportunities to contribute to protection outcomes where protection risks to beneficiaries may be reduced by WFP assistance. All efforts will be made to ensure that targeted beneficiaries have meaningful access to assistance provided, based on need and without prejudice. Beneficiary identification is informed by vulnerability assessments that categorize households into 4 main categories varying from those that are severely food insecure to those that are not food insecure. Food assistance is geared towards the most and moderately food insecure. Empowerment is done through engagement of local communities in food management committees, mothers support groups, project management committees and community nutrition volunteers. Efforts are also underway to build beneficiaries’ capacities and assets through asset creation programmes, in FFA and PSN programmes. Accountability both to beneficiaries and donors underpins all of WFP’s work. The country office is in the process of reviewing/establishing/expanding CFMs to ensure that beneficiaries have the ability to provide information and feedback on our programmes, and get responses as necessary.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability is one of core values that helps WFP to provide the best possible service to the people it assists. AAP is defined by WFP as an active commitment to give account to, take account of, and held to account by the people it assists. During the preliminary stages, WFP provides accurate, timely and accessible information to affected people about its assistance. The information provided must be clearly understandable by everyone, irrespective of their age, gender or other characteristics. Upholding the ‘centrality of protection’, the design and planning phase also considers food and nutrition assistance to be provided in a safe and dignified manner to the targeted population.During implementation, regular meetings and beneficiary reference sessions will be held were held with community leaders and representatives of different community groups to ensure that they understand the project objectives and intended outcomes, and to solicit their feedback about any necessary changes or adaptations needed to make the programme a success. During implementation, programme adjustments if needed can be done in consultation withprogramme beneficiaries)In all monitoring and evaluation exercises, WFP monitors the proportion of assisted people informed about the programme, as well as the proportion of activities for which beneficiary feedback is documented, analyzed and integrated into programme improvement. This also applied for this project, with all complaints and/or feedback received through the Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs) being addressed with relevant programme units, and appropriate beneficiaries being informed about the action taken or resolution.
