Project Summary

based on the HNO 2021 and a comprehensive needs assessment carried out during 2019-2020 for beekeepers in North West Syria (NWS), Seeds of Hope (SOH) aims to protect and develop the livelihoods of 300 Households working in beekeeping (in Aleppo/Al-Bab & Azaz districts) by providing them with Vocational Training that enhances productivity and marketability of their honey, in order to create feasible income that saves their dignities and sustains their businesses and protect them from negative coping strategies under the stress of conﬂict. The project includes the study of the Honey value chain in the local context and ﬁnding solutions for main barriers hindering this social/economic activity from reaching its maximum limits. The project will provide the following services for the targeted beekeepers: 1- Market Analysis 2- Cash for Work 3- Organize the profession locally.

4 - Needs

The project will address the following assessed needs: 1- Productive Assets owned by beekeeper (quantity, quality, and suﬃciency for supporting livelihood of HH) 2- Practices followed by beekeepers technical and business-wise (maturity level) 3- General constrains aborting beekeeping activity (Security, Checkpoints, Agricultural practices ...etc.) 4- Availability of inputs necessary for production and affordability to beekeepers. Seeds of Hope based on HNO 2021 and in coordination with donors carried out needs assessment for beekeepers on 4/5/2021 and the logframe/workplan for the donor is under preparation.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented by professional agricultural engineers specialized in beekeeping, and utilizing reliable connection and relationship in the local communities targeted by the project, and according to the FSL Cluster framework to standardize the intervention. The project Intervention (activities) will focus on the following: Expert beekeeper (Consultant) will evaluate the beekeepers businesses/livelihoods from two aspects: 1- Professional practices of beekeepers in utilizing existing beekeeping assets in production (to be followed by Vocational Training whenever necessary). 2- Supporting small beekeepers to increase their production and generate suitable income, (to be achieved by distribution of production assets such as "beehives", "queens"...etc. or by providing cash assistance for operational expenses)

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat 1: Training will not be utilized by beekeepers to enhance livelihood activities, Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium, Mitigating Measure: Consult community throughout program cycle to determine the reasoning behind the poor practice of training and solicit their feedback with regards to solutions, Monitoring: Enhance data collection: (i) align PDM tool with global standards; where possible, (ii) collect qualitative data through FGDs with different segments of the community; and (iii) consistently review and address contents of feedback mechanisms. Resources: Staff time to (i) enhance tools; (ii) conduct FGDs; and (iii) regularly monitor feedback mechanism.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

PSEA Risk/Threat 1: beneficiaries sexually harassed or abused in exchange for project aids, Likelihood: low, Impact: high, Mitigating Measure: Consult community throughout program cycle to determine the safest mechanism for handing over the cash/in-kind assistance, Monitoring: Enhance communication: (i) distribute brochures before/during aids distribution including hot-line numbers for reporting PSEA incidents (ii) avoid 1 to 1 connection with beneficiaries (SOH front-end team will consist of 1 male and 1 female at least), and (iii) provide training for SOH field staff, and ensure they commit to humanitarian principles and code of conduct signed after recruitment.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The project took into consideration the following risks (beyond the project's control):1- Mass displacement from Idlib to Aleppo based on conflict escalation (SOH contributes actively with local stakeholders during crises and has plan B to shift operation to Turkish/Syrian borderline).2- Markets may be un-accessible because of insecurity (modalities will be changed if necessary according to local security conditions/challenges).3- Some humanitarian actors may conduct uncoordinated assistance for beekeepers without informing SOH (coordination with the local council and FSL cluster will take place before implementation).
