Project Summary

UNHCR will strive to ensure that Rohingya refugee children aged 3-18 years can access equitable quality education opportunities through existing programmes in the two registered camps in Cox’s Bazar, as well as through the delivery of additional interventions across other camps. This includes maintaining existing learning and WASH facilities, recruiting, training and deploying teachers, and procuring teaching and learning materials. UNHCR will construct additional classrooms with gender-segregated latrines and WASH facilities to accommodate additional learners. In support of the full rollout of the Myanmar Curriculum, a two-month basic pedagogy training will be provided to teachers from a range of education partners, along with refresher training for master trainers participating in the ongoing mentorship program. Considering Government of Bangladesh (GoB) directives on the non-use of the Bangla language, the introduction of the English version of the Bangladeshi curriculum or the use of any other English language curricula - as applicable to the two registered camps only - will represent a critical undertaking for 2024. An English language acquisition program for registered camp schoolteachers and continuous professional development for all teachers will be provided to ensure the required language competencies and teaching skills. Regular training on cross-cutting issues (e.g., child protection, gender and disability inclusion, gender-responsive pedagogy, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, and mental health and psychosocial support) will also be provided. The project will recruit Rohingya women, who will be trained and deployed to ensure gender parity. There will also be a focus on disability inclusion through identifying children with disabilities, ensuring accessibility to the learning facilities, and providing individual support for the most vulnerable. In addition to mainstream education for 17,316 refugee children, UNHCR will continue providing Early Childhood Education (ECE) interventions for 1,400 children aged 3-4 years through community-based learning facilities aligning with sector standards. Additionally, UNHCR in collaboration with BARD College will implement a Language, Images, Analytical Thinking (LIT) youth empowerment and upskilling programme to promote the English language and soft skills related to analysis and communication including basic ICT skills development, as r those refugees who possess previous lower secondary level education but who have not previously received education either through the Myanmar Curriculum in the learning centres or registered camp secondary schools. After completing LIT, students will have the opportunity to move to the bridging programme and will be supported. Adolescents, especially girls who have completed the highest available level of education, will be organized into groups where they will participate in activities aimed at not only boosting their life skills and literacy skills, but also keeping them safe. UNHCR will continue ensuring community engagement in the overall project cycle through regular community consultations, monthly parent meetings, and monthly school management committee and Education Support Groups (CESG) meetings while maintaining Age Gender, and Diversity (AGD) principles. It will enhance school community governance and participation mechanisms by including the community in risk reduction activities, as well as integrating school community-based participatory planning and conducting community awareness on key areas as they emerge. UNHCR will continue its host community support by maintaining existing access to technology, renovating classrooms and WASH facilities, and providing classroom furniture and supplies. To ensure social cohesion and peacebuilding, the project will observe different days including 16 days of Activism, literacy days and refugee days, and organize annual cultural events and sports competitions.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

As per the results of the 2023 Multi-Sector Need Assessment (MSNA), access to education by Rohingya refugee children in the camps remains challenging. In the registered camps of Nayapara and Kutupalong, 33% of school-aged learners are not yet enrolled in any kind of education facilities. In all camps, while the number of education facilities increased on a very limited scale in 2023, the number of school-aged children also increased, with many pre-primary learners promoted to primary education which resulted in the need for additional classrooms. The MSNA also indicates that the lack of an appropriate curriculum represents the primary reason (indicated by 65% of respondents) for not attending school in the registered camps, while across the camps a lack of qualified teaching staff including teachers was highlighted as the main challenge. Low literacy levels amongst the Rohingya refugee population result in a lack of qualified and trained teachers which remains one of the major barriers to providing quality education. This challenge is further amplified amongst female teachers. While all teaching and learning materials are available in non-native languages (English or Burmese), teaching in a multilingual setting is very challenging, especially for facilitators/teachers with no qualifications and limited training. In the Registered Camps, based on the findings from a series of community consultations, refugees are unwilling to accept the Myanmar Curriculum and wish to continue to access the English version of the Bangladesh Curriculum, resulting in significant numbers of children not attending school. There is a need to find a solution to curriculum access for this group to ensure community buy-in and improved attendance. Additionally, the results of several stakeholder consultations, including individual studies, demonstrated that ongoing professional development training delivered by Education Sector partners - including UNHCR - led to the provision of a two-month-long certified training on pedagogy for teachers. However, there is a need to take the Myanmar language acquisition program and scale up training on pedagogy and subject-based training. In addition, UNHCR has received reports of early marriage, teen pregnancy, child labour, and children engaged in harmful practices, which have the potential to lead to student dropout and mental health and well-being challenges, making it challening for children to focus on lessons and make the necessary progress to move forward with their education. As per the Education Sector Dashboard (as of 30 September 2023), less than 1% of children with disabilities are enrolled in learning facilities against the 2023 target of 3% target. These reports indicate a need to improve the identification of and access for children with disabilities and ensure that learning facilities and educators are well prepared to meet the needs of this group. The September 2022 Skills Needs Assessment supported by UNHCR and conducted by USEP Bangladesh found that - based on its sample of 813 respondents across 33 camps - 31% of Rohingya youth had received education whilst living in the camps, however, private tuition represented the most accessed provision, which highlights the need to prioritize this group in education activities. UNHCR will work with its partners and communities to remove barriers to girls’ education and promote gender equality in education. Finally, certification and clear pathways for higher education remain one of the most significant challenges requiring cross-border and regional level engagement.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

UNHCR works towards an integrated approach through its engagement in coordination, service delivery and relations with governments. In Bangladesh, UNHCR has coordinated with UNICEF to reduce duplication of interventions related to the scale-up of the Myanmar Curriculum, resulting in the handover of such interventions to UNICEF while UNHCR will continue its footprint in both of the registered camps through one partner implementing activities across all education intervention areas. To avoid duplication in service provision, UNHCR coordinated with UNICEF and UNESCO to segregate the responsibility for teachers' capacity-building among the three UN agencies. UNHCR also reviews its implementing partners' activities to ensure alignment with the Joint Response Plan (JRP) and current sector strategies and minimum standards. UNHCR advises all implementing partners to regularly participate in camp-level coordination platforms and consult with relevant sector working groups regarding newly emerging activities to ensure compliance with technical standards and the standard package. To ensure predictability and continuity (principle 4) UNHCR has a multi-year strategy in place (2023-2025) which seeks to respond to the education needs of refugees by increasing learning opportunities in a safe and protective environment, ensuring an inclusive learning environment for boys and girls, including persons with specific needs. UNHCR has also secured multi-year funding opportunities (e.g., Education Cannot Wait MYRP II). The selection process of implementing partners provides weighting to agencies who have worked in the same sector and same camps during multiple years, recognizing that it takes time to build relations with Government agencies, refugees and other implementing partners in the camp. However, some exceptions will be made given that some partners have been requested to move their operations to new locations as part of the rationalization process amongst programme partners.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended adverse effects of the interventions, which risk increasing people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. Access to assistance and services will be ensured in proportion to needs and without discrimination, gender, disability or age-related barriers. Special attention will be paid to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services, e.g., persons with disabilities, elderly persons and children. Specific objectives for implementing partners are included for teacher training on key protection areas such as child protection and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) to ensure that safety and dignity are prioritized and maintained. Implementing partners will ensure that PSEA and child protection referral mechanisms are in place, while awareness-raising activities will be conducted with students, parents, and teachers on how to access such mechanisms, which will be monitored by UNHCR through its multi-functional reviews in conjunction with protection colleagues. Each learning facility will have a PSEA focal person (including one female), while child protection will be mainstreamed throughout the project by training and sensitizing project and partner staff. Community-based child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) mechanisms to monitor, identify and address child protection and GBV concerns will be supported and strengthened. Community/parent support groups will help monitor, report and refer identified protection cases within education interventions. Safe learning spaces will be provided, while facilitators/instructors will receive training on child protection principles and how to identify and refer cases. Social and emotional learning (SEL) and life skills interventions will equip girls and boys with key knowledge and skills to help them mitigate, prevent and respond to GBV.

3.3 - Age & Disability

UNHCR will provide education interventions for children, adolescents and youth ranging from 3-18 years, tailoring support to meet the needs of each specific group. UNHCR's implementing partners will also develop Community Education Support Groups (CESGs) comprising a range of community members of various ages, following age, gender and diversity (AGD) principles, who meet on a monthly basis. For children aged 15+ years, adjustments will be made to enable their participation in an age-appropriate and needs-based Language, Images, and Analytical Thinking (LIT) youth empowerment and upskilling programme to enable the continuation of their learning through higher education or other types of livelihood/labour pathways. UNHCR acts to protect children (persons who are under 18 years of age) by responding to their specific needs and the risks they face. It protects and campaigns against all forms of discrimination; prevents and responds to abuse, neglect, violence and exploitation; ensures immediate access to appropriate services; and provides durable solutions in the child's best interest. UNHCR will ensure that its implementing partner follows sector guidelines on new classroom designs to ensure access fo disabled students. Teachers will receive integrated models on disability inclusion as part of their induction and training packages. UNHCR will request implementing partners to use Washington Group questionnaire tools to identify children with disabilities, and act appropriately to ensure the classrooms are inclusive and individual cases are referred to specialized organizations for assistive devices, if required. The project will emphasize community-based learning/blended learning approaches to minimize access barriers, especially for the most vulnerable including children with disabilities and adolescent girls, as well as students of religious beliefs from religious institutions.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNHCR has provided a helpline (+88 017 359 3300) to enable Rohingya refugees to speak directly with a dedicated UNHCR staff member on various issues. UNHCR's email address is also used by many refugees to raise their concerns. The issues raised are then channeled to various sections within UNHCR to provide an appropriate solution. The community is consulted periodically through focus group discussions (FGDs) and key informant interviews (KIIs) to identify their opinions on the services provided and identify areas of improvement, as well as particiapte in consultations related to the project planning and evaluation process. UNHCR monitors and evaluates its intervention through mid and year-end multi-functional reviews in which parents, school managemnet committee members and community education support group (CESG) members play a vital role in providing feedback. Implementing partners will also have their own complaints and feedback mechanisms in place - such as suggestion boxes available at all schools - to ensure their programmes are adaptive and responsive to the needs of the community. Implementing partners will organize and engage SMCs and CESGs and ensure that monthly meetings and capacity building sessions are maintained for the purpose of encouraging ownership by the community and to inform them of their rights with regards to education.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNHCR is fully committed to gender-responsive safeguarding and has policies and practices in place to reduce the risk of, prevent, and respond to any incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by UN personnel, implementing partner staff and other relevant persons involved in the humanitarian response. UNHCR will continue delivering training and refreshers for teachers on prevention of SEA and gender-baesd violence (GBV). UNHCR's implementing partners will operate sex-disaggregated schools for secondary education, as well as sex-disaggregated language, imaging and analytical thinking (LIT) centres for adolescents, and ensure a proportion of at least 50% female teachers (where possible) and 100% female teachers for early childhood development (ECD) for the purpose of increasing female participation and community acceptance. Separate WASH facilities for boys and girls will be available at all schools, while menstrual hygiene and sanitation emergency kits and bins will be available in latrine facilities at all learning facilities. In 2024, UNHCR will continue to monitor the safety of its learning centres through the continuation of its PSEA/gender-based violence (GBV) Safety Audit which started in 2022 and is conducted across all the learning facilities of all implementing partners.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNHCR through its partners maintains a budget for contingency planning for the purpose of procuring teaching-learning and learning centre tiedown materials to be used during emerency periods during monsoon or natural disasters. Additionally, each school has a small-scale tree plantation plan in place, considering the available space. Furthermore, in each school a solar panel will be set up to alternate with the electric supplly as a way of providing renewable energy.
