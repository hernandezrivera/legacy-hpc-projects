Project Summary

UNRWA Technical and Vocational Education and Training Programme (TVET) will continue to provide short-term courses at Damascus Training Center in addition to Homs, Aleppo, Daraa and Lattakia training centers, based on availability of funds. As demand for TVET services grows, the Agency will continue its efforts to better link students with the job market in an effort to increase employability rates of graduates.
Building on 2020 learning and good practices in response to the COVID-19 pandemic, the courses will be delivered through a mixed-modality (in-class or online) or remotely (online) to avoid overcrowding in classrooms. Self-learning resources will be made available to students. Hygiene measures and physical distancing will be ensured across all TVET premises.
Finally, depending on the availability of funds, UNRWA will explore the feasibility of conducting small-scale cash-for-work initiatives in 2021 mainly focusing on job placements supporting works in camps and Palestine refugee gatherings.

4 - Needs

Out of the 438,000 Palestine refugees currently estimated to be inside Syria, two-thirds have been displaced at least once since the beginning of the conflict. Prolonged displacement further compounds existing instabilities, and entrenches unemployment, and loss of livelihoods opportunities within established Palestine refugees’ communities and camps. A socio-economic assessment of living conditions conducted by UNRWA in December 2017estimated that 91 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in absolute poverty (less than US$ 2 person/day) prior to receiving UNRWA cash assistance. In 2020, the already deteriorating economic situation has been further compounded by measures taken to limit the spread of COVID-19, which had a negative impact on the availability of employment opportunities. As such, an assessment conducted in August 2020 by UNRWA on the socioeconomic impact of COVID-19 indicated that Palestine refugees’ ability to secure their income has faced a devastating setback. Close to 30 per cent of Palestine refugee respondents said that the head of the household had lost her or his job over the past months, while 30 per cent also said that the wages of the head of household had reduced due to COVID-19. Close to half of Palestine refugee households mentioned that they currently have sufficient money to cover daily needs just for one day and only 21 per cent said the household has a stable monthly income. In such context, livelihood interventions are critical to increase the resilience of Palestine refugees in Syria, who continue to endure the damaging impact of the conflict. 5 - Implementation of activities

In 2021, UNRWA will continue to fundraise for livelihood activities, which are critical to increase the resilience of Palestine refugees in Syria and to support their access to employment. To support youth employment and livelihood capacity, the UNRWA Technical and Vocational Education and Training Programme (TVET) will continue to provide short-term courses to 800 students (including 460 female, 340 male and 32 persons with disabilities) at Damascus Training Center in addition to Homs, Aleppo, Daraa and Lattakia training centers, based on funds’ availability. As demand for TVET services grows, the Agency will continue its efforts to better link students with the job market in an effort to increase employability rates of graduates through developing their employability and soft skills. Building on 2020 learning and good practices in response to the COVID-19 pandemic, the courses will be delivered through a mixed-modality (in-class or online) or remotely (online) to avoid overcrowding in classrooms. Self-learning resources will be made available to students. Hygiene measures and physical distancing will be ensured across all TVET premises. Finally, depending on the availability of funds, UNRWA will explore the feasibility of conducting small scale cash-for-work initiatives in 2021. Through this intervention, UNRWA aims to provide short-term employment opportunities to 5,000 people (including 3,443 female, 1,557 male and 392 persons with disabilities) and mainly focusing on job placements supporting works in camps and Palestine refugee gatherings.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Exposure to Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) for Palestine refugees participating in cash for work programmes especially those focusing on reconstruction and rehabilitation of UNRWA installations in newly accessible areas. Mitigating measures: 1. Training to staff, contractors and community members including vocational education students and microﬁnance beneﬁciaries on reducing exposure to these risks will be undertaken. UNRWA staff received ToT from UNMAS. 2. Thorough assessments are undertaken prior to site selection. 2) Risk of exploitation and abuse by staff members or service providers providing cash for work, microﬁnance, and vocational training opportunities. Mitigating measures: 1. All UNRWA staff sign the code of conduct on commencement of their contracts. 2. Training to staff on the code of conduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA. 3. Reinforced messaging to community members on the importance of reporting SEA and ensure mechanisms are in place to ensure conﬁdentiality and set up accessible and eﬃcient complaint mechanisms for beneﬁciaries to report concerns about sexual violence, exploitation, corruption or other abuses. 4. Frequent communication to recipient communities about the available services and clear criteria for accessing the same. 5. UNRWA will adapt work hours and locations, as appropriate, to reduce exposure to sexual violence. This may require shortening workdays to only daylight hours, including times for travel to and from work sites. 6. UNRWA has a mechanism to address SEA complaints including investigations and sanctions.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1. Humanitarian needs of of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. Risk UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lack of funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Last year, UNRWA received only around 30% of its requirements for Syria. Mitigation: 1. continue to monitor the situation and to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Ensure robust financial, planning and management systems 4. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status of funding. 5. Continue to monitor its interventions through a results-based monitoring framework. The RBM approach to programming ensures evidence-based decision-making and accountability. Risks are also mitigated by ensuring a sound and solid monitoring of implementation. Risk UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. Risk: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. PPEs will be provided to frontline staff and sanitization materials will be made available at offices and distribution centres.
