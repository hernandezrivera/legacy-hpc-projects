Needs

The Palestinian Food Security Sector (FSS) was established based on the need for a single coordination structure tailored to the specific protracted crisis in the West Bank and Gaza Strip (WBGS), while inter-linking humanitarian and development interventions. The FSS synergizes the efforts of active partners across three main pillars – food assistance, agriculture/livelihood and cash-based programming. The sector builds on strong partnerships to support sustainable, comprehensive and locally owned food security solutions. The FSS works in coordination with the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA), the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD), the Ministry of Finance and Planning (MoFP), and other dedicated governmental bodies like the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistic (PCBS) and the AreaC Coordination Office (ACCO). The FSS is composed by approximately 45 members through open fora and thematic platforms that include technical working groups, and ad-hoc committees. The FSS is co-led by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) and the World Food Programme (WFP), in close liaison with UNRWA, the Palestinian Authority, with the involvement of local and international NGOs.The FSS aims to: 1. reinforce the information management capacity to inform joint analysis of underlying causes of food insecurity; 2. optimize interaction between information/analysis and planning/implementation; 3. maximize complementarities among agencies and interventions; 4. coordinate responses, within a structured vision to enhance their efficiency. Data from the last food security survey (PCBS, WFP 2016, unpublished) and related projections for 2018 indicate that 1.6 million people, or 31.5% of the overall population, is considered in need of food security support. Building resilience is confirmed as a common objective across humanitarian assistance, which requires achieving a common understanding of risks and opportunities, and identifying and prioritizing what contributes most to resilience building, benefiting also from the SEFSec analysis. An experimental resilience marker exercise was initiated in 2016, and will be employed in 2019. It provides a concrete tool for assessing projects ability to contribute to the resilience capacity of beneficiaries. Programme designers need to improve the effectiveness of projects toward resilience building. In WBGS, dedicated resources are required to maintain and reinforce food security analysis and response. Food security analysis needs to mainstream cross cutting issues, highlighting the different needs of women, men, boys and girls, elderly, and people with disabilities. Where possible, these data will be used to provide accurate information about the evolving situation and to guide interventions, also in order to reduce vulnerability and maximise resilience building within the Humanitarian Response Plan. Centralized monitoring of the achievements along the years is also needed. The collection of data and related analysis needs comprehensive and coordinated approach to humanitarian and developmental domains, in order to create links and synergies among all actors. The complex situation in the country requires that the FSS works within wider coordination fora, including those managed by OCHA or including developmental actors. In order to further build on existing achievements, it is crucial to ensure an adequate level of dedicated staffing as well as drawing on technical expertise for food security assessments and analysis. A next round of SEFSec survey is scheduled for 2020 and additional critical food security indicators, need to be considered, promoted, and possibly incorporated into institutional surveys. Dedicated staff will continue to provide independent support to achieve the expected deliverables.The FSS coordination structure will continue to work at the inter-cluster level in order to develop efforts to link with other clusters, to ensure a holistic response.

Activities

The project envisages strengthening the food security (FS) coordination system, in five main areas:1.FS priorities inform strategic planning of FSS partners through inclusive and participatory processes, avoiding duplications and increasing efficiency and effectiveness of FS-related assistance, including:•Humanitarian Programme Cycle (HPC): collect, analyse and plan for FSS needs, gaps and responses with involvement of all FSS stakeholders;•Inform the Pool Funds (HPF) and other ad-hoc funding opportunities;•Coordinate response to demolitions of agricultural assets in the WB and other ad-hoc responses.2.Support the production of information required to enhance analysis, planning and coordination of FS interventions, focusing on resilience building and disaster risk reduction (DRR), including:•Engage relevant actors in sharing information on planning, priorities and findings, and assessing gaps and duplications, to promote complementarity among activities and joint programming;•Maintain an updated inventory of who-is-doing-what-where-when of FSS partners and to maximise the use of the FSS projects module within existing MoA information systems, envisaging a phased transfer of responsibility to MoA, including 4Ws-style information regular updates to OCHA.•Support the making of the SEFSec 2018 report, as well as the consolidation of following rounds of food security surveys, also by promoting the transfer of required competencies to PCBS and introducing innovative indicators like the Food Insecurity Experienced Scale (FIES). This will be considered in strict cooperation with PCBS, over a phased plan, based on consultations and feedback from all stakeholders, also within the SDGs tasks;•Carry out analytical work on resilience based on livelihoods type, with focus on gender equality. This work will complement and expand the FS profiling from the SEFSec survey.•Undertake or facilitate required mapping and assessment exercises, meeting the developing situation on the ground, like the energy crisis in GS and the adoption of renewable energy.•Revalidate the resilience marking (RM) exercise, and roll it out for the period 2019-20.•Maintain and improve FS related parts of the inter-cluster contingency plan in coordination with OCHAand line ministries.3.Support development of local capacities for effective coordination of humanitarian interventions linked with developmental ones. This includes:•Appreciate capacity of NGOs, line ministries and decentralized institutions in relation to practicing and leading coordination functions. This includes line ministries’ capacity and willingness to directly manage core coordination functions, initially in the West Bank, keeping the focus on response to demolitions, and possibly exploring a wider range of actions and achievements within year 2020;•Promote joint work between FSS and related government entity(ies)’s;•Foster qualitative programming components like Protection and People Centric issues also by delivering trainings for FSS partners. FSS gender focal points will continue to work in coordination with UN focal points and feed FSS partners with inputs/analysis on gender-related issues.•Promoting coordination government-led coordination structures (central and decentralised), LACS, AIDA, PNGO, NGOs consortia, to achieve consistency between humanitarian and development interventions.4.Advocating to address root causes of food insecurity. This includes:•Promote collaboration among FSS partners for increased awareness of root causes of food insecurity among all stakeholders;•Facilitate linkages between national initiatives and FSS partners;•Promote inclusion of advocacy elements into programmes.5.Contribute to inter-sectorial coordination at the central and governorate levels, including identification of needs and response modalities that may require inter-cluster harmonised response. Interactions with the HN cluster to coordinate on the relevant nutrition issues.

Indicators

This project aims to strengthen all aspects of the FSS Response Plan; a sector wide evidence-based needs identification and harmonized response among active sector members should lead to more effective interventions including food and cash assistance as well as agriculture. The staff hired under this project will provide support to the various technical working groups of the FSS. Overall outputs-A sector-wide, evidence-based and inclusive response framework linking food, cash and agricultural interventions is improved;- FSS participation in the HPC process for the years 2019-20 is efficiently facilitated;-The response framework activities links emergency response to development interventions;-FSS partners are facilitated to share best practices and improve their technical capacities in line with recommendations from line ministries;-Food Security related indicators and data published when available;-The resilience marker is implemented in the HPC cycles 2019-20;-FSS advocacy priorities are embedded into national plans as well as reflected within FSS partner programmes.-Food Security analysis includes gender gaps in food security, resilience, livelihoods and access to productive assets for females in agriculture as key drivers of resilience for households dependent on agricultural livelihood.
