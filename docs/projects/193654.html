Project Summary

Access to food in Gaza remains a challenge for most refugees struggling with deteriorating socio-economic condition compounded with recurrent hostilities and the impact of global emergencies including the Ukraine Crisis and COVID-19 pandemic. Soaring rates of unemployment and limited income are key drivers to poverty and food insecurity among the population. The HRP 2023 Strategic Objective 3 states that the capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
Through the Cash for Work intervention, UNRWA aims to support Palestine refugees in Gaza coping with protracted threats and shocks, and mitigate food insecurity through offering short term employment opportunities to approximately 15,400 poor Palestine refugees. These will be equal to more than 1,540,000 workdays, and will inject about US$ 19 million into local economy, generating a 5,500 full time job equivalents (FTEs). The short-term job opportunities will include more than 11,300 unskilled positions, more than 3,900 skilled positions and around 200 professional positions. UNRWA aims to provide 40 per cent of skilled and professional job opportunities to women; about 40 per cent of all CfW opportunities will target youth and young adults (aged 19–29) to expose them to the workplace. The project directly contributes to the HRP 2023 FSS Objective 2: Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs by providing CfW opportunities.
UNRWA ensures that beneficiaries under the Cash for Work programming have access to occupational health and safety norms for the work they agree to undertake by identifying the functions and way the Cash for Work modality can be deployed for UNRWA activities safely and in a dignified manner in order to continue providing income generation opportunities to refugees to achieve livelihood objectives.

1 - Needs

Over 15 years of blockade with its restrictions on movement, imports/exports and fishing zones continued to be one of the main factors of economic de-development in Gaza. Today, 81.5 percent of individuals in Gaza, 71 percent of whom are Palestine refugees, live below the national poverty line (UNRWA-PCBS, 2022: Gaza_15 years of blockade | UNRWA). While people continue to struggle to endure the impact of COVID-19, recurrent hostilities, most recently in August 2022, the socio-economic situation in Gaza continues to further deteriorate due to the war in Ukraine in 2022 globally affecting the prices of essential needs including food. Unemployment continues to be high as small and medium sized businesses strive to survive. Poor infrastructure, electricity outages, outdated Information Communication Technology (ICT) and scarcity of water, all contribute to deteriorating socio-economic and overall living conditions in Gaza. The ongoing salary crisis for civil servant of both the PA and the de facto government adds new layers of the population needing immediate assistance (HNO 2023). Within this context, an already impoverished Palestine refugee population, almost two-thirds of Gaza's population, faces increased vulnerabilities. According to the Palestine Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), the overall unemployment rate in Gaza was 44.1 per cent during the second quarter of 2022. These trends have a particularly severe impact on vulnerable groups including women and young people. Unemployment rate is especially high among women with 65 per cent (PCBS, Q2-2022), facing both lack of opportunities and social barriers to employment in Gaza. Young people between the ages of 15 and 29 are also significantly affected with average unemployment rate of 72 per cent (PCBS, Q2-2022). Despite being highly educated, young people in Gaza face the prospect of unemployment, remaining dependent on already strained families and isolated from the outside world, rather than creating an independent future for themselves. According to OCHA’s oPt Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) findings in Gaza, 60.3 per cent of surveyed Gaza households reported a member of their household being unemployed and looking for work at the time of data collection, and the most frequently reported obstacles to any member of their household finding work, at 64.1 per cent is due to the increased competition and not enough jobs. Consistently high unemployment rates and deteriorating purchasing power have eroded many households’ capital and coping mechanisms, rendering most of the population of Gaza dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs. The MSNA 2022 also found that there was a widespread reported use of negative coping strategies, showing 56 per cent of households in crisis level and 12 per cent in emergency level. Furthermore, 73.2 per cent of surveyed households in Gaza reported that they had received the assistance of some type in the last six months prior to data collection, yet 79 per cent of households also reported their households having recently taken on debt for any reason in the 3 months prior to data collection (MSNA 2022). UNRWA provides food assistance to more than 1.1 million Palestine refugees in 2022, up from just 80,000 in 2000, showcasing the 1,324 per cent increase (UNRWA-PCBS, 2022). UNRWA has been experiencing the influx of people desperately seeking for jobs, even for a temporary employment such as Cash for Work opportunities. Currently, an average waiting time for a family to receive such opportunity is more than four years.

2 - Activities

Through its Job Creation Programme (JCP), UNRWA will support Palestine refugees coping with protracted threats and shocks and mitigate food insecurity through offering short-term employment opportunities to approximately 15,400 poor Palestine refugees. It equals to more than 1,540,000 workdays generating almost 5,500 full time job equivalents and injects about US$ 19 million into local economy. The full-time job equivalents (FTEs) are determined based on the workdays generated, which means that one FTE may be filled by 4 beneficiaries working 3 months each. The short-term job opportunities would include more than 11,300 unskilled positions, around 3,900 skilled positions and around 200 professional positions. Examples of unskilled positions include school attendants, guards, and food packers at distribution centers; skilled positions include midwives, nurses, accountants, and teachers; and professional positions include medical officers and engineers. Priority will be given to applicants from households who have assessed as living below the poverty line, and the applicants from households that do not have any members of families receiving CfW opportunities. As a part of the Agency-wide Gender Mainstreaming Strategy, UNRWA aims to provide 40 per cent of skilled and professional job opportunities to women (1,640 out of 4,100 positions) and about 40 per cent of all CfW opportunities to youth between 18 to 29 years old (6,160 out of 15,400 positions). To ensure benefits are spread widely, contracts for unskilled positions are offered for three months and up to four months, and contracts for skilled positions are offered for three months and up to nine months. The duration of contact for professional positions I is up to 12 months. Only one member of a given household is eligible for cash for work contract in the same time period, and there will be minimum 12-month period between the expiration of a contract and another member from the same family being signed up to a new CfW contract. The salaries provided will remain below average wage level so as not to distort the local market. The cash transfer amount will be paid in the local currency to avoid the impact of fluctuations in the exchange rate and will range from US$ 10 to US$ 20 per day, depending on the skill level and the number of workdays per week. The JCP field monitoring team will ensure appropriate placement of refugees and will monitor work attendance and performance. In 2023, being COVID-19 vaccinated will form a condition of employment through the CfW. Palestine refugees benefitting from the CfW intervention, who may need to suspend their assignment due to exposure to COVID-19, will be offered the opportunity to complete their assignment in a subsequent period once they have completed their self-isolation period and/or received the necessary COVID-19 treatment. As a member of PSEA network, UNRWA commits to all PSEA mandatory activities across its programmes, and the interventions included in this proposal are not exception. UNRWA adopts a safe complaint channel(s) for beneficiaries based on consultations with the beneficiaries and accessible to different groups. The PSEA focal points are trained to receive complaints in multitude of ways, including a 24/7 WhatsApp number available through text or phone for SEA complaints, and a centralized confidential online/telephone/email complaints mechanism to respond to specific confidential complaints are in place. To ensure that the beneficiaries are aware of PSEA mechanism, PSEA awareness raising material will be disseminated to all project recipients. UNRWA regularly engage with HCT oPt PSEA network, and project staff will engage with UNRWA’s representative to this body via training and through project monitoring.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services andoperations with a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestine refugees' rights.An integrated humanitarian response, with the inclusion of Cash for Work, is directlyrelated to a growing focus on resilience to withstand and recover from crisis. The cashsupport reduces current risks by providing relief to strained families and enhancingrefugees’ sense of dignity and self-reliance. Gauging the impact of such very-shorttemporary assistance is impossible; especially in a volatile and chronic-emergencyGaza context; however, without such cash assistance, the vulnerability of poor familieswill be worsened and hence this entire project will contribute – among other factorsand interventions- towards “resilience.” The salary provided will remain below averagewage level so as not to distort the local market, and will range from US$ 10 to US$ 20per day. Also note that although salaries are indicated in US dollar, beneficiaries will becontracted and paid in local currency to avoid the impact of fluctuations in theexchange rate. UNRWA aims to meet gender mainstreaming targets through ensuringgender equity in providing jobs by developing and implementing projects andinitiatives that are appropriate and sensitive to local culture to absorb the large numberof female JCP applicants, in course of conducting needs assessment to identifyopportunities that were both aligned with actual needs and viewed favorable byprospective female employees.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA services are open to all Palestine refugees who are seeking for assistance.However, in terms of the Cash for Work opportunities, the priority will be given toapplicants from households who have been assessed as living below the poverty lineof less than US$3.87 per person per day, considered as food insecure. Targetingcriteria will also include skills and qualifications, location, age and gender. UNRWAaims to provide 40 per cent of skilled and professional job opportunities to women andabout 40 per cent of all CfW opportunities to youth (18 to 29 years old). Offering CfWopportunities to unskilled women in Gaza remains one of the main challenges for thisintervention, mainly due to cultural constraints. To overcome these challenges, someof the available CfW opportunities specifically targeted women. Moreover, new jobopportunities were created to help absorb more vulnerable unskilled women in otherworking fields, such as school attendants, guards, cleaners, and food packers atUNRWA distribution centres. In order to ensure benefits are spread widely, contracts forunskilled positions are offered for three months and up to four months, and contractsfor skilled positions are offered for three months and up to nine months. Only onemember of a given household is eligible for cash for work contract. With the limitedprospects of employment opportunities in Gaza, the Agency through its Job CreationProgramme, managing the Cash for Work, addresses these economic hardships andensures that families are self-reliant. The provision of CfW opportunities are expectedto allow families to cover some of their basic expenses, to improve living conditionsand contribute to their copying mechanisms and resilience.
