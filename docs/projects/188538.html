Project Summary

Syria’s shortage of health infrastructure has negatively affected the availability and quality of health services across Syria, including to the most vulnerable populations. In addition, the health care system continues to face chronic challenges to respond to disease outbreaks including COVID-19.
Thus, UNOPS proposes to support the strengthening of the healthcare system in Syria by rehabilitating damaged public health facilities, providing critical medical equipment, supplies and ambulances, equipping the rehabilitated facilities with renewable energy solutions and delivering the related training in five governorates in Syria.
The objective of this project is to contribute to enhancing the health sector capacity for a timely response to disaster outbreaks, including COVID-19. This project is directly linked with the Strategic Objectives 2 (Enhance Protection) and 3 (Increase resilience and access to services) of Syria Humanitarian Response Plan 2021 and the immediate response priorities of the Health Sector for 2022 in Syria (1).
Target locations of this project may include Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Homs, Damascus and Al-Sweida governorates. These potential target locations are identified based on the analysis from the Health Sector, taking into account the great barriers, lack of availability and high cost of services, and the ranking on the OCHA severity scale. In addition, the priority needs have been identified in close coordination with the MoH and the humanitarian partners. Once the project is launched, UNOPS will conduct gender-sensitive and technical assessments to finalize the selection of the target locations, the exact scope of work, and the specifications and type of the needed equipment and ambulances.
Through this intervention, it is expected that four public health facilities will be rehabilitated and repaired. The scope of work may include the rehabilitation of isolation centers for COVID-19 and the restoration of other critical health facilities, and procurement of medical supplies, equipment (e.g. ICU and non-ICU beds, ventilators, radiographic units, and syringe pumps, etc.) and ambulances.
To fill the gaps in the shortage of electricity in public health facilities, the four rehabilitated health facilities will be equipped with solar energy solutions through the procurement, installation and commissioning of photovoltaic (PV) hybrid systems (controller, solar panels, inverters, monitoring systems, mounting system etc.) and operations and maintenance training will be delivered to the hospital staff.
It is expected this project will enable targeted public health facilities to offer enhanced pivotal health care services to the most vulnerable population, benefitting up to 1.64 million people in targeted sub-districts of Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Homs, Damascus and Al-Sweida governorates.

4 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Syria continues to severely disrupt the healthcare system, with civilians - particularly women and children - bearing the brunt of the crisis. In addition, the numbers of COVID-19 cases have been sharply increasing over the past months. As of 15 January 2022, a total of 50,674 confirmed COVID-19 cases, including 2,944 associated death cases (CFR 5.8%), were reported in Syria since the start of the pandemic in March 2020. (2)According to Syria HNO 2021, only 58% of hospitals and 53% of primary health care centers are fully functional. Access to basic service is severely hampered and increasingly unaffordable, and the number of hospitals providing surgeries and trauma declined by 13% than last year 2020. Thus, considerable support is required to reinforce its capacity through the rehabilitation of damaged public health facilities.As for the electricity sector, according to the HeRAMS (Health resources and services availability mapping system) annual report for 2020, the availability of electricity generators continues to be highly demanded with the current situation, where electricity power is widely disrupted and the majority of public hospitals are dependent on generators’ power. Thus, equipping health facilities with renewable energy will fill the gaps in the shortage of electricity in public hospitals and ensure uninterrupted provision of critical health care services. Note2. WHO Syrian Arab Republic: COVID-19 Weekly Epidemiological Bulletin

5 - Implementation of activities

The objective of this project is to enhance the health sector capacity for a timely response to disaster outbreaks and for a more resilient health care system to respond to the exacerbated needs of healthcare services to the most vulnerable populations in Rural Damascus, Damascus, Al-Sweida, Homs and Aleppo governorates. This project contributes to SO 2 (Enhance Protection) and 3 (Increase resilience and access to services) of the Syria Humanitarian Response Plan 2021 and the immediate response priorities of the Syria Health Sector for 2022. The planned project activities are as follows:- Rehabilitation public health facilities: gender-sensitive and technical assessments to determine the exact scope of works and target locations; Preparation of technical engineering design, technical specification, and bidding documents for rehabilitation works; Tendering process; and Rehabilitation work; Handover; Defect Notification Period (DNP) for 12 months.- Provision of medical equipment and supplies (e.g. ICU and non-ICU bed for COVID-19 case management, ventilators, radiographic units, and syringe pumps, etc.) and ambulances.- Provision of renewable energy solutions: Technical assessments (electrical and structural integrity); System design and design reviews; Procurement, installation and commissioning of photovoltaic (PV) hybrid systems (controller, solar panels, inverters, monitoring systems, mounting system etc.); Provision of operation & maintenance training; and Handover.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEAH risks are present in the direct contact between UNOPS and it’s contractors’ staff and local community members (beneficiaries) during the rehabilitation works and installation of equipment. An on-site SEAH risk assessment will be conducted before initiating rehabilitation works and installations at all targeted public health facilities and mitigation measures will be included in the project Environmental and Social Management Plan (ESMP). All UNOPS personnel will take a mandatory online PSEA training upon joining the project while contractor personnel working on the project will receive a PSEA training after having signed their contract with UNOPS. The contractor in turn shall train all workers engaged in the project from their side, as well as have them sign a Code of Conduct. Works contractors will also be responsible for conducting regular refresher sessions for their workers as well as for putting up PSEA awareness raising posters adapted to the local context on all work sites to facilitate reporting of cases. For this purpose, the posters shall include the referral pathway for reporting cases, i.e. the contact information and uptake channels of the project GRM. In addition, SEA clauses will be included in all UNOPS personnel and contractor/supplier contracts.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:Security situation in Syria is assessed by United Nations Department for Safety and Security (UNDSS) as permissive for UN operations for the duration of the project in the targeted governorates.Syrian authorities will ensure access to the working sites, and provide the necessary permission and clearance to UNOPS to receive, deliver and install the procured goods before project ends.COVID-19 situation does not deteriorate further. Risks:Political situation deteriorates, resulting in limitations to freedom of movement.Access restrictions are imposed, limiting ability to respond to the needs.Project implementation delays, mainly due to delays in obtaining the necessary approvals from the Government may lead to larger financial risks where personnel costs will be incurred ahead of the delivery.COVID-19 related restrictions on movements imposed by the Government.Mitigation ActionsUNOPS will maintain operational readiness and position staff to support alternate activities, as required. Coordination will continue to ensure tasks are reprioritized in accordance with needs.UNOPS will maintain an active engagement with MoH and other relevant authorities and advocacy to access is granted.UNOPS has a robust internal control framework with internal and external oversight mechanisms to mitigate the risk of fraudulent activities.UNOPS will continuously monitor the situation, keeping all stakeholders informed and will maintain close coordination with the Government.
