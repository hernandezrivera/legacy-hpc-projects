Project Summary

With over 1.2 million and 1.6 million people including men, women, boys and girls in the conflict-affected BAY states (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe) of Northeast Nigeria targeted for GBV and child protection assistance in 2019 respectively, CHAD will employ an integrated approach, which aims to address the distinct protection needs of these extremely vulnerable population including unaccompanied and separated boys and girls, children and adolescent survivors of different forms of conflict-related violence, abuse and exploitation including sexual violence, and children formerly associated with armed groups through provision of safe, tailored and culturally-relevant child protection and GBV response and prevention services.
Over 104,600 beneficiaries across 11 LGAs in Borno State including girls, boys, women and men will be reached with critical, life-saving child protection and GBV assistance while exploring opportunities to contribute to strengthening the resilience of communities affected by the crisis through initiatives supporting improved capacity of community-based organizations and committees in collaboration with relevant government institutions and the Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development (MWASD) to address protection concerns related to child protection and GBV in Borno local government areas.
Through this intervention CHAD will also be taking forward its commitments under the Call to Action on Protection from Gender-Based Violence (CTA) led by ECHO and the Nigerian government, which was officially launched in July 2018 as a multi-stakeholder initiative focused on eliminating gender based violence in Northeast Nigeria.

Needs

As a result of conflict and displacement, IDPs and host communities residing in over-congested camps and undignified living conditions in Borno state face protection concerns including sexual violence and exploitation, kidnapping, arbitrary detention, brutal violence. Thousands of children have been separated from their families or exploited by armed groups. Women and girls, already exposed to harmful gender dynamics, face increased vulnerabilities to GBV. Reports from IDP camps and host communities during a recent assessment in August 2018 highlight an increasing number of rape and sexual assault incidents amongst adolescent girls. Accounts from residents of IDP camps strongly suggest high levels of sexual exploitation by state armed groups mostly affecting teenage girls due to scarce resources. Harmful traditional practices like early marriage and female genital mutilation are still being perpetrated amid high-levels of under-reporting mostly due to fear of stigma and limited trusted GBV services in the Northeast. There is a need to scale-up GBV response and prevention services including case management for survivors in order to better protect vulnerable boys and girls. Critical concerns also remain around the processing of civilians including girls and boys at military screening sites. In most cases few clear and child-friendly procedures and protocols are in place and arbitrary detention of civilians, including children, is common place. On-going military operations by the Nigerian army in the deep forests and recess of Borno state portends an increase in the number of UASC as most family separations have occurred from flights from violent confrontations and hostile attacks by parties to the conflict. The increase in movements relating to returnees from Cameroon and Chad, military-assisted freedom from captivities and internal relocation of IDPs necessitates the need for FTR services and, alternative care placements and follow-ups. 987,264 people are estimated to be in need of child protection services including boys, girls and women and Over 39,500 UASC have been identified to be in need of case management services (HRP 2019). The psychological distress that children and their caregivers, including adolescent survivors of sexual and gender-based violence are subjected to as a result of the hardship, experience and threats of violence and abuse in the hands of combatants will require an integrated psychosocial support framework that provides structured and culturally relevant recreation and learning opportunities in order to enable them cope better, heal from emotional abuse and revitalize their positive outlook to life in general. Vulnerable caregivers, especially women and child/adolescent-caregivers with limited abilities to access basic needs for survival will require sustainable livelihood interventions to build their resilience, aid recovery while improving the protection outcomes of UASC and other at-risk children under their care. Investments in practical field-based information management solutions complementary to the Child Protection Information Management System (CPIMS) and Gender-based Violence Information Management System (GBVIMS) will be required to support CHAD child protection especially case management processes in accordance with global guidelines in order to inform better targeting of services, and accurate representation of data for reporting and effective advocacy.Also, Local actors haven’t been greatly supported to play a leading role in addressing child protection concerns in terms of funding and capacity development opportunities, putting a constraint on the degree to which humanitarian programmes are defined by actors closest to the issues. Rates of reunification of UASC remains very low -at below 10% in May 2018 due to several factors including lack of available funding for strong FTR actors. (Despite this constraint, CHAD has achieved over 90% of FTR successes reported by the CPSWG sector).

Activities

- Provision of quality integrated Child protection and gender based violence GBV case management services to Unaccompanied and Separated boys and girls, children and adolescent survivors of GBV and those at-risk of other protection concerns.- Ongoing and safe and ethical referrals of children and adolescent survivors of GBV to specialized support services including access to justice - Provision of alternative care placements and follow-ups, and Family tracing and reunification (FTR) services for unaccompanied and separated boys and girls - Establishment of foster caregivers support network in targeted communities - Structured psychosocial support services for psychologically distressed boys and girls to enhance their psychosocial and mental well-being - Establishment and management of Woman, girls and adolescent safe spaces- Establishment of and capacity building for community-based protection mechanisms on basic child protection and GBV priniciples- Development of community-focused GBV and CP referral pathways - Ongoing Community outreach and awareness raising on safe referral pathways for GBV survivors, child protection GBV, and gender equality.- Coordinated policy advocacies and capacity building for technical and field officers from the state Ministry of women affairs and social development on relevant child protection and basic GBV principles related to handling of children survivors of sexual and gender-based violence and case management procedures.- Distribution of protection related kits to vulnerable and at-risk girls and boys to reduce vulnerabilities to child protection and gender based violence related risks- Socio-economic reintegration, empowerment and direct cash transfers to GBV survivors, vulnerable caregivers and boys and girls formerly associated or recently freed from captivity including foster parents and female/child headed homes- Ongoing Safety audits on GBV risks with community members and target beneficiaries- Ongoing Monitoring and evaluation of programs- Guided internal development and implementation of institutional strengthening strategies to increase CHAD's capacity as a national leader in addressing child protection and GBV risks in emergencies and development contexts
