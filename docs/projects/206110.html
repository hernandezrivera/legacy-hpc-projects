Project Summary

This project will contribute to the reduction of protection risks and address some of the persuasive adversaries emanated out of the conflicts, environmental and cultural shocks. The project will enhance people sensitivity and disengagement on conflict and an act of violence that are factoring into gender based violence (GBV), human-trafficking, child labor and sexual slavery, children recruitment into arms group and early child marriage. It will look into key protection risks which warrant general protection (GP) and specific protection (SP) of children, women, elderly and persons with disabilities by devising protection messages; protection monitoring and reporting; awareness raising campaigns; training beneficiaries and host communities leaders on protection; coordinate and facilitate safe referral pathway with specific protection working groups; incentivized survivors and victims in emergency with cash and in-kind support; and build beneficiaries skills and capacity on responsive recovery and resilience.
A population target of approximately twelve thousand (12,000) will be covered by this project out of which: twenty five (25) are key informants (KI) to be interviewed twice in a year per each of the ten (10) Payam totaling to five hundred (500) for the purpose of getting relevant information; on general protection (GP) in emergency - four thousand (4000) to be monitored upon arrival in South Sudan from Sudan; two thousand five hundred (2500) women, men, girls and boys with protection concerns or specific needs to be reached with protection awareness raising messages; two thousand (2000) women, men, girls and boys with protection concerns or specific needs to be identified and referred through static or mobile means; one thousand (1000) women, men, girls and boys to be reached with protection interventions intended for durable solutions including legal aid, access to documentation, reintegration, cash and in-kind based support.
GOAH through integrated approach will develop harmonized key messaging that is also informed by the findings from the protection monitoring analysis. That is to ensure the messages are consistent with the finding for purposes of identifying the most vulnerable people with specific needs.
GOAH will work with the affected population such as elderly, women, IDPs, returnees, refugees and host community and the protection partners; develop key protection awareness raising messages and information on the availability of the services. That is to enable the accuracy and access to the information by the beneficiaries and various stakeholders.
With our routine protection monitoring, GOAH will work with protection partners, develop and implement static or mobile activities targeted at identification and referral of persons with specific needs (PSNs), especially in emergency situations.
GOAH in collaboration with protection partners and the affected population representatives will work to establish and strengthen or enhance existing community-based structures such as youth leaders, women groups, and traditional leaders and implement activities that constitute alternatives to displacement and enable persons of concern to live in dignity.
Working with protection partners, development and peace actors as well as authorities and existing community-based structures and implement activities designed to enhance community resilience, psychosocial support (PSS), prevention from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), peace-building activities and trainings through age, gender and diversity (AGD) approach.

1. Needs

The protection and humanitarian situation in South Sudan is clouded by several factors including the adverse legacies of civil wars and internal conflicts starting from 1955 – 2018; natural calamities with the recent devastating flooding from 2019 to date, coupled with eruption of Sudanese conflict in April 15th, 2023. In accordance to Protection Cluster 2023, there is an estimated number of 3.85M people in need (PiN) of General Protection (GP) as the result of the above conflicts and environmental shocks. The former Southern Sudan which is currently South Sudan had undergone subsequent civil wars and internal conflicts starting with Anyanya I and Anyanya II from 1955 – 1972, 1974 – 1983 respectively, and the SPLM/SPLA era from 1983 to the independence period in 2011. Moreover, two years after independence in 2013 the country slipped back into civil war ignited by the political differences among SPLM leaders which led to a massive displacement and human rights violations across the country. Several people crossed to the neighbouring countries in search for refuge as refugees or asylum seekers. Those who remained in the country under UN and partners protection as internally displaced persons (IDPs) are heavily depending on humanitarian assistance. The 2019 flooding which engulfed mostly Upper Nile and some parts in Equatoria and Bahr el Ghazal regions had devastating effect on local people and internally displaced persons (IDPs) with the larger margin in Panyijiar county of Unity State. Apart from indigenous community of Panyijiar, the county is hosting internally displaced persons (IDPs) from neighbouring counties such as Leer and Koch as the result of 2013 and 2016 conflicts. These people have minimal access to humanitarian and government assistances given the geographical inaccessibility and present of few humanitarian agencies with limited resources. The flooding has come with several disadvantages including the overrunning of the farming lands, crops destruction and failure, waterborne diseases, snake bites and people are confined to only limited highlands which attract domestic and gender based violence (GBV) which warrant protection monitoring and humanitarian assistance. Moreover, as the Sudanese conflict erupted and intensified earlier this year, the already fragile humanitarian situation in South Sudan is worsened with the continuous influx of South Sudanese returnees and Sudanese refugees into the territories of South Sudan through the northern corridors of Upper Nile, Unity and Northern Bahr el Ghazal respectively. In accordance to Medecins Sans Frontieres (MSF), between April to November 2023, a population of 240 thousand South Sudanese returnees and Sudanese refugees entered into territories of South Sudan as the result of Sudanese conflict and are in dare need of both humanitarian and protection services. The vulnerable groups such as unaccompanied children, lactating mothers, persons with disability, elderly persons and women are facing challenges related to first aids, food and safe-drinking water, shelters, transportation facilities to their final destinations, families tracing with possible reunion, gender based violence (GBV) and high chances of human-trafficking. In reference to the above chronological events and their devastating effects ranging from but, not limited to object hunger, diseases, children rights violation, Gender Based Violence (GBV), support-less elderly and persons with disabilities, trauma and psychological disorder inherited from the conflicts among others. GOAH in collaboration with partners and beneficiaries will embark on protection monitoring; devise protection messages and awareness raising; identified people with protection need; train returnees, refugees, IDPs and community leaders on protection; offer temporal in-kind and cash assistance on critical issue that require an immediate attention; coordinate with partner a safe referral for better services among others.

#1 GOAH through integrated approach will develop harmonized key messaging that is also informed by the findings from the protection monitoring analysis. That is to ensure the messages are consistent with the finding for purposes of identifying the most vulnerable people with specific needs. #2 GOAH will work with the affected population and groups such as elderly, women, IDPs, returnees, refugees and the host community and protection partners, develop key protection awareness raising messages and information on the availability of services. That is to enable the accuracy and access to the information by the beneficiaries and various stakeholders. #3 With our routine protection monitoring, GOAH will work with protection partners, develop and implement static or mobile activities targeted at identification and referral of persons with specific needs (PSNs), especially in emergency situations. #4 GOAH in collaboration with protection partners and communities of affected people will work to establish and strengthen or enhance existing community-based structures such as youth leaders, women groups, traditional leaders and implement activities that constitute alternatives to displacement enable persons of concern to live in dignity. #5 Working with protection partners, development and peace actors as well as authorities and existing community-based structures and implement activities designed to enhance community resilience, psychosocial support (PSS), prevention from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), peace-building activities and trainings through age, gender and diversity (AGD) approach.
