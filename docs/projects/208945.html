Project Summary

The health cluster seeks to reduce avoidable morbidity and mortality for conflict-affected people, with a strong emphasis on ensuring accountability to affected populations. In line with the cluster strategy, HDPO plans to provide quality basic health service focusing on integrated primary health care in 20 Primary health facilities across localities including support for the Stabilization Center in Talodi and Habila) and 10 in North Darfur (Dar As-Salam, Al Fasher, At Tina, Kebkabiya, Kernoi, Tawila, and Um Baru) and 10 in South Darfur (Beliel, Buram, Gereida, Um Dafoug in Darfur South, Um Bada in Khartoum, and then Beida, Ag Geneina, Jebel Moon in West Darfur as well as Adila and Yassin in East Darfur in areas with high numbers of conflict-affected households (including newly displaced persons, long-term IDPs, vulnerable host communities, and some returnees. HDPO will continue support to rural and hard-to-reach facilities in these localities through mobile clinics using experienced trained mobile teams.
The key health services will include reproductive Health ( EmONC and referrals), EPI, ANC, family planning, early warning and outbreak response, support and training for health staff, clinic running costs, provision of drugs, medical supplies and equipment, and support for the local Health Department, including the Health Information System management. HDPO will also provide community health through Care Groups, community health promotion, defaulter tracing for RH and OTP services, and the usher nutrition-sensitive programs like deworming, Vitamin A supplementation, and approaches to prevent and treat Moderate Acute malnutrition.
HDPO will ensure the quality of basic quality primary health care in all supported facilities, and HDPO will continue to closely coordinate through the State Ministry of Health (SMOH) in El Fashir, Nyaland Khartoum, and will also engage in ongoing State level coordination forums with other partners in each locality to ensure no overlap. HDPO provides a package of lifesaving primary healthcare services in all supported facilities. PHC services include curative consultation, provision and dispensing of essential drugs, managing injuries, EPI to increase the immunization rates in children and pregnant mothers, nutrition screening for children under 5, reproductive health services, disease surveillance, outbreak response, and health education. Lessons learnt from the previous projects tend to deduce that an integrated program works best, with linkages between health, RH, GBV, community health, and nutrition activities, especially at the community level and in defaulter tracing. Midwives play an especially critical role, in both distributing Clean Delivery Kits which encourage skilled deliveries, and identifying and referring SGBV cases through HDPO’s existing RH referral pathway, which allows for another development objective of the provision of essential health services project is to increase access to an essential package of health services in the Republic of Sudan. The project will also strengthen the capacity of PHC workers and community health workers to investigate and detect disease outbreaks in the target states at the local and state levels. HDPO will collaborate and coordinate with local and state development committees in identifying and mobilizing people for project intervention. At the local and state levels, the already established networks and links with the committees mentioned will be strengthened by HDPO in all stages of the project from designing, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

HDPO staffs will be responsible for ensuring that beneficiaries safely access their services, without causing harm, and for promoting meaningful access, accountability and participation of beneficiaries in the provision of health assistance. By mainstreaming protection into health programming, HDPO will maximise the positive impacts of health programs on people’s safety and dignity and supportaffected populations access and enjoy their rights.The key actions HDPO will engage in to ensure the protection mainstreaming will include:Prioritise safety & dignity and avoid causing harm (Ensure the location and access routes to health facilities and services are safe• Identify central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, straybullets, crossfire or violence.• Installing lights outside and on the access route to the facility.• Ensure that the health services are respectful and inclusive of cultural and religious practice.• Consider separate waiting areas for males and females• Have a proportionate number of female health staff. If female doctors/nurses are not available,consider a female doctor rotation between locations. In this case, women must be adequatelyinformed of which days a female doctor will be available.• Employ health staff with skills and experiences working with children and women.• Be aware of how power dynamics between a health staff and the patient can affect the patient’sresponse and attitude towards the staff/organisation and service provided.• Ensure that patient consultations and documentation are confidential and private) Promoting equitable and impartial access for all(Collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming.) ensuring accountability to affected populations, Strengthening participation and empowerment, Strengthening safe identification and referralWhile there i great uncertainty about the current conflict in Sudan, particularly in Khartoum and Darfur regions, there are several lessons for provision of essential health services strategies based on experiences from past. This is an important moment to bring in that learning and ensure that risk communication is evidence based and does not reinforce stigma or discrimination. HDPO will expand the consideration of who is at risk, while focusing on targeted community engagement. This project will focus on health promotion-based strategies and work with community-based leaders and organisations with histories of outreach and engagement in Darfur. Since health resources are limited, community participation will be encouraged during the implementation of this project conflict or remote areas like in IDPs and host communities. Here effectiveness of community-based outreach program will be designed to improve the preventive health services in different localities in the Khartoum, North, South, West and East States who lack health services.

Through community participatory feedback sessions and gender, disability, and social factor analysis conducted by our project staff (health, nutrition, Gender, and protection) in collaboration with other key sectors ( WASH, FSL, nutrition, Social Protection/Protections, Education) on monthly, quarterly, bi-annually and annual basis to assess the perception, acceptability, knowledge and practice changes towards positive mindset for improved health. This will be done through our activities by Face to face with individuals (in homes) during home visits in defaulter tracing and active case findings, Face to face with individuals (in other venues/locations) at health facility, schools, Face to face with group consultations Face to face with community leaders, Billboards, Flyers, Complaints/suggestions box, Help desks/kiosk, Project will not share information.The prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) policy and course is mandatory for all HDPO staffs and forms part of HR policy hence not negotiable to all staffs. HDPO will ensure all the staffs adhere to the PSEA key principles of knowing that mistaken belief regarding the age of a child is not a defense, exchange of money, employment, goods, or services for sex, including sexual favors or other forms of humiliating, degrading or exploitative behavior is prohibited. AAP is an active commitment HDPO staffs to use power responsibly to take account of, give account to, and be held to account by the people we serve and all HDPO staffs adhere to the five commitments of leadership, transparency, feedback and complaints, participation, and design, monitoring and evaluation.
