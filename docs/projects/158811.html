Project Summary

The project meets strategic and cluster objectives:
Strategic objective 02: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.
HEALTH AND NUTRITION CLUSTER OBJECTIVE 1: Ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to the most vulnerable communities in Gaza and West Bank/EJ, including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building.
Comparing the HNO of 2020 and 2019; it was estimated in the 2019 document that 10,000 neonates are at risk, while in the 2020 document, it is estimated that 15,000 neonates will be at risk. A recent study also showed that NMR is 16.1 per 1000 which is high (2018, more under the needs assessment). This is a clear indication of how vulnerable this specific group is to the deterioration in health in Gaza due to the many factors such as lack of equipment, lack of drugs, disposables and infection control supplies, and the lack of a trained cadre to take care of the urgent needs of these neonates.
This intervention will address the needs of this vulnerable group in coordination with the efforts from other partners such as UNICEF, WHO and QRCS. The efforts will not be duplicated as MAP will coordinate all its efforts through the Gaza Neonatal Network, and as it has some unique activities such as the NLS, NST, RoP screening, Sepsis surveillance data and other activities that will complement the efforts of others in the field for the benefit of the neonates in Gaza.
In general, this project will contribute to addressing the life-saving needs for high risk babies at neonatal units in the Gaza Strip and improving neonatal care management in order to contribute to reducing mortality, morbidity and long-term disability among newborns.
The project’s main two objectives are:
1- Saving neonatal lives by ensuring the availability of life saving items and essential infection control materials at the neonatal units. MAP aims to achieve the project’s objectives in coordination with the Ministry of Health, UNFPA, UNICEF, WHO, and other stakeholders.
2- Improving the skills and competencies of staff at the neonatal units on neonatal life support, neonatal safe transport, and infection control.
The above aims will be met through:
Provision of urgently needed equipment for the NICUS.
Provision of urgently needed drugs and disposables for the NICUs.
Provision of urgently needed infection control supplies for the NICUs.
Provision of advanced unique training courses to MAP that are essential to the care of neonates such as: NLS, NST, RoP, infection control workshops and sepsis surveillance.

1 - Needs

There are 1.94 million people of vulnerable groups across Gaza in which 900,000 people need humanitarian health interventions, 54 % of them are female and children (HNO 2019). In Gaza, neonates and children under the age of five remain vulnerable, with 15,000 neonates in need of transfer to nursery and neonatal intensive care units for specialized life-saving treatment. These specialized units face shortages in equipment, drugs, disposables, and staff, contributing to the stagnant neonatal mortality rate, which is currently at 16.1 per 1,000, even more than 2006 levels of 12.1 (Maartje M. van den Berg et al, 2018). MoH recent zero stock report indicates that average shortage of drugs related to maternal and child health are 69% while shortages in medical consumables in general are 28%. According to WHO, three quarters of all newborn deaths occur in the first week of life, moreover, dangerous overcrowding in neonatal intensive care units is threatening the lives of Gazan’s most vulnerable babies, worsening maternal nutrition, rates of premature and low-birth weight babies have increased the number of babies requiring intensive care which is the second cause of death among neonates in Gaza. MAP also found from the annual data we collect throughout 2019 that death rate due to neonatal sepsis reached 21.8% which suggests there is a need to continue the provision of supplies of infection control materials and training (MAP, 2019). Therefore, implementation and the possible expansion of the neonatal projects is very important with the other stakeholders, that are not too many and MAP is considered one of the most prominent INGOs with very responsive approach to cover the these specific shortages. Without MAP’s support, there will be huge gap that simply could not be filled by others and a huge number of babies will be at risk. For the last few years and with the support of MAP, the neonatal units were regularly assessed and visited and found to be lacking the essential medicines, disposables, equipment, infection control materials and staff with updated skills to provide good quality and safe neonatal care. Neonatal morbidities and mortalities are considered as serious problems that mostly result in dramatic complications with heavy medical, social and economic burden on individuals, families, communities and government. MAP has been intervening in this area since 2008 and continued to provide NLS/NST, ROP early detection and prevention and infection control on-job training courses and workshops to health professionals and provided lifesaving drugs, neonatal equipment, infection control supplies to respond to newborn’s needs who were critically ill, the need to continue and expand this support is critical. It should also be highlighted that special attention should be given to newborns as a component within the frame of continuum of care.

2 - Activities

In coordination with MoH, UNICEF, UNFPA, WHO and QRCS, and all related parties, procurement of life saving neonatal drugs and consumables to be used for 15,000 at high risk new born babies/year including female and male babies admitted to neonatal ICU to benefit from the neonatal care project’s interventions. In coordination with MoH, UNFPA, WHO and UNICEF, procurement of infection control supplies and educational materials to be used for 15,000 high risk new born babies/year including female and male babies admitted to NICU and who will benefit from the neonatal care project’s interventions. In coordination with MoH and NGOs, procure necessary equipment that is urgently needed to address the over-crowding in NIUCs. In coordination with MoH and NGOs, conduct three NLS and three Infection Control training courses for neonatal and obstetric department for 200 health professionals. 114 female and 86 male health professionals working at the neonatal and obstetric units of MoH will be targeted. In coordination with the aforementioned parities, conduct three on job NLS training courses, and procure the needed equipment for the courses. Plus all the emergency services that we will provide will contribute to the reduction of disabilities in neonats as these babies are endangered and subject to death or develop long-term disabilities. With regards to gender, the project will target male and female neonatas but for the training, more emphasis will be given to female health practitioners as they form the majority of the workers at NICUs, plus, the local trainers’ cadre that MAP has invested in, who hep the consultant in provision of all course, are a mixture of both genders to address cultural sensitivities.

In fact, MAP cancelled an agreement with a donor late 2018 due to their lack of respect to the protection rules that MAP has to enhance protection for the babies. As for M&E, MAP is the only organisation, with MoH, that collects monthly sepsis data, and monthly ROP screening data. In addition, monthly, we visit the 6 NICUs to monitor infection levels, and also to collect feedback forms and technical sheets that MAP prints and distributes at the 6 NICUS. Let alone the fact that we have per-post test results to monitor training and that we are a lead organisation in the special body, Gaza Neonatal Network under which activities are always discussed and coordinated.
