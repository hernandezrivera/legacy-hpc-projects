Project Summary

South Sudan remains one of the most food-insecure countries in the world. Multiple factors, including natural disasters, dry spells, communal conflict, internal displacement, and refugee influx, cause acute food insecurity. Intersectoral findings stated that 8.5 million people (including refugees) will be in need in 2024. The FSN Monitoring Survey (2023), estimates that 7.76 million people, or 63% of the total South Sudan population, will face IPC level phasing severe acute food insecurity (IPC phase level 3+) and need urgent action (Ref: HNO SS 2023). FEWS Net (https://fews.net/east-africa/south-sudan) reports that emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes remain widespread. The worst-affected areas in terms of food insecurity are the targeted areas of this project. As per the IPC analysis published on November 6, 2023, a total of 325,678 people in Tonj North, 93,819 in Wau, 143,601 in Kapoeta East, 67,064 in Kapoeta North, 72,736 in Yei, 52,184 in Kajo-keji, and 29,437 in Renk will face severe acute food insecurity (IPC 3+). If emergency assistance is not provided, all current crisis IPC levels will deteriorate significantly.
The project Enhancing Food Security and Resilient Livelihoods for Flood, Drought, and Conflict-affected Communities in Warrap State (Tonj North), Eastern Equatoria (Kaoeta East and Kapoeta North), Central Equatoria (Yei and Kajokeji), Western Bahr el Ghazal (Wau) and Upper Nile (Renk) aims at saving life and building resilient livelihood of 53,470 vulnerable people (6,951 Boys, 13,368 Girls, 14,437 men, and 18,751 women). The project will provide Emergency Food Assistance (CO1): unconditional food assistance – in kind or cash, CFW; Livelihoods Support (CO2): distribution of main season agricultural kits, distribution of dry season agricultural kits, livestock treatment, and vaccination, training on farming and homestead-based food production; and Resilience Building Support (CO3): individual and group capacity building, strengthening group approach (FFS, GSLA, etc.) asset building, market linkage development. These interventions will contribute to improving food and livelihood security.
The project will provide food security and livelihood assistance to prevent acute and chronic food insecurity and improve food consumption, dietary diversity, and coping strategies by employing accountability to the affected population (AAP) and core humanitarian standards (CHS). IRSS will monitor project progress, conduct a midterm review, and take corrective actions as needed. IRSS will also ensure project indicators that are on track and lessons learned are drawn.

1. Needs

According to the IPC acute food insecurity analysis report released on 6 November 2023, the acute food insecurity in South Sudan remains worrying with the latest data showing that 5.79 million people (45.8 percent of the population) are projected to face IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse), including 25,000 people likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) and 1.71 million people likely to be in Phase 4. In the lean season projection period of April to July 2024, the food security situation will deteriorate and an estimated 7.1 million people (56.3 percent of the population) will be severely food insecure (IPC Phase 3 or above). 79,000 people are likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) while an estimated 2.34 million people are likely to be in Phase 4 (Emergency). The most food-insecure populations are in locations affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation and high food prices), conflict, and insecurity - including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan, causing forced displacement, and low agricultural production. Food insecurity is highest when shocks and stressors are compounded by persistent vulnerability and poverty associated with the protracted crisis. This situation is further exacerbated by progressive funding reductions against increasing humanitarian needs, which has forced agencies to reduce entitlements and prioritize the most vulnerable households. Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster (FSLC) estimated that the current situation will deteriorate significantly if assistance is not provided. Considering the reduction of funding FSLC targeted 3,882,500 people needed for humanitarian response in 2024. As per the IPC projection, during the period of December 2023 to March 2024, a total of 260,355 people in Tonj North, 63,486 in Wau, 96,113 in Kapoeta East, 41,176 in Kapoeta North, 54,841 in Yei, 44,973 in Kajo-keji, and 26,976 in Renk will face severe acute food insecurity (IPC 3+). During the period of April to July 2023 the food security situation will worsen and 325,678 people in Tonj North, 93,819 in Wau, 143,601 in Kapoeta East, 67,064 in Kapoeta North, 72,736 in Yei, 52,184 in Kajo-keji, and 29,437 in Renk will face severe acute food insecurity (IPC 3+). According to IRW independent assessment conducted in October 2023 indicated that the situation will worsen as other partners' support has significantly reduced due to funding reduction. The market functionality is a big challenge in the proposed project area due to the poor road network. The market is very far with 58.2% spending more than one hour to reach the next market. In the proposed action area high levels of food insecurity, violence, and cattle raids affected vulnerable people. The violence severely impacted response operations in the area. People’s homes and humanitarian facilities were looted and burnt down, this included warehouses of relief commodities and vehicles. The restriction in movement by security forces affects access to farmland by the civil population and compromises on delivery of humanitarian services. All these have contributed negatively to the IPC levels as indicated in the paragraph above. Therefore, IRSS shall contribute to addressing the needs of 53,470 food-insecure vulnerable populations through FSL cluster objectives in Tonj North, Wau, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North, Yei, Kajo-keji and Renk Counties.

2. Project activities

Given the current level of food insecurity and projected trends in 2024, IRSS proposes to provide lifesaving support and safeguard the livelihoods of the most vulnerable in the target areas. In line with South Sudan’s FSLC HRP strategy, this project aims to enhance food and livelihood security for community members through diverse interventions including resilient agriculture among vulnerable households. There are several proposed activities designed to support the capacity of target groups to meet their basic needs, reduce vulnerability, and sustain livelihoods and dignity. The proposed interventions include: under FSLC objective 1 i.e. Emergency Food Security Assistance: i) Food assistance (in-kind): 5,400 vulnerable people will benefit from the unconditional food assistance (in-kind) during the lean season covering three months of food needs to prevent famine and improve food consumption and dietary diversity. The food packages will be in line with Sphere Standards and minimum expenditure basket and based on FSL cluster recommendations for a six-member household. ii) Unconditional cash distribution: 9,000 vulnerable people will benefit from the distribution of unconditional cash assistance during the lean season covering three months of food needs to prevent famine and improve food consumption and dietary diversity; iii) Conditional cash distribution: 3,600 vulnerable people will benefit from the distribution of conditional cash distribution: community asset building; Under the FSLC objective 2 i.e. Livelihood support to strengthen and sustain emergency food production: iv) Distribution of agricultural kits: 3,000 vulnerable households will be provided with inputs necessary to engage in crop and vegetable production, including the distribution of livelihood kits, crop seeds, and quick-maturing vegetable seeds. All kits are based on FAO and cluster recommendations, including tested drought-resistant and improved varieties. v) Distribution of fishing kits: 300 vulnerable fishing community households who live along the river Nile and river Kaya will be provided with fishing kits to recover and engage in fishing. vi) Livestock treatment and vaccination: 2,600 households will be supported through livestock treatment and vaccination of around 60,000 livestock. Under the FSLC objective 3 i.e. Resilience Building: vii) Training of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs): 40 incentivized CAHWs will be trained and provided start-up vet kits to carry on livestock treatment and vaccination campaigns. This will help to address the issues of animal health and prevent zoonotic diseases. viii) Training of Community Agricultural Extension Workers (CAEWs): 30 CAEWS will be trained to carry on extension activities. ix) Develop demonstration gardens for good agronomic practice. x) Capacity-building training on Gap and resilient agronomic practice: 3,000 beneficiaries will be given training in resilient techniques of food production. xi) Establishing FFS: 30 FFS to promote good agronomic and resilient techniques of food production for the livelihood beneficiaries and establish market linkage. xii) Establishing GSLA: 30 GSLA: to enhance financial capacity and undertake market linkage.
