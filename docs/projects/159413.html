Project Summary

This proposal is in-line with the objectives and framework of the OPTI country HRP strategy under strategic Objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL. Further, this project is also aligned with the WASH cluster HRP objectives as follows: (1) Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient WASH services in accordance with dignity of the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank, (2) Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank to reduce threats and to cope with emergencies and shocks.
The specific objectives include of the project are: (1) To provide an appropriate, integrated WASH response for vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in fragile and critically un-serve and under-served communities by improving sanitation and sewage networks at the community and HHs levels. (2) To iImprove access to safe drinking water and enhance safe water handling at the HH level in targeted communities. The objectives are in line with the needs identified in the 2020 HNO and Oxfam’s recent assessments.

1 - Needs

The needs assessment is premised on the OCHA bulletin (August 2019), the community vulnerability mapping study conducted by Islamic Relief in 2018, the WASH assessment at household (HH) level conducted by GVC in the Gaza Strip (July 2017) and the gender sensitive vulnerability risk assessment (VRA) conducted by Oxfam and partners in 2019 to address accessibility to safe water and adequate sanitation services and their impact on women and girls. The population in the Gaza Strip is negatively affected by ongoing deficits and needs in the WASH sector (HNO, 2020). This includes 815,270 women and 856,719 children who are exposed to public health risks associated with poor water quality, poor wastewater collection and treatment, lack of stormwater infrastructure and lack of proper hygiene practices. The electricity crisis is also seriously affecting access to WASH services with dire consequences and an increasing burden on the most vulnerable households. 96% of Gaza's coastal aquifer is below WHO standards for drinking water, mainly due to increased contamination risks as a result of extraction and sewage contamination. People living in overcrowded houses suffer from the consequences of harsh weather conditions due to inappropriate thermal insulation or good ventilation. The substandard housing units affect specifically the living conditions of people with disabilities and persons with chronic diseases.In September 2019, a coordination discussion took place with Oxfam, CMWU, PWA, ACF, GVC, and Save the Children in order to identify and prioritize interventions in the most vulnerable communities. Accordingly, Amer and Aslan communities in Bait Lahia have been identified to be targeted under this intervention by Oxfam. WASH needs were prioritized to improve adequate water and sanitation services including proper sewage water disposal through installing of sewage water connections. Oxfam and MA’AN conducted a rapid assessment in August 2019 (35 HH visits, 3 FGDs and 2 KII). The assessment revealed that 100 HHs in Amer and Aslan are not connected to sewage networks and consequently rely on unsafe sewage disposal systems including cesspits and septic tanks. The assessment highlighted the need to improve access to adequate water and sanitation services at HH level for 130 families. The assessment also found that the majority of the population of Aslan area has poor hygiene practices especially in terms of water handling which increase the risk of contamination. Furthermore, 26% of the surveyed people reported that they had at least one diarrheal case in the last two weeks noting that 55% of the cases are among children. Among these cases, 72% are assumed to be caused by contaminated water, while 20% considered swimming in polluted sea water as the main cause. Moreover, Oxfam’s assessment indicated that women are mostly affected by the lack of access to safe water and sanitation services, reporting high concerns about their children’s hygiene, health and safety. Women carry the responsibility of dumping/emptying cesspits during sewage flooding which overburden women’s household chore and put them at higher risks.On average, nearly 120 million litres of raw sewage and poorly treated effluent are discharged into the sea every day (OCHA bulletin, August 2019). In June 2019, Environment Quality Authority in Gaza, revealed that 64% of Gaza’s beaches were contaminated compared to 74% as recorded in April 2018. Even if the situation is slowly improving, the current pollution levels remain higher than the international environmental health standard which can lead to diseases. To date, there is still a lack of comprehensive seawater quality tests with international standards. Accurate mapping and monitoring of the coastal zone is vital, knowing that the poor environmental status of the coastal areas requires sustainable planning and management.

2 - Activities

This project will respond to needs of targeted male and female beneficiaries including children, elderly and people with disabilities. The rehabilitation and upgrading of sanitation facilities will contribute to specifically alleviate the burden on women and children. The complementary hygiene promotion, seawater pollution study and public awareness campaigns will enhance gender and age responsiveness by improving women’s knowledge and awareness. MA'AN will implement the project in coordination with Oxfam with joint responsibility for Monitoring and evaluation activities and stakeholder engagement. MA’AN was fully engaged in the project’s design, needs assessment, and community consultations. MA'AN works closely with vulnerable communities in diverse locations including farmers, women, children and youth and has extensive experience in WASH and access to sustainable and safe water, sanitation services in Gaza. Output 1.1: Rehabilitated & maintained WASH facilities for underserved targeted communities at the HH & community levels Activity 1.1.1: Install 1,600 meters of sewer lines and house sewage connections in Amer and Aslan communities. Target: 100 households (580 individuals, 293 males, 287 females) Activity 1.1.2: Rehabilitate/maintain HH WASH facilities for vulnerable and under-served families in Amer and Aslan communities. Target: 130 HHs (754 individuals, 381 males, 373 females) Output 2.1: Increased public awareness on public health risks. Activity 2.1.1: Carry out awareness raising sessions on Gender, safe water handling and hygiene promotion at the community levels in Amer and Aslan communities. Target: 900 HHs (5220 individuals, 2636 males, 2584 females) Sub activities:2.1.1.1 Carry out awareness raising activities on hygiene promotion and gender at HH level using the total behavioral change approach. 2.1.1.2 Raise hygiene awareness on safe water handling through community sessions, events to minimize water contamination and the incidence of diarrheal diseases identified through the need assessment. 2.1.1.3 Distribute hygiene kits including essential personal hygiene items and household cleaning materials to 450 HHs. (2610 individuals, 1318 males, 1292 females) 2.1.1.4 Carry out a public health awareness campaigns in Amer and Aslan communities. Activity 2.1.2: Carry out a study on seawater pollutionfor the north beach including awareness campaign on its health hazards. This study will enrich WASH sector information about the health impact of sea water pollution on Gaza’s population especially in the north. The results will support hygiene promotion actions to reduce the diarrheal diseases, identified through the needs assessment, through a comprehensive mass media campaign.Target: (200,000 individuals, 101,000 males, 99,000 females) Sub activities: 2.1.2.1 Carry out a mapping of coastal zone including classifying risks and safety zones. 2.1.2.2 Carry out a comprehensive assessment using WHO standards about the long-term impact of sewage on the sea water quality and outline measures to ensure the safety and attractiveness of the coastal zone. 2.1.2.3 Carry out a study about health risk hazards of swimming, fishing, or living in close proximity to high risk and polluted zones. 2.1.2.4 Develop technical guidelines for sea water pollution monitoring and control procedures.2.1.2.5 Carry out a mass media campaign on seawater pollution and public health risks.

3 - Indicators

Based on Oxfam’s Common global Approach to MEAL and Social Accountability (CAMSA) the project will integrate a comprehensive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) framework. The MEAL framework will promote gender sensitive, collaborative and effective, results-focused and accountable MEL and social accountability practices throughout the project implementation. Learning and feedback will also be key practices that will be an integral part of the MEAL framework and will be documented by Oxfam in close coordination with implementing partners. In case of emergency, Oxfam’s minimum standards for MEAL and gender in emergencies are applied. Gender-relevant needs, actions, and issues will be monitored during the project implementation ensuring gender sensitive project reviews and evaluations. Monitoring tools, data gathering analysis, and reporting will encompass qualitative and quantitative gender and age disaggregation and analysis. This will help in tracking performance against indicators. Monitoring tools such as baselines and endlines will be used. key Informative Interviews (KII), Stories of change, Focus group discussions (FGDs) and others will be included as relevant. In OPT, Oxfam developed a comprehensive Feedback, Complaint, and Response Mechanism (FCRM) under its Saving Lives Program (including under this project) and will be piloting a mobile case management tool to capture real-time data from the field through the use of Information Communication Technologies (ICT). The FCRM ensures access for women and men to submit their feedback with safety and privacy. Feedback may be received either online, verbally or through hotlines and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam’s MEAL Officer. Furthermore, complaints related to safeguarding, GBV, misconduct, or fraud, will be referred to Oxfam’s Country Director and safeguarding team and will be treated with confidentiality to ensure privacy. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection principles are considered in the project as part of the humanitarianprinciples governing Oxfam’s humanitarian interventions. Oxfam premised its projectdesign on the ‘do no harm approach’ ensuring that project activities do not present anyharm to the targeted population by facilitating access to the essential WASH needwhich would have put them at risk, had it not been available. Technical assessmentwill be conducted in a way that takes into consideration the safety, dignity andconfidentiality of the served population while also keeping the protection of data andhumanitarian actors uncompromised. Preserving dignity is essential to maintainingself-esteem and confidence, which is important to cope in stressful and potentiallyoverwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem andconfidence will reflect positively on and enhances the roles each member of thehousehold fulfills and will result in improved wellbeing of the family, especiallychildren as care receivers.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

On a community level, local community committees (with equitable gender and agerepresentation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement with targetedcommunities and beneficiaries throughout the project, thus enhancing communityengagement and reducing any potential protection risks at community level. Buildingthe capacities of targeted communities to improve their self-protection mechanismswill be ensured through conducting orientation sessions to inform targetedcommunities about services that will be provided as well as on safety measures andaccess modalities. Moreover, the project design has considered gender and protectionprinciples by emphasizing on the participation of women in the project’s committee aswell as further exploring women and people with disabilities needs. More over, Oxfamwill ensure safe exit for the project through providing more sustainable solutions forthe identified needs and the proposed intervention by following an inclusive approachwhich ensure the targeted women, men, PWDs and other vulnerable groups are part ofour interventions as the main strategic way of working to ensure sustainability.
