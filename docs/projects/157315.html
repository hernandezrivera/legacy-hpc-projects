Project Summary

The proposed project will create a platform where incidences of women and young girls rape cases will be reduced in the conflict affected states of South Darfur, East Darfur and Blue Nile. The community safety management systems and structures will be enhanced in order to reduce the prevalence of FGM and early marriages. Religious leaders will be engaged owing their wide influence in changing the community practices. Many harmful traditional practices will be handled by this new strategy of engaging religious leaders.
Child Protection risks that leads children to be separated will be responded to using the FTR services through multi-sector and Multi-Agency approach. To ensure the identity of children is well understood, the project will engage the department of Registration to provide birth registration to the deserving children. Awareness to the community and government on dangers of recruiting children by armed groups will continue and ensure no children fall victim for child soldiers. Sexual violence on girls and women regardless of its nature will be discouraged at all levels and addressed through the community safety nets while the weakened and overstretched social institutions will be strengthened to offer adequate services.
The project targets boys, Girls, men and women of all ages since protection is a lifesaving intervention to ensure beneficiaries living in the 9 localities of Blue Nile (3), East Darfur (4) and South Darfur (2) are living their lives without fear of attacks and at risk of exposure to violence. A new strategy of engaging religious leaders as the main audience of choice by WVS called Channels of Hope (CoH) will be employed. This strategy has been found to be very popular among WVI Programs especially in highly religious dominance communities.

Needs

Gender-Based Violence (GBV) is common among IDPs and refugees in Sudan. Throughout the lengthy conflict, the rape of women and young girls, often by multiple militiamen, has been reported at staggering rates. So too, the prevalence of female genital mutilation/cutting (FGM/C) was 87% among women aged 15-49 and 32% among girls aged 0-14 in 2014 in the country. Almost 38% of women were first married before age 18 (12% before age 15). There are also severe shortages of specialized response services (e.g. psychosocial support) for GBV survivors, which are unavailable in over 80% of affected localities. Girls and boys (who represent 60% of displaced people in camps), face numerous child protection risks including becoming separated from their families, lack of birth registration, recruitment by armed groups, sexual and physical violence, child labor, child exploitation, and risky coping mechanisms. The report published by the Secretary General on children and armed conflict (May 2018) outlined the following grave violations in Darfur: 24 documented cases of recruitment and use of children in armed groups, 94 verified incidents of killing and maiming children (116 boys, 30 girls), and 36 verified incidents of rape and other forms of sexual violence which affected 44 girls. Community safety nets in the country have been weakened and overstretched social institutions cannot provide adequate protection services for those affected (HNO, 2018).

Activities

-GBV awareness with communities and faith leaders using the Channels of Hope (CoH) model- Strengthening of referral pathways and psychosocial support for Boys, Girls, Women and Men survivors - Welfare and livelihood Support to Boys and Girls UASC. - Engagement with Ministry of Social Welfare and department of Registration to provide birth registration certificates fpr boys and girls- Advocacy against recruitment of Boys and girls by armed groups. - Campaign against Sexual and physical violence affecting boys, girls, men and women.- Campaign against Child labor, - Campaign against Child exploitation for boys and girls- Formation and support for CBCPN considering men and women- PSS through Multi-Purpose and Women and Girls Safe spaces for men, women, boys and girls.
