Project Summary

The proposed project will ensure headways or progress among the youth (ages 15-24) of the host communities of Ukhiya & Teknaf through enhanced life skills and skills training, in cooperation with NRC. Youth and adolescents from the host community will be supported with non-formal education focusing on life skills, and vocational training, under the education and livelihoods umbrella. The project will be a skills training program focusing on formal school dropouts for both men/boys and women/girls, with an opportunity to learn a new skill and start a business based on market needs. Specific activities will include:
• Establishing 50 centres for 2000 adolescent/youth, where they will get life skills and vocational training on IGA. Youth will come to these centres for two hours daily, five days in a week. Time of the sessions will be decided with the consultation of the youth, parents and stakeholders. Two Center Facilitators (CF) will be recruited for each of the centre. S/he will be trained on conducting life skill sessions. They will conduct regular sessions and coordinate all the centre-based activities. They will also receive regular in-service training throughout the course of the project.
• Six months skills training program, skills will include different life skills like self-esteem, problem solving, creativity, child rights, gender, reproductive health, child marriage, eve teasing, dowry, neglect and exploitation, trafficking, drug addiction and abuse, HIV and AIDS. When needed, basic literacy and numeracy will also be provided.
• Market Survey to study the desired trade of youth and adolescents and the saturation of the market in Ukhiya & Teknaf due to newly arrived refugees. The market survey should build on those already conducted by partners (and include collaboration with FSL) as well as include feedback from youth and adolescent focus groups. Youth will also contribute to conducting the assessment and selecting relevant activities.
• Vocational training and financial support on IGA like hygienic food preparation, small machine repairs, cell phone repairs, sewing, plant nurseries/gardening, computer skills, beauty parlour, etc. After the final training, based on the assessment youths will be provided 5 thousand taka grant to start the IGA.
The CF will arrange a community meeting to start the centres at the community. The CF will select the centre place and the youth with the consultation of the parents and local communities. Before starting the activities, the CF will prepare a baseline of the youth on life skill. The project will be managed by a Local Center Support Committee (CSC). The seven members CSC will include parents, the CF, representative(s) from the local government, community leaders and the youth.
Finally, this project will seek to enhance dialogue between the refugee and host community populations, as well as target particular social cohesion activities related to the environment. The youth and adolescents will be involved in regular recreational activities with the refugee focused on reforestation and environmental management. The youth and adolescents in this program will be community mobilizers spreading awareness on environmental management, but will also be responsible for beautification, tree planting, and gardening activities within the community, targeting a total of 25
schools that will have school gardens planted. Apart from social cohesion between refugee and host communities, the youth centres will also be a place where dialogue is encouraged between boys and girls, men and women ages 15-24. Some life skills courses will be taught jointly in order to encourage mutual understanding between genders, particularly on issues such as street harassment, the value of women's education, and issues related to dowry. However, keeping in line with cultural sensitivities, life skills activities will also be done separately as needed.

Needs

77% of the host communities of Ukhiya and Teknaf have been reported as hosting refugees, one of the most economically depressed regions of Bangladesh. Prior to the recent influx it hosted around 300,000 of what the government preferred to term as “undocumented Myanmar nationals” (UMN). Ukhiya is a sub-district of Cox’s Bazar district hosting over 900,000 Rohingya. In these communities, 178,000 host community children, adolescent and youth (3-24) have been identified in the HNO as very much vulnerable. This is due to illiteracy, smuggling of arms and drugs, regional fundamentalism, lack of awareness, lack of employment, and mental and physical disabilities. In Ukhiya, violence, early marriage, dowry, child labor, abuse, sexual harassment and exploitation against girls, women and children are reported as frequent. Ukhiya was identified as a sub-district with bellow average performance regarding “net attendance rate of primary education’. It is identified as worst regarding “proportion of population using an improved sanitation facility” indicator. School Attendance 46.5%, while it is 52.7% at the national level. Thus, is it evident that, Ukhiya is lagged behind in term of total literacy and school attendance rate. Communities of Ukhiya and Teknaf were the first responders to the humanitarian crisis of the Rohingya people. But, now a days, these host communities perceive the influxes negatively with the refugee population taking its toll on a number of areas of the lives and livelihoods of the host communities. Resentment is rising among the local Bangladesh community as a result of the increased demand on common services and water in particular and a price hike of essential goods and services. There is also concern that there are reduced job opportunities and the unskilled wage rate has decreased with additional labour available in the market. In general host communities do not consider themselves economically better off than the refugees and many (over 20%) are living below the poverty line. Fares of all transportation, including rickshaws, registered an increase by 100 to 200 percent. Thus, while the people of Bangladesh in other regions empathies with the hapless Rohingyas, and among them the compassionate and active try their best to raise funds for Rohingya relief, it is the people of Ukhiya and Teknaf who are bearing the brunt of the refugee flow. Youth of the host community are suffering from various challenges. From one research, it has been found that, the main barrier for girls accessing education is that the host communities can’t afford to send them to school. There is clear perception amongst the respondents (61%), from refugees and the host community, that more boys than girls are being subjected to trafficking risks and practices. In the host community and the host community population groups indicated the highest rates of children engaging in paid and unpaid work (93% and 85% respectively). Moreover, children, adolescents and women in both the Rohingya and host communities are exposed to high levels of violence, abuse and exploitation including sexual harassment, child labour and child marriage and are at high risk of being trafficked. Considering the above-mentioned situation, to ensure an effective and much-needed harmony among the Rohingya and host communities, COAST will endeavor to promote youth empowerment which can be one iconic effort to promote synchronization among these two communities. In one hand, it will ensure youth empowerment and as well as it will help in establishing the interaction among these two communities.
