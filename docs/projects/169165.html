Project Summary

RI will provide multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) to 2,000 vulnerable households (approximately 10,000 individuals) affected by the explosion in Beirut over a three-month period. This time period will allow households to meet immediate needs following the explosion, but also acknowledges the ongoing and overlapping COVID-19 and economic emergencies which will be exacerbated. Vulnerable households have been identified by the Ministry of Social Affairs (MOSA) and other NGOs (both local and international), and will be verified by RI ahead of project implementation using an economic vulnerability assessment tool. Each household will receive four emergency transfers of 400,000 LBP (USD 102.57), over the course of the project, in line with Basic Assistance Sector guidelines and MOSA requirements. This transfer value recently increased from 260,000 LBP to account for rapid inflation and exacerbated need in light of COVID-19 restrictions.

1 - Needs

Since September 2019, the Lebanese Lira has lost 80% of its value against the dollar and the price of basic items such as food and shelter has risen by 169%.1 An estimated one-third of all Lebanese are unemployed, approximately 910,000 people in greater Beirut alone do not have enough money for food.2 Restrictions imposed to prevent the spread of COVID-19 have further exacerbated this, and a World Food Program survey found that two-thirds of households in Lebanon have suffered a loss of income due to the pandemic.3The explosion, which sent shockwaves through the city, causing damage as far as 10 km from the explosion site, will exacerbate the economic crisis. Thousands of homes and businesses have been destroyed, leaving up to 300,000 people homeless and countless without any source of income. Lebanon’s government holds no strategic stockpile of grain and the explosion destroyed the privately held stocks at its only large silo, requiring families to rely on smaller facilities for their wheat purchases. Beirut’s port sustained catastrophic damage during the explosion, through which Lebanon receives 80% of its imports, and are now out of service. This will likely push the price of available food even higher, worsening widespread food insecurity.Given the economic state of the country, any recovery will likely be slow and many households will be reliant on humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs including food, health, and shelter. Multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) will allow households to prioritize their immediate needs as medium and longer-term solutions are put in place.
