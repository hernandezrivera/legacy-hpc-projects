Project Summary

This project will improve the livelihood and resilience of food insecure and crisis affected populations by providing gender sensitive lifesaving and livelihoods assistance in our project operation locations. Particularly, the aim of this project is to alleviate hunger and destitution among the communities that we serve. Plan international will implement the following activities to achieve this goal, including cash based interventions, distribution of livelihoods inputs and youth economic empowerment activities such as skills training, entrepreneurship development and access to finance. This project will be implemented in some counties of the six state that Plan International has presence including Lakes, Central Equatoria, Eastern Equatoria, Western Equatoria, Upper Nile and Jonglei.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is undergoing a serious political, economic, social and environmental crisis. These multiple factors have caused serious economic, social and psychological suffering through loss of assets, displacement, deaths, lack of proper health and education services. According to the latest IPC report (Published on November 3, 2022), about 6.6 million people, or over half of South Sudan’s population (54%), are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity, classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse between October and November. All these are compounded by high poverty levels where four out of five south Sudanese live below the international Poverty line caused by many years of war that led to deprivation, marginalization and neglect. Due to all these factors the vulnerability of many people has increased and therefore their coping strategies have shifted from normal to abnormal coping strategies, thus affecting their resilience to most of the shocks and stresses. The shocks and stresses have affected the populations differently. Children, women, the youth, the disabled, people living with HIV/AIDS and the elderly have been worse affected. dependence on subsistence, rain fed agriculture exposes farmers in Upper Nile, Jonglei, Eastern Equatoria, Central Equatoria, Western Equatoria and Lakes States to livelihood consequences of climate change, especially delayed/erratic rain, drought, soil degradation and flooding. Poor access to inputs, training and financial services particularly for women contributes to low production. In 2022 in the States (lakes, Jonglei, Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria, Western Equatoria and Central Equatoria), faced threat of climate, economic, health and conflict related shocks, including delayed rains, prolonged dry spell and worse floods in Jonglei that affected the crop yield leading to hike in food prices. violent conflict drove widespread displacement, blocked farmers from crop-fields, and scorched farms. Cattle raiding in Greater Kapoeta, Jonglei and Lakes states have led to loss of livelihoods assets. The slow implementation of the revitalized peace agreement has led to increased insecurity in some parts of the country and therefore, continued people displacement, loss of assets, disruption of livelihoods including market and adaption of destructive copping strategies such as cutting trees for firewood and charcoal, child labor, abandonment of children. (IPC April 2022 projection report). In order to prevent a dramatic deterioration of food insecurity, malnutrition and associated social crisis and child protection risks, the need to provide timely and appropriate recovery and resilience building humanitarian and livelihood assistance in 2023 is more needed and important than ever. Based on our previous experience in programming in areas of our operation informed by the various assessments and review meetings done with various stakeholders plus discussions with communities, other NGOs and local government, the following needs have been identified: 1. The immediate need is to continue providing safe access to food through direct food distribution through in-kind and/or Cash/Voucher to the most vulnerable households. 2. Getting support to protect and rehabilitate their livelihood assets through provision of Livelihoods inputs to enhance their capacities to cope with recurring shock and reduce their reliance on food aid. Further to that, other needs that come up include creation of community assets through cash for work projects 3. Communities and youth need to be food secure and build sustainable livelihoods through youth economic empowerment initiatives such as skills training for the youth, formation of SACCOs and VSLAs, business start-ups and creation of access to loan services.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

• Plan International have conducted several market assessments and PDMs in Malakal, Rumbek and Kapoeta in 2022 to determine the feasibility of cash-based intervention in the proposed locations. the assessment and PDM shows how feasible the interventions are and how the communities prefer them.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The areas that cash based interventions will be used have functional markets. These are places that Plan International have done similar interventions in the past and have been successful. Cash is preferred by most beneficiaries in areas with market because it give them choice. the snowball effect of CVA in benefiting indirectly the whole community, while in kind benefits only receivers.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Beneficiaries will be consulted on their preferred feedback mechanism. Based on their preference the project will utilize it and in addition, community leaders will assist in reaching Plan International on behalf of their beneficiaries and AAP committees in the project location will collect feedback from community members and relay it to plan staff

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During the project design, consultative meetings in the community will be carried out through mobilization meeting to create awareness. Plan International has a success feedback mechanism strategy in place that includes help desk, suggestion box and hotline that is used to get feedback .

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Plan International has dedicated staff in our project locations to ensure that issues on PSEA are monitored, reported and addressed. in addition to the dedicated staff, all staff joining the organization are taken through PSEA training and orientation

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback collected from beneficiaries will be shared with relevant managers that will follow up and decide on the specific issues that emerge from the beneficiaries feedback. Actions that need to be taken will be taken and the the response taken is communicated to beneficiaries. the feedback could result to change for instance in distribution arrangements, information provided, items distributed, training schedules/contents and trainers.
