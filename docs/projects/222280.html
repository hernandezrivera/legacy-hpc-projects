Project Summary

Despite of comprehensive peace agreement signed in Khartoum, South Sudan has been afflicted by ongoing conflict, emergent crises, and harsh environmental conditions notably seasonal flooding. These have contributed to weakening of the health system over the years resulting in reduced access to and utilization of quality primary health care services. South Sudan has experienced some of the worst indicators globally including a maternal mortality ratio of 1123 per 100,000 live births and under 5 mortality rate of 98.8 deaths per 1000 live biths. Unity State, one of the most flood-affected regions in South Sudan, faces significant humanitarian challenges with over a population of 32,948 people in Panyijiar and 158,851 in Rubkona in need of humanitarian assistance.With the ongoing conflict in Sudan, South sudan has seen a significant number of returnees and refugees who all are in need of assistance. 1. Needs

The adverse effects of conflict, erratic weather patterns, lack of skilled and adequate personnel, issues with access continue to undermine efforts to attain acceptable standards in health service provision in Unity State. With a protraction of the humanitarian crisis, communities are stretched in their efforts to cope with emerging needs, resulting in increased morbidity and mortality. Since May 2020, heavy rainfall has affected parts of the country especially in Greater Unity state affected by flooding and the flooding continues until now disrupting livelihoods. This has had an impact on access to clean water and sanitation with resultant heightened risk of water borne diseases including acute watery diarrhea and cholera, exacerbating the acute food insecurity and continues to impact the humanitarian situation. The South Sudan IPC phase classification analysis for December 2024 –March 2025, placed Rubkona and Panyijiar among the 1.17 million people in emergency IPC phase 4, caused by cumulative effects of flooding, insecurity, Sudan crisis, inflation, limited health facility functionality and poor coverage. However, the situation in Unity state is expected to worsen during the lean season indicated in the second projection of April-July 2025, where 330,000 people are classified in IPC phase 4 and 10,000 People are classified under catastrophic IPC phase 5. According to the November 2024 Cholera outbreak situation report, 696 cases of cholera have been reported with 7 deaths and a CFR of 1.0%: Rubkona is among the counties experiencing this emergency having six reported cases, with 2 testing RDT positiveThese continuous and emergent crises have contributed to South Sudan’s poor health indicators including an immunization coverage rate for measles, PENTA and BCG at 76.1%, 69.3% and 71.4% respectively. Under five mortality rate is 98.8 per 1000 live births and a maternal mortality ratio of 1123 per 100,000 live births. Findings from the 2024 ISNA show that only 57 per cent of the population can access the nearest health facility in less than an hour, while 44 per cent cited long distance as a barrier to accessing health care. Thirty-eight per cent indicated that the reason their children were not vaccinated was due to lack of a health facility nearby. Women and girls in remote and crisis-affected areas facing additional challenges due to, limited trained personnel, recurrent supply chain breaks affecting family planning commodities and essential drugs for clinical delivery assistance and treatment of sexually transmitted infections.Sociocultural barriers have also limited uptake and utilisation of primary health care services.The revitalised agreement on the resolution of the conflict in the republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS) and the tension in Sudan has resulted in increased returnees population. This increase in affected population is likely to stretch the existing health systems in Payinjiar and Rubkona Counties. Coupled with the slow recession of the 2020 floodwaters, and the onset of the recurrence seasonal rainfall by september, the number of floods affected households in Panyijiar county increased spontaneously with an estimated 24626 affected households according the RRC of Panyijiar county in November.

2. Project activities

The AFAA will procure and distribute essential drugs, equipment, medical supplies to all target Primary health Care Units and the Mobile health clinics.This will also include costs for last mile distribution of the equipment and commodities. AFAA will also recruit essential health staff for the running of mobile health teams/PHCUs and PHCCs including (2 clinical officers, 7 nurses, 5 trained and skilled midwives, 5 dispensers,25 CHWs, 7 MCHWs, 5 card registers and 7 EPI vaccinators, 5 clerks, 3 medical waste operators) .These staff will provide basic essential health services including consultations, skilled deliveries, immunization, and provision of essential medicines to the beneficiaries as well as triage and also integrated WASH in the health facilities. The staff at the health facilities will be provided with the capacity to conduct nutritional assessments and screening in particular for children under 5 years. Children screened positive for moderate or severe acute malnutrition will be referred promptly to nutrition sites to be initiated on treatment. AFAA will provide capacity building of key essential staff on monitoring, prevention, detection and response to epidemic-prone diseases by conducting a training on IDSR and EWARS. Staff will also be trained by AFAA staff on screening for people living with disability (PWDs) using the Washington group set of short questions, on disability mainstreaming and inclusion, MHPSS and WHO mhGAP training will also be conducted to provide staff with essential skills in managing mental health disorders in treatment and psychological care. With a good institutional capacity in GBV, the AFAA will provide training to health workers on Clinical Management of Rape in Clinical Care of Sexual Assault Survivors (CCSAS). AFAA will also recruit Mental health and psychosocial support counselors and also emloy the Friendship Bench model in handling mental health challenges among the affected populations.Through existing conflict resolution mechanisms, Boma health committees and community leadership, AFAA will document and escalate any reported cases of attacks on health care workers. AFAA will support the referral of emergency medical and obstetric cases to the nearest or AFAA supported Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal care (BEmONC) and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal care (CEmONC) facilities for prompt responses as well as support referral of PWDs, including clients on treatment for mental health illnesses, people living with disability for further rehabilitation services. To improve the status of WASH in the health facilities, the project will support the rehabilitation of Rubbish Pit and incinerators while ensuring the availability of safe water for human. AFAA will also establish strong Community Feedback Mechanisms like holding regular community meetings to solicit feedback, establishing hotlines and putting suggestion boxes in place to foster responsive and inclusive programming that adapts to the evolving needs of affected populations.
