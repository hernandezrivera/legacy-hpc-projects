Project Summary

Takaful Al-Sham aims to support the food security of the most vulnerable IDPs and part of the host community in Idleb and Aleppo governorates,
Main interventions:
First: providing most vulnerable IDPs and HCs standard food baskets on monthly basis, targeting 8000 HHs 40000 individuals (8640 men -9776 women – 10560 boys -11024 girls ) in Idleb and Aleppo governorate, The distribution will be conducted at specified food distribution points (FDPs), safe FDPs will be chosen, away from the military centers with enough and safe distance and beneficiaries have easy access to them. For beneficiaries Who have difficulties to reach to distribution points such as families headed by child or Disabled persons, the baskets will be transferred to their location using light vehicle. The HHS who live in camps will receive baked bread in. the HHS which will receive the bread will be 1000, 5000individuals (men 1080 - women 1222 - boys 1320 - girls 1378).
Second: providing newly IDPs with Ready to Eat rations (RTE) which enable them to provide their families with required food for the first week of the displacement. This intervention targets 6000 HHs 30000 individuals (6480 men - 7332 women - 7920 boys 8268 girls ) The identification of these IDPs is the responsibility of the Takaful MEAL team, who track the movements of the IDPs, collect their data, and verify these data.
Third: Cooked Meals: In this intervention, Takaful Al-Sham will provide 6,000 cooked meals to 6,000 individuals (1296 men, 1466 women and 1653 girls 1584 boys), The new IDPs from the areas that have been displaced by the recent military operations will be targeted. The meal will contain the calories agreed to in the standards set by the Cluster.
Takaful will coordinate with the food security cluster and with other organization who works in the same sector and field to reach the right beneficiaries and avoid any duplication.
The project will ensure equal access for both genders with the priority for women, children headed household, disabled person, sick, PLW and elderly in addition the project staff will include female and male members trained on the protection principles and gender equality.

4 - Needs

Based on results from a countrywide household assessment, an estimated 7.9 million people in Syria are food insecure and an additional 1.9 million people are at risk of food insecurity. Food security exists when all people at all times have physical and economic access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food to meet their dietary needs and food preferences for an active and healthy life. Some 70 percent of the assessed households reported that food accounted for more than 65 percent of their expenditure. More than 37 percent of food insecure households reported having outstanding debt and 57 percent of food insecure people had adopted negative coping strategies. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Make effort to target a percentage of vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs 3. Establish feedback mechanism and conduct follow-up visits if needs be 4. Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process 5. Agree to minimum assistance package across sector 6. Assess and validate any changes before they occur 7. Identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions Assumption 2:the project will not cause sexual exploitation to beneficiaries, especially women and girl. Risk:Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. Probability:High Impact: High Mitigation strategies (MS):All of our staff sign on code of conduct in the same time of signing the work contract and Train staff on code of conduct in addition; we will provide each distribution point with compliant box in addition to other compliant mechanism Assumption 3:Select the beneficiaries of the most vulnerable in the project Risk: Beneficiaries are not chosen among the most vulnerable, and choosing them among non-deserving and are not the most vulnerable Probability: medium Impact: High Mitigation strategies (MS): A clear mechanism will be established to select the most vulnerable beneficiaries based on prioritizing during selection from the most vulnerable beneficiaries such as families headed by a child, a woman, a family with a pregnant or breastfeeding woman, a family with children under two years, and the presence of persons with disability

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1: The Information that the project built on it is true and neutrality. Risk: there is a risk of giving incorrect data to our team. Probability: Low Impact: High Mitigation strategies (MS): Our team will take in consideration IDPs tracking tables from CCCM and after that will crosscheck the data from local relief offices in the targeted location in addition to site visits to samples of supposed beneficiaries. Assumption 2: The access of all beneficiaries especially women and elders are secured. Risk: There is a risk that some selected beneficiaries has no access to distribution points. Probability: low Impact: Medium Mitigation strategies (MS): We will establish multiple distribution points to facilities the receiving of rations and reducing risks. Assumption 3: The activities are done according to planed timeline. Risk: Because of weather in winter may causes a delay in some activities. Probability: Medium Impact: Medium Mitigation strategies (MS): when designing work plan and the timeline we will consider the weather and its effect.Assumption 4: All roads are open and there is a full safe access to targeted location\sRisk: Road closures and lack of access to targeted locations. Probability: low Impact:High Mitigation strategies (MS): Delay the distribution for several days pending the calm situation - develop an alternative plan to deliver assistance to the beneficiaries in the new situation - ask suppliers to secure the material long enough.
