Project Summary

The project aims to provide comprehensive quality health care services to crisis-affected people, prevent and respond to health threats, and strengthen the health system. This goal aligns with the the 2024 HRP’s (Humanitarian Response Plan) objectives of saving lives, reducing health risks, and enhancing coping and recovery. The project will use a multi-faceted approach to address the problems being faced in the health system. The project will address challenges from 3 sides, that is the health service delivery, community, and individual components. Health service delivery: WR will work with the State Ministry of Health (SMOH) to support and capacity build the CHD in project leadership, program implementation, supervision, and monitoring. WR, together with the CHDs, will recruit qualified frontline staff to work in the HFs and they will receive regular supportive supervision, on the job training, and mentorship, including trainings on BEmONC, IMNCI, family planning, clinical management of rape (CMR), EPI, IPC, PMTCT, and drug and laboratory management. Through the UNICEF and WHO supply pipeline also project contingency funds the project will ensure that there is consistent supply of medicines and supplies. The project will create a conducive environment through rehabilitation of existing infrastructure and construction of new semi-permanent and permanent structures. Furthermore, the project will strengthen safe motherhood package by ensuring that ANC, labour and delivery, PNC services are available 24/7 in all PHCCs being provided by competent health workers. EPI services will be provided at both static and outreach sites. The project will implement and strengthen integrated mobile outreaches to ensure that health services are brought to the people and no one is left behind. To ensure effective continued and consistent communication between referral centers, referral pathways will be strengthened through the provision of ambulances and communication means in both counties. PHCCs will be equipped to provide PMTCT/life-long ART services as well as functional gender-based violence (GBV) referral pathways. The project will reach 353 904 U5s with HF and community consultations whilst 707 808 Vitamin A supplementation doses will be provided to U5s. In addition, 22 586 children under 1 (U1) will be reached with DPT3 vaccination and 20,328 U1s with measles vaccination. A total of 88,477 U5s will be screened for malnutrition and referred to Nutrition Centre. The project will also reach 31,620 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) with maternal, infant, and young child nutrition interventions and 141 159 women with safe family planning counselling. Community: With a focus on social behavior change, the project will intensify community awareness raising activities focusing on health service availability and utilization of health services. Various information dissemination methods will be used (IEC materials, radio channels, community meetings, etc.). Furthermore, the project will use promotional materials for ANC, Penta 3, PNC, and HF deliveries, motivating people to access health services. Promotional materials may include grocery hamper, t-shirt, and pads. The project will also train gender champions selected from the BHI and other community cadres to champion gender equality issues in the community, focusing on ending early child marriages and GBV as well as report GBV cases for timely management. Furthermore, community ownership will be created through engagement with key influential leaders so that they can lead in social behaviour change. Individual: The project will aim to build assertiveness of women and men to seek health services, through one on one engagement with community mentors. The Boma Health Committees will encourage equal gender representation to ensure equity in access of health services. To ensure comprehensiveness of services the project will be working WASH, Protection, Nutrition and FSL partners.

1. Needs

Fangak, Canal Pigi, Koch and Rubkona counties grapple with some of the worst health outcomes in South Sudan. After decades of conflict, the vast majority of the population lack access to essential health services, safe water, and sanitation. Current estimates indicate that South Sudan has a maternal mortality ratio of over 1,223 per 100,000 live births – one of the highest in the world (MMEIG, 2022). According to an ISNA survey 2022, approximately 112 under-five (U5) children per 1,000 live births die each year and at least 18.5% households reported losing a relative. Teenage pregnancy is estimated at 30% among girls 15-19 years, contraceptive prevalence rate for all methods is 6% (with modern methods at 5%); the unmet need for family planning estimated at 29.7% for 2020 (FP 2020). According to ISNA 2023, the main barriers to access and utilisation of care services were lack of medicines, staff and equipment transportation cost, insecurity, floods, long distance to HF and high cost of healthcare (mentioned by host communities, IDPs, and returnees). Only 49% of the population can access HFs in one hour’s time. While 66% of the population has difficulty in accessing health services, women and girls specifically faced additional barriers. Inadequate health workers, limited supplies, long waiting hours, water, maternal health services and lack of follow up care were reported. Malaria continues to be the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounting for 63% of all morbidities and 69% of all mortalities in 2023. All the 4 counties being targeted had malaria outbreak reported in 2023. Furthermore, Hepatitis E cases significantly rose to over 3,473 cases (26 deaths) and still remained above epidemic thresholds in 2023 (HNO 2023-Health). Fangak had a declaration of HEV outbreak in September 2023. Unity State is currently facing a problem of increasing influx of people fleeing war that erupted in the neighboring Sudan in April 2023; so far, 21,877 people have arrived in UnityState, exerting pressure on the fragile health system in the state. The same applies for the other counties, with the number of returnees and refugees increasing every month. According to the Nutrition and Mortality SMART Survey of 2022, Fangak County remains in an emergency food crisis situation and prevalence of GAM, MAM, and SAM were 17.5%, 14.1% and 3.3%, respectively. EPI coverage Fangak remains one of the lowest in the county, with an average of 53% and 27% coverage for Penta 3 (EPI Coverage Report, August 2023). The national prevalence study in 2022 found that violence against women is common in South Sudan with physical, sexual, psychological, economic violence, and forced marriage affecting many women. Furthermore, among the post-conflict populations depression rates are high. An IRNA done in September 2022 indicated an estimated 45,777 individuals in (12,472HHs) were in critical need of humanitarian assistance as a result of the flooding that affected areas of Koch town, Kuachlual, diel, and Bhang Payams. According to the report, there were increased cases of malaria and diarrheal diseases, Children 0-11 months not receiving routine EPI services, increase cases of snake bites, and pregnant mothers have to walk a longer distance through the water to access MCH services.

2. Project activities

1. Objective 1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services to crisis-affected populations. The project will create a conducive environment through rehabilitation of existing infrastructure and construction of new semi-permanent and permanent structures in Koch and Fangak Counties. Furthermore, the project will strengthen safe motherhood package by ensuring that ANC, labor and delivery, PNC services are available 24/7 in all PHCCs being provided by competent staff. Primary healthcare services will be through PHCUs and PHCCs including outpatient consultations, reproductive health services (ANC, Normal deliveries, PNC), EPI, and health education. Health facility staff will be trained on, IMCI, BEmONC, CMAM, CMR. Community Health Volunteers will be mobilized to provide health education in communities and refer people who are ill to health facilities for treatment. Health promotion messages will be generated from the weekly disease epidemiological trends of health facilities integrated with COVID-19 messages. Integrated community case management of Malaria, AWD, and Pneumonia will be implemented in Fangak County and Koch County. Referral of complicated cases to the nearest available secondary level of care. Provision of routine EPI services through static HFs and mobile outreach sites. Procurement of supplementary drugs, obtaining EHK, and RH kits from the core pipeline, and maintaining the uninterrupted pharmaceutical supply chain. Identification and referral of patients with mental health problems for treatment and psychosocial support. Clinical management of rape cases and referral for psychosocial support. Identification and referral of patients with disabilities to the nearest available service point. Community mobilization and Sensitization on SGBV. Training staff on infection prevention, control, and medical waste management. Provision of Hand-washing stations in the facility, consultation rooms, Maternity rooms, and waiting areas. Conduct an awareness campaign for community leaders and other stakeholders on family planning.2. Objective 2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases. This will be achieved through training of mobile outreach teams, health and nutrition staff on epidemic-prone diseases and outbreak surveillance. Case definitions for common communicable diseases will be provided to all healthcare workers for early identification and treatment. All data will be compiled and submitted into EWARS/DHIS 2 for analysis and decision making through the Health Cluster. Aberrations and risks of epidemics will be identified and controlled at the very lowest level through health promotion efforts. WRSS will participate in any outbreak response in coordination with stakeholders, other partners, and Health Cluster. The establishment of a waste management area at the facilities and implementation of standard precaution procedures for infection prevention and control.3. Objective 3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. All project activities will be implemented in collaboration and coordination with the existing Health Pool Fund or World Bank supported health activities in these locations. Coordination with CHD and other partners on the ground to avoid duplication and increase coverage within these locations. Quarterly Joint-monitoring and supportive supervision with the CHD to the health facilities will be conducted as well as monthly community consultative meetings to identify needs.
