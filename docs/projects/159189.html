Project Summary

The overall purpose of the project is to improve the protection environment and to improve the access to quality of protection services for displaced, conflict affected persons as well as returnees. The delivery priority will be to populations located along the 'contact line' and in non-Government controlled areas of Ukraine.

1 - Needs

UNHCR identified the needs of its persons of concern based on Participatory Assessments organized in both 2017 and 2019, as well as continuous feedback from those receiving the assistance through feedback mechanism. Desk review is also used.The protection situation is particularly dire for persons living within five kilometers of the contact line where ongoing armed conflict affects their daily lives. Among the population that did not flee this area, 31% are older persons, of whom 71% are female; these older women have nowhere to go and no one to help them. Their greatest concerns are security and basic needs. In areas within five km of the contact line, 86% of households report concern over the risk of shelling, compared to 29% in areas 5-20 km from the contact line (REACH survey). In the participatory assessment, residents spoke about the impact this protracted violence and isolation has had on their mental health. In 2018, UNHCR filled a critical gap in providing psychological support in locations close to the contact line to address this need. The small settlements and rural areas in this zone have lost their economic base, and recovery efforts are not underway there. Roads and public transportation are poor, creating challenges for the delivery of assistance, as well as access to services. In the non-government-controlled areas (NGCA), persons along the contact line face similar challenges relating to ongoing violence. Since the areas along the contact line in NGCA are more likely to be urban or suburban, the size of the affected population is larger. As an illustration, the Shelter Cluster estimates that 70% of shelter damages in 2018 occurred in NGCA; OHCHR found that in 2018, 78% of civilian casualties caused by shelling or small arms fire occurred in NGCA. Persons in NGCA report challenges with employment and meeting basic needs. They face legal difficulties in accessing pensions and social benefits, and obtaining identity and civil documentation, including birth certificates. A major concern is the situation at the five checkpoints with GCA, which register a million crossings per month. Waiting times remain long (over three hours) and dangerous, as the checkpoints are affected by shelling and mine contamination. While shelter and sanitary conditions have improved, the journey remains difficult, involving multiple bus connections and restrictions on the transfer of personal goods. The impact is serious, especially considering that over half the persons crossing the checkpoints are older persons who travel mainly for family reasons or to obtain access to their pensions. Compared to before the conflict, the number of persons residing in NGCA who receive their Ukrainian pensions has declined by 700,000. Three-quarters of IDPs living across other parts of Ukraine have no plans to return in the near future. In the participatory assessment, IDPs stated that their priority is to find durable housing solutions, since most spend a considerable portion of their incomes on housing and utilities. Employment levels have stagnated among IDPs (43% employed compared to 58% among general population). To facilitate local integration, IDPs advocate for their right to vote in all elections. They would like the right to participate fully in public affairs. They say they face discrimination in accessing pensions and certain administrative services.

2 - Activities & outcomes

1. Objective: Comprehensive Solutions strategy developed, strengthened or updated; Outcomes: strategic advocacy at national level and effective advocacy at local level conducted by UNHCR to facilitate durable solutions, integration of IDPs and establish conditions for voluntary return. UNHCR intends to conduct advocacy through training, exchange of experience with other countries, and expert policy advice. Advocacy also takes place at the local level to intervene on specific persons or settlements.2.Objective: Law and policy developed or strengthened; Outcomes: Advocacy conducted UNHCR will analyze legislative and policy developments and implement advocacy actions (e.g., participation in legislative working groups and parliamentary committees, high level meetings, roundtables, public information). 3. Objective: Protection from effects of armed conflict strengthened; Outcomes: Protection by presence provided; Outcomes: Situation of persons of concern monitored UNHCR and partners will continue protection monitoring across Ukraine with emphasis on the areas along the line of contact, as well as areas hosting large numbers of IDPs. Protection Monitoring will be conducted at community level through regular interaction with local institutions, community groups and affected populations. 4. Objective: Access to legal assistance and legal remedies improved; Outcomes: Capacity development supported for the FLAC; Outcomes: Legal assistance provided UNHCR will support the state-run Free Legal Aid Centers (FLAC) to develop their capacity in order to reduce legal assistance provided by NGOs with a gradual handover of IDP-related cases to FLACs in 2019. 5. Objective: Services for persons with specific needs strengthened; Outcomes: cash grants or in-kind assistance provided; support to persons with specific needs provided. UNHCR will target assistance to persons with specific needs, using a cash modality where appropriate. Outcome: provision of psychosocial support for those with psychosocial needs. 6. Objective: case management of SGBV survivors; outcomes: UNHCR will provide case management to SGBV survivors in full coordination with authorities and UNFPA in areas near the 'contact line'. 7. Objective: Participation of community in SGBV prevention and response enabled and sustained; Outcomes: UNHCR will undertake information awareness raising campaigns in the field of SGBV; 8. Objective: Peaceful co-existence with local communities promoted; Outcomes: Projects benefiting host and displaced communities implemented UNHCR will support small-scale local infrastructure projects designed to meet the needs of IDP and hosting communities, as well as to promote social cohesion. UNHCR will work with affected communities and local authorities to identify, implement and contribute to projects. 9. Objective: Community mobilization strengthened and expanded; Outcomes: Community groups supported UNHCR will create community support initiatives (CSI) that utilize and build on the experience, skills, and capacities of affected populations to protect themselves and promote the inclusion of IDPs in host communities. UNHCR has feedback/complaint mechanisms, but will strengthen them and ensure a proper AAP system is in place. 10.Objective: Coordination and partnerships strengthened; Outcomes: Co-ordination mechanisms established UNHCR will support the coordination of the Protection Cluster. UNHCR will also engage international development actors and financial institutions.
