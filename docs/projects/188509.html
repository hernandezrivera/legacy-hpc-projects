Project Summary

During the last ten years of the conflict in Syria and the great damage it caused at all levels, the families were the most affected by its results, as they created many social and health crises, especially the economic ones, where, according to OCHA reports, nearly 90% are below the poverty line.
As-Suwayda Governorate is located in the far south of Syria, with a population of approximately 425,000 people, according to the Syrian government’s statistics for the end of 2019, an average of 120,000 families who depend for their economy on agriculture and the travel of their children abroad who support their families. It is also characterized by its mountainous nature and its cold winter weather, especially in the high and mountainous areas where Winter temperatures drop dramatically below zero, and with the repercussions of the Syrian crisis, the governorate had a large part of it, as the agricultural sector suffered from many problems, from difficulty in securing water as a result of many wells being out of service, in addition to economic difficulties suffered by children traveling abroad, especially with The ban measures due to Corona, which greatly reduced the aid sent to their families.
And with the great suffering of families in the winter and the difficulty of securing fuel for heating and its high prices in the black markets, the inability to purchase, in addition to the deteriorating security situation in the province, which made it difficult for people to work.
The project aims to distribute mattresses and blankets to affected families, returnees and expatriates residing in mountainous areas, where it is scheduled to target 10,000 families in 2022 and 10,000 families in 2023 by distributing mattresses and blankets to them in the amount of five blankets and five mattresses for each family to help them in facing the difficult weather In the governorate, it reduces the spread of chronic diseases, especially among families with children, the elderly, or the disabled, and which have many members.
The population in the targeted villages is more than 150,000 people, approximately 40,000 families. We aim to target twenty-five percent of these families in 2022 and twenty-five percent in 2023.

4 - Needs

According to the HNO report, nearly four million nine hundred thousand people are in need of non-food support, including fifty-six percent of women and forty-four percent of men, as nearly 54 percent of the displaced live in affected areas. The displacement is currently more than six years old, knowing that the displaced are They are the highest renters currently reported in As-Suwayda. The percentage of need exceeds fifty thousand families. The population of As-Suwayda Governorate is 450 thousand people, with a number of 125 thousand families.Estimates indicate that there are six million nine hundred thousand displaced, of whom approximately three million and seven hundred thousand are residing in unsuitable shelters, and there are reports that 70% of them are unable to meet the basic needs, with the expectation that the need will rise with the deterioration of the economic situation further.Fifty-five percent of the returnees live in unfit shelter, and 80% of them are unable to provide for their needs, and the host communities suffer, approximately 30% of them are affected

5 - Implementation of activities

We will select the beneficiaries based on the targeting conditions in each region according to the need, which will determine the number of beneficiaries from each region based on this need. After receiving requests for those wishing to benefit from these materials and verifying that the criteria and conditions match them, the field survey teams will conduct field visits to verify From data compatibility, taking into account our monitoring and evaluation at all stages of implementation from data to beneficiaries until the end of the project according to periodic reports to avoid any obstacles and verify the application of eligibility conditions and gender ratiosThe distribution operations will take place through the distribution outlets of the center and in the remote villages, we will go there in order to reduce the burdens on the beneficiaries as much as possible, in coordination with the local community in order to ensure safe entry to these areas.Where we will coordinate with community figures in advance to ensure the greatest amount of cooperationWe will also take all preventive measures to combat the Corona virus, including spacing, continuous sterilization, and wearing masks for the supervisory staff and the beneficiaries.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Because of the customs and traditions in As-Suwayda Governorate, which may cause a number of restrictions imposed by the society according to age and gender factors (such as: lack of participation of women and girls in the project - lack of acceptance of the youth group - non-acceptance of assistance from them - non-acceptance of the idea of ​​support from humanitarian agencies)2- In addition to the cultural and religious sensitivities in the local community, which stem from the fact that a number of the targeted areas are the most conservative of these cultures.3- The inability to access understandable information about the available needs and services (whether by protection workers or by the beneficiaries) due to the reservations of many families about declaring their financial and social status because they are in a conservative society that depends on not showing their real needs ashamed of society.Work will be done to overcome these risks in coordination with the social figures in each region to ensure the safe entry of the teams according to the customs of each region, which ensures obtaining the desired results from the project and full cooperation from the communityThe implementation of this project secures the protection of the child as he is one of the targeted segments of the families, where the distribution of these non-food items will be positively reflected on the child by protecting him from cold weather and communicable diseases


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Gender-based violence has become a feature in Syrian society, which has caused many women to be deprived of their rights as a result of gender inequality, in addition to imposing many restrictions on women, in addition to physical and verbal violence and forced marriage, as many women suffer from insecurity in their communities as a result of these conditions in certain areas. This also resulted in inequality and denial of assistance provided, especially to widows and divorced women, as a result of social extremism.All these points were taken into account when developing the project, as the project will ensure equal service provision for both sexes and equitable distribution, especially in light of indications that 57% of women are deprived of this assistance.We will ensure that the distribution is equal and equitable according to the data received and in cooperation with the local community.Especially in light of the worsening economic situation, which may cause them to be further deprived of their rights.In order to ensure that the beneficiaries are not exposed to any blackmail or sexual harassment, we will clarify the complaint processes and mechanisms, emphasizing that a complaint is an acquired right for the beneficiaries while maintaining the confidentiality of the complainant, as the number of those subjected to violence is approximately 11 million people, and more than 92% of them are women and 7% are men This is a very high percentage that was taken into account


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risks that may hinder or delay the implementation of the project in As-Suwayda governorate are numerous, the first of which is the security situation in the governorate, which may cause the inability to reach the target places of the project due to road cuts, which can be overcome through permanent coordination with the local community and relevant government agencies, which ensures Continuous coordination of field project operationsSecondly, the increase in the spread of corona and the return of the government to taking preventive measures and restrictions in order to reduce gatherings, where we can avoid this matter by taking preventive measures such as sterilization of materials and spatial distancing of beneficiaries and taking preventive measures for the working staffThird, the delay in financial transfers to the project, which may delay the field operations of the teams and distribution operations. This can be bypassed in coordination with the suppliers, informing them of the new delivery date and paying the working staff from the center’s budget until the financial transfers arriveBad weather, which we may be exposed to during distribution operations, such as interruption of roads as a result of snow or ice, where work will be coordinated with the municipalities to find out the time needed to open roads and compress work schedules to suit the changes taking place
