Project Summary


The proposed intervention aims to improve the livelihood and food security of vulnerable farmers and their families and reduce reliance on humanitarian aid through increasing their productive capacity to increase their household income to enable them to meet their basic needs. The project is in line with the HRP’s strategic objective 3“ The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks” as it will be supporting small farmers and breeder to resume their normal livelihood activities and enhancing their income which is align with FSS’s Objective 1.
The project will support the livelihoods of small farmers and poultry breeders across the Gaza strip, where blockade, destruction of livelihood assets and environmental challenges exacerbate the risks of poverty and food insecurity
The project will directly enhance livelihood and food security of 330 small farmers and poultry breeders, at least 50 of which are women and 10 are persons with disability. However, the number of women beneficiaries is expected to increase since there are many women who run farms registered to their husbands or fathers. The project includes rehabilitation of 110 dunums of open farms for 55 farmers and 35 greenhouses, and equipping 80 small poultry farms and rehabilitate 8 agricultural wells and equip them with solar energy units to improve access to water for 160 farmers . SCI will implement the intervention in partnership with Beit Lahia Development Association (BLDA) in Gaza and North Gaza Governorates. SCI with BLDA will implement the project activities in 3 localities in North Gaza (Jabalya, Beit Lahiya, Beit Hanun) and 3 localities in Gaza (Joher El-Deek, Moghraka and Al-Ziaton). SCI will directly rehabilitate and equip 8 agricultural wells with solar energy units in North Gaza and Khanyounis, Partner will coordinate with the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) and the FSS to ensure there is no duplication, as well as ensure consistency and high quality of implementation across the different locations. To increase the project impact and ensure sustainability and to do no harm, the project activities will be implemented in close coordination with farmers and the local communities through existing Community Protection Committees (CPCs) to ensure that the intervention is aligned with the existing Community Protection Plan.
SCI and its partners will select the most vulnerable farmers based on a socioeconomic and technical assessment and a selection criteria in coordination with the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA), which will provide a list of potential beneficiaries. The project will then conduct socioeconomic assessment for the nominated farmers to verify the information shared and will then crosscheck the list of potential beneficiaries with the FSS to avoid duplication and confirm the need in the identified farms. To enable farmers to rehabilitate their greenhouses and open farms, SCI will provide farmers with Cash assistance .
The rehabilitation activities will be identified based on a detailed need assessment and rehabilitation plan that will be developed for each farm with the targeted farmers by SC and partner agronomists using the BBB (Build-Back Better) approach for increased resilience.
The partner will review the rehabilitation plan with MoA to ensure the quality of design. SC and partner will follow up on the implementation based on the agreed plan through conducting field visits. SC will distribute the cash assistance in 3 installments based on progress in each farm.
To support the income of small poultry breeders, SCI and its partners will equip poultry farms with solar energy units to reduce dependency on electricity for lighting and fans, especially during outages.

1 - Needs

The economy in Gaza continues to deteriorate as a result to the ongoing 15-year blockade, cyclical violence and destruction, restrictions on economic activity, limited market access and poor governance. With an employment rate of 47% - 65% of which are women -unemployment rates in Gaza are amongst the highest in the world . According to FSS HNO 2023, approximately 1.19 million people (1 out of 3 people) in Gaza are in need of humanitarian assistance. The MSNA conducted in 2022 indicated that 81% of households in Gaza reported challenges in meeting their basic needs and over 50% reported aid and assistance as their primary source of income. Families are forced to adopt negative coping mechanisms, such as reducing food intake, rationing or skipping meals altogether. Many are incurring in debt to be able to afford food. Female-headed households are among the most at risk of poverty and food insecurity; they spend 30% less on food than in male-headed households . In September 2022, SCI conducted a socioeconomic needs assessment for food assistance with 1,652 households across Gaza, nominated by the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD). Results showed that the monthly average income for surveyed households was 390 Israeli Shekel (ILS), which is far lower than the Minimum Expenditure Basket of 1,717 ILS indicated by the Cash Working Group. Increased food insecurity has also led to an increase in the number of malnutrition cases reported among the children; the community nutrition screening for children conducted via an ongoing SCI project in four localities in Rafah and Khan Younis in 2021-2022 revealed an 8.5% increase in malnourished amongst screened children in 2022. Agricultural livelihoods are crucial to improving the food security of vulnerable communities in Gaza, and are an essential source of income for households to meet others basic needs. However, environmental degradation, the increased costs of production inputs and poor productivity pose obstacles to any improvement in food security in Gaza. Border security tensions and the destruction of agricultural assets add new challenges to farmers. According to the MoA in Gaza Strip, the recent hostilities in August 2022 destroyed a variety of agricultural lands, greenhouses, livestock, and poultry farms, causing damages and losses estimated at one million USD. Vulnerable and low-income farmers are unable to afford the cost of repairing or upgrading their farms; their production is also affected by the deteriorating quality of soil and water salinity, and the diminishing availability of water. Additionally, recurrent electricity outages lead limit access to electricity needed to supply the energy needed for lighting and for operating cooling fans in poultry farms, shortage of water for irrigation, which negatively affects production and profitability. To have a better understanding of the specific challenges of farmers and poultry breeders, SCI and its local partners, BLDA and Bayader, conducted three focus group discussions (FGD) in August and September 2022 with 34 (14 women and 20 men) small poultry breeders in North Gaza, Khan Younis and Rafah. Participants stated that the main challenges they face are: limited access to electricity due to power outages, which may result in the death of poultry especially during the summer, and the inability of small breeders to purchase alternative energy systems. Furthermore, the FGDs indicated that many small farms needed rehabilitation to protect poultries from weather conditions. All participants agreed that solar energy would be the best solution for power outages, enabling breeders to operate fans and light farms throughout the night, while reducing operational costs and increasing productivity. Similarly, in August and September 2022, Bayader and BLDA conducted four FGDs with 44 farmers (18 women and 26 men) in Khan Younis and North Gaza. Participants stated that their livelihoods were suffering deeply from the blockade, the increased cost of agricultural inputs and poor quality and shortages in water for irrigation. The FGDs indicated a huge need to rehabilitate their farms to be able to resume normal agricultural activities and improve income generation to meet their families’ basic needs, including food. Female farmers had additional challenges as women face discrimination and social exclusion, which increases obstacles in managing farms and marketing the products, hence increasing their vulnerability to poverty and limits their ability to access opportunities to enhance their professional skills and their access to and/or control over productive assets. Although women's ownership of farms is limited, Women play key roles in farm activities such as harvesting crops and preparing them for market . In smaller farms, all members of families take part in activities to share workloads and increase the income. When a small-scale farmer has to find a temporary job elsewhere, the woman takes over full responsibility for the farm.

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1: Selection of beneficiaries: SCI and its partners will target the most vulnerable small-scale farmers, at least 15% of which are female-headed households. Beneficiaries will be selected based on a socioeconomic and technical assessment and selection criteria developed by SCI and its partners. The selection criteria will consider the farmer’s HHs income, HHs size, source of income, farmers by loss of agricultural productive assets as a result of escalations and poultry breeders by increase in agricultural inputs and shortages in electricity. Partners will conduct outreach sessions and communicate the objective and selection criteria with the target communities and will invite farmers to submit their applications. SCI and its partners will screen the applications in coordination with the MoA and the CPCs based on the selection criteria and through verification visits, which will prioritize female-headed farms, HHs with persons with disability and chronic diseases, as well as HHs with children under the age of five. HHs outreach visits will take place to identify beneficiaries. SCI will crosscheck the list of potential beneficiaries with FSS to avoid overlapping. Activity 1.2: Conduct technical assessments and develop rehabilitation and upgrading plan for the selected farms: SC and partner’s agronomists will conduct a socioeconomic and technical assessments to assess the needs of each selected farm (Green houses and open fields and poultry farms ) and will develop the rehabilitation plan (for each farm with farmers using the BBB (Build-Back Better) approach for increased resilience. The plan will include also BoQ of the required work and materials, pricing of the activities and required items based on current market prices and time plan for implementation based on the value of each installment. The project partner will review the rehabilitation plan with MoA to ensure the plan meets the required technical specification. Activity 1.3: Provision of cash assistance to support the rehabilitation of 35 greenhouses owned by 35 vulnerable farmers; SCI will support 35 farmers, at least 5 of which are women and farmers with disability, by providing cash assistance to support the rehabilitation of their greenhouses to plant vegetables, each farmer will receive 3,000 ILS in three installments to rehabilitate their greenhouse, based on the agreed rehabilitation plan (activity 1.2). SCI will manage the provision of cash assistance while partners will follow up on the implementation of rehabilitation activities through field visits. BLDA will follow up on the rehabilitation of 20 greenhouses in Gaza and 15 greenhouses in North Gaza. SCI will distribute the first cash installment (40%) after the farmers sign the contracts. The partner will conduct regular field visits to verify that the agreed rehabilitation activities are being implemented in line with the rehabilitation development plans, provide required technical support to farmers and review all financial documents and receipts. SC’s agronomist will conduct verification field visit to ensure the quality of implementation. Based on the results of field visits, SCI will disburse the second installment (40%) to the farmers who have successfully implemented the rehabilitation work in their farms according the agreed plan, After the target farmers completed the required rehabilitation work and based on the partner reports , SC will distribute the third (final) instalment (20%). Activity 1.4: Provision of cash assistance to support the rehabilitation of 110 dunums for 55 farmers: SCI will support 55 small farmers, at least 9 of which women and 2 farmers with disability, by providing cash assistance to support the rehabilitation of open farms (2 dunums per farmer) to plant seasonal crops such as cucumbers, peppers, tomatoes, etc. each farmer will receive 3,000 ILS, in two installments rehabilitate two dunums of his open farm their farm, based on the agreed rehabilitation plan. SCI will distribute the first cash installment (40 %) after the farmers sign the contracts. Partners will follow up on rehabilitation activities through field visits and provision of technical support. BLDA will follow up on the rehabilitation of 50 dunums in Gaza and 60 dunums in North Gaza. SCI will disburse the second installment (40%) to the farmers who have successfully implemented the work in their farms based on the agreed rehabilitation plan, SC will distribute the third (20 % final) installment for the farmers who have successfully completed the rehabilitation work in their farms. Activity 1.5: Equip 80 small poultry farms (maximum 100 m2 of broilers farms) with solar energy units, based on the needs assessment conducted under Activity 1.2, SCI will support 80 small poultry breeders, at least 12 of them are women and 2 breeders with disability, through equipping their farms with solar units. BLDA will hire an external consultant to develop the design of the solar energy units (1 kW for each farm). Based on the design developed, partners will select qualified contractors to install the solar units through a public tender. BLDA will follow up on the installation of solar energy units across 50 farms in Gaza and 30 farms in North Gaza. Activity 1.6: Equip and rehabilitate eight agricultural wells with solar energy units to enhance access to irrigation benefitting 160 vulnerable farmers, SCI in coordination with MoA will select the wells which serve farmers suffering from poor access to irrigation. SCI will hire an external consultant to develop the design. SCI will hire a qualified contractor to rehabilitate five wells in North Gaza and three wells in Khan Younis through a bidding process. Integration: The proposed intervention will be integrated with SCI’s ongoing projects. CPC members, 50% of which are women will encourage farmers to participate in the activities of its existing CPCs, including the development of PRPs.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

To ensure the quality of work, the project will be implemented in coordination with MoA and the FSL cluster. Additionally, the intervention will be integrated with SCI’s current livelihood interventions to increase the impact and enhance the sustainability of the project and its interventions, building on its existing projects and extended partnerships with the different stakeholders towards a comprehensive approach for strengthening positive coping mechanisms for the beneficiaries. Both partners have a long track record of sound implementation in delivering similar projects and strong organizational capacity. Given that they have access to these communities, they will support SCI in the selection of the target groups, facilitate SCI access to these communities, and ensure that the implementation of activities is up to quality benchmarks. Through this project, SCI will aim to increase the partners' capacity and experience, along with administrative guidance provided by SCI on the general project implementation through a hands-on practical approach to their learning. This is to ensure quality services are delivered for target beneficiaries, and that the support delivered is contributing to the achievement of the planned results. To ensure the involvement of farmers, SCI and its partners will work with each farmer to develop the rehabilitation plan for his farm. SCI and its partners will implement the rehabilitation of farms through the provision of conditional cash which will enable the farmers to procure the materials based on their needs, after SCI reviews the rehabilitation plans to provide technical support and ensure the quality of materials. Given to farmers' lack of experience in solar energy systems, SC will implement the provision of solar energy units for poultry farms and agricultural wells through contractors under close supervision of SC and partners' engineers.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

SCI is committed to being accountable to communities by sharing appropriate and timely information and supporting them to influence processes and decisions that affect them by enabling them to provide feedback and share complaints about our work. Feedback and complaint mechanisms will be in place and disseminated at the start of the project, including SCI’s toll-free hotline (1800-724-726), complaint boxes and community sessions. SCI pays special attention to ensure that beneficiaries are encouraged to raise any concerns and make use of their preferred communication channel. Therefore, the sensitizations sessions will be utilized to raise the awareness about the rights and responsibilities of the beneficiaries, as well as about the feedback and complaint mechanisms (FCM) and how we identify and respond to feedback. SCI will also train stakeholders and teams in SCI Child Safeguarding, Gender and financial policies with a focus on preventing fraud and corruption. A specific session on PSEA, with a focus on identification and reporting, will be delivered to partners and providers, ensuring these incidents are also reported through the cluster system. SCI PSEA mechanism is integrated into our broader FCM.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

SCI’s risk analysis ensures that all the activity risks will be identified such as the unavailability of materials for rehabilitation, farmers' failure to use cash assistance for agreed purposes and restrictions to reaching the target areas due to security risks. The relevant mitigation measures will be put in place to enable SCI to follow a conflict-sensitive approach that ensure the timely identification and mitigation of potential negative risks and consequences. SCI will ensure strong community engagement to ensure we do no harm; that activities are culturally sensitive and that both men and women are engaged in project activities. Community consultations will include discussions on targeting criteria to ensure a transparent selection process, which has been one of the key feedbacks received by affected populations. Beneficiary selection will be triangulated through field visits, cluster consultations and local stakeholder discussions. SCI will maintain its neutrality and impartiality. Staff will not engage in discussions on sensitive political and community issues while working with beneficiaries. SCI will continuously monitor context and risks and adapt activities and their schedules to ensure access and reduce risks. SCI will check the safety of the construction of farms and make sure that the farms cause no harm to neighboring communities. It will analyze the potential interaction between the planned activity, the changing context, and potential implications for the relations between stakeholders and communities. SCI`s strong MEAL approach will identify any emerging harmful trends and activities and introduce course correction to programming as required. SCI will strictly enforce its Child Safeguarding policies and processes including support to partners as needed.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The selection criteria will ensure that all beneficiaries have equal rights to the proposed assistance based on a clear targeting criteria. SCI considers it imperative that the project is inclusive of individuals living with disabilities, especially given that current services for them are limited. The project will prioritize the most vulnerable groups including persons with disability (at least 3% of the targeted farmers) and women (at least 15%) to enhance their economic independence and productivity. The selection criteria will focus on female-headed households. Female CPC members will facilitate women participation in the project and will identify the challenges faced by women and their specific concerns during implementation e.g. procurement of rehabilitation materials, to enhance and promote their participation. During monitoring visits, SCI and its partners will ensure that both men and women are actively participating in the project and sharing feedback.
