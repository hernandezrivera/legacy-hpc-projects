Project Summary

SAMS in this project is part of FAST Consortium. Through this project, SAMS in collaboration with consortium members are prioritizing the reduction of morbidity and mortality through improving the access to quality-health care (primary and secondary health facilities, mobile clinics) in 20 health facilities in Aleppo, Idleb and Hama.
In order to achieve this objective, SAMS will work on supporting the HFs. This will include coverage of staff incentives, monthly reimbursement of running costs, as well as provision pharmaceuticals and other medical and non-medical commodities, also SAMS will assist the supported HFs with repairs and rehabilitation to maintain the health network. This service will only be provided in response to any damage to health infrastructure in case of airstrikes or shelling against supported HF. SAMS will provide recruitment support, capacity building, and ongoing medical supervision to ensure the HFs are staffed with qualified medical personnel and to enhance compliance with standard treatment protocols. Guided by the Do No Harm principle, these initiatives are aimed at ensuring safe, high quality health care for beneficiaries and investing in local capacities and sustainability at the same time. SAMS will continue to provide training to the HFs on a variety of topics, including management of communicable and non-communicable diseases, pharmaceutical supply management, patient data management, and sexual and reproductive health (RH), gender-sensitivity and protection. SAMS will increase the capacity of the HFs so that minimal inputs will be required to sustain the services.
As per the Essential Primary Health Services Package adopted by the Health Cluster, SAMS will provide the following services: reproductive health services (ante-natal care, post-natal care, family planning, normal deliveries, gynecological consultations, and clinical management of rape), integrated management for child illness, vaccines, prevention and control for communicable and non-communicable diseases, nutrition, MHGAP, health awareness, and basic laboratory and radiology services.
Services at the secondary health care level will include gynecologic surgeries and caesarian sections, trauma management, major surgeries, hospitalization services, blood transfusion, and intensive care unit.
FAST partners will continue to coordinate with other health actors through the Health Cluster, Nutrition Cluster and the NGO forum where routine data is gathered and shared through the 4Ws and HERAMS. FAST partners are also coordinating with other humanitarian actors at the field level to assess the needs, strengthen referral pathways, and respond to emergencies, among other ways. Moreover, SAMS and the FAST consortium will closely coordinate to share information about needs, ensure an effective and coordinated response to new emergencies, harmonize the salary scales and staffing structures across HFs, coordinate on immunization campaigns, and verify the quality of pharmaceuticals donated.

Needs

Now approaching its eighth year of crisis, the overall scale and complexity of humanitarian needs of people in Syria remain staggering in terms of magnitude and severity with an estimated 13.2 million people who are in need of health assistance in Syria. Females make up 72 per cent of the PiN. Vulnerable groups include 5.2 million women of reproductive age, 2.2 million children under 5 years old, and 2.3 million people aged over 50, (HNO 2019). Based on NWS Needs Assessment (A Visual Analytics Report), March 2018 and HNO 2018 and HeRAMS:Health Systems & Clinical Support: A common finding was that there continues to be vastly insufficient health services in NWS to meet the extensive needs. Less than half of Syria’s pre-war health infrastructure is functioning at capacity, and health facilities (HF) continue to be deliberately targeted. The continued damage and destruction of public health infrastructure puts existing HFs under additional strain, limiting the ability of vulnerable people to access sufficient medical assistance. Communicable Diseases: Furthermore, Syria is at a high risk of disease outbreaks due to disruption in routine vaccinations, which further adds to vulnerability, especially for women, children, and displaced populations. High concentrations of IDPs in camps facilitated spread of communicable diseases, such as measles and respiratory infections. The multi-sector needs assessment (MSNA) found that overcrowded settings, lack of shelter, limited access to vaccination, and the risk for malnutrition due to the low caloric intake had deleterious effects on the health and general well-being of IDPs. Further, it also found that respiratory infectious diseases and child diarrhea were the most common health issues reported in northwest Syria. HNO 2018 reports that low immunization coverage Non-Communicable Diseases, Injury & Mental Health: Chronic diseases were the third most common reported health issue in the region. As access to life-saving medications and care is restricted, people with life-threatening, chronic diseases such as diabetes, kidney failure, asthma, epilepsy, and cardiovascular illness are at continued risk of death or developing complications. Again, the MSNA found that war-related injuries and complications remain the most common cause of mortality (45% of reported deaths), followed by heart diseases (39% of reported deaths). Furthermore, HNO 2018 indicates that additional support is urgently needed to scale up services to address increasing mental health needs, with an estimated 27,635 people in need of mental health services through health centers, 61,788 people needing outpatient psychiatric care, and almost 12,000 requiring inpatient psychiatric care.Reproductive Health: There is a significant lack of RH Facilities and qualified medical staff, particularly female gynaecologists. According to HeRAMS, less than 50% of all HFs in NWS have ante-natal care (ANC) and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and New Born Care (CEmONC) services. Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) services are reported to be available in less than 15% of all HFs and family planning (FP) services at only 40%. According to HNO 2018, 149 sub-districts in Syria do not have any GBV programming. Where GBV services exist, women and girls have reported a positive impact on their lives, which is confirmed by service providers receiving more disclosures. Pharmaceutical & Other Medical Commodities: Availability of essential medicines and medical equipment are major shortfalls in health care in Syria. Before the conflict, Syrian pharmaceutical industry was able to meet 90% of the country’s drug needs with only 10% of the medicines imported. According to WHO, Syrian pharmaceutical factories are 90% inoperable, covering only 20-30% of the total country’s needs while medication to treat chronic diseases and conflict-related trauma injuries are in high demand.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Sexual Exploitation and abuse by Facilities staff toward beneficiaries. Possibility: high. Effect: high. Mitigation measures: 1. Set PSEA Policy and effectively implement the policy in Hiring the staff and oriented them on the policy, training them, and make sure every staff sign the policy, 2. Effective complaint mechanism in place, 3. Awareness raising for beneficiaries on PSEA, 4. Clear complaints channels for beneficiaries and distribute brochures and leaflets in all places in the facilities (waiting rooms, entrance, Etc.).Risk: Sexual and Gender Based Violence in the facilities between beneficiaries. Possibility: high. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Put cameras in the entrance and every floor of the facilities and corridors, 2. Use or establish separated toilets for men and women, 3. Establish spacious waiting rooms to host huge number of patient. 4. Put keys for the empty rooms if any. Risk: Facilities are inaccessible for the disabled patient and elderly persons. Possibility: moderate. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Build ramp for disabled people at the facility entrance, 2. Make sure of the availability of logistics supplies needed for elderly persons and people with disabilities. Risk: impure or spoiled medications and supplies that could be harmful to beneficiaries. Possibility: moderate. Effect: High. Mitigation measures: 1. Procure medication with long time validity (taking into consideration the transportation time for these medication and deliver it to the facilities then to beneficiaries to be used), 2. Procure the medications from reliable sources, 3. ensure cold-chain transportation for drugs requiring low temperatures, 4. Regular monitoring of the storage conditions of the medications and the validity date.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Control by unaccepted groups / entities or GoS. Mitigation measures: Anti-Terrorism Check (ATC), Pause / Cessation; Information security SOP; Strengthening the relations with the local communities; Relocating the activities locations.Risk: Attack by GoS and its allies / others.Mitigation measures: Selecting fortified locations; Security assessment (site & building); Evacuation SOP; Sourcing alternative locations, in case of need for relocation.Risk: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salariesMitigation measure: Potential high turnover of staff receiving salaries; Repeating the trainings regularly to ensure that all new staff delivering it; Adopt appropriate salary-scale to retain the staff as possible.Risk: Abduction (kidnapping)Mitigation measure: Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Security Briefing; Counter Abduction; Abduction Management training for staff and partners.Risk: TheftMitigation Measures: Safety and security upgrades for SAMS facilities, including CCTV cameras and guards; Photo verification of received shipments, signed waybills, and notification if any missing items noted in HF count within 72 hours; Review of stock/inventory records during monitoring visits; Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Quarterly physical inventory of warehouses; review of monthly consumption reports; Security Briefing for staff and partners.
