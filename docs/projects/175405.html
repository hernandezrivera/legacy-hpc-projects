Project Summary

Prolonged conflict, natural disasters and limited government investment in health services have resulted in weakened health systems in South Sudan. Poor health facility infrastructure, inadequate human resources for health, and inadequate health financing has resulted in limited access to equitable and quality health services across the Country. This intervention will target underserved and vulnerable populations in Aweil East County (Northern Bahr El Ghazal State), Rumbek East (Lakes State) and Panyijar, Koch, and Mayendit Counties (Unity State).
Through this proposed intervention, the IRC will support 2 static primary health care centers (PHCC), 3 integrated Rapid Response Mobile (RRM) outreach teams and 40 Boma Health Workers (BHWs) in Aweil East County. In Lakes State, the intervention will constitute 2 integrated RRM teams and 40 BHWs in Rumbek East County to serve hard to reach populations. In Unity State the intervention will provide essential integrated lifesaving health interventions as follows; support 1 PHCC, 1 integrated RRM outreach Clinic and support 40 BHWs in underserved areas of Koch County, 2 integrated RRM Outreach teams and 40 BHWs in Mayendit County, and 1 PHCC, 4 RRM outreach teams and 40 BHWs in Panyijar County.
The intervention will work closely with the County Health department structure as the custodian of health and nutrition services at the County level to foster coordination and collaboration with key actors in health and nutrition in the target Counties. This will also support County led services mapping to ensure complementarity of interventions proposed by this project with other ongoing health and nutrition services delivery partners. County led micro-planning, disease surveillance and epidemic preparedness and response activities including for the COVID-19 pandemic will be integrated into this intervention to ensure rapid response to epidemic prone diseases at the County level and mitigate associated mortality and morbidity.
The RRM teams will work through a flexible approach to reach underserved and displaced populations residing outside a 5km radius of the nearest health facility. BHWs will provide community level treatments for common causes of childhood morbidity and mortality i.e. malaria, pneumonia and diarrhea while also supporting health promotion, screening for immunization defaulters and acute malnutrition while providing referral linkages to the nearest health facilities. The IRC will recruit qualified health personnel to support quality health services delivery at the supported static PHCCs, PHCUs, the RRM mobile clinics, and they will receive in service training on key areas including use of the South Sudan Treatment guidelines, Rationale prescription of drugs, Integrated management of childhood and neonatal illnesses (IMNCI) and Clinical management of Rape (CMR). The intervention will take into account COVID-19 risk mitigation measures through provision of Personal Protective equipment (PPEs), stringent Infection prevention and control (IPC) measures including bi-weekly support supervision using the WHO’s IPC-At Facility checklists and strengthen risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) activities across all service points. BHWs will be trained in no-touch techniques and provided with job aids to support community based surveillance. This will be linked at the facility level to the Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR), Early Warning, Alert and Response System (EWARS) to strengthen County level disease surveillance. Strengthened pharmaceutical supplies management will be the backbone of the intervention through adequate supplies forecasting and preposition in hard to reach areas to minimize the risks of tracer medicine stock outs, while ensuring continued service delivery throughout seasonal variations.
The intervention will mainstream COVID-19 risk reduction measures across all service delivery channels to ensure reduction in transmission.

1 - Needs

The formation of the transitional government of national unity (TGoNU) in Feb 2020 has resulted in a fragile stability. However, increasing humanitarian needs are driven by cumulative effects of years of recurring conflict and violence, and aggravated by subsequent shocks including the COVID-19 pandemic, relatively lower oil prices, and consecutive years of flooding. The Country is ranked as the most dangerous country for people to live in during the COVID-19 pandemic due to its structural deficiencies including a weak and under-resourced health care, high poverty rate and internal displacement. South Sudan has one of the highest maternal mortality ratio globally estimated at 789 per 100,000 Live births. Only a quarter of women had facility births in Jubek State further leading to an increase in maternal mortality . In addition to access constraints and affordability, perception related constraints have also been shown to have an impact on the uptake of maternal health services in South Sudan and need addressing. The COVID-19 pandemic further exacerbates disruptions in the continuum of care for immunization, antenatal care services and treatment for chronic illness including mental health, TB/HIV and non-communicable disease care. As of February 8th 2020, the country has reported 4,642 cases and 68 deaths from COVID-19 , Case fatality rate 1.5%.Low immunization coverage (Less than half of children are fully immunized) has resulted in continued transmission of vaccine preventable diseases like measles. In 2020, 8 Counties reported measles outbreaks across the country. Seasonal flooding in concurrent years has resulted in displacement of vulnerable populations further limiting their access to essential lifesaving health services. The heavy flooding further resulted in disruption of essential pipelines for pharmaceutical commodities, vaccines and reproductive health kits. Health infrastructure, among other basic services, were disrupted by the floods resulting in damage to health facilities and drugs storage facilities. This impacted on the number of functional facilities available in a country where 7.5% of the reporting 1,763 reporting facilities provide basic services . Only 44% of the population in South Sudan live within a 5 kilometer radius of the nearest health facility. Access constraints following heavy flooding further compounds this. The Boma Health initiative seeks to bridge this gap by providing community level treatments for the common causes of childhood morbidity and mortality. The integrated food security phase classification (IPC), Nov 2020 shows populations in 6 counties facing ‘famine likely’ or ‘catastrophe’ conditions including in Aweil South County, Northern Bahr El Ghazal (NBG) State. People in these affected Counties are in need of an integrated multi-sectoral emergency preparedness and response. Aweil East, Aweil South and Panyijar Counties are classified in Emergency (IPC phase 4) while the other Counties targeted by this intervention are classified in crisis (IPC phase 3) Acute Food insecurity. Low crop production because of flooding events during the agricultural season, the continued effects of COVID-19 on their livelihoods and incomes, the ongoing economic crisis and currency devaluation that continues to erode their purchasing power, and limited access to milk and wild foods are some of the contributing factors to this situation. COVID-19 motivated changes in SAM and MAM admission criteria for children have exacerbated access to services alongside other factors such as heightened inter-communal conflict and insecurity, worsening economic crises, flooding, among others. Of the targeted Counties, 2 are in IPC AMN phase 2 (Alert), 3 in IPC AMN phase 3 (Serious) and 3 Counties in IPC AMN phase 4 (Critical). All arelikely to deteriorate in the next lean season if humanitarian interventions are not provided.

2 - Project activities

The proposed intervention will implement three modalities to meet the health needs of the underserved populations as follows; 1) integrated rapid response mobile (RRM) clinic approaches, 2) support to static health facilities including Primary health care centers (PHCC) and primary health care units (PHCU), and 3) a community health model anchored on the Boma Health initiative (BHI). The intervention will provide essential primary health care services to men, women, boys and girls including persons with special needs (people living with disabilities, mental health and the elderly). Preventive and curative services will be provided through strengthened community health promotion activities with community members at its core and through trained providers for curative health services. COVID-19 mitigation will be integrated across the intervention through dynamic risk communication and community engagement, infection prevention and control and strengthening surveillance and coordination at the community level and health facilities.The intervention will recruit qualified health personnel to deliver quality health services in the static and mobile health clinics as follows; 1 clinical officers, 1 nurse, 1 dispenser, 1 CHWs, 1 MCHWs, 1 card registers and 1 EPI vaccinators in each RRM clinic and 1 Clinical Officers, 2 Nurses, 1 dispensers, 2 midwives, 2 MCHW’s, 4 Support staff and 1 Vaccinator in each static facility. The staff will provide essential primary health care services to populations in need at the supported facilities and support referrals to higher levels of care as needed.The intervention will select and train 20 BHWs per boma located 5km away from the nearest health facility and the BHWs will be supervised by 1 supervisor across each supported County based on MOH BHI gap analysis. All selected health providers will receive in service training based on training needs assessments in order to improve quality of care. Training on clinical management of rape (CMR), Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI), Infection prevention and Control (IPC), rationale use of Personal protective equipment (PPE), COVID-19 case identification and case management and Integrated disease surveillance & response (IDSR) trainings are planned.A stable pharmaceutical supplies pipeline is essential to the provision of lifesaving health services through adequate forecasting of commodities, timely procurement of essential and effective last mile distribution of commodities to end user facilities. Standard warehouse management protocols at the County Drugs stores and at health facilities will be strengthened through staff mentorship and support supervision. This will apply for all pipeline items including vaccines and RH kits. The intervention will work closely through community structures including mother to mother support groups, women groups, youth groups and organizations of people living with disabilities to create demand for health services and ensure that services provided reach the most vulnerable. Health providers will be trained on the Washington group short set on functioning (WG-SS), GBV screening tools and referral pathways. Mental health GAP training modules will be provided to key clinical providers to equip them with needed skills to manage clients with mental health.Frontline health staff including BHWs will be trained on COVID-19 prevention, equipped with PPEs and IPC supplies to reduce the risks of disease transmission. Coordination with existing mechanisms will be strengthened to support timely detection, isolation and referrals for care continuity. RCCE activities will be scaled up at the community level and integrated into routine health education sessions, during door to door visits at the households and through mass media. In the event of vaccine availability in South Sudan, the intervention will seek to repurpose to support the national effort in line with national deployment strategies.

3 - Project indicators

The project success will be measured through IRC's Monitoring for action framework which identifies a set of monitoring actions across all stages of the program cycle to ensure that project activities are conducted as planned. A dedicated Monitoring and evaluation officer will be recruited to oversee all aspects of project monitoring, evaluation, and learning are implemented and remain on track. The project will utilize the project cycle management check points to assess and track progress. The project will track key project indicators to ensure that project activities are conducted as per work plans. MOH health information management system tools will be used at all service delivery points to collect relevant project data and client information, which will be stored and managed with strict confidentiality. The data will be summarized and tallied, verified, validated and shared through the District Health information Software (DHIS 2.0) where these will build on the County level planning and data management. The project will put in place two way client feedback mechanisms that will serve to capture feedback from clients including vulnerable groups (people with disability, women, girls, elderly and mental health). This will inform project direction and adaptations to ensure that the most vulnerable in society are able to fully access and benefit from health services. The project will keep track of health education sessions using health education records that will include number of clients reached with messages and specific topics shared during the meetings. Training records including details of pre and post-tests performance by the participants, number of participants and specific training modules will be tracked through this intervention. The project will seek to strengthen disease surveillance across all target Counties. At health facilities and RRM sites weekly Integrated disease surveillance reports will be tracked and entered through DHIS 2.0. Community based surveillance will be strengthened through training BHW and BHW supervisors on Community based information management system (CIMS) tools, weekly information on surveillance will be collated at the health facilities and into DHIS 2.0, while monthly HIS reports from the community will be shared with health facilities and incorporated into the DHIS 2.0.Project performance will be reviewed and evaluated on a monthly basis against project key performance indicators which align with the health cluster strategy, using the indicated means of verification to ensure that the project implementation is successfully tracked. Action points will be solicited during each review meeting and followed through to ensure catch up activities are implemented for any indicators that will lag behind.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will be governed by the IRC Guidelines for a survivor centered approach to reporting safeguarding misconduct and the Health sector specific safeguarding checklists will be used in setting up the intervention. These measures will ensure that clients receive care in safe environment and protected from safeguarding issues. The IRC ‘raise concern’ policy has elaborate and confidential channels to allow for staff and client reporting of any safe guarding concerns which are then independently verified and action taken. The project will ensure clients are briefed on reporting concerns and awareness will be raised to ensure that clients are aware of rights and understand sexual exploitation and abuse. Client feedback mechanisms will be set up at all clinical areas to allow for self-reporting and provide a confidential channel for clients to raise any safeguarding concerns. Regular monthly meetings with key stakeholders will also provide an opportunity for accountability concerns to be raised and shared with all stakeholders concerning health services delivery.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Through random sampling of clients accessing services through the project, the most vulnerable in the populations will be accorded a chance to share feedback through the feedback mechanisms. Feedback questionnaires will be made simplistic and easy to understand and the intervention will employ the use of easy to access helpdesk where persons with specialized needs including clients and caregivers with disability, mental health needs and illiterate community members. Client confidentiality will be maintained throughout the intervention through adequate data protection measures including assigning anonymity to individual client feedback._________________________________________________________________________________________________________

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will engage in Project opening meetings at the community level which will bring together key stakeholders at community level including women and youth leaders as well as leaders of persons with special needs. Through this meetings, focused group discussions and key informant interviews the community will be requested to provide their key needs including decisions on key areas to set up RRM clinics to ensure safe access by women and girls at all times. The Community members will be engaged to understand project scope as well as available budgets for service delivery, commuinty complaints mechanisms through the Village health committees and Boma Health Committees. Through this engagement the commuinty will be advised on the BHI selection criteria for volunteers and supervisors to ensure that the best candidates are selected to support services delivery. Health promotion messages will be tailored to the needs of the community taking into account the cultural sensitivities and barriers to uptake of health information and services to ensure that culturally appropriate messages are disseminated by the project to increase uptake of services and improve health outcomes

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The intervention will strive to engage with communities through channels that the community know and trust to ensure that there is buy in. Efforts will be made towards alerting the community of the mechanisms to share their feedback in an open and candid manner, and confidentiality of clients will be protected through strong information management. The project will maintain a data base via the Kobo toolbox to ensure that all information is digitized and to allow for more detailed analysis of the feedback information for action and response. The feedback channels and mechanisms will be iterative to ensure that any learning during the project duration is captured and applied to improve on the processes. The client feedback mechanisms will include sessions with key community leaders in dialogue forums to provide feedback on the findings and engage the community teams in driving changes to the intervention logic as needed.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project will ensure adequate coordination with key stakeholders at the States and County level to map out key actors providing services including Gender Based Violence and other protection services. At start up, the project will ensure that existing GBV referral pathways are cascaded to all project staff to ensure that this information is easily available to clients. IRC staff will be oriented on safeguarding as well as trained on the IRC safeguarding policy to ensure adherence. Client feedback sessions will be organised with various key groups to ensure that all persons with special needs and vulnerable groups are targeted and heard from. This will ensure that consultations around key project activities and decision making processes for intance locations for mobile services, selection of community health workers and other demand creation strategies are inclusive.To ensure limited interference from external stakeholders in impartial and needs based selection of clients, the project will hold community wide consultations at start up where all project activities will be discussed in an open and transparent forum. Criteria for selection of beneficiaries including as community health workers will be made available and shared with community members to mitigate negative impacts on social cohesion and potential for conflict over access to resources.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

This intervention will mainstream protection through the project duration by ensuring that women, men, girls and boys have equitable access to quality health services. The intervention will strengthen linkages with protection partners across the project locations for referral pathways (Sexual and Gender based violence). This will include ensuring that the pathways exists, are routinely updated and furthermore ensure continued coordination through existing mechanisms and with the support of the County Health Departments and the ministry of gender and social welfare. Health workers will be trained on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), and standard treatment guidelines will be printed and disseminated to all services delivery points to guide health care providers in providing prompt, quality and inclusive services.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The intervention will target children, people with disability, older persons and women headed households through engaging with local community structures. The intervention will coordinate with multi-sectoral state level and sub-state level coordination channels to strengthen collaborations across food security and livelihoods actors and nutrition sector actors to improve dietary diversity and build on the food security gains from previous years. Interventions to reduce morbidity and mortality and improve health outcomes will result in reduction of the negative effects of the seasonal flooding with healthier populations being able to contribute more to livelihoods. COVID-19 risk reduction measures, community based surveillance and risk communication and community engagement activities will likely mitigate the negative impact of COVID-19 on food security.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Through focusing on screening of clients seeking services at health services delivery points using the Washington group of short questions on disability, working closely with organizations of people living with disability to tailor health needs of people living with disability and through targeted rehabilitation of health facilities and supported outreach sites to make them disability friendly, this intervention will ensure latrines are segregated and built with ramps, hand rails and other adaptations that make it easier for people living with disability to access while seeking basic and essential health services. Disaggregated data collected at services delivery points will be collated and analyzed to assess whether all groups are accessing services and this will inform decisions in placing mobile outreach services in areas that can be accessed by all community members including people with specialized needs.
