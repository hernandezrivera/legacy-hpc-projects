Project Summary

The Scale up Lifesaving Emergency Healthcare access to Vulnerable Populations project focusing on major
causes of morbidity and mortality in Nasir and Ulang will improve access to integrated basic health care and avert excess morbidity and mortality among 30,516 women, children and men. The project targets the most vulnerable population whose right to access to health care services is in jeopardy following the continued intercommunal violence, floods and the Covid 19 pandemic. Of the targeted 30,516 people; 4,883, 4,272 and 21,361 will be IDPs, returnees and host community respectively. thebreakdown according to gender , age and disability is reflected below. one percent of the targeted will be people with disabilties translating to 305 individuals.

The project will support provision of critical lifesaving interventions aligned to the Humanitarian Response Plan criteria and the South Sudan Basic Package of Health and nutrition through a network of 13 PHCUs, 8 PHCCs, 1 Hospital, and 8 mobile clinics in hard to reach locations. The project activities will focus on prevention and case management of epidemic-prone diseases such as malaria, pneumonia, acute watery diarrhoea, cholera, and measles through both static and outreach services; provision of essential medicines and medical supplies accessed through the core pipeline; capacity building of staffs through on-job mentorship, disease surveillance including EVD and COVID 19, and routine monitoring of health information through strengthening the EWARS/DHIS2 system.
The clinical package will include; Health education and promotion, curative outpatient and inpatient services, routine immunization including supplementary immunization activities focusing on measles and polio, Maternal New-born and Child Health Services (MNCH), and HIV/TB services including e-MTCT. The project will integrate Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) at PHCCs and Hospital; management of severe cases of malnutrition with medical complication at the stabilization centres; identification, and referral for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS); and Water Hygiene and Sanitation (WASH) at health facilities.
This project will contribute to addressing the needs of the 30,516 of the estimated 5.5 million women, children and men that will need basic essential health services in South Sudan in the year 2022 and hopefully contribute to a reduction in high maternal mortality rates (789 deaths per 100,000 live births) and under five mortality rates (90.7 deaths per 1,000 live births) in women and children and mitigate the transmission of vaccine preventable and other infectious diseases.

1 - Needs

Ten years after independence and three years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, people in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Their situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education and water, sanitation and hygiene and health services. (Humanitarian Needs Overview, 2022). In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021 of which 5.5 million will require health interventions of which 50% are mainly females and 52% children.The country’s health system and infrastructure are among the poorest in the world. Health facilities are poorly equipped and staffed. There is a severe shortage of trained health professionals in all categories. Thus, people rely on inadequately trained or low skilled health workers to provide their healthcare. A quarter of internally displaced and returnees reside in settlements further than 5km from a functional health facility. There is extremely low immunization coverage (49 per cent Penta 3 coverage) country wide and only 23% in Upper Nile State and 31% in Warrap state has made children more vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases. Malaria is the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounting for 52.6 per cent of all morbidities and 58.2 per cent of all mortalities in 2021. While no new cholera outbreak has been declared since the end of 2017, the number of reported AWD cases is increasing and 20 counties are now classified as cholera hotspots with a high risk of new outbreaks. Further, Hepatitis E cases significantly rose to over 1,800 cases with 11 deaths and still remained above epidemic thresholds in 2021Maternal mortality in the country is one of the highest in the world, with 789 per 100,000 live births .Only 21% percent women are availing recommended four Antenatal Care Services during pregnancy in Upper Nile State (Ulang and Nasir Counties) and institutionalized delivery as well as skilled birth attendance are low at 12.3 per cent , 12.5 per cent in Nasir and Ulang and counties respectively. Access to BEmONC is very low and contributes to pregnancy related and new-born morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable populations.There are severe health gaps and critical needs in Nasir, Ulang ) as a result of flooding, and inter-communal fights (IRNA Ulang November 2021, IRNA Nasir October 2021). According to the IRNA assessments, over 50,000 people in these two counties (Nasir and Ulang ) were internally displaced and the recurring health challenges are; increased morbidity, inaccessibility to health services including labor and delivery, shortage of basic medicines, overcrowding, and destroyed WASH facilities.This project will contribute towards reducing excessive morbidity and mortality in the two counties (Ulang, and Nasir ) in the two states of Upper Nile. 2 - Project activities

UNKEA will prioritize the delivery of life-saving health services in line with the Humanitarian Response . The focus will be on providing primary health care that is aligned to the South Sudan Basic Package of Health and Nutrition through a network of 13 PHCUs (9 Nasir, 4 Ulang ), 8 PHCCs (5 Nasir, 3 Ulang), 1 hospital (Nasir), and 8 mobile clinics.The clinical package will include; (a) Conduct Emergency preparedness and response: UNKEA will build train and mentor health workers to effectively implement infection prevention and control in the facilities, perform early reporting of suspected cases, collect samples and facilitate preliminary isolation of suspected highly contagious cases including COVID 19. UNKEA will ensure that supported health workers compile quality data for submission in the EWARS/DHIS platforms. (b) Provide case management for common causes of morbidity. UNKEA will train, equip and facilitate health workers to provide static and mobile curative outpatient and inpatient services focusing on common causes of morbidity such as malaria, pneumonia, diarrhea, and measles. Support will include the provision of incentives, medicines, medical equipment, HMIS tools, and job aids. (c) Provide routine and supplementary immunization activities. UNKEA will support the health workers to provide both routine and supplementary immunization activities targeting vaccine-preventable diseases. Support will include training on Immunization in Practice, developing and implementation of micro plans, requesting and management of vaccines, and monitoring of uptake of vaccine services.(d) Support management of SAM with medical complications. UNKEA will train clinical officers at the supported PHCCs and hospitals to manage cases of SAM with medical complications at the established stabilization centers. The project will work closely with the nutrition departments and ensure the establishment of a robust referral and follow-up mechanism(e) Strengthen WASH in health facilities. The project will support the establishment of gender-disaggregated pit latrines, construction of incinerators and placenta pits, provision of handwashing facilities, training of health workers in Infection Prevention and Control, and provision of waste segregation buckets. In addition, handwashing will be promoted at all health education sessions. UNKEA will also integrate assessments for WASH at health facilities in its implementation activities. (f) Strengthen sexual and gender-based violence services through training in CMR: UNKEA will ensure that all clinical staff especially the clinical officers are able to provide CMR services. The health workers will be provided with CMR kits received from UNFPA, and GBV registers for easy documentation. (g) Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Services (MHPSS). UNKEA will train health workers in the identification, referral, and management of patients with mental health issues. Health workers at the lower facilities (PHCUs) will focus on identification and referral, while those at the PHCC and hospital will manage the referred cases. (h) Scale-up COVID 19 response. UNKEA will ensure that the health workers conduct COVID 19 risk communication and community engagement activities through providing on job mentorship and Information Education and Communication (IEC) materials. In addition, UNKEA will orient the health workers in the management of COVID 19 that includes strengthening Infection Prevention and Control measures at a health facility, wearing of masks, availing and use of PPE, and isolation and referral protocols. (i) Monitor uptake of services by special groups. The project will ensure that special groups such as old people, women, and people living with disabilities are reached and are using services. Tools such as the disability tracking tools will be used to monitor service uptake.j) Conduct health awareness and promotional activities

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

UNKEA has built a wealth of experience in using beneficiary feedback to adapt project assistance. At project design, community feedback is incorporated in the selection of project locations, operating times, and service packages. On a quarterly basis, the project will synthesize the beneficiary feedback for emerging themes and recommendations. This feedback will be used to guide the outreach locations but also the mix of service providers. For example, UNKEA used the feedback from the beneficiaries to change the location of the outreach site in one of its projects which resulted in the easing of access to services by women. Also, feedback (client satisfaction surveys through clinic exit interviews) on the quality of services will be used to guide the subsequent mentorship and support supervision. As a result, the services provided will meet the needs and expectations of the beneficiaries.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will purposely ensure that people living with disabilities, people who are not literate, women, and girls participate in the project inception meetings, joining monitoring visits, and community feedback meetings. The project team will ensure that meetings are provided at locations accessible by all persons. In addition, the community resource persons will include all the different categories of people. Information both at the meetings will be provided in both English and the local language thus ensuring that information is received and understood by the participants. The project will instruct the focal persons to receive information both verbal and written and in both English and local language. The translation to the local language, inclusion of women, girls, and people living with disabilities in the community feedback structures will ensure that the needs of all people are catered for and their voices are heard. UNKEA selection criteria for community feedback groups clearly include representation of the special groups.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All UNKEA staff and contractors are expected to sign a code of conduct and UNKEA's PSEA policy. At inception meetings, UNKEA will categorically inform the participants of the PSEA policy. At the health facility, the health educators will include sessions on PSEA while the community focal persons will take lead in disseminating information on PSEA. The project will also print abridged versions of the policy in the local language and have the policy displayed at strategic locations at all service delivery points. In all the project meetings both for staff and the community (monthly feedback meetings), dissemination and discussion on PSEA will be included on the agenda.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will use various platforms to provide beneficiaries with information about the project, feedback mechanism, and complaint mechanism. At project inception, meetings will be conducted in the communities targeting key stakeholders including women representatives, youth, local authority, representatives from IDPs, and people with disabilities. UNKEA staff will disseminate the project objectives, activities, and monitoring plans. At this forum, UNKEA will disseminate the feedback/ complaint mechanism whereby the contact person and associated telephone numbers will be shared. The project will also identify and assign community feedback focal persons that will include women, youth, and people with disabilities. On a monthly basis, the project will convene meetings to seek feedback from the focal persons. Also, there will be a focal person at the health facility to seek feedback. UNKEA will ensure that the name of the focal person is displayed and also how to contact the person. Whenever possible, a suggestion box will be placed at strategic points of service delivery. However, the project will adapt the feedback mechanism to lessons learnt along with implementation.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project will ensure that all staff is screened, all staff will sign the code of conduct, and that the PSEA policy is well disseminated. UNKEA protection specialists will monitor the implementation and adherence to the policy. The project will provide services at locations that are safe and close to the communities. The use of chaperones will be encouraged. The project will train health workers and non-clinical staff in the clinical management of rape and GBV service provision. This will promote the identification of clients for timely service delivery. In addition, the project has integrated the design of the referral pathways that will be linked with the county GBV referral pathways. This will ensure that beneficiaries will access services and those requiring referral referred in a timely manner. The project plans to implement a robust community feedback mechanism and will purposely ensure that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, boys, girls, and women are included. Part of the process indicators will involve monitoring numbers of people from special groups that participate in eh feedback activities. This will be used to guide and adapt the project intervention. Only qualified health workers will be recruited. The health workers will be reminded of their oath to patient confidentiality. The project will ensure that only information required by the MOH will be collected on patients using the standard M& E tools. All primary data tools will be stored in safe locations and accessible by only qualified and authorized staff.
