Project Summary

Zimbabwe is currently facing periodical food insecurity and shocks that are mainly caused by climatic shocks and poor performance of the economy. Vulnerable populations are experiencing food shortages in rural, urban and peri-urban areas as a result. It is estimated by Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Committee (ZimVAC) report for urban food security that 2.2 million people in urban areas will be food insecure during the peak hunger period up to March 2020. Due to poverty estimated by World Bank to be at 72.3% and high unemployment rates, vulnerable populations in urban areas are failing to provide for their family’s basic food commodities for the day.
According to ZimVAC 2019 urban assessment report, the two areas Masvingo urban and Gutu peri-urban had proportions of 27.7% and 32.8% food insecure people which are high. Vulnerable families are likely to continue exposed to food insecurity risks and increasingly engaged in negative coping strategies because of the geographical locations of the areas in natural region iv and v where food production is a challenge under dryland agriculture. The surrounding districts to Masvingo and Gutu which are Mwenezi, Chivi and Buhera are already in Integrated Phase Classification 3 and 4 (IPC 3 and 4) of crisis and emergency phase. This implies that staple cereal supplies by farmers from surrounding communities into Masvingo urban and Gutu peri-urban is limited because of cereal deficit in surrounding communities.
It is in view of this food security situation in Masvingo Province that Zimbabwe Council of Churches is appealing to assist 3,000 acute food insecure women, men, boys and girls with monthly food baskets through mobile cash based transfers targeting to save lives as the project will ensure that targeted people are able to meet basic food and nutrition requirements through purchase of food of own choice from January to April 2020.
The project will target beneficiaries in IPC 3 and 4 through community-based selection process that will be agreed between all the stakeholders and the community. The community will define food insecurity indicators of own choice that matches with IPC 3 and 4 characteristics. Special considerations will be given to marginalised groups such as people living with disabilities, chronical illness, the elderly, widowed, orphans and child headed. Verifications of beneficiaries will be conducted through purposive and random sampling to reduce inclusion and exclusion errors.
Since 2009, the organisation has implemented various food security and livelihoods programmes in the the two districts funded by Canadian Foodgrains Bank (CFGB), Act for Peace of Australia and the United Church of Canada. Currently it has running, ’Food Aid Program” funded by CFGB Gutu ward 2. The program started in November and it will be implemented up to the 31st of April 2020. Zimbabwe Council of Churches will utilise the advantage of its presence in the district to synergize previous efforts with the proposed response to maximize the benefits of humanitarian response to affected people.

1 - Activities

The main project activity to be conducted by the project is the provision of monthly food baskets through mobile cash-based transfers targeting 600 families. Each beneficiary will receive Zimbabwean Dollar monthly entitlement equivalent to US$13.20. As such a household with 5 family members will receive Zimbabwean Dollar equivalent to US$66 at the prevailing bank rate. Other activities that will be conducted are weekly market monitoring and monthly post distribution monitoring activities. The weekly market monitoring will track the changes prices and availability of food commodities on the local market and Grain Market Board in response to the cash transfer. The post distribution monitoring activity will be conducted every month 2 weeks after the cash disbursements in beneficiaries mobile phone numbers.ZCC will coordinate all the planned activities with existing programs in the districts of operations. the organisation will attend all the coordination meetings that will be called by government departments, other partners and the food security cluster.

2 - Indicators

The success of the project will be measured through determining the percentage of women, men, girls and boys in need including people living with disabilities, illnesses, child headed and the widowed who received emergency food assistance through mobile cash-based transfers. The success of the project will be determined through comparisons of the baseline and endline situations. The monthly post distributions monitoring will inform the success of the project on monthly bases by tracking on the outcomes of the project to the targeted households. Baseline and endline data will be collected using a combination of qualitative and quantitative techniques consisting of focus group discussions with communities, key informant interviews with line government ministries, household questionnaires and review of relevant food security documents prior to the start of activities to establish benchmarks. The post distribution monitoring will be undertaken 2 weeks after the cash distributions to gather information on outputs and outcomes of the project as food consumption scores, coping strategy index, food consumption patterns, and cash utilization trends through focus group discussions.
