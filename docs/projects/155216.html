Needs

As per the ISCG situation report of the Rohingya Refugee Crisis, 34% of the people in camps still do not have access to safe drinking water, 32% of the people in camps have not received WASH & Hygiene Kits and 35% of the people in camps do not have access to latrine facilities. As per ISCG/WASH-Sector reports non-functionality of water options (tube-well) is 27% in camp 17 while it 17% in camp 4 . It was expressed that non-functionality of tube-wells stems from declining water table, having no head and broken pipes. They also shared that about 50% of people in the camps do not have enough water for their needs. Women typically spend several hours per day collecting water, though men and children also help. Further, they informed that 28% of bathing facilities are out of order due to unavailability of water facilities, uncleanliness, distance from the shelter, no privacy, and broken door/fence and drainage problems. More than 65% of people use communal bathing facilities. Most of the people do not have designated bathing facilities, and thus use tube-well platforms. On the other hand, around 35% of latrines are non-functional and not in use due to a lack of privacy/no separation between men and women, toilets being full, not being safe (no door, no lock, etc.), and being too far away. These challenges around latrines and bathing places especially affect women because they did not feel safe using the facilities at night and there was no segregation of facilities for men and women, a problem due to cultural norms. Additionally, females’s menstual hygiene needs are largely unmet. In a joint agency gender analysis on the response, children and adolescents also raised concerns about safety when accessing WASH facilities. Girls were prevented from using latrines that were being used by both men and women, due to the lack of segregation and privacy. Girls also had problems with long queues, overcrowding and the lack of lighting at night at latrines. Boys raised concerns around the proper maintenance of latrines and lack of lighting and felt unsafe when using wells because of the long queues. All girls surveyed felt that fetching water from tube wells was unsafe because of the distance from home. Adolescent girls emphasized that the presence of men at wells made them feel uncomfortable, and there have been cases of adults preventing girls from collecting water and even extorting money from them. Mobility also prevents elderly men and women from accessing facilities especially due to distance and lighting. CB conducted another field observation visit on 9-10 May 2018. It revealed that the existing tube-wells were installed rapidly by different organizations. It was noticed that some tube-wells did not work shortly after installation due to insufficient ground water level, while some have become non-functional due to a lack of maintenance/repairing.

Activities or outputs

As many wash facilities were installed on emergency basis, there is need to decommssion some of the latrines. For that reason, Caritas Bangladesh is planning to install 100 new latrines, 80 bathing space at Camp 4 and Camp 1W. Undertake feasibility study that for selection of suitable WASH facilities that needs rehabilitation in camps 4 and 17 and 1W, Rehabilitation of 500 latrines, 200 bathing cubical and 100 handpumps. For the regular maintenence of WASH facilities , Caritas also have a plan to recruite 110 Rohingya community volunteers with latrine cleaning kits.

Key project/performance indicators

Access to safe drinking water, gender sensitive sanitation and culturally adapted hygiene facilities and practices are ensured.
