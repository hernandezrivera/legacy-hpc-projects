Project Summary

GBV situation in South Sudan, exacerbated by conflict, displacement, and socio-economic challenges remains critical in South Sudan. The country has one of the highest GBV prevalence rates in East Africa, with intimate partner violence (IPV), sexual violence, and child marriage being widespread. A 2023 study revealed that nearly 50% of married women aged 15–49 experience physical or sexual violence, while 65% of women and girls in conflict zones report some form of GBV. Additionally, 34.6% of girls are married before the age of 18, with specific regions like Lakes and Jonglei reporting much higher rates.
Inter-communal fighting, conflict, climate and displacement, along with a deteriorating economy, further expose women and girls to violence, including exploitation and trafficking. Displacement camps, which host over two million people, often lack adequate safety measures, leading to heightened risks. Climate-related crises, like severe floods, food insecurity and drought have also contributed to resource scarcity, forcing many women into precarious situations that increase their vulnerability.
Efforts to address GBV in South Sudan face significant obstacles, including weak legal systems, widespread impunity, and deep-rooted patriarchal norms.
In 2025, UNFPA, in partnership with its implementing partners, will target priority counties 1 and 2 throughout the response year, while targeting priority counties 3 during the first 6 months of the response. As part of this response, UNFPA aims to reach 118,935 internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities in priority counties 1, 2 and 3 of Abyei Administrative Area (AAA), Central Equatoria State (CES), Eastern Equatoria State (EES), Jonglei, Lakes, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity, Upper Nile, Warrap and Western Bahr el Ghazal states. UNFPA activities will include raising awareness through door-to-door visits, mass media and campaigns, including international events; supporting GBV prevention and response services through SASA and EMAP; supporting GBV case management and integrated service delivery through women and girls’ friendly spaces and one-stop centers; distributing cash assistance and dignity kits; and strengthening referral pathways for GBV survivors. UNFPA will also support GBV coordination at national and subnational levels to ensure effective coordination of the GBV response under the overall leadership of the Protection Cluster and in close collaboration with line ministries.
During the response, UNFPA promotes localization through partnerships and capacity building. In addition, UNFPA supports effective community engagement and the inclusion of people with special needs, including persons with disabilities. In all service delivery points, UNFPA will establish a complaint and feedback mechanism through which community feedback will be received, analyzed and addressed.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan is dire, GBV being a pervasive and devastating issue exacerbated by years of conflict, displacement, and socio-economic instability. High rates of sexual violence, domestic violence, and forced and child marriage continue to increase the vulnerability of women and girls. According to 2025 HNRP, 23 counties have severity needs of level 4, while 53 are at Severity 3 and 3 at Severity 2. Women and girls in Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile states face extreme conditions, with most counties labelled Severity 4. Displaced individuals, returnees and those living in poverty are disproportionately affected, enduring severe physical, sexual and psychological abuse. South Sudan has one of the highest rates of child marriage globally, with girls married off as young as 12 due to cultural practices or economic hardship. Displaced women and girls, both in IDP camps and urban settings, face increased risks of sexual violence and exploitation, including survival sex. Young girls are often pulled out of school due to early marriage, caregiving responsibilities, or the risk of violence.In IDP camps, Protection of Civilian (PoC) sites and transit centres, lack of privacy, lighting, and secure sanitation facilities increases risks of sexual violence. Women and girls living among host communities often face additional risks due to limited resources and social tensions facing increased economic insecurity, which can make them more vulnerable to exploitation and abuse. GBV survivors often fear reporting incidents due to stigma, rejection by families, or fear of retaliation from perpetrators. In remote and rural areas, lack of access to essential GBV services, including health care, legal aid, and psychosocial support is a challenge. To this end, survivors often suffer from severe mental health challenges, including depression and PTSD, with minimal access to psychological support.The health infrastructure of South Sudan in generally weak, with many facilities lacking the resources and trained staff to manage GBV cases, particularly CMR in conflict zones. Access to justice is a challenge with perpetrators of GBV often face no consequences due to weak judicial systems, corruption, and traditional practices favouring men. Although laws exist at national level, enforcement is minimal, and survivors struggle to navigate the legal system. Patriarchal norms also discourage survivors from seeking justice or speaking out.GBV prevention and response programs are critically underfunded despite high needs. Traditional attitudes towards gender roles and GBV create resistance to programs addressing these issues.Addressing GBV in South Sudan requires sustained, multi-sectoral efforts and adequate funding to protect vulnerable populations and empower survivors.

2. Project activities

In line with the priorities of HNRP, UNFPA will carryout the following activities:1. Strengthening awareness raising: UNFPA will use different approaches to raise awareness on GBV. These include home visits, targeted group interventions, mass campaigns and radio broadcasts. UNFPA supports community action groups, non-violent change makers, male champions and community complaint mechanism networks to raise awareness on GBV, including sexual exploitation and abuse. UNFPA also develops and distributes information, education and communication (IEC) materials for awareness raising. In all these approaches, UNFPA delivers consistent messages on GBV risks and promotes access to GBV response services, including GBV case management and CMR. To strengthen community-wide activism and engagement, UNFPA uses the SASA framework to help change norms and attitudes towards GBV. UNFPA also encourages the involvement of men and boys in awareness raising through the EMAP approach to focus on male accountability and foster behaviour change in how men and boys use their power in their relationships and communities. 2. Provision of GBV case management: UNFPA provides GBV case management to survivors. The case management service will be delivered by trained and skilled GBV case workers in women and girls’ friendly spaces and one-stop centres. UNFPA will also integrate GBV case management into mobile activities, particularly in remote areas, to improve the availability of these services for all. 3. Establishment and support of women and girls’ friendly spaces: UNFPA will establish and manage women and girls’ friendly spaces to provide a welcoming environment for vulnerable women and girls to network, de-stress, access psychosocial support and other GBV prevention and response services. UNFPA will support vulnerable women and girls and GBV survivors through income-generating activities, including the production of fuel-efficient stoves. 4. Supporting safehouses: UNFPA continues to support existing safe houses and will explore expanding these shelters to remote areas. The safe houses is a temporary shelter that provides accommodation and protection services to GBV survivors. 5. Supporting one-stop centres: UNFPA supports one-stop centres that provide integrated psychosocial, medical, legal and case management support to GBV survivors. The multi-service centre will be effectively linked to other GBV service delivery points, such as women and girls’ friendly spaces and mobile services.6. Multi-sectoral safety audits: UNFPA works in collaboration with the Protection, Health, Nutrition, Food Security, WASH and Education clusters to conduct regular multi-sectoral safety audits that assess the vulnerabilities of women and girls. 7. Dignity Kits distribution: UNFPA procures and distributes dignity kits to vulnerable women and girls. The dignity kits will be distributed at GBV service delivery points and at the community level. Beneficiaries of the dignity kits will be determined based on their level of vulnerability. The dignity kits aim to provide personal hygiene and menstruation support services to women and girls. 8. Referral pathways: UNFPA will support the updating of GBV referral pathways in collaboration with GBV partners. Referral pathways will be updated quarterly in line with standard operating procedures (SOPs) to ensure that services are available and accessible at all levels.9. Support for GBV coordination: UNFPA supports the coordination of the GBV sub-cluster at the national and sub-national levels. In subnational areas where the GBV sub-cluster is co-led by NGO partners and government line ministries, UNFPA provides technical support to ensure that the coordination platform is aligned with the GBV Area of Responsibility principles and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines.10. Supervision and Monitoring: UNFPA plans routine program monitoring throughout the whole year. Monitoring visits will cover all project sites.
