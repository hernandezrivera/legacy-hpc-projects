Project Summary

CRS will expand its current CCCM activities to cover more IDP settlements in Afgooye Corridor (Banadir/Lower Shabelle regions) where CRS is providing multi-sectoral interventions, focusing on coordination of service provision, accountability to affected populations, enhancing protection of most vulnerable groups within communities.

Needs

Afgooye Corridor continues to host large numbers of IDPs, many of whom are recently displaced due to conflict or have experienced forced eviction. Access to basic services is limited; IDPs rely upon humanitarian assistance that is often short-lived; malnutrition and morbidity remain extremely severe due to vulnerability in virtually every sector (inadequate shelter, lack of livelihoods driving food insecurity, lack of basic WASH services, difficulty in accessing health and nutrition services, etc)A majority of IDPs are women or children. Women and girls face highly elevated risks of GBV, especially due to traveling long distances to seek work or social services. Inadequate lighting and poor location of infrastructure, especially latrines and water points, leaves female IDPs at risk. Children live in a traumatic, unsafe, and unhygienic environment, and are often left alone by caregivers forced to seek livelihoods or services at distant locations. Negative coping mechanisms that violate children's rights are widespread: children often participate in child labor, even walking long distance to Mogadishu; Adolescent boys face risks of forced recruitment and adolescent girls face risks of child marriage.Integrated multi-sectoral approaches are critical to effectively meet the needs of IDPs; at a minimum, strengthening coordination of service delivery across sectors and actors can improve outcomes of humanitarian assistance. Very often, needs assessment and service mapping is outdated or inadequate. Moreover, effective two-way feedback that creates real accountability between beneficiary communities, humanitarian actors, and other critical stakeholders is extremely challenging without a focal point of coordination with mandated responsibility. Many displaced communities in Afgooye Corridor remain with inadequate CCCM interventions or with none at all, even though these districts host some of the largest and most vulnerable IDP populations in Somalia.

Activities

CRS will target IDP settlements in Afgooye District (Lower Shabelle), and Daynille and Khaxda districts (Banadir), that lack CCCM interventions, where CRS is already implementing integrated health/nutrition/WASH/protection. CRS will scale up its ongoing CCCM project in Afgooye corridor, deploying an additional team of field monitors to be based at the womens crisis center at KM13, ensuring strong integration with ongoing protection interventions. CRS will work closely with community/camp leaders and appropriate local authorities, prioritizing the following: 1) updated service mapping on an ongoing basis. 2) Regular updates of DSAs and other CCCM tools; participation in other needs assessments or data collection activities. 3) Establishment of robust feedback mechanisms that are practical for all site residents, especially those with particular needs and vulnerabilities. 4) Mapping/ testing/ improving referral pathways for GBV, child-protection issues, or other rights violations 5) Ensuring committees are representative and trained; Training of camp leaders on camp management, sensitization on protection issues, hygiene health and nutrition. 6) Initiation of a participatory process to write community protection action plans, recognizing the particular needs of vulnerable groups (e.g. disabled and elderly persons) and committing to take steps towards inclusivity, safety, and dignity of all residents; these plans could also identify preferences and practical ideas for durable solutions 7) Provision of community infrastructure: solar lights, signboards, and community spaces that are safe and accessible for all 8) Targeting the most vulnerable with cash-for-work to improve or maintain essential community infrastructure or advance community awareness (e.g. hygiene promoters); providing a multi-purpose cash transfer (conditional, unrestricted) paid via mobile money. 7) Facilitation of forums bringing together communities, humanitarian orgs, and other key stakeholders

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project will strengthen community structures to improve their management of IDP settlements. The project will enhance community ownershipa and capacity to engage humanitarian actors, participate in the projects that affect them, and maximize the benefit they receive from assistance. The project will facilitate participatory processes by which communities can identify the relative safety/stability of thier own situation and how protection can be improved for all community members, creating a more optimal environment to move towards durable solutions, and as part of this process communities will be encouraged to express their preferences around durable solutions and brainstorm practical solutions that meet their needs. Moreover, engagement with critical stakeholders especially government officials at the appropriate (local) will help lay the groundwork for community owned durable solutions that move towards management by government rather than NGOs. This project will not be sufficient in time or resources to achieve these outcomes, but has the potential to lay important groundwork and create a more conducive environment for durable solutions to be achieved.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The project will seek to improve physical risks in informal settlements, reducing access to services and improving community infrastructure as feasible to enhance safety and dignity and respect cultural norms. The project will promote positive enagement between IDPs, host communities, and other key stakeholders. The project will promote accountability and transparency of humanitarian actors delivering services to IDP communities. The project will use a participatory community-based targeting process with clear vulnerability criteria to target beneficiaries for cash-based assistance.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The project will implement a robust feedback loop in line with the AAP framework, to ensure all groups (men, women, older and younger adults and adolescents -- including marginalized groups and various social groups) are able to provide and receive feedback from the project. Indicators in the monitoring plan will track data disaggregate by sex and age where applicable . The monitoring plan will include indicators directly tracking effectiveness of the feedback mechanism, and feedback received from multiple mechanisms will be formally incorporated into the monitoring plan for review by project management to inform adaptive implementation and included in reporting. CRS will ensure gender balance in staffing and that staff are representative of target populations. All staff will undergo protection mainstreaming training and sign a code of conduct meeting UN requirements, including mandatory reporting for PSEA. CRS will ensure camp committees are representative of men, women, older persons, and will encourage participation of youth.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

CRS will train and empower community structures (camp committees / community patrols ) that provide security for site residents. The project will ensure reporting and referral pathways are functional for victims of GBV and other forms of violence, abuse, or neglect, and will prioritize training and sensitization of community members to use these referral mechanisms. This will create greater accountability and deter violence in these communities.
