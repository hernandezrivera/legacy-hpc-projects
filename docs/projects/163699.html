1 - Needs

Food security remains a priority for the vulnerable populations in Libya as conflict continues to generate displacement. An estimated 317,000 people are identified as in need of food security assistance. Of those, an estimated 62 percent are Libyans – whether displaced people, returnees or host communities. Trend analysis between 2018 and 2019 shows a significant improvement of the food security situation in most of country. This improvement can be partially explained by the improvement of the economic situation (economic reforms led to the decrease of food prices). However, the current situation in Libya has affected the food security of non-displaced Libyan households the most, with a 53 percent increase in the number of people in need of food security assistance (164,000 in 2019 versus 107,000 in 2018). According to the 2019 MSNA, the food consumption of Libyan households is relatively good (96 percent) and has improved in relation to 2018. However, with the use of “crisis” and “emergency” livelihood coping strategies being widespread indicate that the food consumption of many households is likely to deteriorate in the future. 64 percent of Libyan households employ crisis or emergency coping strategies to afford food, including relying on less expensive food, reducing the number of meals eaten per day, reducing the size of meals, withdrawing children from schools, or selling assets. On average, 47 percent of Libyan expenses are on food. The situation also varies significantly between mantikas. In Aljfara, Aljufra, Azzawya, Ghat, Murzuq, Sebha, Sirt, Zwara, 90 percent of Libyan households use livelihood coping strategies to afford food.While food is generally reported as available, 30 percent of households reported food items as being too expensive to afford. The median cost of the minimum expenditure basket (MEB) in September 2019 was 706 LYD, a drop of 9 percent from 2018. While the east and west experienced a large reduction in the cost of the MEB, the prices in the south continue to rise. The clashes in Murzuq since August forced most local markets to close. Shortages of key MEB food items have been reported, forcing households to rely on food stocks and purchase key food products from informal markets with significantly higher prices. Moreover, it is estimated that 26,465 individuals are displaced from Murzuq due to the clashes, with approximately 19,000 of the IDPs residing in the surrounding cities, thus increasing the demand in local markets for certain cities in south Libya. As the demand increases in certain areas, prices are likely to rise before the markets can adapt. For example, the MEB is much more expensive than the median in the neighbouring cities to Murzuq, such as Algatroun (62 percent), Ubari (60 percent), and Wadi Etba (44 percent).

2 - Activities

Using the geographic severity needs and vulnerable groups identified in5 the HNO, the post-distribution monitoring findings in areas affected by the Murzuq crisis, and in consultation with local crisis committees, the Ministry of Social Affairs branches and the local NGOs, the activity will prioritize response interventions addressing the specific needs of different population groups and targeting IDPs, returnees and non-displaced hosting communities with a priority to female-headed households, or large households with pregnant and lactating women or girls, children under two, elderly persons, people with chronic illnesses and/or people living with disabilities. The activity will prioritize around 45,000 most vulnerable Libyans from an overall target of 100,000 – whether displaced people, returnees, or host communities – across Libya, with a priority in Murzuq, Sebha, Ubari, Tripoli, Azzawya, Benghazi and Misrata. In case of emergency, WFP will prioritize the newly displaced by providing immediate and life-saving food rations in the framework of the inter-sectoral Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) triggered when a new displacement due to the armed conflict or hard to reach areas arises.

4 - Protection risk analysis

The key protection risks which beneficiaries may be exposed to through the implementation of this activity are: SAFETY: Presence of armed militia at distribution sites, Inability to control the crowd at the distribution sites that might lead to seriously impact safety and security of BeneficiariesINTEGRITY: some more vulnerable households/ individuals might be excludedDIGNITY: Beneficiaries do not want registration to impact on their privacy

5 - Mitigation measures

Protection concerns are integrated into all activities to ensure that people most in need can access assistance in a safe, dignified and equitable manner. In order to minimize the risk of unexpectedly having militias at the distribution point, the activity engages community leaders to improve awareness about the program and manage expectations and provides visibility materials to the cooperating partners that will help them to advocate the role of the program and communicate with armed militias. For the sake of having better control of the crowd at the distribution points, the program, at the design phase, undertakes a comprehensive assessments of the distribution points locations in terms of security, capacity and access control.As for the risk of disclosing beneficiaries identities, the program continues to integrate digitalized solutions that are privacy-enhancing.In order to minimize the exclusion risk, the Program keeps ensuring at all the project phases that the complaint and Feedback Mechanism is addressing cases of potential exclusion from the beneficiaries lists.
