Project Summary

IOM will provide life-saving shelter solutions and NFI assistance to 100,000 crisis-affected individuals in Northern Mozambique. Prioritizing vulnerable groups, the project ensures safe, inclusive, and gender-responsive support while strengthening local capacity through Build Back Better (BBB) practices, fostering resilience, and promoting early recovery for displaced persons and host communities.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Mozambique is facing a complex humanitarian crisis exacerbated by ongoing conflict, climate-related disasters, and seasonal health outbreaks like cholera, which peaks during the rainy season (November–April). Cabo Delgado, in particular, has been under attack by armed groups for over five years, resulting in the displacement of over 716,878 people. The situation has also affected neighboring provinces, including Nampula, Niassa, Zambezia, and Sofala, leading to significant displacement overflow. While more than 610,981 individuals have returned to their areas of origin, many of these returns are unstable due to the persistent lack of services, poverty, and the ongoing humanitarian challenges in these regions. These returnees, particularly in northern Cabo Delgado, face insecurity, limited access to basic services, and high vulnerability to climate-related hazards. The broader crisis has left 1.3 million people in urgent need of humanitarian assistance and protection as of 2024, making it critical for the international community to scale up support.In response to this crisis, IOM plans to target 100,000 individuals, including 30,000 women, 23,000 men, and 47,000 children, aiming to provide essential household items and shelter solutions to the most vulnerable groups. IOM will prioritize gender-responsive assistance, with 22,000 individuals receiving immediate life-saving shelter NFIs based on needs assessments. This intervention will address the urgent need for protection, dignity, and resilience among populations affected by both conflict and climate shocks. In addition to NFI distribution, IOM will implement shelter solutions for 42,838 individuals, including the construction, retrofitting, and upgrading of shelters. These shelters will be designed to withstand harsh weather conditions, which is critical in areas that are vulnerable to both the insecurity driven by armed groups and the adverse effects of climate change. By improving shelter conditions, IOM aims to provide safe, dignified, and resilient living spaces that will help affected populations regain a sense of stability and security. The program will also emphasize the importance of sustainability and resilience, applying the Build Back Better (BBB) principles through community-level training. Local artisans will be equipped with knowledge and skills to apply these principles in shelter construction, ensuring that the response builds long-term resilience in the communities.Furthermore, as the Shelter Cluster lead, IOM will support the Shelter Cluster's ongoing efforts to manage a common shelter and Non-Food Item (NFI) pipeline, which is expected to reach over 70,162 individuals. This shared pipeline, which has been operational since its creation, provides harmonized and standardized household and shelter items to Shelter Cluster partners. The coordination of these efforts will allow for a more effective and efficient response, ensuring that partners have timely access to essential supplies and can better meet the needs of affected populations. By pooling resources and knowledge, the Shelter Cluster enhances its overall preparedness and response capacity, ultimately improving the quality of humanitarian assistance provided to vulnerable communities in northern Mozambique.In summary, the IOM's intervention plan for 2025 is designed to address the urgent and evolving needs of 100,000 individuals in northern Mozambique, providing life-saving shelter and NFI assistance while promoting long-term resilience through capacity building and sustainable practices. By working in close coordination with the Shelter Cluster and other partners, IOM aims to ensure that vulnerable populations, including women, children, and returnees, receive the protection, support, and services they urgently need to rebuild their lives in the face of ongoing crises.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Priority Activities:1. Maintain the Common Pipeline System: Ensure the ongoing operation and accessibility of the common shelter and NFI pipeline for Shelter Cluster partners, serving as a last resort for critical needs in response to emergencies in northern Mozambique.2. Shelter Cluster Coordination: Lead regular Shelter Cluster coordination meetings with humanitarian actors, local stakeholders, and other Clusters to effectively plan and prepare for assessments and responses in crisis-affected areas.3. Participatory Beneficiary Identification and Assessment: Collaborate with local stakeholders and partner organizations to identify, assess, and select beneficiaries based on pre-defined vulnerability criteria, ensuring transparent and inclusive processes aligned with the overall IOM response plan.4. Procurement of Shelter NFIs: Procure shelter NFIs based on identified needs and context-specific requirements, ensuring value for money in terms of transportation, delivery speed, and appropriateness for local conditions.5. Procurement of Shelter Construction Materials: Source locally available materials, such as wooden poles (estacas), bamboo, roofing, and fixing materials, as well as innovative and sustainable construction materials like stabilized earth bricks.6. Transportation and Distribution of Shelter Materials: Plan and execute the transportation and distribution of shelter materials in line with the distribution plan, ensuring compliance with Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) guidelines.7. Dignified Distribution and Awareness Raising: Carry out material distributions in a secure and dignified manner, providing beneficiaries with necessary information and training on how to use the materials to maximize their benefits and durability.8. Promote Vulnerable Group Participation: Ensure active participation of vulnerable groups, including women and children, in the beneficiary selection process, and foster women’s working groups for integration into shelter-related activities.9. Orientation and Capacity Building for Local Artisans: Conduct orientation sessions for local artisans, young adults (both men and women), and community members on Build Back Better (BBB) construction techniques, strengthening local capacity for sustainable shelter solutions.10. Post-Distribution Monitoring: Implement post-distribution monitoring to assess the effectiveness of shelter interventions, gather feedback from beneficiaries, and analyze data to inform future programming and adjustments.Geographical Areas: The activities will be carried out in the districts of Chiure, Ibo, Macomia, Metuge, Mocimboa da Praia, Nangade, and Quissanga, in Cabo Delgado province, northern Mozambique.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project addresses potential risks by mainstreaming protection concerns within multi-sectoral teams, ensuring that vulnerable populations, including those at risk of gender-based violence, are supported through targeted referrals and services. IOM, an active member of the Protection cluster, conducts shelter-protection assessments and household surveys to identify vulnerability criteria, integrating protection aspects into the design of program activities. This approach ensures that gender and social inclusion, safeguarding, and accountability are prioritized, and that interventions are tailored to mitigate risks and promote the safety and well-being of affected individuals.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The proposed project encourages meaningful access to assistance by ensuring that it is inclusive and accessible to all, particularly women and elderly individuals. This is achieved by actively involving these groups in the decision-making process, including needs assessments and beneficiary selection, using gender-sensitive criteria to ensure their specific needs are met. Women and elderly persons are empowered through capacity-building activities, such as training in shelter construction techniques and participation in community awareness campaigns, which give them the skills and knowledge to contribute to the project and enhance their self-reliance. Additionally, the project fosters women’s leadership by supporting their involvement in local committees and ensuring that their voices are heard in program design and implementation. Safe spaces for women and elderly individuals are created, and gender-sensitive distribution processes are implemented to ensure these groups have equal and dignified access to shelter and non-food item assistance.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will identify people with disabilities through targeted assessments and consultations with local communities and partners, ensuring that individuals with disabilities are not overlooked in the needs assessment process. IOM will collaborate with health and protection sectors to refer individuals with disabilities to appropriate services through Individual Protection Assistance (IPA). The shelter kit content will be tailored to accommodate the specific needs of people with disabilities, ensuring that materials and shelter designs are accessible and safe for all. In addition, infrastructure will be adapted to ensure physical accessibility, with ramps, wide doorways, and other modifications where necessary. Disability inclusion committees will be set up at resettlement sites to facilitate the identification of needs and ensure that people with disabilities are actively involved in the decision-making process. These committees will help remove barriers to participation and ensure that people with disabilities have equal access to assistance, services, and support throughout the project.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project will have a positive impact on the environment by integrating sustainable construction practices throughout its lifecycle. IOM will conduct environmental assessments to identify potential environmental risks and opportunities for mitigation before the project begins. These assessments will include research on local building materials, natural resource availability, and potential environmental impacts, ensuring that construction practices are environmentally responsible. The project will promote green building practices, such as the use of eco-friendly materials, efficient energy use, and water conservation measures. Additionally, waste management plans will be implemented to minimize debris and waste generated during construction, with an emphasis on recycling and reusing materials where possible. Nature-based solutions, such as soil stabilization and green infrastructure, will be explored and integrated into the shelter designs to enhance resilience to climate-related disasters and reduce environmental degradation. The project will monitor its environmental impact and adjust practices as needed to ensure sustainable outcomes throughout implementation and the long-term use of the shelters.
