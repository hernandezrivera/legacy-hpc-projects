Project Summary

Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels in children aged 6-5 9 months remain consistently concerning, with over one million children suffering from severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) across the BAY states because of the prolonged/protracted insurgency/crisis in the 3 BAY states.
According to IPC report September 2023, it is estimated that nearly 4.4 million children aged 0 – 59 months are suffering and will likely continue suffering from acute malnutrition in the period of May 2023 – April 2024 in northwest and northeast Nigeria. This includes 1.04 million cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 3.37 million cases of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). In addition, 585,000 pregnant or lactating women are acutely malnourished and will likely continue to be malnourished and need treatment.
In the last quarter of 2023, more than half of the 133 Local Government Areas (LGAs) included in the analysis are in IPC Acute Malnutrition (AMN) Phase 3 and above. Of the 62 LGAs analysed in northeast Nigeria, 14 were classified in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious) 27 in IPC AMN Phase 2 (Alert) and 21 in IPC AMN Phase 1 (Acceptable). Acute malnutrition levels are expected to remain the same in the first projected period of October to December 2023. In the second projection period of January to April 2024, the situation is projected to worsen slightly in the northeast region.
A lot of factors has contributed to the malnutrition in the northeast region such as: weak health infrastructures; poor infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices; limited access to safe water and health and sanitation services; prevalence of water-borne diseases; poor hygiene conditions; and seasonal food insecurity.
GREENCODE proposed services is expected to yield the following results; Provide services to treat SAM children at the 3 OTP sites, to build the capacity of government staffs for the provision of appropriate nutrition services in the OTP sites, Integrate other nutrition services to address the most urgent need in Mafa and Konduga LGAs among the under 5 children, GREENCODE is proposing to implement active case finding, referral and treatment of SAM with medical complication at SC site with behavioral change on proper nutrition and health and psychosocial support counselling (both facility and community based), treatment of MAM children with tom brown,

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

GREENCODE’s mandate of our humanitarian intervention is rooted in the 'Do NO HARM’ principle, also GREENCODE approach to protection is governed by the SPHERE Standards and Protection Principles, the Core Humanitarian Standards. Protection concerns were considered in the planning of all nutrition activities, to ensure that any risk of harm, abuse, and exploitation faced by the target population is mitigated.GREENCODE has the following policies in place that guide and protect both staff and beneficiaries (Gender/Inclusion, PSEAH, Child Protection, and Safeguarding).The proposed interventions will be carried out in a non-discriminatory way protecting the safety, dignity, and integrity of the beneficiaries who receive the services and their caregivers. The protection approach to nutrition services is consistent with humanitarian principles and human rights-¬based programming encompasses a variety of internationally recognized human rights and not just the right to health services.GREENCODE will ensure easy access to the nutrition sites by women e.g mobile to hard-to-reach areas (less than 1hr walking), minimal waiting time; adequate shaded areas will be provided at the waiting areas. In nutrition and health facilities a clear mark will be place at strategic locations or on the facilities to separate toilets for men and women.Similarly, the project will focus on the most vulnerable including orphaned children, women-headed households whose children are more likely to be malnourished.GREENCODE will undertake 2 days GBV mainstreaming and awareness training for both CNMs and project staff. Also, GREENCODE project staff will be conducting GBV awareness by mainstreaming GBV talk and discussion in a support group meetings and cooking demonstrations as well as OTP facilities activities as well as Tom Brown group sessions.GBV referral pathways existing in project communities set up by other GBV actors will be listed and shared among project beneficiaries.Gender equality will be fully integrated throughout the project, starting from targeted inclusive implementation processes to staff recruitment and the participation of both men and women in project structures and evaluation processes. Women and men will be given equal chances during project staff recruitment, although women will be highly encouraged to apply for positions.In addition, because of their important role in successful community mobilization, the project will deploy as many female field workers as possible (CNMs and supervisors) as possible.While all program staff is accountable for project gender equality and safe programming goals, a Gender and psychosocial support Officer will work closely with staff members to ensure gender and child protection standards integration into interventions. Training/orienting all project staff, partners, and stakeholders on the gender-related barriers, child protection, and building their accountability on safeguarding standards to address issues and concerns on the risk of harm will also take place.The activities proposed are specifically designed to better meet the needs of children of different age and gender groups. Project includes estimated sex and age disaggregated data for the target groups based on the findings of the needs assessment and feedback mechanism.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GREENCODE is committed to the principles of participation and both upward and downward accountability. GREENCODE believes and holds dearly to the humanitarian principles and Core Humanitarian Standard which also lay the pathway to accountability.In the implementation of this project, GREENCODE will ensure the involvement of beneficiaries especially the mother, men, parents, and caregivers in all phases of the project.We will ensure its accountability to the project beneficiaries by constantly briefing them about the project for them to understand what the project is all about in terms of goals, objectives, targets, activities, role of stakeholders, approved sum/amount including, procedures, decision-making processes, and financial flows of the projects.As part of our Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), GREENCODE project team will organize a series of community dialogue and feedback sessions- this will enable us to obtain and respond to feedback and track the progress of the project in line with the approved proposal.Where possible and applicable GREENCODE will sign an MOU with the concerned government partner organizations (Primary Health Development Agency of Borno state to show our close collaboration and the part they will be playing throughout the project.In addition, GREENCODE will be working closely with the State Nutrition Officer, LGA Nutrition focal points, and community leaders, and beneficiary representatives for them to better understand project objectives, procedures as well as their roles and responsibilities during project implementation. During the execution of project activities, coordination with other humanitarian actors will be emphasized to extend the number of services. The team will also explain the beneficiary selection criteria, which are informed by the national protocol admission and discharge criteria for CMAM activities focusing on MAM/SAM treatment in OTPs.
