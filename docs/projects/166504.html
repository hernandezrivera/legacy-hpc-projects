Project Summary

The coronavirus pandemic is an ongoing pandemic caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2. The outbreak was identified in Wuhan, China, in December 2019. The World Health Organization declared the outbreak a Public Health Emergency of International Concern on 30 January 2020, and a pandemic on 11 March 2020. As of 29 April 2020, more than 3.11 million cases of COVID-19 have been reported in 185 countries and territories, resulting in more than 217,000 deaths.
Nigeria faces multiple crises, with a protracted conflict in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states, which has spread to neighboring Cameroon, Chad and Niger, intercommunal clashes in the northwest that have led to over 40,000 people crossing the border with Niger for safety, and climate shocks such as drought and floods. In the absence of a viable peace and reconciliation process, the most likely scenario for 2020 is that the situation in North-East Nigeria’s Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states that have suffered from ten years of conflict remains static at best, and a deteriorating security environment at worst, as seen in 2019.
In the BAY states, at least 802 schools remain closed and 497 classrooms are listed as destroyed, with another 1,392 damaged but repairable, while 2.8 million children are in need of education-in-emergencies support. While the BAY states strive toward bouncing back from the impact of the insurgency and other crisis, the Coronavirus pandemic has disrupted schooling in these states, as well as halted educational intervention. Schools were closed before the end of the second term 2019/2020 academic session and there is no sign of resumption as scheduled in the current school calendar.
JRS Nigeria will work to sensitize 10,000 community members on the Coronavirus, its mode of transmission, signs of infection and prevention by developing, printing and distributing Covid-19 awareness materials to schools and other community members. School heads, SBMCs, PTAs, parents etc. will be sensitized and updated on the pandemic to keep them informed on new developments. This will be carried out in a manner that does not contravene the social distancing protocol.
JRS Nigeria will engage 60 volunteer teachers who will be trained on new teaching techniques, active learning, social distancing and psychosocial support. The volunteer teachers will also learn how to use the distance learning curriculum during the training. This training will be done in batches in their respective LGAs (10 volunteer teachers per LGA). At the end of the training, the teachers will receive teaching aids that will enable them facilitate sessions in clusters withing their communities. The teaching aid will include Solar radios, talking books, text books and other teaching materials. JRS will provide mats, face masks and gloves for the learners and volunteer teachers. The learners will also be given exercise books and pencils. Children between the ages of 5 and 17 years leaving in close proximity will be identified and placed in groups of 20, and a volunteer teacher will be assigned to handle 5 groups. The groups will convene twice every week for their learning sessions where snacks will be served with disposable plates.
Counselling will be provided for parents and children based on need by the trained volunteer teachers. These activities will be monitored by 60 volunteer teachers who will be selected from teachers leaving in the communities. JRS will also distribute hygiene kits and other Non-Food Items to ease their suffering during the pandemic and encourage parents to stay at home.
In order to ensure safe return to quality learning when the pandemic is over, JRS will work to rehabilitate 18 schools and provide basic school furniture for teachers and learners. These schools will be provided with WASH facilities (Toilets, water points, Sanitizers, soap etc.) while schools used as isolation centers will be disinfected. Learning kits will also be provided for 3000 scholars

1 - Needs

Despite the widespread of Covid-19 in Nigeria, and the continuously increasing number of confirmed cases reported daily by the NCDC, a percentage of the population still do not know about how the virus is transmitted or prevented. And some believe the virus is not real. As a result, JRS will mobilize community members, school heads SMBCs, PTAs etc. to create Covid-19 awareness in the communities. Awareness materials will also be developed, printed and distributed in these communities. Again, following the closure of schools and restrictions on public gatherings and no information on when schools will resume again, there is need to engage pupils and students in learning activities in their homes to maintain some sort of normalcy and routine and keep the children engaged, while they continue to learn. There is also need to provide core relief items like dignity kits and other NFIs to reduce the pain caused by the lockdown imposed by the federal and state governments. Volunteer teachers who will be selected from the communities will also have the opportunity to earn some income during the lockdown as they will be engaged in the project

2 - Activities

• Develop, print and distribute covid-19 awareness materials to 10,000 individuals• Mobilization and sensitization of SBMC, School heads, teachers, students and community members• Organize family learning/reading clubs• Train 30 volunteer teachers on new/improved teaching techniques, active learning and PSS to reach out to 3000 pupils• Provision of Hygiene kits and other NFIs to 606 households (3,030 individuals)• Distribution of learning kits to 3000 pupils• Provide WASH facilities to 10 schools
