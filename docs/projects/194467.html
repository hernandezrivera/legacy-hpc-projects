Project Summary

Africa Development Aid intends to provide quality protection services to crisis-affected children and their caregivers in Ayod, Pigi, Nyirol and Fangak Counties of Jonglei State, Maiwut county of Upper Nile State. This project will seek to provide an immediate lifesaving response to the vulnerable crisis and flood-affected children from returnees, IDPs, and host communities through strengthened comprehensive case management including family tracing & reunification; referral for services; provision of critical age and gender appropriate psycho social support services including parental skills sessions in a timely, holistic and child-friendly manner as well as to the traumatized adult caregivers whose children are also facing trauma, coordination monitoring and assessments of child protection related risk. The projects will also conduct awareness-raising activities on prevention of family separation, COVID-19 prevention, and response while putting into practice the COVID-19 preventive measures. ADA is having active Child protection activities in Canal/Pigi, Ayod and Fangak Counties. However, under this project ADA will provide static and mobile critical age and gender appropriate psycho social support services in individual and group sessions including Psycho social support to caregivers of children who are traumatized and undergoing PSS activities, identification, registration, interim care, family tracing & reunification to unaccompanied and separated children, advocate for GBV related risk prevention including early marriage, Sexual abuse, exploitation and violence, referral to other services such as health, education, nutrition, shelter and NFIs among others to those at risk and other vulnerable children including those facing other forms of violence, orphans as well as Justice for children (J4C) in a timely, holistic and child-friendly manner and also promote resilience to the traumatized ones.
The project will also seek to enhance the protection environment of vulnerable crisis-affected girls, boys, and youths to enable durable solutions through the establishment of community-based child protection networks, foster family groups, youth/adolescent clubs, Child-Friendly Spaces, child rights clubs, and recreational centres while observing COVID-19 guidelines. This will create enabling conditions for the realization of solutions and prevention of further displacement which leads to family separations; identification of risks, dangers, and impediments; advocacy and mitigation measures, psycho social support, peaceful co-existence initiatives, and resilience.
In Ayod County, the project will cover the following payams, e.g.,Pajiek, Wau Menime, Haat and Kuach-deng payams, Ayod, Pagil, Paguong and Mogok, ADA will also target Atar, Kurwai, Wulieth, kaldak and Khorfulus payams of Pigi County . In Fangak County, the project will concentrate in Mareang, Keew, Paguir, Toch and Manajang (Juaibor), Barboi, New Fangak and Old Fangak Payams. Meanwhile, since there was no Funded Partner for Child protection activities in Nyirol County in 2021, Identified gaps have been noted in Lankien, Tut, Pultruk, Waat, Nyanbor, Chuil, Pading and Keeth payams. ADA will also response in Mawuit County targeting all its payams namely; Jotoma, Kigila, Mawuit, Jekow and Olang.
A total of 25,000 people (12,500 Males and 12,500 females) will benefit from this project. Out of this, 15,000 will be children (7,500 girls; 7,500 boys) and 10,000 will be adults – 5,000 males and 5,000 females. However, out of the total beneficiaries 30% will be from the host community while 10% will be from Returnees and 60% from IDPs.

1 - Needs

According to South Sudan Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO 2022), the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. Meanwhile 53% are children, 24% women and 15% are persons with disabilities. Out of the above, 5.6M people are in need of protection assistance of which 3.5 are in need of child protection services, 83% are children, 17% are their caregivers and 15% are persons with disabilities. Continued conflict and instability in the country combined with flooding have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement. At the same time, limited improvements in some areas have prompted some people to spontaneously return. In addition, the government, with the support of some humanitarian agencies - has facilitated returns to certain areas. Due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return, populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. At least 90,000 returnees from abroad remained displaced within the country, unable to reach their homes. In that regard, a number of protection concerns were noticed; harmful traditional practices such as early or forced marriages for girls, child abduction, child labor, neglect, abuse and exploitation. Children and their caregivers` physical well being, living standard and coping mechanisms are expected to further deteriorate in 2022 to 2023. The needs are vast and the 2022 locations with severity of needs include Fangak, Pigi, Nyirol and Ayod Counties in Jonglei State (Severity 4) and Maiwut and Nasir Counties of Upper Nile State ( severity 4). According to October 2021 REACH report in Canal/Pigi County, May 2021 IRNA assessment in Ayod County and August 2022 IRNA in Old Fangak & Dec 2021 & IRNA assessment in New Fangak conducted by different partners and April IRNA assessment in Nyirol County, number of protection concerns such us early/forced marriages, child abuse, exploitation, violence, child abduction (Ayod), domestic violent, Cases of snake bites, Psychological distress, families separation and cases displacement were identified and reported. Meanwhile in Nasir and Maiwut Counties, almost similar concerns were also notices with additional concern of the influx of returnees from Ethiopia (IRNA report Sept. 2021 in Nasir REACH 2021 in Maiwut). ADA will provide CPiE services; Family tracing & reunification, psychosocial support to traumatized children and their traumatized caregivers via static and mobile CFS services • Mine risk education, and provision of case management services to UASC, • Children in Conflict with law (J4C) and referrals for other services). • Community sensitization and awareness-raising on the safety of children, child right and COVID-19 prevention. • Establishment, incapacitating and strengthening community-based child protection. ADA intends to target 25,000 people (M=12,500 and F = 12,500) in all five targeted Counties through the HRP 2022 and 2023.

2 - Project activities

ADA will provide comprehensive case management for children and their caregivers, family tracing & re-unification, Justice for children (J4C), interim care for unaccompanied children and psycho social support to traumatized children and their caregivers. The activities will be conducted in Ayod, Pigi, Nyirol and Fangak Counties of Jonglei State and Mawuit County of Upper Nile State. The project will target the following Payams; Pajiek, Wau,Kuach-deng Menime, Paguong, Mogok, Ayod, Pagil and Haat payams in Ayod County; Mareang, Keew, Paguir, Toch, Manajang (Juaibor), New Fangak and Nyadin and Old Fangak Payams in Fangak County; Atar, Khorfulus, Kuorwai, Kaldak, Wulieth/Canal and Alam Payams of Pigi County and Lankien, Tut, Pultruk, Waat, Nyanbor, Chuil, Pading and Keeth Payams of Nyirol County, Jonglei State how ever, this project will also target Jotoma, Kigila, Mawuit, Jekow and Olang Payams of Maiwut County, Upper Nile State. The specific ADA interventions will include the following activities: 1: Recruit 40 CP staff (20M, 20F) from Nyirol, Fangak, Pigi,Maiwut and Ayod Counties and train them on revised CPMS, CPiE, CPIMS+ and Case Management SOPs. Case workers will identify; document; assess the protection needs of children; plan and implement interventions on the identified needs including referral to other actors for services, and follow up of cases using global child protection case management minimum standards. PSS facilitators will be trained on basic skills like how to communicate with children, signs of children facing distress or other forms of abuse, psychological first aid, planning and management of mobile & static Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) activities that are age, gender and culturally appropriate. Provide psycho social support activities including parental sessions and also select and train community-based child protection networks and foster parents on CPiE for all the targeted locations. 2: Strengthen case, management approach including provision of cash-based assistance for most vulnerable cases of UASC, J4C cases as well as to their traumatized parents. 250 most vulnerable children (125 boys & 125 girls) will be supported with cash-based Assistance (CBA) as part of case management support. Identification will be done using priority list from Standard Operating Procedures for case management. The well-being of all children with critical protection concerns will be monitored through follow up visits to ensure new protection needs and risks are prevented or responded to in good time. 500 UASC/missing children will be identified/registered, documented and referred for family tracing. 3: Provide psycho social support (PSS) services to 15,000 children and some of their 5,750 traumatized caregivers through provision of mental Health and Psycho social Support Services e.g., recreational activities, livelihood services and parenting skills sessions via mobile & static response. 4: Conduct 15 awareness sessions on prevention of family’s separation, COVID19 and harmful traditional practices prevention to 25,000 adults and children, these will be the same targeted parents and children for PSS. 5: Identify/train 330 community volunteers (CBCPN & Foster Parents) on CPiE in Ayod, Pigi, Nyirol, Maiwut and Fangak Counties. 6: Establish strong referral pathway in five targeted counties of Maiwut, Pigi, Nyirol, Fangak and Ayod Counties this will include service mapping to know which partners is doing what to ensure children access other services that the project will not provide, eg. child survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) will be referred to GBV actors etc.

3 - Project indicators

The success of this project will be measured through the following indicators; • # of girls, boys, women and men provided with individual, group, specialized PSS and other related services (e.g., recreational activities, livelihood services, parenting skills) • # of women and men who receive psychosocial support & participating in parenting sessions • # of girls, boys, women and men with protection concerns supported through case management and referral services- • # of girls, boys, women and men supported with cash based Individual Protection Assistance as part of case management and referrals • # of unaccompanied and Separated girls and boys identified, documented, and receiving family tracing and reunification services (UASC) • # of girls, boys, women and men reached through awareness-raising activities • # of women and men trained on child protection in emergencies (CPiE) including Case management and PSS . # of men and women (community volunteers and staffs) trained on CPiE.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

ADA is a member of Cash Working Group in South Sudan and beside that, ADA has been supporting the vulnerable communities of Ayod, Fangak and Nyirol Counties with Cash based assistance supported by other different donors such us Christian Aid. ADA has done market assessment in the targeted locations and ADA has a staff trained and will be able to carry out the work successfully. The cash provided will be unconditional cash transfer to the beneficiaries through known vendors (commercial banks) that ADA has been successfully using in other cash-based interventions. The vendor will provide cash to identified beneficiaries for unrestricted household, mostly domestic needs like food, clothing, soap, salt, sugar, etc. These items are readily available in the local markets in Ayod, Fangak, Maiwut, Pigi and Nyirol. In Ayod, Pigi, Nyirol and Fangak, there are functional markets and shops, with goods being supplied from mostly Juba (but on a small scale, from Sudan) through flights and boats. In Maiwut, traders obtain their goods supply from Ethiopia. In all targeted areas, the basic items expected to be bought by the beneficiaries are available in functional markets, with reliable supply roots, and reasonably acceptable market prices based on the context of each location.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

ADA will provide cash-based assistance to the most vulnerable UASC cases who are unable to support themselves. This case-based assistance will also be provided to some traumatized adults caregivers who are facing serious economic challenges to at least manage to get some of their basic need through the cash assistance. This is in line with case management support. This cash assistance will help the most vulnerable beneficiaries to have a chance to access multiple needs that they are not able to access. Due to the flooding, communities lost their belongings e.g., food items cultivated, livestock, and other food items for a living. The unconditional cash transfer will provide ample choice to the vulnerable persons to choose whatever they would prioritize to alleviate suffering meted by the floods.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

ADA will setup feedback or complain mechanism including suggestion boxes that will enable beneficiaries to address their concerns on the project implementation.The front-line staff will be reaching out to the beneficiaries who are unable to reach to the ADA field offices or complaint desks as part of their daily outreach activities. This will help those with disabilities and others who cannot easily access the feedback and complaint desk to express their feedback on the services provided by ADA in the community. The outreach staff will fill for them and what they recommend to be done to at least satisfy their basic needs. A good practice that ADA has used in the past is to ensure that people with disabilities, different vulnerabilities, girls, elderly etc. are represented in the monitoring teams and specifically gather info from their own category. Special desks, lines, suggestion boxes that suit their needs are provided. Oftentimes, special people who can translate or have special ways of rapport with the various categories are identified to be representatives in a feedback loop on project matters (including complaints). These methods will be employed to ensure the project feedback mechanism is representative and inclusive of the various categories of beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

ADA has a policy on, and extensively uses feedback mechanisms in project implementation. This provides for a complete loop of communication between the field, programs and management. In essence, ADA employs four key steps in providing beneficiary feedback: design, implement, collect data and analyse; provide feedback through an agreed feedback loop. In this project: 1. ADA will use available procedures in previous projects to adapt to the feedback needs of the current project, by involving beneficiaries on how best they would like their feedbacks to be recorded, analysed, and handled in a manner that is satisfactory to them. This will be area-specific and handled with utmost confidentiality and practicality. The modalities used by ADA include suggestion boxes, phone calls, use of SMS, face-to-face meetings, focus group discussions, project implementation committee appointees, complaints and complements forms, and questionnaires. Beneficiaries make their choice and this informs the course of action. 2. At the implementation level, ADA will agree with the beneficiaries on how to participate in collecting/gather complaints and complements realistically and in a timely, realistic manner, meeting the needs of beneficiaries in specific locations (Ayod, Pigi, Maiwut, Fangak, and Nyirol) 3. ADA will agree with beneficiaries on how data collected (complaints and compliments) will be analysed, by whom and at what frequency to provide needed feedback. This stage includes concrete action points on matters raised and possible redress or way forward 4. ADA will agree in advance on how the actions generated can be shared internally or externally to create the needed change or course of action that was desired by beneficiaries. At this stage, the communities are informed of necessary changes, by when and how. ADA will use the beneficiary feedback to demonstrate its respect and promotion of human rights, foster good cooperation with affected communities, empower rights-holders, build trust and stronger relationships with project stakeholders. Also, the beneficiaries’ feedback shall help to strengthen project ownership amongst partners, improving the chance of long-term impact. The action points will provide feedback on what is working well and not so well, hence, strengthening the organizational understanding of the context in which the project operates, including identifying unintended consequences that are impacting negatively on some stakeholders, allowing for remedial action to be taken so that these do not become a risk to the project’s success. Positive feedback motivates staffs to put more efforts to assisting the affected communities, and also help to pick up on issues such as fraud and misconduct in beneficiary communities. Finally, feedback gives empowerment and voice –capacity to challenge and hold ADA and other decision makers to account for their actions against the affected communities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

ADA through project front line staff and community volunteers will provide awareness and sensitization in the community on Protection against Sexual Exploitation & Abuse (PSEA) . ADA has staff trained on PSEA that will train and guide the front-line staff while carrying out the activities. A strong referral pathway will be built where the survivors of SEA will be referred to the concerned actors in line with cluster data management policies. ADA Juba headquarter will assist its field offices to mainstream inter-agency complaints mechanisms at community level, strengthen communication with communities, support survivors to access services through referral pathways, and related activities. The project will institutionalize PSEA minimum operating standards, and strengthen linkages to IASC thematic areas of intervention. Besides, the project will strengthen accountability to affected people (AAP) through ensuring its employees are trained and signs the PSEA code of conduct, and apply accountability and inclusion standards, taking into account gender, persons with disabilities, and mental health and psycho-social support. ADA shall establish coordination and support functions for PSEA and sexual harassment with field level protection and child-protection sub-clusters and implementing partners. The project aims at conducting an awareness raising and sensitization on the forms of sexual abuse and exploitation to community project committees, government authorities and opinion leaders in the affected communities. The project will address stigma and cultural barriers hampering reporting of PSE or SH cases through awareness raising and also provide information on who to report to and how to report preparators. ADA will map out PSEA partners and establish safe referral pathways to services providers. Finally, ADA will ensure zero tolerance policy with regard to sexual exploitation and abuse throughout its projects as being member of the protection and child-protection sub-clusters. It is considered serious misconduct and can have severe consequences, including staff dismissal and taking legal steps against perpetrators. Any suspicion of sexual exploitation or abuse of beneficiary or female employee will be reported immediately to the relevant authorities for action.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

ADA has staff trained on PSEA that will be able to capacity build other staff on how they can support the community through awareness and sensitization on prevention of PSEA in the community. Case workers and other CP staff will create more awareness on Child Protection activities and how the community should access services. ADA together with other partners in the targeted locations will sit and establish strong referral pathway systems to help the community have a clear direction on how to access services from the right partners. ADA is already having complaints and feedback mechanism system/policy in place. The system will be applied during the implementation of this project to ensure that feedbacks are received from the communities. A complaints desk will be installed at ADA PSS centres and ADA field offices to allow the community easily access them. ADA Child Protection staff are trained on data confidentiality and therefore, this will be implemented as required by the data management system. All the data and information on children and other supported adults will be kept confidential. During identification of vulnerable children, the case workers and other concerns staff plus the community volunteers will be trained on CPiE to be able to identify right people in need. This will include training on how to identify UASC and other children having high vulnerability. In summary most of the mitigation measures are dependent on ADA’s thoroughness in ensuring very good training of staff on handling people with vulnerabilities in a manner that is dignifying and satisfactory; meeting the objectives of the intervention while reducing possible risks associated with disregard of rules and procedures, ADA staffs will carryout an education awareness to the communities both adults and children to avoid moving to hazard suspected areas, ADA will also create a referral link with the concern Mine action partners to handle such situations especially in Pigi County.
