Project Summary

A Multi sectoral approach to address the underlying causes of Diseases emerging from poor hygiene and health education amongst communities in Uror , Nyirol and Duk Counties of Jonglei state requires an integrated WASH in Health and GBV strategy , with CHO planning a WASH integrated frontline intervention adapted to the vulnerabilities of the communities and local contexts. Chronic WASH needs continue to emerge in the locations of Uror, Nyirol and Duk Counties of Jonglei state due to displacement, insecurity and the lack of basic services as well as the recent flood in the areas.
The seasonality of the location marked by the end of the rainy season and onset of the dry season poses great challenges of the communities to uphold standard WASH practices due to the migratory patterns of mostly an agro – pastoral community characterized by upsurges in faeco - oral diseases due to lack of adequate clean water, sanitation facilities, displacements of populations caused by insecurity linked to livelihoods of communities.
WASH’ Health integration packages will be provided to communities and HHs affected by Acute diarrhea related to poor hygiene and flooding mostly affecting children and their families, in coordination with Health implementing partners in the counties which includes Tearfund, Hold the Child. WASH+GBV will also be considered to mainstream gender during the implementation, these activities will ensure reduction of GBV occurrences attributed to inadequate WASH facilities. CHO will further use supported schools in the graeter Jonglei state as platforms for a robust programming targeting mainly young and teenage girls.

1 - Needs

Poor WASH conditions and services in the locations of Greater Jonglei State (Duk, Uror and Nyirol Counties) play role in undermining efforts by actors and communities to reduce levels of Diseases and malnutrition and instances of GBV. The latest IPC projections indicate dire situations in food security and nutrition. High levels of malnutrition continue to emerge with the main underlying factors attributed to food insecurity, poor WASH standards and disease. Nearly half of the population requires humanitarian assistance, a trend that is mostly significant amongst females, mainly adolescent girls and elderly women. WASH surveys mainly carried out by REACH indicate low coverage of latrines, with over 60% of the populations in these areas practicing open defecation.Although food security presents a primary concern for the affected population, access to safe drinking water is insufficient resulting in many people collecting water from open surface sources such as swamps. In addition, dire economic hardships meant cut off of basic commodity chains, resulting in lack of much needed WASH NFIs such as soap and purification tablets. The affected populations lack appropriate water containers which limit the volumes that can be collected at a given time, thus increasing the number of trips for collection and elevating risk to those collecting the water (generally women and girls). Lack of containers also results in unsafe water storage, disrupting the safe water chain. (UNICEF, 2019) GBV issues are related to WASH services, communities with safe, adequate and gender appropriate WASH facilities less likely to have GBV issues. Women and girls continue to be exposed to protection threats due to insufficient WASH services, with water points located in placed deemed unsafe, and latrines without locks and lacking dignity. (Protection Cluster – UNHCR, 2019)Adolescent school girls are likely to be inconsistent in school attendance, due to lack of menstrual management supplies, thereby making them more susceptible to teenage marriages. Provision of WASH services will reduce the instances of malnutrition by preventing oral fecal diseases such as AWDs, and mitigate WASH related GBV amongst women and girls.Provision of WASH services at nutrition centers and in communities prone to malnutrition in the counties of Duk, Uror and Nyirol counties will reduce instances of malnutrition amongst HHs affected, as there’s over 30% chance of a SAM case to reccur if not followed up after discharge (ACF, 2019). CHO has presence in each of these locations implementing WASH and Education and therefore will leverage on previous and ongoing gains to achieve objectives at a minimal cost - while delivering quality programming. Meanwhile, many remote communities especially in Uror remain without clean drinking water sources, and are spending more time collecting water thereby placing girls and women at risk of sexual assault. Many community members have resorted to unclean river water instead. Hand pumps frequently break from overuse, since they provide water for both human and livestock consumption and without trained mechanics or access to spare parts to repair the boreholes they fall into disrepair and became non-usable.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members as a measure of accountability to the affected population, the following key components of accountability to affected populations are intrinsically linked and build on each other. They are applicable in all CHO’s operations and provide a framework for the practical integration of accountability into programming.1. Participation and inclusion. CHO will continuously engage communities in the implementation, assistance and solutions programming, securing their involvement while acknowledging and building on their capacities.2. Communication and transparency. CHO will provide all persons of concern with timely, accurate, and relevant information on their rights and entitlements, the roles and responsibilities of CHO and its partners, and the organization's programmes, commitments and processes.3. Feedback and response. CHO will ensure that all formal and informal communications from persons of concern, both positive and negative, inform protection, assistance and solutions programming, and that corrective action is taken as appropriate. To achieve this, CHO will: • Establish and maintain effective feedback systems (including comments, suggestions, and complaints), using a variety of communication channels that are accessible to all persons of concern and that are appropriate for both sensitive and non-sensitive feedback.4. Learning and adaptation. CHO will document, analyze, and report on feedback from persons of concern throughout the operations management cycle. Feedback will inform CHO's interventions, planning, course corrections, and evaluation.
