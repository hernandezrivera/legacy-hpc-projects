Project Summary

The project aims at minimizing potential damage and suffering of population exposed to military conflict in the Southern buffer zone. The population of 24 settlements will be enabled to
• appropriately behave and react in case their location is struck by an emergency
• have sufficient mental strength to take responsibility for their own safety and protection
• create a sustainable and self-functioning public awareness and information system.
In order to reach the above, Caritas Mariupol, will closely cooperate with relevant state authorities, international institutions and INGOs. Caritas Mariupol itself is located in the Southern buffer zone, is well-networked and has been delivering humanitarian and social services to the target region since 2014.

1 - Needs

In order to reduce the number of future victims and unnecessary suffering, it is essential that the population in affected villages and settlements knows how to assess and mitigate risks, has sufficient mental strength to take responsibility for their own protection and knows how to behave and react in case their location is struck by an emergency. This is especially important, as it takes on average at least 2-3 hours until state services are able to reach an affected location, while the deployment of rescue operations depends on the military situation at the demarcation line. Thus, at the initial stage of an emergency situation, the population can only rely on themselves, their know-how and their ability to mobilize local resources within their reach. Unfortunately, the population’s awareness of risks and ability to appropriately react in critical life-threatening situation remain extremely low, even in the fifth year of conflict. There are no established mechanisms to systematically inform and prepare affected population (with the exception of mine safety). According to a study by REACH, (https://www.humanitarianresponse.info/ru/operations/ukraine/document/reach-protection-assessment-isolated-settlements-government-controlled), isolated settlements have a high concentration of vulnerable persons: 41% of household members were over the age of 60, and 13% had a disability. Such proportions may relate to the fact that people of working age have been able to leave, while older, more vulnerable people are less likely to be able to afford relocation and cost of living in safer areas. Indeed, residents of the assessed settlements were more than twice as likely to be over the age of 60 than were IDPs as assessed by International Organization for Migration (IOM) across Ukraine, and just less than twice as likely to be over the age of 60 than the overall population of Ukraine.Correspondingly, households in isolated settlements were significantly smaller than the national average (average of 2.1 members in isolated settlements and average of 2.66 nationally). Isolated settlements also had a higher proportion of women, 61% of household members compared to 56% in the area within 20km of the contact line and a correspondingly lower proportion of children, with 14% of household members under the age of 18 in isolated settlements and 18% in the 20km area.

2 - Activities & outcomes

• Activity 1.1: Development and distribution of methodological material in cooperation with relevant state structures (Regional Department of Civil Protection, Ministry of Emergency Situation).• Activity 1.2: Provision of trainings in 24 settlements of the buffer zone via Mobile Teams of Caritas Mariupol in close cooperation with relevant state structures (Regional Department of Civil Protection and Ministry of Emergency).Result 1: Raising public awareness on correct behavior in different kind of emergency situations through distribution of information material and training• Activity 2.1. Development of a methodology for psychologists related to behavioral psychology aimed at increasing readiness of people for right behavior during emergency situations. • Activity 2.2. Psychologists of Caritas Mariupol - using the new methodology of behavioral psychology - conduct at least 72 group trainings for children and parents to improve their readiness for the correct behavior in the emergency zone (at least 3 consecutive group trainings in each settlement).• Activity 2.3. Organization of recreational and psychological rehabilitation camps for children to continue activities of Caritas psychologists outside the area of children's residence. Conduction of psychological sessions in children's camps (2 camps, each for 20 people during 14 days).Result 2: Increasing the psychological readiness of population to behave appropriately during an emergency situation and enable them to take responsibility for their own safety & security • Activity 3.1: Facilitation of preliminary round tables to bring together local authorities, regional representatives of Ministry of Emergencies and local activists, in order to reach an agreement on collaboration mechanisms (between Ministry and local authorities), as a prerequisite for the provision of a stable system of emergency preparedness.• Activity 3.2: Localisation of role of regional offices of Ministry of Emergencies by including their representatives into mobile project teams and by working out local alert and control mechanisms in each settlement. Schedule of regular educational sessions provided by Ministry of Emergency will be worked out for each settlement.• Activity 3.3: Local authorities of settlements as well as local activists will be included into the process of raising emergency preparedness of residents of their locations. They will be equipped with necessary posters and hands-outs.• Activity 3.4: Design and implementation of accountability mechanisms of local authorities towards local residents in each settlement (supervised by Ministry of Emergencies).Result 3: Creation of a sustainable and self-functioning public awareness and information system in selected settlements based on local village authorities and self-organized communities. Accountability Mechanism (AAP):The Caritas system of Ukraine has a single mechanism and procedures for feedback from beneficiaries (http://caritas.ua/feedback/). Director Caritas Mariupol is always up to date with cases of complaints that relate to our center.Caritas Mariupol also uses the following feedback tools:- boxes for on-site reviews- links to the feedback form on the site- regular PDM with open questions about beneficiaries' complaints / proposals- project evaluation process and also the process of including beneficiaries during project planning, implementation and evaluation
