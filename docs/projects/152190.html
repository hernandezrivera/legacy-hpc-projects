Project Summary

This project will target IDPs and returnees in three affected states, Yobe, Adamawa and Borno with provision of emergency shelters, Non-food Items (NFIs), transitional and shelter repair kits as protection live-saving assistance aligned with Shelter/ NFI/ CCCM Sector strategic objectives for the HRP 2019. In 2018 UNHCR continued to effectively respond to shelter needs with a total of 5,231 households, 26,155 individuals in formal and informal camps, host communities and newly accessible areas. As the number of returns and displacement increases due to military operations, there is need to provide shelter related assistance for returnees who are residing in their plots and as well as for host families whose resources have been overstretched. Subsequently acknowledging that the humanitarian situation is likely to further deteriorate; there is need to provide shelters and NFIs for life-saving emergency assistance to promote the protection, safety and dignity of affected Persons of Concern (PoCs). Despite the achievements in the past, the continuous needs for IDPs, though shaped by very different dynamics, revealed that quite a significant majority of people are living in congested camps. The recently concluded shelter needs assessment carried out in September 2018 by Shelter/ NFI and CCCM Sector actors indicated that a total of 265,332 HHs are in need of shelter assistance with 82,144 HHs in camps while as 183, 188 HHs in host communities. In 2019, UNHCR shall build upon progress being made in 2018 and proposes to respond to unmet needs of IDPs in Adamawa (Fufore, Hong, Yola North, Yola South, Mubi North, Mubi South, Madagali, Michika, and Numan), Borno (Bama, Banki Damasak, Damboa, Dikwa, Jere, Kaga, Konduga, Gwoza, Pulka, Maiduguri, Mobbar, Monguno, and Ngala) and Yobe (Damaturu Geidam, Gujba, and Potiskum) States through provision of; - 1. 10,000 Emergency shelters 2. 35,000 NFIs to IDPs in both formal and informal camps 3. 5,000 Shelter repair kits to returnees with provision of conditional CASH component of NGN 30,000 Naira adequate to cover for the cost labour and molding of mud bricks among other unforeseen needs. 4. 3,000 Transitional shelters to IDPs and returnees within host communities. All the shelters are designed to cater for Women, Men, Girls and boys, persons with specific needs and partitioned to mitigate SGBV cases. The selection of beneficiaries will be based on needs assessment and vulnerability screening in the locations at the time of implementation. Identification and verification of beneficiaries are guided by UNHCR’s Protection principles, and endorsed by community leaders, NEMA, and SEMA officials at LGA areas. UNHCR will advocate for the development of a transparent process of beneficiary selection and the prioritization of needs of vulnerable groups. The project targets the IDPs and Returnees households suffering from protracted displacement and returning home. UNHCR will work to ensure that potential beneficiaries are aware of their responsibilities during the project and of options available to them. It is important to recognize that not everyone can manage the construction/ repair of their own house and that their time might be better used to generate income. The families’ decision to participate in this programme will be achieved through sensitization meetings, detailed assessments, participatory planning and prioritizing the provision of technical training and capacity building. A community-based feedback mechanism through regular mass meetings and focus group discussions will be established to enable IDPs and Returnees to express their opinions on issues affecting their assistance need. The discussions will also provide beneficiaries the opportunity to understand the shelter selection and allocation process.

Needs

With the intensification of military operations against Boko Haram insurgent held LGA’s within Borno and surrounding states and the liberation of areas formerly under the control of Boko Haram, its estimated that the number of internally displaced persons in Borno and affected states will reach up to 400,000 IDPs in 2019. In responding to the shelter needs of IDPs, UNHCR needs to ensure sufficient and coordinated delivery of emergency shelters to respond to the immediate needs of IDPs, new arrivals and /or suddenly displaced IDPs without proper shelters residing in camp and camp like settings. UNHCR also need to aim at contributing to broader government initiatives, facilitating access to durable solutions through provision of shelter repair kits in transition towards recovery and reintegration. The UNHCR shelter repair kit complements the IDP’s efforts in reconstruction and repair of their shelters and targets vulnerable IDPs returning to their areas of origin who find their shelters either partially or completely damaged. This provides sufficient materials for a substantial beginning, which can be built upon, including a small cash grant to allow some flexibility for IDPs to supplement it according to their specific needs. UNHCR needs to advocate through the Shelter/NFI/CCCM Sector and the Protection sector for the identification of durable solutions targeting IDPs willing to resettle permanently at their present location. This will be undertaken through the development of concept notes, alignment of resources and through advocacy activities at various coordination forums at national and state level to establish formal satellite villages, supporting the issuance of land title deeds, provision of support in reconstruction, incorporating proper urban planning principles as well as a provision for agriculture to support livelihood activities. In 2019, it’s expected that returns to Borno, Adamawa and Yobe state will continue to increase. UNHCR proposes to target 265,000 IDPs and Returnees with 30,000 returnees expected from Cameroon and 20,000 returnees expected from Niger. UNHCR needs to prioritise the improvement of reception conditions, through provision of emergency shelters and NFI along the border areas particularly through Banki, Ngala, Damasak in Borno state and Adamawa and Yobe states. As part of the repatriation package, UNHCR needs also prioritise the provision of the shelter repair kit and provision of technical guidance to returnees. UNHCR needs to collaboration with the relevant ministries for reconstruction, land and housing to explore possibilities to provide long term settlement for returnees willing to resettle permanently at their present location. This initiative will aim to establish formal satellite villages supporting the issuance of land title deeds, provision of support in construction, incorporating master plan principles in settlement design, provision for agriculture while prioritizing local building practices and materials in shelter construction.

Activities

Activities or outputs: Activity 1: Shelter beneficiary selection to determine vulnerability through registration and vulnerability screening. Activity 2: Undertake proper site selection and settlement planning. Activity 3: Emergency and transitional shelters response to respond to sudden displacement in formal and informal camps. Activity 4: Procurement and distribution of shelter repair kit materials to vulnerable families in area of return. Activity 5: Provision of build back safer capacity building training for beneficiaries of shelter repair kits. Activity 6: Procurement and distribution of NFIs displaced and returning IDP households Activity 7: Increased advocacy for establishment of formal satellite cities with proper urban planning, incorporating masterplan principles for IDP’s not planning to return to areas of origin. Activity 8: Increased advocacy on access to appropriate land, housing and tenure Activity 9: Monitoring and evaluation.
