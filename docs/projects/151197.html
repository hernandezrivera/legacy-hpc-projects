Project Summary

Affected people have access to adequate protection and means of livelihoods; have improved access to quality and affordable psychosocial support (PSS) services and related assistance.
PIN has been providing PSS services to individuals affected by the conflict, and promoting activities, which enhance community cohesion and resilience. For PSS services, PIN sends mobile teams of psychologists to the conflict zone to provide free counselling to adults and children in order to promote psycho social well-being. PIN also manages a free, 24-hour psychosocial hotline, and provides trainings to local PSS practitioners and other protection partners. The team of PSS specialists, focusing on area along the contact line, alleviates, mitigates, and averts life-threatening physical and psychological harm or threats to people directly affected by the conflict. While PIN PSS services target all affected people who needs such support, two key groups are being prioritized in terms of vulnerability specifics , such as children and GBV survivors.

Needs

Conflict in Eastern Ukraine has, among other negative consequences, also severe psychological consequences in conflict-affected people’s lives. The need for psychosocial support is often viewed as not visible and not measurable as other material needs, but it definitely needs special attention in any humanitarian crisis. Psychosocial support (PSS) related risks are often linked to lack of, or limited access to, PSS support and services. Health related risks (including psychosocial condition) impact people in different ways, depending on a range of factors, including age and gender. There is a clear need for psychosocial support to all categories of conflict-affected population in the Donetsk and Luhansk provinces of Eastern Ukraine. According to data published by UNICEF in May 2018, 55 thousand children reside in the 15-kilometer zone near the contact line .The children’s morale and psychological state are low, which affect their school education negatively. As hostilities Affect social and economic lives of the children’s families, in turn, parents’ emotional behaviour involves improper parenting and negative practices causing even more harm to children. Schools lack qualified staff, and taking into account the mean age of the pedagogical staff, this problem will only get worse with time . PIN protection team confirms the UNICEF’s finding that the need for psycho-emotional support is still not fully satisfied. Constant stress and reduces memory and concentration, and harm both the ability to learn and general health. In addition, the fighting has a very negative impact on the socio-economic situation in the region. The precarious economic situation that the children’s families are often founds, also have their own negative influence on their psychological and emotional state. There is a significant need for psychological and educational assistance from those who have special training to work with children under constant stress. Special attention must be paid to the psychological state of students in schools near the front line. These professionals will help children from settlements located close to the contact line adapt to normal life with minimal negative consequences possible in their particular situations. We propose to further develop the mobile psychological groups that work with the district departments of education. The primary focus of the PSS implementation actions will be on the most vulnerable (women and children, IDPs, people with disabilities, retired large families, single mothers, low-income people, victims of the military conflict) and those most at risk living in the areas along the ‘contact line’, where daily fighting has severed their access to basic services and access to healthcare. To effectively address the needs of all affected people, PIN will include women in decision-making at all levels, promote and support women’s leadership and increase support for local women’s groups. Through its work, PIN will promote gender equality, strengthen the participation of women and men in the implementation, and promote the enhanced protection of women and girls from SGBV. There is a need to provide inclusive assistance, including psychosocial support, to LGBTIQ IDPs and conflict affected person .

Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: Affected people have access to adequate protection and means of livelihoods.Activities:1. 1. Capacity building for PSS practitioners (school psychologists, educators, social workers, volunteer psychotherapists, local and international organizations, etc.)Rationale: PIN will strive for building capacity of local partners and counterparts as a means to increase access to aid and ensure meaningful transition and exit strategies, which are part of a process of moving from emergency to rehabilitation and development. This activity will target identified community specialists in the Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts, especially along the line of contact in GCA. An assessment will identify their neds to improve their technical competencies concerning PSS services, including through workshops on PSS, Minimum standards for child protection in humanitarian actions). This will equip local staff/specialists to improve their own services and continue applying the newly acquired competencies independently, beyond the project intervention phase. Such improved capacity will have long-term effect for their further work and for the well-being of beneficiaries.2. 2. Information Dissemination (GBV)3. Although PIN has been implementing PSS programs that have informed target communities about PTS and risks of abuse and violence, these interventions are still new and need some time to be instilled in the local culture. People may often be hesitant or shy to apply for PSS service. Those with risk of abuse and violence are most likely to hide and stay “invisible” for PSS services. Hence, awareness raising events and information sessions have proved to be critical tools to not only increase public awareness on those issues, but also encourage people to apply for help. To this end, PIN will utilize its PSS hotline and the capacity of mobile psychologists’ team, who have experience in addressing PSS needs of gender-based violence (GBV) survivors.Outcome 2: Affected people have improved access to quality and affordable services.Activities:1. 1. Providing Psychosocial Support through Child Friendly Spaces (23 CFS, created by PIN), Group Sessions, or Outreach;2. 2. Providing Individual Psychosocial Support (psychological)3. Rationale: As an extension of 2017/2018 programming, PIN will continue to provide psychosocial support for children and parents, adults in vulnerable communities in the form of mobile teams of psychologists, which will bring such PSS services (awareness raising sessions, individual/ group consultations/events) to the field primarily across the LoC. The cognitive and psychosocial programs will be directed to stabilize and improve the psycho-emotional state of children and adults, reduce the negative consequences of the military conflict (anxiety, aggressiveness, neuroses, imbalance, fear, apathy and etc.).PSS mobile teams will offer sessions and consultations with a focus on early warning signs of GBV, non-violent ways of communication, strategies for response in the case of violence. Participants will also receive confidential options for psychosocial support, including individual consultations/group therapy, while also utilizing referrals to special centers and other local institutions supporting women in need. PIN’s experience with such activities and partners’ observations prove the need of many women whose hard social-economic conditions may trigger GBV risks. PIN PSS services to GBV survivors will complement interventions of other international NGOs providing GBV response programming, while closely coordinating with them.
