Project Summary

Jami Al Hakeem Foundation is a nonprofit, non-governmental organization, registered with the Corporate Affair Commission (CAC) in 2008 has been operating in North-east Nigeria since its inception, delivering humanitarian assistance in conflict-affected areas. J-HF works closely with its national partner organizations in GBV and WASH in BAY State
IDPs face critical challenges in accessing WASH services due to overcrowding at water points and latrines, long walking distances to WASH facilities, dilapidated sanitation facilities, and inadequate water sources across several camps. WASH non-food items (NFIs) remain a critical need, with the majority of women and girls lacking access to menstrual hygiene management kits and services. Open defecation in camps, which contributes to health risks (including the outbreak of water-borne diseases such as cholera), will likely increase with new displacement and the influx of people into camps. Furthermore, GBV continues to be a major protection concern facing IDP women and girls in north-east Nigeria. The protracted nature of the conflict continues to displace millions of people, the majority of whom are women and girls. They often find themselves trapped in a vicious cycle of violence, including SGBV, exacerbated by pre-existing gender inequalities. GBV incidents, especially intimate-partner violence (IPV) including sexual violence, continue to be reported across the BAY states. Following the history of cholera/AWD outbreaks in Girei, Yola South, Gombi and Demsa LGAs Adamawa state, Konduga and Jere LGAs Borno State, Damaturu and Geidam LGAs Yobe State which has led to the death of many individuals in the year 2021, also, the outbreak already started in some of the LGAs within the state as stated by the Health Sector coordination team. These has propelled J-HF to propose a 12-month project that seeks to Mainstream Protection and Strengthening Good Hygiene Practices in the affected population within the LGAs especially women and children that are living in IDP's and Returnees Communities that are prone to Cholera/AWD and other water-borne diseases while mainstreaming protection as a sub component of the project to protect women, adolescent girls and children against GBV in the target locations in BAY States.
The proposed project seeks to initiate an intervention to Mainstream Protection and Strengthening Good Hygiene Practices in IDP's and Returnees Communities in BAY States respectively, which includes Yola South, Girei, Gombi, Demsa Jere, Konduga, Damaturu, Geidam LGA's respectively; while mainstreaming protection as a sub component of the project to protect women, adolescent girls and children against Gender Based Violence in the target locations. This project seeks to establish and strengthen the capacity of hygiene promoters\volunteers in the target communities in order to raise awareness on the need for good hygiene practices. The project will strengthen the capacity of the local communities and leaders to be able to support the success of the project and take ownership during exit period for sustainability. This project will be supporting 15 health facilities and several other communities in the hard-to-reach locations using the trained personnel as well as the government staff on ground during implementation. Furthermore, sensitization on GBV prevention, community engagement and training of non-specialized service provider on GBV prevention services.
J-HF will maintain the established Women and Girls’ Safe Spaces where integrated case management and psychosocial support counselling services are provided and will be strengthened to ensure women and girls receive the life-saving, confidential and holistic services they need to heal and recover and gain important life skills. Summarily, this project will be targeting 25,000 individuals with over 65% been children; 30% been women and the remaining 5% is men.

1 - Needs

The conflict stemming from the insurgency of non-state armed groups (NSAGs) in north-east Nigeria continues as intensely as ever. The attacks and insecurity have displaced millions of people, devastated agricultural production and other livelihoods, cut off essential services, and caused a crisis of protection. No early end to the conflict is foreseen. Some 8.4 million people(2.6 M girls, 2.3 M boys, 1.8 M women and 1.6 M men) in the north-east states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY states) will need humanitarian aid in 2022 (ocha_nga_humanitarian_needs_overview_feb2021 (1).pdf), only slightly fewer than a year ago. Of these, 2.2 million are internally displaced; 1.5 million are returnees who lack essential services and livelihoods; and 3.9 million are members of communities affected by their hosting of internally displaced people. This figure also includes the majority (an estimated 733,000) of the 1 million people in areas currently inaccessible to international humanitarian actors of the following groups: (i) internally displaced people (IDPs), (ii) returnees (former IDPs and refugees alike), and (iii) host communities or vulnerable conflict affected population According to Humanitarian Need Overview 2022, About 5 million people are still in dire need of humanitarian health assistance. Among the key risks are the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic (https://www.humanitarianresponse.info/en/operations/nigeria/health). The BAY states have the highest maternal mortality rate in Nigeria, estimated at 1,549/100,000 live births (https://www.cdc.gov/reproductivehealth/index.html). Approximately 18% of IDPs (396,000 people), 17% of people in host communities (663,000) and 23% of returnees (345,000) do not have sufficient access to safe water for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene. The high prevalence of GBV is a key challenge in the BAY states, especially for female IDPs, who are frequently exposed to sexual violence, or forced into survival sex and forced/early marriages. Mental health challenges are also rife in the BAY states, with statistics indicating that around 20% of affected people in humanitarian emergencies develop mental health conditions. Women, girls, and children face grave protection risks including abduction by NSAGs, impeding access to critical services including health facilities especially those cited outside camp/ living areas.

2 - Activities

Activity 1 - Establishment and training of male and female hygiene promoters in target locationThis will be achieved by identifying hygiene promoters and volunteer’s resident in the community. They will be trained on personal hygiene which includes menstrual hygiene, health talk and mainstreaming of Covid 19 prevention and response. These volunteers will be responsible for carrying out sensitization and focus group discussions on personal hygiene. Activity 2 – Information Education and Communication Materials Hygiene promotion leaflets\ flyers distribution Information Education and Communication materials will be developed and disseminated during sensitization this will enable beneficiaries access to user friendly awareness guide. Activity 3 -Hygiene promotion house to house visit Sequel to the training of Hygiene promoters and volunteers, a house to house visit will be conducted to sensitize the households on the importance of maintaining good personal hygiene while distributing IEC materials to sustain the awareness. Activity 4 - Hygiene promotion focus group sessions Focus group discussions proof to be an effective means of passing information to the beneficiaries. in this regard, the hygiene promoters and volunteers will organise a monthly focus group discussion. key stakeholders such as women, adolescent girls, lactating mothers, and children will be in attendance. this will give us the opportunity to collect relevant feedback on the project. Activity 5 - Monitoring of hygiene promotion activities Routine monitoring and evaluation will be conducted to collect both qualitative and quantitative data. This will help the organization to assess the progress of the programme and also identify challenges that might occur during the implementation of the project. Activity 6 - Initial hygiene kit distribution Hygiene kits and PPE materials will be procured and distributed in the target community. PPE's will be kept in an identified social place where there is constant gathering such as schools, markets and religious places Activity 7 - Post distribution monitoring Post distribution monitoring will be conducted after each distribution activity to ensure compliance with good personal hygiene and covid 19 prevention protocols. Activity 8 sensitization on GBV prevention, community engagement and capacity building in the target communities Activity 9 Training of Non specialised service providers on GBV prevention services.Activity 8- SGBV Prevention and Response: J-HF will maintain the established Women and Girls’ Safe Spaces (WGSS) where integrated case management and psychosocial support (PSS) counselling services are provided and will be strengthened to ensure women and girls receive the life-saving, confidential and holistic services they need to heal and recover and gain important life skills. The WGSS will also serve as a space where ASRH programmes will be carried out through an established time and day with adolescents and women Referral pathways will be regularly updated in coordination with service providers in order to facilitate timely referrals. Confidential survivor information will be managed through the GBVIMS platforms
