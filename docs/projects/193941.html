1 - Needs

Mounting challenges in water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) in the Gaza Strip pose dire risks to the human health, environmental health and livelihoods of nearly two million residents. The Gaza Strip is WASH fragile, and an estimated one in 10 households is not connected to the piped water network. For homes in Gaza with a water connection, the Coastal Aquifer is the main source of piped water for domestic use. Over abstraction, caused in large part by increased demand due to rapid population growth, has depleted the aquifer at four times its natural recharge rate, leading to seawater intrusion and increasing the salinity of the piped water supply. Approximately 95 percent of Gaza’s piped water is unfit for human consumption due to salinity alone, and more than one-third of households reported that their piped water quality deteriorated between 2013and 2017. Gaps in household connections and low water quality mean nearly all households in Gaza purchase treated water from private vendors; it can be up to 50 times more expensive than piped water and can take up to one-third of household income, well over the UN recommended maximum of 3 percent. Approximately one in four households in Gaza is not connected to the wastewater network. Access varies drastically by location, and the majority of unconnected households are outside of Gaza’s five largest cities. Households without connections rely on Gaza’s 40,000 cesspits and septic tanks, which are often near homes. Where connections do exist, aging infrastructure is in dire need of maintenance, repair and renovations. As a result, untreated sewage leaks into and pollutes the ground water, contaminating the fragile aquifer, and aging treatment plants struggle to handle the volume of sewage they receive. Chronic power shortages further limit the regular operation of treatment plants, leading to the improper disposal of poorly treated wastewater that contaminates the coastline and infiltrates the aquifer. On going exposure to the chemical and biological contaminants in poorly treated or untreated sewage poses serious health risks to Gaza’s residents. Nearly one-quarter of known cases of illness in Gaza are water-related, and water-related diseases are the most common cause of morbidity in children. Improper and insufficient stormwater drainage systems compound Gaza’s WASH challenges. From September to April each year, the 560,000 residents in 60 communities located in Gaza’s low-lying areas are at high risk of flooding due to increased winter rainfall and large storm events, with the worst flooding occurring in rural areas.More than 93,000 people are under the risk of flooding. Floods displace thousands of people and destroy homes and assets; they also cause cesspits to overflow, contaminating streets and homes with raw sewage. Import restrictions continue to delay the implementation of critical projects designed to reduce the risk of flooding during large storm events and mitigate the impact of floods when they occur. In addition, Women bear the brunt of the hardship in flooded homes, as they are expected to continue fulfilling their domestic duties under these adverse conditions. Bayader and BeitLahia were selected because of their track records of successfully implementing similar programs within targeted neighborhoods. We assessed their capacity and together structured their scopes of work to leverage and build on their current capacities. Global Communities will conduct a competitive process for contracts to assess applicants’ abilities to achieve results cost-effectively and efficiently. Paymentdisbursements will beincremental and tied to deliverables. The project specifically aims at providing an appropriate, integrated WASH response for a total of 57,244 individuals (13,159 men, 12,942 women, 15,124 boys, and 14,439 girls and 1,580 elders) of vulnerable populations in the areas of eastern parts of the Gaza Strip to enhance sufficient access to WASH services. Targeting the most vulnerable groups namely; women, children, girls, and boys in fragile and critically under-served communities. Bayader currently has 41 programmatic staff, the majority of whom have over 5 years of experience with the organization’s diverse programming spanning emergency response and early recovery, community development and civic participation, child protection and education, and economic development.Recent work relevant to this project includes identifying target neighborhoods for project activities to connect households in Khan Younis governorate to water and wastewater networks, mobilizing participants for awareness sessions on hygiene, and preparing BOQs and tender documents for WASH and sewage rehabilitation projects. Beit Lahia was established in 1994 to support the sustainable development ofNorth Gaza Governorate. They increase community participation in their work, which spans WASH, infrastructure, agriculture, environment, education, culture, and emergency relief. Beit Lahia has partnered with international NGOs and works with 33 committees and240 volunteers across North Gaza. Recent WASH work includes supporting vulnerable communities inARAs, installing main and sub-main pipes for water and drainage networks, WASH-related rehabilitation in schools, rehabilitating kitchens and bathrooms to improve water and sanitation facilities for vulnerable households, and installing water tanks and water pumps. Their activities are frequently accompanied by WASH educational workshops and training for household members and school students.

3 - Indicators

Global Communities has extensive experience in monitoring WASH programming activities. The Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL)Plan will outline the anticipated results and the tools and mechanisms through which Global Communities and implementing partners will monitor and evaluate progress over the life of the program. The project will respond to the needs of targeted male and female beneficiaries in the targeted vulnerable areas, including children, the elderly, and people with disabilities ensuring high safety standards and Do No Harm principles. This plan will provide information about the overall approach to performance monitoring, a description of the proposed methods for collecting, analyzing, and reporting high-quality program data. Global Communities’ proposed plan will ensure that gender and youth are fully integrated into the MEAL system. Further, an illustrative learning and adaptive management plan will be put in place to promote stakeholder collaboration and capitalize on best practices and lessons learned. Monthly written reports will be submitted by the partner to the GlobalCommunities will be involved in developing both the interim and final reports to the donor. In order to assess the appropriateness and effectiveness of interventions in line with the overarching goal,Global Communities has designed a holistic MEAL approach that employs rigorous quantitative and qualitative methods to inform an array of performance indicators that include: index/composite indicators, indirect/proxy indicators, complexity-aware monitoring, standard foreign assistance indicators, as well as several custom indicators to monitor the program’s success in designing and implementing an inclusive approach to achieving sustainable WASH outcomes. Regular field visits and on going monitoring will be conducted during the implementation of activities and will be verified through team progress reports, in addition to the partner's monthly reports. In regards to the supplies, they will be purchased directly by Global Communities based on the list reviewed and shared by WASH Cluster. The installation work will be carried out by contractors that will be supervised by Global Communities and its partner site engineers on a daily basis in the field. Further, Global Communities will monitor the project activities on a regular basis to ensure that the needed equipment and materials are delivered as planned. In addition. Global Communities will closely coordinate with the Wash Cluster and other stakeholders to ensure no overlapping occurs and enhance coordination with other relevant agencies working on similar interventions. Global Communities implements projects with strict social accountability measures in involving beneficiaries in needs assessment; announcing the project using website and workshops, grievance/ complaint system, pre, and post-evaluation, monitoring and evaluation, and focus group discussions during implementation to discuss possible deficiencies/ gaps in the quality of services and challenges faced, as well as opportunities for improvement. Global Communities engages, as part of the accountability process, stakeholders including women and heads of household beneficiaries in transparent dialogues about the project’s results and impact on their lives from health, economic, and social perspectives. Also, GC adopts four principles of social accountability including transparency, feedback, participation, and monitoring and effectiveness. That said, a strict complaint mechanism s put in place allows beneficiaries to directly and through partner organizations to file complaints and express concern regarding the provided services and the level of satisfaction along with any allegation pertaining to the various aspect of the project implementation. To ensure beneficiary satisfaction, GC will maintain direct communication with local beneficiaries, which involves frequent visits to the communities to ask about their satisfaction with the activities (see M&E section below). The beneficiaries will also be provided with the phone numbers of the GC offices in Gaza and Ramallah to enable them to reach anyone in the organization in case they want to share any matters of concern about the project design, targeting, and/or implementation. Additionally, GC will inform the beneficiaries about the Complaints Box which will be placed at municipality centers, GC and Beit-Lahia Development Association’s offices in Gaza, and local councils of target communities, to submit their complaints. Based on the received complaints, GC and our partner will coordinate with the project supervisor and coordinator to check the issue and verify the complaint with the person who raised it in order to ensure an effective response. Project implementation will be modified accordingly, should the complaint be related to the implementation mechanism or modality. We have a feedback mechanism (FM) that includes a mobile phone and WhatsApp hotline number for beneficiaries, other stakeholders, and the affected population in general, to provide feedback (positive or negative). Information about the mechanism is distributed broadly through activities (i.e. poster at public areas and during outreach), we will modify program implementation to address concerns raised by affected people. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Prioritizing safety and dignity for men, women, boys, and girls, and avoiding harm by preventing and/or minimizing unintended effects of the program to both physical and psycho-social risks is a critical foundation for all programming that is guided by a protection perspective. In order to operationalize this, the four basicProtectionPrinciples will inform this project, specifically: a) Do No Harm; b)Meaningful Access; c) Accountability to Affected Populations; and d) Participation &Empowerment. As stated above, and consistent with Global Communities' standard approach to humanitarian programming, the project is designed using the Do No Harm framework, promoting the dignity, agency, and safety of beneficiaries at all times. Program activities are designed to be culturally appropriate and tailored to the specific context while preventing competition over resources. While designing the technical intervention, GC was committed to using a participatory approach and non-discrimination principles to empower the marginalized by addressing WASH barriers. To ensure successful intervention, cultural sensitivity matters were taken into consideration and were incorporated in the monitoring and evaluation plan so that unforeseen obstacles can be dealt with in an effective and efficient manner. Participation of stakeholders at the appropriate time during the program cycle is imminent; it enhances ownership, power relationships, accessibility, accountability, and sustainability. GC has ensured that identified WASH stakeholders in general and beneficiaries specifically are fully engaged in the program design. Furthermore, GC and its partners are committed to disseminating information to all stakeholders (men, women, boys, girls, people with disabilities) throughout the program to ensure informed decisions are adopted utilizing our own resources. In order to maximize its effects on beneficiaries, the project will adopt a deeply integrated protection approach (IPA) throughout all stages of the project cycle. The project partners will adhere to doing no harm by ensuring all staff in contact with beneficiaries will receive prior training on protection, so as to ensure they interact with beneficiaries in an appropriate way, respectful of supported individuals’ privacy and dignity. We will take great care to broadly disseminate information about the opportunities that the project presents. The program will guarantee access through the consistent engagement of community members to identify those who are most in need. Conclusively, we will work with Beit Lahia Development Association and Bayader on their approach to communication, i.e.how to present the program transparently in a way that mitigates conflict, anticipating perceptions of unfairness or other issues that could create conflict, and managing expectations. The project will ensure the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries by ensuring the availability of a safe water supply and creating a clean living environment in the targeted locations. The project services will be allocated based on the number of beneficiaries.
