Project Summary

According to the Midyear HRP review, Oromia regions have 4.4 million needy people, and the number of people targeted by the region is an increased compared to the original HRP 2021, because of conflict and climate-rated shocks including floods and drought the number of needy peoples increased by 7 %. Currently, communities in the Oromia region are experiencing the most severe drought following the three consecutive rainy seasons. Millions of people in Oromia have been affected by conflicts, which may also hinder people's freedom of movement as they seek reprieve from drought. Hence, according to East Hararghe zonal post-harvest assessment shows that the conflict along the Oromo and Somali border continues, and over 149,200 IDPs are living in the collective center and within the host community, and 201,553 returnees reside in the East Hararghe zone. Those who settled within host communities are engaged in some agricultural activities, while those who reside in camps or towns are reliant on daily labor, and few have started petty trading. Therefore, a total of 350,753 people are needs food and nonfood item assistance including emergency shelter and WASH, and livelihood intervention. Hence, to address the ESNFI needs of conflict affected peoples in East Hararghe zone, CARE proposed an emergency in-kind or cash for emergency shelter, emergency shelter, and NFI and shelter repairing response for the drought and conflict-affected peoples in the Oromia region. The objectives of the project are to improve the protection risks by ensuring the conflict-affected people have adequate safety, dignity, well-being, and equitable access to shelter solutions; and to strengthen access to appropriate live-saving Emergency Shelter and Non-food Items (NFI) to safeguard the health and protection of the displacement affected population. Through this project, a total of 2855 Households or 15,705 individuals will be targeted in the Oromia region and assisted with Emergency shelter, emergency shelter, NFI, and shelter repair kits

1. - Projects activities

First, CARE would be conducted introductory meetings with the local stakeholders to share the selection criteria and ensure that all stakeholders understand the project. The meetings will involve zonal, Woreda, Kebele, and crises affected IDPs representatives and CARE. Then, in consultation with woredas, the sites will be selected in the target woredas, prioritizing those with a higher number of IDPs concentration with high shelter and NFI needs and protection risks. Then the beneficiary’s identification and registration committee will be established that comprised of the relevant parties from woreda DRMO, kebele officials, representatives from crisis-affected peoples, and CARE will work with the committees. CARE with the committees’ will be started the beneficiaries targeting considering the vulnerability criteria. The targeting criteria which will be applied include separated children in need of shelter, large families with six children or more and no livelihood or other means of survival households with a high dependency ratio, Women at risk: Women 18 years old or above who are at risk because of their gender (Single mothers or caregivers, a household headed by a single female, widowed, divorced, older women, survivors of violence, pregnant and lactating. In polygamous families, the female lead of the household will be considered an independent household), Child-Headed Household, Person with physical or mental disability and/or chronic illness, older Person-Headed Household, single parent: Man/Woman of 18 years old or above, head of household with children, families who didn’t receive assistance. CARE will conduct a verification assessment to ensure that the targeting is done according to the criteria. CARE will conduct distribution of shelter repairing kits, Emergency shelters, Non-food items, and ESNFI items through in-kind or cash for 15,705 individuals (2855 Households) in the East Hararghe zone, Oromia region. 1. Provision of Emergency shelter for 714 HHs (3926 individuals) in Fedis, Gursum(OR), and Jarso (East Hararghe)woredas 2. Provision of in-kind or cash shelter repairing kits for 428 HHs (2355individuals) Fedis, Gursum(OR), and Jarso (East Hararghe)woredas. 3. Provision of in-kind or cash ESNFI kits for 714 HHs (3926 individuals) Fedis, Gursum(OR), and Jarso (East Hararghe)woredas; 4. Provision of in-kind or cash non-food items for 999 HHs(5497individuals) Fedis, Gursum(OR), and Jarso (East Hararghe)woredas. COVID-19 mitigation protocols will be set up at each distribution point, and throughout the beneficiary selection and registration process. In addition, CARE will establish a user-friendly complaint and feedback mechanism to gather the grievance of both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. CARE will provide awareness-raising training on PSEA and GBV for the project staff and stakeholders engaged in beneficiary identification, targeting, registration, and items distributions. Finally, we will conduct Post distribution monitoring: PDM will be conducted two weeks after the item’s distribution.

2.1.1 - Is it better to use cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash has a multiplier impact on the local economy by initiating the market system, and it increases the choice of the target beneficiaries and gives them the flexibility to decide based on their priorities needs. Similarly, the cash improves the control power of the target and allows for greater dignity and personal security. CVA can be implemented relatively quickly and is not reliant on logistics-heavy systems. Distribution costs for CVA are usually lower than those for in-kind items. Transfers of cash also eliminate the possibility that beneficiaries sell distributed in-kind items to buy preferred goods and services. In addition, the CVA program enhances the financial inclusion of the project beneficiaries by using financial services and products such as savings and payments.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

CARE will mainstream Protection principles while designing and implementing the project. During beneficiary targeting, selection, and item distribution, CARE will pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. In addition, the project staff will conduct regular oversight including monitoring visits to ensure access and service delivery to vulnerable groups who need special attention. The community mobilization will be held with IDP/ crises affected people’s representatives including women and will include information sharing through banners and announcements to ensure that community members are aware of the project aims and planned activities, and how to access the feedback mechanism.
