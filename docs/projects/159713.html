Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to contribute to the Health cluster objective #, 1 aligned to the HNO strategic objective # 2
More precisely, the project will emphasize on the following specific objectives :
1-Promote psycho-education and public health awareness in conflict-affected communities about youths vulnerability with regards to self-harm, risky behaviors and suicide
2- Support the responding Capacity of the 'Emergency Sawa Hotline 121'in oPt in detecting, supporting and referring youths presenting self-harm, risky behaviors and suicide vulnerability
3- Strengthen mobile clinics capacity in Area C (PMRS and HWC) in responding to youths MHPSS needs
4- Integrate MHPSS at ER department at general hospitals in both the WB and Gaza for better detection, response and referral for youths risky behaviors.
5- Enhance the specialized MHPSS provision of care to vulnerable youths at CMHCs level
The project will mainly target Palestinian youths, aged from 15 to 29 years old, presenting MHPSS concerns, due to chronic exposure to violence related to the protracted conflict. The 2014 Birzeit University study conducted in both West Bank and Gaza, on the psychosocial health of adolescents, showed that 16% of them show moderate to severe level of distress, while the 2016 UNFPA study highlighted higher rates of psychological distress in young women as age increases, due to the heaviness of social norms in the Palestinian society. Moreover and based on a mixed study conducted by MdM-CH during 2019 on the risk factors of attempted suicide patients and prevention strategies in Palestine, it was found that 52% out of the total attempted suicide patients was youth amidst 16-25 years old.
The project activities will be implemented in oPt covering the following locations : West bank : area A, B, C and H2 and Gaza strip, in an effort to reach people the most in need of MHPSS interventions, including the most vulnerable and marginalized communities.
The project proposes activities based on the 5 specific objectives above mentioned, aiming to :
1. Decrease stigma related to Self-Harm and Suicide in Palestine and increase detection and referral of vulnerable cases to specialized MHPSS care, through specific actions at community level.
2. Enhance the SAWA Emergency hotline in detecting, supporting and referring youths presenting self-harm and risky behaviors.
3.Support PMRS and HWC Mobile Clinics operating in WB and Area C in front-line screening and detection of youths at risk of self harm and/or suicide.
4. Integrate MHPSS at the ER department of general hospitals in all oPt for better detect, respond and refer of youth risky behaviors by emphasizing on mhGap intervention.
5. Enhance specialized MHPSS provision of care for youths at CMHC's and specialized hospital level.

1 - Needs

Mental Health and Psychosocial issues represent one of the most significant public health challenges across the oPt. Although the precise extend of mental disorders in Palestine is not known, the findings of a study published in 2017 (The Burden of Mental Disorders in the Eastern Mediterranean Region, 1990-2013) indicates that Palestine has the largest burden of mental disorders among the Eastern Mediterranean Region.The recently issued '2019 WHO new estimates of mental disorders in conflict settings'shows that more than one in five people (22·1%) in post-conflict settings has depression, anxiety disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder, bipolar disorder, or schizophrenia.Adapted to the Palestinian Population, and considering the flare-up of confrontation zones in the country (e.g. the Great March of Return in Gaza, the East Jerusalem Neighborhood hotspots, H2, Seam Zone, etc.) as potential theaters of risk behaviors for individuals fragilized by long-standing Mental health problems, it represents 250,026 individuals presenting life-saving MHPSS related need of intervention.The new WHO estimates also highlight that YLDs (years living with disabilities) per 1000 people for depression and post-traumatic stress disorder are in such conflict affected contexts, more than 5 times higher than the existing global mean burden of disease estimates.In parallel, field workers reported, both in Gaza and West Bank, a sharp increase of -attempted and/or completed- suicide in recent years, whilst consolidated National data still lack about this specific causes of death due to the associated stigma that leads families and health care professionals to under-report these cases.Worldwide, suicide accounts for 1.4% of all deaths and is the 18th leading cause of death (WHO, 2018). Globally, suicide is the second leading cause of death among youths 15-29 years of age (idem). The Arab police and government records report annual completed suicide rates of 1.1/100,000 to 6.2/100,000 (Karam, Hajjar and Salamoun, 2008). Recent statistics estimate that 79% of suicides occurs in low- and middle-income countries (WHO, 2018).Studies emphasized that, understanding the risk factors for suicide and suicide methods is very significant towards the development of effective preventive strategies. It is the reason why MdM-CH proceeded with an assessment study in 4 Palestinian Public Hospitals of the Southern West Bank in order to identify specific risks factors and Suicide prevention strategies in the country. The results highlighted a crucial lack in detection of people at risk, due to severe psychological distress, of risky behaviors and self-harm, as of well as to the paucity of cross-sectoral responses. It recommended consolidated, crosscutting, efforts to build a sustainable system of detection, alert and referral of people in need of urgent life-saving MHPSS interventions, and advocates for further prevention national strategies.Taking into account the study findings, this joint project between WHO and MDM-CH proposes evidence-based activities tailored to the unique and volatile Palestinian context.Thus, the main objective of this project is to enhance the capacity of services in detecting and responding to youths' most risky behavior such as self-harm and suicide ideation-plans and attempts, by scaling up prevention measures, detection, referrals and specialized treatment.The lack of suitable intervention& referral pathway at mobile clinic and ER, and the high stigma associated to suicide in Palestine are leading to a gap in responding to this urgent -currently increasing- lifesaving need.

2 - Activities

Based on the specific objectives described above (see summary) the project proposes the following activities listed bellow : - Organize national media campaign around self-harm, risky behaviors and suicide and the needs of early detection and support- Organize psychoeducation campaigns for community leaders specifically religious leaders on the importance of seeking professional help for youth with risky behaviors such as suicidal attempts and self-harm, and to fight misconception on 'Djin' and evil spirits.- Distribute brochures and leaflet about youth vulnerability to self-harm, risky behaviors and suicide- Desensitization and orientation workshops for health care providers at general hospitals on mental health specifically on misconceptions about youth risky behaviors such as self harm and attempted suicide.- Operation of the SAWA helpline (15 operators to be available 16 hours/7 days a week)- Training of 15 new helpline operators through 110 hour training, to be included in the pool of available Helpline operators- Specialized training for all Helpline operators (seniors and juniors) about youth vulnerability to self-harm, risky behaviors, suicide ideation and plans- Ad hoc & systematic clinical supervision of helpline operators for cases of self-harm, risky behaviors and suicide, 12 months- 10 days training on mhGAP intervention guideline 2.0 for MC staff, emphasizing youths vulnerability to self-harm, risky behaviors and suicide- 2 days training on detection of suicidal risk behavior for ER and MC teams.- Sensitization session to the SAWA helpline services available for Mobile Clinic Users- Provision of one clinical supervisor for each mobile clinic team- 10 days training on mhGAP intervention guideline 2.0 for ER staff, with a special emphasis on dealing with cases of self-harm, suicidal attempts and GBV.- Provide a clinical supervisor for 12 months to provide support for the ER teams.- Develop referral protocols between ER departments in general hospitals and Community Mental Health service in MoH.-Develop registry and guidelines for ER department to deal with MHPSS cases.- 10 days specialized training on youths vulnerability to self-harm, risky behaviors and suicide and specialized MHPSS interventions- Ad hoc systematic clinical supervision for self-harm, risky behaviors and/or suicidal cases.

Responding to these needs, this indicator will enable the teams reaching the desiring impact successfully. -3.750 patients with access to MHPSS services. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

At all stages of the Health Project Cycle management : Diagnosis, Implementation and M&E, protection mainstreaming is a cross cutting theme for WHO and MdM programs and projects. WHO and MDM-CH understand and fully adhere to the core principle of “Do No Harm” as a humanitarian imperative, a cornerstone in all programmes followed by the pyramid of IASC minimum standards in regards to MHPSS. Both partners remain vigilant throughout project implementation to identify and prevent unintended negative consequences of their actions. In this regard a number of steps and measures are adopted to ensure that its interventions maximize positive impacts for community stability, mainly by strengthening PIN capacities and resilience, while minimizing harm. Moreover, in order to further enhance protection mainstreaming the project will ensure that health staff will be capacitated on the following 4 topics: - MHPSS IASC minimum standards: Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings (WHO guidelines) - GBV: Minimum Standards for Prevention and Response to Gender-based Violence in Emergencies (following UNFPA standards) - Child Protection Minimum standards in Humanitarian action (following Unicef and Sphere guidelines) -Minimum Standards for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (following Sphere standards and MdM guidelines) - Disability mainstreaming and health: CBR(Community based rehabilitation guidelines) health component of WHO.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Both WHO and MdM-CH are basing their interventions in ethical and humanitarian principles. This project aligns with the principalist approach that is widely accepted in the humanitarian community through such vehicles as the SPHERE Humanitarian Charter (and SPHERE MHPSS). Ethical principles in health care include respect for persons to advance human dignity and for individuals’ autonomous choices; beneficence, the promotion of others’ well being, commonly known as “do no harm,” as described above (9.1)and justice, encompassing both the fair distribution of resources and fair processes for decision-making. Humanitarian principles is the a second source of values and norms that animate both applicants actions. HP include humanity, requiring that suffering must be addressed wherever and for whomever it is found; neutrality, the duty of humanitarian actors not to take sides in a conflict; impartiality, which stipulates that humanitarian actors must not discriminate or give preference to any nationality, race, religious belief, class, political opinion, or similar status; and independence, which demands that humanitarian actors retain their autonomy and remain independent of political or military objectives of other actors.
