Project Summary

The ongoing conflict between armed opposition groups continues to have widespread impact on communities in the Far North, particularly on their livelihoods and food security, limiting communities’ ability to meet their basic needs and exposing them to various protection risks. The proposed intervention aims to provide direct support to beneficiaries to meet their basic needs across multiple services areas. These include provision of drinking water, promotion of hygiene and sanitation, the restoration of livelihoods, and actions to promote community-based protection and prevent gender-based violence (GBV). This will be done via the following: provide holistic support to survivors of gender-based violence, strengthen the capacity of GBV service providers in case management including MHPSS, strengthen GBV prevention activities at community-level including sensitization of conflict-affected communities, local authorities, traditional leaders, and religious leaders; build the capacity of women led organization (WLO) on GBV response, prevention and coordination in humanitarian context. IRC will also conduct construction of gender-sensitive latrines in IDP sites / host communities, emergency WASH needs assessments, survey, studies and others, promotion of safe hygiene and sanitation practices amongst vulnerable people, capacity-building of WASH actors in WASH relative issues. Furthermore, IRC will be strengthening the process of issuance of civil documentation to conflict-affected people, provide targeted protection assistance to the most vulnerable people (men, women, boys and girls, PWD) and also provide legal assistance to crisis-affected people on the protection of their rights, provision of multipurpose cash as as well as grants for both agricultural and non-agricultural income generating activities.
The IRC will continue to position itself to respond swiftly to spontaneous movements with mobile services that enable access to hard-to-reach areas in persistent and precarious environments. To support sustainability beyond the project period, the IRC will also continue to reinforce local capacities to ensure a smooth exit strategy.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The Lake Chad Basin, including Cameroon, has been facing incursions and attacks by non-state armed groups (NSAGs). Since 2014, Cameroon’s Far North (FN) region has been the theater of brutal attacks by Boko Haram, causing about 608,000 people (358,000 IDPs, 115,000 Nigerian refugees, and 135,000 returnees) , and leaving 1.2 million people in urgent need of humanitarian aid. The continuous violence, leading to displacement, includes recurring incursions by NSAGs, but also clashes between communities. Indeed, recent intercommunity conflicts in the Logone-Birni division at the beginning of December 2021 have forced approximately new 60,000 persons to flee to Chad and 40,000 persons to internally flee to other areas , due to clashes over diminishing water resources. In the FN, NW/SW the IRC registered over 3,088 cases of rights violation between October 2021 to September 2022; a total of 10,612 protection incidents against civilians were recorded by the protection cluster within the same period, including physical violence, killings, kidnappings, and armed incursions of NSAG. According to Cameroon OCHA situation reports, 1,259 cases of GBV were recorded in the Far North region between January and August 2022 through the GBVIMS including denial of resources (41%), physical assaults (29%), emotional abuse (14%), rape (5%), forced and/or early marriage (4%) and sexual assaults (7%). As women and girls are often afraid to report abuse for fear of retaliation, stigma, shame, and social restrictions, the number of cases remains highly underreported. 88% of reported GBV cases were perpetrated by an intimate partner and/or member of the family. According to the protection cluster evaluation report relating to the displacements caused by flooding in Logone-et-Chari and Mayo-Danay in November 2022, among the GBV cases recorded, 25% represent denial of resources, 18% sexual assaults, 17% rape while services offered are only rated currently at 9%.The recent ‘cadre harmonise’ released on December 1, 2022, published by the West African cash working group indicates that out of Cameroon’s 27,190,906 people, 16,825,443 are in IPC phase1, 6,768,449 are in phase IPC phase 2, 3,356,781 are in IPC phase3, 240,233 are in IPC phase 4 and no persons in Phase 5 (IPC phase 5) which is the famine or catastrophic. In the IRC needs assessment, in the three targeted divisions, food appears as the first priority need for the people interviewed at 89.2% in the Logone-et-Chari, 57.8% in the Mayo-Sava and 60.8% in the Mayo-Tsanaga . Among the displaced people in the Far North region, 44% of them cite access to potable water as a priority need before access to food and health care . 86% of IDPs report drinking water from a borehole, 31% from unprotected wells and 25% from rivers , but even when populations have access to a borehole, water quality issues have been reported by 61% of interviewees . In order to respond to the most urgent needs of the displaced and vulnerable host communities and given the continued context of insecurity in the Far North, the IRC in Cameroon has prioritized lifesaving and life-sustaining assistance through a multi-sectoral, coordinated response to address needs in WASH, protection for vulnerable individuals, emergency basic needs and economic recovery. The most vulnerable groups impacted by the crisis will be supported to access critical services and avoid negative coping strategies through both static and mobile services as the IRC remains agile in the volatile context to address spontaneous displacements or newly arising needs, all in coordination with other actors and local authorities and community structures. The multi annual programming will help to build and strengthen local capacities and community resilience to face future hazard and shocks.
