Project Summary

The principal objective of the project is to restore water access for 4,381 beneficiaries in Mellit locality in North Darfur.
4,381 individuals will benefit from restoring life-saving WASH services including the rehabilitation of hand dug wells, boreholes, MWYs and WYs coupled with hygiene and sanitation activities and efficient VWUC trainings.
Training project staff and LHP on CLTS facilitation, CLTS triggering and post triggering follow-up will be conducted in 4 villages in the targeted locality.

Needs

Key findings from COOPI KAP surveys conducted in specific areas within Mellit shows that water availability is always below 15 liters per person per day (with an average of 9.68 liters). During the dry season the average time to reach a water source is between one and three hours and waiting time at the water source is regularly between one and three hours. Over one third of the respondents do not own a separate container to store water at their house. Open defecation is a common practice for over 95% of the respondents. Use of water and soap for hand washing is still not a common practice at critical times including before food preparation and consumption, after use of latrines and care of children. Malaria and diarrhea have been identified as the most common diseases affecting children and adults, but their cause is mostly unknown among the targeted populations.COOPI Water Resource Mapping exercise identifies areas with a higher number of non-functional water sources (water yard, mini water yard and hand pump boreholes). Those areas should be prioritized for rehabilitation campaigns as well as critical interventions to strengthen the VWUCs.

Activities

COOPI will improve access to water for 4,381 individuals through the rehabilitation and upgrading of 6 critical water infrastructures in prioritized villages.4,381 individuals will meet the minimum requirement of 15lt/person/day by rehabilitating and upgrading 1 WYs, 2 MWYs, 3 Hand Pump BHs whose repairs are beyond the capacities of the communities. People living in the villages nearby the rehabilitated schemes will benefit as well from the planned activities, as additional functional water schemes will reduce the pressure and breakages of the nearby water sources.
