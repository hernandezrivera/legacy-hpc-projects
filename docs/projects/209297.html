Project Summary

Multi-sectoral interventions including Protection( GP, CP, GBV), WASH, Education, Energy & Environment and Food Security & Livelihoods (FSL) interventions for Refugees in Khartoum, Blue Nile, Al Jazirah, North Kordofan, South Kordofan and West Kordofan States. The total targets are 18906 beneficiaries (12289 children, 4727 Adults, 1891 elderly and 1891 PWD). PROTECTION intervention (GP, CP and GBV): AORD will Provide legal counselling and assistance for 140 survivors and raise awareness on general protection topics for 1780 beneficiaries, establish 32 Community-Based Structures (51 % from the females), Capacity building for 380 service providers on general protection topics and Provision 180 individual protection services (cash or in-kind) to increase the income for the targeted beneficiaries. AORD will support 2530 individuals through the distribution of direct material Support (dignity kit) 60 kits for identified SGBV survivors assisted with appropriate support. AORD will conduct 15-community awareness raising to raise awareness about GBV and support 160 GBV survivors by receiving individual case management and Capacity building on GBV topics for 45 people. AORD will support 2276 children through the provision of Case Management and referral services for 96 children. Run and Maintain two child-friendly spaces with furniture, stationaries and equipment (Solar Power System, computer) and assistance to 60 children with disability by providing direct services as well provide mental Health and Psychosocial Support services to 15 cases to improve their care and well-being. AORD for NFIs targeting 2100 persons supported by NFI kits, Tent and cash/vouchers. The project will support energy and Environmental support for 140 HHs targeting them by Providing alternative cooking fuels such as ethanol, Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) and bagasse to households, Providing HHs with clean and sustainable energy solutions such as solar cookers, fuel-efficient stoves, Provide training for to use clean energy sources, Providing solar torches/ lamps for household lighting. The project will support WASH intervention for 3720 beneficiaries through Water supply by water trucking for 140 HHs, Operating and maintaining 16 water sources (including water quality treatment and monitoring, rehabilitating shared communal latrines 16 (multiple households), conducting hygiene promotion in communal setups accompanied by the Provision of Personal Hygiene Materials.
AORD will improve the education environment by implementing educational activities for 3160 children, AORD support 400 Teachers by providing training, 1400 School furniture sets will be provided to primary schools and 16 classrooms will be rehabilitated in primary and secondary schools supported by 24 Hand washing facilities inside the targeted schools. AORD will support 1100 beneficiaries through FSL interventions through the provision of agricultural support for 320 persons and livestock support for 320 persons. AORD will conduct vocational and skills training for 320 people and provide support activities for 140 youth.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Escalating conflict and insecurity, pre-existing crises, and deep-rooted chronic child protection concerns have had serious, long-lasting, harmful effects on hundreds of thousands of girls and boys and their families in Sudan. The Protracted displacement, loss of assets, abuse, physical and psychological trauma, fractured societal structures, and chronic long-term economic, social, and health problems negatively impact not only children and their families but also child protection organizations themselves and their ability to deliver life-saving child protection assistance. Children with disabilities are constrained and challenged to access humanitarian services due to the limited data availability on how they are impacted and the lack of accessible information on the available services. In 2024, AORD is committed to adhere to the Child Protection Area of Responsibility (AoR) and maintain a community-based approach in its interventions and will continue to tailor the response to the fast-changing situation and the emerging needs through the following modalities: uniformed and functional child protection information management system, strengthened sub-national coordination mechanism, localization strategy, enhanced intersectoral and interagency coordination, and remote modality where access is a constraint. Protection will be incorporated across all sector interventions to promote meaningful access, safety and dignity during the implementation of the project. The approach will be based on Protection Principles as per the Humanitarian Charter and Minimum Standards of Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (CPMS). In addition, AORD will focus on engaging with organizations of persons with disabilities in humanitarian action to establish systemic, meaningful, mutually reinforcing collaboration and to strengthen reach to more marginalized populations. Moreover, efforts in local empowerment and ownership of community-based child protection networks (CBCPNs) in all the targeted states shall be given due priority, including hard-to-reach areas.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

AORD is committed for whom it provides humanitarian assistance to be effective, and have clear, concise communications, input into planned activities, feedback on the provision of humanitarian assistance, and participation in decisions affecting their lives are all important. AORD will ensure AAP is in line with humanitarian standards. To ensure “Do No Harm”, methods of receiving feedback confidentiality will be identified and communicated to community members appropriately to avoid exposure to further risks from sensitive issues. As appropriate, mechanisms and approaches will be tailored to the needs of both Refugees and host communities. AORD is committed to aligning with the CHS focusing on standard # 5 Community feedback mechanism and response. To ensure the quality and accountability of the project, AORD will promote transparency, meaningful participation and inclusion through the following steps: 1. Enable the targeted populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence 2. Share project information at an early stage and on an ongoing basis with girls and boys, parents and caregivers, community leaders, and government stakeholders; 2. Host regular consultations/ listening forums with established groups, including women, boys and girls; 3. Ensure partner communities are fully aware of PSEA, Protection and Safeguarding Policy including how to make complaints or raise issues. 4. Share information on progress and lessons in relevant coordination groups and key messages on child participation 5. Ensure a robust and accessible complaints mechanism and response procedure is put in place and appropriate for different groups. The mechanism and timelines for expecting responses and how complaints and feedback are managed to inform programming; 6.Ensure that all staff, and stakeholders, are familiar with existing referral pathways both to service providers for supporting identified cases and referring to other agencies. In facilities, posters and leaflets will be posted and shared on program objectives, key activities, and ways in which beneficiaries can provide feedback to the implementing partner. Feedback will be collected where appropriate via FGDs, interviews, boxes, and a dedicated call-in number and meetings. Compliance boxes at the community level are the mechanism put in place by AORD for suggestions and complaints(SGBV), ensuring proper periodic review, analysis, reporting, and documentation. Through capacity building and referral pathways, AORD will increase knowledge of AAP, GBV, and disability among service providers and community workers.
