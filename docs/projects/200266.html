Project Summary

The education sector is paralyzed and hurtling from crisis to crisis. This position is underpinned by a lack in human, financial, and managerial resources. In 2023, the sector continues towards ultimate collapse, leaving 520,000 children at risk of losing out on quality learning. RMF’s proposed project adopts a rights-based approach to rescue education for all, through securing safe and protective access of children (Lebanese, no-Lebanese, in the first and second shift schools, dropped out / never schooled) to learning opportunities using multiple modalities for maximizing the benefit to children, and ensuring the highest reach at the lowest possible cost. Within the ERP framework, an integrated programming is followed, stressing on the right of schooling seeking:
-Further involvement of existing entities in referring children to public formal education or Multiple Flexible Pathways.
-Increased access to an integrated package of quality services that contributes to learning retention of 1,140 OOS and OOL.
-Enhanced capacity of 750 caregivers, 216 teachers in formal education, 12 school principals and 180 educators in non-formal education for supporting children’s education.
-Larger engagement of 200 children with disabilities in educational programs.
-Use of the SEL and PSS frameworks/ observation tools, and digital tools and resources
To achieve this, RMF will be working throughout both ERP’s outcomes through the following model:
Outcome 1: Activities will be adapted in-line with the mitigating the crises’ impacts, ensuring that children are receiving equitable, safe, and inclusive access to quality learning opportunities considering the Social emotional framework aspects. RMF’s socio-ecological approach focuses on engaging the community, schools, and families, to mitigate the learning losses efficiently and effectively. This will be achieved through:
-Organizing Back-to-Continuous Learning campaigns in the targeted three governorates for advocating and communicating efforts through creating a map identifying all active stakeholders and NGOs to get out-of-school (OOS) and out-of-learning (OOL) children, back to education/learning. RMF, will rely on existing QUDWAs in targeted communities to act as active advocators.
-Providing an integrated package of services for 1,440 children registered in grades 3, 4, 8, 9, 11 and 12 in both morning and second shift schools, who are struggling to learn including. This includes cash for education, healthy snack pack, and retention support classes.
-Providing 900 OOLs aged 8-14 with foundational literacy and numeracy classes.
-Involving 200 children with disability in formal and non-formal education, through the provision of assistive technologies and devices that enable their access to education and working on differentiating approaches and adding tools and adaptive materials.
-Supporting 12 schools in Akkar, North Lebanon and Mount Lebanon in covering their operational cost for guaranteeing continuous education.
-Offering low-cost support solutions for 1,440 children to link them with the education support platform established by UNICEF in partnership with Teach for Lebanon, to receive the needed support while studying.
Outcome 2: Its outputs are cross-cutting with Outcome 1 and they complement each other, especially that RMF will be closely collaborating with CERD for offering 216 school teachers, 12 school principals and 180 educators in non-formal education with several capacity building trainings, focusing mainly on approaches to teaching and learning in crisis time, as well as social and emotional learning. Finally, 1,440 targeted schooled children will be benefiting from extra-curricular activities and SEL/ PSS through establishing and equipping 12 school clubs; whereas 900 OOL children will be participating in community based psycho-social support activities SEL as a means for increasing resilience. Furthermore, RMF will be organizing 12 school-led SEL initiatives.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

To meet the project’s objectives, in holistically implementing a recovery program for most Vulnerable Out-of-School and Out-of-Learning children, by engaging all the components comprised within the education sector ecosystem, RMF will:•Organize Back-to- school Campaign to mobilize resources and introduce innovations that will improve the quality of the learning environment and catalyze the reconstruction of sustainable education systems over the long term•Provide cash assistance to targeted children•Procure and deliver healthy snacks for enrolled children •Provide retention support sessions•Provide Foundational Literacy and Numeracy classes•Provide children with disabilities, with assistive technologies•Conduct awareness sessions to caregivers aiming to improve their involvement in the education of their children•Cover operational costs of the 12 targeted schools•Support children with low-cost solutions for inclusive remote learning•Provide capacity building trainings for teachers/ educators in both formal and non-formal education based on RMF’s relation with CERD•Conduct trainings on different teaching and learning materials and classroom instructional approaches•Conduct SEL/PSS activities to all targeted children whether in formal or non-formal education•Organize School-led SEL initiatives with the involvement of parents and caregivers•Establish SEL/PSS School Club as a sustainable platform for ensuring the implementation of SEL/PSS curricula endorsed by CERD.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

RMF is committed to taking all necessary actions to prevent and respond to sexual misconduct and to put the protection, rights and dignity of participants at the forefront. PSEA and child safeguarding are embedded in activities through policies which provide staff and partners mandatory requirements, awareness and trainings for the protection from violence, abuse and sexual exploitation. RMF also adopts a gender transformative approach to ensure gender equality and inclusion and aims to reshape unequal gender and power relations. The intervention approach includes gender and inclusion analysis, using Gender Transformative Marker to identify areas to be strengthened tackling gender inequality & exclusion. RMF ensures PwD accessibility, sensitization of staff and communities on disability inclusion including identification and mitigation of barriers to physical and psychological inclusion. Data disaggregation include disability, gender, age, and diversity considerations.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

To ensure Accountability to Affected Population, RMF has an age- and gender- appropriate feedback and complaint mechanism (FCM). All RMF staff are trained on this mechanism. At the beginning of project implementation, FCM is adapted, through consultations with stakeholders, children and project participants to select appropriate channels, to fit the purpose and context of the project (geographic location, situation…), and to be aligned with existing mechanisms within the organization. In case of remote implementation, the main channels that will be used include hotline, WhatsApp and surveys. Following consultations, a process will be set to ensure tracking feedback and complaints, referring to relevant focal persons and closing the feedback loop in a timely manner. Additionally, RMF will conduct a FCM survey with all the project beneficiaries to assess their satisfaction with the services and assistance provided.
