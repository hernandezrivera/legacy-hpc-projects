Project Summary

TADO recognizes that ,In South Sudan, the vast majority of women and girls will survive at least one form of gender-based violence (GBV) — be it rape; sexual assault; physical assault; forced/early marriage; denial of resources, opportunities or services; or psychological/emotional abuse. Many categories of GBV are pervasive and ingrained in the social fabric of society. All tribes and geographic regions have some differences in terms of prevalence, but the thread of GBV sadly runs throughout the country, with bride price as a cornerstone of the nation’s economy.
GBV is rooted in discriminatory social norms and power inequalities between men and women in social, economic and political spheres of life. In a number of contexts involving armed conflict globally, rape and sexual assault has been used as a tactic to humiliate, intimidate, displace and traumatize communities Gender-Based Violence (GBV) – domestic violence, forced and early marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), abduction, discriminatory practices within the legal system and harmful traditional practices – is a persistent and serious problem in South Sudan.
The project seeks to target 10800 direct beneficiaries with a percentage of 65%women and girls, 20%men and boys and 15% people with disability in Aweil East, Gogrial East, Tonj South and Tonj North Counties in South Sudan, with the aim of supporting and championing gender-based violence prevention and response activities such as, Establishment of Women and girls friendly spaces, case management , PFA/PSS ,Legal awareness, assistance and counseling, Protection awareness-raising and prevention messaging. The project will directly contribute to protection cluster strategic objectives in the area of improving the prevention of violence against women and girls through changes in knowledge, attitudes, and practices. This will be achieved through specific result areas of; 1) Improved ability of community based structures such as local leaders, chiefs, women leaders, women, teachers and youth groups to prevent, mitigate and respond to GBV concerns ,formation or use of already existing groups, structured and unstructured training programmes targeting men and women, regular monitoring and mentorship support to established structures, as well as support of existing GBV risk mitigation strategies such as GBV risk assessments and patrolling in suspected unsafe places. 2) Improved knowledge and attitudes of women, girls, men, boys and duty bearers towards GBV prevention and redress mechanisms through vigorous awareness campaigns such as community based dialogues involving men and women, Psychotherapeutic /counseling interventions, participatory community engagement in line with AAP principles through performance arts such as music, dance and drama, in school children engagement through debates, music, dance and drama, use of radio talk shows as well as well as IEC materials. 3) Community bylaws and state policies in place that protect women and girls against violence. This will be realized through influencing policy at community and state level on early/forced through advocacy meetings with key stakeholders as well as training of key duty bearers such as chiefs, Payam County and state including referrals to legal aid. The intervention will seek to contribute to the joint development of GBV Standard Operating Procedures (GBV SoPs) in partnership with other actors and GBV working Groups in the project locations. This will contribute to streamline the manner in which GBV operations are carried out so that there is the coherence of the quality of GBV services. In addition the project will seek to distribute fuel efficient cooking stoves to female headed households targetting especially young mothers.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is one of the few countries in the world where it’s worse in dis-empowering when growing up as a female. According to Care - Girl has no right report 2014, the vast majority of women and girls survive at least one form of gender-based violence (GBV) — be it rape; sexual assault; physical assault; forced/early marriage; denial of resources, opportunities or services; or psychological/emotional abuse. Many categories of GBV are pervasive and ingrained in the fabric of society while many see gender-based violence (GBV) as a normal part of life and the burden of women and the acceptance of violence towards women are rarely challenged by men or by communities In Gogrial East, Tonj South, Tonj North and Aweil East, all tribes and geographic regions have some differences in terms of prevalence, but the thread of GBV sadly runs throughout the country, with bride price as a cornerstone of the nation’s economy. GBV affects men, women, boys, and girls, but it disproportionally affects women and girls. According to data collected by the Gender-based Violence – Information Management System (GBV-IMS) in South Sudan during 2016, approximately 98% of reported GBV incidents affected women and girls. In addition to gender, age and disability can increase risks to GBV, with adolescent girls, unaccompanied children, elderly women and persons with disabilities among the populations most at risk. GBV in South Sudan is not just a matter of concern, it has reached epidemic levels. 1 in 2 women has experienced intimate partner violence and 1 out of 4 reported cases of conflict-related sexual violence as a child. The scale and severity of GBV in South Sudan negatively impact the protection, health, and development of individuals and the nation. It requires humanitarian action that responds across multiple sectors to immediate needs while contributing to prevention and the establishment of conditions for recovery and longer-term initiatives. This project seeks to contribute towards mitigation of the current aggravated GBV situation in the country while ensuring that communities actively participate in GBV prevention and risk mitigation activities .The 2013 and 2016 conflicts have further aggravated the GBV situation and UNFPA estimates that 24,500 South Sudanese women and girls have been exposed to sexual violence from 2013 to 2018. The Transitional Constitution and Bill of Rights (2011) provides guarantees for the equality of men and women. It recognizes the historic inequalities between women and men in South Sudan and sets out a 25% Affirmative Action quota for women in legislative and executive bodies though its actual implementation is weak.Since the intensification of internal armed conflict in 2013, humanitarian GBV programming has been concentrated in but not limited to the UNMISS POC sites with more focus being centered on reactive protection/GBV response. Little to no attention has therefore been paid to the prevention of GBV through activities such as awareness creation and training of a spectrum of stakeholders. Proactive approaches are urgently required in order to inculcate the ethos of gender equality in the fabric of the community , improve AAP and prevent SEA..Raising awareness of the different forms of violence against women and building of reliable and sustainable mitigation structures is, therefore, an important element in the prevention of violence against women, This project will adopt an are based approach and work with other sectoral partners on ground to intergrate GBV risk mitigation approaches in WASH, Health, FSL, CCM and Education.Further, the project will spearhead the joint development of GBV standard operating procedures (GBV SoPs) in partnership with other actors to guide the provision of coherence of quality GBV services in the target areas and beyond.

2 - Activities

The overall response to GBV in relation to the levels of need remains inadequate in reach, quantity, and quality. The majority of women and girls, men and boys at risk for GBV in South Sudan currently do not have access to even basic life-saving GBV services. Although collectively there are services available in each sector, a multi-sector response is not available in most locations. Prevention and risk mitigation interventions are also not sufficient. This project proposes to contribute to GBV interventions through the following activities: Project Activities:1. Organize & participate in protection assesments /risk assesments and safety audits in Aweil East, Gogrial East, Tonj South and Tonj North counties in partnership and coordination with all Humanitarian actors in WASH, Health, Education , CCM and FSL. 2. Conduct case management and fiollow up targeting boys, girls, men and women.3. Conduct GBV Risk mitigation activities including provision of fuel efficient stoves to women and girls. 4. Training and capacity building of frontline workers on case management and safe referrals.5. Strengthening referral pathways including organizing outreaches to sensitize communities on the use of the referral pathway6. Distribution of dignity kits and providing education on GBV and WASH issues to women of reproductive age in the target communities. 300 kits to be distributed.7. Provision of psychosocial support focusing on community-based and non-specialized support through women and girl’s friendly spaces ,existing groups and home visits.8. Capacity building on Income Generating Activities for GBV survivors including direct support and referrals to access livelihood opportunities . This activity will be implemented in close collaboration with FSL cluster partners .9. Organize GBV prevention dialogues and activities among the stakeholders to change harmful social norms causing GBV risks. This will include carrying out community awareness and campaigns through FGDS, Drama, talkshows on GBV consequences, available services, and risk prevention and mitigation.10. Distribute fuel efficient cooking stoves to female headed households, targetting vulnerable mothers, survivors of GBV.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, Since National Conflict 2013, Aweil East, Tonj South, Tonj North and Gogrial East are relatively stable, with access roads and security improvements. Government services are inadequate but exist to some extent. TADO has conducted protection risk assessments in coordination with other protection partners in the area and is keen to employ humanitarian principles of do no harm and encourage communities through AAP activities to adopt durable solutions and positive coping mechanism which protect women and girls from harm.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. TADO will deliberately and pro-actively Increase engagement with local actors, including authorities and NNGOs to both strengthen the local networks for humanitarian response and at the same time inculcate ownership of the interventions. Local partners and local authorities will be encouraged to take an active role in the implementation and sustainability of the project as a core exit strategy. Beneficiaries will be expected to take a lead role in the ownership of the interventions. In principle, the exit point for emergency assistance is the moment when the needs which initially justified the intervention have been satisfactorily and durably addressed. This may be in absolute terms, meeting local or global humanitarian standards; relative to the capacity of humanitarian interventions to address such needs; or may take into account other donors, partners, and local capacity, and their respective comparative advantages. In practice, however, time constraints of emergency funding will often dictate the end of the assistance. Consequently, emphasis on the durable outcomes of emergency assistance, and on establishing synergies with development and/or other social protection programs, will be a priority for TADO from the outset. TADO will prioritize interventions that will move the beneficiary communities from emergency to recovery involving participatory planning and implementation processes to identify priorities and to optimize capacity building, ownership and sustainability by beneficiaries.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

TADO embraces AAP principles in all programming and will partly use a complaint and feedback mechanism to achieve its AAP objective .. At the onset the target communities will be brought on board through beneficiary project committees to plan, monitor and evaluate the project. Project planning and budgeting will be a shared responsibility between project team, local authorities and the local communities. Before the selection of beneficiaries, communities and authorities will be informed about the selection criteria, target population and the support they will receive from the project. Communities will also be informed about the funding, implementation and reporting modalities of the project. TADO will conduct training for Beneficiaries in project accountability processes including but not limited to the formation of Beneficiary Feedback and Accountability committees, who will follow through and participate in planning and implementation as well as monitoring of project activities. The committee will be properly constituted taking into account gender, age, regional as well as special considerations or needs. The beneficiaries will be given orientation in protection and the “Do no harm” principles and awareness created to ensure that they can fully participate in key decision making processes on the project. Beneficiaries will be instrumental in the identification of needs and in determining the appropriate locations for implementation. They will be actively involved in giving feedback to the project team through direct engagement with the Beneficiary Feedback mechanisms and also through the Beneficiary Accountability Committee.TADO collaborates with the local community especially during assessments, where the communities are hired as part of the assessment team.
