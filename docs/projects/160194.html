Project Summary

Despite the signing of the revitalized peace agreement last year South Sudan still faces one of the world’s most dire humanitarian crises as a result of the 5 years brutal war that has left 1,465,542 internally displaced persons (IDPs) , of whom 182,112 are living in protection of civilian (PoC) sites. Due to dire needs identified throughout its previous Child Protection activities implemented in 2019, and in line with the desire to implement life-saving activities, enhance protection and strengthen coping capacities with an eye to support the latter, this proposal aims to work in hard-to-reach areas where UNCDR has identified compounded risks and observed the impact of protracted periods of under-servicing, denoting the priority of this intervention. More specifically, UNCDR proposes a response that is static in 2 locations: 4 locations in Maiwut (Jekow, Jotome, Maiwut and Pagak) and Fashoda (Dethok, Kodok, Kodok Town and Lul) with regular field-based roving activities to other hard-to-reach areas.
This project will be targeting 10,000 IDPs, host communities and returnees in Maiwut & Fashoda of Upper state with significant impact aiming at improving continuation support at helping children and young people affected by armed conflict. With provision of comprehensive Case Management and FTR, process, organize structural and semi-structural and strengthening access to community led Child Friendly Spaces, Psycho-social Support, developing healthy and safe coping mechanisms to reduce the risk of children and adolescents resulting in recruitment by armed groups/forces, becoming street working children, or being left without care takers in hard-to-reach of Maiwut and Fashoda counties as prioritized areas, improve protection of conflict affected males, females under the age of 18 years and internally displaced children, especially separated and unaccompanied children are better protected through: 1) comprehensive case management system and provision of dignity kits and life-saving material assistance; 2) building the capacity of the community-based Child Protection network on mechanisms and approach; 3) improve Referral system for GBV and CP cases; 4) provide alternative care support to children without care givers, conduct Family Tracing and Reunification; 5) CP mainstreaming into Education, WASH, Health, Nutrition and FSL and conducting awareness raising to prevent and respond to child protection issues.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has experienced a long history of civil war, and since its secession from Sudan in 2011, two violent conflicts have erupted in 2013 and 2016. In spite of the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS) on 12 September 2018, fighting and displacement have continued to affect the civilian population in various locations in South Sudan. The implementation of the peace agreement by the warring parties has been slow, particularly around the defining of boundaries and cantonment. Armed conflict in certain areas, and increased inter-communal violence and criminality have demonstrated that the prospect of political peace may not necessarily lead to an end of the ethnic conflict, and that protracted protection crisis affecting the civilians could persist for multiple years in spite of a potential improvement in the political environment. According to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), approximately 5,725,000 million South Sudanese women, men, and children would face protection risks and violations in 2019. These risks are a direct result of the continued violence and conflict affecting civilians since 2013. Displacement, caused by conflict and rights violations, continues to be one of the main protection concerns, with 2,183,028 South Sudanese refugees in neighbouring countries, 299,123 refugees in South Sudan, and 1,465,542 internally displaced persons (IDPs), of whom 182,112 are living in protection of civilian (PoC) sites. People living in Upper Nile State is highly affected by these violations and the widespread impunity. In the mid of this year armed fighting broke-out on 30 July 2019 in Maiwut Town within SPLA-IO Division 5 and later escalated to the surrounding villages, caused displacement of over 20,654 people (IRNA & ROSS). The prolonged effects of conflict and displacement have majorly affected women and children in the locations prioritized. Assessment findings in Fashoda and Maiwut underlines a high level of UASC, child survivors of violence, abuse and exploitation and GBV cases (IRNA). IDPs children are not attending school and considering the high presence of armed actors, they represent an easy target for armed groups' recruitment. No recreational activities in the area and the psychosocial needs of these children and young people are not addressed. There is need for timely protection services from the FGDs held with IDP community, GBV concerns included early and forced marriage, domestic violence and SGBV cases. No any service available in order to guarantee PSS to the GBV-survivors. UNCDR through its intervention in Upper Nile observed high child recruitment, abuse and exploitation are still main concerns. UASC have been identified and there is still consistent need to proceed with further identification, registration and reunification with their families. In the hard to reach prioritized areas locations, CP interventions are very limited while the needs are constantly increasing. Thus, UNCDR will scale up its activities in protection in order to complement the current multi-sectorial and address the gaps in coordination with the CP and GBV sub clusters. UNCDR intends to respond to the needs of the youth, who have been exposed to armed activities. UNCDR will address the identified needs and achieve the expected outcomes through a response strategy:, improve protection of conflict-affected males, females under the age of 18 years and internally displaced children, especially UASC are better protected through: 1) comprehensive case management system and provision of dignity kits and life-saving material assistance; 2) building the capacity of the CBCPN on mechanisms and approach; 3) improve Referral system for GBV and CP cases; 4)provide alternative care support to children without care givers, conduct FTR; 5) CP mainstreaming into Education, WASH, Health and, FSL and conducting awareness raising to prevent and respond to CP issues.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, beneficiaries rights will be legally recognized, respected and promoted through this project design in accordance to the United Nations Deceleration on Human Rights, international human rights treaties and commission. The human rights deceleration requires that projects that intends to address crisis, emergency or catastrophes disasters be designed to allow maximum participation for all affected persons regardless of their color, race, religion, affiliation, sexual orientation. This project is designed to allow equal participation and engagement done through consultation of the affected persons to ensure interventions respond to solve the immediate and priority needs of the affected people. The beneficiaries have the legal right to report tribalism, sexual abuse and exploitation, mismanagement of project funds including diversion of funds for person gains by Agency staffs to the donors, cluster and the government for action and accountability. This can be done through established complaint reporting and feedback mechanism.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

This project target to reach to 10,000 people from IDPs, Returnees and host community in Maiwut and Fashoda counties. Caseload allocation; 3,625 individuals will be in Maiwut and 3,625 individuals in Fashoda, which is in line with the needs analysis of the affected populations. Caseload allocation per beneficiaries category; 2500 individuals will be IDPs, 4500 individuals will be Returnees and 3000 individuals will be the most vulnerable Host Community, in regards to the project beneficiaries. The project intended benefits will be awareness raising campaigns, comprehensive case management including PSS activities, Family tracing and reunification and will preach child protection risk prevention messages including early/ forced marriages, GBV etc. to both children and adults. The project is designed to allow for maximum meaningful participation and engagement of targeted people without discrimination and therefore, it will provide a durable solution through resilience, transparency and accountability to the affected persons. Most vulnerable people will be identified and will be included in decision-making on project matters that impact on them. A complaints and complements mechanism and desk will be established to ensure fair play within the project implementation system.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, UNCDR will jointly work with the government mainly RRC/ROSS through project coordination and joint cluster missions which are planned collectively and aligned to the government priority plans. Prior to project inception, meetings for project implementation will be conducted with the local authorities at the State, County, Payam or Boma levels as safety audit measures for projects links with government plans. The communities in question were consulted prior to the project design phase through needs assessment and existing community feedback mechanism and the interests, needs, aspirations and future goals were included into the programming and implementation plan. This project complements the many contributions of the national government, Upper Nile State and Child Protection Sub cluster plans and objectives on the communities needs in Maiwut and Fashoda Counties. It recognizes and will endeavor to work with existing network of local community structures; utilizing the support of local leadership and other partners such as NH, DRC, UNKEA, RI, WVI and WOCO etc. in Maiwut and Fashoda areas as well as the beneficiaries to ensure that only the most people in need receive the project inputs and skills. During the selection of the community structures, UNCDR will consult with the local authorities and provide the existing selection criteria for the community structures which includes community-based child protection networks and foster parents.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, this project is aligned to the national plan or/framework for returnees, IDPs reintegration and it is consistent across clusters in the country. The humanitarian relief programming and coordination is regulated by the Relief and Rehabilitation commission at the national, state and county levels in conjunction with OCHA support. The Child Protection sub cluster and UNHCR does the alignment and coordination with support of sub cluster state focal points to ensure that project goals, objectives and activities are in line with national objectives and that the implementation criteria does not deviate from planned cluster methodology. The project developed its goals and objectives from the overall cluster general objectives which were developed in consultation and participation of national Ministry of Gender, Child and social welfare resources priorities for 2020. The project aligned these to its organizational strategic plans and priorities for Maiwut and Fashoda counties. The Child Protection Sub cluster has been and is working hand in hand together with UNHCR the general protection cluster lead to ensure the cluster priorities are well aligned to the national returns/refugees framework.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the security situation of Maiwut and Fashoda counties is currently calmed and stable as a result of reduced armed conflicts and the revitalized signed peace agreement between the warring parties. This allows opportunities and an enabling environment for affected populations’ participation and engagement in productive activities. These include community-based psycho social support that can be linked to existing child friendly spaces; awareness raising activities in the community; monitoring of the child protection related risks in the community; comprehensive case management in the community. Some slight inter communal incidents do happen, but the authorities on the ground usually handle the situation without further escalation especially in Maiwut County hence, still the situation is expected to be normal as the project supports the needy people in a safe environment.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, UNCDR proposes to start linking the various PSS and Child Protection services to relevant Government line ministries as national duty bearers. This will be discussed with the local authorities in Maiwut and Fashoda. Actionable points will be derived for post-2020 scenario with prospective partners of Government in handling the situation, including close relatives of the affected. UNCDR’s experience in Maiwut and Fashoda Counties and involvement of other partners, will help the team to identify and map other service providers, ensuring that all the gaps identified are reported to specific partners for further intervention as needed. Furthermore, UNCDR will apply its funding strategy to solicit for other donor resources for these locations to ensure that vulnerable children that may be left out in this rapid response will be covered with none HRP funding. UNCDR will provide trainings to the selected community structures and its staff in order to carry out what it takes to sustain the activities initiated by the project. This will help promote community self –reliance and resilience. It will be stressed severally that the intervention has a life span and that the community should create a sustainability option in the absence of external donor funds. As stated above, UNCDR will maintain its presence in the three counties and will be able to monitor the child protection situation once there is funding.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

UNCDR will carry out activities that aim at reducing and mitigating protection risks including the cases related to GBV, family separations and recruitment of children into armed groups. UNCDR will continue promoting an environment where protection and assistant are delivered in safety and with dignity. Risk, Vulnerability and Capacity Analysis: UNCDR will create a context-specific protection risk and benefit analysis. UNCDR will analyze relative importance (likelihood and impact) and manageability (prevention or mitigation) for different groups and individuals. Desk Review and Needs Assessment: Gather situational protection information on the major risks for the affected population (what and for whom), sources of risks, and any community-based or self-protection mitigation mechanisms. This information could come from protection needs assessments, case management, and feedback mechanisms. UNCDR will build a monitoring system and an accountability framework on the basis of identified protection risks and benefits. UNCDR will continue to ensure that protection is mainstreamed in all of its activities as noted in the previous sections. UNCDR being protection actor will plan to further strengthen its links with protection cluster in 2020. UNCDR is a protection implementer and will remain as protection actor to continually monitor the protection needs in order to adequately serve groups at risk, such as youths, adolescents, child headed households especially female headed ones, separated and unaccompanied children, children engaged in worst forms of child labor, exploitation and abuse; and other most vulnerable children. In line with protection referral systems. UNCDR will continue to conduct comprehensive case management and PSS activities for especially vulnerable children. UNCDR will also continue to coordinate with Mobile response to take the available service nearer to the people in need and UNCDR will promote strong measures for data management.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

UNCDR will use Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) tool to ensure the community have multiple ways to share complaints and information about their lives with beneficiaries, with respect to this project. UNCDR will improve an inclusive system of interacting with communities and promote minimum standard for all mechanisms for regular tracking, reviewing and action feedback for the project. A complain desk during project implementation will be installed in UNCDR offices and Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) to allow complains and feedback from the beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. All cases will be recorded and addressed accordingly, confidentiality will be assured across the process. Complains will be managed with care, dignity, confidentiality and respect whilst reporting to complaints desk. During the project implementation UNCDR will set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, or address concerns and complaints. UNCDR will hold meetings the local authorities to aware them of the activities of the project including the budgeted duties.
