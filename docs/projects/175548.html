Project Summary

An estimated 7.7 million people are expected to experience acute food insecurity and worse (IPC 3, 4 and 5) in 2021 according to the latest IPC analysis published in December 2020 (HNO 2021). GBI seeks to deliver a 12–month integrated livelihoods, resilience and emergency food production responses to the vulnerable and affected people in South Sudan through provision of food assistance, supporting emergency food production with complementary kits and livelihoods resilience programmes for the vulnerable people (Bor South, Twic East and Duk of Jonglei state, Pibor of GPAA, Aweil East, Aweil Center and Aweil North of Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Juba and Terekeka in Central Equatoria and Kapoeta East, Ikotos, Torit and Kapoeta North Counties of Eastern Equatoria) as well as Jur River and Raga Counties of Western Bahr el Ghazal state. In line with the FSL cluster strategy, HNO 2021 and the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), GBI is proposing to scale up response in these locations through distribution of emergency food production kits (vegetable and fishing kits), livestock vaccination and training of CAHWs, cash transfers, assets creation and unconditional food assistance) supporting ongoing or planned interventions.
The overall objective of this project is to improve the food security and livelihood status of vulnerable households (IDPs, returnees and host) in Jonglei, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Jonglei, Eastern and Central Equatoria and Northern Bahr El Ghazal states to avert the worsening food security situation in IPC 3, 4 and 5 locations. This project is designed to reduce vulnerabilities and risk associated with the current food insecurity and poor economic conditions as a result of cumulative years of violent conflict, seasonal variability and displacement. A total of 9,240 household beneficiaries comprising (returnees 22%, IDPs 13% and host 65%) will be targeted directly with lifesaving livelihoods and food security interventions. 51% of total beneficiaries will be female while 49% will be male. The project will require $462,000 to provide the intended services and activities throughout the intervention period.

1 - Needs

An estimated 7.7 million people are expected to experience acute food insecurity and worse (IPC 3, 4 and 5) in 2021 according to the latest IPC analysis published in December 2020 (HNO 2021). Heavy rains and seasonal flooding since July have adversely affected more than 1 million people and resulted in widespread agricultural and property damage (USAID Dec, 2020). Jonglei State had more than 400,000 people affected by floods as of late November, the greatest number of any state; flooding has also inundated areas of Central Equatoria, Lakes, and Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity, Upper Nile, Warrap, and Western Equatoria states. In Jonglei, one of the worst-affected states, flooding had damaged approximately 45 percent of the total cereal-producing area across nine flood-affected counties as of late August (FAO August 2020). The severity and scale of acute food insecurity are high across the country, driven by the loss of productive assets linked to conflict, poor macroeconomic conditions, and large-scale crop and livestock losses during the 2019 floods (FEWSNET July 2020). Food shortages, climate shocks, a deepening economic crisis, insecurity and insufficient agricultural production at household level have kept levels of hunger and acute malnutrition alarmingly high (FAO 2020). Food security was also reportedly precarious in EES and CES during the first quarter of 2020. The vast majority of assessed settlements reported facing inadequate access to food (96%), and approximately half of these settlements (54%) reported this hunger was severe in March 2020 (REACH March 2020). (IOM DTM, Sept 2020), up to 86,720 individuals (17,344 households) were displaced from lowlands in Gemmeiza, Nyori, and Terekeka Town to highlands of the latter due to serious flooding resulting from heavy rainfall. Floods also forced 2,520 individuals (420 households) to move from lowland areas to highland areas of Jabal Lado in Juba County. According to Oct/Nov 2020 IPC projections, 34 counties across the country were classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity including Ayod, Bor South, Duk, Pibor and Twic East in Jonglei State and Terekeka in Central Equatoria State; and Kapoeta North in Eastern Equatoria State while the IPC projection for April – July 2021 puts Aweil East, Aweil North in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Twic East, Duk, Ayod in Jonglei state and Pibor in GPAA as well as Kapoeta East and Kapoeta South in Eastern Equatoria. The IPC projections for Duk County have worsened over recent years, with a re-classification from Crisis (IPC Phase 3) levels of food insecurity in 2016 to Emergency (IPC Phase 4) levels in 2020 (CSRF 2020).GBI through SSHF implemented Integrated WASH and Nutrition project targeting 1,600 SAM children and over 17,000 individuals in communities with safe water and sanitation in 2019/2020 and Emergency WASH flood response in Twic East County supported through Rapid Response Fund (RRF) as well as COVID-19 Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) activities with WASH NFIs support to 300 HHs in Mangala Payam in 2020/2021. In 2019/2020, GBI reached 12,623 households with emergency food production in Aweil East through provision of crop kits, tools, fishing and vegetable kits while providing capacity building to selected farmers in groups and kitchen gardening in nutrition centers.In line with the call for proposals and 2021 HRP FSL Cluster objectives, GBI plans to scale up in greater Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Central and Eastern Equatoria where we have operational presence. Activities integrating FSL, WASH, Health, Protection and Nutrition programming at households, institutions and community level while mainstreaming GBV and COVID-19 risk mitigation, prioritizing the immediate provision of lifesaving livelihoods and nutrition services to the most food insecure populations in IPC 3, 4 and 5. GBI through WFP SFP will be supporting 62 schools reaching 23,798 school children in 2021in Aweil East.

2 - Project activities

1. In-kind food assistance through General Food Distribution (GFD), Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme (BSFP) and School Feeding Programme (SFP) to 4000 HHs. These activities help provide IDPs/Returnees/Host communities with an in-kind food basket that meets all their basic food and nutritional needs. GBI supported by WFP will be implementing SFP in Aweil East in 62 schools reaching 23,798 school children in 2021.2. Provide livestock veterinary services to curtail livestock losses, improved meat quality and milk yield, thereby stabilizing and safeguarding rural and urban livelihoods through livestock vaccination and treatment on common diseases as well as surveillance of diseases of 15000 animals.3. As a capacity building activity in livestock, 300 Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) will be provided with drugs and trained on disease investigation, vaccination and treatment and best management practices to increase output and minimize livestock losses.4. Procurement, operation and maintenance of 2 cold chains for safe storage of livestock drugs.5. Cash and voucher-based transfers – beneficiaries meet their immediate cash needs in locations with functional markets while ensuring that all households are receiving training in financial literacy to make informed and effective decisions relating to their financial resources, including on how to generate, invest, spend and save money. Trainings will be delivered jointly to male–and female–household members, as a means of promoting joint decision-making over household budgets.6. Distribution of seeds, tools, fishing kits and quick maturing vegetable seeds to vulnerable and conflict affected 5000 households for emergency food and livelihoods support.7. Capacity building and training on demonstration fields on best management practices such as pest and disease management, post-harvest handling, value addition and marketing 924 beneficiaries representing 10% target HHs.8. Formation of producers’ groups, village saving and loan schemes for diversification and restoration of livelihoods.

South Sudan has been affected by widespread violent conflict and flooding for two consecutive years impeding humanitarian access by road. This normally affects delivery of sufficient aid materials to remove parts of the country. Livelihoods materials are normally bulky constraining their transportation. Where a functional market exists, beneficiaries can use the cash and vouchers for accessing their immediate needs.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

GBI has a policy on PSEA which is served to all project staff for their understanding. Every time a new project is being launched, staff are oriented on PSEA, as well as vendors and beneficiaries. In case there are SEA cases, GBI through referral pathways available refer the cases to the protection partner to investigate and help the survivors.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Feedback from beneficiaries are reviewed from time to time for adjusting or guiding the intervention. If the feedback is within the project scope, the agency will respond positively and revise the projects to address the needs of the beneficiaries and the most vulnerable. The project will conduct entry meetings with local authority, RRC, beneficiaries as part of community mobilization and with different groups for example, PWDs, women groups, youth representatives, chiefs, local authorities and beneficiaries’ representatives at the project inception. The aim of the meetings is to orient and mobilize different stakeholders about the project’s project duration, target beneficiaries and selection criteria, distribution site selections, donor and the role of the community in selection of beneficiaries and volunteers. The project will prioritize the needs of vulnerable people such men, women, boys, girls, PWDs, PLW, elderly etc. and this information is communicated through communication and inception meetings in presence of local authority. The project will consult people with disability and collect disability disaggregated data which allows them to identify specific needs/vulnerability, risks and barriers which prevent people with disability from accessing services on equal basis with other people.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Individuals accessing humanitarian assistance are the primary stakeholders of any humanitarian action. This means that they must be able to receive communications in a form they can understand, provide feedback on the delivery of humanitarian assistance and to be included in decisions that affect their lives. Before distribution, beneficiaries are informed about their entitlements, rights and contribution towards the project. The AAP committees formed are used as channel through which beneficiaries who can't present their own complaints can speak to the organization. Rapid Post Distribution Monitoring (RPDM), end user surveys, exit interviews are conducted for beneficiaries to express their satisfaction or dissatisfaction about the project inputs and through one on one or face-to-face discussion with beneficiaries. Beneficiaries are also provided with suggestion boxes through which they can share their complaints or feedback i.e. for those who know how to read and write. Gender issues are also addressed through AAP committees by ensuring gender equality in selection of members.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Male and female, women and men or boys and girls are differently affected by humanitarian crisis, situation or disaster requiring an intervention. Community mobilization brings together all people of different sex, age group and status to decide for themselves as far as humanitarian assistance is concerned. The project will prioritize the needs of women and girls by involving them in key decision making processes and by being part of AAP. 70% of beneficiaries of target population are women and 30% men, 45% are children (31.5% girls and 13.5% boys). Special support will be given to women groups in form of cash transfers, training, and loan and assets ownership. Forced taxation of Humanitarian Assistance and access restrictions is mitigated by seeking letter of clearance from Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) in Juba for humanitarian items moving from Juba to field locations. The risk is also transferred to third party by giving transportation work to logistics companies which take the responsibility of safe delivery of items. While delivering within the locality security reports or situations that might arise to forced taxation are analyzed before authorizing delivery. Movement of staff are planned to take place between 9:00am – 4pm when security of guaranteed – no voyages allowed. Normally, the local authority and local leaders try to corrupt the process of identification of beneficiaries by bringing their close relatives instead of vulnerable people in need of assistance or even having themselves registered. To mitigate targeting of wrong beneficiaries GBI conducts community mobilization before identification of beneficiaries to inform them about the selection criteria. We also work with Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) Committees established at Boma level to help in identification of beneficiaries. GBI also works in close coordination with relevant government ministries who are aware of the humanitarian work.
