Project Summary

The objective of this project is to provide lifesaving assistance to South Sudanese refugees under the Regional Refugee Response Plan (RRRP 2019) and Outcome 1: Lifesaving assistance for new arrivals/vulnerable groups is prioritized to respond to urgent humanitarian and protection needs of anticipated new arrivals. IOM will provide Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) assistance in both settlements and in villages where South Sudanese are being hosted by local communities by increasing access to safe drinking water, constructing adequate quality sanitation facilities and by improving personal and public hygiene practices. The proposed project is in line with IOM’s commitment to seek viable prevention and progressive solutions to complex migration and displacement challenges and with the Multi Year Humanitarian Response Plan Strategic Outcome 1: "Populations affected by natural or man-made disasters receive timely assistance during and in the aftermath of the shock." And Outcome 2: “Displaced populations, refugees, returnees and host communities meet their basic needs and/or access basic services while increasing their self-reliance.”

Needs

Since the outbreak of the conflict in South Sudan in December 2013; a total of 848,091 South Sudanese refugees have fled to Sudan with 3,111 newly arrived refugees in 2019. Due to continued conflict and famine in South Sudan, affected populations have continued to cross the border into Sudan by affected populations in Abyei, South and West Kordofan, White Nile and East and South Darfur. While there is a major humanitarian presence in White Nile, East and South Darfur significant gaps remain in South and West Kordofan, where access constraints have hindered the provision of critical humanitarian assistance. WASH needs of the population are high both in and out of camps. Many refugees are living among host-communities that already have very limited access to basic services; for example in Keliak, West Kordofan a lack of water 4 Liters are available per person per day (l/p/d), which is below the Sphere standard of 15 l/p/d and the increased influx of South Sudanese continues to create tensions between the community and the refugees. A lack of proper water management, sanitation facilities and poor hygiene practices have also been observed among these communities waste management is limited and open defecation is widely practiced. Therefore, there is a great need to support these communities with construction, rehabilitation and maintenance of water structures and safe sanitation facilities; as well as facilitating hygiene awareness campaigns and waste management campaigns.

Activities

1. Monitor water depletion levels and construct / rehabilitate / chlorinate water points to improve access to safe water sources and increase quantity to sphere standards of 7.5 -15l/p/d. 2. Assess area needs and provide sanitation facilities to reduce open defecation and improve community sanitation. 3. Preposition spare parts, tools, and water purification kits for necessary maintenance and rehabilitation of WASH facilities. 4. Train mechanics and chlorination technicians to ensure that WASH facilities remain safe and functional. 5. Distribute Hygiene Kits to assist vulnerable South Sudanese households (HH) who have been newly displaced. 6. Launch hygiene campaigns encouraging the community to develop waste management protocols such as garbage collection to improve the environment.

Impact on environment/natural resources

Protection and environmental sustainability are an integral part of the overall approach of the WASH interventions. The aim of the water intervention is to increase environmental sustainability by reducing the need for water trucking (which can cause water depletion), as well as ensuring the protection of women and children through the selection of the location of the water points. Water distribution points will be installed at each of the VIP latrines to ensure that safe sanitation practices will be maintained and that the the services available will be improved through the direct access to water for hygiene purposes, especially for women and children.
