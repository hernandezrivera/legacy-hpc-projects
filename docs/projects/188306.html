Project Summary

The intervention aims to provide wide-ranging support for household and community-based agricultural, livestock, and livelihoods assistance in linkage with the value chain during 2022 and 2023 years. The project will target the most affected people in NWS and their youth girls and boys and engage them to effectively attempt to be self-reliant in the best possible way. The intervention plan anticipates that this will lead the families and their youth to think constructively and be self-reliant in the medium to long term. It will target all populations of IDPs, returnees, and host communities and take into consideration the most vulnerable people such as the women-headed families, elderly-headed families, and child-headed families. Moreover, the project will take into consideration the PWD.
The project is designed be implementad over 2022-2023 years to increase the self-reliance for both HH and communities through:
1. Distributing of agricultural inputs (seeds, fertilizer, pesticides, irrigation cost, harvest cost) coupled with related training, good production practices for 2500 families in NWS, 1250 during 2022 and 1250 in 2023.
2. Supporting small-scale food production for crises-affected people by targeting for food processing training with tools and equipment, it will be targeted 200 HH during 2022-2023, 100 HH over each year.
3. Support 1000 HH by distribution laying kits coupled with folders and veterinary services over 2022-2023, 500 HH each year.
4. Provide health care services and vaccination for 100,000 animals in 2022-2023, 50,000 animals as target for each year.
5. Support market-driven income-generating activities (IGAs) with vocational training for 200 HH in 2022 and 200 HH in 2023 from the most crisis-affected people.
6. Strengthen the local communities’ capacity for supporting the local population through rehabilitation of the second phase of the value chain on infrastructures (bakeries, flour mills, silos for storage, in addition to the irrigation channels), in addition to establishing early warning and DRR systems.
7. Support wheat marketing through purchasig the wheat from the farmers. QC seeks to purchase 10,000 MT of whaet over 2022-2023 years, 5,000 MT each year.
The total beneficiaries to be reached by the project over 2022-2023 over 24 months are 347,850, 147,850 out of them in 2022.
The total budget for this project is 15,100,000 for 2022-2023, 7,100,000 USD for 2022, about 2 M USD out of 2022 budget are secured.

4 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian emergency characterized by over 10 years of ongoing hostilities and their long-term consequences including widespread destruction, explosive ordnance contamination, and the largest number of internally displaced people in the world. More than ten years of crisis have inflicted immense suffering on the civilian population who have experienced massive and systematic violations of international humanitarian and human rights law. The food security Sector has seen an increase in the severity of needs, especially regarding the lack of food and agriculture-based livelihoods. The Sector FSA/FSLA assessments for 2020, as well as the mid-year review process for 2021, highlight increased food insecurity across the whole country. This alarming increase in food and agriculture needs is due to a complex combination of inter-linked contextual drivers which can only be addressed through an integrated approach. The levels of acute food insecurity are projected to worsen in Syria in 2022, due largely to the drought-like conditions the country experienced in 2021 and the subsequent poor harvest, shortages of essential inputs for farming combined with the overall economic crisis in the country, as well as the livelihoods and resilience of crisis-affected families and communities

5 - Implementation of activities

The intervention aims to improve the food security status of families and their youth girls and boys and engage them to effectively attempt to be self-reliant in the best possible way. The project will ensure all stakeholders are involved from the beginning through the community mobilization to explain the intervention concept and outputs. In coordination with local authorities, the beneficiaries will be selected through criteria application, QC will finalize the beneficiaries list by applying eligibility and vulnerability criteria based on the activity type. The registration will be available in equal opportunity for both females, males HH, IDPs, and PWD. In parallel, QC will purchase the program deliverables to ensure the delivery on time. Regarding the distribution process, the beneficiaries will be informed individually through the QC field team about the date and location of the distribution, to avoid any crowded ensure social distancing and avoid delays, and take into consideration the safety and security of the beneficiaries. QC will take all the protection principles through the distribution. The COVID-19 measures will be applied to the distribution points. MEAL activities will accompany implementation over the project period, it will be conducted Base and End line, PDM, FGDs, KIIs, household and community surveys, and direct observation. All rehabilitated infrastructure will be handed over to the local authorities through hand-over the agreement.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

During the project implementation, the following protection risks may arise and actions to mitigate the risks are also outlined. 1. sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers(risk of harassment) of women and girls. Mitigation: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, train staff on a code of conduct and code of conduct in all staff contracts. Address any PSEA complaints. Recruit female staff and train them. The field MEAL staff with monitor the situation by conducting PDM. 2. violence from beneficiaries among themselves/with humanitarian workers when huge onset occurs (crowd control). Mitigation: Identification of suitable sites/ time. Set up several distribution points on sites to reduce crowds. make a joint distribution with another partner 3. Diversion of aid. Mitigation: Coordination meetings withcommunity leaders/gatekeepers/local councils on targeting criteria. Sign MOUs with local authorities 4. The Cash/voucher overspent on non-food items negatively impacts food security outcomes. Mitigation: Provide vouchers or e-cards, not cash, vary the payment days. MEAL staff will continue vendor monitoring systems (i.e. the selected shops). 5. targeting for IDPs versus Host communities. Mitigation: From localbenefeciaryselection committees with equitable representation of both IDPs and host communities.Set clear guidance to the Community Focal Points (CFPs) on the beneficiary selection process and criteria.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The current security situation that tends to be stable in most areas inside Syria will facilitat the intervention implementation to achieve the planned impacts. in addition to th good understanding and cooperation from most of the local stakeholders, some risks beyond QC control may affect the intervention implementation: 1. A huge number of DPs may reach the targeted areas if the East Euphrates operation started, so the demand for the food will be increased suddenly. Mitigation: Keep close coordination with the local councils and active organizations in the targeted area in order to harmonize the intervention and advocate through the FSL cluster to the donors to address the gap and big demand for the food. 2. Risk of Delayed deliveries due to closure of borders or closed internal roads between targeted areas for unforeseen reasons. Mitigation: QC will keep close coordination with the Turkish government since the humanitarian aids have exceptions, otherwise the procurement will be shifted to Syria. Close coordination with the local councils and main actors on the ground in order to facilitate the aids movements between the different areas and arrange warehouses in the different targeted areas. 3. Risk of New conflict may start in Idleb areas between the Syrian regime and other army groups or between different army groups themselves. Mitigation: QC safety and a security officer will be continuously following up and monitoring the security situation and update the safety plan.
