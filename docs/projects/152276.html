Project Summary

The year 2018 was marked by opportunities to access new populations in need of essential live savings health interventions. Subsequent migrations, large displacements of fragile populations and their concentration in camps have further exposed affected populations to increasing health risks.
The objectives of Health Sector Coordination are:
1. To synergize and harmonize the response across states.
2. To better identify gaps and reduce overlap.
3. To support protection and gender mainstreaming efforts through health programming and trainings with health partners.
4. To Strengthened Health Information Management (HIMS) at cluster/working group level to track essential morbidity and mortality data, creating an evidence base to drive forward health programming.
5. To support joint structures and information on joint contingency and preparedness planning for response to disease outbreaks such as cholera and meningitis across the states.
6. To support increase of size and capacity of the health workforce.
7. To strengthening partnerships with national and international non governmental organizations.
8. To support rehabilitation of damaged health facilities (including human resources, and equipment/supplies) for health service delivery.
The project will focus on :
1. To strengthen health sector coordination and health information systems to improve the life saving health response for people in need.
2. To fill critical gaps in the health services delivery through provision of technical and operational support to partners.
3. Health human resource development, promotion of humanitarian-development nexus approaches.

Needs

In north-east Nigeria, around 5.2 million people are in need of life-saving and essential health care services during 2019. The crisis in NE is a protracted crisis with huge developmental needs combined with extreme poverty and ongoing insurgency. During the evolution of more than two years of humanitarian support lots of achievements were reported with tangible impact towards more sustainable health service delivery in most of the LGAs. The ongoing population displacement, the influx in some LGAs of IDP and refugees and returnees into overcrowded and under-serviced camps and settlements exacerbates the risk of disease outbreaks. People, particularly women and children, are left increasingly susceptible to disease outbreaks, especially cholera. The north-east remains highly endemic for diarrheal diseases due to seasonal patterns, lack of access to potable water and sanitation infrastructure, and vulnerabilities as a result of continuous displacement and low access to essential healthcare. Critical gaps still remain in functional primary and secondary health services. This includes lack of regular nutrition screening in all catchment areas, weak community mobilization on key health issues and public health risks, lack of government staff, and unavailability of dedicated support to secondary health care facilities and general hospitals (deployment of medical specialists, supplies, medicines, etc.). Populations face significant obstacles specially to access secondary health care and referral services in hard to reach areas due to a combination of critical issues including loss of ambulance services, lack of specialized health care providers and financial barriers. Only 39 per cent of health facilities have a functional referral mechanism to a higher level of care and one out of two secondary health care facilities is damaged/destroyed and non-functional. The freedom of movement, for both patients and healthcare providers, is often curtailed by general insecurity as well as by specific restrictions, including for IDPs to move in and out of IDP camps. GBV is widespread in the region, amplified both by the conflict, in which sexual violence is rampant, and by the conditions in IDP camps and informal settlements, including inadequate WASH facilities, lack of safe access to firewood, and restrictions on freedom of movement in and out of camps. Essential medical care is needed through Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) and for victims of domestic violence. People with conflict related disabilities and serious injuries need priority medical care and support. Mental Health and Psycho-social (MHPSS) services are also limited in many areas while specialized psychiatric treatment services are only available at Federal Neuro Psychiatric Hospital in Maiduguri. The conflict has taken a major toll on the health care system and its revitalization and strengthening is vital. Two thirds of health facilities in the BAY states have been damaged while many are still under construction by the government and health partners. Those that are functioning are short of staff, and lack safe water, basic drugs and equipment. More than 100 health facilities have been rehabilitated or reconstructed by the government and health sector partners. In hard to reach and newly accessible areas population face serious challenges in accessing health services due to various reasons e.g. lack of medical staff, unavailability of drugs and supplies, security barriers, transportation issues, damaged/destroyed health facilities. Other common challenges in accessing health services are related to the high direct and indirect costs of healthcare (reported by 75% of IDP, 61% of returnee and 80% of non-displaced households), including the price of quality medicines (reported by 45%, 41% and 34%, respectively).Cash Transfer Program is an important area which health sector will support for effective health services delivery to affected population.

Conduct and co lead regular coordination meetings at states level in collaboration with the FMOH and SMOH to deliver an appropriate and timely package of healthcare. Support coordination through joint, strategic, contingency and preparedness plans for disease outbreaks and in response to changing conflict dynamics across the states and the humanitarian hubs Strengthen health logistics supply chain / distribution networks Produce regular Health information products (eg. sitreps, sector bulletins and epi reports) on evolving health risks, population needs and access to services are developed and disseminated jointly with the health authorities and health partners Advocate for the protection of health care staff and patients at health facilities Mainstream protection efforts throughout health programming through coordination fora and training/ workshops with health partners, with focus on increasing access to hard to reach areas and militarily encircled areas Strengthen the Borno State Health EOCs by provide operations support for expanded EOCs (office/ICT/fleet) to cover all health sector operations to all hazards response. Establish technical working groups for each strategic objective/response strategy under EOCs to harmonize planning and operations Develop/agree technical standards, approaches and guidelines Conduct the HeRAMS assessment for all affected states Conduct joint operational review against plan every month through 2019 to support operational plans and priorities update Developed health related GIS mapping Undertake advocacy, resource mobilization for addressing immediate health needsSupport Cash Transfer Program support for effective health services delivery to affected population.Undertake promotion of approaches to effectively respond to survivors of GBV and other domestic violence. Improve quality of health care and set quality standardsDevelop all hazard plans Develop preparedness and readiness plans for key diseases. Provide operational, technical and financial support to partners for critical gap filling in new emergencies.Promote HDN concepts for transitional strategy.
