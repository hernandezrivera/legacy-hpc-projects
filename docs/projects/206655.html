Project Summary

The complex and dynamic humanitarian context in South Sudan requires accurate, triangulated, timely and readily accessible information. This project aims to provide the humanitarian community with well analysed and actionable data to fill existing and anticipated information gaps on displacement, returns, and general population movement across South Sudan and inform a fair and evidence-based humanitarian response delivery and early recovery programming. The main deliverables will include:
- A regularly updated IDPs and returnees baseline at location and area level. Information will include population presence, movement reasons, and needs of IDPs and returnees.
- Providing regular information on sudden displacement and return movements requiring humanitarian assistance through IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) event tracking mechanism.
- Biometric data management for accountable, effective, and targeted delivery of humanitarian assistance.
- Representative Inter-Sectoral Needs Assessment (ISNA) on the household level to provide information on needs, vulnerabilities, and intentions to feed into South Sudan’s Humanitarian Response Planning (HRP) and Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO).
- Regular updates on population mobility at border points and key internal transit hubs, including displacement sites, through DTM’s flow monitoring mechanism.
- Data for Solutions to Internal Displacement (DSID) to measure progress towards durable solutions

1. Needs

South Sudan is complex specifically due to its unique mobility dynamics, based on spontaneous and organized population movement (displacement and returns). As a result, deploying a well-informed humanitarian response and early recovery programming in South Sudan requires timely, well-triangulated, and readily accessible information. Data that would inform evidence-based humanitarian, peace-building, and recovery planning actions must also be accurate, actionable, and able to provide regular updates to humanitarian, development, and government partners. IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Mobility Tracking Round 14 data indicates a total displacement population of 2 million IDPs, including more than 562,000 persons displaced due to the floods in South Sudan and 2.3 million returnees from internal displacement and across the borders as of the beginning of April 2023. The number of returnees has increased in 2023 since the onset of fighting in Sudan, with flow monitoring at the border indicating close to 300,000 South Sudanese nationals had arrived by mid-November 2023. Over 7 million people in the country are expected to experience high levels of acute food insecurity by July 2024 (South Sudan IPC Analysis, November 2023). Continuous sub-national violence, armed clashes, and imminent flooding further exacerbate preexisting vulnerabilities. The multi-layered emergency situation in the country increased the need for adequate and well-analyzed data in order to effectively plan and deliver humanitarian assistance with limited funding. With many years of being at the forefront of humanitarian data provision globally and in South Sudan, IOM’s DTM information management system has been well-positioned to support the population in need by providing real-time data on displacement, returns, spontaneous events, flow monitoring, biometric registration data, and related surveys to partners who respond through the various clusters across the country. IOM DTM has been working closely with the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) and the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) within the existing cooperation agreements. The RRC and NBS have endorsed the DTM methodology, regularly support data collection, and have approved the IDP and returnee baseline as the official data for the country.

2. Project activities

DTM will capitalize on the successes achieved since its inception by providing quality data to stakeholders, partners, and the government. DTM’s country-wide recognition as the country’s main source of displacement data among other essential data for humanitarian response and transition. DTM will conduct mobility tracking assessments with a presence in all 10 states (78 counties) and the Abyei Administrative Area (AAA) to provide regular estimated displaced and returning population figures, locations, reasons for movement, and priority of humanitarian needs for IDPs and Returnees. DTM will leverage its network of key informants and enumerators in over 3,900 locations to ensure the regular provision of IDP baseline data. DTM will undertake two rounds of the Mobility Tracking activity. The event tracking mechanism will build on its existing network of 350 enumerators across 500 sub-areas within the country to track new events in 2024 - spontaneous displacements due to floods (new and recurrent), and sub-national and localized conflicts. Prompt tracking and reporting will contribute to early warning efforts, informing upon rapid responses and conflict prevention. Spontaneous updates will be provided for critical events or those requested by humanitarian partners and regularly shared with the Needs Analysis Working Group (NAWG) as the key inter-agency platform for discussion on geographical prioritisation of the response. Furthermore, bi-weekly updates will be shared with partners and available at an interactive public dashboard. In addition, IOM DTM will conduct a representative Inter-Sector Needs Assessment(ISNA) at the household level in coordination with OCHA and will continue to collaborate with partners on the design, implementation, and analysis through the Technical Working Group (TWG). In collaboration with the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), IOM will continue using enumeration areas as the basis for creating the sampling frame. Methodology and tools will be decided in collaboration with partners and relevant stakeholders to ensure information is collected and analyzed in a timely manner to feed into the Humanitarian Needs Overview and Humanitarian Response Plan for 2024. DTM will continue with daily data collection at over 20 strategic border points and internal transit hubs through flow monitoring registry. The network of observation points will be maintained and adjusted through regular monitoring and evaluation process to ensure coverage over key mobility corridors. Data collected and analysed from flow monitoring actives will provide timely information on displacement, mixed migration and return routes which would improve knowledge of migrant profiles, movement intention and motivation. Further on, the data will be analysed and shared with partners to inform planning and response in case of any public health emergencies (e.g. Ebola, COVID-19). DTM will continue with scaled-up joint flow monitoring at border with Sudan in response to ongoing Sudan Crisis, working together with UNHCR and RRC and publish arrivals on joint dashboard. With over one million uniquely registered individuals in DTM’s secure database, IOM will undertake biometric registration of new beneficiaries as requested by partners through the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) at both federal and state levels, among others. Biometric maintenance will be conducted to update the status and locations of beneficiaries in the database IdpReady particularly focusing on the main displacement sites in Juba (IDP Camp 1 & 3), Bentiu (Bentiu IDP Camp), Malakal (Malakal Protection of Civilian Site) and Wau (Naivasha IDP Camp, Masna Collective Center). Biometric data sharing will be provided as regulated through relevant data sharing agreements and in adherence to IOM’s data protection principles. IOM DTM will also support partners with regular population counts at major displacement sites (Juba, Wau, Malakal, Bentiu) and other areas upon request.
