Project Summary

To improve equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive sexual reproductive health (SRHR) services for Rohingya refugees and host communities, UNFPA will continue to strengthen health systems and community engagement through coordination and partnership within the health sector and key players, including the gender based violence [GBV] Sub-Sector and community health worker [CHW] Working Group actors to provide strategic, technical and programme guidance related to provision and delivery of a continuum of care package for essential SRHR services to the last mile.
UNFPA will ensure continuous availability of family planning and other related SRH commodities, supplies and equipment through improved supply chain management, including contraceptives to meet the unmet needs of refugees and host communities.
The project will improve and maintain the quality of services provided, strengthen referral pathways and transfer of obstetric emergency cases, maintain continuous supply of life saving SRHR commodities and client centered quality midwifery led and respectful antenatal care, safe birth, postnatal care, menstrual regulation, cervical cancer and obstetric fistula screening and treatment, adolescent SRHR, quality assurance and referral for obstetric emergency care provided through mentorship and support to service providers. Health providers' capacity will be strengthened in the provision of integrated client centered quality services, including family planning. In addition, community based SRHR services and behavioural change approaches will be strengthened.
The COVID-19 pandemic continues to stretch the health system and widen the existing gaps in the provision of the comprehensive SRHR services and key interventions. To ensure the continuity of provision of SRHR services during COVID-19, interventions will be continued, including operationalisation of maternity red zones and Isolation Treatment Centres [ITC] with maternity care.
UNFPA will also strengthen readiness to meet SRHR needs during disasters through capacity building, stock prepositioning, and SRHR policy formulation.

1 - Identified Needs

There are over 324,359 (Rohingya refugees 211,079 : Host communities 113,280) women of reproductive age in Cox’s Bazar and more than 10% of them are anticipated to need delivery care in 2021. SRHR services are not available 24/7 across facilities and the capacity to provide emergency obstetric care to ensure safe deliveries is insufficient. These coupled contributed to the higher level of maternal morbidity and mortality of the country. The proportion of facility-based deliveries have improved over a period of time from 32% at start of 2019 to 53% in January 2020 and steadily increased to 60% in October 2020 with appropriate interventions in response to COVID-19 from the partners in the community and facilities. To sustain these gains, there is a further need to reinforce the system in terms of efforts and support to all the partners to ensure provision of quality services. Furthermore, in the purview of the morbidity related to SRHR, there is an urgent need to scale up midwifery led intensified cervical cancer and obstetric fistula screening and treatment across the refugee and host communities, including targeting elderly women. Family planning methods are widely available at health posts and primary health centres within Rohingya refugee camps. From the half year of 2020 period, a total 39,943 revisits from Rohingya refugees and host communities for any family planning services. In alignment with Government of Bangladesh priorities, one of the current objectives is expansion of access to Long-acting reversible contraceptive methods (i.e., intra-uterine devices and implants) in the refugee community. There is therefore, need for capacity development of mid-level service providers to improve the provision of LARC. Community engagement is needed to address the social and cultural barriers to contraceptive use present a major challenge within Rohingya communities. Adolescents constitute approximately 13.9 % of the population however; their access to adolescent friendly SRH services in the existing health facilities is limited. Hence the need to build capacity of service providers to enhance provision of adolescent friendly services at the existing facilities and in the spirit of leaving no one behind, strengthening interventions to address the SRHR needs of the vulnerable population (e.g. LGBTQI, sex workers, adolescent and people with disabilities) is critical in achieving comprehensive SRHR provision. Furthermore, the impact of COVID-19 has continued to stretch the health system and widen the existing gaps in the provision of the comprehensive SRHR services. There is need to continue with the strengthening infection prevention and control (IPC) measures at service delivery points, including ensuring access to personal protective equipment (PPE) and capacity building for health workers. There is also a need to continue engaging community members including through gatekeepers to regain lost confidence in utilising SRHR and related services due to fears of COVID-19 infection. COVID-19 focused SRHR interventions remains a priority in both refugee camps and host communities. Interventions such as establishment of maternity red zones in the SRH facility, COVID-19 Isolation and Treatment Centres with maternity care, provision of emergency transportation, strengthening of the referral system and community based surveillance remain instrumental in preventing excess maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality. For instance, there are currently 42.6% (23 out of 54) 24/7 SRH facilities with established maternity red zones. It is imperative to address the ever growing needs of this vulnerable population in order to maintain the continuity of the essential SRHR services in the context of the COVID-19 and beyond.

Expand the Community Health Volunteers Network to strengthen community engagement programmes on SRHR, including in the context of COVID-19, to improve the uptake of services16. Engaging the religious leaders and community leaders as well as involvement of men as SRHR partners to raise awareness on key SRHR Topics17. Ensure accountability to affected populations through maintaining and establishing communication, community participation and feedback support mechanism as well as strengthening the PSEA network.18. Reinforce, repair, and maintain vital sexual and reproductive health structures.19. Continue to strengthen SRHR coordination through provision of support and leadership to the SRHR Working Group and ensuring that SRHR and GBV services are delivered in an integrated manner, to improve delivery of quality services20. Improve Information Management Systems through design and use of innovative tools; timely distribution of SRHR information products and streamlining with other reporting systems in coordination with Health Sector, to promote evidence based decision making
