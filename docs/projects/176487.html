Project Summary

The project’s overall objective is to improve household food and livelihoods opportunities for persons with Disability and vulnerable populations affected by crisis and natural disasters in South Sudan. The project aims to contribute to an improvement in food and livelihoods security for IDPs, poor and marginalized women, men and children, using an integrated approach, covering the sectors of livelihood support through cash assistance for elderly, income generating activities (IGA) for female-headed households and youth with disabilities, targeted Business Development Support, including business development plans and asset transfers, capacity-building on financial and asset management. The Livelihoods component will be complemented with the essential technical support to food security and livelihoods actors on inclusive livelihood programming.
This project directly targets 10,000 vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host population (4,000 male and 6,000 female) which will include the most vulnerable refugee returnees. The project will target 60 % returnees, new and protracted IDPs residing with host population. To ensure adherence to the Do No Harm principle, 40% poor host community members will also be selected based on similar vulnerability criteria to address their urgent needs as vulnerability is not exclusive to IDPs and returnee population and to reduce community tensions.

1 - Needs

A total of 5.29 million people (45% of the total population) are already facing at least crisis levels of food insecurity as of January 2020, anticipated to increase to 6.48 million people (55% of the population) due to the impacts of conflict, climatic shocks, low crop production, and macro-economic decline according to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) . Amid optimism for rebuilding, elevated population movement is also expected to bring additional challenges with increased strain placed on already limited resources. In addition, the global COVID-19 pandemic has had a significant impact and its effects are likely to remain in the months to come. Statistics or comprehensive information on the number and situation of people with disabilities in South Sudan is lacking. However, most estimates suggest that it is likely to be at least as high as the global estimate of 15% (1,680,000) of 11.2 million population of South Sudan. In South Sudan, persons with disabilities especially women and their family members are facing significant obstacles in timely and meaningful access to food security and to secure an income to meet daily expenditure and reduce dependency on humanitarian aid, family and kinship structures for basic needs.The majority of people with disabilities of working age are unemployed and begging is often the main mean of survival, owing to limited or no access to education and skill training, coupled with misconceptions of disabling factors and structural inequalities they’re prevented from being equally competitive with their non-disabled peers on livelihood activities. The proposed action aims to facilitate the access of persons with disabilities to skills, information and services that can increase their social-economic resilience and integration, particularly in light of the impact of COVID-19 on their means of livelihoods and increased levels of vulnerability. This will contribute to increasing opportunities for persons with disabilities to earn their livelihoods through working activities they choose and value, and therefore enhance their social protection.Persons with disabilities face major challenges of discrimination, negative attitudes as well as the low knowledge & information of service providers about basic and specific needs of persons with disabilities in accessing food security and sustainable livelihood opportunities. As such, the objectives of the action is to engage those actors from the public, private and nongovernmental sectors that play a key role in facilitating the access onto the livelihoods in order to enhance cooperation, harmonized actions, and disability awareness among them.

2 - Project activities

HI will develop a twin-track approach to ensure that both the immediate response to most acute needs and the actions towards livelihood resilience are inclusive of persons with disability. This approach can be detailed into the following main activities:* Assessments and beneficiary identification through a community based approach, including both a need assessment and a market assessment* Support to identified persons with disabilities and vulnerable people through unconditional multi-purpose cash assistance for persons with disability and/or injury and vulnerable people, including Post Distribution Monitoring.* Provide targeted business development support for persons with disabilities for inclusive income-generating activities. This includes supporting the development of business plans, asset transfers to start the IGA and capacity-building in financial and asset management. * Support to service providers for more inclusion of persons with disabilities, including Barriers and Facilitators Assessments, development of individualized Action Plans to make their services more accessible for persons with disabilities.* Support to livelihood service providers, including vocational centers, through capacity-building on disability inclusion and mainstreaming to make services more accessible

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HI has a robust feedback and complaints mechanism for beneﬁciaries that combines both unsolicited communication channels such as suggestion boxes, text and phoneline as well as an anonymous reporting email service, with solicited forms such as monthly focus group discussions, interviews and post-intervention monitoring surveys. Detailed sensitization of communities on when, how and why to report is critical for the functioning of the system, as is HIs internal review, response and feedback protocols – designed to close the loop. To ensure the most vulnerable community members can access feedback and complaints mechanisms, HI will include considerations such as the use of local languages, reasonable accommodation for persons with disabilities (i.e. accessible venues, sign-language interpretation, sighted guides, accessible information materials), as well as genderspeciﬁc mobilization and messaging to minimize any barriers these key groups encounter. Moreover, HI is proposing many entry-points for feedbacks/complaints to reach all: 1. In-person office visit to FCMS Desk2. HI community & group meetings3. Complaint/Feedback Box4. Toll-free telephone Hotline and SMS5. HI email address 6. Beneficiary Satisfaction Surveys

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The information is provided by HI for each project/intervention. For this specific project, HI will use already experienced media, including:- All group activities (launching workshops, training, capacity-building, activity monitoring, etc.) - During each of the distributions, community trainings, community events or any other community gathering, a banner with the HI FCMS hotline number and email will be displayed- Printed material (flyers, banners, printed hotline number, etc.)- HI staff (they are at the frontline, working directly with beneficiaries in the field and are trained/refreshed to provide such information)- Hoarding / billboard in strategic places, including in camps- Local radio station (included in all radio messaging for awareness campaigns)

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Any complaint related to HI programming and areas of operation that is non-sensitive is categorized as an operational complaint. This includes but not limited to complaints such as:• dissatisfaction with the provided goods and services• quality and quantity of the distributed items• failure to provide timely assistance or request for additional assistance• issues related to elilgibility, registration, or distribution • lack of general project information• non-serious, yet impolite or irresponsible behaviour of HI staff or partner staff Operational or a programme relevant complaints are critical for the FCMS focal point as they need to track them until they are resolved and closed. For operational complaints, the procedure is as follows: 1. Operational complaint is forwarded as email to the relevant project/program manager, copying area manager or Senior Project Liaison Officer (PLO) and where appropriate technical specialist of respective sector (with MEAL Manager in cc) on the same day of receiving the complaint 2. Complainant receives a written receipt (see External Acknowledgement Form) in their preferred modality: paper copy, email, or SMS text message within max. 7 calendar days3. Relevant Program staff reviews the complaint, brings forward recommendations for action, and shares these with Complaints Focal Point and/or MEAL Manager. Some complaints may require Country Director oversight or consultation.4. Complaint Desk provides feedback to the complainant in his/her preferred modality: paper copy, email, or SMS text message, and informs about the appeals procedure within max. 21 calendar days The complainant has the right to appeal the feedback provided by HI within a period of maximum 30 calendar days. Appeals shall be dealt with on a case-by-case basis unless case-type precedence has been established. Complaints may require immediate action, for example an issue that poses a security threat to beneficiaries, HI staff, HI resources/assets or HI reputation should immediately be acted upon. In such a scenario, the complaint focal point needs to contact the relevant person, either by phone or by visiting them (If they are in the same office) such as area manager, safety advisor or his/her replacement and country director, deputy country directors etc. These managers should decide the future course of action by assessing the severity and the nature of the complaint. Following this, the complaint focal point should write an email containing all the details about the complaint provided by the complainant to ensure the formal submission along with adding this to FCMS centralised data sheet. All non-programmatic HI related complaints should be forwarded to the relevant staff head of the department by copying their immediate supervisor. The relevance of the complaint will be determined by the nature of the complaint as explained above. Operational complaints will be processed and delegated internally by the complaints and response focal person by forwarding the complaints and response log to the Area Manager. If the complaint entails a senior member of staff or is about the Area Manager/PLO, the complaint will be sent directly to the CD in Juba. If the complainst is about the CD, the complaints will be sent to the HQ via email : alert@hi.org However for most other programmatic complaints, the Area Manager/PLO will review the complaints listed and refer them by email to the relevant department and person For urgent action, the feedback will directly be communicated for immediate reaction. It will be included in the project action plan as soon as possible to ensure the problem will be solved timely.Regarding project/program related feedback - the feedback will be taken into account through usual coordination mechanism, including coordination meetings (including operations and support services), to ensure the project adapt to the feedback.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

8.10. Given that certain target locations particularly away from urban centers are heavily contaminated with cluster munitions, ERW and UXOs it is critical that due diligence and coordination with mine-action partners is completed before conducting outreach activities in rural areas. The presence of explosive hazards in communities would heavily impact the delivery of activities, but also factor into risk awareness and livelihood approaches. 8.9 In all locations disputes over housing and land are expected, particularly given the nature of land rights and legislation South Sudan. The nature of conﬂict and displacement has also complicated claims to land, and led to expropriation of displaced populations property. To mitigate this effectively, HI will monitor HLP issues in communities. Strong coordination with HLP partners, as well as government is required to mediate issues effectively and mediate disputes. 8.2 In target locations there are high numbers of female IDPs living in insecure and unsafe settlements. HI will coordinate closely with GBV partners, camp management (if available), UNHCR and health providers to ensure post-incident support to survivors, as well as to develop solutions to address risks with protection partners. 8.8 Given that the project is intending to support vulnerable beneﬁciaries it is essential that needs-based selection criteria are adhered to. To mitigate external interference, HI will utilize standardized vulnerability and targeting criteria, and consult all local stakeholders on this process to avoid any bias in the process. Furthermore HI will work closely with community-based organisations and representatives of marginalized groups to ensure we reach the intended beneﬁciaries.
