Project Summary

The intervention is designed to offer conflict-affected out-of-school children, adolescents, and individuals with disabilities access to safe, protective, and inclusive education as well as vocational training in both formal and informal environments. This initiative prioritizes reintegrating displaced people while assisting communities hosting returnees in the states of Adamawa, Yobe, and Borno by establishing temporary learning spaces that ensure safety and accessibility. Key components include constructing semi-permanent educational structures; working alongside other sectors to provide WASH facilities; promoting open defecation-free (ODF) practices; implementing protection services such as comprehensive case management for birth registration; addressing gender-based violence (GBV); offering mental health support (MHPSS); providing nutrition programs. Essential educational materials are distributed along with cash transfers aimed at reducing families' education expenses. Furthermore, gender-inclusive and disability-friendly education services bolster teachers’ abilities to enhance students' welfare. Scalable learning models like Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL), Kano Reading Initiative (KaRi), digital platforms or radio-based instruction aim at improving academic achievements tailored specifically towards this context. 1 - Needs

Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States in Northeast Nigeria continue to experience a severe education crisis with many out-of-school children due to conflict, climate challenges like droughts and floods, poverty prioritizing survival over schooling. Damaged infrastructure worsens teacher shortages leaving nearly two million kids without schools in these states. Attacks on school institutions continue to raise safety concerns while kidnappings deter access further. Urgent educational support is needed for internally displaced persons (IDPs) as 60% of IDP camp children lack school facilities; those in host communities also struggle to accommodate students among approximately one million school-age displaced individuals aged 3-17 years across the BAY states. In 2024, floods impacted over 250 schools in the BAY states, limiting education for 112,500 children-60,490 girls and 52,010 boys plus around 9,000 disabled children (about half of them girls). Climate change continues posing significant threats to children's wellbeing and disrupting educational services. The Education Sector reports that 1.6 million children aged 5-17 affected by crises need urgent educational support. Expected cuts in humanitarian funding for 2024 could hinder education efforts, worsening child protection issues like labor, marriage, pregnancy, gender-based violence (GBV), and armed group recruitment. Many children in the BAY states face persistent GBV-such as sexual exploitation and abuse-when lacking access to education services. About 24% of GBV survivors seeking help are minors up to age eighteen. Addressing gender needs today involves creating safe learning environments for girls, supporting menstrual health management, implementing a gender-responsive curriculum, and providing aid for out-of-school girls. Support should also extend to female educators and address adolescent reproductive health,Mental health, life skills development, and vocational training. Promoting equality by empowering all children is crucial; inclusivity must prioritize assistance to those with disabilities. This demand for essential educational support is expected to persist due to security challenges, climate crises, service gaps, and economic effects on IDPs, returnees, and host communities.Notwithstanding, Government plans to close camps may result in significant relocations back into areas lacking basic education services , hence the need for this intervention. The intervention aims to provide conflict-affected out of School children (OOSC), adolescents, and those with disabilities with access to safe, protective, and inclusive education and vocational skills in both formal and non-formal settings. The initiative focuses on reintegrating displaced individuals and supporting communities hosting returnees in Adamawa, Yobe and Borno states by setting up temporary learning spaces and ensuring safety and accessibility. Key activities include establishing semi-permanent structures, collaborating with other sectors to offer WASH facilities and campaign on ODF, and implementing protection services that support Comprehensive case management, Birth registration,GBV, MHPSS and nutrition programs. Essential educational supplies are distributed, and cash transfers to families help mitigate education costs. Additionally, gender and disability-inclusive education services are provided, enhancing teachers' capabilities to support children's well-being. Contextually relevant learning models, such as TarL, KaRi, and digital/radio education shall be scaled up to improve educational outcomes. Government and community efforts shall be bolstered with training in emergency response and skill development programs that align with crisis-affected children's lives. Rehabilitation of damaged schools and introduction of alternative learning models aims to accommodate growing learner numbers and facilitate smoother transitions for students will guide the intervention.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming within the outlined project, several modalities will be implemented across each activity. All construction and repair efforts will adhere to safety standards that incorporate inclusivity and accessibility, particularly for children with disabilities. The establishment of semi-permanent learning spaces in IDP camps will include protective measures to ensure child safety within these environments. Comprehensive child protection services will include mechanisms for birth registration, psychosocial support (PSS), and gender-based violence (GBV) support. Security measuresDistribution of educational materials will be done equitably, ensuring that vulnerable groups, such as out-of-school children, are prioritized. Cash transfers to families will be monitored to prevent misuse and ensure they support educational costs effectively.Programs will emphasize inclusivity and gender responsiveness, incorporating a curriculum that addresses menstrual health management for girls and supports female educators, thus promoting gender equality and safety.Learning models like Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) and digital education will incorporate safeguarding principles, ensuring that their delivery methods prevent exploitation and uphold learners' safety.Teacher training will include components on child protection and wellbeing-focused education. Capacity-building programs will also prepare community members and government officials to respond to emergencies effectively, emphasizing protection considerations.By strengthening community structures, the project will foster environments conducive to educational initiatives and child protection. Community leaders and stakeholders will be engaged to ensure sustained commitment to these goals.Nutrition programs will incorporate safety guidelines to ensure that food distribution within schools is conducted equitably and safely. Regarding the mechanisms to prevent and address sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), the project will implement a comprehensive PSEA communication strategy. This will include raising awareness among all stakeholders about the prohibited nature of SEA and providing clear guidance and training on how to report concerns. An appropriate complaints mechanism will be established, ensuring anonymity and confidentiality to protect those who report or are affected by SEA incidents. This mechanism will be rigorously monitored to maintain its effectiveness and responsiveness.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To gather feedback from the most vulnerable, the project uses various strategies: 1. Community Engagement and Support: Engaging local leaders helps create feedback loops for gathering insights directly from the community, including marginalized groups. 2. School Infrastructure Rehabilitation and Expansion: Community meetings at each stage will ensure all voices are heard, with feedback mechanisms like suggestion boxes or forums for input from every sector, including vulnerable groups. 3. Safety Measures: Implement child protection services for safe feedback from children and families. Use PSS and GBV support to empower vulnerable groups, like women and children, who face or fear these challenges. 4. Inclusive and Gender-Responsive Education: Gathering feedback from children with disabilities and female students through regular interactions and specialized focus groups is crucial for assessing inclusivity. 5. Alternative and Non-Formal Learning: Diverse programs can be assessed for adaptability, effectiveness, and participant feedback on their experiences. 6. Empowerment and Capacity Building: Training programs enhance feedback collection by equipping teachers and community members with skills to empathetically interact with vulnerable populations, fostering an environment where individuals feel empowered to provide input. 7. Strengthen School Nutrition Support: Use school nutrition programs to engage vulnerable families through surveys and parental sessions, gathering feedback on nutrition and education impacts. The approach ensures thorough feedback through direct, safe, and empowering methods. Continuous dialogue and inclusive mechanisms enable the project to effectively address the needs of the most vulnerable.
