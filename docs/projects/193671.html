Project Summary

The multi-sectoral refugee response is targeting vulnerable South Sudanese refugees, both protracted and new arrivals, in West and South Kordofan States. This intervention links with and responds to the 2023 Refugee Response Strategy under the HRP to strengthen the protection environment for refugees, to enable access to timely protection and lifesaving assistance as well as to provide equitable access to basic services for refugees and strengthening opportunities for resilience and self-reliance. Under the refugee response, Concern will provide support for 36,889 individuals in both South/West Kordofan are targeted across 9 localities. Concern will deliver integrated health, nutrition, WASH, and FSL/livelihoods services for vulnerable refugees in host community settings in South and West Kordofan through health/nutrition facilities and their catchment areas.
Under the health sector, the project will deliver primary health care services through medical consultations and treatment, reproductive health and outpatient services for refugee children, PLW and family members.
Under nutrition sector, through an integration with the health services in the catchment areas of the facility, the program will respond to severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) treatment for refugee children under 5 years of age, and PLW through CMAM approach.
The proposed plan is integrating WASH services in the same catchment locations by renovating water facilities and constructing water points in health/ nutrition centers, and strengthening hygiene promotions. The project will also provide soap and sanitary kits for refugee women to improve personal hygiene for vulnerable refugees and emphases on disease outbreak sensitive WASH programming.
South Sudanese refugees in West and South Kordofan state will be provided FSL services such as agricultural support (in-kind e.g. seed packages and farmers tools), livestock support, and vocational training especially targeting youth. Conflict sensitivity, gender, inclusion, and protection approaches will be mainstreamed.

1 - Needs

In view of the 2023 humanitarian programming cycle, and as of 30 September 2023, 1,143,339 Refugees & Asylum Seekers are registered in Sudan. Out of them, 7,240 arrived in the month of September, and cumulatively 48,406 arrived between January and September 2022. South Sudanese Refugees & Asylum Seekers in Sudan are currently 811,444, out of which 5,895 arrived in the month of September and 35,942 came between January and September 2022. Since mid-August, 398 SSR families (1,941 persons) have been reported in El Leri alone, South Kordofan State. In 2022 sustained economic decline continues to affect Sudan, with the annual inflation of the Sudanese pound recorded at 148% in June. Lower than average harvests have reduced cereal availability, contributing to rising food prices, and exacerbated by the conflict in Ukraine as Sudan is largely dependent on Russia and Ukraine for wheat imports. Similarly, fuel prices have risen significantly. Rising prices, high inflation, and challenges in supply chains combined have significantly reduced household purchasing power and resulting in a projected 11.7 million persons across Sudan likely to be in Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Phase 3 or higher between June and September 2022. Refugees, largely cut off from formal economies, are particularly vulnerable to the effects of declined purchasing power, heightened food insecurity, associated negative coping strategies and protection risks. Within this context, persistent and large-scale needs shape the humanitarian context affecting refugees across Sudan. South Sudanese communities living in the greater Kordofan continue to face serious access constraints to basic health services and livelihoods opportunities living conditions that do not meet minimum standards. Health: the currently running health facilities lack minimum PHC services for both refugees and host families and the state MoH has extremely limited resources (both human and financial resources) to run and further develop the existing services. In West Kordofan particularly Concern is operating in Meriam, Kharasana locations where the majority of the refugees settled within the host communities. In South Kordofan Leri, Talodi and Gadeer, apart from Kadugli are the focused areas of response. All these locations have limited PHC services (weak referral services, medicines, few medical expertise etc.) that requires Concern to continue PHC services. Nutrition: according to Concern's SMART survey conducted in West and South Kordofan operation areas (15 facilities) in 2021 indicates critical need for nutrition intervention e.g. GAM 17.4% in SK, 19.2% WK, SAM 3.8% in SK, 5.1 in WK, Child Min. Diet Diversity 60% in SK, 58% in WK. The Coverage Survey also indicated that only 49% of OTP services available, TSFP coverage is 41%. WASH: the health facility assessment conducted in West and South Kordofan, including institution coverage, reported only 15.7% average score across the surveyed 15 health facilities capacities domain. Lack of potable water, latrines, incinerators, hygiene practices undermine the PHC minimum requirements. FSL: the IPC Phase 3 is projected to worsen in all our operational areas in Kordofan. In line with these projections, the main identified needs flagged by refugees themselves in the areas where Concern is working are agricultural inputs support such as drought-tolerant seeds and farm tools, livestock support with adequate training and capacity building how to manage the given resources in a sustainable way, considering the climate change and resilience driven needs.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will not have direct significant impact on the environment. However, it is acknowledged that health and nutrition services that the project seeks to support can have negative impacts on the environment, especially in camp settings. Poor medical waste management might pose health risks. The project will ensure proper training and awareness raising for the health workers on environmental protection particularly related to proper waste management (segregation, storage and incinerating medical waste). The project will also provide basic facilities to support appropriate segregation and disposal of medical waste according to best practices. As per any rehabilitation planned, the use of locally available materials will be suggested to reduce the environmental footprint and increase cultural sensitivity. During the renovation of facilities, Concern will ensure that renovation debris are properly handled during and after the renovation. WASH: similar to the health/nutrition component, renovation work will be done with careful handling of construction debris. FSL: the distribution of drought tolerance seeds will contribute to the climate change driven needs to use significant water, capacity building training for refugees e.g. Climate Smart Agriculture (Concern signature training) will provide knowledge and improved practices on how to adopt to the new climate while cultivating the plots/land.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create; and reducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied for this intervention. Among other vulnerable individuals, refugee mothers and their children from the host communities who walk many hours to reach the centers will be oriented about ways to protect themselves on the way. Each HF, where the mobile services will refer to, will maintain the availability of a protection focal person and the communities will be informed about the role of this person. Traditional healers will be targeted by behavior change activities at community level. Focal persons in each HF (one male and one female) will be responsible for dealing with GBV cases and related protection issues. In the community where refugees settled, there will also have one-one protection focal point (possibly one female, and one male) for dealing with sensitive issues. Children will be in high focus, with field staff duly reporting on safeguarding. Beneficiaries will be oriented about their rights regarding the project objectives (e.g. access to information, access to services). In order to increase equal access for the most vulnerable from different ethical groups and backgrounds (IDPs, refugees, and host communities), information sharing about project implementation, achievements, selection of beneficiaries, will be provided continuously through a selected focal person in each group.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by two central documents, notably the organisation’s Programme Participant Protection Policy and Code of Conduct; and the 2010 Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) Standard in Accountability and Quality Management. Concern’s Programme Participant Protection Policy sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries – children, women and men. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create; and reducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. As a certified member of HAP, Concern is committed to implementing the HAP Standard related to different aspects of beneficiaries’ accountability. Three of these aspects are particularly relevant to conflict sensitivity and protection: beneficiaries’ access to information; beneficiaries’ participation in all the stages of the programme; and establishing a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM). As of August 2022, Concern Sudan’s Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM) was updated and aligned with overall staff and communities’ expectations as well as ground realities through a consultative exercise undertaken with the aim to adjust Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). As a part of the scheme roll-out in West Darfur, Concern will incorporate beneficiaries’ feedback through two ways. Every 6 months Accountability team will conduct FGDs with programme participants, as well as interviews with other stakeholders related to effectiveness of CRM system. At the time of conclusion of a complaint, each complainant will be asked for their feedback using a Satisfaction and Consultation form. Based on data received from these two sources, Concern will regularly evaluate and re-design the CRM system to improve its accessibility, responsiveness, efficiency and anonymity. Accountability towards affected populations as well as awareness raising is ensured through information materials as brochures, posters, business cards in Arabic, as well as through an informative video developed for the process. All posters, brochures, and business cards will be distributed to programme participants and will be posted/available wherever Concern Sudan work to ensure transparent information sharing.
