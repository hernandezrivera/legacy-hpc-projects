Project Summary

The objective of this project is to enhance and sustain emergency food production through provision of complementary and integrated agricultural crop and vegetable seeds, farming tools, fishing kits and agricultural extension support. The other objective of the project will be to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that builds the resilience of households. This project will be implemented using standard operating procedures that seek to prevent the spread and transmission of COVID19. It seeks to enable vulnerable IDPs and host communities to recover from crises, with a focus on seeking more durable solutions and building the capacity of communities to cope and withstand recurrent shocks and strengthen resilience to future shocks. It will strengthen self-reliance in own food production by improving the agricultural productive capacity of farmers.
The communities have endured food insecurity caused by a complex interplay of climate variability induced cycles of droughts and floods, below average crop productions and poor farming skills. These have been compounded by intensified inter-ethnic conflict, livestock raiding and worsening economic situation across the country.
This project will provide integrated streams of livelihood inputs including crop and vegetable seeds, farming tools including ox-ploughs as well as provision of fishing kits. The project will undertake seed fairs to support local market. This will be accompanied by a comprehensive farmer training package on sustainable agroecological farming practices and climate smart agriculture including soil and water conservation techniques. The project will be implemented through a network of farmer extension services using recruited and well trained and equipped extension workers. The training will also include improved fishing practices, hygienic handling of fish and fish preservation for fisher folk.
The project will reach 9,000 H/holds (54,000 people- IDPs and host communities) in Gogrial East and Gogrial West Countries of Warrap State and Yirol East Country in Lakes State. The community will be involved in targeting and a clear vulnerability criteria will be agreed. As part of beneficiary selection CAFOD will also deploy its SADI (Safety, Access, Dignity and Inclusivity) Framework to ensure inclusion of vulnerable and often hard to reach groups such as female headed households, disabled and elderly persons. To realize the inclusion of persons with disability, the project will ensure that at least, 10 percent of the benefiting households have a person/s living with disabilities.
Despite this being a single sector application, through complementary funding from other sources, CAFOD will incorporate other sectoral themes to this project, integrating WASH and Protection services to achieve greater impact which is in line with the cluster strategy. It will incorporate improving access to water by drilling new boreholes, rehabilitation of existing water sources. It will undertake hygiene promotion and sanitation activities. In addition to protection mainstreaming the project will use other funding streams to integrate protection specific activities such as GBV case management and psychosocial support services to survivors of SGBV.
The project will collaborate and work with other agencies working on Food Security and Livelihood intervention to achieve synergy and avoid duplication of efforts. The project will work closely with the agricultural ministry to utilize their expertise and staffs who will be playing key roles in farmer training and extension services. It will collaboration with HAGI, NPA, and WFP or its partners such as CRS involved in food distributions in the area. The project will also collaborate with health and nutrition actors such as HeLP and Ministry of Health to target and support households with malnutrition cases with vegetable gardening.

1 - Needs

South Sudanese people’s resilience continues to be tested by complex and protracted humanitarian crises. South Sudan is already facing a worrying humanitarian situation with almost half of all counties having a convergence of high needs related to food insecurity, protection, and water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH). The Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) Round 25/ 26, cited in HNO 2021 about52.6 per cent of household are acutely food insecure while only 36 per cent of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. According to South Sudan Consolidated Findings from the IPC Technical Working Group and External Reviews the counties of Yirol East in Lakes State and Gogrial East and West in Warrap State which are CAFOD’s area of operation are under IPC 3, that is, crisis level food insecurity and from the overall population circa 50% require humanitarian assistance. The situation is projected to deteriorate to IPC Phase 4 in the period April to July 2021. During this period around 58% of Warrap State and 57% of Lakes State population will be in crisis (IPC 3 and above) whilst 4% of Warrap State population will be in IPC Phase 5. This deterioration is driven by the seasonal depletion of food stocks, likelihood of high food prices, reduced market functionality and access challenges occasioned by the onset of rainfall that leads to the worsening of road conditions, as well as the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, economic shocks and currency devaluation that will continue to erode households’ purchasing power . According to the IPC AMN projection analysis of 2021, seasonal deterioration of acute malnutrition situation is expected during the lean season (April to July 2021) due to increased morbidity, high food insecurity and poor infant and young child feeding practices. From the overall total people falling under IPC 4 AMN, 8% are from Warrap State.The multisectoral need assessment carried out by CTP and TOCH in November 2020, highlights the high level of food insecurity in Gogrial East and West. The cumulative impact of the war including recent post-independence conflicts, resulted in reduced resilience capacity deepening impoverishment and increasing exposures and vulnerability to external shocks. Warrap State and Lakes State continue to bear the brunt of intensified intercommunal violence which has displaced communities resulting in loss of lives and productive assets as they fled, making it difficult to pursue viable livelihoods activities. Despite the generosity of host communities in welcoming displaced persons they also experience poverty, and their coping capacity has been stretched to the limit. Climate variability has resulted in increasing frequency and intensity of cyclical droughts and floods. The already precarious humanitarian situation has been exacerbated by the floods which displaced many people across South Sudan. The floods have left a trail of destruction for the second year in a row . The floods incidence in 2020 are said to be the worst in 50 years. Due to flooding in Jonglei State in 2020, about 1,800 households were displaced into in the neighbouring Lakes State, settling in Malek Payam, Yirol East county which is CAFOD’s area of operation. Likewise, due to floods in Warrap State, 758 HHs were displaced from Ajiep Payam in Gogrial East and Manyang Payam in Gogrial West County . CTP assisted and supported the persons displaced into Yirol East with one ration of food supplies.

2 - Project activities

The project will assist crisis affected communities’, households, and individuals to produce enough food sufficient to meet their food requirement for a year or until next harvest season. This will be achieved through provision of locally adaptable, short maturing and high yield crops and vegetable seed varieties and fishing equipment. Seeds for traditional staple crops grown in the area like sorghum, cow peas, and ground nuts which are also major source of income and contributes immensely to food security will be provided. The quantity of seed will be based on the UNFAO crop and vegetable seed kit standard. Farmers will be provided with farm tools and implements such as oxploughs. This support will be accompanied by contextually tailored extension support to increase uptake of sustainable and improved agroecological and climate smart agricultural practices. Training will cover appropriate seeding levels, soil and water conservation, mulching, tie and ridge farming, pest management and post-harvest handling. In order to bring deep and sustained changes in the food security and livelihood status of the households, the project will provide a comprehensive and tailored agricultural extension services to the beneficiaries in order to improve productivity and production. The project will deploy agricultural extension staff and assistants from CAFOD, local partner and collaborate with state and county agriculture department to educate and train farmers targeted under the programme. Opportunities will be explored for collaboration with WFP and FAO through the voucher or cash for assets to support poor resourced farmers to plough, plant and weed farmlands. The intervention will be implemented in ways that support local markets through seed fairs based on seed market assessments. Given the high levels of malnutrition in most of the project areas CAFOD training package also includes training on preparation and consumption of nutritious foods given especially mothers and those with malnourished children. This will involve training on how to prepare various dishes using locally available foods and vegetables. Households affected by malnutrition will be prioritized for support with vegetable seeds and assorted hand tools to enhance food dietary diversity of that household. The area where this project will be implemented has high potential for fishing activities. As a result, the project will provide training on fishing activities, along with this, will also provide recommended fishing kits according to UNFAO fishing kit standards. Among the training package, will be how to make and repair nets, hygienic handling of a catch of fish and preservation methods such as smoking, salting and fish storage. To ameliorate work burden of women collecting firewood’s, and health hazards caused by smoke inhalation when cooking such as respiratory and sight problems the project will promote adoption of locally adaptable fuel-efficient cooking stoves. This will also minimize deforestation. The will incorporate COVID19 messaging for infection prevention and control (IPC) as well as adopt standard operating procedures to prevent and avoid infection. Conflict sensitive approaches will be used. Although not part of this concept note this project will be complemented by funding from other sources to integrate by WASH activities It will incorporate improving access to water, hygiene promotion and sanitation. It will use other funding sources to integrate protection specific activities such as GBV case management and psychosocial support services to survivors of SGBV Gender equality and women empowerment will be mainstreamed. Conflict sensitive programming and the promotion of peaceful co-existence will be mainstreamed in the project. The project will be implemented in collaboration with local agriculture ministry, and in coordination with local the county FSL cluster in which the local partners of CAFOD are members.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

CAFOD implemented commodity voucher for food and non-food items in previous projects in Yirol East in 2020. The venture into CVA was after comprehensive market assessment and feasibility analysis which informed the decision to undertake CVA in both Yirol East, and Gogrial East and West counties. The market assessment to ascertained the market potential to support the approach. Things considered in the assessment included functionality and access to the market by the affected population, availability and source of goods and services, and traders’ potential to supply the market. Cash transfer programming (CTP) refers to all programs where cash (or vouchers for goods or services) is directly provided to beneficiaries. It is an approach that builds upon linkages, capacities, incentives, and relationships to encourage effective market recovery. The logistical burdens and risks that are usually experienced by agencies in transporting in-kind supplies to end beneficiaries are thus transferred to the vendors or suppliers. The use of vouchers or cash reduces overall costs of the assistance and thus more people can be reached with limited resources. The engagement of local vendors in supplying items to the beneficiaries is intended to support the local market dynamics. Through this approach, the cash or vouchers given to beneficiaries and exchanged with local vendors for supplies would advance financial linkages in the local markets as well as boosting the purchasing power and local economy. Cash and vouchers assistance (CVA) is globally felt to be a more dignified form of assistance for humanitarian aid recipients because it promotes dignity of the participants. Individuals accustomed to supporting themselves and their families suddenly become dependent on aid and charity. While their sense of dignity cannot be easily and fully restored, cash and voucher transfers offer some choice and a small degree of control in a situation where many feel they have none. The affected populations preferred the flexibility that cash offered them and the choice it offered them to prioritise their own needs. We have realised that use of cash and vouchers empowers participants through ownership of their needs and the opportunity to purchase locally preferred supplies. Since CAFOD intends to provide seeds through vouchers or seed fairs this will further boost the agricultural productivity potential of local farmers and trigger market growth. Local purchase of seeds will save local farmers of transportation costs in search of better markets in Juba, The local participants will also benefit on acquiring seeds with adaptable to local ecological conditions.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CAFOD has strict policy on PSEA which guide all staff in their daily interactions with beneficiaries. All staff and volunteers, consultants hired by CAFOD must adhered to CAFOD’s PSEA policy and staff sign staff code of behaviour and safeguarding policy. Staff are inducted on the policies upon recruitment, volunteers and contractors are also oriented and expected to abide by the policies. Programme participants are made aware of the policies and what to expect from CAFOD’s staff. CAFOD has safe and confidential channels for reporting SEA concerns directly to the Country Representative or focal person in UK or through ethics point website, helpdesk, hotline, information kiosk and other channels which is regularly checked, and timely responses provided to the complainant. CAFOD carries out regular community awareness on the existing channels of airing out their complaints and feedback as well as making them understand that it is their right to complain. In addition, there is a policy on whistleblowing which protects the whistle-blower.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will use SADI framework which is a tool for protection risk assessment of the various categories of the vulnerability. It ensures the design of activities is accessible to the most vulnerable across Sex, Age and Disability, as well as targeting the most vulnerable groups including women, boys, girls, PLWD, and elderly. CAFOD has created women and girls’ friendly spaces where they can give their feedback and complaints in a safe and dignified way to trained staff who know how to handle such issues. This has cultivated confidence in the vulnerable people coming forward to air out their views. Focus group discussions are conducted with different categories of vulnerable persons and key informant interview with service providers who work with vulnerable groups such as people living with disabilities, elderly and women and girls. CAFOD works with local partners who are embedded in the local communities. They employ local staff who understands the culture and languages of the communities. This improves understanding of the local context and cultural structures in the areas of operation which is important for access and acceptance, and in-depth analysis of the needs of different vulnerable groups. Some project documents are produced in local language e.g., posters, IEC materials, and information on complaint and feedback mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CAFOD has an information kiosk which is a stand, booth, or type of gazebo that has one open side, and includes free informative literature about the project. The information kiosks agents’ main purpose is to help guide community members by offering information and advice about the project. CAFOD has developed information sharing pack which is disseminated to Project participants. CAFOD has a robust, safe, and responsive feedback and complaints handling across its programme for every group in a community, particularly the most marginalised, and that there is consultation in the project design, the feedback loop is closed, and that the effectiveness of the mechanism is monitored. This allows room for flexibility in programmes to ensure they have a system that is appropriate and adapted for their context. 7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Complaints received/logged will regularly be analysed by the focal person and timely responded to and the programme adjusted on real time to ensure gaps identified are urgently addressed. The project carries out regular monitoring, project reviews, evaluations, community consultations, which are used to inform the project decisions. For example, the project carries out various project distribution monitoring to assess the satisfaction levels of various aspects of the project. Lesson learnt are used to make adjustments to projects, e.g., CAFOD had to prefinance seed procurement in response to community feedback for timely provision of seeds before the back donor agreement was signed. In another project, CAFOD responded to community request to provide maize flour instead of sorghum. In Yirol East, CAFOD increased the number of distribution points from seven to eleven to reduce the distances moved by beneficiaries which is crucial in minimising protection risks. These are some of the examples of how the beneficiary feedback is used to adapt project assistance.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project will make a deliberate effort to explore, understand and mitigate programme related risks leading to the development and implementation of strategy that minimize and address all ranges of threats and risks both external and internal. In practice, this means that all intervention responses will be guided by Core Humanitarian Standards to which CAFOD is certified and Do-No-Harm and conflict-sensitivity approaches through analysis of risks to programme participants and environment. In relation to risk 8.1 on long distances to distribution centres CAFOD has proactively mitigated this risk in its projects and will do so for this project. In the past there were 7 distribution sites in Yirol, but because of protection risks related to long walking distances, an additional 4 distribution centers were created to reduce distances. In Gogrial distribution points were increased after consultations with traders who were asked to open temporary shops closer to participants. As part of its responsibility of mitigating risk 8.2 and 8.3 on SEA, CAFOD has various policies on PSEA policy, code of behaviour and safeguarding policies which are signed by every staff member, volunteers and contractors before any official engagement to ensure protection of project participants. Project participants are also made aware of these policies through various information sharing ways including setting up of information kiosks and agents in the project locations. CAFOD and its partners have a robust complaint and feedback handling mechanism in place, and hotline to receive complaints and feedback. CAFOD carries out regular community awareness on the existing channels of airing out their complaints and feedback as well as making them understand that it is their right to complain. In addition, there is a policy on whistleblowing which protects the whistleblower. CAFOD has clearly laid out procedures on handling and responding to feedback and complaints. Complaints are categorised and serious, sensitive complaints such as SEA are handled by senior management with clear investigation and disciplinary protocols it must be resolved within set time limits and feedback provided to affected persons. In relation to risk 8.4 on no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors CAFOD has robust protection response and prevention activities in most of its projects. CAFOD programmes provided community based GBV protection measures such as case management, psychosocial support, creation of women and girls friendly spaces, CAFOD and its partners have mapped out referral pathways trained service providers on their use. There is also training of various service providers (health workers, police, chiefs etc.) in Yirol East and Gogrial to strengthen their capacities on GBV case reporting, survivor centred handling and management of cases.As regards risk 8.8 where the risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries CAFOD has employed measures to restrict or limit this influence. CAFOD makes use of a targeting committee comprised mostly of women from the community. Targeting criteria are agreed with the community in inception meetings and their inputs incorporated to ensure no vulnerable families are left out. When targeting is completed a public meeting of community members is called for public verification and acclamation of selected persons. CAFOD also uses its SADI (Safety Access, Dignified and Inclusivity) framework to mitigate exclusion of marginalised and vulnerable groups in a project. The framework carries out a comprehensive assessment of at-risk groups and how they are affected differently by a disaster or conditions of their vulnerability using such diversity statuses such as sex, age, disability, being a refugee, or IDP, minority status and others. This leads to design of projects that meet the specific needs of groups of people.

It will build the capacity of communities to cope and withstand recurrent shocks and strengthen resilience to future shocks. It will strengthen self-reliance in own food production by improving the agricultural productive capacity of farmers. In addition to crop inputs, vegetable seeds, farming implements the project will be accompanied by a comprehensive farmer training package to improve the farmers productivity and production. Through the increase in food production and self sufficiency in own food requirements this project will help to decrease the proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

CAFOD will work closely with the IP to ensure targeting criteria prioritize female-headed households and girls, elderly and people living with disability. The participation/involvement of diverse groups will be emphasized during the entire project cycle to ensure the project contributes to changes in gender attitudes. During needs assessments and meetings, separate focus group discussions will be conducted for women and girls to ensure they feel comfortable fully express their views on sensitive issues. Men and boys FGDs will encourage awareness of the contribution of all demographic groups (women, boys, girls, men) to sustainable progress. A complaints/feedback mechanism already in place will be utilized, monitored and strengthened. In addition, gender, disability and protection concerns will be the major focus during the design, construction and utilization of project services CAFOD programme with our local partner who are part of this programme and with the required expertise on gender will greatly influence the gender sensitivity of this programme. CAFOD's implementing partners, CDOR and TOCH South Sudan have gender and protection officers and Monitoring and Evaluation Officers to ensure the programme design and implementation and monitoring is gender sensitive.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Although this project will not address directly this collective outcome on decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4, it will however contribute indirectly to the outcome. This project will address part of the underlying root causes of malnutrition in children under 5. This project will provide vegetable seed kits for the production of nutritious vegetables. Households affected by malnutrition will be prioritized for support with vegetable seeds and assorted hand tools to enhance food dietary diversity of that household. Training package will includes training on preparation and consumption of nutritious foods given especially mothers and those with malnourished children. This will involve training on how to prepare various dishes using locally available foods and vegetables.The areas where this project will be implemented has high potential for fishing activities. As a result, the project will provide training on fishing activities, along with this, will also provide recommended fishing kits according to UNFAO fishing kit standards. Among the training package, will be how to make and repair nets, hygienic handling of a catch of fish and preservation methods such as smoking and salting. Provision of vegetable kits and fishing equipment would increase household food consumption and diversified dietary intake
