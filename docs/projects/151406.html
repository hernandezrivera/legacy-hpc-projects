How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Mercy-USA will devise multiple activities that seek to prevent children from dropping out, reduce the number of children from pastoral families relocating with their parents during drought spelt, and improve the quality of primary education. School Feeding Programs: In six of the poorest schools in the district, Mercy-USA will provide school meals to 6 schoolsSchool enrolment and retention tracking system: Mercy-USA will train teachers in children attendance and retention tracking monitoring and will put in place school attendance and retention system and will also sought for the support of the community education committee to ensure children who are enrolled are in schoolSchool Holiday Clubs: These will be established in order to prevent children from returning to the homes or streets during school holiday periods when schools are closed. The clubs provide meals to children and engages them in non-formal education activities (including life skills education, basic literacy and numeracy, counseling, and basic sports and recreational activities). School uniform, fees, and equipment: Mercy-USA will provides financing for the neediest of students special those from single female headed house to obtain uniforms and other basic school supplies, given that it is the cost of these basic supplies that can contribute to the poorest children dropping out of school. Teacher Training: Mercy-USA will works with tutors and local agencies to support teachers in the classroom. It will also provide teacher training in management and teamwork skills. Advocacy: Mercy-USA will conduct a series of advocacy workshops to empower children to secure their rights. These workshops involve the children themselves, teachers, social workers, and health practitioners. Topics include children's rights, child sexual abuse, and parental responsibilities.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

In most cases IDPs are not included in decision-making processes and have problems accessing education facilities due to barriers associated with cost and distance to near-by schools and in most cases the lack of integration with the host communities. Scarcity of social services causes barriers for the IDPs to access these services.All programmatic and M&E activities will be delivered in partnership with the respective MoEs and in coordination with the Education Cluster. Mercy-USA, in collaboration with the MoEs, will undertake a needs assessment of hard to reach or disadvantaged areas in the regions to identify 20 emergency affected schools in greatest need, thus targeting the most vulnerable children, including IDPs and returnees. Mercy-USA will work with other clusters to maximize the use of the established Teaching learning spaces (TLS), eg. the health cluster members as screening and immunization center, food security cluster as food distribution center and Nutrition cluster as center for screening malnourished children. As an exit strategy CECs will be trained on resources mobilization, management and maintenance of TLS, while the community will be sensitized to take charge for the continuity of the schools. This will enable them manage schools in the absence of external support.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Vulnerable persons therefore experience a cycle of vulnerability and poverty, which exposes them to protection concerns, such as isolation, exploitation and abuse. Children trek long distance in search of water distraction their learning and exposing them to harm’s way. Distance between children’s’ home and the school forces them travel to and from school exposure to further risk of harm. In adequate infrastructure and preference by parent to have their children attend secular schools have made schools to shift the timing of classes to afternoon which compromises the children’s’ safety as classes end late thus giving children sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. Children are usually not cautious when handling sharp objects or during playtime thus may get hurt. Most schools lack properly function WASH facilities and privacy which exposes them to unhygienic condition and the girl-child avoid using communal latrines which may be detrimental to their health and the privacy of the girl thus leading to drop-out. Single mother headed who are faced by economic hardships, opportunity cost takes precedence over the children schooling since children are engaged in child labour to fend for the family. Mercy-USA proposes to scale up and complement its existing education intervention (funded by Educate a Child-EAC) with anticipated funds to improve retention and wellbeing, as well as boost enrollments. Classrooms will be constructed and ensure that the timing of classes are such that the timing of classes give children sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. Single female-headed households will be provided with incentive in the form of conditional cash grants and provision of sanitary kits, girl friendly education kits. Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The recurring drought and absence of alternative source of livelihood and scarce resources has in most cases acted as triggers of conflict. The presence of large number of light arms in the hands of civilian breeds imminent use of the arms to settle scores within communities leading to lost of lives, disruption of livelihoods and displacement of vulnerable people. Mercy-USA will strive to reduce triggers of conflict by advocating for peaceful co-existence through constituting peace committees drawn from communities living within the schools catchments using the community education committees and organizing peace clubs in schools to act as peace ambassadors .
