Project Summary

Space of peace will work on improving the quality of community-based child protection through support to community structures and psycho-social support interventions, providing case management to the most vulnerable children and raising community awareness about war remnants. SOP will ensure that children, including children with special needs in Darkosh and Qourqeena can join child-friendly space which provide comprehensive child protection services and are physically safe
Child friendly spaces (CFS), one of space of peace’ emergency interventions, provides children with protected environments in which they participate in organized activities to play, socialize, learn and express themselves as they rebuild their lives. CFS will be established to implement services, and will be synchronized with services based on the minimum standards of Child protection, and this will enhance the access of girls and boys who desperately need a safe space for them to alleviate the psychological and social pressures they experienced during displacement. The project will provide children with psycho-social support, recreational and artistic activities in addition to that it will deliver mine risk education messages in communities, IDP locations such as (mobile) CFS on the risk presented by unexploded remnants of war for children, men and women.
While we will maintain our existing essential services, our teams will also be able to absorb additional beneficiaries if there is an acute emergency and activities need to be diverted to large numbers of new IDPs. This proposal therefore presents a comprehensive protection CP and Mine action program. SOP will implement this project directly, drawing on our existing operational presence, relationships with local authorities, and our capable technical staff.

4 - Needs

From 1 December 2019 to 1 January 2020, almost 300,000 people fled from their homes, mainly from southern Idleb governorate, moving further north away from the hostilities. An unstable security situation prevails with daily reports of bombardments, affecting civilian infrastructure, such as schools and IDP camps. Displacement during winter is further exacerbating the vulnerability of those affected. The continued shelling and airstrikes in the south of Idleb, particularly in civilian populated areas, puts the lives of women, men, girls and boys at risk and has resulted in significant numbers of deaths and injuries, including of children, and is significantly impacting children’s and caregivers’ psychosocial well-being. The basic needs of many displaced children and their caregivers are not covered. In such a situation, children are at risk of many protection concerns such as child recruitment, child marriage and worst forms of child labour.On 15 January 2020, SOP conducted a need assessment in the targeted locations as there is as 11,700 IDPs in Darkosh and 10585 IDPs in Qourqeenaand numbers are increasing every day, the Data Collection Methods Used were observation checklists and questionnaires together with FGDs. Based on the assessment SOP will work on improving the quality of community-based child protection through supporting community structures and psycho-social support interventions, providing case management to the most vulnerable children and raising the community awareness about war remnants. The project will ensure inclusiveness, targeting People with Special Needs for assistance, including girls and boys at risk, unaccompanied and separated children, and persons with serious health conditions. We will also prioritize those with disabilities. Mine risk education messages in communities and IDP locations are needed to be conducted to protect and reduce the impact of explosive hazards on boys, girls, women and men safety.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will contribute significantly to the gender marker as it specifically addresses services to vulnerable population. Child protection seeks to prevent and respond to abuse, neglect, exploitation of and violence against children in emergencies. Providing CP specialized Services to including case management and PSS services risks are: The lack of quality services exposes beneficiaries to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. The mitigation measures for this risk is the services mapping including those that can provide CP centered services in coordination with CP sub-cluster. Another risk is the Privacy concerns along with confidentially and safety which might prevent boys and girls to access CP response services. The mitigation measures for this risk is to ensure that confidentially principles are in line with global standards and CP SOPs.Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. SOP will provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1-Girls, boys, women and men's physical security is put at risk as a result of the violence associated with civilian populations/spaces being targeted by armed parties to the conflict; a lack of consideration for the safety and security to access/receive services; lack of consideration of contextual information (e.g. aerial bombardment) and targeting of civilian infrastructure.2-Risk that existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles (e.g child rights messaging is perceive as a threat)3- Risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups/ individuals at the exclusion of other groups/Individuals.4- Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality.5 -Children with disabilities have no access to the services.Mitigation Measures:1- Immediately suspend all activities to preserve the safety of staff and beneficiaries. While external attacks may occur, we are more likely to face internal fighting and civil unrest. Our teams receive up-to-date guidance and information from our dedicated security team.2-Liaise with "neutral" parties as much as possible and triangulate all information from all sources prior to decision making.3- Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for.4- Ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remote managed context.5- Ensure our centers are accessible to children with disabilities, particularly those with physical disability through rehabilitating the WASH facility in both centers and the center entrance to be more easy access.
