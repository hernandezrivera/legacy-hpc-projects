Project Summary

The project will be implemented by Wellspring Humanitarian Foundation (WHF) in Yobe State across 6 LGAs with total number of 7,500 learners (3,500 Male, 4,000 Female) of school-age (6-15) with a budget of 712,500 USD. The project is for 3 years from 1st March 2024 to 30th December 2026 to remove a barrier to access to education for people with special concern, IDPs, returnees and host communities in Yobe State, Northeast Nigeria.
Thereby, Promoting an inclusive education system that caters to the diverse needs of learners with special concern. Advocating for the development and implementation of a curriculum accommodating various learning styles and supporting students with disabilities.

1 - Needs

Northeast Nigeria Joint Education Need Assessment-November-2021 showed that with the participation of 647 key informants (mostly Head Teachers, Deputy Headteachers, and SBMC members) and 26 Focus Group Discussions with 212 children (8 – 17-year-old), the Joint Education Needs Assessment (JENA) 2021 - 2022 brings actualized data and analysis about Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States (BAY) in Northeast Nigeria.The main objectives of the JENA report are to:• Provide better data, information, and analysis for stakeholders and partners to understand gaps and needs of Education in Emergencies (EiE) in the 3 states -Inform the prioritization of geographic response areas• Provide recommendations on the most effective activities, approaches, and delivery mechanisms• Provide recommendations to inform decisions on resource mobilization and response planning Nigeria - Poverty despite resource wealthNigeria's paradox of poverty amidst abundant natural resources is a complex issue influenced by various factors, including mismanagement of resources, corruption, inadequate infrastructure, and socio-political challenges. Despite being rich in oil and other natural resources, a significant portion of Nigeria's population lives below the poverty line. Security Concerns: Yobe and Borno States face significant security challenges due to activities of insurgent groups like Boko Haram, leading to displacement, destruction of infrastructure, and hindrance to economic activities.Internal Displacement: Continuous conflicts and insecurity have led to the displacement of many residents, disrupting livelihoods and exacerbating poverty in these host communities.Living Situation in Host Communities (Yobe and Borno States)Displaced Population: The host communities in these states often struggle with accommodating internally displaced persons (IDPs) who flee conflict zones. This strains resources and services, contributing to poverty. Limited Access to Basic Services: Many in these host communities lack access to adequate healthcare, education, clean water, and sanitation facilities due to inadequate infrastructure and the impact of conflict. Economic Challenges: The conflict has disrupted agriculture, which is a primary livelihood for many in these regions. Lack of economic opportunities, coupled with the destruction of infrastructure, worsens the poverty situation.

WHF policies recognize protection as a key in activities and seeks to mainstream the protection into all programs. Protection mainstreaming will be through Capacity Strengthening, Protection Analysis, Protection, Monitoring and Accountability to Affected Populations. WHF has an internal gender policy, a health policy and has minimum standards for gender in emergencies as a tool to measure consistency in promoting gender equality through interventions. The Organization trains its programme personnel in gender mainstreaming and has malaria and PSEA focal points that work closely together to mainstream gender into programmes and projects.
