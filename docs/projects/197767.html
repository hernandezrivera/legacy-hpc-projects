Project Summary

This project is aimed at improving access to Legal Identity rights for crisis-affected populations (returnees, displaced persons, refugees and host communities) in Mayo-Sava, Mayo-Tsanaga and Logone-et-Chari divisions in the Far North region of Cameroon.
NRC will assist beneficiaries to access civil and legal documentation through the provision of information, counselling and legal assistance. NRC will use mobile teams to ensure that beneficiaries in hard-to-reach areas can have access to LI services. Sensitization sessions will be organized and enhanced through a combination of mass media, and community-based approaches to deliver culturally adapted messages on legal and civil documentation to affected populations.
One of the main target groups for these services will be children along with their parents who need birth certificates to sit for public exams, as well as people affected by displacement who face obstacles to access civil and identity documents to enjoy freedom of movement and access to other rights and services.
In addition to improving awareness on LI and especially on the procedures for accessing documentation, NRC’s ICLA teams will provide legal assistance to beneficiaries regarding the judicial and administrative processes of late registration of births. NRC will also address financial barriers impeding vulnerable populations from accessing civil and identity documentation by covering the cost of the establishment of birth certificates through indirect registration. Moreover, ICLA will advocate for a reduction in the fee imposed by judicial authorities for late registration of births as well as for the medical certificate required for late registration. NRC will join forces with other humanitarian actors within the regional and national civil documentation platforms to advocate for the revision of national laws and procedures on legal identify and registration of civil status events.
In order to improve the quality of services including the issuance of documentation void of errors, NRC will strengthen the capacities of civil status structures (courts, civil status centres, health personnel, etc.), local authorities and humanitarian actors on the legal framework and procedures for the registration of civil status events. NRC and BUNEC will facilitate the workshops and together with officials from MINDEVEL, the team will carry- out joint supervision to civil status centres to ensure proper use of knowledge and provision of quality and timely services to beneficiaries.
The project will therefore increase access to civil documentation and protection for vulnerable populations including freedom of movement, and access to basic services, including but not limited to education.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Violence perpetrated by armed groups in the Far North of Cameroon has led to forced displacement of populations and caused many human rights concerns. In Round 24 (September 2022) of the DTM , 82,586 households of 573,184 individuals (43% male, 57% female) were identified, including 385,372 IDPs (67%), 49,660 out-of-camp refugees (9%) and 138,152 returnees (24%). Following massive displacement in the crisis affected regions, the already weak civil status service system continue to deteriorate. Some civil registries are no longer functional due to destruction or flight of personnel owing to insecurity. This continue to affect access to civil documentation especially in localities receiving IDPs and refugees as the pressure and demand have also increased. In the Multi sectorial need assessment conducted in September 2022 by IOM, 69% of adults do not possess legal identity documents. This is same for children as the majority of them are in need of a birth certificate. Besides ignorance and limited knowledge of procedures, the non-possession of birth certificates and National Identify Cards in the region is attributed to poverty, as many claim that they do not have the finances to pay for documentation due to reduced income. Distance is also an obstacle to accessing civil documentation with most civil status have been closed due to the conflict so people have to travel long distances to access the centres in other locations. NRC will work with the affected communities to address the challenges affecting access to civil documentation and support them in the establishment of the documents through information, counselling and legal assistance (ICLA) provided by mobile teams.Information and counselling sessions on legal identity: ICLA Mobile teams will organize sensitization sessions on LI with focus on birth registration procedures (including late birth registration), and on the importance of a birth certificate. They will be deployed in pairs (male and female) in displacement and return areas to provide guidance on birth registration procedure (including late birth registration) and facilitate access to birth certificates for beneficiaries. Legal assistance on legal identity: In collaboration with judicial authorities, NRC will organise mobile court sessions in the communities for hearings and declaratory judgements for the establishment of birth certificates for children whose births have not been registered within the legal period of 3 months. This activity will bring together magistrates, clerks, civil registration officers and secretaries, children and their parents. NRC will also address financial barriers impeding vulnerable populations from accessing civil and identity documentation. Capacity building of stakeholders: NRC will strengthen the capacity of institutions, local authorities, as well as humanitarian actors (e.g., civil registration centers, courts, traditional leaders, etc.) through training workshops on legal identify in collaboration with BUNEC. Capacity building will include support to civil status structures with materials and equipment needed to boost civil status registration and timely production of documentation.Coordination: NRC will coordinate the response with other actors through the civil documentation platform; including information sharing, joint advocacy and monitoring and evaluation.
