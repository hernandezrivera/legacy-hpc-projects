Project Summary

Activities :
The proposed project aims to address the critical needs of vulnerable conflict-affected populations living in sites, settlements, and surrounding areas in Northern Syria, through camp and coordination support. Specifically, ACTED will implement CCCM responses to coordinate service provision and build local capacity for self-management. ACTED’s proposed assistance, through its static and mobile CCCM teams, will include key information management activities such as service mapping, risks and demographic assessments, running of coordination structures through regular engagement and advocacy and the strengthening of self-management structures through trainings. ACTED will focus on the reinforcement of accountability systems (e.g. Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms). In addition to providing CCCM core activities, ACTED CCCM mobile team will conduct rapid needs assessments throughout Northern Syria which will primarily use to inform the programmatic decision to deploy.
In NES, ACTED will continue operating in sites where it has a static CCCM presence, and continue conducting rapid assessments to identify informal sites most in need of mobile support, which it will select based on the assessed level of need and its own response capacity. ACTED will continue to provide static CCCM support through maintaining the Camp Management function in Areesheh; Washokani camps (Al-Hasakeh governorate) and Abu Khashab Camp (Deir-ez-Zor governorate), its mobile support to Serekaniye camp (the mobile status will be re-assessed regularly and might evolve depending on situation on the field), as well as provide support to sites and settlements in Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor governorates. This support includes the coordination of service providers to ensure the coverage of in-camp multi-sectoral needs, site maintenance, and support to camp committee and communication mechanisms. For in-camp livelihood activities not targeting entire resident populations of targeted sites, ACTED will apply standard demographic and vulnerability criteria for beneficiary prioritization. In NWS, ACTED will continue providing support in sites based on coordination with Cluster partners, assessed needs, and its own capacity. ACTED will target camps in the Azaz district (Northern Aleppo governorate) through site maintenance and operational assistance, and support to camp committee and communication mechanisms.
Regarding its general CCCM approach through 2023, ACTED plans to maintain full static presence in camps that do not have viable and durable solutions or transitional capacity with a focus on capacity-building and resilience programming. In static sites which do have avenues for durable solutions, in the short term, ACTED will start sensitization of local authorities, service providers, humanitarian actors, and communities and ensure that the transition processes are established in a collaborative, coordinated manner, and abide by key indicators within CCCM minimum standards. In the long term, ACTED will retain some level of CCCM presence through site management support and strengthen the quality of the coordination and management within the camps. This project will target 231,664 individuals

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Needs : Across Syria, over 2,4 million persons are expected to be in need of CCCM assistance as per the (unreleased) 2024 HNO. The population in ten camps with a static camp management has increased by 4,320 individuals since the beginning of 2022, while the rate of new arrivals significantly outweighs the departures, with 7,000 individuals on camp waiting list. This is alarming, specifically for women, girls and persons with disabilities who can face increased protection risks. While instances of significant population displacement are rare, they remain a concern with potential displacement events in the area and formal camps already at their limits or are undergoing relocation plans due to floods. Smaller numbers of IDPs are also settled in informal sites around main cities, with very limited assistance coverage despite high level of needs, calling for a continued mobile CCCM response. Across areas, IDPs remain heavily dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs due to lack of livelihoods. Priority needs remain food, winterization needs, as the camps and settlements are susceptible to cold weather as well as flooding, bearing a heavy toll on shelter units and access roads, respectively, particularly highlighting needs for CCCM-led camp maintenance works. Chronic underfunding and access constraints have led to severe gaps in camp management. In NWS, 76% of IDP sites do not have a camp management system in place.PSEA: ACTED has developed training packages on the matter which are delivered to all its staff members during the first month of employment and through regular refresher trainings. These trainings will also be delivered to incentive workers, CfW participants, and Camp Committees and Camp Administration members involved in ACTED’s activities under this award. Lastly, these training sessions will also be delivered to all the contractors under this intervention to ensure beneficiaries’ safety during the delivery of the services. ACTED AME Unit will also conduct monitoring of the services delivery through contractors to ensure it is conducted in a safely and timely manner. ACTED AME will also conduct satisfaction surveys with beneficiaries at the end of the services delivery to gather information on the beneficiaries’ satisfaction with these services and on the behavior of ACTED staff and contractors, enabling ACTED to take corrective action in a timely manner.
