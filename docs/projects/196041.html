Project Summary

UNHCR will strive to ensure that Rohingya refugee children aged 3 to 24 years have access to equitable quality education opportunities through the continuation of existing programs. This will involve maintaining existing learning facilities, recruiting, training and deploying teachers as well as procuring teaching and learning materials, expanding learning facilities by constructing and maintaining existing classrooms with gender segregated latrines and WASH facilities. Considering the impacts of school closures during COVID-19 on learning, UNHCR and its partners will implement Accelerated Education programs and a range of EdTech interventions to tackle gaps in learning and to support language acquisition needed to access the newly scaled up Myanmar Curriculum (MC). The continued scale up of the MC, Accelerated Learning and the transition of Learning Competencies Framework and Approach (LCFA) level III students into the MC will be a critical undertaking for 2023. To ensure the provision of quality education, Myanmar language training and continuous Teacher Professional Development will be provided to ensure educators have the required language competencies and expected teaching skills to deliver inclusive quality education. In addition, a two-month basic pedagogy training will be provided to teachers’ along with refresher training of the on-going mentorship program. The continuation of regular training on cross-cutting issues {such as child protection, gender and disability inclusion, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and mental health, psychosocial support (MHPSS) and gender responsive pedagogy} will also be provided. The project will recruit Rohingya women, trained and deployed to ensure gender parity and also focused on disability inclusion through identifying CWD, ensuring accessibility to the learning facilities, and providing individual support for those most vulnerable. In addition to mainstream education for 41,246 learners, UNHCR will also continue to provide interventions for 9,901 pre-primary children aged 3-4 years with early childhood development (ECD) sessions aligning with the ECD working group standards and guidance. In addition, UNHCR will continue with adolescent clubs, scaling up to reach 20,743 adolescents and youth, whereby basic literacy, numeracy and prevocational activities will be provided. In close collaboration with the livelihood sector, UNHCR will provide upskilling and bridging interventions for 620 adolescents and youth including gender diverse people looking to enhance their problem solving, computer literacy and academic skills. UNHCR will continue ensuring community engagement in the overall project cycle through regular community consultations, monthly parent meetings and Community Education Support Groups (CESG) whilst maintaining Age Gender and Diversity (AGD) principles. It will enhance school community governance and participation mechanisms by including the community in risk reduction activities as well as integrating school community-based participatory planning and conducting community awareness on key areas as they emerge. It will continue its host community support targeting 3,000 students through maintaining and operationalizing of the community technology Access and the satellite teachers training facility, constructing, or renovating classrooms and WASH facilities and provide classroom furniture and supplies. To engage with the refugee led education activities within the camp such as madrassa education and private schools, it will seek to include refugee teachers from these groups into training opportunities to building a more cohesive relationships between different education provisions existing within the camps. To ensure social cohesion and peacebuilding the project will observe different days such as 16 days activities, literacy days, refugee days and organize annual cultural events and sport competition.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

As per the 2022 ACAPS-NPM Thematic report, access to education for Rohingya children in the camps remains challenging. While the number of education facilities increased sevenfold between 2017–2020, the number of school-age children also increased, and many older children currently receive either no formal education or education with private tutors that must be paid for. Education assessments found that more than three quarters of Rohingya children continued their studies from home during the pandemic. However, remote learning has been challenging for children in the Rohingya camps given the limited resources. In 2020, 62% of households reported facing challenges in supporting their children’s remote study (J-MSNA Rohingya 2020), a challenge which has likely continued as schools only reopened in camps in early 2022. In addition, UNHCR has received reports of early marriage, teen pregnancy, child labor and children engaged in harmful practices. The consequences of the above could lead to student dropout and mental health and well being challenges, making it difficult for children to focus in lessons and to make the necessary progress needed to move forward with their education. Low literacy levels amongst the Rohingya population results in a lack of qualified and trained teachers which remains one of the major barriers to providing quality education, with this challenge further exaggerated amongst female teachers. While the implementation of the Myanmar Curriculum scale-up, challenges in the accessibility of the curriculum continues. Whilst all teaching and learning materials are in a non-native language, (English or Burmese) teaching in a multilingual setting is very challenging especially for facilitators/teachers with no qualifications and limited training. There is a significant challenge in the Registered Camps who, based on community consultations, are not willing to accept the MC and wish to continue with access to the NCTB leaving significant numbers of children out of school. There is a need to find a solution to curriculum access for this group to ensure improved attendance in lessons by children and school support from the community. The 2021 Age Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment conducted by REACH found that significantly lower numbers of children with disabilities enrolled in formal or informal learning centers. While 65% of children with disabilities aged five to nine years, who are enrolled, reportedly attend learning centres for at least four days per week, in comparison, 88% of children without disabilities in the same age group are enrolled. Similarly, the proportions of children reportedly not having completed any education were higher among children with disabilities relative to children without disabilities. These results indicate a need to improve access and identification of children with disabilities and ensure learning facilities and educators are well prepared to meet the needs of this group. In the 2021 J-MNSA report young adults were largely reported as not having attended any formal education – only 19% of females aged 18-24 and 36% of males reportedly regularly attended formal learning. The September 2022 Skills Needs Assessment supported by UNHCR and conducted by USEP Bangladesh also found that from its sample (of 813 respondents across 33 camps) 31% of Rohingya youth had received education whilst living in the camps, however private tuition being the most accessed provision. Thus, highlighting the need to prioritize this group in education activities. UNHCR will work with its partners and communities to remove barriers to girl’s education and promote gender equality in education. UNHCR will prioritize efforts that enables all girls to complete the full cycle of education and develop knowledge and skills required for life and work.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

UNHCR checks its implementing partners' (IP) activities to ensure they are in line with the JRP and current sector strategies and minimum standards and advises all IPs to consult with relevant sector working groups of newly emerging activities to ensure it complies with the technical standards and standard package. Edtech is an area identified by UNHCR for further capacity strengthening thus will work closely with the TWiG and sector to ensure that the program meets the various standards and offers quality services streamlined with the sector. UNHCR monitors how its IP activities align with the sector. As per principle 3, UNHCR works towards an integrated approach through its engagement in coordination, service delivery and government relations. UNHCR has 2 IPs; uses 1 IP per camp; makes efforts to select IPs that provide across all education interventions. UNHCR and IPs regularly participate in camp-level coordination platforms. UNHCR works with UNICEF to reduce duplication of MC scale-up camp-wise intervention across 8 camps, but further rationalization is required at block level. UNHCR increases its footprint due to scaling up Adolescent clubs. Camps were identified based on existing Play Lab facility space hence rationalizing facilities. To ensure predictability and continuity (principle 4), UNHCR has a multi-year plan spanning from 2023-2025 in respond to the education needs by increasing refugees' learning opportunities in a safe and protective environment, ensuring an inclusive learning environment for children, including persons with specific needs. UNHCR also secures multi-year funding opportunities. The selection process of IPs provides weighting to agencies working in the same sector and same camps for multiple years, recognizing that it takes time to build relations with government agencies, refugees and other IPs in camps. However, some exceptions will be made as some IPs requested to move their operations to new locations as part of the rationalization

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Specific objectives for IPs will be included for teacher trainings on key protection areas (such as Child Protection and PSEA) to ensure safety and dignity is prioritized and maintained. IPs will ensure PSEA and child protection referral mechanisms will be in place and awareness raising activities will be conducted with students, parents and teachers on how to access these. UNHCR will monitor this through its multi-functional reviews in conjunction with protection colleagues. Each learning facility will have a PSEA focal person (including one female). Risk mitigation plans are required for IPs implementing Edtech solutions to prevent and mitigate any unintended adverse effects of such new interventions are minimized.

3.2 - Gender

UNHCR is fully committed to gender-responsive safeguarding and has policies and practices to reduce the risk of, prevent and respond to SEA by UN Personal, IP staff and other relevant persons involved in humanitarian response. It continues training/refresher for teachers on PSEA and GBV; its IPs will run sex-disaggregated classes for adolescent girls and ensure one female teacher for LCs and 100% female teachers for ECE and KG be present during lessons to increase participation and community acceptance; female teacher's pool will be created through identifying Rohingya women, training them on basic pedagogy and content knowledge and deploying as needed. At all LCs, sex-disaggregated WASH facilities and menstrual hygiene and sanitation emergency kits and bins will be available. UNHCR will monitor the safety of its LC through the continuation of its PSEA/GBV Safety Audit starting in 2022; its upskilling intervention will also target a few gender-diverse adolescents.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

UNHCR provides education interventions for children, adolescents and youth ranging from 3-24 years to meet the needs of each group; its IPs develop Community Education Support Groups from various community members of different ages following its AGD principles. Adjustments will be made for children aged 11+ to participate in age-appropriate accelerated education programs condensing the Myanmar Curriculum to catch up on learning and enter mainstream education at a grade. UNHCR responds to their specific needs, prevents and responds to abuse, neglect, violence and exploitation, ensures immediate access to appropriate services, and provides durable solutions in the child's best interest. Its education sector with child protection unit work with its IPs to ensure that IP's CSP policy has CP minimum standards incorporated and that CSP minimum standards are met by sensitizing IP staff, educators and Community Education Committee member and establishing proper referral mechanism at each LCs.

3.4 - Diversity

UNHCR will ensure its IP will follow sector guidelines on new classroom designs which ensure disability access to students. Teachers will receive integrated models on disability inclusion as part of their induction and training packages. UNHCR will request IPS to use Washington Group questionnaire tools to identify CWD, and act appropriate to make sure the classrooms are inclusive and refer the individual cases to the specialized organization for assistive devices if required. The project will emphasize community-based learning /blended learning approaches to minimize access barriers, especially for the most vulnerable including children with disability, adolescent girls as well as students of religious believe from religious institution.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNHCR has provided Rohingya refugees with a hotline and UNHCR BGD email line for them to speak directly with its protection staff on various issues; and the issues are channeled to various sections within UNHCR to provide an appropriate solution. The community is consulted periodically through FGDs and KIIs to identify their opinion on the services and the areas of improvement, and during project planning period and evaluation process by forming MFT with members from education unit and cross cutting sector, head of IPs and the community group. UNHCR monitors and evaluates its intervention through mid and year-end MFT reviews where the community group members provide feedback. IPs also have their own complaints and feedback mechanisms in place to ensure their programs are adaptive and responsive to the needs of the community and organize and engage CESGs to ensure monthly meetings and capacity building sessions are maintained for the group to encourage its ownership and education rights

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Annual refresher DRR training for teachers and learning center community groups will continue. Solar panels in the Registered Camps are used to provide electricity to education facilities. Tree planting activities are planned for 2023 to make LC’s greener and support with flood absorption and shade provision. The MC includes lessons on DRR and environmental awareness which will be taught to all students. Early Childhood Development IPs focals, with the support of UNHCR and the ECDWG will conduct regular material development workshops which focuses on reusable and recyclable low/no cost materials to support learning.
