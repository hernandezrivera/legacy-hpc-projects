Project Summary

The escalation of conflict in Sudan between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) added a new dimension to an already deteriorating humanitarian situation in the country as, ten months on, the violence continues to make healthcare delivery increasingly difficult. , Sudan HNRP launched in November 2023 identified Over 14.7 million people are facing critical challenges in accessing essential life-saving primary health care as the health sector is collapsing due to the reduction of functioning health facilities along with an acute shortage of medical supplies, water and fuel, and the increase of attacks on health facilities, as result of these, devastated health situation across the country has been reported either directly due to above reasons or due to access challenges that hindering humanitarian actors to respond to the growing needs. Since the eruption of conflict in Sudan, Humanitarian needs are reported high in all times, according to HRP the Health cluster aims to assist 4.9 million people, representing 33 per cent of the total people in need. WHO in coordination with Health cluster (HC) partners will focus on disease outbreak preparedness, detection and response in line with the three HC strategies for 2024 HRP, these are (1) Support essential public health functions (including emergency trauma response) with a focus on strong primary health care services, (2) emergency preparedness, response, and all-hazards emergency risk management, and (3) Address the needs of vulnerable groups who are disproportionally affected by health emergencies.
Disease outbreaks are increasing in the face of disruptions of basic public health services, including disease surveillance, functioning public health laboratories and rapid response teams. In addition, insecurity, displacement, limited access to medicines, medical supplies, electricity, and water continue to pose enormous challenges to delivering health care across the country. About 65 per cent of the population lack access to healthcare and between 70 - 80 per cent of hospitals in conflict-affected areas are no longer functional. Almost 9,000 suspected cases of cholera, including 245 associated deaths, were reported as of 6 January 2024 from 46 localities of nine states, according to the Federal Ministry of Health (FMoH) and the World Health Organization (WHO) Sudan Outbreaks Dashboard. This is an increase of about 43 per cent compared to the number of cases reported on 6 December 2023. However, this indicates a downward trend and a much lower increase compared to previous months. Between 6 November – 6 December the number of cases increased by 143 per cent, and between 6 October and 6 November the increase was 175 per cent. During December 2023, oral cholera vaccination campaigns have been organized in Gedaref and Aj Jazirah states, with about 2.2 million people vaccinated. There are 2,746 suspected cases and 95 associated deaths in Red Sea; 2,036 suspected cases of cholera and 50 associated deaths in Gedaref; 1,860 suspected cases and 26 associated deaths in Aj Jazirah; 1,246 suspected cases and 32 associated deaths in White Nile; 525 suspected cases and 26 associated deaths in Khartoum; 346 suspected cases and eight associated deaths in South Kordofan; 121 suspected cases and four associated deaths in Sennar; 99 suspected cases and four associated deaths in Kassala; and three suspected case in Blue Nile State (OCHA). INGO forum reported 17 humanitarian colleagues who were at the frontline providing humanitarian assistance in Khartoum and Darfur before they lost their life.
Its estimated that Over 8,000 civilians have been killed and about 10,000 individuals have
been injured and about 4.2 million people at risk of gender-based violence.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project focus is to improve access of affected population regardless of their gender and sex to essential health care services, putting the services closer to the affected communities protects the most vulnerable entering in damaging coping strategies that increases their vulnerability to exploitation and abuses (especially women and children). The need for improving the access to free-of-charge health care for the communities affected by humanitarian crisis is even greater in Sudan where the out-of-pocket expenditure is more than 70%. During monitoring of the health situation, WHO coordinates with community leaders' committees which set to accommodate the affected communities’ concerns and satisfaction while early identification and response to ongoing outbreaks as mitigation and control measures for public health threats/outbreaks is the most efficient way to prevent emergencies became disasters, protecting not only the well-being of affected group but also the host communities, both IDPs and host communities will benefit getting access to the supported clinics as do no harm act. Proper medical waste management (incinerators, pit sharps) is included into the sector and MOH standards for the protection of staff, patients, communities and environment. All the activities that will be implemented in this project will use environmentally friendly procedures and equipment. The mitigation procedures will be in line with the concepts and understanding of the beneficiaries in the way that will keep their dignity especially in the indoor vector breeding sites treatment and adult flies control using products approved by the WHO and ensuring proper disposal of chemical waste. Reducing the burden of diseases for the vulnerable household, would reduce the expenditure for health protecting the families entering damaging coping strategies that will make them more vulnerable to abuses and exploitation.
