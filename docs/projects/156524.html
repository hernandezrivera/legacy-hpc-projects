Project Summary

The project concept and design are in line with sector objective: 1. Providing streamlined life-saving, humanitarian multi-sectoral assistance and strengthening the basic infrastructure support in IDP sites.
Target 1: Improved physical conditions and IDP sites by upgrading infrastructure in IDP sites to meet the minimum international standards. By preparing and improving one of the registered camps infrastructure to host Living containers and tents. Target: Site renovations and improvements to key infrastructure of one of the registered camps this will also include several fire extinguishers to be distributed. Target: 100,000 m2 of Upgraded infrastructure in an IDPs site to meet the minimum international standards. Total Fund: $300,000.
Target2: Provide shelter for 3,500 displaced families located in the northern countryside of Syria (Idlib and Aleppo) in one of the official camps or outside the camp depending on the needs. the tents and caravans are intended to replace worn out and damaged tents and shelters, unless needs are changed. Target: 1,500 caravans/2,000 tents and tarpaulins - Target individuals: 21,000 Cost per unit: $3000 car/$550 tents and tarps. Total funds: $5,600,000.
HLP: due diligence guidelines will be integrated throughout the program cycle. and will fulfill all the guidelines milestones checklist before, during and after the implementation and that includes: Planning and before implementation: - identify local HLP actors - establishing feasibility of intervention in local HLP context - establish a relationship with community members and relevant stakeholders - establish HLP eligibility and exclusion criteria - ownership verification - sign agreements by all relevant parties. During implementation: - establish a monitoring system for HLP issues - have referral system in place for when HLP issues arise Evaluation: - establish a feedback mechanism - HLP relevant questions integrated into post-implementation monitoring.

Needs

According to the HNO 2019, more than 96,000 monthly population movements was tracked which is 17% more than last year and more than 200% from 2016. The main causes of these displacements were renewed military offensives in Idleb, Aleppo, Hama governorates. Cross-line displacements following local ceasefire agreements and intense hostilities have also caused large movements of people to and within northwest Syria. Northwest Syria alone has 322 were there are coordinated response, this number includes 286 informal settlements. Approximately 25 and 26 per cent of the population in sites, do not received adequate shelter and NFI assistance, respectively. Throughout the 322 IDP sites in northwest Syria, children make up 54 per cent of the population, or about 226,856 individuals. In IDP sites in Idleb and Aleppo, the Cluster has tracked 1,401 female-headed and 466 child-headed households. Furthermore, there are 1,573 acute or chronically ill people, 1,620 elderly persons, 1,451 persons with physical disabilities, 389 persons with mental disabilities and 76 separated children in need of assistance, these will be included in QC’s criteria while selecting beneficiaries. According to the CCCM Cluster 31% of the communities reporting that shelter is unsafe and 43% reported that shelter is in poor condition. In 2019, these needs will continue to compromise the ability of Shelter partners to tailor responses based on the intersection of individual needs and vulnerabilities with the localized capacity of markets and supply chains.

Implementation of activities

The activities of this project will carry out through: 1. Survey of the needs in camps in Northern Syria regarding shelter needs and conditions. Coordinating with active partners on the ground. 2. Situation analysis which includes but not limited to women’s cultural practices, cooking practices, specific clothing or NFI etc. 3. Procurement and selection of caravans and tents. 4. Before distribution, Registration of all targeted households. And cross checking with available data by the sector (Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) data; gaps analysis; priority location mapping and results of multi-sectorial assessments / RRM) and/or by collecting information from different partners. 5. Distribution, Coordinate with head of Community/IDP representative and insure that the distributing team have all their essential equipment for distribution (safety equip. first aid kits, drinking water etc.) and the presence of an experienced team to install the caravans and tents, also will consider the involvement of the beneficiaries in installations. 6. Post distribution monitoring, a strong mechanism to assess the extent to which these distributed items are according to the expectations of the beneficiaries and/or have being properly used or not. The findings will be indicative of errors throughout distribution process, and/or some IDPs are selling or exchanging the items to obtain essential items that have not been distributed.

Protection Risk Analysis

1- Create pull-factor and expose IDPs to physical safety risks when (if the camps and settlements are in unsafe areas) Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation: 1- Maintain the CCCM guidance and position on camp establishment and camp support ensuring that camps are the last-resort. 2- Tents provision is the beginning of any potential camps regardless if formal or informal. Any distribution of tent should follow CCCM guidelines to minimize this risk. 3- As ISIMM tool captures only the camp that respect the above mentioned principles, ensure that only the locations ISIMM are supported by the cluster members. 4- Provide a full-service package of support (Food, WASH, Health, NFI) tailored to needs where possible. Avoid piecemeal and ad hoc distributions when possible. 5-Ensure that girls, boys, women and men are physically safe and live fully free of any personal-risk 2- An increase in tensions within the camps, as space (a scarce resource) is occupied by these infrastructures and create differences between the camps in terms of services. Likelihood: High. Impact: Medium. Mitigation: 1. Residents should be consulted before the construction of any infrastructure and be made aware of the risks that the current project was attempting to mitigate. 2. particular consultants with girls, women and boys to explore any special risk they might have - disability, women-headed family consisting of girls and boys.3- Increased risk of inter community tension due in part to unequal level of assistance given to camp residents. likelihood: High, Impact Mitigation: Ensure that the camps are all needs based, and that residents are amongst the most vulnerable. (See CCCM Guidance on site verification) Clearly communicate selection criteria to the community, community leaders and beneficiaries prior to the announcement of the results of the verification/selection exercise. Make effort to target a percentage of vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs. Establish feedback mechanism and conduct follow up visits if needs be. Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process. 4- In displacement situations, the women, girls and young children become most vulnerable and may face exploitation from service providers. Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigation: complaint response mechanism will be established with detailed orientation to the beneficiaries, women, girls and children. The same orientation will be provided to the distribution management staff with zero tolerance on exploitation of beneficiaries in any sense. In addition to it, Do No Harm Principle and the humanitarian principles will be effectively implemented in beneficiary selection and in all the project proceedings. Core humanitarian standards will be followed for response wide effectiveness.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: 1-Overcrowding and/or integrating groups that do not have social histories together has the potential to increase risks of exposure to different forms of GBV. Mitigation: Use the GBV specific assessment questions to understand the different GBV risks present in the collective centers and/ or camps as well as different households' specific needs to feel safe in/around their shelter structures. Drawing on this, implement GBV risk reduction activities through specific shelter and settlement design-focused interventions. These will vary by context, culture, gender, emergency and group that practitioners are working with. 2- Shelter designs do not meet women and girls' needs and, consequently, increase their exposure to existing GBV risks. Mitigation: Draw on shelter assessment information to identify women and girls (or other groups) needs related to their homes, household activities, and safety.
