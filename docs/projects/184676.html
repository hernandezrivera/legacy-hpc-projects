Project Summary

The project aims at providing life-saving and life sustaining livelihood support to the worst crisis affected IDPs and most vulnerable host communities (women, men, boys and girls) as well as those living with disabilities and the elderly in Bay, Lower Shabelle and Lower Juba Regions that are facing very serious food gaps owing to current severe drought conditions. Their plight is attributed to recurrent shocks that continue to erode their coping strategies and undermine their resilience against the crises. They are particularly affected by two successive seasons of erratic and below normal rains (Deyr 2020 and Gu 2021) coupled by past successive droughts seasons, floods and serious impacts of desert locust and covid-19 pandemic. They are further affected by conflict and insecurity that has disrupted trade and affected the prices of food commodities in the local markets. According to FSNAU-FEWS NET 2021 Post Gu Technical Release of September 9, 2021, around 3.5 million people in Somalia face acute food insecurity Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes in late 2021 while there is high likelihood of poor Deyr 2021. Moreover, current forecasts show that drought conditions will persists till mid 2022 thus escalating the already precarious food insecurity and malnutrition rates among the most vulnerable IDPs and marginalized rural non IDPs.
The above plight calls for appropriate and timey livelihood interventions by Providing
appropriate safety nets while restoring livelihood conditions and increasing their income
sources through provision of appropriate livelihood inputs and technical support in rural
and peri-urban settings and support household and community resilience to shocks,
through rehabilitation of community assets. The project targets 24600 beneficiaries (10234 women, 9446men, 2460 boys and 2460girls) including persons living with disabilities and the elderly in the project targeted locations in Lower Juba, Lower Shabelle and Bay Regions Somalia.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1. Inclusion of minority and marginalized groups such as minority clans, IDPs, the elderly and persons with disabilities will be ensured through their comprehensive inclusion and participation in the consultations on the project right at the needs identification stage where their concerns will form part of key decisions in prioritization of the needs for intervention by the project. This will further include their continued participation in the project activities as beneficiaries and participants during regular monitoring exercises where their concerns will be part of recommendations and suggestion for the impending project activities hence their needs covered by the project.2. The project will increase protection of women and girls from sexual violence by ensuring that project activities are at centralised locations for easy access by all targeted beneficiaries particularly women and girls to limit them from covering long distance beyond the safety of their settlements and homes when accessing to limit cases of sexual violence. In addition, project activities will be implemented during the day to ensure women and girls have enough time to reach their settlements and homes before the cover of darkness that is synonymous with sexual violence. 3. The project will further be implemented under very strict adherence to PSEA policy by ensuring women and girls are fully aware of their entitlements/ food value and quantities, voucher value etc before the commencement of the activities to mitigate incidences of sexual exploitation and abuse during the project by the project staff, authority and security agencies. The project will further ensure that complaints and feedback mechanisms are in place for easy access by targeted beneficiaries and other community members
