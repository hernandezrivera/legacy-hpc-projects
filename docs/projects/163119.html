Project Summary

The current micro- economic and climate induced shocks in Zimbabwe are impacting negatively on the attainment of most of the Sustainable Development Goals targets for 2030. The reduction of poverty and hunger amongst other commitments are being derailed by the current situation in Zimbabwe. As such Zimbabwe is carrying an acute burden of food insecurity and the current statistics by the Food Security Cluster of Zimbabwe reveals that about 7.7 million people in rural and urban areas are at risk of food insecurity. People living in Chivi and Mberengwa districts can not be exempted from risk of food insecurity shocks because numerous vulnerabilities which they are exposed to. The two district are adjacent to each other and are overlapped by 2 agro-ecological regions iv and v which receives erratic rainfall of 450-600mm per year. The rainfall has not been sufficient enough to sustain crop production under dryland agriculture resulting in extremely low crop yields.
According to Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Committee (ZimVAC) rural livelihood report of 2019, Chivi and Mberengwa districts were positioned on 2nd and 19th ranks in terms of cereal insecurity proportions of 84.7%% (149,365 individuals) and 64.4%% (126,987 individuals) food insecurerespectively. Chivi district was classified in integrated Phase 3 and 4 for crisis and need of emergent support. The gap analysis map by Food Security Cluster released in October 2019 shows that Chivi and Mberengwa districts have 61,693 and 39,437 people in IPC phase 3 and 4 respectively. Due to poverty and diseases, the livestock situation in the districts has also been deteriorating and the mortality rates of 10.1% (Chivi) and 13.5% (Mberengwa) for cattle reported by ZimVAC report of 2019 are above the national recommended rates between 3-5%.
It is in view of the situation in Chivi and Mberengwa that ZCC is appealing for an emergency drought response which will adopt a holistic approach through intervention layering and it will target 800 poorly resourced farmers in Integrated Phase Classification 3 and 4 (IPC 3 and 4). The project will minimize the risk of further food insecurity deterioration by improving access to basic crop and livestock inputs; enhancing access, functionality and availability of critical community assets for crops and livestock production through infrastructure rehabilitation and construction. Through the layering approach the project is also aimed to optimize the benefit of agricultural support through extension and advisory services to improve agricultural yields and manage crop pests and livestock diseases in Chivi and Mberenngwa districts in Masvingo and the Midlands Province respectively by December 2020. The project will adoption of climate smart agriculture technology and production of drought tolerant crops such as sorghum and millet, and diversification of livelihoods activities such as nutrition gardening and small livestock production in an environmental friendly manner that will discourage the use of agriculture chemicals and pesticides.
Beneficiaries of the project in IPC 3 and 4 are to be targeted using a participatory community-based selection process that will be agreed between all the stakeholders and the community. Beneficiaries will be selected using the agreed community-based targeting selection criteria which will be guided by IPC 3 and 4 indicators, household access to land and water amongst others. Special considerations will be given to marginalised groups such as people living with disabilities, chronical illness, the elderly, widowed, orphans and child headed.
The organisation has been present in the districts since 2009 implementing the, “Conservation Farming and Livelihood Program” funded by Act for Peace and the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT) both of Australia. The project has reached its 10th year.

1 - Activities

An integrated approach will be undertaken during the implementation of proposed activities to reach the beneficiaries in a layered manner. A total of 6 water sources will be identified, rehabilitated and solarised for supplying water to 6 nutrition gardens that will also be established by the project. A sum of 300 farmers participating in nutrition gardens will be supported with garden input kits comprising of 20g tomatoes, 20g carrots, 20g leaf vegetable rape and cabbage, 100g butternuts, 20 g cucumber, 20g onions and 500g beans or peas per farmer. In addition, the project will provide 20 cuttings livestock fodder per farmer for fodder production. The livestock restocking activity will provide 6 hens, a cock indigenous chickens and 50 kg poultry feed per farmer, 2 females goats per farmer and 1 buck in community at a ratio of 1buck per 13 females in the community. The project will rehabilitate 4 diptanks and livestock handling facilities at dip tanks. The project will also provide field crop drought tolerant inputs comprising of 5kg maize drought tolerate variety, 5 kg sorghum and 5 kg millet per farmer. As the use of artificial fertilizers in the districts have in the previous years proved to be not working because of poor rainfall quality, the project will encourage the use of livestock manure. Complementary activities will be conducted in which farmers will be trained on nutrition garden production; livestock fodder production, livestock production and animal health; climate smart agriculture, diseases and pest management; small grains value chain development, post-harvest handling, storage, processing and marketing. Planned activities will be implemented in coordination with line government departments, local authority, food security humanitarian organisations and local leadership. Government technical department will offer technical expertise in facilitation of project activities. The project will coordinate its activities with other programs in the district to minimise duplication of efforts and ensuring that the most vulnerable people are targeted. The organisation will attend all the meetings that will be invited through the Food Security Cluster.

2 - Indicators

The success of the project will be determined by measuring the following indicators:-number of households receiving season sensitive emergency crops and livestock inputs-number of critical community assets for crop and livestock production rehabilitated - number of vulnerable small holder farmers receiving extension and advisory services to manage crop pests and livestock diseases.
