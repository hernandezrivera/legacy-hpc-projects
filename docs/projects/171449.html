Project Summary

The health situation in Galkayo, Hobyo, Afgooye, Belatwyne and Abduwaak districts seem to be worsening due to the compounded negative impacts of covid-19, flooding, desert locust infestation and lack of basic health services. The average morbidity rate among under-five children in these districts is 19% while the coverage for vitamin A is 59.8% with coverage for measles vaccination standing at 61.5% while the clear water access is only 28% (CESVI progress report, 2020). COVID 19 pandemic has an adverse effect on the service delivery where access to health and nutrition services of the population has been greatly hampered. The impacts of these threats not only led to aggravation of the shocks but also
exacerbated the challenges of the vulnerable groups.
To address the challenges enumerated above, CESVI will implement a life-saving health interventions through 7 static and 5 mobile Health clinics to increase access to basic health services. This action will address endemic diseases like pneumonia, anemia, acute watery diarrhea and ARI which are the leading causes of infant and child mortality in the target villages. CESVI will provide primary, and referral health care, with special attention to maternal and child health, including basic emergency obstetric care (BEmONC), Expanded Program on Immunization. This project will create demand for health services including family planning, antenatal care services and delivery at health facilities through sensitization of the communities by trained Community health workers on the importance of these services.
Health mobile clinics will serve IDP camps and villages in the far flung villages to deliver quality primary health care services to populations without a static MCH in close proximity. Moreover, Health facilities will be equipped (after minor rehabilitations) with essential drugs, vaccines and post- rape treatment drugs including medical equipment. Further, this intervention will enhance a referral system from primary to secondary health services and TB services and from nutrition to health services and vice versa.
Community Health Workers (CHW) will be engaged as part of the emergency response team to actively act as first responders for health and nutrition issues. They will play a key role in the referral system ensuring better access to health care services for the population in general, but for the most vulnerable in particular.
Monitoring of activities and epidemiological surveillance will be maintained and strengthened through strong partnership with MoH and using official and revised HIMS MoH tools to support identification of risks, monitoring of incidence and to increasing efficiency towards timely response to health programs. Community health workers appointed as part of MCH staffs will be will be equipped with skills on alert thresholds for epidemic diseases as per WHO standards. They will be issued with cell phones and in the course of discharging their duties of promoting community awareness, they will also act as sentinels to detect any change in the context in terms of emergency, epidemic outbreaks and relay information to CESVI senior Health staff who will immediately contact the MoH and WHO for case verification to facilitate immediate response.
To prevent AWD/cholera epidemic outbreak, hygiene promotion campaigns will be organized regularly throughout the action within the target villages.
The project will provide trainings targeting thematic areas such ANC and BEmONC, drugs consumption and GBV/Protection mainstreaming among others. Joint supportive supervision will also be undertaken with MoH as per of efforts to build their capacity.

Mitigation Measures

In order to mitigate exposure of beneficiaries to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services, this action will deploy 5 mobile clinics to the villages without static health facilities within their vicinity. These mobile clinics will be equipped with trained staff and nutrition supplies to deliver both quality curative and preventive nutrition services to the affected populations with health facilities located in the far flung areas. As part of efforts to reduce the risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.), this action will mainstream protection components within the projects ‘s grain. Both staff working in the static and mobile clinics will be trained on basic protection aspects like psychosocial counselling and referral services. The mobile clinics will also be used to transfer protection cases to various service provision points. This will be done by printing pre-referral cards for GBV/ protection cases which shall be used for referral of such cases. The project will ensure that all staff is trained on PSEA (and subsequently sign CESVI PSEA policy) and attendant consequences as a result of a violation of this policy - including dismissal from work communicated to them. This measure will ensure that any staff in contact with beneficiaries of thisproject does not engage in any form of sexual exploitation and abuse when handlingbeneficiaries. This action will put in place measures to ensure that all marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, take part in consultations or decision-making processes affecting the project. This will be done through discussions with their leaders through various open fora where their concerns and needs will be factored in during project implementation. This will also be done during project evaluation where findings from such sessions shall be used to improve project activities. Specifically, Health promotion services for prevention of further disability and promotion of optimal health in general will be given utmost importance to improve the quality of life and health status of persons living with disabilities.Barriers that can potentially hamper access to health services for persons living with disabilities will be addressed through the following mechanisms; physical inaccessibility to health facilities will address by ensuring that there are accessible pathways that can be used by persons living with physical disabilities. Mobile clinics will be used, when the need arises, to transfer persons living with any form of disability to the health facilities for treatment of endemic diseases which cannot be addressed through the outreach sites. Moreover, CESVI staff will be adequately trained on aspects aimed at equipping them with skills and competencies to be responsive to the needs of persons living with any form of disabilities. Care will be taken to meet the health needs of those with invisible disabilities, particularly those with psychosocial disabilities and other mental health conditions.
