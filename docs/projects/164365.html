Project Summary

The project proposes to ensure provision of quality and comprehensive Primary Health Care services, including SRH and MHPSS in targeted PHCCs according to WHO quality standards by enhancing implementation of “The Essential Primary Health Care Package in Northern Syria” as well as the IASC Guidelines on MHPSS in Emergency Settings, the WHO Pyramid Framework and SPHERE minimum standards for Health in humanitarian settings. The Local Health Authority (LHA) will be supported to ensure provision of essential health care services package (EHSP) according to quality standards in 8 target PHC centres (PHCCs) in Aleppo (Serrin, Jellabiya, Qanaya, Tel Ghazal and Khanik) and Al Hassakeh (Tel Brak, Tel Hamis, Kalasa) governorates, and expand services to, at least 2 new facilities.
A minimum support package will be provided to the targeted PHCCs to include technical support supervision and on-the-job training for PHCCs staff, financial support for human resources and running costs, minor rehabilitations, provision of drugs, consumables and medical equipment. The package geared towards enhancing the capacity of LHA in implementation of the EPHS in the targeted PHCCs, will be provided by establishing quality standards on diagnosis, treatment and timely referrals for Child health – Integrated management of childhood illnesses and wellbeing, screening for malnutrition and referral to specialized care, Sexual and Reproductive Health for pregnant and lactating women including Antenatal care, deliveries by skilled attendants, postnatal care and essential package for family planning, Communicable and Non-Communicable disease management including cost-efficient treatment for chronic illnesses, Mental Health and Psychosocial support (MHPSS) services and timely referral for protection and to secondary health care.
An integrated approach will be applied to incorporate MHPSS into programming for a holistic patient-centered primary healthcare delivery through identification of Mental Health disorders, management of clinical patients and referrals, provision of psychological and psychosocial support. The PHCCs will count on qualified general practitioners (GPs)/doctors, midwives, nurses, trained psychologists and case managers in mobile or static teams depending on the catchment population. GPs trained on mhGap will deliver medical treatment to severe and moderate Mental Health cases. In addition, the proposed intervention will support LHA for an approach of community participation in primary health care based on the principle that communities can be drivers of their own care and change and should be involved in all stages of health programming. Through this support, Community health workers (CHWs) employed by LHA and attached to each PHCC will be trained and tasked with information, education and communication through sensitization and awareness raising for medical and MHPSS in the PHCCs and in the community and conduct community health education session. To achieve this, community structures such as organized women and/or men groups, health committees, community leaders and representatives and school going children will be targeted as agents of change. The NGO in the project will implement strategies to reduce the stigma of persons with disabilities true community awareness sessions and true IEC material in the PHCs. All the services at the PHCs will be accessible for all people, which includes providing transport (2 ambulances) for persons with disabilities to health facilities. The NGO will monitor the project true remote management. Several reports are produced on a monthly basis: A Monthly SitRep, compiling context and security situation, programme achievements and challenges,A monthly tally sheet at each PHCC, including new and follow-up consultations, unique number of beneficiaries by age and gender, acute, chronic and MH morbidities, indicator progress tracking table,An online/offline monitoring tool (Monitool).

4 - Needs

Since the Turkish intervention in NES, and according to the last OCHA report, North-East Syria displacement - 18 December 2019, an estimated 70.590 people remain displaced from Al-Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa and Aleppo governorates. Most health facilities are only partially functioning and many have been closed or relocated to safer locations. SRH services are insufficient for the high number of displaced women. Stocks of drugs have been diverted from regular programming in order to meet the most urgent needs.In the targeted intervention areas, there lacks an elaborate prevention component to complement the treatment for common illnesses especially communicable disease and sexual/reproductive health and MHPSS. Therefore, there is need to espouse community health approach to enhance community participation in health for information, education and communication. There is an imperative to enhance primary health care (PHC) services for the increasing number of PHC consultations across NES, encompassing the management of communicable and non-communicable diseases as well as cost-efficient treatment of chronic diseases, sexual/reproductive health, integrated management of childhood illnesses and MHPSS. In the assessments carried out after the crisis in the supported PHCCs, increased needs were identified in Serrin PHCC (Aleppo) as well as in a new facility in Kalasa (Al-Hasakeh), where additional shifts are necessary to respond to the increased demand of consultations and an outreach team to provide health services in the nearby collective shelters. In NES, mental health (MH) is highly stigmatized and MH services are not available in the public healthcare system. There are only a few psychiatrists in the country and Clinical Psychology degree is not available, only the Faculty of Education offers courses on psychological counseling and psychology. According to data collected in our supported PHCCs, 51% of MHPSS consultations concern children under 18.

5 - Implementation of activities

The PHCCs are managed by the LHA, and we provide financial support for the recruitment of PHCCs staff, running costs, minor rehabilitations and maintenance, donation of drugs, consumables and medical and non-medical equipment. . Supported facilities will have at least 1 doctor, 2 nurses, 1 midwife, 1 data clerk, 1 pharmacist, 1 psychologist, 1 case manager and 4 CHWs and support staff depending on the catchment population. In order to ensure a continuous supply of essential drugs by WHO quality standards, the project will procure drugs and consumables internationally from Humanitarian Procurement Centres and ship them to NES through the KRI. Drugs will be stored in our pharmacies in Duhok, Amuda/Al-Hassakeh and Kobane and donated to each PHCC on a monthly basis. Support to LHA for rental, equipment, maintenance and running costs of two ambulances owned by the LHA in order to facilitate timely referrals from the PHCCs to secondary health care facilities.Psychosocial support sessions as well as group sessions in the community and in the PHCCs will be provided by the MHPSS staff, made of a psychologist, a case manager and trained community health workers (CHW). Together with a close cooperation with the existing community committees this will be key to identify cases, reduce of stigma, prevent potential mental health and psychosocial problems and facilitate referral to the available services in the PHCCs. Training on various components of the Essential Primary Health Care Services Package will be provided by project staff or by external trainers or accredited training body to the PHCCs staff (doctors, nurses, midwives, psychologists, case managers and CHWs). MhGAP training will be delivered to doctors. Trainings NGO will provide are Communicable and NC Disease prevention, GBV and case management, CHWs induction training, Managing High-risk pregnancies PLWs and neonates, PSEA, PFA, Integrated Management of childhood Ilnesses(IMCI), Stress management and selfcare.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Because of the repeated displacements, numerous protection risks arise: 1) Loss of documentation along with movement restrictions, including self-imposed restrictions in fear of conscription. 2) Child protection and GbV risks are increased. 3) Family separation remains a risk, whilst unaccompanied minors face renewed risks. 4) Single women and women-headed households are at increased risk of GbV, while those with specific needs and the elderly face protection concerns and barriers to access services.5) Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers is present in the conflict zone and will influence the work of PHCCs.6): The lack of Community engagement and participation in NES is risk factor for humanitarian workers. Collective information provision, feedback, and participation of affected communities can hinder effective humanitarian coordination and response.7) Lack of “Duty of care” policies in NES. Syria has lost up to 50% of its pre-crisis qualified medical personnel capacity due to immigration, displacement, violence, and criminal activities. 8) Risk of Conflict Sensitivity. Our workers must be aware and informed about the different groups on the ground. Conflict can potentially affect the ability to deliver assistance by limiting access, undermining the sustainability of assistance and creating uncertainty with regard to local partners and PHCCs. Assistance can alter the structural causes of instability and insecurity and can affect what resources are available to local actors, potentially changing the relationships between different groups. Communications in Syria must be extremely context-sensitive and agencies must be mindful to communicate in an impartial manner.
