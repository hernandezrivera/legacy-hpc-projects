Project Summary

The intervention aims to respond to the shelter and winterization needs in NWS, where we plan to upgrade unfinished buildings for hosting 600 HHs from the newly displaced IDPs, we also plan to implement semi-permanent shelters for sheltering 600 HHs from the newly displaced IDPs. The winterization activity will be implemented in Atmeh Camp where we expect the population to reach to 40,000 HHs of IDPs.
The plan will require $ 8,262,540 for supporting 247,200 people (36,150 men, 52,470 women, 86,830 boys, and 71,750 girls) with the winterization and shelter support.

4 - Needs

The large number of displacements is severely exacerbating shelter and accommodation shortages across northwest Syria. According to most recent data, which was reported from REACH, HNAP, CCCM and SNFI Cluster, almost 5.6 million are considered people in need for shelter in Syria, and 3.4 million are considered people in need for NFI assistance in Syria, in the accessible areas in NWS,1.4 PiN/Shelter, 1.2 PiN/NFIs the most urgent need and challenge for the newly displaced population is shelter. During the displacement which occurred in December and January, due to winter conditions of heavy rainfall, and a lack of available tents and capacity in camp settings, it was reported that many IDPs did not choose to settle in formal or informal tented camps during the major wave of displacement. The majority of newly arrived IDPs were preferring to seek refuge in Collective Centres, Unfinished Buildings, and Hosted in the community, but these accommodations were temporary and intentions were to move elsewhere when weather conditions improved. The unfinished buildings, many of which have no doors or windows and lack the basic sanitary services. For the Winterization in Atmeh, Global Communities was able to cover the needs during 2016/2017 and 2017/2018 winter seasons, however for the 2018/2019 winter season, none of the SNFI actors responded to the winterization needs in the camp, we could observe that the most vulnerable population in the camp are either not heating their shelters, or burning the plastic and clothes for heating them selves and their families. Displaced persons in last resort sites continue to be among the most vulnerable people in Syria and, having exhausted all other options, are dependent to a high degree upon humanitarian assistance and services to meet their daily needs Vulnerable groups, such as women and children – headed households, pregnant and lactating women (PLW), people with disabilities, the elderly and others with diminished coping mechanism

5 - Implementation of activities

For activity#1: 1.4.2 upgrading the unfinished buildings, GC will identify the safe and proper locations for implementing this activity, this will include conducting assessments in the accessed areas. after selecting the targeted locations, we will work closely with the targeted communities for identifying the buildings after ensuring the validity of ownership after applying the HLP due diligence. after selecting the buildings, we will initiate the procurement process based on the BoQs for the required rehabilitation works needed, we will select the contractor through transparent procurement process. We will identify the beneficiaries either from those on move and don't find any shelter, or from the overcrowded collective centers and camps in unsafe locations. We will give priority for the most vulnerable registered/identified beneficiaries. In every rehabilitated building, we will establish housing committee representing the different groups, after that we will sign tri-parties housing contract. For the activity#2: 2.1.2. Construction of semi-permanent shelter structures, and taking into consideration that almost 80% of the currently existed camps in Northern Idleb are semi permanent since the people are staying there for more than eight years, we will map the public land around the existed camps, we will select the land after applying the HLP due diligence, we will select the beneficiaries as listed above. We will coordinate with the other actors for complementary activities including the CCCM, FSL, ERL, Protection and WASH. For the activity#3: 1.2.1 Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs including winterization, fuel, and summerization , M&E team in Global Communities is regularly registering the newly arrivals to Atmeh Camp, after the verification process, every HHs receive a card with par-cod. They can receive all the in-kind and vouchers distributions via those cards. We are planning to target all the camp with the heating fuel .

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

During the implementation of the planned activities, we expect the following risks;There is a high probability of risk of increased community tension due to different assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria or poorly timed distributions leading to a feeling of discrimination. Tension can also arise between host community and IDPs, due to a lack of assistance to the host community. this can be mitigated by 1. Standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners.2. Ensure beneficiary selection criteria is uniform/agreed among sector members and with the government and is clearly communicated widely within a community and among different groups. 3. Establish complaint mechanism at community level.4. Training to humanitarian staffs. There is also a high probability for the risk of crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender segregated sanitation facilities can lead to increase in GBV and sexual exploitation. This can be mitigated by 1. Advocate for provision of alternative accommodation/expansion of existing sites.2. Provision of gender segregated mass shelter in case of large displacement/lack of space to provide individual family shelter unit.3. Provision of gender segregated sanitation facilities sufficiently close to dwellings.4. Provision of lighting around shelter and WASH facilities.5. Work closely with protection and other sectors to understand the issues and take necessary actions together to address them.Another risk is eviction from camps/transit sites/spontaneous settlement due to lack of approval from property owner/conflict in property ownership, this can be mitigated by, 1. Work closely with local authorities to ensure that there is a written approval from owner for specifid period to use the property to develop the site.2. Start developing the site only after written approval is produced.3. Advocate with local authorities to facilitate approval for spontaneous settlement.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The following risks and assumptions are expected while implementing the activities;1.Fake beneficiaries / Duplication / Non-adherence to selection criteria.2.Selecting service provider with low capacity (technical/financial/project management)3.Implementing of unneeded rehabilitation works or over reporting.4.Procuring with high price, Over budget, A contractor with low capacity/quality 5.Safety and Security risk during the implementation 6.Market failure - Limited supply and large demand The existing and planned mitigating measures;-Review assessments by senior staff and return to field team with correction action-Feedback mechanism -Beneficiary registration and verification process-Post Distribution Monitoring-Proper inventory management-Reviewing the data/ purchasing requests by senior technical team member, send correction notes to the field.-Precise market assessments-Negotiation process-Safety procedures
