Project Summary

THEMATIC ENVELOPE
This proposal submission is for Thematic Envelope Two (2): National Women-led organization leading women-led innovative solutions.
CONTRIBUTION TO THE PROTECTION OF GIRLS AND VULNERABLE WOMEN
This project is strategically designed to contribute significantly to the protection of girls and vulnerable women through a comprehensive and community-oriented approach. The project's multifaceted initiatives aim to empower women and girls with a strong focus on safeguarding their rights, addressing gender-based violence (GBV) and enhancing access to sexual reproductive healthcare and economic opportunities.
• Inclusive Beneficiary Targeting: The project’s explicit focus on women (60%) and girls (25%) ensures that the most vulnerable groups receive tailored support. The inclusion of males (10%) recognizes their role in fostering gender equality and preventing GBV.
• Community Sensitization and Awareness Campaigns: the project reduces the vulnerability of women and girls by fostering knowledge and awareness through the sensitization of 38,500 women and girls on right awareness, GBV prevention and reproductive health
• Economic Empowerment: the project also equips beneficiaries with essential skills, promoting economic independence among women and girls
• Improved Access to Support Centers: through the establishment of community-based safe spaces and SRH Hubs, the project offers secure environments for GBV survivors, staffed by trained professionals providing comprehensive support.
• Improved Access to Health Services: health system strengthening through partnership and support of existing healthcare facilities, capacity development of healthcare providers on SRH, CMR and other GBV-related health needs for GBV survivors
• Mobilizing and Leveraging Existing Community Structures: Enhancing project ownership and sustainability utilizing established structures, networks and community dynamics such as government representatives, community leaders, legal experts/systems, police, CSOs such as FIDA, SARCs, etc
This project's collaborative and community-centered approach, coupled with strategic planning and resource allocation, positions it as a catalyst for sustainable change and the protection of vulnerable women and girls in the targeted communities in Yobe.
INNOVATION
• IDEA – SRH Hub
The CBI SRH Hub is an innovative and community-led initiative that complements GBV prevention, SARCs, and addresses SRH gaps. This unique concept serves as a secure space for women and girls, providing essential SRH services, including counseling on Family Planning and contraceptives for adolescents, along with mentorship for young girls. The Hub conducts community outreach and education, specifically targeting hard-to-reach areas, and integrates GBV services in coordination with SARCs. All services are delivered with cultural sensitivity and inclusivity, reflecting a pioneering approach to empower communities. This innovation aims to bridge women and girls access to SRH services gaps and significantly enhance the overall well-being of women and girls in rural and hard-to-reach areas in Yobe State.
• APPROACH – Community Based
The innovative community-based approach in the project involves actively engaging local stakeholders and leveraging existing community structures; such as government agencies, community leaders, religious leaders, women's groups, youth organizations, and People Living with Disabilities (PLWDs), Healthcare facilities, police, local CSOs, SARC, etc. Through trainings, sensitizations and awareness campaigns, the project focuses on empowering women, girls, and PLWDs, addressing gender-based violence (GBV), and enhancing access to sexual reproductive healthcare and economic opportunities. This approach, targeting specific beneficiaries and involving community stakeholders and structures, ensures project ownership, sustainability, and effectiveness in fostering positive and sustainable change within the targeted communities.

1 - Needs

Women and girls face heightened vulnerability to Gender-Based Violence (GBV), encompassing domestic violence, sexual assault, early/forced marriage, Intimate Partner Violence (IPV), etc. Disturbingly, 1 in 3 Nigerian women has suffered one form of gender-based abuse or the other by the age 15 (UN Women). The need for GBV prevention and response services is evident, with 39% girls, 16% boys, 35% women, and 6% people with disabilities requiring assistance. Demographically, 43% are from host communities, 32% are internally displaced persons (IDPs), and the rest are returnees. GBV, sexual violence, child abuse, and intimate partner violence persist across BAY states. A report by the Sexual Assault Referral Centre (SARC) recorded 5,623 GBV cases in four years in Borno, Yobe, and Adamawa. A striking 24% of GBV survivors seeking help are children (GBVIMS 2023 database).The risks of GBV are anticipated to rise due to conflict, climate change, camp closures, cholera outbreaks, and the influx of refugees. Challenges in service delivery include a shortage of skilled workers, with a health worker to population ratio of 5.8 to 10,000, falling far below the WHO standard (23 to 10,000). This affects various health services, particularly clinical management for rape survivors, GBV case management, and mental health/psychosocial support. The insufficient presence of SARCs in BAY states and institutional shortages compound the complex landscape of GBV in the region.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

In order to 'do no harm' and minimize the potential for conflict over who gets what assistance, clear communication with beneficiaries and community leaders about the project's objectives and activities will be an essential part of the implementation of the project. For the selection of households that benefit from this project, extra care is given to prevent exclusion of marginalized IDP households. CBI staff is trained in the Do No Harm principles and CBI will ensure that they treat all beneficiaries in a dignified manner. The feedback system will also provide a way of ensuring this.CBI will ensure that beneficiaries - especially women and girls - are involved in site selection for project activities in order to reduce the risks of sexual violence and abductions. CBI respects and is sensitive to the norms and values of the community, hence will ensure approaches that are culturally acceptable.CBI protection and Gender officers will also be involved in the project implementation for the purpose of ensuring that all gender and protection issues are handled with immediate alacrity.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

CBI is committed to establishing a robust Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (CFM) for this humanitarian intervention. The collaboration between our mobile medical outreach team and the Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) department will ensure a weekly implementation of the feedback mechanism, utilizing diverse methods such as field visits and interviews for routine data collection. Information gathering will extend to safe spaces and Sexual Assault Referral Centres (SARCs), focusing on relevant services and referral pathways. To comprehensively assess project success, CBI will conduct periodic feedback meetings with stakeholders, along with entry and exit surveys; will adopt the existing feedback/complaint mediums within implementation areas, such as suggestion boxes and review points. Where none exists, CBI will construct and make these tools available for use throughout and after project implementation. In our commitment to inclusivity, all population categories will actively contribute to the project design and implementation, fostering participation in coordination and project meetings to gather diverse opinions and advice that will enhance the overall success of the intervention.
