Project Summary

The project aims to improve living conditions and access to inclusive humanitarian assistance for populations affected by displacement due to complex humanitarian crises (IDPs, refugees, returnees) and members of host communities. With special attention to the needs and rights of persons with disabilities, measures in the two sectors of health and protection are implemented in selected regions of the countries Cameroon, Democratic Republic of Congo, Niger and Nigeria in cooperation with local partner organisations. The goal is achieved through three complementary packages of measures: (1) provision of primary health and rehabilitation services through mobile health teams and establishment of a referral system, psychosocial counselling and treatment for affected populations, strengthening of existing health infrastructure and training of staff (2) provision of inclusive protection services for persons with disabilities as well as other vulnerable persons, with particular emphasis on support services for survivors of SGBV, (3) Measures to mainstream inclusion in the activities of other humanitarian actors and in the humanitarian coordination system.
CBM implements the project in the four countries mentioned above in cooperation with four different local partner organisations, which bear the main responsibility for the individual activities within the framework of a distinct localisation approach. CBM is responsible for the overall project management, harmonisation of activities, strengthening of exchange for learning in the synchronously implemented bundles of activities as well as technical advice to the implementing partners in trainings of external actors (especially for the packages of measures 2 and 3). The four partner organisations and CBM work closely with the disability rights movement and Organisations of Persons with Disabilities (OPDs) in all four contexts and basically follow a participatory approach in implementation, which is also target-oriented in the context of the Do-No-Harm strategy. All activities are coordinated in the corresponding clusters or coordination systems at regional level (by the local partner organisations) and national level (by CBM) and, if necessary, approved by the respective governments.
