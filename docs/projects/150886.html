Project Name

Start Date

01/01/2019

End Date

31/12/2020

Project Summary

This project will continue seeking to improve the protective environment for those affected by conflict, improve access to and quality of protective services and advocate for the sustainable changes in the legislation and policy nationwide. R2P interventions will:
- Monitor the situation of internally displaced persons and other affected populations along the line of contact and other areas of intervention, collecting, verifying, and analyzing information in order to identify human rights violations and protection risks encountered by IDPs and other affected populations. Entrance-exit check points (EECP) monitoring will continue at all 5 EECPs. The protection response will be delivered to people of concern. This will include both individual and community level monitoring;
- Improve access to Legal Assistance and Legal Remedies, by providing high-quality counseling and legal assistance to the internally displaced and conflict affected population;
- Promote National Law and Policy which is consistent with international standards on internal displacement. This will be completed through advocacy, assessment and analysis, commentary on legal acts and drafts, and strategic litigation.
The project will primarily serve internally displaced persons living in the most densely populated areas in Donetsk, Luhansk, Kharkiv, Dnipro, Kyiv, Lviv and Zaporizhia regions, with a specific focus on the territories along the line of contact and on the people crossing EECPs.

Needs

People living close to the contact line (LoC) remain the most vulnerable because of the security situation and lack of access to admin and basic social services. There is no state support for these people as they are not officially displaced. Local, national and INGOs remain the key service providers, ensuring their access to protection measures including legal aid. They also have poor or no access to public transport. This restricts freedom of movement and does not allow for travel to raion centres and obtain their social and medical services Many people, especially those living in locations of high concern and in the NGCA, still have considerable legal aid needs. Many lack information about legal options to protect their rights. Lengthy litigation procedures often discourage people from applying to courts. People prefer extrajudicial means of problem solving. Access to social payments and pensions remains the greatest need to burdensome procedures of verification and identification introduced by Cabinet of Ministers (CoM) Resolution 365, adopted in June 2016. Age/gender analysis- The elderly are particularly vulnerable as Resolution 365 disproportionately affects them. The elderly are also the largest plurality of people crossing the line of contact per R2P’s monthly monitoring of crossing the line of contact, and thus are subject to lengthy periods waiting outdoors in all weather conditions. Additionally, per a recent Helpage publication, 99% of the elderly in GCA Donbas rely on their pensions as their primary source of income. Newborns from the NGCA are at a risk of statelessness given that Ukrainian authorities do not recognize documentation issued in the NGCA, including birth certificates. Children and adolescent psychological health is at risk due to shelling and the constant presence of military men in. They also face limited access to education and safe places to play. Women and girls along the contact line face a high risk of SGBV due to high concentration of military personnel. Women and girls are unlikely to report abuse due to stigmatization and fear of retaliation. Additionally, women face diminishing access to reproductive health care given that Ukraine’s medical reform has resulted in closing of many health facilities in Donbas. People of both sexes along the conflict line sometimes travel to the NGCA for medical care. Men in these localities face a risk of conflict, discrimination and violence from military personnel and paramilitary members. R2P’s protection monitoring demonstrates that the People with disabilities remain vulnerable among IDPs and conflict affected persons. No mechanism for paying pensions for people residing in the NGCA was developed by the government. Freedom of movement remains critically limited for many categories of conflict affected population:1) The number of people crossing all five EECPs is between 28,000 to 50,000 persons per day. The total capacities of the EECPs remains limited while their management is not always properly organized. The key concerns for the people as identified by R2P in the course of monitoring of EECPs are queues and poor infrastructure at the CPs. There are still no official persons (representatives from Coordination Group) responsible for resolving Temporary Order issues at 3 checkpoints.2) People living along the line of contact (LoC) remain limited in movement due to their residence closer to the LoC or due to militaries being located in their settlement.3) The majority of services and payments for officially registered IDPs are linked to place of their residence registration. Change of the registration location may result in suspension of payments or cancellation of IDP registration, which is another reason for limited freedom of movement. R2P’s EECP monitoring demonstrated that almost half the people travelling across the contact line do so with the intention of obtaining documents and\or social payments and pensions.

Activities & outcomes

● Activity cluster 1 will consist of national and local advocacy interventions, protection monitoring visits, interviewing people throughout the course of protection monitoring, capacity building, and information dissemination. The outcome will be strengthened protection for PoCs. ●Activity cluster 2 will consist of provision of primary and secondary legal aid, provision of protection counselling, and capacity building through group training sessions. The outcome will be PoCs receive access to services and enjoy their rights. All activities will be subject to R2P’s Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) framework. R2P respects and adheres to all 5 IASC principles on AAP. Specifically, R2P is committed to providing accessible, publicly available, and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them in both English and Ukrainian, as well as in other languages when necessary. All staff are subject to R2P’s code of conduct, which is available to clients for review. R2P has a zero tolerance policy for sexual exploitation, harassment, or abuse. All clients will have the opportunity to report feedback/complaints in person, phone or email. All complaints will be evaluated/investigated and reported back to the complainant. Feedback will incorporated programming. Additionally, R2P will hold regular focus groups with clients to obtain feedback and improve service, including from the elderly, women, families, the disabled, and minorities.

(12,000 women, 6,000 men; 18-35 – 4,000, 35-60 – 6,000, 60+ - 6,000; 4,800 persons (at least 40%) will be assisted in the localities near the Line of Contact);;-- Provide 600 Individual Protection Assistance packages (IPAs) (450 – women, 150- men, under 18 – 40, 18-35 – 130, 35-60 – 210, 60+ - 220 – based on analysis of existing IPA beneficiaries; 270 IPAs (80%) will be provided in the localities near the Line of Contact);- Provide 500 persons with “improved access to justice” assistance (legal “IPAs”) (375 – women, 125 - men, 18-35 – 100, 35-60 – 200, 60+ - 200).
