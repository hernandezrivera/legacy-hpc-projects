Project Summary

Under the umbrella of the ECW MYRP Phase II , UNESCO will continue to improve access, quality, mental health and psychosocial support (mental health and well-being of teachers and administrators), disability inclusion, gender equality, continuity, children protection, mobilization resources and enhancement of the education system.
Objectives: UNESCO intended the following objectives under JRP 2024: strengthen the capacity of parents/caregivers, teachers and other key community members to improve access to learning opportunities in a safe and protective environment for girls and boys, build the capacity of the stakeholders for ensuring gender and disability inclusive, safe and child friendly teaching and learning environment created for girls and boys, improve system related to process, inclusive, safe and equitable educational provision and increase resources for the expansion of quality, inclusive, safe and equitable education and skill development for teachers and administrators on Burmese Language Training in partnership with the Government of Bangladesh, the Cox’s Bazar Education Sector, UNHCR and its implementing partner(s).
Target beneficiaries: In this JRP 2024, 1376 parents/caregivers (70% female), 600 teachers/administrators/partner staff (400 will be female) will be trained on MHPSS, and 2,450 trainers/teachers and 200 staff from local NGOs (70% must be female) will benefit from Burmese language training. Furthermore 330 community leaders, govt. officials, school management committees will be sensitized on gender, crisis, disability disability-sensitive educational planning and management in 2024.
Partnership and implementation approaches: UNESCO has already successfully established a Project Support Team (PST) headed by the District Primary Education Officer (DPEO) and members are other government line departments (Women Affairs, Youth, Social Affairs) and development partners including UN consortia and NGOs. This programme in the host community is directly coordinated by the DPEO and supervised by Upazila Education Officer. The trained Government Primary Teachers are conducting parenting sessions twice in a month in 74 Government Primary Schools. In the camps, World Vision is implementing this parenting session at 70 Learning Centers in coordination with RRRC, CICs, UN consortia and implementing development partners. In order to develop the Myanmar Language Competency and Assessment Framework and Assessment Tools, UNESCO, DU and Greent Trara will work together to review the existing Myanmar Language Competency and Assessment Framework and adapt it for use in Cox’s Bazar in line with the Myanmar curriculum adopted for Rohingya refugee education. The assessment framework will provide a structured conceptual map of the learning outcomes of Burmese Language Training along with details of how achievement of the outcomes can be measured. The assessment manuals and tools will also be developed.
Establishment of Two (2) Language Labs: In coordination with UN consortia, ED Sector, the location will be identified and seek approval from Govt (RRRC). The labs will be used for both self-paced learning and organized training. The use of language labs will be integrated into the teacher training programme, emphasizing teachers’ Burmese language listening and speaking skills development. 2000 Refugess women, girls, men and boys will be directly benefited from this intervention in 2024, 70% will be female. Total of 2,450 teachers will be trained. It is expected that the trained teachers will work on improvement the language barriers and advocate for the gender-responsive educational system.
UNESCO will also arrange a conference at Cox’s Bazar with 1000 participants including representatives of government, donors, UN agencies, NGOs, CSOs, and teachers, and raise the voices for funding for crisis-affected populations.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

In the refugee camps parents and caregivers have reported various challenges including a lack of awareness of child education, newborn care and child development, nutrition, crisis management, mental coping with stress, trauma, child protection, gender sensitivity issues, etc. Many refugee children, adolescents, and women arrived in Bangladesh psychosocially distressed after witnessing and experiencing traumatic events. Additionally, where there is limited or restricted access to learning facilities, increased risks of violence in the home (including gender-based violence), child labor, children going missing, and child trafficking. Similar challenges are faced by the parents and adolescent girls in host communities. This trend is exacerbated by social and religious barriers that restrict the mobility of girls in public once they reach adolescence, as well as the prevalence of early marriage. This contributes to social isolation and exclusion, low levels of foundational literacy, psychological distress, exploitation, and abuse. Children with disabilities also face negative attitudes and stigma related to attending the same learning centers as children without disabilities. During the humanitarian crisis, including forced displacement and conflict, can have severe psychosocial impacts on individuals and families. The parenting education programme offers support to parents in dealing with stress, trauma, and emotional challenges, helping them provide a stable and supportive environment for their children. This intervention enhances parents' understanding of child development, health, and nutrition. This knowledge is vital for ensuring the overall well-being of children, especially in crisis settings where access to healthcare and other essential services may be limited. Parenting education programs can address issues related to the protection of children, including preventing violence, exploitation, abuse, and crisis coping mechanisms. This helps parents develop strategies to navigate challenges, address their own emotional well-being and promote gender equality by challenging traditional gender roles and empowering both mothers and fathers in their parenting roles. The government officials, community leaders of host community, administrators, and teachers will train on crisis management, psychosocial well-being and gender-responsive planning, coordination and management. In the case of teachers and teaching, it has been observed that if the teachers are not prepared, no other means are enough to ensure quality education for children. It is more important for the case of crisis-affected children, particularly the Rohingya Refugee Children. Myanmar Curriculum is used in the Learning Centers (LCs) Burmese is not the mother tongue of Rohingya Refugee children and teachers. Following the roll-out of the Myanmar Curriculum in the Cox’s Bazar camps, teachers have reported various challenges, with the topmost challenge being their current low level of mastery of the Burmese language and the need for more teaching and learning materials to support the implementation of the Myanmar Curriculum. It is even more challenging for female teachers as around 75% of female teachers are from the host community and are Bangladeshi.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Introducing Burmese Language Training for teachers in Rohingya Refugee Camps in Cox's Bazar and parenting education in the host community can have several rationalizations: 1. Rohingya refugees often come from Myanmar, where Burmese is the official language. Teaching the teachers Burmese can enhance communication between them and the Rohingya students, facilitating a more effective learning environment. 2. Cultural Sensitivity: Learning the language of the refugees demonstrates cultural sensitivity and respect. It fosters a more inclusive and understanding atmosphere, which can contribute to better relationships between the teachers and the Rohingya community. 3. Community Integration After Repatriation: Introducing Burmese language training contributes to integrating the Rohingya community into the Burmese culture and society. This can help break down language barriers and promote a sense of belonging and cooperation after repatriation. 4. Parenting Education in Host Community: Introducing parenting education in the host community can have positive effects on the overall well-being of families. It promotes healthier family dynamics, better child-rearing practices, and can contribute to the overall development of children and women will empower make their own decision. 5. Improved Social Cohesion: Parenting education initiatives can strengthen social bonds within the host community. Shared parenting knowledge can create a sense of solidarity and support among community members, fostering a more cohesive and supportive environment. 6. Humanitarian Values: The introduction of language training and parenting education aligns with humanitarian principles, emphasizing the importance of education, understanding, and support for vulnerable populations such as refugees and host communities.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This project contributes to reducing vulnerability while also fostering and protecting dignity by motivating and educating young women and men that transmits to community leaders, teachers, learning center committee members, school management committee member, faith leaders and local government and community influential leaders. The project carefully considers potential protection risks/threats in the community and work to mitigate and respond to these alongside community members, protection committees and local authorities of host communities and refugee camps. UNESCO is targeting 100 female and male community leaders inside the camps and 294 government administrators, School Management Committee members (SMCs), govt officials, head teachers, and female leaders in host community and sensitized them on gender, inclusion, and child protection, prevention of child marriage and child trafficking, reduction of child abuse, sexual exploitation issue. Parenting Education for parents and caregivers, strengthening capacity of LC management committee, Strengthening capacity of parental support group, strengthening capacity of community leaders, interaction between community level stakeholders particularly adolescent and young women group, community-based social mobilization activities, cultural events, celebration of national and international events together will help to ensure social cohesion and co-existence.In addition, specific events on Global Citizenship Education (GCE) and Learning for Empathy were introduced that also contribute to social cohesion intervention including peacebuilding/co-existence.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

This project is fully accountable to the affected population and can enhance accountability to the Rohingya communities in several ways: In the crisis, including forced displacement and conflict, can have severe psychosocial impacts on individuals and families. Parenting education programme will be implemented both in refugee and host community with the offer support to parents in dealing with stress, trauma, and emotional challenges, helping them provide a stable and supportive environment for their children. Parenting education enhances parents' understanding of child development, health, and nutrition. This knowledge is vital for ensuring the overall well-being of children, especially in crisis settings where access to healthcare and other essential services may be limited. This helps parents develop strategies to navigate challenges, address their own emotional well-being and promote gender equality by challenging traditional gender roles and empowering both mothers and fathers in their parenting roles. By providing Burmese Language Training to teachers, there is a direct effort to bridge the communication gap between educators and Rohingya students. This ensures that teachers can better understand the needs, concerns, and experiences of the students, leading to more responsive and accountable educational practices. This responsiveness contributes to a more inclusive and effective educational environment, directly addressing the unique needs of the affected population. To add with, as repatriation is a complex process involving a return to Myanmar, providing Burmese language training is a practical and accountable step in preparing Rohingya students for potential reintegration into the Myanmar education system. In essence, Burmese Language Training for teachers is a proactive step toward being more accountable to the Rohingya population in Cox's Bazar. It reflects a commitment to understanding and meeting the unique needs of the affected population.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, in this project DRR/DRM elements have been clearly highlighted and taken into special consideration. In the Parenting Guidebook, Chapter 10 covers Child Care and Protection in Crisis with the learning outcome including the topics of crisis and disaster, the role of parents and caregivers in supporting children in crisis, major child protection issues exacerbated by man-made and natural disasters, strategies for parents and caregivers to protect children from these major child protection issues. The teachers’ wellbeing guidebook attaches great importance to their wellbeing in crisis-affected contexts. A teachers' wellbeing guidebook can significantly contribute to DRR/DRM by fostering a resilient and prepared educational environment. By addressing the psychological and emotional well-being of teachers, the guidebook can enhance their capacity to cope with stress and trauma during and after disasters. Training of education stakeholders on crisis-sensitive and gender-responsive education planning, coordination, and management plays a pivotal role in addressing Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM). By incorporating a crisis-sensitive approach, education systems are designed to anticipate and respond effectively to the specific needs and vulnerabilities of individuals, particularly considering gender dynamics. This involves creating inclusive policies and strategies that ensure equal access to education during and after crises, accommodating diverse needs and concerns. A gender-responsive framework goes further by acknowledging and addressing the unique challenges faced by different genders in the context of disasters, fostering resilience and empowerment. Together, these approaches enhance the overall preparedness, resilience, and inclusivity of education systems in Rohingya camps and host communities, contributing to more effective DRR and DRM outcomes.
