Project Summary

We seek to provide CP services for IDPs and HC in our already existing child-friendly space in Idleb city and the 3 outreach teams who cover (Idleb, Harim, and Dana) communities and support children and their caregivers with different CP activities, including CP CM linked with emergency case fund, CP case management, structured PSS such as child resilience program by SCI, PSS recreational activities, awareness-raising on CP related topics: child marriage, violence, exploitation, etc, parenting skills, in addition to the establishment of CP community committees. 3 CP community committees will be established (10 members each) and trained on basic CP and identification and referral to empower their local role in advocating for child protection needs and supporting their future affairs. Moreover, IYD will keep the CP SC informed on the emerging CP concerns and needs in the 3 target locations by conducting the interviews and assessments designed by the CP situation monitoring task force CPSMTF to who we will report on a monthly basis.
Our CP team is trained and CPiE, CM, PSS, awareness-raising facilitation, and other related topics and they follow the recommendations of CP SC and the shared resources: SOPs and guidelines. The field team consists of 1/CP supervisor, 2 CP case workers/managers, 2 PSS workers, and 6 CP workers/outreach. The field team will be supervised by the CP specialist and Protection Sr. Officer in Turkey and administratively managed by the project officer.
We plan to start the project on January 01st 2023 and close it on December 31st 2023, and target 4,837 beneficiaries including: 3,634 IDPs and 1,203 HC, and: 2,800 Females, and 2,037 Males, of them 204 are PwDs through our 1/already established Child Friendly Space in Idleb city, and the 3 outreach teams: Idleb, Harim, and Dana communities. PwDs will be provided by transportation to ensure their safe access to the CFS which is already rehabilitated to fit the needs of PwDs while enjoying the activities.
IYD is an active member in the Protection cluster, CP SC, GBV SC, Mine Action SC, GBV & SRH TWG and will continue adhering to 4Ws, and will take benefit of its multisectoral activities provided under different grants: Cash, Shelter, SFL, NFI, ERL to enrich the referral of CP cases (internally) to the mentioned services as needed and thus to complement the response. IYD has been providing quality remote management through the cross-border Turkey Hub with frequent field visits by senior managers to NWS, projects sits, to continue the direct monitoring and evaluation beside the other M&E activities: Base line & endline, regular interviews with BNFs, stakeholders, .
WHO’s recommendations to prevent COVID-19 and Cholera will be closely followed and applied by the teams for further safety for them and beneficiaries.
Throughout the implementation period, the HQ and field teams will intensify the coordination with CPSC, CP implementing partners, stakeholders, and community actors to ensure the smooth process and the effective referral pathways as per the most updated service mapping.

3 - Needs

According to CP MSNA Findings in NW Syria, the scale of children in need of humanitarian assistance is on the rise, increasing 27% from 2020 to 2021, with 6.1 million children now affected. 75% of people in need for protection services are women and children, where children with and without disabilities comprise 44% of the people in need across Syria. Approximately 2.8 million IDPs were living in 1,389 camps or informal sites. Also, as top reason, 21.6% of HHs reported that children between 3 and 17 years of age do not go to school because they are working to support their families. In Idleb Governorate, 25.6% of HHs confirmed this statement, while in Aleppo 26.4% of HHs confirmed it. According to HNO 2022, insecurity, economic hardship and COVID-19 exacerbate (CP) Child protection concerns. Girls and boys continue to experience persisting violence including physical, emotional, sexual and domestic violence. These fuelled harmful coping mechanisms, and increased child labor and child marriage.According to IYD need assessment there was a significant rise in child labour, child marriage, and the overall psychological distress among girls and boys due to the deteriorating economic situation and hard living conditions. 38% of adolescent girls are out of schools, whereas 42% of adolescent boys reported the same problem. Child marriage is still one of the reasons for the separation of girls from their families in Idleb at 65% , while for boys was financial reasons in Idleb at 41.7

4 - Implementation of activities

Once the project starts, we will review and apply our proposed plans: recruitment, procurement, M&E, media, financial, safety and security, etc. CP field teams will be trained on the proposed programmatic topics and skills, including CPiE, PSEA and child safeguarding, case management, PSS, PFA, facilitation and communication skills, etc, and will also be supervised by the CP field supervisor and the CP HQ specialist to ensure the quality of the day-to-day implementation of the proposed activities: CP case management linked with ECF, awareness raising, PSS structured, PSS recreational, parenting skills, CP situation monitoring assessments, and the establishment of the CP community committees. Referral to CP and other services from different sectors (internally to IYD) or externally, including education, health, cash, FSL, shelter, GBV, GP, etc, will be harmonized as per the most updated service mapping. The project field team will introduce the project’s aim and interventions by visiting target local councils, schools, community centers and institutions, camps, volunteer teams, and committees. Outreach teams will be deployed to cover Idleb, Harim, and Al-Dana communities and will be the entry point for the early detection of CP concerns and needs in target locations and thus promote our timely and effective proposed prevention and response actions. M&E teams will be trained on the CP basics and the relevant CP M&E tools and considerations to conclude the lessons learned

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The PSEA Standards will be adopted by the IYD team by taking an active role in PSEA efforts by monitoring and considering SEA risks that may be present in our field activities and how those can be effectively responded to mitigated, and prevented, in addition to ensuring that PSEA mainstreaming issues will be considered during the program implementing process through the procedures explained below: • IYD has adopted the inter-agency Code of Conduct Which condemns the SEA ( Endorsed by the Humanitarian Liaison Group - 2020) for the Syria cross-border response, which is intended for all staff and representatives of IYD as well as the associated contractors, and volunteers. • IYD is an active member of the PSEA network, and it is already familiar with the network’s monthly reporting tool, where IYD reports its PSEA monthly activities such as (the number of beneficiaries reached by the distribution of awareness-raising materials, the number of beneficiaries reached by providing PSEA awareness-raising sessions, the number of field staff with direct interaction with beneficiaries who trained on PSEA). • IYD has established an internal SEA reporting and investigative mechanism which will not tolerate an atmosphere of impunity toward SEA. Zero tolerance means that active measures will be introduced to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse by IYD and that appropriate disciplinary action will be taken against all persons who are found to have violated the IYD standards of conduct.
