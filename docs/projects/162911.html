Project Summary

The main objectives of this project are:
1. To ensure the provision of quality, lifesaving nutrition services for acutely malnourished children (boys and girls 6 - 59 months of age) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) among highly vulnerable communities
2. Improve equitable access to integrated program interventions for the prevention of under nutrition among affected communities
The project targets children aged 6–59 months and Pregnant and Lactating Women and Girls with specialized nutritious foods and social and behaviour change messages aimed at the prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition across selected states and localities in Sudan.
A comprehensive package of food and non-food-based interventions are provided taking into account nutritional vulnerabilities.
The proposed response from WFP is in line with the Nutrition Sector Strategy and with the Multi-Year Humanitarian Response Strategy of Sudan. Through this project, WFP will be responding to the nutritional needs of targeted beneficiaries under Outcomes identified by nutrition sector.
With a particular focus on Do No Harm and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles, WFP Sudan strives to enhance protection mainstreaming through the following priority areas: 1) Training and capacity building of key WFP and Cooperating Partners (CP); 2) protection risk analysis focused on safety, dignity and integrity of beneficiaries; 3) integration of protection indicators into program tools, notably Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) tools; 4) design, implementation and adjustment of programs in line with identified protection concerns particularly for those disabled and with special needs; and 5) strengthened partnerships and coordination on protection issues.

1 - Needs

Acute and chronic malnutrition are widespread and pervasive and constitute a significant public health problem. Over 2 million children under 5 suffer stunting annually, and 2 million wasting. There are several causes of undernutrition, including limited access to food, marginal and unstable livelihoods, poor quality of health services. Sudan Ministry of Health conducted national nutrition survey in 2018 (S3M II) which indicated slight reduction has been noted in the main nutritional indicators in the S3M II (2018) when compared to S3M I (2013) but the nutrition situation remains poor:  The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate remains concerning at 14.1%, out of which 9.70% are Moderately Acutely Malnourished (MAM) and 4.4% are Severely Acutely Malnourished (SAM);  The prevalence of chronic malnutrition is 36.6%, meaning that one in every 3 Sudanese children do not grow to their full cognitive and physical potential. These indicators have shown a minor improvement from 2013 (decreased from 38.2%), however the scale of the problem remains concerning;  2.32% of children are overweight with the Gedaref state having the highest prevalence at 4.39%; Surveyed population indicated that the top two most preferred channel for health and nutrition messages was through outreach workers (37.7%) and radio (23.8%) followed by TV (17.9%); 81.2% of carers were able to reach health facilities within an hour. However, only 45.8% of these carers were attended to by a health worker at the facility; For IYCF indicators, exclusive breastfeeding is relatively high at 61.52% for all of Sudan. Age appropriate continued breastfeeding is also high at 73.79% as is age appropriate meal frequency at 63.79%; Age-appropriate dietary diversity, on the other hand, is low at 24.11%; Household iodized salt coverage is poor across the whole of Sudan with the proportion of households with adequately iodized salt at 4.67%. The proportion of households with substandard or no iodized salt is at 95.33%. Across states, this low rate of salt iodization persists with very little inter-state variation except for South Darfur which has a significantly higher proportion of households with adequately iodized salt at 38.09%. Using the GAM prevalence’s at state and/or locality levels as entry points, WFP’s targeting approach for the nutrition interventions, mainly the Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP), will be guided by those key aspects: In WFP operational areas: 1. All localities with GAM > 10% to be assisted with the Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme;2. All localities with GAM < 10% and food insecurity of 50% or higher to be assisted with the Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme;3. All localities with GAM < 10% and food insecurity less than 50% to be transitioned to the government with assistance provided for the implementation of non-food-based approaches such as the NIPP circles. That support will be articulated through the yearly workplan signed with the MoH for example;4. Continued focus on ensuring Social and Behaviour Change Communication (SBCC) and Nutrition Sensitive approaches are mainstreamed across all nutrition activities. Special focus should be placed on strengthening community outreach and radio use as these are the most preferred methods of communication by the beneficiaries; 5. Efforts to enhance linkage with resilience activities with the expected outcome of improved dietary diversity for children under 5 years of age. WFP’s targeting of states is determined through an analysis of a set of indicators including food insecurity, malnutrition, access to education etc. At the time of the development of WFP’s Country Strategic Plan, the latest food security and nutrition surveys were utilized to determine areas of highest need.

2 - Activities / Outputs

WFP activities are align with nutrition sector strategy outlines which composed of active case finding, screening and referrals of MAM& SAM cases to address their needs at nutrition respective services . WFP approaches to ensure sector provision of services are classified as below: 1) Provide preventive and curative nutrition activities to children aged 6–59 months and PLWG: Immediately following a shock and until relevant indicators stabilize, WFP will conduct emergency blanket supplementary feeding for all PLWG and children aged 6-59 months in affected populations, complementing general food distribution. 2) Provide curative and preventive nutrition activities to children aged 6–59 months and PLWG and capacity strengthening to national and state health institutions. In addition to moderate acute malnutrition treatment, emphasis will be placed on making prevention activities the foundation of WFP’s nutrition interventions in targeted areas. 3) Food-based prevention of malnutrition targeting the first 1,000 days of life to prevent stunting and wasting, consisting of nutrition rations 88 for at-risk PLWG and children under zero-23, accompanied by context-specific nutrition-sensitive interventions 4) Home fortification interventions through the distribution of micro nutrient powders free of charge to children 6-59 month 5) Social and Behavior Change Communication (SBCC) as an integral part of all prevention and treatment activities
