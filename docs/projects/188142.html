Project Summary

Responding to the increasing demand in the overburdened communities as a result of the continuous arrival of tens of thousands of forcefully displaced people. The project proposed interventions seek to improve the humanitarian situation for the war-affected population by rehabilitating the houses for the IDPs and host communities, rehabilitating of collective shelters which will provide more security and privacy to the inhabitants, prepare the settlements/campsites with the needed intervention to improve the inhabitants IDP families’ quality of life, and distribute cash assistance for the in-need families
The project will be implemented for the affected population in Northwest Aleppo and Northern Idleb (AL Bab, Afrin, Aghtarin, Azaz, Daret Azza, Dana, Daret Azza, Qourqeena and Salqin sub-districts). 32,000 people will benefit from all activities (5,632 men, 6,272 women, 8,448 boys, 11,648 girls) while coordination with local councils, shelter centers’ management, and other actors in the area is the first mandatory step for all output:
• 700 houses are rehabilitated with medium rehabilitation activity (800.00$ per house), in Al-Bab (150HHs), Afrin (200HHs), Daret Azza (150HHs), Salqin (100HHs) and Dana (100HHs)
• 100 HHs in 5 collective shelters will be assisted through rehabilitation activity with medium rehabilitation activity (10,000$ per center), in both Dana and Daret Azza.
• Distribute of 5,000 cash for winter to new IDPs in Al-Bab 1500HHs, Daret Azza 1000 HHs, Salqin 1000HHs and Dana 1500 HHs/150$ per HH
• Campsite preparation with all infrastructure needed in Azaz, Qourqeena, Dana and Maaret Tamsrin sub-districts, 1200$ per HH for 400 HHs, the activity will include removing obstacles and levelling the land, Strengthen the land with backfilling materials, graveling the site to leave it in the best shape possible, providing all the infrastructure and services needed as, sanitation, drainage, water systems, electricity system, fencing, facilities etc.
• construction 200 transitional housing units with all infrastructure needed in Al-Bab and Afrin sub-districts. 1600$ per HH with all needed facilities inside the unit.
As the document “dignified and safer living conditions” shared by SNFI cluster
Rehabilitation works will include:
o Ensuring internal living spaces’ security and privacy by providing doors and windows for the houses
o Ensure the most of the beneficiary’s safety against life-threating issues by implementing minor rehabilitation works to the house’s structural elements (walls, ceilings, roofs, floors ...etc.)
o Ensuring a healthier environment by promoting hygiene and maintaining the WASH facilities in the house
o Providing illumination and a light power source in each room by providing the needed electrical points (lamps, switches, wires...etc.) and connect them to the local power source
o Ensuring better living conditions for people with special needs by providing special architectural elements (metal grips and handles, metal railings, special needs people suitable toilets...etc.)
Buildings with damaged structural elements will be excluded
IRD will coordinate with the local authorities and target the most crowded communities, centers or camps if applicable
To be accepted by the project criteria, HHs and building owners should agree on the terms of assistance and sign a contract that allows the BNF to stay in the building for at least one year
Valid HLP documents that verify the ownership of the properties most be presented
Coordination at all levels and with all stakeholders will be continuous throughout the project duration, and the implementation will be monitored and eventually evaluated

4 - Needs

Shelter and NFI needs in Syria are widespread and acute resulting in signiﬁcant living standard consequences and affecting physical and mental well-being and protection of affected populations. Overall shelter severity has increased across Syria, with a corresponding increase in PiN in 2021 from 2020 by 20 percent to 5.65 million people.1 Of this, 3.8 million (69 percent) are in areas of severity 4 or higher. Geographically, these areas are concentrated in Idleb and rural of Aleppo, up to one million IDPs reside in last-resort informal and spontaneous settlements. MSNA data demonstrates the cumulative impact of the crisis. More than three-quarters of that paying rent are unable to afford it. Shelter adequacy is critical in 238 out of 272 sub-districts. This translates into families not having the security of tenancy while others living in an inadequate shelter may be left unprotected from the elements, in insecure situations or overcrowded housing, raising possible risks to personal safety and protection. IDPs are particularly affected by shelter damage and deterioration - there has been a 30 percent reduction in the number of IDPs with access to undamaged shelter.As the SNFI 2021 results framework, the activities were chosen to respond to the increasing needs under both sector objectives 1 & 2.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

IRD adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards sexual exploitation and abuse. And it is committed to the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse both within IRD and within the framework of IRD programs and their beneficiary.To this aim, IRD’s Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: • Encourages prevention; • Promotes detection; • Sets out a straightforward investigation procedure; • Sets out a mechanism of sanctions IRD fully upholds principles of freedom of speech and protection of victims of sexual abuse and exploitation. As such, IRD has developed mechanisms that can contribute to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse such as • Signing of the Code of Conduct by the staff in order to clarify unacceptable behavior and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; • Information to the beneficiaries, staff, and all stakeholders about the existing Complaints and Response Mechanisms - CRM which can allow them to send their concerns and complaints in a confidently way without fear • Implementation of training and awareness sessions to train and empower the teams, particularly for staff exposed to working with minors or the most vulnerable population; • Identify risk factors on an ongoing basis and in particular throughout the project cycle; • Put in place evaluation procedures for IRD staff, partners, and stakeholders by carrying out due diligence, particularly with regard to their ethical commitments and past behaviors.
