Project Summary

According to the 2021 GHI (GHI SCORES BY 2021 GHI RANK), Somalia has the highest level of hunger with a score of 50.8, considered extremely alarming. At 59.5%, Somalia’s 2018–2020 prevalence of undernourishment was the highest of any country with available data. In recent years, Somalia has faced multiple crises that have increased food insecurity, including droughts, floods, desert locusts, and the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic. In 2021 the situation remains dire:
- Political situation: the country is dealing with political tensions and related violence, due to the delayed electoral process and power struggles at the leadership level.
- Climate Change: Somalia is expected to experience its third consecutive season of below-average rainfall, mostly developing into a major drought by early to mid-2022, and leading to nearly 3.5 M people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through the end of 2021 (FSNAU-FEWS NET 2021 Post Gu Technical Release - Sept 2021). At the same time, large parts of Somalia remain prone to severe riverine and flash flooding.
- Conflicts and insecurity: hundreds of thousands of people fled their homes in 2021 and more displacement is expected in 2022. Urban centers are receiving large waves of forcibly displaced people and rural-urban migrants, leading to competition for resources. 2.9 million people (2.2 in urgent need of assistance) are estimated to be internally displaced throughout the country FSNAU-FEWS NET 2021 Post Gu Technical Release - Sept 2021, and Somalia HNO 2022).
-The pandemic: COVID-19 heavily impacted on the country’s fragile health systems, with a resurgence of cases and documented infections in the first quarter of 2021, and significant economic and humanitarian consequences.
Following the delayed onset of Deyr rains, many zones in Galmudug are in a dire situation as severe drought conditions are hitting the most vulnerable communities. If no rainfall comes before the end of 2021 Deyr season, it will be the third consecutive poor rainy season. The scarcity of water and pasture resulted by the pronged dry spell triggered many rural communities to move to nearby towns after losing their animals and in search for support. Deyr rainfall expected in early Oct. 2021 has also delayed weakening the animals and reduced their price. The region has also faced recurrent conflicts including inter-clan clashes and Non-state armed actors causing displacement of more than 40000 households and restricted access to water source, grazing lands and other services (WASH Cluster Drought Affects Needs Assessment in Galmudug State, Oct. 2021).
Beletweyne district is suffering from the cumulative impacts of multiple weather shocks and violent clan conflicts, coupled with the ongoing recovery from the 2020 economic contraction. The rains received from May 2021 triggered flash floods in Beletweyne district affecting food security and livelihoods. The floods usually destroy hectares of farmland, disrupt learning in schools and damages infrastructures. Continuous climate shocks will cause displacement, jeopardize access to safe water, contribute to an increase of water-borne diseases and negatively impact livelihoods.
In this context Cesvi intends to support vulnerable communities (5600 HHs = 33,600 individuals) in Galmudug, Hirshabelle, South West State and Banadir with MPCAs (6 months), CfW addressing small scale infrastructures repair; IGA and self help group business for women and livelihoods support; emergency livestock assistance through provision of animals treatment distribution of agricultural inputs.

1. - Projects activities

Objective 1: Improving access to food through conditional and unconditional transfers. Activity 1.1: Provision of 6 months MPCAs to: 1000 HHs in Galgaduud and Mudug regions (Mudug, Hobyo, Abudwaq districts), where the intervention will target pastoralist communities affected by the drought. 500 HHs in Hiran region (Beletweyne District), targeting communities affected by the recurrent and expected floods. 1000 HHs in Lower Shabelle (Afgoye district), targeting IDPS and vulnerable and marginalized communities. 1000 HHs in Banadir IDPs. Each HH will receive 6 months of UCT based on the local MEB. The mode of payment will be through mobile phones.Activity 1.2: Rehabilitation of public infrastructures through CfW activity in Mudug, Hiran and Lower Shabelle regions. The project will target 500 HHs in Galkayo and Hobyo districts, 400 HHs in Beletweyne district (such as river banks and canals, feeder roads) and 100 HHs in Afgoye district. Selection of the structures to be rehabilitated will be done in consultation with the communities and according to the priorities identified by the communities. Objective 2: Seasonal livelihood inputs support (agriculture and livestock). Activity 2.1: Provision of animal treatment kits and refresher training to 50 community animal health workers (CAHWs) in Mudug region. The project will provide animal treatment kits to trained community animal health workers (CAHWs) in Hobyo and Galkayo districts. The activity will be implemented in collaboration with the Central Regions Livestock Professional Association (CERELPA), a private professional association which brings together veterinarians, Vet assistance and Para-vet within and outside of Central regions Somalia and the Galmudug Ministry of Livestock. The CAHWs will provide vet support (examination and treatment of animals/heard and vaccinations) to an estimated target of 450 HHs. Activity 2.2: Provision of agricultural inputs to: 100 farmers in Herodhagaxley village under Hobyo town, who be provided with locally purchased agricultural inputs tools such as seeds, fertilizers, farming equipment among others; 500 farmers in Beletweyne district will be supported with agricultural seeds (maize, cowpea, mung beans, onions, hot peppers and sweet potatoes) and basic farming tools. Activity 2.3: Income generation activities/self-help group business grants, livelihood inputs. In March 2021, Cesvi carried out a study to understand the specific needs of young people in specific locations in the country and to identify the most sustainable income-generating activities. Findings from the study are key to identifying priorities and trends in the world of work in Somalia, as well as exploring the links between vocational technical training (TVET) initiatives, private entities and local labor markets. Findings from the study will be the basis to support 100 individuals in Mudug, Hobyo, 100 in Beletweyne . Among the above mentioned targets, 60 people will be involved in VSLAs activities. Participants in VSLA groups are predominantly women, engaged in small business activities. The project will actively involve women in all its activities and all phases. They suffer the most from macro- and micronutrient deficiencies. Food-price spikes have negative repercussions for female HH heads. They suffer labor market discrimination, which confines which confines them to informal and casual employment, as well as pay inequity. Persons with disabilities, persons from minority clans, marginalized communities will also be significantly involved.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The main objective of this Action is assisting the most vulnerable people through both unconditional and conditional cash distributions. Emergency cash support to households in need is the life-saving emergency intervention foreseen wit this Action. Cesvi’s approach will put people at the centre of the interventions to reduce humanitarian needs, risks and vulnerabilities in the medium to longer term, by, at the same time, strengthening synergies and complementarities with development, early recovery and resilience initiatives. The modality chosen for the intervention are both Unconditional (MPCAs)and Conditional cash transfers (CfW). Use of cash gives beneficiaries the purchasing power and they can access multiple items from the market, at their own choice. Both MPCA and conditional cash transfers, as a modality have the capacity to empower individuals to solve a wide-array of problems from healthcare access to business training to nutrition to increased social standing, to income generation. This approach preserves people’s dignity as it gives them greater choice. Cash transfer modality is seen as an effective means to aid displaced populations, supporting a dignified assistance and value for money. In details, MPCTs allow beneficiaries to choose exactly which purchases are most critical to them, and where and when they want to spend the money. Frequency of payment and the amount of the cash transfer can influence how MPCTs are spent: one-time, larger transfers are often spent on rebuilding/ recovering livelihoods, whereas multiple, smaller transfers are often used to cover basic household needs like food, medicine and clothing. For Conditional Cash Transfer the action will use CfW aiming to provide consistent short-term wages to a targeted group of vulnerable, crisis affected households when they are not actively employed in other activities. The objective of the Action is to provide life-saving assistance to prevent a deterioration of the situation through both integrated modalities allowing different beneficiaries to cover basic needs as shock response. For a better and effective integration of both transfer modalities beneficiaries will be selected with the local communities according to their ability and needs. Also, injection of cash into the market boosts the local economy and creates a multiplier effect. The existing market is able to respond to huge cash injection, and furthermore, there is an active mobile service provider with a large coverage area in the country with good network access and financial access through the phone. For this intervention, the following factors were considered:Advantages of MPCAs:• Quick to distribute andcirculate.• Low administration costs.• Very flexible form of CTP.• Can quickly reach the most vulnerable.• Long experience of Cesvi Risks/disadvantages related to MPCAs:• Difficult to monitor usage after transfer.• Level of risks associated to targeting and registration are high because cash is desirable to everyone.Advantages of CfW;• Easier to target beneficiariesthan vouchers or cash transfers.• Creates community assets.• Keeps beneficiaries engaged in recovery.Risks/disadvantages related to CfW:• High administration and operational costs• Some of the most vulnerable and food insecure households may not be able to participate(e.g., PLW, elderly, ill, PWD)• It's not an immediate support (longer organization needed).• May interfere with labor markets or other household activities or priorities.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Cesvi, through the Building Resilience Communities in Somalia (BRCiS) consortium, is investing in Market Based Programming in the areas targeted by the proposed action, in order to encourages a holistic and sustainable approach to meeting objectives, adding value to programme quality, purpose and strategic relevance. Within the framework of Cesvi's programmes, MBP can:• Enable exit by contributing to support economic growth, linking vulnerable populations with the systems that will continue to support them in the future, and expanding access to basic services, which are critical in developing competitive and inclusive economies and reducing poverty.• Increase the sustainability and impact of interventions that support the local economy, potentially creating cost-efficient and cost-effective programmes, with lower fraud and protection related risks.• Promote a ‘do no harm’ approach as MBP can assist in the understanding of how and why market systems serve various groups (youth/ women/ men) differently. This is through designing interventions that are market sensitive, gender and protection orientated. More in general, Cesvi’s interventions in Market Based Programming cover all types of engagement and interventions within market systems, ranging from actions that deliver immediate relief to those that work towards proactively strengthening and catalyzing local market systems or market hubs. In principle Cesvi works to facilitate access to markets and support producers in negotiating prices. Cesvi’s work revolves around 3 main pillars, with a stronger focus on 1 and 2: i. USING MARKETS: using existing markets, where still functioning, to assist populations affected by crisis.ii. SUPPORTING MARKETS: re-establishment/ reinforcement of supply chains/ essential services.iii. DEVELOPING MARKETS: longer term engagements to bring about change in a market system In Somalia, Cesvi interventions over the years evolved from a humanitarian-first approach into a carefully designed graduation model for community resilience. Over time, its components were combined with the provision of assistance services for value chain development and various training activities aimed at building the planning and disaster management capacities of community committees, as well as fostering individual technical and business skills.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1) Risk 1: MM1. Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by identifying and prioritise the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalised groups. MM2 Apply a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries, otherwise vulnerable people such as older people, may easily be excluded as other more mobile and vocal population groups are registered. MM3 Ensure lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing services. MM4. Make sure beneficiaries know how to obtain food assistance and livelihood opportunities, where and when the distributions will take place, what they will get, how long it should last and how to use the items. MM5 Communicate information through various means to reach the broader community. MM6. Coordinate with civil society specializing in working with persons with disabilities to help identify such individuals Risk 2: MM1. Distributions should take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. MM2. Ensure the mode and frequency of distribution minimises safety risk and be aware that beneficiaries may face theft, intimidation, sexual assault and extortion whilst taking their rations home. MM3. Keep lists of beneficiaries and personal data confidential and safe from unintended use by applying effective data protection measures. MM4. Work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from food distributions
