Project Summary

This project aims to provide comprehensive WASH, and Nutrition prevention to crisis IPC affected community of Fangak, Duk and Aweil East counties that are affected by multiple humanitarian crisis including conflict, displacement, flooding, food insecurity, and population return burdens.
MTA will respond to the critical WASH needs of 29,103 Individuals including men, women, boys and girls with and without disability by enhancing access to basic WASH services with specific activities for GBV mitigations that will Contribute to reducing WASH- related morbidity and mortality.
In Nutrition, MTA targets 48,257 Individuals with increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents, and women and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PBW in prioritized locations of Duk, Fangak and Aweil East counties.
MTA have a proven track record of implementing nutrition prevention programs in collaboration with Action Against Hunger in Duk, Fangak, and Aweil counties, integrating essential WASH and FSL components

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to grapple with effect of conflict, displacement, insecurity, flooding, economic difficulties, and the influx of returnees from Sudan further straining the already limited resources and deepening extreme poverty.The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating as a result of the economic crisis, repeated climatic shocks – primarily widespread flooding – and conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation – putting additional pressure on an already fragile country. (IPC South Sudan Acute Food Insecurity Malnutrition September 2024 July2025 Report)Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analysed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity or IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal County (Upper Nile State) and an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above. (IPC South Sudan Acute Food Insecurity Malnutrition September 2024 July2025 Report)The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. (IPC South Sudan Acute Food Insecurity Malnutrition September 2024 July 2025 Report)In the second projection period of April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analysed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4 and 63,000 people likely to be in Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The contributing factors for acute malnutrion include limited access to WASH facilities, high disease burden, poor child care and feeding practices among others. The counties of Duk, Fangak and Aweil East are currently classified under the critical phase (IPC 4) and are expected to remain in IPC 4 from December 2023 to July 2024. This situation is forecast to affect 51,000, 31000, 71,000 and 89000 individuals in Duk, Fangak and Aweil East County respectively, by July 2025. highlighting an urgent need for intervention and support to address the escalating crisis. (IPC South Sudan Acute Food Insecurity Malnutrition September 2024 July 2025 Report)Access to WASH basic services is limited in South Sudan. A significant portion of the population (41 percent) lacks access to improved water sources, and 37 percent need to travel over 30 minutes to reach their primary water source, posing particular hardships for persons with disabilities and making women and girls more vulnerable to GBV. Access to sanitation is even more limited with prevalent open defecation, as 88 percent of the population lacks access to improved latrines, according to the 2023 FSNMS data and 2024 HNRP.

2. Project activities

Mission Trust Aid will conduct the following activities; 1) Providing standard kits paired with hygiene promotion to SAM children/ care takers2) Providing access to sanitation facilities through latrine constriction/renovation in schools and nutrition centers taking into consideration floods resilience and disability inclusiveness3) Providing access to safe water at nutrition facilities, schools and community through construction/ rehabilitation of water points taking into consideration floods resilience4) Hygiene promotion including how to make flood-resilient HH latrines from local materials.5) Distribution of WASH kits during emergency6) Training women to make reusable sanitary pads using available materials7) Safety audit to improve access to water and Sanitation for women and girls 8) Training nutrition workers on WASH components.9) Establishment of mother-to-mother support groups10) Training of Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs)/Booma Health Workers (BHWs) on MIYCN/prevention of malnutrition11) Training of health workers on MIYCN/Prevention of malnutrition12) Provision of counselling to primary caregivers of children aged 0-23 months who on Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) Counselling.13) Provision of counselling services to pregnant women who received MIYCN 14) Training of pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls (PBWGs) and caregivers on Family MUAC to screen children for acute malnutrition15) Screening of of children aged 6-59 months for acute malnutrition16) Provision of WASH kits to children under 5 years with severe acute malnutrition paired with hygiene promotion17) Conducting Safety audits of nutrition site18) Establishment of kitchen gardens for demonstration at nutrition sites19) Integrating Early Childhood Development program at nutrition sites20) Training of frontline nutrition staff trained on GBV basics, including how to respond to GBV disclosures safely and appropriately.
