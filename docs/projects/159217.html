Project Summary

Nearly three decades of cyclical droughts, resurgent conflict, lack of governance and rule of law coupled lack of long-term development programs continue to negatively impact the lives of vulnerable Somali women, girls, boys, and men. The sustained rain failures of three seasons has led to a severe drought in the country, crop production in the breadbasket region as well as most Agro-pastoral areas that depend on seasonal planting have declined decimally. This escalated increased food insecurity among the Agro-pastoral rural communities that mostly depend on own crop production for food source. The drought also led to widespread depletion of pasture and water in the predominant pastoral areas. Food security have severely declined among pastoral communities due to high loss of livestock’s, a situation that led to reduced income and low purchasing power for poor households. Other major drivers of food insecurity include, increased food prices in contrast to decline of livestock prices, poor livelihood diversifications and reduced access for areas under Al-shabab control. According to FSANAU Sept 2019 update, 2.1M across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps, high acute malnutrition, or depletion of assets indicative of crisis(IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through December 2019, Moreover, an estimated one million children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through mid-2020. Sustained and large-scale humanitarian assistance, which is planned and likely through September, is currently preventing more severe outcomes in many areas. In the absence of humanitarian assistance from October to December, however, food security is expected to deteriorate. Current high levels of food insecurity are driven by drought conditions from late 2018 through mid-May 2019, exacerbated by below-average and erratically distributed rainfall during the 2019 Gu season (April-June), which began late and finished early. Many households are still recovering from the severe 2016/2017 drought or have been affected by conflict, and 2.6 million people remain displaced. The Gu cereal harvest failed in most regions, leaving most poor agropastoral and riverine households unable to meet their minimum food needs given significantly below-average food stocks and income from seasonal agricultural employment. In pastoral areas, the impact of the drought on livestock led to a lack of access to milk, and many poor households have accumulated large debts while struggling to feed their families and rescue their remaining livestock. Although the 2019 Deyr (October/December) rains are forecast to be average to above average, the positive impact on food security will not be realized until late 2019. However, seasonal production activities will mitigate more severe deterioration during this period. These findings are based on the 2019 post-Gu seasonal food security and nutrition assessment conducted across Somalia during June-July 2019 and subsequent analyses, jointly led by the Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit for Somalia (FSNAU, a project managed by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations) and the Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWS NET, a project funded by USAID) and carried out with the active participation of Government institutions, UN and NGO partners. In order to address this problem, NCA will undertake a sustained food security and livelihood support intervention in 2020. The geographical coverages of the response include regions of Bari, Nugal and Gedo. The specific activities to be undertaken will be largely be cash based interventions to improve purchasing power and food access for households in emergency situations, Recovery and Livelihood protection activities covering livestock and crop production support, economic recovery and diversification of livelihood opportunities will also be undertaken

Project Activities

To achieve the proposed objectives NCA will undertake the following activities in each of its three objective areas.The specific activities to be undertaken will be largely be cash based interventions to improve purchasing power and food access for households in emergency situations, Recovery and Livelihood protection which will support economic recovery and diversification of livelihood opportunities be undertakena) To improve immediate food security status for households affected by drought and conflict emergencies in Somalia. The activities to be undertaken under this objective will mainly be live saving emergency responses that will exclusively target communities faced with acute food insecurity and classified to be in IPC 3. The target groups include new displaced households, returnees and host communities severely devastated by the droughts who have completely lost their income and food sources opportunities. The broad-based focus is to save the lives of the target households by extending to them opportunities to immediately access food either through improving their immediate income sources and purchasing power to access food from the local markets and or direct in-kind food transfer. A total of 1800 Households (10,800 persons) will be reached through the following activities. 1. Unconditional cash transfer targeting 1000 households during the peak period of emergency 2. Conditional Cash transfer through Cash for Work that targets 800 Householdsb) To protect and restore livelihood sources of the drought and conflict destabilized households to realized enhanced food and income sources. The activities under this objective will be largely recovery-based initiatives that will target vulnerable households facing severe food insecurity and these groups requires livelihood protection to avoid them from the risks of slipping further into destitution as their livelihood sources remains under the threat of increased depletion. The number of households to be targeted under this objective is 800 households (4800 persons) and the specific activities will include the following. 3. Provision of fodder seeds to 400 HH this activity will be partnered with the line ministry of Agriculture, irrigation and climate 4. Provision of season specific agricultural inputs such as seeds for planting, assorted certified seeds and solar Pumps, farming hand tools, bush clearances, extension services training GAP for 400 householdsd) To build community resilience’s to withstand the effects of the current and future shocks. The activities under this objective will focus on resilience building initiative meant to enhance the target communities coping strategies to future shocks. Focus will be given to activities that will promote self-reliance’s among the target communities and uplift them from sustained dependency on humanitarian assistance. The target number for this object is 400 HH (2400 Persons) and the priority activities will includes; 5 Community managed Disaster Risks Reduction strategies and Disaster early warning systems (10 communities) Training of local Government staff, Community Animal Health Workers, Leaders and pastoralists, this will ensure that communities have the skills and knowledge needed to plan for and cope with future livelihood shocks they will be trained on risk reduction and early warning. The training will use iterative methodologies, with communities encouraged to identify issues, early warning signs that they can monitor and helped to develop tentative response plans. 7. Social facilities infrastructure rehabilitation through Cash for work (5) Irrigation canals rehabilitation, (5) sand dams/de-silting of dam (800HHs)

Mitigation Measures

NCA is committed to gender-sensitive programming which recognizes and addresses the different needs, experiences, capacities and responsibilities of women, girls, boys and men, including female-headed households. Program interventions will be designed to address the varying vulnerabilities, capacities and needs at community and household levels, recognizing that some groups are disproportionally impacted by shocks and stresses. Assessment, implementation, and disaggregated M&E will be informed by gender analysis to ensure that different needs of women, girls, boys and men are correctly identified and addressed. The program will promote empowerment of women by creating greater opportunities for their involvement in key institutions and decision-making processes. It will identify gender roles and responsibilities; access to and control over household productive assets and resources; household decision-making and risk management strategies; access to and control of program benefits; NCA will ensure that both women and men participate equally in community initiatives and influence the design of projects and other decisions taken around CFW activities in recognition of women and men’s different considerations in accessing or using productive assets. NCA will examine the differences between threats identified by women and those identified by men to further refine gender sensitivity in subsequent NCA activities. Mainstreaming of basic protection principles in all projects will be a paramount importance to NCA. The project will be designed based on a clear analysis of the threats, vulnerabilities and capacities of target populations and ensure that the response prevents and responds to the risks identified. Additionally, as stated above, NCA will continue to prioritize the strengthening of safe measures in line with the ‘Do No Harm’ principles to ensure that interventions do not cause harm to the target population. Inclusion and participation of all relevant stakeholders in all processes will be a key priority. The project will also response to meet minimum SPHERE standards, consult IASC Gender Handbook on humanitarian Action, safeguard participatory approaches and address protection issues. The project will make deliberate effort to maintain a protective environment at community level so that girls and women are not exposed to harm such as sexual and gender-based violence while undertaking their daily chores. Further, gender equity will be observed while implementing all project activities. Men, women, Boys and Girls will be treated equally with their different needs being targeted and support provided accordingly. The project will mainstream messages on HIV and Aids into all aspects of the programme and ensure equal participation of all categories of beneficiaries without discrimination. The involvement of religious leaders, community elders and local authorities, will particularly be crucial, as, in the Somalia context, they are well respected and have the moral authority to see to it that potential sources of conflicts are handled before they escalate. NCA will employ both women and men as staff members, so that they can communicate with right-holders of both sexes thus build local capacities for peace by identifying dividers and connectors and develop strategies for addressing them with a view of addressing potential sources of conflicts.
