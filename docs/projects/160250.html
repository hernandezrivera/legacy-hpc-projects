Project Summary

South Sudan Development Agency – SSUDA is one of the national organizations supporting vulnerable community in hard-to-reach areas in Upper Nile State. Upper Nile being targeted by this project was one of the most affected States with massive displacement of population and loss of livelihoods. The consequences of the conflict have severely caused inaccessibility, market dysfunctional, disruption basic services delivery including humanitarian assistance. Since 2015, SSUDA with support from FAO has been supporting conflict affected and vulnerable communities in Panyikang, Malut, Fashoda and Manyo to rebuild their livelihoods, with support from FAO SSUDA is currently supporting 8,500 food insecure HHs IDPs and returnees in Manyo and Fashoda Counties with assorted quick maturing vegetable seeds, crops, tools and fishing kits. SSUDA is phasing out on a 3 year EU funded PRO-Resilience in 3 counties of Manyo, Fashoda and Malakal.
In line with FSL Cluster objectives and South Sudan Government recently launched National Framework on the Return, Reintegration and Relocation October 2019, SSUDA plans to support to 15,000 HHs in IPC 3 and 4 by providing agricultural and livestock production and productivity leading capacity enhancement to produce sufficient food for household consumption and build their resilience to future eminent shocks. Specifically, the support will be delivered through provision of main crop kits which will be supplemented by vegetable or fishing kits and Provision of essential veterinary supplies (vaccines, drugs and equipment) promoting agriculture extension services through training of community animal health workers on basic livestock health and veterinary services for improved response to livestock diseases and protection of livestock assets to agro-pastoralist communities. This project will target a total of 90,000 individuals desegregated as 46,800 women, 27,000 men including 16,200 youth boys and girls. Through this project, SSUDA is planning to promote establishment of kitchen and school gardens at household level by women and school learners in a number of school as pilot. Strengthening of households farming techniques and storage will be embedded in the project through women groups. Fishery training and support simple fish preservation will be promoted to enhance market-led fishery. On animal’s health SSUDA will conduct vaccination and treatment of livestock (cattle, sheep, goats, chicken) against common diseases with priority given to women and child headed families.
SSUDA will conduct both Onsite and Post Distribution Monitoring to determine the effective and efficiency of this project. Through the project cycle including gender and age segregated assessment, verification, registration, distribution and Post distribution Monitoring (PDMs), SSUDA will ensure that full and active participation and accountability to the affected population is entrenched at all levels. SSUDA will equally ensure that protection, GBV and environment is mainstreamed during implementation of this project. The role of women and youth in food production are recognized fully and therefore SSUDA will engage the two fully in the project implementation.
Under Quick impact project, SSUDA with support from UNMISS established a veterinary livestock clinic in Fashoda, under this project, SSUDA intend to work with FAO and State Ministry of Agriculture to improve the structure and make it fully functional to better serve community, SSUDA will work with FAO to avail cold chains in the facility for keeping vaccines, a total of 30 community animal health workers will be trained on veterinary services to support community and have the veterinary facility fully functional and serving its purpose.

1 - Needs

According to the Integrated Food Security Classification Phase (IPC) of August 2019 report, some 6.3 million (54%) of the population in South Sudan were estimated to be severely food insecure. More than half of the country’s population most of whom are women, children and the elderly struggle to survive and need food assistance and support for asset recovery. Acute malnutrition level among the children exceeds emergency threshold in most parts of South Sudan. The report further estimates that 10,000 people are currently in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5), and facing an extreme lack of food lack of food, while 1.7 million are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and another 4.6 million people are expecting Crisis (IPC Phase 3) levels of acute food insecurity). The Greater Upper Nile where SSUDA operates continues to be the most food insecure, and will need urgent humanitarian support to save their lives. According to the South Sudan Nutrition Cluster analysis, there is an increase in admissions for malnutrition (Severe Acute Malnutrition—SAM, and Moderate Acute Malnutrition — MAM)- Recent Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) indicates that unless humanitarian assistance is provided, about 1.3 million children between 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2020 with Upper Nile amongst the states observing the highest level of acute malnutrition and therefore warrants special focus (WFP South Sudan Situation Report #251, 9 August 2019). In addition, according to Education Cluster (July, 2019 update) Food insecurity is one of the major factors that contributes to low primary school net enrolment rate (NER). Nearly one million school-aged children, majority are in conflict affected areas including Upper Nile (UNICEF South Sudan Report - 2019). According to FAO, the consequences of conflict, compounded by repeated outbreaks of vicious cycle of violent conflict in some parts of conflict affected areas, the deterioration of the food security situation and subsequent economic shocks have negatively affected households’ coping capacities and livelihoods. Communities dependent on agriculture have seen their productive assets depleted, looted and destroyed, limiting their ability to recover and increasing their reliance on external aid. The conflict has also impacted heavily on the youth as rate of unemployment has hit the youth hardest. Livestock plays an essential life-saving role as a mobile asset. However, disease outbreaks will increase in the coming days following the on abnormal floods (South Sudan Metro logical Department Aug, 2019). Displacement and loss of livestock assets results in poor resilience and disease outbreaks contributing to food insecurity and malnutrition among under 5s, lactating and pregnant mothers (poor access to milk and meet proteins), boys, girls and elderly. Women, girls and boys are more at risk to zoonosis due to their roles; boys are much closer to livestock as herders. The capacity of local government in the targeted areas to deliver emergency livestock services is very limited. With support from UNMISS/RRP SSUDA has constructed health Clinic and trained 35 Animal Health Workers in Fashoda. There is a plan to scale up animals’ health service delivery in all target locations. With the signing and implementation of the Revitalized Agreement on Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan, Upper Nile counties and South Sudan in general has reported an influx of returnees in the recent past. The return may also be reinforced by the recent passing of the National Policy Framework on the Return, Reintegration and Relocation of displaced person in South Sudan which is expected to inform a massive voluntary repatriation programmed in the country.

2 - Activities

Inception meeting - The purpose is to inform the targeted population on the project and its objectives and to generate interest. SSUDA will work with the communities to select community support committees that will work closely with SSUDA to effectively deliver the project. The committees will be selected with representation from cross-sections of the communities include people with special needs. In every committee selected, women will be not less than 50% of the members of the committees. Gender and Age segregated assessment – A FSL specific assessment will be conducted in each of the target locations using a cluster-developed assessment tool (questionnaire) for collecting data. The enumerators will use this tool to interview prospective beneficiaries to identify the extent of food security needs among the affected population include women, men and children from IDPs, Returnees and most vulnerable communities. Observation and focus group discussions (FGDs), including interviews of key informants, will also be used. One of the FGDs will be a women-only group with a female facilitator and translator. Project mobilization and sensitization forums – organized to create a platform to share and review action plans and manage high expectations raised by the assessment. Further meeting will within the project period will be the platform for beneficiaries to generate feedbacks necessary to inform project implementation and changes. Identification of beneficiaries – with collaboration with community support committees, (from IDPs, Returnees and Host communities), SSUDA will organize beneficiaries’ identification, verification and registration. SSUDA with the committee will jointly develop and execute a targeting criteria agreed with the community stakeholders to minimize bias and/or conflicts. SSUDA will give priority to women, children and people with special needs. 50% of beneficiaries will be female-headed households, or women to ensure gender equality. Distribution of tools, crop and vegetable kits and fishing kits – SSUDA will acquire the non-supplies through the FSL pipeline (FAO) and transport them to a temporary storage facility by the Logistics Cluster. Volunteers and temporary workers (50% women) will be selected, to work with SSUDA staff and community support committees to deliver supplies to the beneficiaries. Establishment of kitchen and school gardens by women and school children has been proven to be effective with rural women and SSUDA will promote it further. Seed Fair – SSUDA will us the existing farmer field schools to implement seed fair. Community seed fairs provide farmers with the opportunity to exchange knowledge and experiences on the old as well as the new crops they grow and to generate information about local-level seed-production capacities. They also enable the trading, exchange and sharing of propagation material among farmers and the creation of market linkages. Women farmers will be given priority. Animals Health services - In collaboration with State department of livestock, SSUDA will increase livestock diseases surveillance in line with Cluster priority, strengthen preparedness for livestock emergencies through facilitate refresher training of cold chain technicians and Community health workers with priority to women (50%), and support vaccination and treatment of animals. Provision of basic orientation and practical skills on vegetable and fishing production and preservation – support the formation of small groups of about 15 – 25 members such as fish folks, farmer field groups and VSLA. Training women on low cost vegetable cultivation and fishing techniques and strengthening of resilience through market orientated activities. Hygiene in fish preservation will also be promoted and included in the curriculum. Post Distribution Monitoring of crop, vegetable and fishing kits – Post distribution monitoring targeting a percentage of the total beneficiaries per project location.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The beneficiaries rights will be fully respected through the life cycle of this project. SSUDA requires that all staff members to sign a code of conduct alongside their contract. The aim is to set out expectations of how staff members should conduct themselves and clearly stipulate zero tolerance policy towards any form of exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries. SSUDA staff members are expected to stand for the values of the organizations in which all human rights are respected and safeguarded at all time.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

SSUDA is aware and promotes the Kampala Convention and The Guiding Principles on Internal Displacement detail the rights and guarantees relevant to the protection of IDPs from forced displacement to their protection and assistance during displacement up to the achievement of durable solutions. IDPs in South Sudan area experiencing immeasurable difficulties especially the crowded conditions in the PoCs where most have a desire to return to their homes. It is in this basis that SSUDA will make sure that IDPs and returning communities are accorded maxim support through this project.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

This project is linking with the government and community systems. SSUDA is planning to work closely with the government structures among them the country line departments such as County department of Agriculture, fishery and forestry and wildlife.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is in line with the recently approved National Policy Framework on safe and dignified return, reintegration and Relocation of Displaced persons in South Sudan. The project is also supporting the South Sudan Development Plan 2018 of realizing freedom, equality, justice, peace and prosperity for all especially on Aid Strategy that sets out the Government of South Sudan principles and framework for the management of humanitarian development assistance.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project will take into consideration any potential negative impact on the environment when planning the activities. SSUDA is the national focal point for Global Network of Civil for Disaster Risk and the proponents of Climate change adaptability, disaster risk reduction and ecosystem and restoration and management (Integrated Risk Management) which includes promotion of afforestation and use of clean energy. SSUDA will ensure the proper use and management of natural resources include prevention of soil erosion and effective farming techniques and inter-cropping

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

SSUDA has a strategic plan to maintain its presence in the areas including both Eastern and Western Banks of Upper Nile. SSUDA has various funding streams over multiple sectors to further support the most vulnerable communities to cope with any emerging shocks. SSUDA is working to integrate FSL with other projects such as Quick Impact project, Peace building and DRR activities that SSUDA is implementing in the targeted locations. SSUDA’s activities and presence is expected to promote and contribute to peaceful coexistence and enable communities from both sides of the river to engage in food production and access emerging markets once they are functional and reduce overdependence on relief items. At the end of the project activities, SSUDA expects that this project would have improved community resilience and capacity to remain stable in terms of food security.
