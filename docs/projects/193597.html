Project Summary

The daily lives of Palestine refugees living in the West Bank, incl. East Jerusalem, continue to be heavily impacted by the military occupation that has been in place for over 50 years. The resulting protection threats are wide-ranging and severe. Freedom of movement, economic opportunity, security and political rights are heavily curtailed due to the occupation, the pervasive operations and constant presence of Israeli armed forces.
This action is based on UNRWA's protection mandate for more than 890,000 Palestine refugees in the West Bank. Focus is given to the needs of Palestine refugees living in 19 refugee camps who are particularly impacted by the occupation and military incursions. So far in 2022 (by 18 October), the Israeli Security Forces (ISF) has conduct 7739 military operations in the West Bank, which is more than any other year in UNRWA's records, even with more than two months left of the year. 598 of these operations took place in the refugee camps. The action seeks to address the acute needs of the Palestine refugees exposed to conflict-related violence and the risk of displacement and/or forcible transfer.
The project has three key aims:
(1) to enhance the protective environment for Palestine refugees exposed to protection threats. Based on monitoring and documentation of protection incidents, UNRWA will advocate for the protection of Palestine refugees in accordance with International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL) – including persuasion, mobilisation and denunciation – targeting duty bearers as well as international and national interlocutors.
(2) to respond to protection threats linked to the risk of displacement and/or forcible transfer, in addition to military operations and settler violence. UNRWA will mitigate the consequences, improving emergency preparedness, and reinforcing the resilience of affected persons. This is achieved through a combination of shelter support to refugees who have been displaced and/or who have had their homes damaged, and through the provision of Psychological First Aid (PFA) and short-term case management services by dedicated protection social workers. In addition, community-wide initiatives are carried out in affected communities. Shelter support is provided in the form of cash transfers that address immediate needs linked to protection threats. Whilst PFA and case management services provide those affected with the mental health support they need to deal with the trauma, improve access to other UNRWA services and specialised partners, and reinforce the resilience of affected persons. At the same time, community-wide initiatives help improve the emergency preparedness of communities under constant protection threats, and/or coordinate comprehensive response after medium to large-scale events.
(3) to improve the safety and accessibility of complaints, including complaints of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). The project has multiple channels to receive and respond to complaints - including a free hotline - and SOPs are in place to receive and respond to SEA complaints in accordance with the oPt PSEA SOPs on Inter Agency Referrals. Complaints of SEA will be handled by PSEA focal points who will ensure a survivor centered approach in how the complaint is handled and support is offered to the survivor. UNRWA WBFO works closely with the PSEA Network and will raise issues requiring technical support and/or external referral through this forum.

Implementing Partners

The United Nations Relief and Work Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East (UNRWA) has a fundamental humanitarian operational presence in the West Bank, both geographically and in terms of sectors of intervention. Next to the Palestinian Authority, UNRWA is the biggest service provider in the West Bank. In total, the Agency serves 1,111,064 Palestine refugees and other registered persons (UNRWA, Registered Population Dashboard, January 2022), which amounts to one-third of the population in the West
Bank, of whom more than half a million are women and girls. UNRWA was established by the General Assembly in 1949 and is mandated to assist and protect some 5 million registered Palestine refugees in the region. As the main service provider for Palestine refugees in the region, UNRWA delivers primary education, health care, relief and social services, infrastructure and camp improvement, microfinance, and emergency assistance. The Agency has extensive experience providing emergency assistance to Palestine
refugees during humanitarian crises to save lives, preserve human dignity, and prevent and respond to the impacts of such crises. In the West Bank, the Agency's programmes seek to reduce the impact of the protection crisis stemming from over 50 years of the Israeli occupation by addressing the critical needs of the most affected individuals. In terms of response, UNRWA provides critical services to those exposed to protection threats, including psycho-social counselling, medical assistance, cash-based interventions, and the provision of dedicated programming to specific vulnerable groups.

1 - Needs

In 2022, we have witnessed disconcerting development in the protection figures that UNRWA tracks. The number of Palestinian fatalities in the West Bank because of ISF operations or settler violence has reached 103 during the first nine months of 2022, which is higher than any other year-to-date figures since UNRWA records began ten years ago. Of those killed, 37 were refugees, incl. 7 refugee minors. In total, 24 Palestinian minors were killed. Palestine refugees living in the West Bank's 19 refugee camps across the West Bank are facing significantly more protection challenges than those living outside camps. According to UNRWA data, ISF is more likely to use live ammunition during operations in refugee camps: 49% of injuries occurring in refugee camps are caused by ISF use of live ammunition, compared with 13% of injuries in non-camp locations. In general, we see a trend where large-scale and heavy-handed ISF operations in the refugee camps often result in armed confrontations, affecting the populations in the refugee camps and causing UNRWA to suspend health, education, and relief services. Refugee camps in Jenin and Nablus Governorates have been particularly affected in the last period (fall 2022). 5,844 Palestinians have been detained during the first nine months of 2022. These are the highest year-to-date figures in the UNRWA records. 427 of the detained were children, incl. 63 refugee children. At the same time, we are witnessing record levels of settler violence – such as physical attacks against Palestinians, uprooting of trees, and destruction of private property – and these illegal activities are usually carried out with impunity, even with the ISF present. From January to September 2022, UNRWA recorded 1,566 violent settler incidents recorded, resulting in 348 Palestinian injuries. During the first 9 months of 2022, Israeli authorities demolished 683 Palestinian structures, which is the highest figure UNRWA has recorded since 2016. 776 Palestinians were displaced as a result of the demolitions. Nearly half of those displaced this year are children (367). 423 of the displaced are female, incl. 206 girls. 271 – or 35% – of the displaced are Palestine Refugees. The MSNA highlighted situations where the situation for refugee households (HH) is more precarious than non-refugee HHs. Of the HHs reporting to employ crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies, 70% were refugee households (34% of them in camps and 36% out of camps). Last year’s MSNA showed that refugee HHs (6%) were twice as likely to report being at risk of eviction than non-refugee HHs (3%). In 2022, UNRWA provided assistance to more than 1,146 HHs that endured a protection incident related to armed conflict. Damages due to armed conflict have been high this year, with around 70% of assisted HHs found eligible for cash transfers. At the time of writing (October 2022), UNRWA had documented human rights violations involving a total of 58 individuals, including 13 women and 42 men. Indeed, some of these incidents have affected a large number of children in UNRWA schools, but these are not recorded individually. Human rights violations affect the refugee population differently—both in terms of the risk of occurrence and the impact of the violations. UNRWA continuously develops a detailed analysis of the situations of violence in WB, the population directly and indirectly affected and the dynamics and trends of these situations. The protection department has a 24/7 radio room that covers all incidents of political violence related to the conflict and displacement. This information is cross-checked with key informants, and questions are always asked about gender, age, disability of primary and secondary victims. This information is shared with the protection team, which includes a technical team that supports the needs analysis and the specific intervention route for the different vulnerable groups. Thus, the team composed of Gender Protection Officer, Disability Protection Officer and Child Protection Officer are involved in the analysis of each case, in order to ensure that these cases are addressed with all the required complexity. The Research team within protection collects, verifies, and systematizes the information and disaggregates it by gender, age and disabilities, and refugee status. The vulnerability of the target population to protection risks following rights violations and the relative power imbalance between humanitarian workers and refugees requires UNRWA to ensure that safe complaints and feedback channels based on consultations with beneficiaries, are accessible and known to the target population. This includes feedback and complaints on goods and services and more serious complaints related to misconduct and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Consultations undertaken by the PSEA Network for the oPt confirm the need for all organisations to strengthen SEA policies and processes. In 2022, UNRWA trained all Protection Social Workers, on PSEA. Furthermore, MSNA 2022 data found that 8.5% of refugee HHs reporting awareness of how to access and use complaint or reporting mechanisms (compared to only 1.7% of non-refugee HH). Information gaps remain on the needs, preferences, safety, and confidentiality concerns of refugee women and adolescent girls, especially women with disabilities, single-headed HHs, pregnant/lactating women and new mothers, and elderly women. This information will be obtained through surveys, interviews, and focus group discussions UNRWA plans to implement across the West Bank in 2023 to inform and strengthen UNRWA’s SEA channels and processes based on the consultation and participation of affected groups.

2 - Activities

Outcome 1: Enhanced accountability and improved practices of duty-bearers vis-à-vis their obligations under International Human Rights Law and International Humanitarian Law to Palestine refugees in the West Bank. Activity 1.1: Incidents of IHL and IHRL violations in targeted communities concerning excessive use of force, violations against children, and those linked to the risk of forcible transfer are systematically and thoroughly documented. Technical protection specialists in UNRWA will also respond to rights violations that impact individuals with intersecting vulnerabilities or special needs. Activity 1.2: Where consent and protection risk assessments allow, documented IHL and IHRL violations are addressed on a systematic basis with relevant authorities during meetings or through protest letters. Activity 1.3: Donors and other international stakeholders are sensitized to the impact of IHL & IHRL violations on Palestine refugees when relevant and possible. Briefings highlight the different risks and impact of violations on Palestine refugees with intersecting vulnerabilities, with a focus on people with disabilities, the elderly, youth, and women. Activity 1.4: Mobilization activities through IHRS mechanisms, and internal Monitoring, Analysis, and Reporting Arrangements. Attention will be given to the gendered nature of protection threats and assessing the particular needs of vulnerable at-risk groups. Activity 1.5: Public advocacy initiatives on protection issues affecting Palestine refugees in the West Bank. The different risks and impact of military operations on groups based on gender, age, disability, and other intersecting factors includingBedouin / herder communities will be highlighted throughout these activities. Activity 1.6: Protection staff will monitor the protection risks and needs of different populations to ensure that all UNRWA programs in the delivery of their services respect and uphold the humanitarian principles, and also to mainstream protection principles into overall service delivery. This includes Integrated Assessments of UNRWA installations throughout the West Bank, incl East Jerusalem. Outcome 2: Refugee households affected by protection threats are better able to copeActivity 2.1: Refugee households affected by forcible displacement, military operations, and settler violence are identified and assessed by Protection Social Workers. The assessment utilizes a cash eligibility tool that focuses on vulnerability and factors that are likely to decrease beneficiaries’ ability to recover from the protection incident without external assistance. This includes the head of household being elderly, a child, or a female, having a disability in the family, or having a large number of dependents, among other factors. Activity 2.2: Services are provided to affected Palestine refugees, including Psychological First Aid (PFA), short-term case management services, cash assistance, and/or referral to internal and external services providers. Whilst PFA is provided to all affected households, other services are offered according to identified needs and eligibility. Activity 2.3: Families under continuous protection threats are provided with cash assistance for the implementation of specific preventative measures to reduce the scale or/and the prospect of attack/s.Activity 2.4: Cash assistance and case management services are monitored regularly through meetings and field visits. This will be done through routine household visits, monitoring visits, and meetings with staff. In addition, UNRWA conducts a closure survey with each HH to assess the effectiveness and impact of the action, in addition to a satisfaction survey that measures the household’s satisfaction. Activity 2.5: Community-wide initiatives are carried out in communities facing continuous protection threats and/or following a medium to large-scale protection incident. The initiatives may include but are not limited to, emergency preparedness training, mental health activities, and workshops tailored specifically to community needs. Outcome 3: Safe, accessible, and child-friendly mechanisms are in place for reporting complaints and feedback, including complaints of SEA, particularly in high-risk areas. Activity 3.1: Consultations are held with target groups, including vulnerable populations (women and girls, including women with disabilities, single-headed households, and elderly women) to inform the feedback and complaint channels for SEA. This includes a survey, interviews, and focus-group discussions identifying reporting barriers and mitigation measures. Activity 3.2: This project has a built-in clear process for receipt and referral of complaints of PSEA in accordance with CBCM SOPs and internal SOPs on Case Management which supports frontline staff to safely identify and refer protection cases within the agency. Beneficiaries will be informed of the available hotlines and their ability to report SEA complaints through it. Complaints of SEA will be handled by PSEA focal points who will ensure a survivor-centered approach in how the complaint is handled and support is offered to the survivor. Activity 3.3 Age and gender-sensitive PSEA awareness-raising materials are disseminated to all project sites containing information on rights, safe complaint channels, and how to access survivor-centered assistance. Activity 3.4 All project staff who have not received PSEA training, including those operating complaint channels and feedback reporting mechanisms, will complete at least one PSEA training during the project period, to strengthen SEA safeguards for reporting SEA and referrals. Activity 3.5 Project staff will indirectly engage in the HCT oPt PSEA Network, through the active participation of UNRWA PSEA focal points and senior management, including contribution to the development of interagency work plans and SOPs.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Feedback and complaints relating to specific cases is followed up according to internal SOPs used by project staff to adapt services, interventions and referrals for support services to meet the needs of families and individuals. Feedback obtained through consultations on SEA complaints channels will alsoinform improvements to accessibility for vulnerable populations.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project adopts a do no harm, conflict sensitivity approach, including specific measures to ensure that identified risks are addressed within the action,and unintended negative consequences are minimized. UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. UNRWA’s Case Management SOPs ensure that protectioncases are identified and referred to specialized support services. SOPs cover compulsory reporting on SEA and support to survivors. UNRWA has existing guidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in this project. In UNRWA's Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritization of safety and dignity is explicitly address as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneficiaries, communities or staff, including risk mitigation for SEA. On a biannual basis, all of UNRWA's activities are subject to a protection-mainstreamingaudit, which assesses the integration of protection principles in UNRWA's work. This audit included extensive interviews and focus group discussions with beneficiaries, and sought to assess UNRWA's application of the four main protection principles, including the principle of Do No Harm. In 2019 UNRWA developed a new information management system for the collection of sensitive protection monitoring and documentation data; specifically the system now separates out biographical data from incident related data, with individual cases being only referred to through generated case code. For the purposes of ensuring that briefingsinvolving affected persons, are carried out in a way that prioritizes the safety and dignity of these persons, UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. This includes a checklist to guide all staff working in this capacity. UNRWA utilizes proxy means testing formula to determine the poverty level of the household, which considers not only the consumption levels but also other aspects of vulnerability, suchas the presence of chronic diseases, housing characteristics and accessibility to utilities, as well as household composition (e.g.the number of elderly and persons with disabilities,and female-headed households). UNRWA ensures that vulnerable individuals can access assistance by external referrals. UNRWA has developed dedicated case management SOPs and internal/external referral pathways, to ensure that the comprehensive needsare met. In various areas of operations, UNRWA engages with a range of community representatives, village councils, camp resident committees – as well as holding focus group discussions including for women, children, youthand the elderly in target communities. UNRWA has adapted its case management in response to difficulty of physical access to concernedindividuals/households.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA is dedicated to the application of the humanitarian principles in all of its service delivery. Specific measures are taken to ensure that UNRWA's services are neutral and impartial in nature, including through regular monitoring throughout its operations. For the purposes of this project, UNRWA has developed, in coordination with the West Bank Protection Consortium, a dedicated vulnerability assessment tool in order to prioritise assistance to the most vulnerable refugees. This tool was pioneered by UNRWA and has since been adopted by other members of the Consortium and is to be applied throughout the West Bank. UNRWA takes measures to ensure that its assistance under this project is available to all persons, and takes dedicated action to ensure that individuals with specific needs are reached. UNRWA takes actions to ensure that persons with disability, with movement and access restrictions, the elderly, women and children are targeted during outreach and communication - and also are key stakeholders in all community interactions and in accountability processes. Services delivered are tailored to the needs of individuals, including for those with specific needs, the case management services delivered are developed in coordination with the affected persons and are guided by their specific needs. Specific needs according to age, gender and disability are identified at the initialassessment stage of case management, for those affected by damage to residential property, and those exposed to displacement. The individual case plans are tailored to respond to these needs, for example provision of services linked to mobility issues amongst the elderly. In terms of the work in advocacy UNRWA ensures that in its documentation and monitoring dynamics including gender, age and disability are factored into needs analysis, trends analysis and UNRWA conducts a number of thematic briefings per year to focus on the specific needs of vulnerable groups. UNRWA has developed dedicated field briefings on disability, gender and GBV, children and the elderly for this purpose. UNRWA has also developed a remote documentation procedure and guidance, conducting documentation/ verification of incidents remotely, including over the telephone, using additional sources and community contacts. This approach is coordinated with OHCHR and other actors engaged in documentation and monitoring. UNRWA expanded its engagement with 22 vulnerable at-risk refugee communities ensuring continuous community monitoring and coordination within UNRWA of needs, concerns and proposed responses through telephone contact with key community stakeholders. UNRWA has demonstrated an ability to readjust its operations to effectively face the operational and movement restriction-relatedchallenges arising from the COVID-19 emergency. For example, new guidelines for staff on remote investigations of serious human rights violation have been created. Further, UNRWA has implemented alternative transfer modalities, using electronic wire transfer to beneficiaries’ bank accounts and cash transfers or checks for those who don't have bank accounts in response to access restrictions due to the COVID-19 emergency. CIU assessment tools is designed to capture specific needs by assessing the needs of all HH members. CIU cash eligibility tool prioritizes those with special vulnerabilities. Any feedback on the assessment is received and follow up through the various complaint mechanisms outlined. UNRWA has added direct cash payment as a payment modality where beneficiaries from vulnerable groups, such as PwD and elderly persons, receive cash without a need for a formal bank account. Based on beneficiary feedback from home interviews, direct cash payments are preferred as a transfer modality
