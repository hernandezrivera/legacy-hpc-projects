Project Summary

The Proposed intervention aims to improve access to safe and sustainable water, sanitation facilities and promote good hygiene practices for 35000 men, women, boys and girls in IDPs living in Camps and camp-like settings and their host communities as well as under-served and hard-to-reach rural areas.
WATER- Lack of water constitutes a major challenge for harmonious living among the pastoralist and agro-pastoralist, IDPs and Host communities and always makes the source of conflict. Lack of adequate clean water remain to be the major indicator of environmental health conditions such as AWD/Cholera that claims many lives and particularly children under the five years of age.
Most IDPs often settled in areas with no access to water or far away from the main source of water and they lack both communal and household water storage facility. This programme will enhance water storage capacity through provision of cluster approved jerry cans for household water storage and chlorination at the source.
Most water sources have been depleted and access to clean water is not a guarantee in most parts of Southern Somalia. To address this, the proposed intervention aims to construct 3 solar powered/hybrid strategic Shallow wells with elevated water tanks in Kismayo and Afmadow districts to provide clean and sustainable water. In consultations with the relevant stakeholders including PWD, this project will initiate a piping system from the main water sources to the identify sites where the IDP’s and their families are living. Shared water kiosks will be constructed and will be managed by trained WES committee who will report to the management of the main boreholes and M.O.U will be signed.
The project will also ensure that water delivered for use by the IDPs is treated at the household level through distribution of Aqua tabs. Provisions of soaps will be done to help hygiene promotion and improve the health status of the IDPs and their host families since most conflict between IDPs and Host communities often arises from lack of reciprocate support to host communities.
The project will target 2 strategic boreholes and 20 shallow wells for rehabilitation, protection and chlorination work in Lower Juba. Rehabilitation will focus on: generator repairs, replacement of pumps, pump house repairs, provision of borehole consumables and chlorination for boreholes and for shallow wells, rehabilitation will focus on provision of apron/well head cover, hand pump installations and chlorination.
SANITATION: In consultations with the diverse local community members including those affected by disability, appropriate measures to address the devastating sanitation conditions in the area will be drawn to reverse the menace. The project envisages to design and construct gender sensitive and culturally appropriate 120 emergency VIP latrines with hand washing facilities in the agreed sites to eliminate the tradition of open defection and promote better sanitation practices by the target population.
HYGIENE: The combination of poor sanitation conditions, a shortage of safe water, overcrowding and high malnutrition rates, creates the perfect combination for infectious diseases, such as cholera/AWD to spread among the IDPs. The proposed intervention will promote good hygiene practices through dissemination of hygiene messages both at IDP settlements, nutrition centres and health facilities. Hygiene promoters will work closely with mobile health and nutrition teams in camps and nutrition/Health facilities to ensure key hygiene messages are disseminated at nutrition centres and Hygiene Kits distributed.
30 hygiene promoters (dis-agregated by sex) will be trained on basic hygiene practices, proper handling of domestic water, personal health and environmental care among other related issues in PHAST. Hygiene kit composed of 4000 bar soap (800g), 4000 jerry cans of 20L, 4000 stripes of Aqua tabs (10 tablets per stripe), domestic bucket and Face mask will be distributed.

Project Activities

1. Construction of 3 solar powered/hybrid strategic Shallow wells with elevated water tanks in Kismayo and Afmadow districts to reach over 15000 persons (Men, women, boy and girls) including persons living with disability directly with clean and sustainable water. This will include a piping system from the main water sources to the identified sites where the IDP’s and Host families are living. Shared water kiosks will be constructed and will be managed by trained WES committee who will report to the management of the hybrid well. This will enable vulnerable communities to access safe water continuously during and after the emergency period.2. The response will also target 2 strategic boreholes and 20 shallow wells for rehabilitation, protection and chlorination work in Lower Juba. Rehabilitation will focus on: generator repairs, replacement of pumps, pump house repairs, provision of borehole consumables and chlorination for boreholes and for shallow wells, rehabilitation will focus on provision of apron/well head cover, hand pump installations and chlorination. SFH will establish raised platforms and ramps at water points to ensure safe manual lifting to all, especially children, pregnant women, and persons with disabilities3. Design and construct gender sensitive and culturally appropriate 120 emergency VIP latrines with hand washing facilities to eliminate open defection, disposal of child faeces and promote better sanitation practices by the target population. SFH will ensure separate toilet and bathing facilities for males and females. The facilities will be clearly marked in pictorial form for illiterate users to ensure they are used by the indicated sex. The latrines and the bathing facilities will have inside lockers as a protection concern. To ensure access of latrines by persons with disabilities we will install handlebars in the latrines and fit ramps and make the access routes smooths for wheel chairs. 4. Train 30 hygiene promoters (Men and women) on basic hygiene practices, proper handling of domestic water, personal health and environmental care among other related issues in PHAST and COVID-19 risk reduction.5. Hygiene kit composed of 4000 bar soap (800g), 4000 jerry cans of 20L, 4000 stripes of Aqua tabs (10 tablets per stripe), domestic bucket and Face mask will be distributed. To address the gender specific needs-sanitary materials will be part of the hygiene kits as per the WASH cluster standards.6. Training of 15 Gender dis-aggregated (Men and Women) and disability sensitive water Management Committee members on management of water resources and particularly operation and maintenance. Among the 15, 8 key representatives including PDW will be equipped with tools for effective management. Through the project management phases, the specific needs of the target beneficiaries will be taken into account including persons living disability. The project staff will have equal gender representation to ensure enough women are available within the project implementation as a contact point for women in the community. In the procurement and distribution of hygiene kits specific hygiene needs of women, girls and older men are taken into consideration. To ensure those with impaired vision and hearing receive WASH messages, both oral and written modes of communication will be used. Child friendly messages such as drawing and cartoons will be used to reach both boys and girls with messages.SFH has established AAP committees in most of our operation areas to ensure inclusive, transparent and accountable assistance is delivered. The AAP committees composed of representatives of all community members including Men, women, youth, the elderly, PWD and the local governance. The AAP committees will be involved in the sensitization and mobilization prior to execution of any humanitarian assistance and their participation will ensure inclusive and equitable service delivery.

Mitigation Measures

A) Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. Mitigation measures:- 1. SFH will Locate the shallow wells and latrines in visible and central locations that is not far from the community and IDP settlements and by extension liaise with CCCM/Shelter cluster and the particular Camp management agency to consider lighting systems installed along the routes and near the water points.2. SFH will Identify central and accessible locations for distribution of hygiene kits to avoid long travel by the beneficiaries to access the distribution points.3. Hygiene distributions will take place during the day to able beneficiaries’ sufficient time to travel home before nightfall.B) Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Mitigation measures:- 1) SFH will consult with different representatives of the community (women, men, boys, girls), persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific Hygiene kits requirements and preferences and prioritize needs based on there needs.2) Provide disability sensitive ramps near WASH facilities to increase access to people affected by disabilities.3) SFH will ensure access paths are smooth and accessible for wheelchair access to allow the disable persons affect hygiene kit distribution point and prevent discrimination. C) Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. 1) To enhance privacy put locks on the doors to latrines and bathing houses to ensure they are lockable from inside.2) Ensure equal proportion of representation of women and men in water management committees, registration and distribution teams to ensure specific needs of the women are catered.3) SFH staff and contractors are required to sign PSEA policy and Child protection code of conducts.
