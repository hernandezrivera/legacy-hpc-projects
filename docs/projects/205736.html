Project Summary

Twelve years after gaining independence, people in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions, driven by the cumulative and compounding effects of years of conflicts, sub-national violence, food insecurity, the climate crisis, public health challenges and poor governance (June 2023 OCHA South Sudan Humanitarian Snapshot). The county of Maiwut in Upper Nile state displays some of the lowest standards in WASH parameters according to the most recent WASH cluster severity mapping.
Gains in service provision over the years have been eroded by adverse weather effects resulting in prolonged flooding, leading to the destruction of WASH infrastructure, displacement of communities, deprivation of markets, and access to most parts of the states in focus. Flooding has further increased WASH-related mortality and morbidity affecting mainly under 5, pregnant and lactating women, persons with special needs as well as persons living with HIV/TB. Lack of adequate WASH services directly affects the well-being of persons and communities, contributing to health, nutrition, food security, education, and protection indicators within affected communities.
Universal Network for Child Defence Rights (UNCDR) works in Maiwut County in Upper Nile State and plans a robust WASH response aimed at improving community well-being while integrating and working closely with other thematic areas.
The project will focus on fostering community-driven WASH responses to triggers. The project will further seek to enhance the reduction of WASH-related morbidity and mortality, through the provision of adequate, safe, and clean water for both domestic and livelihood purposes, mitigating WASH-related GBV and preventing livelihood deprivation associated with inadequate WASH standards.
The proposed WASH in emergency project for the year 2024 will reach out to 11,111 beneficiaries in the county of Maiwut in Upper Nile state, 50% of these females, 35% of males and 15% of persons with special needs through both static and mobile approaches, working to ensure value for money with $500,000 fund support if granted, at a cost per beneficiary $45.
UNCDR further will roll out an integrated response modality, seeking to implement jointly WASH, Health, Nutrition, FSL, Education, and Protection projects in the aforementioned county. UNCDR has existing nutrition, health, GBV and CP projects already running with support from UNICEF, WFP, UNFPA & IOM. All activities throughout the project will be shared with stakeholders and beneficiaries through an existing mechanism for transparency. As required UNCDR will ensure accountability of resources as well as proper Monitoring and evaluation of activities and reports in Maiwut County is well implemented.

1. Needs

South Sudan has some of the worst health indicators in the world, and the majority of the population lacks access to essential health services, safe water, and sanitation. In addition, the country ranks seventh in the world for share of population exposed to river floods. One in two South Sudanese live in areas exposed to moderate flood hazards; lack of access to safe drinking water supply and sanitation is also a core concern: more than 60 percent of the population use unimproved sources and 75 percent practice open defecation. Women and girls tend to be disproportionately impacted by these water-related threats. In Greater Upper Nile in particular, the protracted humanitarian situation is persisting due to the effects of conflict and current flood challenges, access, service delivery, and adverse weather conditions. Communities in the location of Maiwut County in Upper Nile State continually experience unacceptable living conditions due to poor access to clean and safe water, sanitation, and hygiene services; Greater Upper Nile has the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3, 4) or worse acute food insecurity, with over 1.25 million people food insecure. With joint populations exceeding 1,427,399, more than 50% of these communities fall under IPC 3 and 4 (October 2022 to July 2023 – IPC Phase). Poor WASH conditions contribute to high levels of disease, malnutrition, and food insecurity affecting mostly under 5s, pregnant and lactating women, the elderly, and person with disabilities (PWDs). And high GAM rates are linked to poor access to safe and clean water, and sanitation services hence leading to high cases of water-related diseases. GAM prevalence exceeded the WHO emergency threshold in Greater Upper Nile. REACH, Cluster Severity surveys indicate that over 78% of the communities in the locations lack access to a secure source of water, with over 86% using surface water for both domestic and personal use. Open water sources are most times contaminated due to poor fecal disposal as a result of a lack of latrines. Latrine usage is below 20% in the locations, with the majority of those with no access to safe and secure latrines being women and girls. Latrine HH surveys indicate low latrine coverage, especially in Greater Upper Nile. Over 80% of the communities reported practicing open defecation. The insufficiency of water points has further been worsened by an increased concentration of IDPs in remote secure locations, an increase in returnees due to stability, breakdown due to over usage, and lack of access by humanitarian actors to carry out routine rehabilitation and repairs. An analysis of the Food Consumption Score (FCS) indicates gaps in dietary intakes among HHs, with low dietary diversification, coupled with grim figures on the household hunger scale. Poor WASH standards continue to contribute to high levels of malnutrition in Upper Nile State – 21.5% GAM; leading to increased incidences of child mortality. Torrential rains in the latter part of 2021, leading to sustained floods have made access to water points difficult and contributed to the destruction of latrines and bathing shelters. OCHA reports indicate over 1,000,000 people are affected by floods, exposing entire villages to water-related diseases. Protection issues are closely linked to access to basic needs such as water, sanitation, and hygiene. Women bear the brunt of collecting water, the majority from distant water points thereby exposing them to gender-based violence and SEA, with girls having less time for school and other livelihood activities. More time spent at water points reduces opportunities for women and girls to access other services. Women and girls lack access to dignity and hygiene kits, resulting in protection issues.

2. Project activities

In line with the WASH Cluster Objectives Integrate WASH response with Nutrition/(Food Security) through; WASH service provision in nutrition facilities (WASH NFI kits and hygiene promotion to SAM children) and access to safe water to facilities in IPC 4/5 locations and provision of sustained water to communities showing high GAM prevalence and poor access to water. This will be mainly through WASH integrated intervention starting at OTP/SC targeting the couple “mother/care-taker – severely malnourished child” until HH level aiming at breaking WASH-related diseases transmission cycle aimed at improving access to safe water and promoting safe hygiene practices in communities showing a high prevalence of GAM. 1. Provide timely rehabilitation of 30 boreholes and upgrading of 6 motorized water yards with a high production yield of 11,111 (women 3,100, girls 4,000, boys 2,511, and men 1,500) 2. Train on maintenance of sustainable water points WASHCOM and mechanized water yards 200 (Men 100, Women 100) 3. Train 150 (women 75 and men 75) WASH Staff on protection and gender guidelines. 4. Construction of 100 gender-appropriate and secured sanitation facilities (men 1,000, Women 1,500, Girls 2,000 and boys 2,500 respectively). 5. Conduct community consultation meetings that target leaders, and representatives of stakeholder groups targeting all the project sites to introduce the project to the beneficiaries (men 500, women 1,000, girls 600, and boys 400). 6. Provide key hygiene promotion messages/sessions to (Men 1,500, women 3,000, girls 5,500 and boys 4,500) that include in community, health facilities, schools, OTPs/SC, marketplaces, schools, and other public gatherings Facilitate gender risk analysis through focus groups discussion with women and girls by female WASH Staff (Women 1,500 and girls 2,300). 7. Conduct focus group discussion on safe sanitation and hygiene facilities access in a secure location agreed upon after a GBV risk analysis for women 1,000 and girls 2,000 respectively. 8. Provide hygiene WASH hygiene kits to children admitted for SAM/MAM treated and discharged from functional OTPs/SC (Girls 2,100 and boys 1,400). 9. Provide safe water to children admitted for SAM/MAM through the construction of hand-dug or drilling of new boreholes and repair of the existing water points at the OTPs/SC facilities (Girls 1,500 and 1,000 boys) 10. Train nutrition, health, protection, and gender volunteers on WASH-related topics (Men 100 and men 110) provide a point-of-use household water treatment to the vulnerable population with safe drinking /chlorinated Water (Men 1,600, women 2,200, girls 1,800 and boys 1,700). 11. Provide vulnerable populations with soaps to functional handwashing facilities to reduce WASH-related diseases such as cholera and AWD with active chlorine 0.05% (women 1,400, men 900, girls 1,100 and boys 1,200). 12. Provide vulnerable populations with a WASH minimum package of activities adapted to their vulnerabilities (men 400, women 1,360, girls 1,200 & boys 1,500). 13. Provision of disability inclusive sanitation facility designed.
