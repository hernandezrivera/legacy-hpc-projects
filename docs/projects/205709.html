Project Summary

The high levels of acute food insecurity in South Sudan is worrisome with 7.10 million people expected to experience acute food insecurity in 2024. The most severely acute food insecure populations remain significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation and high food prices), conflict and insecurity – including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan – causing forced displacement, low agricultural production and a reduction in humanitarian assistance (South Sudan: IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis September 2023 - July 2024).
The prolonged history of food insecurity, due to isolation, inequity, conflicts and climate shocks in Awerial and Yirol West Counties of Lakes State and Bor South County in Jonglei has resulted to severe food shortages. Of the estimated 35,000 people facing catastrophic food insecurity in South Sudan from September 2023 to July 2024 (IPC phase 5), 3,000 people are from Duk and 3,000 people from Nyirol Counties of Jonglei.
To contribute to FSL CO 1 (humanitarian food assistance): Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5 and IPC 4), CINA will reach individuals from the most vulnerable food insecure households with unconditional food assistance in IPC4+ locations not reached by WFP (through cash response) and in-kind food assistance (including GFD: blanket/ seasonal/ targeted and GFD plus); More hybrid rations in-kind plus cash component; Cash & voucher-based transfers with food security/ nutrition objectives; Food, fishing & quick maturing vegetable seeds through IRRM; Conditional cash transfers (social skills & safety nets) through investments in assets, training, or communal work groups/ planning committees etc.; and Conditional food assistance through food for assets.
To contribute to FSL CO 2 and 3 (livelihood and resilience): CINA will enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support; Livestock treatment and vaccination, livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers and community-based extension service providers (CBESP); Training and inputs support; Good Agronomic practices; Integrated Pest Management; Fisheries Training; Provision of water pumps; Irrigation training/support; Ox Ploughs & training; PHL Storage granaries; Provision of Canoe, Fishing, Boating materials/tools; Procurement from local traders through seed fairs and Produce/products aggregation; Village Savings/Loans Associations; Processing Equipment for value addition; Training on value addition, Vocational & other enterprise/business related support; Provision of hives for bee-keeping livelihood groups; Install or strengthen community based EWS by providing required equipment; Support downscaling of climate information-flood early warning information through radio programme; Pre-position grain and seed protection kits; Conduct assessment and review existing early action protocol.
CINA has FSL projects running in Lakes into 2024 and will align this project with FSL Cluster HRP 2024 strategy, by following the guidelines on Harmonization of Food Ration, Intervention Modality, Target Complementarities, Conditionality of food assistance and Response Strategy for returnees from Sudan as well as working with HDP actors to ensure sustainability and localization of food aid. The project will be coordinated with other sectors (Nutrition, WASH, Education) to strengthen synergies and will integrate protection dimensions by caring out FSL activities that contribute to safety, dignity, and integrity of at-risk individuals/groups and do not increase the protection risks faced by the crisis-affected populations.

1. Needs

About 7.10 million people in South Sudan will face acute insecurity in 2024 (IPC Sept 2023 to Jul 2024). South Sudan has one of the worst crisis context globally. The youngest East African nation ranks 194th of the 194 countries in the Human Development Index in 2023 with 4 out of 5 people living under international poverty line. The GDP per capita was estimated to be US$104.8 by the end of 2023. About 99 per 1000 live births of under 5 children die. More than 60% of roads become impassable during rainy season. In these situations, 54% of the people affected are children, 52% are female and 15% are persons with disability. (South Sudan HNO 2023). Recurrent flooding in Fangak and Pigi/Canal Ayod and Bor South Counties of Jonglei has led to widespread displacement of people and livestock and damaged crops and shelter. This has resulted to severe food shortages. In particular, a large proportion of the population in Fangak and Pigi/Canal will face ‘’famine-like’’ conditions moving into 2024. An estimated 35,000 people are facing catastrophic food insecurity in South Sudan from Sept 2023 to July 2024 (IPC phase 5). Between July 2023 and June 2024, an estimated 1.65 million children between 6-59 months and 870,000 pregnant or breastfeeding women are expected to suffer acute malnutrition. A SMART Nutrition Survey in Fangak also showed a GAM rate of 17.5% and SAM of 3.3% (based on weight for height and presence of bilateral edema) . In addition to flooding, sub-national violence in Fangak and Pigi between armed opposition actors and the government forces have led to loss of lives and livelihoods, recurring displacement and destruction of humanitarian supplies. Farmers missed the planting and/or cropping season because of violent conflicts and physical insecurity which forced families to flee their homes and abandon their farmlands. IRNA and Protection Cluster reports from Pigi in April 2023 show that IDPs rely on fishing and foraging for survival, often walking several kilometers in search of food. Some men burn charcoal for income, but face transportation challenges as charcoal can only be sold in Canal or Malakal. Markets in Old Fangak and Pigi/Canal lack essential food commodities, including the wild food (water lilies) and the affected people will likely depend entirely on humanitarian food assistance. IPC projections put Awerial and Yirol West Counties in Lakes State at Crisis food insecurity level (IPC level 3) as from Sept to Nov 2023 due to persistently deteriorating food security situation . Lakes in particular experiences annual flooding during the rainy season which makes agriculture difficult and reduces access to markets. The community therefore, relies on cattle herding and fishing during most of the lean season. A SMART Nutrition Survey in Awerial and Yirol West also revealed a GAM rate of 20.8% and SAM of 3.1% (based on weight for height) . The multiple crises will underpin the limited household's capacity to absorb short term shocks, with femaleheaded households mainly at greatest risk. Food insecurity is expected to persist by the lean season of 2024 in these counties due to recurrent emergencies (flooding and conflict), low production, lost of household incomes, depleted productive assets and fragile nutrition among populations. It is worth noting that if immediate food assistance and close monitoring is not prioritized, there will be limited food consumption due to lack of household food stocks. This means that households will be sourcing food from markets in possible scenarios, but increased debt levels and limited incomes mean that some households will need to sell some assets. Therefore, CINA will require USD 265,500 in 2024 to implement activities that will provide access to lifesaving, resilience and reduction of food insecurity and initiate activities that will cushion communities in time of disaster and help to reduce seasonal starvation and hunger among the lactating mothers and children.

Unconditional food assistance in IPC4+ locations not reached by WFP (through cash response) and in-kind food assistance (including GFD: blanket/ seasonal/ targeted and GFD plus); 2. More hybrid rations in-kind plus cash component; Cash & voucher-based transfers with food security/ nutrition objectives; 3. Food, fishing & quick maturing vegetable seeds; 4. Conditional food assistance through food for assets. 6. Complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support. C7. Distribution of agriculture seeds & tools, fishing, vegetable & crop kits; 8. Nutrition vouchers (plus poultry & goat distribution and Seed fairs; 9. Emergency food & livelihood support through distribution of fishing kits/ vegetable kits, rapid response kits (fish kit/ cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed); 10. Livestock treatment and vaccination, livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers and community-based extension service providers (CBESP); 11. Training and inputs support; 12. Good Agronomic practices (e.g. farmer field schools, Demo plots, post-harvest management etc); 13. Integrated Pest Management (e.g. FAW, Desert locusts, Pesticides etc); 14. Fisheries Training; 15. Provision of water pumps; 16. Irrigation training/support; 17. Ox Ploughs & training; 18. Establish PHL Storage granaries (using locally available resources as much as possible); 19. Provision of Canoe, Fishing, Boating materials/tools; 20. Procurement from local traders (Agro traders, Market groups etc) through seed fair; Produce/products aggregation;21. Establish and support Village Savings/Loans Associations;22. Processing Equipment for value addition (fish, meat, vegetables, fruit, milk preservation);23. Training on value addition (fish, meat, vegetables, fruit, milk preservation, honey);24. Vocational & other enterprise/business related support;25. Provision of hives for bee-keeping livelihood groups;26. Install or strengthen community based EWS by providing require equipment (rain gauge, disaster prediction information tools, etc.);27. Support downscaling of climate information-flood early warning information through radio programme (Radio show, solar radios etc.);28. Pre-position grain and seed protection kits (hermetic bags, tilted buckets, grain bag etc.);29. Provide flood adaptive seeds and approaches (rice, floating gardens), tools and fishing inputs to farmers;30. Conduct assessment and review existing early action protocol (EAP); and31. Provision of Fuel efficient stoves.
