Project Summary

From May 2017 to June 2017 Netherland Red Cross (NLRC) & Haitian Red Cross (HRC), through UNICEF funding, supported the DINEPA (Water Agency) in the South Department in relation to WASH activities. This department is a priority based on "Inter-Sector Severity Needs" from the Humanitarian Response Plan.
A rapid response to ensure potable water provision in the aftermath of disasters is essential. Just after hurricane Matthew hit the South Department in October 2016, the big water systems were damaged and the affected population used unsafe sources of water for human consumption. In a coordinated effort by the humanitarian organizations, the population was first offered potable drinking water by means of water trucking and hand pumps. In the meantime the big water supply systems were being repaired.
This project will improve access to water in appropriate quantity and quality in the aftermath of a new disaster, support hygiene promotion and sanitation, while linking Relief, Rehabilitation and Development.
There are multiple SAEP-reservoirs and also reliable boreholes in the department, some of which could be accessible by trucks. We propose to make an inventory and map these reservoirs and boreholes, select the most relevant ones in coordination with DINEPA and made them accessible for trucks from Gos and NGOs. The open water source located in Labaye is of a particular interest because of the important amount of water available. Here we suggest to do a borehole where a mobile submersible pump is to be installed and run by a mobile diesel generator or with solar energy. A flat area will be prepared in the nearby hill to place bladders that will enable water treatment and rapid refill of water trucking downstream by gravity. Contingency equipment like pumps, generators and bladders will be stored in a safe location, managed by trained HRC staff and used in emergencies or during emergency trainings. The population benefiting from this services is estimated from 50.000 to 100.000 people (pumping test to determine the maximum discharge and ideal exploitation level). In addition at least two strategically located schools and/or health facilities will be brought up to standard to be used as emergency shelters. This will include, among others, water storage, supply, latrines and handwashing facilities.
The HRC & NLRC propose to continue the rehabilitation of Drinking Water Supply Systems (SAEP) based on joint assessments with DINEPA. Indeed basic rehabilitation such as replacing galvanized pipes by polyethylene pipes, retrofitting of infrastructure in vulnerable areas could protect those systems and insure their well-functioning after a disaster. We will be involved on the Cholera-WASH response in areas highly affected by cholera or AWD by implementing urban or rural WASH activities such as chlorination of existing water points, support of DINEPA in the systemic chlorination of SAEP, construction of sanitary blocs around markets and public places, latrine emergency desludging. To contribute to groundwater renewal, reforestation activities around the water springs will be implemented in parallel with awareness raising activities with communities. In order to strengthen governance of water resources, we will support DINEPA in the creation and capacity building of the Water Committees (CAEPAs) and/or Economic Operators.
The strength of HRC is it number of volunteers, more than 200 in Departement Sud, and reinforcing its capacity is crucial. Training of trainers will be implemented on EDAB and MIRA methodologies, Pre Crisis Market Analysis (PCMA) and preparation to cash transfer activities. Volunteers will receive training on hygiene promotion and be trained on water quality testing, bucket chlorination and water treatment at household level so they can provide sensitization to the communities. Indeed good practices on hygiene and sanitation are key to reduce waterborne diseases and contribute fighting malnutrition

Needs

The Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Haiti focused on four Strategic Objectives (SO):- Strategic Objective 1: Immediate assistance to people living in severe food insecurity- Strategic Objective 2: Save lives from epidemics.- Strategic Objective 3: Protection and integration of returnees, and IDPs of 2010 - Strategic Objective 4: Respond to unmet urgent needs from recent hurricanes, and disaster preparedness Our WASH response strategy incorporates 3 axes relates the Strategic Objectives: 1- Ensure access to safe water and sanitation with hygiene promotion in areas highly affected by cholera or other acute diarrheas – relates to SO22- Ensure access to safe water and sanitation with hygiene promotion in highly food insecure areas affected by severe acute malnutrition – relates to SO13- Ensure undisrupted access to safe water, hygiene items and sanitation to populations in disaster prone areas and increase their resilience to withstand water stress and shocks – relates to SO4 The office of NLRC/HRC is located in the town of Les Cayes but there are HRC committees in all the communes of the Departement Sud allowing us to intervene in all the communes thanks to the potential mobilization of more than 200 volunteers. The contingency plan after Hurricane Matthew, showed that there are gaps that need to be covered in order to provide a rapid and efficient response. Among them access to safe drinking water in the area remains a challenge especially during emergencies.Water trucking remains an interesting option in many cases but there is currently a lack of proper infrastructure where trucks could get easy access to water source to fill their tanks. One of the activity proposed is to make some of those water sources accessible for trucks from different GOs and NGOs. One of those places could serve as well as a training place for different institutions during the preparation phase. A proper mapping of those water-sources and boreholes with the water and water-sources characteristics will be developed and shared to DINEPA, Gos and NGOs. Often the public buildings used as emergency shelter are health centers, churches, schools, community halls. However, the current state of those buildings especially schools are unfit to serve as such. Hence, an insufficient amount of evacuation shelters are available especially in rural areas. With some small investment, schools can be made suitable for shelter. Shelter plays an essential role in reducing vulnerability and building communities’ resilience. The NLRC has long experience supporting Haitian Red Cross in the areas of disaster preparedness and risk reduction, including safer schools that could eventually serve as shelter during emergencies, access to basic needs (like water and sanitation) and propose to support the rehabilitation of 2 schools and/or health facilities.The SAEPs proved to have high losses due to maintenance problems in the pipes and private connections. In addition some of the land where the pipes were supposed to be buries have been severely eroded and affected by landslides resulting on exposed and often degraded pipes. It is to be said that pipes were severely damaged even before Matthew devastated the area. Based on this we propose to work on SAEPs rehabilitation following DINEPA recommendations. Capacity Building of existing organizations such as HRC is needed. HRC is a well-established organization, with a presence in all the departments of Haiti including the Departement Sud. There is a need to train new volunteers or provide refreshing trainings to those volunteers so they can act as first responders in case of emergency. We propose to train those volunteers on the latest technics and methodologies (PCMA, Cash Transfer Programming, …).Finally the population´s hygiene and sanitation habits need to be improved as it would dramatically help reduce waterborne diseases. NLRC/HRC will address this issue through the volunteers mobilized on the field.
