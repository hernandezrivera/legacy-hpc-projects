Project Summary

The project provides life-sustaining shelter support and contributes towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure. Targeting both IDPs (70%) and host communities (30%) involves activities. As well as providing restricted cash for house rent services (1,000 units) for those IDP vulnerable groups and prioritizing female (widows)or child headed households and elderly people and considering people with disabilities (1,000 HHs; 5,000 individuals). This programme includes 2,500 house rehabilitation activities which will target both IDPs and host communities (2,500 HHs; 12,500 individuals), this activity includes rehabilitating partially damaged units through ceiling repair, walls buildings and holes fixing, doors and windows, infrastructure, electricity network and solar panels, plastering, plumping and providing the water tank. This component will be guided by the shelter rehabilitation guidance published by SNFI cluster in 2018 and ensuring that HLP documentations are well carried out in parallel with field coordination with the relevant local councils.

4 - Needs

According to SNFI HNO 2020, Shelter needs in Syria are widespread and acute resulting in significant living standard consequences and affecting physical and mental well-being and protection of affected populations. Overall shelter severity has increased across Syria, with a corresponding increase in PiN from 2019 by 20 per cent to 5.65 million people.1 Of this, 3.8 million (69 per cent) are in areas of severity 4 or higher. Geographically, these areas are concentrated in Idleb, Aleppo, and camps in the north-east. Up to one million IDPs reside in last-resort informal and spontaneous settlements.2 The NFI PiN has declined by 22 per cent from 2019 to 3.42 million people in 2020.3 This reflects both a continuing but slow improvement in market functionality and the successful efforts of the sector to distribute assistance across the country.4 However, this progress is fragile as many communities suffer from high prices, unpredictable inflation, and low wages, while populations routinely report need for items outside the core NFI packages.5 There is still ongoing displacement, with roughly one million people expected to be displaced in 20206 while return movements have increased to some areas and an estimated 64 per cent of communities are underserved7. This is exacerbated by the deterioration of purchasing power resulting in families not able to meet their needs during harsh weather with fuel and power sources remaining in high demand.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The Project has considered the Do-No-Harm principle in that it will use local field staff that will evaluate and assess the security situation, so that mitigation and response measures can be taken where and when needed. We are committed towards mainstreaming protection principles into all of our projects and do so through the use of several mechanisms. These mechanisms include the creation of feedback and complaint mechanisms for projects, capacity building of all staff on protection and mainstreaming protection throughout all phases of implementation, protection cluster involvement and creating a work environment that encourages the consultation and active participation of women and children throughout programming.
