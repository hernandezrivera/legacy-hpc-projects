Project Summary

The project “Hygiene, Nutrition and Winterization as Cash as well as Psychosocial Support for Conflict-Affected People in need along the Contact Line, Eastern Ukraine” (2019-2021) aims to improve food security, hygiene conditions, preparedness to winter and psychosocial well-being of conflict-affected populations in the 5-km zone along the 'contact line' in Eastern Ukraine (GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts).
The psychosocial component of the project is implemented by ASB’s local partner, Samaritan Union of Ukraine (SSU). Through provision of psychosocial support (PSS), the project aims at to reduce social isolation and loneliness of conflict-affected older people, children and adolescents as well as to increase their resilience to stress.
The project is currently funded by the Federal Foreign Office of the Federal Republic of Germany.

1 - Needs

The conflict in Eastern Ukraine has negatively affected psychological wellbeing of people in the conflict zone, especially those living along the ‘contact line.’ According to the needs assessment of HelpAge International conducted in rayons near the ‘contact line’ in the GCA of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts (August 2020), 96,43% of surveyed older women and men mention having different psychosocial issues. Apart from the conflict-related stress, mental well-being of older people has also deteriorated due to the COVID-19 pandemic this year. Thus, 100% of respondents (74,84% women) experience anxiety in relation to COVID-19 pandemic (“Humanitarian needs of older women and men in Government Controlled Areas of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, Ukraine”, August 2020). First, older people and persons with comorbidities have a higher risk of COVID-19 infection. Second, their feeling of isolation and loneliness has been exacerbated by the lockdown and other quarantine restrictions. To illustrate, the PDM data collected by ASB in July-August 2020 to assess the interim results of the project “Hygiene, Nutrition and Winterization as Cash as well as Psychosocial Support for Conflict-Affected People in need along the Contact Line, Eastern Ukraine” (14 June 2019-13 June 2021) have demonstrated that provision of PSS through phone or online communication, practiced during the lockdown, is much less preferred by beneficiaries than social activities with peers at the PSS centres. As reported by the PDM interviewees, the main reason for this lies in the lack of live communication with the employees of the Centers and other attendees. This is particularly important for older beneficiaries, who often live alone. Thus, according to the above-mentioned needs assessment by HAI (August 2020), 42,99% of older people (86,02% of them women) live alone. Conflict-related hostilities and COVID-19 pandemic had created psychosocial problems also for children and adolescents in the conflict zone. Children who were born in 2014, the year when conflict started, became schoolchildren this autumn. As a result of harsh conditions experienced at an early age, these young children often demonstrate aggressive behaviour and speech impediments, as noticed by Child Protection sub-cluster partners (СP sub-cluster newsletter, September 2020). Children’s mental well-being is often endangered because of significant stress that their parents and other adults are exposed to as a result of economic hardships and COVID-19 pandemic. Out of 4351 cases registered by the Child Helpline of La Strada and UNICEF in September 2020, the most widespread reasons to contact the hotline were violence against children (around 30%), psychological health (20%) and relations with family members (15%) (СP sub-cluster newsletter, September 2020). As for the bed-bound older people and children with disabilities, they specifically need home-based psychosocial support (e.g. through mobile groups). As demonstrated by monitoring data of ASB, the recipients of home-based individual PSS within the framework of the current project list personal communication with a psychologist as the most preferred PSS activity (as noted by 79.1% of PDM respondents). Therefore, such project activities as organizing leisure for conflict-affected children, adolescents and older people at social centres and providing PSS to home-bound persons through mobile groups or remotely are of utmost importance.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

The PSS activities of the project are designed for children, adolescents and older people in target localities within the 5-km zone from the ‘contact line’ (GCA of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts) who are in need of psychosocial support. The residents of the target area who meet the project criteria (age, need for PSS) have an opportunity to attend the social centre or (in case of home-bound people and persons with severe disability) be attended by a mobile psychosocial group. A mini-van brings children, adolescents and older people from the localities in the 5-km zone to the nearest social centre in Donetska (Druzhkivka) or Luhanska (Zolote or Toshkivka) oblast. In case of strict quarantine restrictions, group activities take place outdoors or in the online format. Three mobile psychosocial groups travel by car to home-bound beneficiaries (persons with limited mobility and/or severe disabilities). Each group consists of a trained psychologist and engaged volunteers from among youth and mobile older people. In some cases, individual PSS to home-bound beneficiaries is provided through phone, Skype or Viber. Beneficiaries who received group or individual PSS at social centres, at home or through phone/internet between 1 January and 30 September 2020 within the framework of the current project demonstrate significant vulnerabilities: - 36% are older persons (89% of them women);- 64% are children (out of these, 46% are girls and 54% are boys); - 18% are persons with disabilities.So, the project activities are tailored in order to address psychosocial needs of vulnerable older persons, both mobile and home-bound ones. The interim PDM demonstrated high satisfaction of beneficiaries with the provided activities (97,8% for PSS activities at social centres and 90,4% for individual PSS at home against the target of 85% for both components). Activities:- Psychosocial Support through Child Friendly Spaces (CFS), Group Sessions, or Outreach- Provision of group and/or individual PSS to children, adolescents and older people at the social centres;- Organization of outbound recreation for children and adolescents (e.g. summer camps);- Conducting trainings on psychosocial support techniques for school teachers.- Individual Psychosocial Support (all types)- Provision of individual PSS to children, adolescents and older people (persons with limited mobility and/or severe disabilities) at home. The above activities aim at improving mental well-being, social inclusion and stress resilience of boys and girls as well as older men and women. The interim monitoring data demonstrate positive impact of the proposed activities on beneficiaries, as indicated by around 90% of PDM respondents. The positive effects for both activities include, among other things, improved emotional state and communication skills, reduction in the feeling of stress and anxiety, acquiring new skills, and diversified leisure. The project will contribute to the Protection Cluster Objective (PCO) 1 “Ensure conflict-affected people, including IDPs, benefit from full and non-discriminatory access to quality essential services and enjoyment of their rights, with particular attention to people with specific needs” and PCO 2 “Improve resilience of conflict-affected people and system of service provision”.

3 - Indicators & targets

The main indicators and targets are the following:• 85% of beneficiaries are satisfied with the aid provided;• 85% of beneficiaries report that the assistance was easily accessible;• 70% of beneficiaries report that they are aware of the complaint mechanisms;• 450 children reached by psychosocial support activities through Child Friendly Spaces (CFS), Group Sessions, or outreach;• 85 older people accessing community spaces; • 50 home-bound older people reached by individual psychosocial support;• 15 home-bound children with disabilities reached by individual psychosocial support;• 500 people receiving PPE and hygiene items (e.g. soaps and/or hand sanitizers)• 30 teachers trained.Project beneficiaries are informed about the complaints procedure and provided with relevant contact details. Based on their preferences, beneficiaries may ask questions and/or provide their feedback through various existing channels (phone, Viber, Skype, e-mail or through SSU staff). Post Distribution Monitoring will be undertaken to measure the effectiveness of the programme. The PDM findings will be used to improve project design and activities in order to tailor them in line with current need and preferences of affected populations. All assessments, including PDM, are designed to allow age and gender disaggregation.
