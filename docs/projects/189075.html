Project Summary

Titi foundation is proposing an intervention under the coordination and common services cluster in line with 2022 HRP sectoral objective 1; strengthen coordination including joint and complementary needs analysis to inform responses. Rapid needs assessments will be carried out following identification of areas of dire humanitarian needs using our data collection tools and titi foundation area of knowledge being a static partner implementing other projects in these proposed locations. The assessments conducted will be fed into the wider responses through proper coordination with partners like the South Sudan NGO Forum, the state ICCG which Titi is also an active member of presenting several clusters such as FSL and S/NFI, the various technical working groups and clusters which will culminate into strengthening the humanitarian response by using reliable and timely set of information.
Since the signing of the revitalized peace agreement in 2018, some parts of south Sudan are experiencing relative calm and as a result making the environment in some locations like Kajokeji, Magwi and Nimule favorable for communities to return to their areas of origin from the neighboring countries like Uganda and even within some parts across the country, a good example was the displacement of populations from Jonglei to Mangala in CES. It should also be noted that there still exists some hotspots across the country which has been as a result of conflicts, inter- communal violence, cattle raids and floods which has also culminated into displacements. Its on this basis that Titi will also intervene with the CCS sectoral objective 3; ensure timely monitoring of new displacements and return movements. In this regard Titi teams will ensure that humanitarian partners are accorded with timely and reliable information which they will utilize for both humanitarian response and programming. This will be attained through conducting surveys, KIIs and FGDs to ascertain the number of needs of IDPS and Returnees in the stated areas of intervention regular and timely updates on population mobility at official and non official strategic points of transit.
During the year of the project’s implementation, M&E will focus on three broad areas: 1) Strengthening of
data management, reporting and M&E capacity building of South Sudan NGO forum members, Partners and Government at the State, County and Payam levels; 2) Full rollout of the Management Information Systems and 3) Implementation of the project’s Learning Agenda. These will be implemented in addition to the routine project M&E activities to incorporate: 1) routine monitoring, evaluation & reporting; 2) Management information systems (MIS); 3) Learning, Knowledge Management & Operation
research; and 4) Capacity building and communication. Data from the hubs will be shared and trends of services and other activities offered during the reporting period reviewed. The review meetings will double as forums for best practice learning in data management and reporting. The Project will strive to ensure the process of data review and learning forum is instituted to enhance transparency and accountability through data and encourage sustainability for continuity beyond project life. Our M and E system will serve as the key resource for monitoring and evaluating progress and ensuring that service delivery partners are delivering services that address the unique needs of each beneficiary household and that fulfill the overall goal of the project. Joint field monitoring visits to partners will be conducted by TF Coordination team and through joint quarterly SMT and Cluster monitoring visits

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to face one of the largest displacement and humanitarian crises globally with increased challenges linked to humanitarian access impacting service delivery to vulnerable populations. Increased sub-national violence and overall insecurity has resulted in high levels of displacement and fluid population movements. The situation is compounded by shrinking humanitarian space as a result of targeting of humanitarian assets and personnel; increased bureaucratic impediments and overall protection concerns. Climatic shocks that include three years (2019-2021) of consecutive flooding affected at least 835,000 people and destroyed critical infrastructure such as roads, schools, health facilities, markets and damaged crops ahead of the next harvest season. Economic shocks resulting in high food prices and the impact of COVID-19 have added to the already complex and dire vulnerable conditions of populations. The 2021 FSMNS+HNOi multi-sectoral assessment indicates that an estimated 8.9 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance. The assessment demonstrates the importance of rigorous, household-level data to support evidence-based prioritization in an atmosphere of increasingly constrained resources.In the coming year, effective, sustainable humanitarian delivery will continue to depend on strengthened engagement with local-duty bearers and partners, enhanced understanding of and appreciation for humanitarian space and improved measures that promote the safety and security of humanitarians.Engagement with authorities in Juba and state level and conducting training on Labour Law and NGO Recruitment Guidelines with humanitarian organizations and authorities remains important in contributing towards reducing interferences with NGOs and practices of bringing competent work force for effective delivery of assistance. The interventions contribute to respect for humanitarian principles that govern the way NGOs work. Engaging with authorities at sub-national levels will facilitate the unhindered and unimpeded access for humanitarians as well as promote safety and respect for humanitarian workers so that they serve the populations in need in a timely and consistent manner. Risk-informed humanitarian action and conflictsensitivity in humanitarian programming will be critical to ensure better preparedness and implement a ‘Do No Harm’ approach. Coordination that is effective and agile and which seeks innovative ways to reach underserved vulnerable people. International and national humanitarian actors continue to play a central role and need to complement and leverage on each other’s expertise to facilitate humanitarian response in a protracted and complex humanitarian crisis. Accountability to Affected Populations and community engagement facilitated through the multi-sectoral needs assessment or the FSNMS+ house-hold survey provided a deeper understanding of underlying protection issues and affected populations’ (HC, IDPs, Returnees) self-identified priorities and preferences around humanitarian assistance to enable a community and conflict-sensitive approach to needs analysis and response planning.The data quality assurance strategies by TF will include review and development of data collection tools that accurately capture all project’s data elements, training and mentorships on the use of the data collection tools and continuous review of data and reports before external sharing. The project will review and upgrade the current MIS database to accommodate the design, facilitate timely updates, and where feasible link data flow from the State & county hubs to the national level. Further, the project will also introduce the use of mobile applications to facilitate real time updates to MIS from community and setting up a centralized web based MIS data hub to support analytics and reporting. The project will apply Routine Data Quality Assessments when there are data quality constraints to be addressed.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback received will assist to adjust most especially our data collection tools such as the survey tools, KIIs and FGDs so as to incorporate the additional information. In other instances communities will be informed on key findings where possible. With the establishment of Local Monitoring Groups in each of the locations we are operational in, community members are able to provide regular feedback on project assistance. Titi Foundation values and takes seriously all of the feedback itreceives through the various mechanisms, and uses it to inform the design of projects and activities where possible. This may include changing the location of activities to increase accessibility, assisting in the identification of referral services needed, or other mitigating measures to reduce risk or harm tobeneficiaries, while also ensuring all projects are inclusive and accessible. Any complaints made against Titi Foundation’s staff or programming will also initiate an internal review and investigation, in alignment with the organizations policies and code of conduct.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

All of Titi Foundations Local Monitoring Groups are inclusive of different population groups, to ensure that the staff are receiving feedback from even the most vulnerable members of a community. The establishment of the complaint desk with a focal person allows for people who are not able to utilize the suggestions box and hotlines or use of the tool questionnaires on feedback to also provide feedback. The Local Monitoring Groups also works in collaboration with our teams and the broader community, and are able to advocate on behalf of their most vulnerable members. Titi Foundation staff also consult with communities to identify vulnerable members to ensure that they have access to the program’s activities and services.so enumerators will be trained on how to include and conduct a survey with potentially vulnerable groups

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Titi Foundation being a protection partner will provide training for enumerators on core protection principles which they will use during data collection to prioritize the safety and dignity of all community members who are engaging during Rapid Needs Assessments. PSEA dedicated questionswill be included in the questionnaire in order to support the National PSEA TaskForce. Through Titi Foundations already existing code of conduct for the staff on zero tolerance to prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) we are able to prevent staff fromindulging in activities that can lead to protection threats during project implementation. Also Titi Foundation has readily available hotlines, help desks, complaint committees which consist of men, women, youth, child headed households, and people living with disabilities and local authorities to address gender equality and through the various means beneficiaries can reach out to higher authorities of the organization to report any possible protection threats in the cause of project implementation. And also for the representatives and Titi Foundation staff to be more accountable to affected populations so as to mitigate any protection threats.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

It should be noted that majority of the beneficiaries of this project will mainly be the humanitarian organizations such as NNGOs, INGOs, UN, RRC and the local communities who will mainly receive the analyzed reports which will be shared through presentations within the various forums such as the clusters, technical working groups and the NGO Forum. The enumerators will also provide information on the project to key informants and community members during data collection. Titi Foundation data collection tools such as surveys, KIIs and FGD questionnaires takes into account feedback information from the various stakeholders. We have also established Local committees in each of the locations that we work in, to ensure that community members are able to provide regular feedback on project activities. we also provide suggestion boxes and hotlines for feedback. Titi Foundation values and takes seriously all of the feedback it receives through these mechanisms, and uses it to inform the design of projects and activities where possible. This may include changing the location of activities to increase accessibility, assisting in the identification of referralservices needed, or other mitigating measures to reduce risk or harm to beneficiaries, while also ensuring all projects are inclusive and accessible. Any complaints made against Titi Foundation’s staff or programming will also initiate an internal review and investigation, in alignment with the organizations policies and code of conduct.
