Project Summary

The goal of HARAF’s project is to education assistance to 15,000 children directly across the BAY States thereby further reducing the rate of out of school children in the North-East by 30th November, 2021. Direct Project participants are to include 8,000 girls and 7,000 boys and provide support to 200 teachers.
To this end, HARAF has planned to:
a. Construct 12 temporary learning space (TLS) in camps within Jere and parts of MMC namely the Muna Dalti IDP Camp and Garba Buzu Sabon Gari camp. This will provide non-formal learning opportunities to children within the camp that cannot be enrolled into formal schools to receive quality basic education.
b. Construct WASH facilities in schools: the absence of functional latrines and WASH facilities has been a large deterrent to quality and access to education while raising protection concerns. Also, with the global pandemic poses threat to handwashing as a major COVID-19 preventive measure. Therefore, HARAF will construct 5 gender-segregated toilets alongside hand washing stations with soap and provide hygiene promotion messaging as well as Menstrual Hygiene sensitization within sites of our intervention.
c. Rehabilitate 10 existing classrooms within 5 schools each in Fufore and Hong of Adamawa State. During a recent rapid assessment to these areas a remarkable need has been seen in some selected school where the condition of existing structures is not safe for learning and raises concerns. HARAF will select 5 most vulnerable schools in each of these LGAs to rehabilitate worst hit classrooms.
d. Rehabilitate existing WASH facilities: In targeted schools HARAF will rehabilitate existing latrines ensuring that gender markers are clearly placed on such and protection concerns are addressed. HARAF also will ensure that slabs for disabled persons are constructed into existing WASH structures and Menstrual Health Management is taken into account for girls.
e. Establish 3 Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) and Child-to-Child Education in Yola, Girei and Hong. There are small settlements of IDPs within Yola and Girei where Child Protection is totally absent. HARAF will fill in this gap by establishing a 1 in each LGA. Each CFS will be equipped with PSS materials and toys for the children. There will be adult facilitators while older children will be equipped to provide Child-to-Child Education within the safe spaces.
f. Provide training as well as mentorship to teachers in the targeted schools: the quality of education is tremendously poor within the targeted communities of Hong, Fufore and Gujba. To increase the capacity of teachers to deliver quality, learner-focused teaching, positive discipline, protection and psychosocial support to learners that come to their school, HARAF will provide mentorship training for 200 teachers of the which 120 are males and 80 are females in targeted schools. HARAF will also provide psycho social materials in all schools selected for intervention including footballs, nets, jerseys, bats board games among others.
g. Provide basic classroom furniture to the schools in Hong and the constructed temporary learning spaces
h. HARAF will work in collaboration with the SUBEBs in the BAY states to provide teaching government approved teaching textbooks to teachers in schools of intervention. Also, other job aid will be provided to improve the quality of lessons delivered.
i. HARAF will distribute learning materials and school uniforms to the children and provide basic remuneration to volunteer teachers and CFS facilitators.
j. Conduct advocacy, sensitization and Campaigns geared towards the enrollment and retention of more learners in schools and learning centres.
The project will be executed in close collaboration with the Education in Emergencies Cluster, the Ministry of Education, the Ministry of Women’s Affair and the State Universal Basic Education Board (SUBEB) across the BAY states as well as other local structures within our communities of intervention.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Response Plan 2020, with 935 schools closed, over 1.4 million girls and nearly 1.3 million boys out of school are facing an uncertain future without access to education. The global pandemic and lockdown in country in from May to July greatly affected Education in the North-East. It is feared that a lot of children may not be able to return to their classrooms due to the effects of this lockdown. Also, in a recent SitRep shared by OCHA the number of persons targeted for Education intervention reaches 3.1 million. Additionally, in Girei formal school attendance rate is set at 49% more so non-formal school attendance is 33%. In Hong, formal school attendance is 55% and non-formal school attendance is 22%. In Jere attendance of formal and non-formal schools were 40% and 25% respectively. In MMC, 48% of the children assessed under the REACH MSNA 2020 were in formal schools and 27% are participants of informal programs. Furthermore, in Gujba formal schools’ attendance rate was at 41% while 56% of the children were in non-formal schools. This shows a significant gap that needs to be covered. In north-eastern Nigeria, 2.8 million children are in need of education-in-emergencies support in three conflict-affected States (Borno, Yobe, Adamawa). In these States, at least 802 schools remain closed and 497 classrooms are listed as destroyed, with another 1,392 damaged but repairable. (UNICEF.org/Nigeria/Education)In a recent assessment to Hong, the female teachers in the Hong the Government School interviewed stated the need of the provision of girls’ dignity kits as it will further encourage girls to continue in learning and strength the hygiene promotion messaging and menstrual hygiene management in the schools.Discussion with government Stakeholders revealed that learning furniture is not sufficient in some of the classrooms and there is need for recreational materials. While the government have been able to intervene to rehabilitate and construct boreholes in some of these schools a lot are still in need of rehabilitation to make learning conducive for the children in a protective environment.FGDs were conducted with parents and SBMC committee members and it was discovered that lack of adequate latrines in the schools constituted a large barrier to continuous attendance of the learners in school especially the girls. The idea of constructing gender segregated latrines was a welcomed idea.IDP learners around Yola, Girei and Damaturu are not able to participate fully in Education as money to buy learning materials and uniforms are difficult for the low-income earning parents. A sample of parents in an assessment conducted by REACH specified that they will want Education support to be provided to them so that their children can continue learning.There is a small settlement of IDPs in Anguwan Fulani Yola where a rapid assessment was conducted to assess the needs of the communities. Through general observation and interview with the community leader, there were over 100 out of school children below the age of 6 roaming about the settlement without any child protection services or safe space to play and receive learning. The community leader appealed for Safe Space to be created and equipped for the learners. This gap was also seen in Hong where the Village head request if there can be an intervention to keep these children engaged and prepare them for school hence the need to create a safe space and implement a child-to-child learning program.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project strongly carries protection mainstreaming and safe guarding by incorporating the basic humanitarian principles, non-discrimination based on tribe, gender, age and disability. Learning facilities and Sanitation facilities will be constructed to protect learners from more harm or violence. Persons with disabilities will be taken into account while constructing learning spaces so that their access is not in any way limited. Part of the school rehabilitation program will involve fencing of the schools, TLS and CFS. Community engagement and participation will be employed to ensure the school siting was done in secure sites.HARAF as an organization has a strong PSEA, Protection and Child Safeguarding Policy guiding its operational environment. Such policy will be translated to all volunteers, partners and vendors operating on the project. The learners as well will trained on the Child Protection and Safeguarding Policy and what to expect during the project as well as safe channels to report protection concerns. HARAF will network with protection actors to provide other services not covered by the project.
