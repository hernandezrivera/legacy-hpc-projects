Project Summary

According to HNO2024, the protracted crisis in Syria, further compounded by the aftermath of the earthquake continues to negatively impact the education system. Where there is a need to support up to 7.1M school aged children and teachers, including CwD, of which 6.9M are children (48% female and 52% male). There are more than 2.45 4million Out of School Children (46% girls, 54% boys) and over a million who are at risk of dropping out. Idleb tops the governorates with the highest percentages of OoSC children reaching 69% ,Arraqa rate is 48% Aleppo rate is 38%. the main driver reducing access to education is economic, affecting both the households and teaching personnel.
Humanitarian development aid organization (HUDA) aims to enhance access to 24,000 children (10,000 girls, 14,000 boys, 960 PwD), teachers, and caregivers by launching a series of supportive educational activities to provide non-formal education through:
1. Establishing 200 temporary education classrooms using tented classrooms, prefabricated classrooms, or light rehabilitation of classrooms.
2. Equipping 200 classrooms with school furniture such as desks, whiteboards, and providing a conducive learning environment.
3. Training 600 teachers and administrators on classroom management skills and modern teaching methods.
4. Providing stationery for students and teachers, and printing the curriculum.
5. Providing incentives for 1,200 teachers in non-formal education classrooms.
6. Holding awareness sessions for students and their families on the importance of education and how to enroll in formal education.
HUDA targets marginalized and hard-to-reach areas where children deprived of the right to education are present, based on an actual needs assessment. The organization operates in Idlib, Aleppo, Raqqa, and Al-Hasakah, where these areas are mostly distant from schools, prompting parents not to send their children there. Therefore, HUDA takes into account the children's circumstances by establishing temporary classrooms in their areas, bringing in mobile teachers from other areas to provide education services. The implementation is carried out through a strict and rigorous system where teachers undergo specialized training to educate children who are out of school under difficult circumstances. A group of educational supervisors and educators is also assigned to regularly visit remote classrooms.
Consideration is also given to children and teachers with special needs (PWD), where these temporary classes are easily accessible and do not include any barriers that prevent them from accessing and learning.
Furthermore, the organization works extensively within our previous programs to achieve gender balance, targeting 40% females and 60% males, while also considering balance in the educational and administrative staff alike.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project's objective is to reach out to children deprived of their right to education, including those who have discontinued schooling for various reasons, ensuring there's no discrimination based on gender or race. It particularly focuses on remote villages and marginalized camps that lack access to services provided by humanitarian organizations.Children who are illiterate and unable to read or write correctly are identified through tests conducted in targeted areas. Following this, the child undergoes a 6-month course alternating between literacy and numeracy education to catch up with their peers. Additionally, teachers undergo specialized training to educate out of school children and those with special needs. The education curriculum used is a developed version of the curriculum approved by the cluster (Curriculum B).The methodology adopted in this project focuses on minimizing costs as much as possible to achieve widespread accessibility. Children are taught wherever they are found, whether in pre-prepared classrooms, rehabilitated abandoned rooms turned into classrooms, or in village mosques, especially in remote areas where distances to schools are significant.According to this model, students do not need to travel to their classes for more than a few minutes. Additionally, the duration of each lesson is limited to just 3 hours, either in the evening or morning, depending on the children's ability to attend school. Some children are sent to work by their parents in the morning, so it is their right to receive education at a convenient time. The monitoring and evaluation team is developing a comprehensive plan to assess the performance of the project, spanning from target area identification to the integration of students into formal education systems. Key activities include: Establishing a baseline through assessment tests to gauge students' levels and identify the need for educational intervention in target areas. Conducting regular visits to educational classes to ensure the distribution of stationery, validated through beneficiary interviews. Monitoring progress in delivering the curriculum by conducting frequent visits to educational classes. Implementing random tests during specific periods to assess educational progress and report on students' learning levels. Organizing parent meetings to evaluate service levels and gather feedback. Randomly attending lessons to evaluate teacher performance and effectiveness. Conducting surveys with students and parents to assess satisfaction and identify areas for improvement. Interviewing teachers to gauge satisfaction and training needs, aiming to enhance project quality. Generating monthly progress reports and submitting urgent reports if tasks falter. Utilizing the Kobo program for electronic data collection. Monitoring students' enrollment in formal schools to ensure target ratios are achieved.
