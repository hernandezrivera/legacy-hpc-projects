Project Summary

The water systems will be supported through in-kind operation and maintenance assistance to the Water Establishments and municipalities managing the systems. The alternative of providing direct support through water trucking would be costlier, presents higher risks in terms of public health and the environment, and might create inequalities between households and localities and create or exacerbate social tensions, particularly as water trucking or desludging is a very visible support modality.
Despite the higher initial cost of investment activities supporting WaSH systems are economically justifiable in the mid-term run and are crucial for mitigating public health risks and for enhancing equity and poverty reduction. Given the ongoing energy crisis that heavily impacts water production and distribution, and focusing on reducing the water dependency on fossil fuels, through systems solarization, powering by other renewable energy, and transformation to gravity-fed systems where feasible.
The systems will be provided with solar systems, and minor repair and rehabilitation will be conducted for the water distribution networks to mitigate the risk of water and environment contamination, targeting cholera high-risk and cholera hot-spot areas to mitigate the risk of water and environment contamination.
In addition, the containment of the cholera outbreak will continue to be done through CATI response, hygiene kits distribution, and cholera awareness sessions, municipalities will be provided with chlorine, training and monitoring tools in an attempt to chlorinate water supplied by private water trucking vendors.
the most socio-economically vulnerable households will be targeted in coordination with the Food Security. Given the alarming results of MSNA and VaSyr assessments highlighting that a significant portion of people from all population groups face issues with accessing adequate hygiene materials, ensuring access to critical hygiene items and water treatment consumables for the most vulnerable.
Women and girls of reproductive age will be provided with menstrual hygiene kits, and awareness sessions including topics related to GBV. People living with disabilities, families with young children and the elderly will be also targeted with hygiene kits and awareness sessions.
The targeting will be executed based on needs estimated by the sector using various data sources available in 2022: MSNA, VaSYR, WaSH Sector Assessment Platform, Water Market Monitoring, and Water and Sanitation Vulnerability Mapping focusing on the feasibility of solarization. Estimated WaSH sector People in Need, estimated area severity, as well as cholera WaSH risk map and cholera hotspots, will be used for prioritization. Interventions will be coordinated with relevant authorities such as Water Establishments, municipalities and concerned ministries.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

The Project will follow WVL safeguarding and PSEA policy and procedures that outline zero-tolerance approach toward sexual exploitation and abuse and the guidelines for reporting and responding to any incidents. Moreover, the team and stakeholders involved in the project will be trained on preventing and responding to GBV and PSEA, as well as promoting gender and social inclusion. Awareness-raising sessions, discussions and meetings will be in place to engage community to raise awareness about GBV and SEA and encourage them to report incidents. Gender-responsive questions will be incorporated into the project monitoring and evaluation and project assessments disaggregated and analyzed by sex, age and disability in order to ensure communities especially women, girls and PWD are involved and their voices and needs are taken into account in all aspect of the project and to understand the impact of the services and remaining challenges for both genders.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

World Vision Lebanon will guarantee the presence of a functional and accessible Accountability system to enhance beneficiary and community participation, as well as ownership of project design and implementation. The Community Response mechanism (CRM) was developed based on the feedback provided by the beneficiaries. Accordingly, the WVL’s Accountability Unit has selected several channels to raise complaints and provide feedback through a needs assessment that was conducted with World Vision’s beneficiaries:World Vision's HelplineHelpdesk during field activitiesFace to face with the Accountability TeamFace to face with the project teamTrusted Community MemberOnline FormThe selected channels reflect the beneficiary’s preferences and it encouraged their participation in the project design.AAP MechanismThe Accountability system will ensure that targeted beneficiaries have access to information packages including information about World Vision.
