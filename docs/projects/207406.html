Project Summary

According to OCHA (October 2023), the situation in the North-West and South-West regions remains unstable with persistent violence exacerbating humanitarian needs. Affected people continue to flee their homes to neighboring villages and communities. In June 2023, humanitarian and security partners reported the displacement of some 9,418 people in these regions because of the violence, which led to the deaths of over 25 people, the destruction of at least 20 homes, the closure of businesses, and the cessation of various forms of livelihood activities.
About 3 million people have been affected- 705,800 IDPs and 360,500 Returnees with 1,968,578 people affected in NW, 782,535 in need of assistance with 232,300 IDPs and 204,298 returnees (Humanitarian Response Plan, 2021). The conflict characterized by the destruction of the already fragile economy, massive displacements due to continuous arm battles coupled with COVID-19, is having disastrous consequences on the populations of the embattled regions with women and children suffering the most.
The Purpose
The purpose of this project is to offer protection services to people in the crisis affected communities of the Northwest and Southwest regions in the domains of:
1. Child protection
2. Gender-Based Violence
3. Housing land and Property
The Project shall Target:
- GBV victims with MHPSS and medical service
- Provide persons in crisis zones with GBV prevention and risk mitigation.
- Strengthen referral pathways for protection services.
- Target vulnerable host communities with CBCPMS.
- Target IDP, Returnees, and vulnerable host communities’ children with MPHSS through CFS, etc
- Identify, document, and reunite unaccompanied and separated children.
- Target victims and children with Comprehensive Case Management provided to vulnerable children.
- Provide support to issue birth certificates to conflict-affected children.
- Target IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable host communities with education and assistance to handle issues of housing, land and property.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Since 2016, the Northwest and Southwest regions of Cameroon have been facing a socio-political crisis that has seriously damaged the local economy and deteriorated the humanitarian situation. Beyond human losses, there are vast displacements; with its corollaries on the social system with education in particular as 54% of schools (Education Cluster, 2023) are closed and only 10% of IDP children access education (OCHA, 2022). Poverty resulting from damage/loss of livelihoods forced parents to move in search of jobs and children for education. This has resulted in an increase in family separation with over 19,000 separated children (HRP, 2023). As is the case in humanitarian settings, these children face other protection risks like physical injuries, psychosocial distress, mental health issues, physical and sexual abuse, and recruitment into armed groups or exploitation. Boys face risks of recruitment by armed groups posing a threat to their safety, exposing others to the risk of arbitrary arrest, and economic exploitation at times under the worst forms of child labour. Girls on their part face abuses like sexual exploitation as more young women and girls resort to survival sex, increased sexual violence, harassment, child marriages, and unwanted early pregnancies. The situation is more precarious among out-of-school children, UASC, and children with disabilities who are 3-4 times more likely to experience abuse (UNCRPD). The CBCHS (2019) corroborated this in research where children with disabilities were 3-5 times more likely to be abused in Cameroon. According to IOM MSNA report round 8, an estimated 28% of IDP girls between the ages <18 years are suffering from different forms of sexual harassment or exploitation, while an estimated 8% of them are being forced into early marriages. Only 5% of safe spaces are available for those people in need of protection with only 4% services available for MHPSS. Furthermore, an estimated 18% of IDP children have left school and 28% of them must go to other destinations in search of jobs. The need for protection services in these crisis regions cannot be overemphasized.
