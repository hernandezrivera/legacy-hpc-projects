Project Summary

WWGVC plans to intervene in Aleppo, Deir-Ez-Zor, and Ar-Raqqa governorates restoring community
services in newly accessible and underserved areas. In line with the WASH sector's objectives, the interventions identified focus on resuming the supply of sufficient and safe water to 85,689 people in need in 2022 and 45,500 in 2023, reducing the impacts of the financial burden of purchasing trucked water, and the health concerns associated with unmonitored water sources for domestic use. WWGVC will coordinate closely with the local water authority of each governorate to assess the infrastructure damage and the possibility of rehabilitation and resuming operations with the funds to be secured. Detailed assessments will also be conducted prior to the prioritization and selection of locations. The assessment will develop a baseline regarding the status of WASH infrastructure and services, the severity of its impact, and the presence of vulnerable individuals that will inform the prioritization decision at the community level.
Water systems in the targeted areas are either out of service or partially functioning with a limited capacity of regular maintenance. The improvement in service provision is achieved by the light rehabilitation of pumping stations, provision and repair of electromechanical equipment for pumping stations, rehabilitation of boreholes, and decentralized water networks rehabilitation and small-scale extensions to ensure water delivery to the affected population. These activities will ensure water systems are operational in all the targeted communities of the project, delivering sufficient water to the households connected to the water networks. In parallel, WWGVC will work closely with the Water Boards of each governorate to build their capacity on water systems management and water safety planning, including training of technical staff and pumping stations operators, along with the provision of equipment, tools, and consumables for an improved and more efficient maintenance of water systems and monitoring of water quality. The above hard component activities will be integrated with awareness campaigns on hygiene promotion (HP) and Covid-19 preparedness and prevention, and distribution of WASH NFIs for the most vulnerable communities. In 2023, in addition to the regular programming mentioned above, WWGVC will also direct focus on solar water pumping and rehabilitation of wastewater networks infrastructure.
The project activities are targeting needs that are similar across all the subdistricts targetted in all three governorates. These include, but not limited to: 1. Damaged pumping stations and catchment areas of water sources prohibiting collection and delivery of water through the networks. 2. Damaged water networks reducing the water system's efficiency in delivering the needed amount of water collected. 3. Low efficiency of water systems management by the water authorities. 4. Inability to afford hygiene items to the most vulnerable populations increasing threat to C-19 and water-born diseases. 5. Reduced awareness and care towards proper hygiene practices caused by the worsening living conditions.

4 - Needs

Access to water in the targeted areas of this project is still affected by damages to the infrastructure, and currently with increased challenges related to the absence of maintenance, the drain of technical resources, the increased cost of fuel, and climate change. As a result, according to Syria HNO 2022, the population relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources increased to 47% in 2022, with no hopes of improvement during 2023. WWGVC field assessment triangulated with external resources shared by the sector showed that in Aleppo, the population targeted reported depending on water trucking and private boreholes to access water with concerns over its quality. The same thing applies to Ar-Raqqa. While in Deir Ez Zor, the pumping stations targeted are not able to provide the minimum water required due to the inefficiency of the system following the inability to perform regular maintenance and repair of sudden failures in operations. The lack of financial and technical capacity of the WBs to provide fuel and perform regular maintenance is apparent across all governorates. Additionally, the unaffordability of hygiene items to the most vulnerable is more apparent than before. The majority of the respondents reported inability to maintain regular hygiene practices because of this, while knowledge on water-born diseases and C19 remains minimal. The community's prioritization of hygiene is reduced because of increasingly challenging living conditions.

5 - Implementation of activities

The implementation of activities will be done by WWGVC Field teams in each governorate in areas under GoS control. A:1 Coordination with the local stakeholders, SARC, line ministries, and the WASH sector will be undergone and approvals to be obtained. A2: Light rehabilitation of pumping stations includes the rehabilitation of structural elements and the supply and installation of electromechanical equipment including pumps, power generators, electric panels, and other accessories.A3: Supply, repair, and replacement of electromechanical equipment and spare parts for pumping stations where no civil works are required.A4: Testing, cleaning, and rehabilitation of water boreholes.A5: Decentralized water network rehabilitation and small-scale networks extension targeting areas with recently rehabilitated/equipped pumping stations.A6: Equipping small to medium pumping stations and boreholes with power-generating solar panels.A7: Capacity building of water board technical staff and pumping station operator on water systems design, operations, maintenance, and water quality, in addition to the provision of tools, equipment, and consumables.A8: Rehabilitation of wastewater networks to be conducted in 2023 only.A9: Conduction of hygiene promotion campaign and awareness on COVID-19 at the community level through mass media, through FGDs and street theater, associated with the distribution of family hygiene kits to the most vulnerable.A10: Monitoring, evaluation, and learning.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Gender insensitive content of kits; MM: follow sector unified kit constitution standardizing gender-friendly kits. 2)Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV; MM: Set an adequate distribution management plan including the involvement of the different community groups in the planning. Train staff on PSEA code of conduct. 3) Risk of theft of equipment, and construction material during or after the intervention; MM: Coordinate with local authorities to ensure local ownership of material and rehabilitated facilities. Ensure proper security protocols are followed by contractors during the rehabilitation period. 4) Potential exposure to explosive hazards during assessment/rehabilitation of water supply infrastructure; MM: Work closely with Mine Action actors and conduct assessments/rehabilitation works only in areas declared cleared; Conduct safety training with staff. 5) Children below 18 work with contractors on rehabilitation works; MM: Add zero-tolerance disclosure on child labor in contracts; Check civil documentation.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV. Mitigation measures: Promote the Establishment of an adequate management plan; Promote involvement /engagement with communities in distribution planning and feedback from different groups on packages for improvement; training of staff and enforcement of code of PSEA code of conduct among partner staff. Risk: GBV/PSEA. Mitigation measures: All staff is aware of and trained on the code of conduct. Regular training sessions is done with staff. Sensitize the community about PSEA and how to report misconduct.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS: a)Stable security in targeted areas b)Safe access for staff/contractors to conduct activities. b)MoWR and WBs maintain planned priorities in the areas c)Local communities and other agencies are positive towards services newly restored. d)Availability of fuel for activities implementation. e)Degree of stability in the exchange rate.RISKS: a)Security situation deteriorates up to prevent the implementation of some activities b)Shrinking of humanitarian and operational space in some areas due to military operation d)Major change in the attitude of authorities and local partners towards planned interventions c) lack of fuel for activities implementation d) steep deterioration of the SYP and increased inflation in the cost of supplies and works.MITIGATION STRATEGY: a)WWGVC ensures continuous coordination with SARC and UNDSS to monitor the situation. WWGVC adopts a security plan and SOP in case of any major accident. b)GVC ensures a good relationship with authorities and a transparent/accountable way of working, informing on planned activities. c)GVC directly ensures M&E of activities. d) Implementation and monitoring plans will be regularly adjusted and optimized based on fuel availability. e) Take into account a degree of inflation in financial planning. Inform sector and donors in case there's a change in planning caused by limited financial capacity caused by the inflation.
