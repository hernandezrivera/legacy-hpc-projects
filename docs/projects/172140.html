Project Summary

The project will increase sustainable access to clean and safe water for 20,610 women and children and 25 community institutions; promote awareness, positive behaviours and practices in safe hygiene and santitation for the same, improve access to hygiene-enabling kits for 25 institutions and 1 000 households in Gwanda and Matobo districts. A total of 25,610 people in need will be reached.The project seeks to address the immediate and ongoing WASH needs of communities affected by multi-year drought which has been exacerbated by COVID-19. Due to limited income sources caused by the pandemic, there is an increased labour burden on women. Anecdotal evidence points to the increase in GBV cases.The proposed intervention aims to improve access to safe water sources and hygiene through drilling, water point rehabilitation, water quality monitoring as well as promotion of positive hygiene practices. Capacity building of pump mechanics and training of water point committees will be part of the intervention. Through this action, Moriti will rehabilitate 60 water points (30 Gwanda and 30 Matobo) and strengthen community-based management by re-establishing, activating and training 60 water point user committees to manage the rehabilitated water points. Water quality monitoring will be conducted for 60 water points and remedial action applied accordingly for the water points requiring treatment so as to ensure that water is safe. To further strengthen the sustainability of the water points in the districts, Moriti will train 40 village pump minders (20 in Gwanda and 20 in Matobo).
The project will also drill and equip 6 new water points (3 Gwanda and 3 Matobo) preferably at institutes. Through the project 2 piped water schemes will be established by upgrading of some existing water points with high yield.
The project will also promote awareness of good water and sanitation practices in communities, public places and institutions. To promote the adoption of good water, sanitation and hygiene practices amongst communities, the project will conduct community engagement through capacitation of Village Health Workers (VHWs) and other stakeholders on WASH considering COVID-19 such as door to door campaigns. Mass media approach such as radio-talk show, roadshow as well as SMS blasting, radio jingles shall be used to promote Risk Communication and Community Engagement.
Result 1: Improved access to sufficient water of appropriate quality and quantity to fulfil basic needs for an estimated 25,610 women, men and children in Gwanda and Matobo
MORITI and DDF will conduct technical assessments which will inform rehabilitation of 60 water points and 2 solar piped water schemes in Gwanda and Matobo. Water quality assessment shall be conducted on the target water points to determine the fitness of the water for human consumption, prior to rehabilitation. Water quality monitoring shall be ongoing throughout the project duration to ensure safety of the water for human consumption. Water point rehabilitation shall be conducted through DDF and Moriti. To ensure sustainability of the water points, MORITI shall train water point committees on the Community Based Management (CBM) concept for the management of the water points. Each committee shall have 7 members comprising of 4 females and 3 males. The project shall also upgrade 2 solar powered water schemes

2 - Indicators

 Number of people with improved access to clean safe water: The project will be deemed successful if 70% of the targeted beneficiaries access water within 500 metre radius through the provision of the piped water system and also if 30% travel less than 2 kilometres to get water from the borehole. These will include the disabled, the aged and the chronically ill people. In addition to the availability of water, hygiene practices will improve whereby personal and environmental hygiene will be enhanced and there will be fewer visits to health facilities. With water within reach, women and girls who usually bear the brunt of doing household chores will have free time for other livelihoods activities. The reduction in water related diseases will indicate success of the project . The involvement of both men and women in community water management will be a mark of the project success. Reduction in gender based violence will be realised as both women and girls will no longer be exposed to such violence and abuse because of the proximity of the water in the villages . Participation and great involvement by communities in the project activities will be an indicator of impact. The number of people taking leading roles and doing it rightly in the project will definitely be a good indicator of success. Proportion of men, women and youth using improved /smart sanitation and hygiene practices : The increase in the utilisation of the constructed latrines and the pace of latrine construction uptake will indicate project success. Restoration of the dignity of the community will be a plus on the project as latrine users will feel safe and protected as compared to the current state where they practice open defeacation. Reduction in diarrhoeal diseases reported by the Village Health Workers and the local health facilities will show that the project has created a positive impact. The project will also register success if all the targeted villages are declared "Open defaecation Free" whereby every household will have a toilet and utilising it properly together with the accompanying hand washing facilities. There will be success in the project if all people with varying disabilities are covered by the project and have their toilets equipped with special entrance and holding rails. Health and Hygiene promotion will be an integral part of the project and success will be realised through the number of Community and School Health Clubs that are created and are functional. The numbers of people reached by Health and Hygiene messages will give an indication of coverage on information dissemination. Behaviour change is a process and the number of homesteads engaging in best Hygiene practices will indicate the impact of hygiene messaging that have been put into practice. The number of people in the village who are not part of the beneficiaries and are seen to be voluntarily instituting some of the good elements of the project will be an indicator of success through project ripple effect. The project sustainability plan that will be crafted and implemented successfully by the community will be an indicator of success. Number of people and institutes reached with WASH hygiene kits: Water collection and storage are vital so the more people observing recommended water storage facilities, the more the health of the community is enhanced. Other components of the Hygiene kits which include soap and Menstrual Hygiene kits will account for the success of the project when communities maintain good personal hygiene and when reports of girls absconding school due to lack of appropriate menstrual sanitary pads are reduced. Success will be recorded when school attendance increases and the girls' confidence is restored.  Number of institutions (schools and clinics) whose access to safe water has been improved: These Institutions record high populations daily and the number of people accessing safe water will highly reduce disease occurrence.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Throughout the project AAP will be mainstreamed into the activities in a manner that ensures the affected people are at the centre of our work, and this will be achieved by ensuring the following; 1. Communities participate in the project in a safe and appropriate manner that ensures equal opportunities for all including the vulnerable. This will be achieved through open dialogue with communities and conducting these dialogues in areas within reach of everyone.2. Communities will also be involved during repair of water points through provision of local materials and labour; they will establish Operation and Maintenance committees for the water points and take ownership of WASH facilities. The project document and budget will be shared with all Stakeholders in the community.3. Communication and information with the affected populations will be shared in a two-way communication channel that ensures the targeted communities are aware of their rights in the project and the obligations of the partner while working in their community. Inception meetings and community dialogues from district level down to ward level and even village level as the project progresses are some of the tools to be utilised. 4. Feedback and complaint mechanisms will be established and utilised throughout the project, to get feedback on experiences and perspectives of the project implementation. Mechanisms such as help desks, suggestion boxes and suggestion boxes feedback sessions will be implemented at a frequency to be determined by communities. All feedback received will be recorded and adequately addressed in a manner that also safeguards the communities. The project will also adopt an open door policy to enable communities to freely discuss the project. 5. The Partner will also work with other existing WASH partners in the districts to ensure that communities are not overburdened, and efforts are also not duplicatedThe project will ensure that PSEA reporting and feedback mechanisms are developed in a manner that safeguards the communities. The DWSSC as well as project focal persons will be trained on PSEA/SGBV issues. The gender analysis will also be used to identify potential risk, as well as identify potential exclusion of vulnerable groups within the community. This project seeks to impact existing gender roles by mainstreaming gender as a cross-cutting component to the project, especially with the shift of the male population abroad to find work, there is an opportunity to empower women. The project will address the key barriers women and girls face in accessing and utilization of WASH services through the engagement of men, boys, women and girls as equal partners in the project. In order to accomplish this, Moriti will advocate for female representation in pump mechanics positions, male representation in VHW positions as well as equal representation of men and women in WUCs and other committees. Moriti will hold separate meetings with women and girls to ensure that their feedback is taken into account in the identification and selection of water points since they are mostly responsible for fetching water for the household from the water points. Gender equity will be enhanced through capacity building and awareness raising at various platforms and trainings involving both men, women, boys and girls in separate fora where they can all feel free to speak up and ask questions.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Impact prediction MORITI will conduct a baseline study in the project area to identify the possible impact of the intended project activities like borehole drilling. The information collected will inform the project of the possible impact. Construction of boreholes may present some significant risks especially when upstream-downstream interactions are considered. Mitigation measures may include working in close collaboration with local water authorities to ensure borehole drilling does not compromise access to water for others, and that water is safe for all. For critical project measures, like drilling of new boreholes or deepening of wells, prior hydro-geological survey, geo-resistivity investigations and water quality testing will be compulsory. The surveys will be done in consultation with the local leadership and traditional water deviners who will have earmarked the possible water places. Impact assessment. MORITI will interpret the results of the Baseline to provide a conclusion, which can ultimately be used by decision-makers in determining the fate of the project application. Mitigation Frequently, the assessment of impacts will reveal damaging effects upon the environment. These will need to be alleviated by removing or reducing the extent of exposure to identified environmental impacts.
