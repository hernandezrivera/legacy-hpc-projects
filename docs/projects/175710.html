Project Summary

This project will aim at reducing high morbidity and mortality from common diseases (such as malaria, diarrhea, pneumonia, COVID-19, measles, cholera, that are aggravated by high burden of outbreak, severe food insecurity, lack of functional health facilities scarcity of medicines and other medical supplies and flooding, insecurity incidents, the macro-economic crisis, and the effects of COVID-19 measures. Between October and November 2020, 34 counties across the country were classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Greater Upper Nile region alone had 20 counties classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 1 county (Pibor catastrophe (IPC phase 5). The project will take into account the gender disparity and try to improve the accessibility to most disadvantaged group in the community, HFO will make sure the community stakeholders are sensitized and informed during project initiation, implementation and monitoring & evaluations. The project will ensure intergrated with other sectors (WASH, FSL, Nutrition, Protection, GBV)
The project shall target a population of 84,465 men, women, boys and girls, through provision of essential primary healthcare services in Mobile responses, and Juaibor, Keew PHCCs and Paguir, Chotbora PHCUs in Fangak Ayod town PHCC+ and Kharmun PHCU in Ayod and Diel, Atar PHCUs in Canal/Pigi and Ayai and Nyoc Awany PHCUs in Aweil South and Ajondit PHCU in Tonj Noth as compelmentarity, using qualified staff to do consultation, Antenatal care, maternity-clean and safe delivery and new born care, home base care for COVID-19 with support from WHO, Health cluster, MoH and County health department (CHD) and community leaders and members for the purpose of ownership and accountability to affected populations it positively anticipated the planned project will achieve it stipulated goals as HRP and health cluster strategic and specific objectives. HFO multi-sectoral approach helps in ensuring that we meet most of the needs of the targeted.
1. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities, there will be proper and timely disease outbreak investigation and response and sharing information with public health Laboratory for confirmation and inform health cluster and CHD, MoH surveillance system for all alerts.
2. Improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through PHC MT (Mobile Team) and community-based health service delivery systems. Exsiting gaps will be addressed in HFs and create more robust outreach that will tend to reach all.
3. Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly.
The project locations will be Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Ayod Counties of Jonglei, Tonj North Warrap state and Aweil South of NBG state respectively. HFO’s Environmental Policy for all programmatic and logistic activities is firstly do no harm’ to the environment as well as to people.
Monitoring and evaluation will be conducted before, during and after the implementation commences, All report will be shared with Health cluster-WHO, MoH and Donor for better informed and further decision making. HFO and the partners will manage the predictable threat to the project, will try as much as possible to mitigate them. Monitoring and evaluation will include integrated disease surveillance and response data, early warning alert and response system data, assessment reports from initial rapid need assessments, multi-cluster/sector initial rapid assessment, public health situation analysis, health service functionality, 5Ws, IDSR, HMIS/DHIS2, the health resources availability monitoring system, public health risks, outbreak investigation reports, mortality and morbidity estimates, and other relevant data analysed from monitoring visits and such information will be shared with donors and partners (HC-WHO MOH.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has been experiencing worse humanitarian disasters in the world such as Conflict that resulted into civil war since 2013, flooding since 2019-2020 in greater Upper Nile region, and Bhar El Ghazel. Two disasters have caused displacement to population within South Sudan from Sudan to Neighboring countries and Internally displaced people that in Protection of Civilians sites-POCs in country respectively, destructions of infrastructure health facilities, schools’ churches and homestead that are hard to rebuild due continuous communal violent all over the country. Despite the ceasefire between two waring parties, the economic meltdown is making life difficult both in urban and rural areas where people are hardly putting food on the table for everyone in the family. Harvest has been destroyed by heavy rain and serious flooding in the most part of the country specially Jonglei, Northern Bhar El Ghazal, Warrap states. The most severe acute food insecurity conditions are in counties where chronic vulnerabilities have been exacerbated by a combination of shocks, including floods, insecurity incidents, the macro-economic crisis, and the effects of COVID-19 measures. As of 31 May 2020, South Sudan had recorded 6,084 COVID-19 cases, and 83 deaths. Between October and November 2020, 34 counties across the country were classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Greater Upper Nile region had 20 counties classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity (i.e. Ayod, Canal/Pigi, Fangak, in Jonglei State;) in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Aweil South in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State, Tonj North and Tonj South in Warrap State). In the first projection period of December 2020 to March 2021, 22 counties are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, with the number likely to increase to 33 counties in the second projection period of April to July 2021 if nothing is done to prevent the already deteriorating situations. Jonglei, and Upper NileIn October and November 2020, an estimated of 724,000 people were in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, 648,000 people were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 11,000 people were in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Pibor County. This have been worsened by current flooding that has inflicted too much displacement almost whole country. In Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Ayod still have no good coverage under World Bank that support about few HFs in these counties and many HFs are destroyed by flood in Jonglei in particular. Over 25,000, 23,000 and 37,000 are IDPs due to submerging of HHs in Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Ayod (IRNA Sept and Aug. 2020). So many HFs unfunctional and no services reaching majority of community. Immunization still low at 55% by 2020 MoH DHIS2 reports. Greater Warrap and NBGIn October and November 2020, an estimated 259,000 people were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 13,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Tonj North County.For Aweil South County which was classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The key drivers of food insecurity included the restrictions put in place to reduce the spread of COVID-19 which negatively impacted the movement of goods and people to and from the State. In Bhar El Gazel the Health Pooled project stilldoes not cover all community and there are good number in both Aweil South and Tonj North, this lack of services coverage led to IPC level affecting upto 13,000 people in Tonj North alone.OPD services and immunization coverage is low and measles epidemic as burden in 2019-2020. Although the developmental partners are trying to reach every child and mother, there is still high need to improve referrals through mobile cllinics and integrated outreach services.The project response will work with WASH, FSL, Nutrition, Protection, GBV) to ensure access to all humanitarian services.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

HFO multi-sectoral approach helps in ensuring that we meet most of the needs of the targeted beneficiaries including protection, inclusion and equality. Healthcare Foundation Organization during the initial stages of the projects conducts a general risk assessment and once the project is assessed to cause risks to both the beneficiaries and the organization, mitigation measures are always employed. In previous projects, mitigation measures such as clear and open communication with the beneficiaries will help in protecting the beneficiaries from being exploited, choosing locations for registration and distribution which are close and safe for the beneficiaries and an inclusive decision making process with the beneficiaries. HFO also works with the government and the community leaders to ensure that adequate information reaches the beneficiaries and the community leaders usually take responsibility of managing the crowds as the community members listen to them more. During distributions and registrations, vulnerable groups such as sick, disabled, pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, children and the elderly are prioritized so that they spend little time in waiting. Confidentiality is also key to protecting the beneficiaries and HFO normally conducts thorough training for the staff before any activities in the field which includes data protection policies etc. 1. During consultation of OPD outreach, ANC, Immunization services, protection activities have to be considered and ensrureed by the team to keep an eye on the vulnerable groups and they are given high priority to get services under registration and treatment. It is very important no one is left under attended for. 2. By mitigating this risk, it important for the project to make this clear from the start that people with disability are part of decision making and any person with diability is identified and assisted to reach where services are or can be reached by services wherever she or he is, this can be done through outreach sessions and their representatives have to have information for where they can be reached easily. 3. The project will work collaboratively with all protection and GBV partners to mark referral pathways and ensure every survivor has access to the services. HFO will have to work with law enforcement agencies; such as police and local leaders to ensure everyone's right to services adhered to and observed for the purpose of accessing huamnitarian services. 4. Marginalized groups are the ones that bear the burden of humnitarian crisis where they are left under served and their problems are always ignored or dismissed. This project will ensure at the community levels women are at the front and during employment, both females and males will be both encouraged to participate fully during the implementation of the project. In so doing, the most marginalized person will definitely be in the picture. And the issues of marginalization shall be minimized.
