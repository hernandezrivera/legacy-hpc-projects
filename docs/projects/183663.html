Project Summary

With the existing health worldwide crisis of COVID19 and its long-term impacts, the number of women in need of GBV services is increasing. In Palestine, the heightened political situation is exacerbating the situation mainly in areas like East Jerusalem, the Jordan valley, and the northern district of the West Bank; coupled with the constant threat of annexation and its impact on Government services. Based on the needs identified through the HNO process, and several analyses of the humanitarian situation specifically in the abovementioned areas; the project and its interventions will aim to strengthen and empower women and girls in Jerusalem mainly the vulnerable and girls and women with disabilities who are exposed to GBV and enable them to live in dignity and peace within their families and communities. Additionally, the interventions will ensure women have access to services being offered through the multi-sectoral approach used by several service providers. The project will also enhance the approach of remote service provision through hotlines and other means in case the current situation of COVID 19 persists but at the same time, readiness to switch back to face-to-face services will also be maintained upon any changes in COVID-19 regulations.. Since the onset of COVID-19, UNFPA has conducted a series of online capacity-building workshops on providing services using hotlines. Those workshops targeted a number of GBV services providers including governmental partners; this experience will be assessed to further develop the virtual service delivery modality.
The project will build on the current one by providing different kinds of support. mechanisms and tools will be promoted through Through this project starting with awareness, building life skills taking into consideration the left-behind women and WWD, and ending up with having GBV survivors treated and equipped with tools and income generation small projects. creating their own income generation small projects, thus empowering them to lead their lives and future. On the other hand, the project will target the GBV survivors as well the providers to match the scaling up of emergency GBV services with building the capacity of government and noncivil society -government actors to provide quality survivor-centered GBV services. Through the enhancement of new approaches, technologies, and platforms used throughout COVID 19 crises, the project will continue to use the multi-sectoral approach in providing the GBV services and build on past UNFPA achievements.
The project will target GBV survivor women and young girls at risk in Jerusalem (old city and Isawyeh,Abu Deess, Anata and Sufat, Al TurTuor, Silwan and Kufr Aqab suburbs surrounding Jerusalem).
Currently, UNFPA in cooperation with it’s partner PFPPA and in through coordination with Hemaya Network in Jerusalem are developing a clear and concise referral pathway between Hemaya members to facilitate and ensure GBV survivors and/ or women that seek protection in Jerusalem can obtain multi sectoral services available securely, confidentially and without discrimination or stigmatization. The Pathway includes a comprehensive mapping of services available to deal with the diversity of cases that can be expected to be received by the members and define the referral system. Training is planned to build the capacity of Hemay network on the referral pathway and developed SoP to better understand the pathway and to address the GBV needs in accordance to the referral.
Furthermore, PESA training also is planning for service provider at the safe spaces and for Hemaya network members and relevant CBOs in the areas of project implemented.

1 - Needs

Based on the PiN analysis in the HNO 2022 process, the total number of People in Need for the GBV sector is 453,660 , with 288,557 women in the Gaza Strip and 165,103 in the West Bank. Those figures when linked to the severity ranking in the HNO 2022 process show that GBV cases residing in East Jerusalem are considered to be under “stress” or “severe stress” due to the fact that 55% to 75% of them do not know about existing protection services. In addition, following the halt in PA coordination with Israeli Authorities, the resulting absence of Government protection services in Area C was considered an aggravating factor in this area which led to the conclusion that all GBV cases in this area are under increased stress, therefore ranking them as “severe”. An assessment study “Gender-Based Violence Needs Assessment in East Jerusalem” which has been conducted in August 2021 indicated that Palestinian Jerusalemite women and girls live in very difficult circumstances, trapped by a combination of community and family violence on one hand, and political violence practiced and imposed by the Israeli occupation authorities on the other. The study revealed that Dduring the COVID-19 pandemic, the rate of GBV increased and led to the emergence of new and/or expanded groups in need of institutional assistance. These groups include women who lack residency status in Jerusalem and persons with disabilities. The already high rates of GBV were clearly exacerbated by the absence of the rule of law and poor living conditions, particularly in Area C of East Jerusalem (Kafr ‘Aqab, Abu Dis, A Ram, and Al Eizariya). Women with West Bank identity cards (IDs) who are married to Jerusalemite men and waiting for family reunification have been subjected to . divorced faced a psychological violence as they are denied access to their children and financial rights by their husbands who take advantage of the pandemic-related lockdowns and restrictions imposed by the Israeli authorities. While women who are still married they also suffer from different type of violence as some husbands used the family reunification process as a means of pressure and abuse against their wives. The research findings concluded that Persons with disabilities lacking full residency status In East Jerusalem without family reunification documents were the group affected the most and were most at risk of being denied their rights during the pandemic. Persons with disabilities were deprived of the services provided by the Israeli authorities to this group Moreover, according to statistics published by SAWA organization, after the crisis a 20% increase in calls regarding mental health and psychosocial support and abuse/violence cases was recorded, the majority of which came from young men and adolescent boys that had experienced abuse from their families at home. Calls from women seeking support increased from 40% to 58%. Moreover, reporting of abuse and domestic violence from partners increased by 38%as soon as the hotline extended its working hours.In East Jerusalem, UNFPA has established three safe spaces and a counseling room in Augusta Victoria hospitals where GBV survivors and women at risk are receiving health, psychosocial and legal support. However, certain areas are still lacking effective GBV multispectral response to GBV survivors, and functional referral mechanism due to the absence of authority, and the inability of local NGOs to protect and provide full support to women and girls in areas under full Israeli control and hence investing in those spaces is of utmost importance. The COVID -19 pandemic l imited access to GBV services especially for those women who hold a West Bank ID and live inside the Jerusalem barrier. Additionally, the Separation Barrier, separating off Jerusalem from the West Bank, has resulted in jurisdictional ambiguity between the obligations of the Palestinian National Authorities (PNA) and the Israeli Police, further fragmenting the vulnerable communities in East Jerusalem.Moreover, COVID-19 has caused socio-economic depression especially for women who are already suffering from vulnerability and domestic violence. The pandemic crisis has affected new women and contributed to a behavioral dynamic of men being more frustrated, unable to fulfill their expected role in a very patriarchal society such as where men are mainly responsible to provide for and protect their families. Some women who had a stable situation before the crisis has become GBV victims due to economic deterioration due to the loss of livelihoods resulting from the recurrent lockdowns. Many of them do not have the savings and capabilities to cope with the change and are now at high risk of violence. All the above-mentioned findings indicate the necessity of mental health and psychosocial structured interventions (MHPSS) to support women and their families. A holistic approach of providing services will be considered through this project, mainly to consider a survivor center approach and to look at the woman as the core of support and address all her needs comprehensively.

2 - Activities

The below interventions will build on the efforts done so far by UNFPA and all its partners in the designated areas to combat GBV and provide a complementary services packages which ensures appropriate monitoring and referrals of GBV cases. Three main aspects will be covered through those interventions:1. Multispectral services to GBV survivors which also include identification of new casesa. Develop counseling services through investment in the already established GBV rooms at the Palestinianthe Palestinian Red Crescent Society safe spaces in the old city and Bab Al Sahreh, and the Augusta Victoria hospital. Capacity building and training will be delivered to the teams in those rooms in addition toother hospital staff, and more emphasis will be put on disseminating awareness messages to women in the targeted areas on the existing GBV services as well as general prevention messages. b. Provide case management to GBV survivors through UNFPA supported safe spaces, multi-sectorial services and counselling, including health, psychosocial and legal services, to 500 GBV cases including women with disabilities identiﬁed and treated through face-to face as well as virtual services provision modality to respond to COVID-19 crisis.c. Support the establishment of small business enterprises and initiatives for 100 women GBV survivors and women at risk. This support will be directed to start small businesses to improve their wellbeing and reduce vulnerability of GBV survivors and support self-protection and resilience specifically if COVID 19 pandemic continues. GBV survivors will be equipped with vocational and life skills, and will be able to create innovative income generating initiatives that can contribute to improving household basic needs as part of the multi- sectorial service package taking into account NO HARM and RIGHTS approaches.d. Provide MHPSS services (psychological counselling, individual and group MHPSS support) for 300 women and family members at risk through networking with HEMAYA Network in East Jerusalem and its surrounding villages, and through active CBOs and service providers in Jerusalem, to reduce psychological distress due to COVID-19 crisis and improve the quality of their psychological well-being. The project will provide case management to GBV survivors, referral to advanced psychological mental health support when needed for GBV survivors and GBV at risk through individual and group counselling sessions to respond to the increased stress and tension as a result of increase the level of violence due to political situation and the impact of COVID -19 pandemic. The MHPSS sessions mains to improve women’s mental health and psychological well-being. This activity will be implemented through local partners under HEMAYA Network in East Jerusalem and its surrounding area of Jerusalem. e. Provide CVA for 500 women and girls survivors of GBV targeted through the safe spaces and the CBOs. it will be provided to vulnerable headed household women; women with disability and breast cancer patients. This assistance will be in partnership with the WFP where beneficiaries are able to choose the commodities he/she needs through retailer shop in a way that preserves dignity and freedom of choice. Commodity voucher assistance in the amount of $100 for one time will be delivered in the framework of case management means providing CVA directly to the survivors for the purpose of supporting them to meet essential needs. CVA is not a standalone activity it complement the multi-sectoral services. Beneficiaries’ selection criteria is based on; 1)GBV survivors who received multi-sectorial services at the three safe spaces in Jerusalem; 2) Poor and Vulnerable women at risk to GBV; 3)Disabled women exposed to GBV. It worth mentioning that special attention will be given to girls and women with disability, a consultation meeting with them will be conducted to better understand their needs and to customize the commodity voucher and select an accessible shop for voucher redemption. Moreover, Beneficiaries will be oriented on PESA through the communication materials to be developed to ensure that beneficiaries know how to report such cases. f.d. Provide counselling sessions and couple therapy for 100 perpetrators of GBV through group and individual counselling sessions. g.e. Establish and strengthen the referral pathway for HEMAYA network in Jerusalem to effectively respond to GBV risks in Jerusalem through conducting two capacity building workshops targeting 50 service providers.f. Carry out 10 self-help support groups for GBV survivors (50 women per group, 25 sessions).Conduct a training session on GBV issues and awareness on detection and referral of GBV cases for Municipalities and CBOs in Kufur Aqab, AL Ram and Al Tura. Conduct capacity building targeting medical staff at Jerusalem hospitals namely Augusta Victoria and Palestinian Red Crescent hospital, and other service providers, mainly on detection and referral for GBV survivors to improve the quality, accessibility, acceptability, and availability of GBV services. b. Strengthen the capacity and enablement of 8 CBOs working in Jerusalem to provide multi sectoral remote services to vulnerable women and family members to communicate, monitor cases and document progress (phones, laptops, etc.)c. Strengthen the capacity of local organizations on PSEA by conducting PSEA training for GBV SC members and services providers, strengthen complaint mechanisms and monitoring capacities and increase awareness on PSEA a. Conduct 50 awareness-raising sessions on GBV and available GBV services among 1000 women and girls at high risk of GBV. it will focus on how to reduce the risk of GBV especially in the targeted communitiesb. b. Conduct 50 awareness session on cyber violence to raise the awareness of women and youth.c. Produce communication materials such as TV& radio spots, printed materials, and social media massages on GBV .

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

“Do No harm” is one of the main GBV guiding principles and approaches according to UNFPA minimum standards and all partners of GBV service providers know how to apply these principles in their work with GBV survivors. The service providers received and continuously have trainings and capacity building to protect the identity and safety of GBV survivors by not disclosing any personal information about the survivor or the incident to anyone without the survivor’s explicit permission and consent. Also, GBV service providers have robust experience in ensuring GBV survivors are protected from harm and in providing psychological and emotional safety to survivors who may be at high risk from the perpetrators or from their family members due to notions of family honour. All UNFPA partners are required to follow the GBV Survivor Centred Approach to provide meaningful access to services, ensuring accountability mechanisms are in place as well as participation and empowerment of GBV survivors. UNFPA will continue to provide capacity building to partners on these principles including on GBV Minimum Standards and safe GBV remote service delivery.The protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and the accountability to affected populations (AAP), are also mainstreamed throughout the program design.Moreover, ongoing monitoring of beneficiaries to adjust any measure that related to safety of beneficiaries and to deal with case-by-case basis to ensure that GBV survivors are protected from harm.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project is designed to provide safe and equal access to adequate multi-sectorial services for women and girls and for the ones with disabilities through UNFPA implementing partners of GBV service providers, who are specialized in providing comprehensive services for GBV survivors and the staff received multiple trainings on manuals that focuses on dealing and including people with disabilities, in addition to adapting their safe spaces to be accessible for WGWDs. Additionally, the service directory for GBV service is now printed in Braille Also, the project focuses on reaching the vulnerable beneficiaries from different areas of Gaza Strip and West Bank, Additionally, the project will target women who are cancer and kidney-failure patients to provide them with responsive services in addition to some special needs and treatment. Moreover, based on UNFPA study about violence on elderly, the project will target elderly people mainly women to ensure their equitable access to health and other services. On the other hand, the awareness, outreach initiatives, advocacy campaigns and the social media materials will cover wide areas to ensure all targeted communities have a better knowledge about GBV, link of COVID-19 and GBV, Cyber Crimes, rights of PWDs and other important topics.Moreover, UNFPA will assess the market to guarantee that all beneficiaries have equal opportunities to access the market. Accessibility to market and retail shops for women with disability and elderly will be considered through selecting participated shops for voucher redemption.
