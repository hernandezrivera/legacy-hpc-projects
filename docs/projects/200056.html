Project Summary

This project aims to address some of the current gaps identified by the SNFI sector in 2023 in 42 priority communes identified in the HNO in Burundi through the provision of comprehensive Shelter and Non-Food Items (NFI) assistance. IOM proposed response will include:
a) Rapid assessments following natural disasters and other displacement events to assess life-saving needs (including shelter and NFI) of affected individuals.
b) Distribution of Shelter assistance that will include emergency shelters, semi-permanent shelters, repair kits and rental support to returnees, IDPs and host communities based on needs assessments and context, including resettlement assistance for IDPs in protracted displacement situations.
c) Distribution of NFI kits to returnees, IDPs and host communities based on needs assessments and context.
d) Establishment of AAP mechanisms to targeted individuals through the provision of a toll-free hotline hosted in the premises of the Burundian Red Cross. This hotline will be in charge of processing the feedbacks and complaints to whomever it concerns and to call back beneficiaries to provide them with a response when needed.

The majority of humanitarian needs for Shelter and Non-Food Items (SNFI) in Burundi are caused by recurrent disasters related to torrential rains, strong winds, floods and landslides. These affect thousands of households and cause massive displacement of persons every year, resulting in significant human and material damage, particularly in rural areas. According to the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM, October 2022), 89 % of the 75,300 displaced persons in Burundi are displaced due to climate related hazards. Between September 2022 and January 2023 only, more than 90,000 individuals were affected dur to climate events, including more than 10,000 IDPs. Additionally, the situation of displacement is prolonged for more than 40% of the 75,300 IDPs who have been displaced for more than one year. On top of the needs of internally displaced persons, in 2023, UNHCR estimates that 75,000 Burundian refugees with high humanitarian needs will return to Burundi, including support on shelter and NFI as they often come back to find their houses destroyed or their land occupied.
In 2023, 218, 438 individuals have Shelter and NFI needs.
IOM's proposed response is in line with the sector's 2023 strategy and will aim to address objectives 1,2 and 3.
In order to meet objective 1 of the sector strategy, IOM plans to, following a thorough rapid needs assessment, distribute 900 emergency shelter kits, provide rental support to 3,200 households and 8,300 NFI kits to natural hazard affected populations.
In order to meet objective 2, IOM plans to distribute 2,250 repair kits, 1,000 semi-permanent shelters and 5,500 complete NFI kits to individuals in protracted displacement situation, returnees and host communities.
Finally, in line with Objective 3 of the sector strategy, IOM will strengthen the accountability to affected populations mechanisms through the funding of a hotline dedicated to receive feedback and complaints for SNFI interventions. Feedback and complaints received through the hotline will be directly oriented toward IOM will be used to improve IOM interventions in line with accountability toward affected population.
Post-distribution monitoring will systematically be conducted to collect feedback on the assistance provided. The results of these monitoring visits will be shared with the sector to share beneficiaries' impressions and lessons learned.
