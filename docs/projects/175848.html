Project Summary

Due to lack of safe overland and air transport options in South Sudan, the United Nations Humanitarian Air Service (UNHAS) under WFP will continue to offer air passenger transport services to enable humanitarian workers to reach most areas of the country. The service will provide weekly flight schedule to 57 destinations combined with ad-hoc special flights for Rapid Response Teams, special field visits, COVID-19 response and emergency evacuations. UNHAS will also provide trainings to civil aviation personnel and support air traffic management projects to improve safety the the South Sudanese airspace.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has poor and/or non-existent surface transport infrastructure, making air service the only viable alternative to access locations with the most affected populations. With no viable commercial alternatives to reach remote locations, UNHAS remains the only common service provider for the entire humanitarian community for the delivery of light humanitarian cargo and transportation of humanitarian workers to deep field locations. Over 90% of the airstrips in the country are unpaved. Only UNHAS has a fleet of aircraft with the right capacity to offer a common service that reaches these locations. Moreover, UNHAS as a common service, supports Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) Rapid Response Missions and assessments to hard-to-reach locations

2 - Project activities

Operate regular passenger flights on behalf of the entire humanitarian community to provide enhanced access to beneficiaries, needs assessments locations and project project implementation sites through safe, effective and efficient air services.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

UNHAS will undertake airstrip assessments and review occurrence reports to ensure current information is available. As If the logistics infrastructure further deteriorates, the UNHAS fleet will be reviewed and restructured as required. Air assets will be aligned to user demands and available financial resources. UNHAS will recover cancelled flight within two days or when conditions permit. Should UNHAS experience restrictions on movement or increased government restrictions, UNHAS will continue close collaboration with key forum including the Joint Verification and Monitoring Mechanism (JVMM). In terms of security, UNHAS internal clearance processes and clearances with key security partners like UNDSS allow for the flight safety. To prevent from uplifting fuel or interrupted fuel supplies at field locations, UNHAS has engaged two suppliers in all operational bases. If there is an acute fuel crisis, UNHAS can flexibly alter operational plans by reducing payload on aircraft. UNHAS will continue close collaboration with its Steering Committee (SC) to advocate resource mobilization. Should UNHAS experience a financial shortfall, UNHAS in consultation with the SC may raise the nominal ticket fee or reduce the fleet. UNHAS shall continue discussion with the government on the operational continuity during the COVID-19 outbreak.
