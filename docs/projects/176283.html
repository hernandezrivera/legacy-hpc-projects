Project Summary

Gender-based violence (GBV) is one of the most critical threats to the protection and wellbeing of women and children in South Sudan. GBV was already rife prior to the conflict, and is now nearing epidemic proportions. The full magnitude of the problem is unclear. However, studies indicate that some 65 per cent of women and girls have experienced physical and/or sexual violence in their lifetime, and some 51 per cent have suffered intimate partner violence (IPV). Some 33 per cent of women have experienced sexual violence from a non-partner, primarily during attacks or raids. The majority of girls and women experience sexual violence for the first time under the age of 18.
According to the 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), 7.5 million South Sudanese remain in need of humanitarian assistance and nearly 4 million remain displaced from their homes, of which 2.2 million are refugees in neighboring countries while 1.5 million are internally displaced. Despite progress with the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS); the country is still facing devastating human and financial costs of the complex protracted crisis, food insecurity and the COVID-19 pandemic. There is need to build community-owned prevention and response mechanism through massive awareness creation, training for community leaders and county authorities, promotion of child participation, provision of support services to the survivors, creating livelihood program for vulnerable groups to help them avoid vulnerability to GBV and help the community to sustain their protection services on their own. Therefore, this project will focus on prevention and response to gender-based violence, and child abuse with particular emphasis on building the capacity of local communities to understand the different vulnerabilities and risks factors faced by women boys, girls and men in a humanitarian crisis and take practical measures to mitigate, prevent and respond to violence against women and children. The project targets 35860 persons (i.e girls 8,800, Boys 10,733, Women 8,858 and Men 7,470).
The overall goal of this project is to improve access to quality GBV prevention and response services integrated Peace building in Bor South County, South Sudan. Activities proposed GBV prevention and response are specifically tailored for women, man, boys, and girls. Special needs of each person will be taken into consideration throughout all stages of the project cycle. Taking into consideration their specific needs and risks, the project will give additional focus to girls and women in Bor
The risk of child marriage remains constant due to conflict, the country’s economic situation and harmful social norms that perpetuate issues of gender based violence. The UNICEF-led Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism has recorded over 385 incidents of sexual violence against children since December 2013, which is being perpetrated by all parties to the conflict. Children comprise around 25 per cent of all reported cases of conflict-related sexual violence, though there are concerns numbers may be much higher.

1 - Needs

Gender-Based Violence (GBV), including rape, sexual assault, domestic violence, forced and early marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse, abduction, discriminatory practices within the legal system and harmful traditional practices is a persistent and serious problem in Bor South County. Sustained violence along political, ethnic, and communal lines in Bor South County has heightened GBV and overwhelmed efforts to develop or maintain coping capacities. Conflict-affected communities in Bor South County face numerous challenges that constrain their ability to become self-reliant. According to HRP 2021, 23 counties including Bor South County are classified as extremely severe (severity 4), with 71,724 people in need of GBV services in Bor South County alone. NEPWU conducted initial assessment in Bor South County and the communities cited sexual violence as the greatest protection concern for women and girls. Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) remains the most common form of GBV in Bor South County, which means women and girls are most at risk in their own homes, from their husbands and other family members. With severe food insecurity and economic crisis across the country, women and girls also have to walk further to forage for food, firewood and other necessities to survive, exposing them to a greater risk of rape, abduction for the purposes of sexual slavery, and other forms of GBV. Survivors may fear stigma or reprisals, hence do not report. Impunity for perpetrators is also due to a weak legal stem, consisting of a mixture of formal and customary laws. For women, customary laws tend to prevail, with rape cases, where reported, are handled by community elders. Most cases of IPV are ignored. GBV affects men, women, boys and girls, but it disproportionally affects women and girls.

Activity 2.1.4 Conduct a 5-day training to community/frontline workers on PSS and peace building applying COVID-19 IPC guidance each attended by 10 personsActivity 2.1.5 Establish one GBV referral pay way at community level
