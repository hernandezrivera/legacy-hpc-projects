Project Summary

ACTED will aim to implement emergency shelter and non-food items interventions in 2020 in Jonglei, Western Bahr el Ghazal and Western Equatoria, in line with the following S/NFI cluster objectives:
1. Improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to newly displaced or populations with new vulnerabilities.
2. Enhance the community’s dignity by increasing their ability to respond to new shocks and building on existing skills.
3. Improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities/affected non- displaced, who are unable to meet their S/NFI needs.
To fulfil the above objectives, ACTED will provide emergency shelter and non-food items to the most vulnerable displaced populations, returnees, and affected non-displaced populations, specifically targeting Jur River, Bor South, Uror, Akobo and Tambura counties. Targeted beneficiaries will be given shelter and non-food items either in-kind or through vouchers/ cash, depending on their needs and location, to enable quick responses to meet emergency need. Cash-based programming and vouchers will be preferred in areas where there is a functioning market and items available. ACTED will utilise different modalities depending on the context and needs, as highlighted in needs assessments. Cash-for-work approaches will be utilised to ensure the most vulnerable recipients are supported to build shelters, and where appropriate host communities will be included as beneficiaries of this to ensure shared benefits.
ACTED’s S/NFI intervention will be closely integrated with multi-sectoral response, including mobile Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM), Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), depending on needs. This will be delivered through our ‘hub of resilience’ model, which is an area-based model aiming to holistically meet emergency needs of vulnerable communities, supporting them to recover from shocks and rebuild resilience. ACTED feels that these multi-sectoral settlements-based responses enhance impact across all sectors, and mitigate against misuse of SNFI items. Responses will be conducted through mobile missions (lasting 6-8 weeks) in tandem with the mobile CCCM response team, in addition to semi-static responses based out of ACTED’s existing static bases in Jonglei, Western Bahr el Ghazal and Western Equatoria.
ACTED highly prioritises Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), ensuring at least a medium term presence in communities (expected minimum 6 weeks) to ensure in-depth assessment and understanding of issues like SEA and HLP. This also enables strengthening of community leadership structures (under the CCCM component) in a way that improves community resilience. Furthermore, ACTED implements a Complaints and Response mechanism in all interventions, ensuring that community feedback is encouraged and acted upon. ACTED’s mobile missions also implement community based complaints mechanisms to ensure sustainability and accountability of leadership to community needs. To further strengthen feedback and response, rapid monitoring will be conducted following distributions, with several full post-distribution monitoring exercises to ensure collation of evidence and lessons learned.
ACTED’s project team utilizes a do no harm matrix during assessment which has a focus on HLP analysis. ACTED will consult the SNFI cluster HLP due diligence guidelines and checklist to ensure that HLP issues fully underlie our do no harm response, and will also collaborate with HLP actors in areas of intervention.

1 - Needs

Years of conflict, in addition to climatic factors such as poor harvests and flooding, have led to significant levels of displacement across South Sudan. As a consequence, many people lack essential shelter and non-food items, having fled their homes without them. Emergency shelter and non-food items needs often intersect with other emergency needs, such as WASH and FSL, therefore ACTED aims to meet these needs in a holistic, multi-sectoral way. In Jur River county, an escalation of cattle raiding and insecurity in 2019 triggered large displacements, leaving IDPs in need of essential NFIs. According to the 2019 South Sudan HNO, 60,200 people were in need of S/NFI in this county at the start of 2019; this is likely to have increased as a result of additional displacement. ACTED has a base in Wau Town and is currently conducting FSL and S/NFI activities in the targeted locations. NFI needs among assessed settlements reporting IDPs in Jur River were reported as plastic sheets (31%), torches (31%) soap (15%), and mosquito nets (15%) in June (REACH, 2019). Reports suggest some market functioning and locally available materials for shelter construction. In Tambura, there is a considerable community of protracted and new IDPs integrated with the host community. Displacement has been a result of fighting between governmental and opposition forces and intercommunal conflicts (relevant more recently) particularly in Wau state. An ACTED assessment in August 2019 found that when it is raining, children of the IDPs are allowed by the host community to take shelter at their homes while the adult population remain under trees. Following displacements in January 2019, RRC with support from the local authorities allocated land for the IDP population who came to Tambura to settle. However, the lack of NFIs such as plastic sheeting has been identified by the community. Jonglei In former Jonglei state, 14% of assessed settlements with IDP presence report improvised shelters as their main shelter type (REACH, 2019). Considerable numbers of IDPs also live with the host community, increasing strain on these populations. According to the 2019 South Sudan HNO, 88,700 people were in need of ES/NFIs in Bor South county; in Uror county this was 38,8000 people. In Bor South, according to RRC, the population of returnees are increasing. However, the shelter and NFI situation is reported to be poor with use of shared utensils and sheltering in school and health centres (INTERSOS and PAH needs assessment, June 2019). In Uror county, more than 40% of REACH assessed settlements reported presence of IDPs in their community. Due to the significant levels of protracted displacement, communities in Uror county are frequently found to be among populations with most severe needs (HNO, 2019). Flooding in late 2019 is anticipated to increase needs. Akobo remains an opposition held territory & has experienced significant insecurity, linked to the national level political conflict as well as inter-communal conflict. Akobo has considerable populations of recently displaced and protracted displaced populations, and influxes of returnees have been reported, many of whom report anecdotally stopping in Akobo as a result of displacement from refugee camps but without being able to return to their place of origin. A significant challenge for these populations is shelter & NFIs, with IDPs arriving empty handed. Flooding in late 2018 is anticipated to increase these needs. ACTED has maintained a longstanding presence in Akobo and has a fully functioning base, and is highly accepted by the local community, and with a positive relationship with the local authorities. In Akobo, ACTED is also the S/NFI Cluster focal point, has conducted a number of assessments, and is currently conducting an S/NFI response.

2 - Activities

Output: To provide immediate humanitarian assistance to displaced and non-displaced affected communities through the provision of S/NFI supportActivity 1.1: Identification of beneficiaries and type of assistanceACTED will design projects alongside the community to ensure focus on the most important needs and targeting of the most vulnerable (FGD with community, KIIs with leadership, ensuring wide representation). Cluster tools will be utilised to conduct needs assessment and identification/ verification of beneficiaries, to ensure that gaps in S/NFI, particularly for the most vulnerable populations in each location, are met. The set of vulnerability criteria will be contextually appropriate and based on guidelines. ACTED will assess market functioning and local supply to ensure that where appropriate, an unrestricted cash or voucher approach can be utilised to stimulate economic activity and support the local market, as well as provide relative flexibility for the beneficiaries regarding which NFI items they prefer. Activity 1.2: Distribution of ES/NFI kits An in-kind NFI kit will be distributed to 4,400 HHs (26,400 ind), of whom an expected 1,100 HHs will also receive shelter support. The kit items will be based on the cluster’s standardised items and guidelines, but will be tailored to the specific needs of the IDPs/ beneficiaries based on assessments of needs that have been conducted, to ensure that their immediate S/NFI needs are met. Items will be procured through the S/NFI Cluster pipeline. Activity 1.3: Distribution of vouchers or unrestricted cash to meet ES/NFI needsInterventions will only commence following a needs assessment and a market assessment which determine a cash response would be appropriate (and which modality). 600 HH (3,600 ind) will be supported with vouchers or unrestricted cash (depending on appropriateness) to provide resources to build their own shelter using locally sourced items and materials, or to buy essential NFIs. This methodology will be used based on availability, to ensure quick responses and to give beneficiaries the dignity to build their shelter and buy items to their specific preferences. ACTED will provide guidance and support regarding materials, structure sizes (targeting sphere standards), protection considerations, identifying spaces and liaising with host communities etc. In situations where beneficiaries are unable to construct their own shelters, support will be provided through a CfW approach.Activity 1.4: Cash-for-Work for shelter constructionTo support the most vulnerable beneficiaries for whom it would be challenging to build shelters, ACTED will support shelter construction through a CfW scheme. As much as possible, Cash for Workers will be selected from host communities to further ensure local buy-in of intervention and mitigate tensions. The CfW activities will be closely monitored by ACTED to ensure quality control, as well as to monitor any protection concerns or complaints. The CfW beneficiaries will be paid in line with the standard labour rate for unskilled labour. The beneficiaries will be selected in coordination and consultation with IDP leaders (male and female), community leaders, and the local authorities, and will be verified by ACTED, based on their vulnerability, to ensure transparency and ownership.Activity 1.5: Monitoring and PDM ACTED will have monitoring mechanisms in place to ensure that high standards are maintained throughout project implementation, protection concerns are identified and addressed, as well as to ensure transparency. During the distributions, ACTED’s program staff will provide quality control and the established Complaints Response Mechanism will be in place to allow beneficiaries to provide feedback and lodge complaints anonymously, and to ensure that they are followed up on. Rapid monitoring immediately following distribution, or full post-distribution monitoring will be completed following all interventions.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

ACTED conducts protection analysis at onset of interventions to identify potential risks to beneficiaries and underlying factors. ACTED utilizes a do no harm matrix during assessment which has a focus on HLP analysis. ACTED will consult the cluster HLP due diligence guidelines to ensure that HLP issues fully underlie our do no harm response, and will also collaborate with HLP actors.ACTED’s mobile interventions all include a protection officer to ensure training of leadership, identification of referral mechanisms and identification of PSNs. However, in mobile interventions which are often remote, it has previously proved challenging to identify protection partners able to take referrals. Therefore, ACTED is increasingly collaborating with protection specialists to run joint mobile missions where possible.Lack of adequate shelter exposes displaced women and girls in particular to sexual violence. They will therefore be prioritized by vulnerability criteria, and as part of the combined SNFI/ CCCM response, ACTED will work with community governance mechanisms to ensure appropriate site maintenance to reduce risks.As much as possible, Cash for Workers will be selected from host communities to further ensure local buy-in of intervention and mitigate tensions that may arise. Beneficiaries will be selected in consultation with community leaders & local authorities, and will be verified by ACTED, based on their vulnerability, to ensure transparency and ownership. ACTED is aware that prevailing gender norms present challenges in ensuring the participation of women in cash for work, nevertheless, the project overall will aim to target an equal amount of women beneficiaries than men. In the past, ACTED has experienced low utilization of CRM in mobile responses. This will be mitigated through clear and consistent communication with community leadership and wider community members about the CRM, as well as establishment of a community based complaints mechanism (CBCM).

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with a dedicated Accountability Officer based in Juba that will set up a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) in WBeG, and oversee reporting for the project to ensure beneficiaries can objectively report (including verbally) complaints and feedback. Standardized CRM tools, including set SOPs and sensitivity levels, as well as the training of project staff on collecting and responding to complaints, and sensitization of the community in accountability practice will be utilized. All complaints will be compiled and reported on each month and the Accountability Officer will ensure that follow up is conducted by the relevant people. The CRM will also ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between ACTED and the community, and improve the quality of our programmes. For mobile responses, ACTED will initiate bi-monthly feedback sessions with the community, in which they will be able to provide feedback. ACTED will ensure that most vulnerable individuals will have access to these sessions. Furthermore, feedback sessions will be arranged in coordination with leadership groups including women and youth groups to ensure broad participation. Overall, ACTED will ensure to collaborate with protection partners, to ensure that the most vulnerable community members will have access to feedback. Following all interventions, ACTED will conduct rapid monitoring or post-distribution monitoring to gather more direct beneficiary feedback. These monitorings will include specifically targeting vulnerable community members such as those living with disabilities to ensure that their feedback is received.
