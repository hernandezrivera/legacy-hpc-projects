Project Summary

The Western Zone and North Western Zones of Tigray are amongst the most affected by the long-standing conflict starting from November 2021. The war has decimated the livelihoods of the people of Tigray. The Western Zone of Tigray is still under the control of these forces. New internally displaced persons have arrived at North West Tigray Zone from Western Zone amid the new developments of Tigray Defense Force (TDF) to neighboring regions on July, 2021. Thus, a total 270,513 (49,030 households) IDPs are currently living in Sheraro. Most of the IDPs are new and not addressed so far by humanitarian partners and the IDP centers upholds largest vulnerable women ( including pregnant and lactating) (27.51%), adult girls (4.7%), under five children (12.96%), and elders (2.19%) who needs care. According to the IOM DTM-8 data NFIs continues to be the major challenge in the IDP sites and IDPs. This implies humanitarian crisis in Tigray region is extremely concerning as the humanitarian partners are not able to provide emergency assistance due to siege, limited operational capacities. Besides, due to relative access and safety they were mainly focused at accessible areas like Mekelle and Shire towns. Though some actors are moving to address the emergency needs, however the humanitarian assistance in remote areas like Sheraro is still very limited in Tigray region.
Therefore, this project is proposed to intervene in providing NFIs humanitarian aid to IDPs that have not been previously addressed by humanitarian partners as the results of the humanitarian blockade, limited aid and cash capacities. This project will provide ESNFI (in-kind) and S&S NFIs from IOM to 2220 and 49030 conflict affected IDPs in North West Zone of Tigray Region.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1: Provision of in-kind or cash for emergency shelter assistance for physical protection and to reduce overcrowdingTo improve access to lifesaving shelters and NFIs for displacement affected populations with a focus on the most vulnerable and at-risk population group to safeguard their health security, privacy, and dignity in-kind or cash for emergency shelter assistance will be provided. After the procurement and delivery of emergency shelter items and or cash for emergency shelter assistance from IOM/RRF, a total of 10,986 beneficiaries will be assisted to improve physical protection and reduce the consequences of health risks. Activity 2: Provision of Emergency Shelter Repair Kit to HHs whose houses are completely damaged that considers the needs of women, children, people with disabilities, and the safety of beneficiaries.Most of the IDPs are living in host communities and open spaces with high privacy problems, especially for women, girls, and people with disabilities. To reduce the consequence of health-related and privacy-related problems, TDA will provide Emergency Shelter Repair Kit to HHs whose houses are completely damaged that consider the needs of women, children, people with disabilities, and the safety of 10,960 beneficiaries. Thus, training and technical support for beneficiaries in constructing emergency shelters and repair kits, through constructing two demonstration shelters. To contribute to improving protection outcomes by ensuring the conflict/displacement affected people have adequate safety, dignity, well-being, and equitable access to shelter solutions, the following activities are planned.Activity 1: Provision of Emergency Shelter and NFI that consider the needs of women, children, and people with disabilities and improve the beneficiaries safety and security. The IDPs have no access to shelter and NFI the health and protection outcomes of the IDPs are deteriorating. Therefore, emergency shelter and NFI assistance will be provided in-kind for 18,938 affected populations to improve their health and protection outcomes. Activity 2: Provision of in-kind or cash for emergency shelter assistance for physical protection and to reduce overcrowding.To improve physical protection, delivery of in-kind (from IOM/RRF) cash for emergency shelter assistance for 2,179 IDP populations to project areas for emergency shelter assistance to improve physical protection and to reduce the consequences of health risks. Activity 3: Provision of ESNFIs to the persons with disabilities, older people, and persons with chronic illnessPersons with disabilities, older people, and persons with chronic illness are the most affected populations. Therefore, ESNFIs will be provided to 2,841 most vulnerable populations (persons with disabilities, older people, and persons with chronic illness) covered by Disability and Inclusion NFI Kits to create access to appropriate emergency shelter assistanceActivity 4: Provision of Non-food items to reduce the likelihood of health and protection consequencesMost of the displacement-affected populations did not have access to the most needed NFI. Therefore, provision of NFI to 21,894 most vulnerable or at-risk populations that improve their safety through delivery of in-kind (from IOM/RRF) non-food item (NFI) kits for the IDPs by TDA from Mekelle to the project areas.Activity 5: Provision of NFIs kits to the persons with disabilities, older people, and persons with chronic illnessSimilar to the ESNFI distribution, most essential NFI kits will be distributed to 5, 264 persons with disabilities, older people, and persons with chronic illnesses to improve their health and protection outcomes.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

It is obvious that the implementation and success of a project by its nature depends on collaboration and partnership with key stakeholders. Community participation takes the lionshare, especially in interventions like Emergency shelter and NFIs provision. The principles of TDA are also in line with IOM that integrate community participation and engagement in all phases of its project implementation. Part this, to effectively implement the Emergency Shelter and NFIs project, active community participation during the implementation processes will follow to ensure affected communities will have a leading voice in influencing and shaping the design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation.Thus, all social groups including children, elders, women, men, and boys will be included. Particular attention will be given to the participation of the most marginalized and vulnerable groups in order to adequately identify the most sensitive and effective strategies best suited to their context and needs.Besides, formal and informal institutions that will contribute to and influence the success and implementation of the project will get involved during the project implementation. Thus, local administrative bodies, church leaders, local leaders, women, and social affairs, and other international institutions that engage in Emergency Shelter and Non-Food items provision will be consulted in all phases of project intervention to avoid duplication, and conflict of interest, sequencing, and layering. Both the community and relevant partners' participation will enable them to contribute during the project implementation, especially during targeting (selection of the needy persons), distribution of ESNFIs, and construction of shelters for disabled persons. Besides, TDA is a member of the Alliance for Civil Society Organization in Tigray (ACSOT). Therefore, TDA will collaborate with the CSOs working on the ground. TDA will also collaborate with NFIs Management Committee (RC), Woreda Disaster Risk Management Office (WODRMO), Woreda Health Office, Woreda Officials, Kebele Disaster Risk Management Committee, and Community leaders will participate during all project implementation phases. Engage in prioritizing and selecting of disadvantaged groups that need NFIs support based on the level of vulnerability. The camp managers will be actively engaged during the project implementation at all phases.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

This project will provide priority to respond the needs of elders who need care, children household heads, disabled and people with special needs in consultation with and active involvement local stakeholders who are knowledgeable about the emergency needs (Site managers, local administrative bodies, representative of the vulnerable groups, social affairs committee and staff members at all levels). The needs of the groups are identified using needs assessment using different techniques. TDA will consider social-inclusion of vulnerable communities at its priority. TDA will act for any types of exploitation, mis-use of resources by partners and or project staff members during project implementation. TDA/IOM has designed its interventions to minimize risks of SGBV, especially for women and girls:Choices allow for dignity and reduce SGBV risks: Mechanisms that will ensure women, girls, children that can make choices to their emergency needs with dignity thus decreasing the likelihood that they will resort to negative coping mechanisms and reducing their exposure to SGBV and other risks like exploitations. Community consultations: will be organized with affected communities to propose measures to mitigate potential negative impacts of the shelter and NFIs assistance including SGBV-related and other protection risks.Community consultations and a safety audit will serve to identify protection risks and strategies to mitigate them for all project activities, raise awareness on PSEA and GBV, and ensure that feedback and response mechanisms are accessible by all community segments.PSEA training for key emergency responders including all stakeholders including community members engaged in targeting or response activities will serve to minimize or prevent SGBVPost distribution monitoring will include monitoring and reporting on protection risks and necessary mitigation measures associated with shelter and non-food items distribution. This will also enable the project to determine how the intervention is implemented whether the beneficiaries were able to meet their emergence needs, capture beneficiary’s satisfactions and document lessons for subsequent interventions. This process will allow for the receipt of valuable feedback from beneficiaries and enable the project staff to revise its implementation modalities where necessary. Grievance mechanisms: TDA/IOM will work with partners to establish complaint hearing committees at the IDP center and processes for reporting grievances.
