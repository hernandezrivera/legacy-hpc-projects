Project Summary

The purpose of the proposed intervention is to respond to continuing health needs in camp and non-camp locations, challenged with protection concerns. IMC will provide an integrated package of services spanning primary health including basic reproductive health, mental health and psychosocial support, community health and incorporating appropriate GBV prevention and response activities. Services provided under the proposed intervention will address specific needs of women, men, girls, and boys, including older persons and persons with disabilities. The proposed program will add on training and capacity-building support across sectors especially to existing government sectoral facilities to ensure appropriate and standardized service provision. All phases of the intervention will rely on close coordination and consultation with affected populations, as well as key stakeholders, including national and sub-national clusters, Directorates of Health, and relevant line ministries. The target beneficiaries will mainly be IDPs, host and returnee populations in areas of priority that continue to experience inadequate basic services, overwhelming existing resources and are yet to experience relatively stable security.
IMC continues to participate within the health cluster as well as conduct bilateral discussions with respective DOH’s to monitor ongoing interventions as part of its exit strategy. Eventual hand-over to IMC’s local counterparts is the exit strategy, thus the continuing dialogue with the relevant stakeholders. The project builds upon the capacities among the staff who have previously participated in IMC’s trainings and programming as well as those affiliated with government agencies such as the DoH.

Needs

As of November 2018 an estimated four million people internally displaced persons (IDPs) had returned home in Iraq; almost half of returns occurred in 2017, compared with only 18% this year. An estimated 71% of IDPs reside outside of camps and the severity hotspots are concentrated in five northern governorates: Ninewa, Salah-Al-din, Kirkuk, Diyala, and Anbar. In the Displacement Tracking Matrix produced by the IOM, approximately 1.5 million returnees are in Ninewa, 1.2 million returnees are in Anbar, and just over half a million returnees are in Salah Al-Din. According to UNHCR in September 2018, many IDPs report repeated displacement due to military operations, financial difficulties and dearth of livelihood opportunities, or unsuccessful attempts to return to their area of origin due to lack of services. Others have faced protection challenges and experienced threats, or have been denied return over perceived affiliation with extremists or other forms of collective punishment in their area of origin. Among the most vulnerable are female-headed households who have reported moving to camps after incidents of sexual harassment in non-camp settings. UNOCHA. Activities

Activity 1. Provision of quality primary healthcare and basic RH services in camp and out-of-camp settlements through support government PHCC’s. IMC proposes to assist in sustaining health care in areas where the DOH/MOH have yet to have the ability to take over. Depending on continuing assessments assistance may be through the supply of essential medicines, equipment and medical supplies, provision of incentives to essential PHC staff, minor rehabilitation works including but not limited to replacing broken doors and windows, purchasing of essential furniture and ensuring a stable power and water supply. IMC will ensure that the PHCCs are staffed with a minimum of two Doctors (1 male and 1 female), four nurses, one pharmacist, one pharmacist assistant, one PHC focal person, one cleaner and three guards and other support staff. The staff structure will vary according to the size and capacity of the health facility and its services.IMC will coordinate and regularly dialogue with the relevant DoH to monitor and agree towards an eventual transition to full DoH support. Clients identified by the CHWs as in need of health care services will be referred to health facilities to access the necessary services. According to their needs, the clients will either be treated at the static PHCCs or referred for secondary or specialized healthcare service facilities. IMC will assess the capacity of local PHCs and hospital services and will coordinate to layout the referral pathways for patients from o the PHCCs or hospitals. Children under the age of five and pregnant and lactating women will be referred for routine vaccination services provided by the DoH. Patients with emergency medical conditions, including deliveries, will also be referred to either main primary healthcare centres or hospitals. IMC anticipates that ambulance services will be supported through the DoH.Activity 2 Contribute to the improvement of health services through capacity building and supportive supervision of health personnel and outreach workersIMC proposes to build the capacity of health personnel and the CHWs through a two-folded approach of competency-based technical trainings and on-the job supportive supervision. This is expected to facilitate the continuous improvement in the performance of the staff and CHWs, and will encourage a participatory capacity-building approach. The supportive supervisions will focus on monitoring the performance towards set goals and using regular two-communication streams for follow-up with staff to ensure that new tasks are being implemented correctly. Health workers, trainers and supervisors will work together to solve problems and improve performance to deliver an enhanced health package.Health personnel will be trained on the delivery of improved health services and training topics will include basic life support, management of communicable diseases (ARI, AWD, Skin conditions) and common morbidities such as hypertension and diabetes.Staff will also be trained on EWARS while Sexual and RH trainings will be guided by the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) and will include family planning and the syndromic management of STIs. MHPSS trainings will include case management, PFA, and the identification and referral of MH patients as per MH-Gap. The trainings and related service package will be focused and tailored to the priority conditions identified in the areas of intervention. IMC further proposes to include pharmaceutical supply chain management trainings and clinical management of rape training to enhance the capacity of clinical staff to respond to the specific needs of women and girl survivors or at risk of GBV. CHWs recruited from within the catchment areas of the service points play an important and crucial bridge between patients/beneficiaries and the supported PHCCs. IMC will continue to train the CHWs based on IMC's regional training curriculum for health education which includes setting up roles and responsibilities, and topics covering RH (ANC, PNC, family planning), childhood diseases, IYCF and nutrition, communicable diseases, chronic non-communicable disease and hygiene promotion.

Establish strong referral networks whereby vulnerable beneficiaries especially women and girls, PWD’s and older persons are provided with information on available resources and most times, referred through a network of community mobilizers and outreach workers3. Establish good relations with local authorities as well as traditional leaders in the area who may have influence on access of services by other ethnic and sectarian groups. How is your project accountable to affected population

IMC involves beneficiaries and local stakeholders in the design phases of its interventions, conducting key informant interviews with IDP and host community populations, PHCC and emergency camp managers, local officials and DoH staff. In addition, CHWs perform need-based visits to understand IDP needs/concerns in target locations and identify prevailing health risks and vulnerabilities. Information gathered by the CHWs is then used by the health facilities and community health team to guide the actual service delivery. Furthermore, IMC conducts patient or client satisfaction surveys in the target locations to receive feedback from beneficiaries on IMC service provision, including Health, MHPSS, and GBV.IMC makes efforts to hire local staff providers for service delivery, which allows for a better understanding of the local operational context, facilitates deployment in a very fluid security environment, and facilitates the movement of staff thorough the checkpoints. IMC also recruits CHWs from among the local communities, who are then involved in mapping out vulnerable individuals, such as pregnant women, older persons, people with disabilities, and people with chronic diseases - and provide targeted support and follow-up.IMC is already coordinating with MoH – PHC department and the MH advisor to select PHCC and training participants coordinating closely with DoH personnel, as well as NGOs, UN Agencies and camp management authorities. Trainings will include PFA, mhGAP-HIG, detection and referral among other topics in order to improve the response mechanisms for IDPs who recently suffered exposure to serious stressors such as displacement and traumatic events. IMC coordinates with DoH and local authorities to select PHCCs for support and information from clusters, local communities and officials, based on new displacements and emergencies to help identify and involve newly vulnerable populations in the action.In 2018, IMC will continue the rollout of community-based feedback mechanisms in all missions, including Iraq, as part of the agency's commitment to further strengthening involvement of beneficiaries in all interventions.Further, IMC encourages beneficiaries to utilize the IDP Call Center, participate in RPAs, monitoring missions including RNAs and Communicating with Communities (CwC) that facilitates dialogue between IMC and the intended beneficiaries among other mechanisms.
