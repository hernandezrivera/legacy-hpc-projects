Project Summary

This integrated CP programme addresses immediate risks and longer-term effects families from IDP, and host communities have endured in Cabo Delgado. The programme will provide case management, work with Community Based Child Protection Committees, provide MHPSS activities, train alternative carers, run income generating activities, FTR, and specifically support GBV survivors.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Street Child will continue its integrated Child Protection programming into 2022, addressing the immediate risks and longer-term effects that the conflict and displacement have had. By running parallel activities the project aims to foster a safe environment for all children and families, from both the IDP and host communities, the key beneficiary groups will be children including SGBV survivors, UASC, and CAAFAG, carers and alternative carers, community based child protection committees.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project’s key activities across Chiúre, Ancuabe, Montepuez, Balama, Meluco will be:• Provision of emergency community based alternative care for UASC• Identification and documentation of UASC• Reunification of UASC with their families or placement in long-term community-based care• Supporting children to receive case management and supporting services• Trainings of community based protection committees, with focal point established• Provision of material and technical support to community based protection mechanisms • Provision of age and gender appropriate MHPSS activities• Activities to mitigate and prevent risks to children including SGBV implemented other sectors• Establishing and supporting community-based child protection structures• Proving vulnerable families with training and start up grants to set up small businesses • Establishing of Integrated protection service in safe spaces, including referral mechanisms• Upscaling capacity to and providing case management to GBV cases • Training case workers on safe and ethical management of GBV data • Provision of material support for GBV survivors • Provide psychosocial support to GBV survivors• Implementing community based protection activities• Establishing community based protection structures (including trainings and material support)• Protection monitoring activities conducted through site visits, needs assessments and risk analysis• Capacity building in child protection and MHPSS, organisation structure, and fundraising for national partners

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

All staff working on this project, including implementing partners, are required to have received training on safeguarding and child protection and adhere to organisation policy prior to conducting any field visits or contact with beneficiaries. In turn beneficiaries will have access to a complaints and feedback mechanism, with the promotion of Linha Verde. Street Child has extensive experience of delivering child protection in emergency projects, working in protracted crises through operations in DRC, Afghanistan, Cameroon and North East Nigeria. IGA, MHPSS and case manager facilitators will be trained to a high standard, and align with the do no harm principle, under which all activities will be designed. The project activities will be delivered through coordination with community leaders and engagement with community ensuring transparency and accountability, as well as to ensure the activities are context appropriate. Should the monitoring or feedback mechanisms demonstrate a change in risk, or need to review certain activities, the project team will work with local authorities and community leaders to ensure it is providing meaningful services to as wide a range of people as possible. All project staff will be trained on PSEA, understand their role and position as humanitarian actors, and be familiar with Street Child’s PSEA policy. The project will have a central PSEA focal point, to which all PSEA allegations will be brought to, from each organisation’s and district’s PSEA focal points. A clear line of communication will be established so that every member of staff knows who to bring concerns to confidentially. PSEA will be mainstreamed into all partner training, who will then cascade this down to communities. The project team will continuously stress the importance of preventing sexual exploitation and abuse, and remind beneficiaries and communities of their rights, and ability to report through project reporting mechanisms, with other organisations, or through Linha Verde. Should a PSEA case arise the project team will work hand in hand with the PSEA WG, following their reporting mechanisms, as well as with Street Child’s internal PSEA policies and structures, culminating in a PSEA focal point at Headquarters. All cases will be dealt with the utmost confidentiality and respect, and be followed through until the end, with the person in question being kept updated regularly, and given the support they require.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

2022 project activities are being adapted based on 2021 feedback, which will be consolidated by an endline engaging a range of the project’s stakeholders. In 2022, initial community engagement will introduce the project in Meluco and Balama, new locations, and we will run an initial rapid assessment in all five districts, with strong child participation on child protection issues. Data from this will be compared to a rapid midline, to ascertain the project’s impact and areas for improvement. Baseline assessments will include a review of available service and referral mechanisms in the areas, and community leaders and key community members will be consulted to validate the vulnerability criteria for beneficiary selection. Feedback is collected on a rolling basis, and next year each community will identify at least one preferred complaints and reporting mechanism in each project area, such face to face reporting, or a complaints box that will be checked weekly. The project team will continuously stress the importance of community feedback to improve the project, so as to encourage honest dialogue. The feedback received will be regularly reviewed and acted upon where possible, with feedback always given back to the community to ensure continued participation in feedback mechanisms. All activities will be developed to be age and gender appropriate, and ensure girls have women to approach, to increase their participation and promoting discussion on issues specifically affecting them such as teen pregnancy and early marriage. This project will provide equitable access to PSS sessions for all children, and facilitators will be trained on how to spot GBV warning signs, support survivors and who to refer them to. Case management services will specifically target those children affected by GBV, child headed households and UASC. IGA’s will be targeted towards primary female caregivers, assisting with empowering independence, reducing reliance on other community members and organisations, increased food security and improving overall wellbeing of children in the household, in turn reducing risks of the use of negative coping strategies. Gender will also be a consideration when training alternative carers, ensuring girls could be placed with female carers. CBCPC’s will have female and child representation, whose meetings will be run in a child safe manner, and will be trained on gender related issues. Finally all assessments will be sensitive to age and gender, so as to encourage the collection of a wide variety of feedback.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Children and caregivers with disabilities will be identified through baseline assessments, engagement with district departments and key communities members. The baseline will be developed modelled on the Washington group tool, to ensure that necessary data to captured for equitable access to support. All beneficiaries will be selected through a vulnerability criteria ensuring that the most vulnerable and marginalised are targeted as part of this project. Messaging on child protection will include the importance of inclusion of all children, and complementary Education projects Street Child will run will advocate for the importance of inclusion of children with disabilities in education. MHPSS activities will also be inclusive to children of all physical and emotional abilities, and we will work with disability centric organisations to mainstream disability into our training, and refer specific cases if needed.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

In the short term caregivers receiving an IGA grant will be encouraged to start up viable businesses that are not detrimental to the environment, such as coal and firewood. Business training will also focus on incorporating disaster risk reduction strategies into development of business plans, and the promotion of resilience and sustainability, as well as proper disposal of single use materials, and commercial waste. In the long term, improved household income impacts on positive child wellbeing and access and retention within education. This in turn increase opportunities of the child and increases chances of breaking the generational poverty cycle, reducing child exposure to negative environmental hazards and educating households on reducing environmental impacts, such as polluted water and risks caused by natural disasters.
