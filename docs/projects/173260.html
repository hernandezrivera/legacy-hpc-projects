Project Summary

Tdh aims at improving safe and dignified access to quality Child Protection and MHPSS services addressing the needs of Rohingya and host community girls, boys, men and women. The project will be implemented in Camps 26, 27 and the adjacent host communities of Teknaf Nhila Upazila. The mutually-reinforcing services will be provided along the layers of a socio-ecological model within Tdh Multi Purpose Community Center. It will include provision of individual Case Management services and provision of structured MHPSS tailored to the needs and interests of girls, boys and adolescents. Additionally, recognizing the importance of nurturing family environments, Tdh will be providing family support and strengthening services and PM+ counselling for female and male caregivers. The community-based protective environments will be supported through integrated community outreach messaging, incorporating Child Protection, Health, WASH and Nutrition. The CP mainstreaming will further be enhanced through continuation of semi-structured MHPSS activities at the Child Play Area within Tdh Primary Health Center in Teknaf. The barriers which prevent adolescent girls, adolescent boys and children with different abilities from accessing the services will be assessed through community-based FGDs and acted upon accordingly. Moreover, recognizing the importance of peaceful co-existence, Tdh will capacitate and empower adolescents and young men and women of Rohingya and Teknaf host communities in peaceful conflict resolution and implementation of youth-led initiatives for a peaceful co-existence.
Tdh will continue its sensitization regarding safety measures during natural disasters, including measures to prevent family separations as well as the activation of LCCMPs and/or ECCCs, PERU teams, and implementation of COVID-19 safe practices.
Tdh already has well established internal mechanism to prevent and respond to PSEA and child safeguarding through local and international PSEA focal points and safe and confidential reporting channels.
Tdh will also continue its ongoing inter-agency representation at and contributions to the CPSS and CMTF.

1 - Identified Needs

Within the fourth year of Rohingya refugee crisis, following their forced displacement from Myanmar and establishment within Cox`s Bazar district, the tension between the host community and Rohingya population is increasing. The key drivers of tension also have a negative impact on intra-community relations leading community members to resort to violent conflict resolution measures. Both Rohingya and host community manifest deeply rooted socio-cultural norms and traditional practices that disproportionately discriminate against women, girls and marginalized community members preventing their access to services and resources. Those discriminatory norms and practices promote unequal power relationship which prevent women and girls from actively contributing to the wellbeing of their families and communities. Harmful traditional practices such as early marriage and a commonly accepted social norm of domestic violence are on the rise in both communities due to the lack of livelihood opportunities, overstretched resources and support systems being further exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic. Men are power holders in key decision making at the household level in both communities and both boys and girls are particularly vulnerable to protection risks due to a strong stigma attached to disclosing abusive experiences and a lack of tailored servicesThose structural factors have negative implications on the safety and wellbeing of children, adolescents and young people as the violent measures of conflict resolution are further perpetuated at the household level. Evidence from the Joint Child Protection Sub-sector assessment 2020 indicates that 85% of adolescents aged 15-20 and 94% of caregivers interviewed stated that parents can discipline their children with violence, 79% of adolescents and 84% of caregivers said that problems can be solved with violence. Adolescents and youths from the Rohingya and host communities are particularly vulnerable groups having limited educational and livelihood opportunities. Rohingya female adolescents, due to numerous threats to their safety and the need to negotiate intra-household decision making channels for accessing services, often prefer staying indoors, participating in very few interactions outside their shelters. Although Rohingya male adolescents report being freer to move in and out of their shelters, they report high levels of risk of exploitation (including trafficking, kidnapping, going missing), physical abuse, illegal activities, substance abuse, imprisonment, physical injuries while performing heavy works. The results of the assessments show that 3% of Camp 26 households reported having a child working in hazardous conditions. 68% of host community respondents knew of children who were involved in dangerous work. In terms of early marriage and other forms of GBV, 24% of the host community respondents reported knowing married children in the community. “Kidnapping” (presumably for trafficking) was ranked as the highest risk affecting girls and boys in both camps and highlighted as a top concern in the host community. 8% of the host community respondents identified violence in the community as a main source of harm to children. Men and boys in FGDs reported adult-to-child physical abuse, bullying and inter-peer physical violence. In parallel, violence at home was highlighted by women and girls as highly prevalent. Importantly, children with disabilities were identified as the most vulnerable group in the host community assessment. As for protective factors, child protection community mechanisms were assessed as weak in the host community. Parents were reported to be the main source of protection for children. In the camps, 51% households reported knowing the community protection groups in their area, indicating greater reliance on community-based mechanisms in the camps than in the host communities. Finally, one of the main gaps cited by host community respondents was lack of access

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Implementation of community outreach activities focused on Child Protection objective, integrated with Health, Nutrition and WASH components, by Tdh multi-skilled teams Activity 2: Organization of Focus Group Discussions about GBV, actively involving men and boys, to mitigate GBV risk factors and to ensure sustainable social cohesion. Activity 3: Facilitation and implementation of youth-led community-based initiatives by Rohingya refugee and Bangladeshi youth for the purpose of enhanced youth participation and social cohesion. Activity 4: Provision of family support and strengthening services to female and male parents and caregivers will be done within the premises of Tdh's Teknaf Multi Purpose Community Center as well as through home-to-home visits during COVID-19 critical response phase. Activity 5: Provision of structured MHPSS activities to Rohingya refugee and host community boys, girls, adolescent boys and adolescent girls will be done within the premises of Tdh's Multi Purpose Community Center in Teknaf as well as through home-to-home visits during COVID-19 critical response phase. The MHPSS activities will utilize an enhanced community outreach strategy to reach out to, mobilize and retain the most difficult to reach children and adolescents, including children living with disabilities, working children, adolescent girls as well as those coming from disadvantaged socio-economic backgrounds. Activity 6: Provision of one-on-one counselling based on Problem Management Plus (PM+) methodology. Activity 7: Provision of specialized age and gender sensitive Case Management services to the most vulnerable and at-risk boys and girls will be done in accordance with the principles of the Best Interest of the Child and Do No Harm to avoid causing any physical or psychological harm to child and adolescent beneficiaries while protecting and promoting their safety, wellbeing and healthy development. Activity 8: Accountability to Affected Populations (cross-cutting): Tdh follows an inclusive and iterative approach with a user-centric design aiming to integrate the feedback of child and adult beneficiaries. The beneficiaries will be consulted on what feedback mechanisms are most appropriate and safe for them so they feel comfortable and safe providing feedback to Tdh for informing the programming on a regular basis. Activity 9: Localization: The project will jointly be implemented with a local partner incorporating both programmatic and institutional capacity building. This process will include robust capacity assessments, provision of formal trainings, regular technical supervision and on-the-job coaching as well as exchanges of best practices and lessons learnt. The ultimate goal of the partnership is to facilitate a gradual handover of Tdh Case Management, MHPSS, community-based programming and management of the Multi-Purpose Community Center to the partner.
