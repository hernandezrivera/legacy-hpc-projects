Project Summary

IRC will deliver multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA) to vulnerable conflict affected communities in Al Hasakeh, Ar Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor governorates in Northeast Syria. The IRC will support the survival needs of 27,660 individuals (4,610 households) through distribution of MPCA. This includes one-time multipurpose cash transfers to 1,842 newly-displaced households (11,052 individuals) as well as multi-round transfers to 16,608 highly vulnerable IDPs, host community and returnees (2,768 households) to be provided in cases of extreme levels of vulnerability. One-time assistance will provide a rapid injection of support to IDPs and recent returnee households cope with sudden financial shocks such as new displacement or depleted resources due to protracted or multiple displacements, disruption to livelihoods and spike in prices of essential services and commodities. To address cases where households are unable to meet their survival needs, the IRC has budgeted for multi-round transfers. Target locations will be selected in coordination with the CWG. Eligible households will be selected either using the IRC's harmonized socioeconomic vulnerability criteria or through a blanket approach based on the severity of need, high uniformity of need in the community, and gaps in assistance by other actors, such as in cases of spontaneous returns or rapid displacements.

4 - Needs

After over nine years of conflict, with inflated prices and fluctuating exchange rates, Syrian household purchasing power is severely weakened, limiting the capacity to meet survival needs and leading the majority of households to rely on negative coping strategies. This situation is exacerbated by gender inequality and poverty, where many families have lost their savings and productive assets, remain still in debt and for the most vulnerable- fall into a cycle of harmful coping mechanisms due to limited access to both opportunities and resources. IRC baseline data collected from 249 households in the target locations in Raqqa (Raqqa city) and Deir ez-Zor (Jazrat sub-district) in mid-2019 indicates that IDPs returnes and host communities alike in the target areas struggle to meet their survival needs. Only 6% of surveyed households have adequate access to water, as defined by Sphere or national standards, and only 23.3% of households have adequate access to essential WASH NFIs. While the majority of assessed households have their own houses, only 2% of shelters met the technical standards. Moreover, 3.8% and 8.4% of respondents were living with other families or in informal shelters. 22.3% households had a borderline or poor food consumption score. Among new arrivals to Al Hasakeh collective centers Washokani informal camp in late 2019 and early 2020, IRC MPCA registration data indicates widespread usage of negative coping strategies to meet basic food and non-food needs. The IRC anticipates similar levels of need among new IDPs in 2020. IRC market monitoring also indicates the availability of SMEB items and that markets are accessible.

5 - Implementation of activities

IRC will provide 4610 HHs with MPCA in Raqqa, Deir Ez Zor, and Hasakeh governorates: 1,842 HHs with one-off MPCA for new arrival IDPs and 2,768 highly vulnerable HHs (IDP, returnee, host) with 3-month MPCA. Locations: IRC targets locations based on identified gaps in coordination with FSL WG and CWG in order to reach the most socioeconomically vulnerable households in underserved communities. IRC works with HAO to secure necessary approvals and liaises in advance with community leaders to explain the project. Targeting: HHs are selected either 1) based on the IRC socioeconomic vulnerability criteria (age and gender of HoH, usage of NCS, displacement status, shelter, income-expenditure and dependency ratios); or 2) through a blanket approach in cases of high uniformity of severe need, such as new displacement. HHs receive cash transfers equivalent to full survival minimum expenditure basket (SMEB) value for 3 rounds for regular MPCA and one-off transfers for emergency MPCA. The USD-SYP exchange rate is determined 24 hrs before the distribution as all distributions are done in SYP. Decisionmaking on the targeting approach and currency is made in consultation with the CWG and IRC senior management. Payment mechanism: The IRC will use hawalas, as there is no formal, functioning banking system banking system. The IRC has in place Master Service Agreements with 3 hawala companies registered in Erbil that have the capacity to meet the cash transfer program demands. CwC: Selected households are informed about the date and time of distribution 2 days in advance. The IRC also provides information on the multiple available feedback mechanisms at this stage so that community members can raise any concerns. PDM: IRC conducts PDM 2-4 weeks after distribution and includes questions around safety, any possible risks or tensions in the household or community as a result of the assistance, and equitable access to and use of distributed items within the household.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Diversion: IRC shares FARM materials and explains to beneficiaries that they are not expected to provide anything to anyone to benefit from the assistance. IRC closely monitors risk using the FARM and PDM, and any evidence of malfeasance is reported to IRC’s Ethics and Compliance Unit to be investigated and addressed in a prompt, comprehensive and safe manner. The IRC’s reporting system provides employees with multiple channels to report concerns, while consolidating all issues in the internal EthicsPoint hotline and case management system. The IRC’s procurement controls also serve to mitigate risk of diversion: individual supplier due diligence is a required step in a competitive supplier bid and contracting process, and includes anti-terrorism compliance (ATC) checks and checking for debarment. The IRC screens all entities using the LexisNexis Bridger database. Loss of access: IRC proposes to implement directly, with mitigation measures in place for remote management. IRC ensures regular review of contingency plans to mitigate against changes in context, including changes in territorial control and access interruptions. Access staff negotiate with local actors, work closely with program leads to manage risks and engage with local councils to avoid any interference or diversion of aid. IRC has zero tolerance for interference and\or diversion of aid to any parties to the conflict. IRC has a Security Management Plan and Standard Operating Procedures to guide operations and staff to provide lifesaving assistance using the safest possible methods. Exchange rate: Before distributions, IRC conducts a price assessment to determine the exchange rate in target areas. IRC conducts PDM about currency, transfer value and access to SMEB items. IRC recently conducted FGDs to understand currency preferences and risks. IRC has a mechanism in place for agreeing on the exchange rate with hawalas to ensure that HHs receive the appropriate amount on the date of distribution.
