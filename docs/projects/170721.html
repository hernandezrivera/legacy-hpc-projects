Project Summary

This project will repair and rehabilitate 400 light and/or 50 moderately damaged residential units within proximity of the Beirut port explosion through a contractor and agency staff. The focus will be on vulnerable households residing in units categorized under damage level 1 and 2 according to and following the prioritization and geo-split of the Shelter Sector. The action will also repair common areas within affected buildings prioritizing interventions that improve safety and security. The project will integrate HLP, protection mainstreaming, and COVID-19 preventive safety measures at all stages. Multi-sectoral needs will be identified and referred following an extensive service mapping exercise and the response's coordination mechanism.

1 - Needs

The explosion that took place on the 4th of August in Beirut, in which over 200 lives were lost, injuring thousands, and affecting more than 350,000 residents of Beirut could not come at a worse time for Lebanon. The ongoing economic crisis has caused the Lebanese Lira to lose 80% of its value since October 2019 with no end in sight. COVID-19 has made the situation even worse causing many Lebanese to lose part of their income, if not all. With a massive increase in poverty (28 to 55 percent) and the middle class shrinking from 57 percent to 40 percent (2019-2020), the majority of the affected households with the blast are left with no capacity to provide basic needs, let alone repair damages caused by the blast. More than half of the housing units assessed so far have sustained some sort of shelter damage. The vulnerable and the low-income households in Beirut are therefore in dire need of immediate support to provide basic needs, pay bills, and repair their housing units before winter to ensure dignified lives in safe shelters and proper access to water and sanitation.

2 - Activities

The project will: 1) Repair occupied residential units including WASH facilities prioritizing vulnerabilities and specific needs of women, girls, boys and men. This activity will be implemented through a contractor and also through volunteering technicians. Improving accessibility of PWSNs will also be considered whenever the budget allows. The activity will target 400 HHs (816 F, 784 M), estimating 1,500USD per each intervention (to be completed within 6 months). 2) Repair and rehabilitate common areas within residential buildings prioritizing works that improve safety and security. This activity will target 50 residential buildings, estimating 4,500USD per each intervention (to be completed within 6 months). 3) Support targeted households by linking them with service providers through tracked referrals to local and international NGOs after developing an extensive multi-sectorial service mapping throughout the action. This shall include protection, health, and shelter (cash for rent as an example). 4) Support vulnerable households targeted with a one-off Emergency Cash Assistance, following the guidance of the Cash Task Force, after an unsuccessful referral for urgent needs accompanied with an existing vulnerability. This activity will target 250 households (515 F, 485 M), for a value of 300USD (to be completed within 3 months). Integrate HLP and protection mainstreaming throughout the action following the Shelter Sector's TTCs guidelines.
