Project Summary

Grassroots Relief and Development Agency (GREDA) intends to implement sustainable agriculture/fishing local food production and kitchen gardens setup at Nutrition facilities/Schools/Household for sustainable economic recovery and resilience targeting 8,000 poorest vulnerable displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host community members living in 8 Payams in Pibor Administrative Area (PAA). In each Payam 1000 beneficiaries will be targeted most of whom are widows, single mothers, pregnant/lactating mothers, youth, old aged persons and persons with disabilities with out monetary capacity to acquire agricultural/fishing local food production and income generation activities inputs. Pibor Administrative Area is classified in IPC phase 4 and 5 emergency and catastrophic food insecurity levels because it is badly affected by the conflict and insecurity, unprecedentedly flooding between the months of July-October. 2023 and Macroeconomic crisis where purchasing power of local currency depreciated by 650% against US Dollars. This project will prevent total collapse of livelihoods and will strengthen beneficiaries to produce their own local food through rainfall agriculture and irrigation along Pibor River. The agriculture, fishing and kitchen gardens setup at Nutrition facilities/Schools/Household are meant to enhance sustainable local food production to improve beneficiaries households' nutrition for better health, increased income generation for economic recovery and local food security resilience in 8 Payams in Pibor Administrative Area (Boma, Gumuruk, Kiziongora, Lekungule, Maro, Muwun, Pibor and Verteth). In accordance to IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Malnutrition analysis September 2023 - July 2024.
Published on November 6, 2023 the population experiencing catastrophe IPC phase 5 acute food insecurity in Pibor Administrative Area is 11,000 people. This project will strengthen the capacities of 4,800 Agro-dealer beneficiaries formed in 48 groups, 1,600 Youth Fishing beneficiaries formed in 16 groups and 8 kitchen gardens setup at Nutrition facilities/Schools/Household beneficiaries formed in 8 Schools to work in groups to increase their agriculture/fishing local food production to improve their households nutrition, income generation, economic recovery and food security resilience in Pibor Administrative Area; the location classified under IPC phase 4 and 5 at emergency and catastrophic acute food insecurity levels, high levels of illiteracy and poverty.

1. Needs

The high level of acute food insecurity in South Sudan remains worrying; with the most severely acute food insecure populations significantly affected by (1) Climatic shocks. The country is highly prone to natural disasters, particularly flood and dry spells that affect agriculture, disrupt livelihoods and hamper humanitarian relief access (2) Conflict and Insecurity resulting from the sub-national, localized conflicts and spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan disrupting livelihoods and forcing the displacement of people. Multi-sectoral humanitarian relief assistance delivery is severely affected (3) Low agricultural production due to drought and severe rainfall (floods) negatively impacting agricultural production, resulting to high humanitarian food needs (4) Economic decline where South Sudan economy is experiencing macroeconomic crisis caused by depreciation of the local currency and high food prices. Between the periods September to November 2023 there are estimated 5.83 million people (46% of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity, classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (crisis or worse) with 1.64 million people in IPC 4 (emergency). An estimated 35,000 people are classified in IPC phase 5 (catastrophe). These are (1) 3000 people in Duk County and 3000 people in Nyirol County in Jongolei State (2) 15,000 people in Rubkona County in Unity State and 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who came back from Sudan due to ongoing conflict in Sudan. The most food insecure States between September and November 2023 where more than 50% of their population are likely to face Crisis (IPC phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity are Unity State (61%), Jongolei State (59%) Upper Nile State (57%) and Northern Bahr El Gazal State (51%).In the lean season projection period of April to July 2024, the food insecurity situation will deteriorate and an estimated 7.10 million people (56% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 79,000 people likely to be in catastrophe (IPC phase 5) acute food insecurity in Pibor County (11,000 people) of Greater Pibor Administrative Area, Awiel East County (40,000 people) or Northern Bahr El Gazal State and 28,000 South Sudanese returnees from Sudan that will be spread across the Country. During this period; an estimated 2.34 million people are likely to face emergency (IPC phase 4) acute food insecurity. The most food insecure States between April and July 2024 where more than 50% of their populations are likely to face crisis (IPC phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity are Jongolei State (68%), Upper Nile State (67%), Unity State (66%), Lakes State (60%), Warrap State (55%), Central Equatoria State (52%), Northern Bahr El Gazal State (59%) respectively.Thus; coupled with the diminishing funding from the International Communities; the level of severe acute food insecurity in the Country remains high, requiring urgent funding to change from humanitarian dependence to self-production and self-employment through (1) strengthening the capacities of agro-dealers groups, youth fishing groups to increase their agriculture/fishing local food production to improve beneficiaries households' nutrition, increase local products market shares to enhance beneficiaries households' income generations, economic recovery and self-reliance (2) Set up kitchen gardens at Nutrition facilities/schools/household to enhance nutrition among beneficiaries to prevent the total collapse of livelihood in the affected Pibor County with high share of population (11,000 people) under emergency and catastrophe (IPC phases 4 and 5).

2. Project activities

The key response priorities/activities are to implement agriculture/fishing and Kitchen garden set up at Nutrition facilities/Schools/households through the following activities:- 1. Carry out mobilization of agro-dealer groups, youth fishing groups and kitchen garden at nutrition facilities/schools/household in 8 Payams in Pibor Administrative Area (PAA)2. Select 30 beneficiary members out of agro-dealer groups, youth fishing groups and kitchen garden set up at Nutrition facilities/schools/household through training of trainers (ToTs) to become skillful local trainers to continue training their peer group members on modern techniques of agricultural/fishing food production, climatic resilient agriculture appropriate skills and skills of managing small/medium enterprises. The selected beneficiaries for the training of trainers shall at least be secondary school leavers to understand the training in English and to become capable to train their peer group members (1) to transfer climatic resilient agriculture appropriate skills to beneficiaries to design solutions to mitigate the impacts of the climatic change (2) to transfer modern agriculture/fishing local food production techniques/skills to their peer group members at the grassroots communities to increase agriculture/fishing local food productivity enough for households consumption and marketing. The ToTs beneficiaries shall be 50% female and 50% male to promote gender equity in project implementation. The ToTs trainings; will be held three times separately (1) ToTs trainings for Agro-dealer groups (2) ToTs trainings for youth fishing group and (3) ToTs trainings for kitchen garden management to specialize on three project lines. The trained trainers will continue to train and mentors members of the three groups (Agro-dealer group, youth fishing group and kitchen garden at nutrition facilities/schools/household).3. Thirty (30) trained trainers will organize 3 practical field demonstration outreach cascade training/mentoring in each of 8 payams in Pibor Administrative Area to target 600 members of agro-dealer groups, 200 members of youth fishing groups and 16 members of kitchen garden management groups per Payam. The total practical field demonstration outreach cascade training will be 24 calculated as follows: 8 Payams x 3 practical field demonstration outreach cascade training/mentoring = 24 practical field demonstration outreach cascade training/mentoring to be held in 8 Payams.4. Constructing 4 semi-concrete vegetable, fruits and fishing market shades each with the size 8 meters by 16 meters using local construction materials to produce shades for the selling of agriculture/fishing products. These 4 market shades will be constructed in central Payams to allow primary producers and buyers to meet in the center to sell/buy agriculture/fishing products. 5. Procuring agricultural inputs (assorted vegetable/fruits seeds, assorted agricultural tools and irrigation cans), fishing inputs (wooden canoe, fishing nets, hooks fish preservation cold chain) and Kitchen garden inputs ( Shovel or spade,Hand till,Compost,topsoil,Garden hand spade,Vegetable seedlings or seeds,Mulch,Raised garden and others) to be distributed to agro-dealer groups, youth fishing groups and kitchen garden groups to facilitate local food production and local income generation activities. 6. Carrying out proper organization of small/medium enterprises and ensure their functioning in selling and buying local agriculture/fishing products and facilitating effective income generation.7. Carrying out continuous monitoring and evaluations.
