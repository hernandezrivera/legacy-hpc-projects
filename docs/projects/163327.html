Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to improve access to safe drinking water, adequate sanitation and hygiene practices for refugees, IDP, Host communities, Returnees in Mayo Sava, Mayo Tsanaga and Logone and Chari divisions in Far North region of Cameroon. Indeed, since 2014, these populations have been victims of the Boko Haram crisis in Nigeria, which forced them to move to villages borders in Cameroon. This situation has a direct impact on the satisfaction of basic needs, as presented by several surveys and assessments carried out both by the Cameroonian government and the international agencies and organizations (IOM, UNICEF, UNHCR, NRC,..). the project will be implemented both in the community and in schools.
In the communities where refugees live, we will construct and/or rehabilitate water points in accordance with SPHERE standards and national standards. Each water point will be staffed by a management committee (WMC) that will be trained and equipped with tools for the sustainability of the boreholes. Aware that the drudgery of water is usually done by women and children, each WMC will be composed of at least 50% women. For drilling for more than 2 years ago, NRC will monitor water quality through physical and bacteriological analysis. Households will also be trained on home water treatment and will receive potabilization kits. In each locality, two hygiene promoters (one man and one woman) will be identified and trained on community hygiene promotion. Through home visits, they will raise awareness on good hygiene and sanitation practices. As a result of participatory profiling vulnerable households will benefit from hygiene kits (bucket, soap, ...) while women and girls on reproductive age will receive menstrual hygiene kits consisting of washable sanitary towels, buckets and soap. These will first be trained on the use and maintenance of the kits.
In schools, in addition to the construction and rehabilitation of water points, NRC will build gender-sensitive school latrines equipped with hand washing equipment and maintenance equipment. Under the supervision of teachers, school hygiene club (SHC) made up of 50% of girls will be set up and trained on hygiene promotion in schools. These SHC will develop action plans that will be supported by the project.
Ultimately, the project aims to reach about 27,280 beneficiaries (i.e. 51% women). Approximately 16,891 children under the age of 18 will benefit from the project's actions, compared to 819 elderly (more than 59 years old).

Besoins / Needs

As in recent years, the conflict in Lake Chad has repercussions on the movement of populations in the far north of Cameroon.According to the DTM Round 19 report:-The most affected departments are those of the Logone and Chari (45%), the Mayo Sava (28%) and the Mayo Tsanaga (21%)- Out of camp refugee repressent 11% of the displaced people in the region. In the WaSH sector, despite all the interventions by actors in the region, gaps exists for satisfaction of internally displaced populations, returnees and host communities in access to drinking water infrastructures,Sanitation and hygiene promotion. Indeed the evaluation of sectoral needs in the 2019 for the WaSH cluster highlights among other things the following information: -The priority departments are those of the Logone and Chari,the Mayo Sava and the Mayo Tsanaga; - Access to drinking water in the North Region is about 56,5%- 22% of the far north population pratice open air defecation;- In 2020, we estimated that a total of 1.6 million people (25% adult women, 51% of children, mostly girls, 4% of elderly men and women) will be in need of assistance for water, hygiene and sanitation (EHA) services in 8 of the 10 regions Cameroon including The Far North region;In addition, since 2018, cholera outbreak has been recorded in North and Far North regions. The situation report on november 20th, 2019 (Ministry of public health) present 347 suspicious cases, 12 confirmed cases and 16 deaths in fart North region. This epidemic affects all the status of displacement and the average age of the affected persons is 12 years distributed equitably between the two sexes.
