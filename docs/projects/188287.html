Project Summary

In Aleppo and Idleb governorates, food production has faltered, leaving 3.5 million people food insecure in a region that once had rich farmland and a robust agriculture sector.
Global Communities (GC) has designed the Assistance to Lives Across Syria (ATLAS) program with the goal of addressing urgent humanitarian needs for vulnerable, conflict-affected individuals in northwest Syria (NWS). This goal directly aligns with GC’s mission to save lives, alleviate human suffering, and reduce the physical, social, and economic impact of disasters through integrated multi-sector responses that are harmonized and coordinated with other actors in the field.
If food insecurity and market deficiencies in critical value chains are decreased through food assistance, agriculture, and early recovery and market systems support; then the ability of affected populations in Syria to respond to shocks will improve and their suffering will reduce.
To increase food security for an estimated 100,000 individuals through food (cash and voucher), free bread distribution and agricultural assistance and improvements in the wheat value chain through market strengthening by the end of the 12-month program.
ATLAS will achieve its purposes through activities within the following sectors and subsectors:
• Food Assistance – Unconditional food assistance
• Agriculture – Improving agricultural production and livestock support
While drawing on evidence generated from prior programming, ATLAS is flexible to be responsive to the ever-changing situation in Syria, including Corona Virus Disease 2019 (COVID-19) contingencies and supporting recovery from the burdens exacerbated by the pandemic. ATLAS features several mechanisms to ensure strong accountability to affected populations (AAP) and mainstreams Safe Programming best practices throughout, and GC remains committed to the safety and security of GC and partner staff and of beneficiaries. ATLAS program will deliver lifesaving food assistance to around 13,500 vulnerable households (67,500 individuals) to increase food consumption, expand nutrition diversity and improve HH resilience. We will target HHs who have been recently displaced, defined as those displaced within the last one to two weeks, For the recently displaced, emergency food assistance is a critical need while HHs navigate their new environments and assess their available resources. For HHs which are relatively more settled, access to a reliable source of food not only helps them meet their nutrition needs, but it will also help stabilize the HH economy and empower them to better plan and manage their assets. ATLAS program will deliver Wheat agriculture kits to around 1,600 vulnerable farmers (9,600 individuals) to enhance wheat production, expand the livelihood opportunities and improve HH resilience.
The IP will target HHs who have access to land and have an agricultural background. For the host communities, wheat agriculture kits will reduce the high production costs, especially with the increase in agricultural inputs prices while HHs suffering their new critical season during the economic crisis, lack of precipitation rates, and to maintain their available resources.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), Syria remains a complex humanitarian emergency characterized by over 10 years of ongoing hostilities and their long-term consequences including widespread destruction and the largest number of internally displaced people in the world. In addition to protection threats, 14.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. In addition to the loss of lives and human capital witnessed as a result of a decade of war, The substantial harvest losses combined with chronic economic deprivation have contributed to the mounting food insecurity as 12.8 million people, or almost 60 percent of the population, are food insecure-a 57% increase since 2019 and the highest number ever recorded in the history of Syria. In line with the rise in food insecurity, malnutrition levels have increased. Compared to 2020, stunting increased by 4%, severe acute malnutrition increased by 29% and moderate acute malnutrition increased by 45%.Reduced food and agricultural production, as well as rising food costs and ongoing population movements, have all contributed to wide-scale food insecurity after 10 years of the conflict and are being further negatively impacted by COVID-19 measures. Similarly, subsidized bread prices have continued to increase throughout 2021,while both size and quality of bread have decreased. Commercial bread prices have catapulted, an increase of more than 300% since Nov last year.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Consistent with GC’s standard approach to humanitarian programming, ATLAS is based on the “Do No Harm” principle, with activities designed to be culturally appropriate and tailored to the specific context, while preventing competition over resources. In settings where humanitarian needs are extreme and resources are scarce, GC recognizes that providing material assistance has the potential to cause conflict or to negatively impact local socio-economic conditions. As such, GC will make every effort to mitigate the potential for conflict stemming from the introduction of program resources by maintaining a conflict-sensitive approach to implementation. For example, we will clearly communicate the nature of interventions, beneficiary eligibility, and prioritization criteria to individuals, HHs, community, and municipal leaders, and other stakeholders, to promote transparency and ensure that all stakeholders understand how and why GC is selecting individuals and HHs for assistance. GC will conduct risk assessments and risk mitigation planning during start-up and at the program mid-term to consider the different protection considerations and needs of older persons, PWD, women, children, and at-risk youth. In order to mitigate is a high risk of Abuse of Authority by actors (PSEA, GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). This can be mitigated through 1. This can be mitigated through 1. Advocate for provision of alternative accommodation/expansion of existing sites, 2. Provision of gender segregated/appropriate privacy in mass shelter in case of large displacement/lack of space to provide individual family shelter unit, 3. Provision of gender segregated sanitation and hygiene facilities sufficiently close to dwellings, 4. Provision of lighting around shelter and WASH facilities, 5. Work closely with protection and other sectors to understand the issues and take necessary actions together to address them, 6. Extending consultations such as on siting of facilities with all groups of population including women, girls and PWD.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

GC actively works to identify and mitigate potential risks throughout all phases of program design and activity implementation. Bab al Hawa and Bab al Salama border crossings between Turkey and Syria will remain open and accessible for humanitarian goods and staff, 1) GC staff continue to have consistent access to target locations; 2) Active violence does not directly impact GC's offices in Gaziantep, or Qah for a sustained period of time; 3) Violence in the targeted areas remains at a level which allows GC to implement program, with both armed and unarmed parties to the conflict allowing access to vulnerable HHs for deliveries of humanitarian aid, and allowing GC to maintain acceptable levels of independence, impartiality, and neutrality; 4) Create CRBs to minimize LC interference , and facilitate access for delivery of humanitarian assistance; and 5) Monitor market functionality and Money transfer through a money transfer company or PTT.6) Coordinate with other actors to avoid overlaps. Through our ongoing programs, GC has contingency plans and mitigation measures in place should the COVID-19 situation in Syria shift during ATLAS2. GC will regularly review and update plans according to the extent of the COVID-19 pandemic in Syria.
