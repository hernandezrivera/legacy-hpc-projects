Project Summary

Unprecedented school closures amidst COVID-19 have resulted in challenging the country’s efforts to build productive human capital. According to estimates, school closures have affected 55.3 million children in Pakistan aged between 5 and 16. Additionally, 22.8 million children are already out of school, with over 50 percent being girls. The pandemic is intensifying the risks and vulnerabilities of an already weak educational system. Natural catastrophes have also worsen the situation. According to Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA), during August 2020, flooding situation in Sindh was unprecedented and on 25 August 2020, emergency was declared in 20 of 29 districts in the province. Essential infrastructure including roads, bridges and homes were severely damaged. In the communities of the 09 districts visited, 55 primary schools have been completely damaged, leaving 2,800 children without possibility to restart the school year in mid-September. Moreover, data from 718 schools (Primary: 592, Middle: 91, Secondary: 35) of 08 districts was collected for Rapid Needs Appraisal (RNA) in rain/flood-affected areas of Sindh, Balochistan and KPK. Of the total 718 schools 8% fully damaged, 18% partially damaged and 5% are used as shelters in the affected areas. The given situation demands rehabilitation of the partially damaged schools and providing temporary learning centers (TLC) for fully damaged schools in the prioritized districts.
Over the years, UNESCO has positioned its self as a key United Nations agency for education by strengthening its partnership with donor community, enhancing relationship with provincial and district education departments, and establishing a network of local communities and key stakeholders. UNESCO has been implementing similar activities with key development partners, i.e. Education Above All Foundation (EAA), Italian Development Cooperation (AICS), and Korean International Cooperation Agency (KOICA) and Government of Pakistan (GoP) through Girls’ Right to Education Programme (GREP), amongst many others before and during COVID 19 pandemic.
The project “Strengthening of Schools and Building Community Resilience to COVID 19 “ aims at improving school infrastructure and raising awareness about COVID-19 to ensure continuation of quality learning in vulnerable districts. This will entail provision of Temporary Learning Centers (TLCs) and repair the existing schools, which have been damaged by flood/rain; will encourage communities to send their children back to school. Since lack of WASH facilities have been affecting educational activities, WASH facilities will be provided in schools, with primary emphasis on girls schools. Provision of teacher’s training on SOPs on safe school return will ensure precautionary and safety measures at learning spaces for students returning. Responding to COVID-19, implementation of safe school operation protocols (Infection Prevention and Control-IPC) will be ensured through orientation sessions with teachers and parents.
The project will target 142,857 beneficiaries (71000 boys, 71857 girls, 3500 male teachers, 3500 female teachers, 3500 SMC members). 20 Temporary Learning Centers will be established , while 40 schools will be repaired. Training for safe schools reopening will be provided to teachers and community members in response to COVID 19. Learning material will be provided for continuity of learning. To maintain gender balance and inclusion of disable community members, equal number of boys and girls will be beneficiaries of the project ( 70,000 boys, 71857 girls).
Monitoring of the activities is the pivotal part of the proposed project. Dedicated technical staff, preferably an engineer, will be part of the monitoring of the rehabilitation sites (schools) along with a staff providing overall monitoring of the orientation sessions, training sessions and community mobilization.

1- Project Activities

(i) establish safe and child friendly temporary learning schools/centers with gender segregated WASH to ensure access to education for children affected by floods.Total of 20 Temporary Learning Centers (TLCs) with gender segregated WASH facilities will be provided in district Swat targeting approximately 3000 beneficiaries (boys, girls). Apart from the classrooms, unavailability of sanitation and hygiene facilities discourages children, especially girls to attend schools. Additionally, to create enabling environment of learning and promote hygiene in the times of COVID-19, it is even more important to sensitize children regarding self-hygiene.(ii) repair and rehabilitation of damaged schools in rain /flood affected areas. A total of 40 partially damaged schools identified through school management committees in flood/rain affected areas will be repaired and rehabilitated targeting 4100 beneficiaries (2050 boys, 2050 girls). These minor repairs will make the physical learning space conducive for the children and teachers, encouraging community members to send their children to school upon re-opening. WASH facilities will be provided in addition to the repair/rehabilitation work.(iii) Implementation of safe school operation protocols (Infection Prevention and Control-IPC). Development and distribution of risk communication material in local language, implementing safe school guidelines; equipping schools with and circulating critical information on disease prevention (Covid-19) As response to COVID 19, implementation of safe school operation protocols (Infection Prevention and Control-IPC) will be done in 900 schools targeting 142857 beneficiaries. Government approved protocols will be followed. Risk communication material (COVID19 precautionary measures) will be developed and disseminated, following safe school guidelines. Additionally, 3500 teachers of the identified schools in Peshawar, Swat, Karachi (Central, East, West, South), Muzaffarabad, Multan, Tharparkar and Hyderabad, will be oriented on SOPs developed by government for schools re-opening. (iv) mobilize communities and PTAs/PTSMCs to ensure effective school management and children's access to education. As a community sensitization and strengthening activity, 6500 community members and PTSMCs will be mobilized to build their capacity on effective school management. Through orientation sessions and Focus Group Discussion (FGDs), community will be enabled to become resilient to the possible extent and ensure continuity of learning for children. Disabled persons will be invited to participate or send their representative to share their views and contribute in betterment of the area. Districts Peshawar, Swat, Karachi, Hyderabad ,Tharparkar, Multan and Muzaffarabad are identified for community and PTCMCs mobilization. (v) organize training of teachers on Child Friendly schools; life skills based education, psychosocial support and teaching methodologies including teaching through different modalities. Within districts of Peshawar, Swat, Karachi, Hyderabad ,Tharparkar, Multan, Muzaffarabad, 3500 teachers will be trained on child friendly schools; life skills based education, psychosocial support and teaching methodologies including teaching through different modalities. The teachers will be equipped with necessary skills to provide psychosocial support to the most vulnerable children. (vi) Support of home-based learning, remedial and catch up classes and alternate / distance learning in the case of schools closure. Package of time table and potential assignments/worksheets for 50,000 children (boys, girls) will be developed by teachers in the schools of targeted districts of Peshawar, Swat, Karachi, Hyderabad ,Tharparkar, Multan and Muzaffarabad. Teachers will be contacted by PTSMCs(vii) Provide teaching and learning material for teachers and children (Including supplemental materials, study guides for teachers).
