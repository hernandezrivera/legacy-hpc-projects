Project Summary

Since April 2018, IOM has operated and managed a total of nine Humanitarian Hubs composed of a base camp in Maiduguri (Red Roof) and eight field hubs located in Gwoza, Bama, Ngala, Dikwa, Monguno, Banki (with two hubs) and Damasak. The Humanitarian Hubs provide humanitarian actors the ability to work in safe and secure accommodations, office space, and meeting/training facilities, in order to access generally insecure locations and deliver interventions necessary to improve and save the lives of hundreds of thousands of conflicts affected populations. The hubs also host the internet connectivity service of the Emergency Telecommunication Sector (ETS). With the ongoing security risks faced by humanitarian responders, especially in locations outside Maiduguri, the continued operation of the nine humanitarian hubs will contribute significantly to ensuring the presence and operational capacities of responders in hard-to-reach locations, which will result in delivering life-saving assistance to people in need where they need it the most. It is estimated that 1.3 million internally displaced persons (lDPs) reside where the Humanitarian Hubs are located.

1 - Needs

The Northeast region of Nigeria experienced violence perpetrated by non-state armed groups (NSAG) since 2015 resulting in massive displacement of residents who fled from the areas affected by the violence. One of the major challenges faced by the humanitarian agencies is the availability of safe accommodation and office facilities for their teams. In response to the need, IOM operates a main hub in Maiduguri (Red Roof) and a total of eight field hubs in Gwoza, Bama, Ngala, Dikwa, Monguno, Banki (2 hubs) and Damasak in deep field locations. The base camp in Maiduguri (Red Roof) has a total bed capacity of 124 beds and 14 offices of non-governmental organizations (NGOs) and UN Agencies that support activities, including the field hubs, while the field hubs each have a capacity of (24 beds in Gwoza, 17 beds in Bama, 14 Beds in Ngala, 23 Beds in Ngala, 20 beds in Monguno and 20 beds in Banki Main Hub, except for the second hub in Banki (Banki Hub Extension) which has a capacity of 72 beds. In addition to providing accommodation, all hubs provide office spaces and meeting/training facilities for humanitarian agencies. In collaboration with the Emergency Telecommunication Sector (ETS), the field hubs also provide internet connectivity to humanitarian workers who are not residents of the field hubs. Despite the establishment of the eight deep field hubs, the continued insecurity in these areas still poses a major challenge to humanitarian presence. IOM put in place a cost-recovery mechanism which supports up to 55% of the total cost of operating the 9 hubs. Additional funding is needed to cover the budget gap in operating the 9 humanitarian hubs and keep their facilities in good running condition to ensure their continued operation. Additionally, the hubs in Banki, Dikwa, Damasak, Ngala and Monguno were directly targeted by NSAG attackers between 2019 through 2021. Although the hub facilities managed to keep humanitarian workers substantially safer from attack overall compared to compounds or guest houses outside the Humanitarian Hubs, the attackers managed to enter the hub in Dikwa (1st March 2021), Damasak (10th April 2021) & Ngala (4th January 2021). During these attacks substantial parts of the hub facilities and two Armored Vehicles (for Ngala & Dikwa) were damaged/burnt, and in Damasak the adjoining mobile storage unit was completely burnt down in April 2021, affecting the structural integrity of some of the hub’s perimeter walls and a 10,000-liter overhang storage water tank. IOM worked with United Nations Department for Safety and Security (UNDSS) in identifying the security lessons learned from these attacks and identified areas that needed to be enhanced in order to improve the overall security facilities of the hubs. The needs were categorized by UNDSS and IOM between priority 1 and priority 2, depending on the criticality of the facility in the overall security set-up of the field hubs. With funding from various donors, IOM have completed the priority 1 needs. However, some of the priority 2 needs are also critical and should not remain unmet. The installation of solar power systems in the Banki hubs (30KVA) and in Monguno (40KVA) are planned to be completed in 2023, particularly as this is important not only for improving the security facility and communication equipment of the hubs but also to lessen some of the consumption of diesel fuel for power generation, thereby reducing operating costs.

2 - Activities

To contribute to ensuring the presence and operational capacities of responders in hard-to-reach locations, which will result in delivering life-saving assistance to people in need where they need it the most, IOM aims to implement the following activities:- Continue the operation of nine humanitarian hubs.• Finalize the installation of solar power systems in at least the following field hubs which are most frequently used by the humanitarian workers: Banki (two Hubs) and Monguno. Although the installation of solar power system was categorized by UNDSS and IOM as priority 2 items, having the hubs equipped with at least 30kw in Banki and 40kw in Monguno, the solar power system will allow the hubs to lower their operating cost contributing to the sustainability of the operation of the hubs. • Ensure the facilities of all the nine hubs are properly maintained and available for the use of humanitarian workers. Certain equipment items, such as generator sets and AC units, will require replacement or at least some of their worn-out parts require replacement to ensure that the hubs will be able to continue to sustain their operation.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

IOM is committed to gender mainstreaming, promoting gender equality and prioritizing the needs and concerns of vulnerable groups. IOM has worked to strengthen its institutional policies and commitments on gender and protection within its crisis operations worldwide by developing the Institutional Framework for Addressing Gender-Based Violence in Crises (GBViC Framework). Addressing GBV is an issue of institutional accountability and at the core of IOM’s mandate. Central to the framework is the need to ensure that the safety, dignity and well-being of all crisis-affected persons, especially women and girls, and their equitable access to services are prioritized, integrated and coordinated across all IOM crisis operations. IOM is therefore committed to the (Nigeria) Call to Action on Protection from GBV. IOM will contribute to promoting gender equality by including both men and women in all components of the project. IOM also provides GBV/ Protection against sexual exploitation and abuses (PSEA) trainings across various locations (namely Monguno, Damasak, Ngala, Bama, Banki and Banki extension, Gwoza, and Dikwa) for all IOM staff hired locally. These trainings are scheduled and organized in each location, depending on staff availability and workload. The humanitarian hubs shall ensure all humanitarian hubs staff undergo orientation/workshop of PSEA to heighten their awareness on PSEA and the reporting mechanisms in place. PSEA posters are posted in strategic common areas (office, cafeteria, gazebo, etc.) of the hubs, including in all the accommodation rooms. In addition, IOM observes gender equality in recruiting staff. In the humanitarian hubs operations, accommodation rooms are assigned to guests taking into consideration their gender. Sanitary facilities in all the hubs shall be designated for female and male and shall be marked accordingly. The hubs assign sanitary facilities that are easily accessible to female guests and staff. All the hubs’ facilities including the bunkers are suitable for the use of both female and male. The personal protective equipment (PPEs) that will be purchased for this project will likewise be suitable for the use of both female and male.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Humanitarian Hubs end users are involved in the project planning and design through the guest satisfaction survey, whereby IOM carefully reviews guests’ feedback to inform its planning. Furthermore, IOM engages the organizations using the services of the hubs through the Humanitarian Hubs Steering Committee and through the One UN System. IOM has been conducting the guest satisfaction survey to gather feedback and input from the guests on how to improve the services provided by the hubs. Hubs management will ensure that the survey is administered throughout the project and that issues and concerns raised through the survey or directly with hub staff are captured and addressed accordingly.
