Project Summary

The project intends to provide an integrated emergency and sustainable WASH intervention package to host communities and IDPs in the communities and in the camps and informal settlements northern Syria.
In response to the costly and short-term water supply in northwest Syria and to reinforce the most urgent life-saving interventions, Watan Foundation aims to enhance the long-term WASH infrastructure alongside with the emergency response services for certain period of time.
This improvement targets host communities and IDPs in 50 camps and 5 communities hosting approximately 300,000 individuals.
Through this project, WATAN will provide water trucking (max. three months) as a transition service, installing networks and connecting to nearby water and sanitation systems, including introducing solar energy, solid waste management, desludging latrines septic tanks (max. three months), constructing new latrines for persons with disabilities and persons without disabilities, operation and maintenance for the existing and new constructed latrines, distributing garbage containers, hygiene kits distribution and hygiene promotion.
As for the host communities, in five communities, WATAN will cover the rehabilitation and operational support to water and sanitation systems, including expansion of water and sanitation networks, rehabilitation of WASH facilities of the critical utilities (schools, HCFs,..), establishing tariff systems, introduce the solar energy modalities in water systems, solid waste management and distributing garbage containers,
The project subscribes to WATAN strategy of alleviating the suffering of people in need from Syria in order to maintain a healthy life and reduce the transmission of water-borne diseases.

4 - Needs

Align with the HRP of 2021, WATAN completed a secondary data collection to prioritize the accessible locations for this intervention (WASH HNO 2021 shared data Cluster Gap analysis – SNFI Priority Data 20210407 – and ISIMM last update of March 2021).A detailed needs assessment was implemented on 20 camps and informal settlements using the recommended WASH cluster assessment tools with additional questions by WATAN WASH team. The assessment took place from 25 March 2021 until 05 April 2021. The core target of this assessment is to highlight the severity scale indicators which can reflect the gaps and the possible mitigation interventions. The needs assessment covered 1247 families living in informal settlements including 1403 women, 3577 children, and 68 PwDs. 5 - Implementation of activities

1.1. Repair and rehabilitation of water systems in the targeted communities including introducing solar energy at the water stations and establishing tariff systems to ensure securing daily water per capita at a rate of 35 L/p/d.1.2. Support to water quality assurance, operation and maintenance of water supply systems.1.3. Repair and rehabilitation of sanitation systems, and supporting solid waste management activities.2.1. Distribution of essential WASH non-food items, hygiene promotion, and messaging on COVID-19.2.2. Improve access to humanitarian life-saving/emergency WASH facilities and services, including strengthening Infection Prevention and Control measures. This will include providing water trucking (max. three months) as a transition service (daily water per capita share will be 35 L/p/d), provision water distribution points designed specifically for PWD, installing networks and connecting to nearby water and sanitation systems, including introducing solar energy, solid waste management, desludging latrines septic tanks (max. three months), constructing new latrines for persons with disabilities and persons without disabilities, support the operation and maintenance for the existing and new constructed latrines, and distributing garbage containers.4.1. Support to WASH facilities and services in schools and child friendly spaces in close coordination with Education and Protection sectors, taking into consideration the availability of proper WASH facilities for children with disabilities.4.2. Support to WASH facilities and services in health care facilities, including COVID-19 dedicated facilities, in close coordination with health sector, ensurnig the availability of proper facilities for elderly and PWD.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

-Loss of cash or assets (operational): the risk owner: the team leader in each site - This continues to be a threat to all areas of operation inside of Syria - mitigation measures: daily inventory, control the treasury better, involve many people in this process to extend the circle of suspicion. -Unforeseen Changes in the Conflict- Risk owners: Senior Management Committee - each office - Conflict is such that organization cannot safely access locations, staff gets besieged in one area of control or operational assets are lost. Mitigation measures: Unlikely Moderate Stable Local information shows an increase in conflict levels, as we as direct reports from staff on the ground. “Continue to gather information from a range of sources. Develop contingency plan to work in alternative location if conflict continues. Continuous efforts by the access teams to gain new assurances and connections to secure humanitarian space. -" Kidnapping / Abduction - risk owners: Security Management/Program Management/Individual - risk mitigation measures: traveling in the day time - transportation in convoys - high coordination on movement tracking - deterrence though local authorities. -Exposure of sensitive information - Risk owners: "Security Management/Program Management/Individual Responsibility/Executive reinforcement of policies" - Communications with anyone inside of Syria are very likely compromised and we must assume that leakage of electronic data will occur. Results will include a Threat to the detention of individuals, reputational harm and compromise of community members or partner safety. Mitigation measures: strict procedures on staff in case there was information exposed. Less visibility of WATAN in critical environments.
