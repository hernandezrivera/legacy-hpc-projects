Project Summary

The proposed project aims at restoring rural and urban livelihoods for conflict-affected communities in Northeast and Northwest Syria. To achieve this goal, in agricultural areas, ACTED will provide temporary cash for work opportunities to vulnerable households. These will include, on the one hand, vulnerable farmers and beneficiaries of kitchen garden kits who need an immediate source of cash to address their basic needs before the harvest; and on the other hand, vulnerable host community’s members and IDPs who do not have access to land but are nonetheless in need of cash. They will participate in cash for work scheme aimed at performing community works such as school painting, canal cleaning, road repairs. In urban areas, instead, ACTED will support existing businesses and start-up with cash grants and training, enabling them to either start or restore and expand their business. This project is estimated to benefit 12,042 individuals.

4 - Needs

As the Syrian crisis enters its ninth year, an estimated 6.5 million Syrians are still food insecure, and an additional 2.5 million are at risk of food insecurity . Across the country, high production costs, the lack of quality inputs and damaged or destroyed infrastructure have been identified as the main constraints to the recovery of the Syrian agricultural sector, who was once one the breadbaskets in the region. Indeed, prior to the crisis, the country had 144 grain-collection centres where the Government purchased grain from farmers at controlled prices but only 35 are estimated to be still functioning today . Additionally, production levels for staple crops have further been impacted by climate change and erratic weather, with Aleppo governorate being one of the five most affected seed-producing governorates by the 2018 droughts. Livestock activities, a sector which once accounted for between 35 to 40% of the country’s total agricultural production, have also seen production levels drop drastically. In addition, large influxes of IDPs have put on strain on shared natural resources and livelihood opportunities in more stable areas. Particularly, in Northwest Syria, a Rapid Market Monitoring exercise conducted by REACH found a 22% increase in the overall Standard Minimum Expenditure (SMEB) value from November 2019 to December 2019 alone . Similarly, in Northeast Syria, ACTED conducted in October and November 2019 and found that 41% of beneficiaries had a poor FCS, even after receiving food ration assistance, resulting from a reduced purchasing power. It is therefore crucial to invest in projects supporting local value chains and production to create livelihood opportunities and improve communities’ autonomy and capacity to supply local markets.

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposed intervention aims at covering immediate households’ food needs as well as strengthening the early recovery and enhance resilience of conflict-affected populations in Northeast and Northwest Syria through a restoration of rural and urban livelihoods. This will be achieved through the following:Restoration of agricultural livelihood opportunities through individual and community-level interventions • Support to agricultural-based businesses (to strengthen the existing value chains more broadly)Restoration of urban livelihood opportunities• Provision of cash grants to existing small-scale businesses and start-up, to enable them to restore and expand their business. • Provision of cash grants to home-based businessesIn addition, ACTED will provide temporary livelihood opportunities to vulnerable households through cash for work schemes. These schemes will benefit different individuals including vulnerable households in the same locations where agricultural activities take place. These households do not have access to land, however they are still vulnerable. They will take part in cash for work schemes of a duration of 1 or 2 months and will participate in community-based works including canal rehabilitation, school cleaning and painting, installation of trees.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups and minorities needs during project design/implementationLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community sensitisation, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and/or community meetings prior to implementation. ACTED addresses where possible gender and protection concerns. In areas out of the scope of programming or expertise, ACTED relies on referral procedures to specialized agencies.Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuseLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as well as the work of the contractors, and to relay any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issue.
