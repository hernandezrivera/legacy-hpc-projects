Project Summary

The protracted crisis in the North east has disrupted livelihoods, destroyed basic infrastructures and services, thus affecting the entire education system. The massive displacements caused has lead to a great number of dropout of schools. According to the education sector, 1,060,129 children (3-17 years) are affected by the humanitarian conflict. In addition to this, pre-existing factors to the insurgency also explicate the low enrollment of girls , these are sustained by socio- cultural attitudes and considerations of formal education in the North- East especially for girls. These include the undervaluing of girls’ education by caregivers and communities who do not see educating a girl as a good investment or a priority because girls will eventually get married and leave the family. Furthermore, young girls are burdened with domestic responsibilities such as taking care of younger siblings in the home and this competes with their study time. Girls are responsible for the majority of household chores, so they either do not go to school or do not have time for their homework. There are also barriers of early marriages. Girls are married off at a young age because of cultural practices, and are unlikely to return to school after marriage. Even when they want to return after giving birth, they are discouraged. There is also the challenge of poverty. Families lack financial resources to support girls’ education including, provision of educational supplies and payment of school fees. There is also a lack of safety for girls at school, including sexual harassment and abuse of older girls. There are health dimensions as well such as HIV and AIDS. Some girls are orphaned by AIDS at an early age and forced to live with harsh uncaring relatives who do not support their schooling. Some girls do not go to school when in their menses or they do not have sanitary wear or special bathrooms at school from where they can freshen up. Unwanted and early pregnancies have also been identified as some of the most critical (reproductive) health challenges hindering girls’ education. Finally and most importantly, lack of positive role models has been identified as an important factor, as most uneducated family relatives do not attach any importance to education. Girls usually do not have other successful females to look up to, and therefore do not get motivated or encouraged when they face challenges with education.
AHA shall therefore increase safe and inclusive education through enrollment and retention of girls and boys in schools.

1 - Needs

The continued insurgency has destroyed means of survival of both men and women in the North East of Nigeria. This insurgency has drastically affected the education system. Schools were completely destroyed in some localities in Adamawa State, this includes, classrooms that were partially or completely brought down by the insurgents. The vulnerable returnees from Cameroon need emergency education. These returnees in lamurde, madagali and michika are mostly women and adolescent girls that came without means of livelihoods. They need assistance to enroll into schools, some of the adolescent girls do not have necessary menstrual materials as they reported that they use rags during their period, while those who were already incorporated into some schools dropped because they did not sanitary pads and dignified latrines to refresh up. In some locations in Lamurde, there is a great need to incorporate WASH facilities in schools in order to facilitate retention and refreshing up of adolescent girls during menstruation, by so doing; it would enable retention of Adolescent girls. Crisis affected men, women care givers, need to be supported with cash-based transfer/voucher to pay for the children schools fees, restoration of existing schools building need to be carried out in Lamurde, Madagali, Michika as well as TLS to provide safe and inclusive education. Girls of reproductive age need to be provided with sanitary pads (reusable/ disposable) in order to encourage their retention and limit female drop-out from schools during menstruation period. Teachers (M/F) as well as returnees, girls / boys need to be provided with learning and teaching materials. teachers need to trained in pedagogy and PSS to deliver quality education and cope with crisis-affected environment.

2 - Activities

Act1 provision of school learning materials to 7,000 crisis- affected returnees by the insurgency in the targeted LGAs (madagali,Michika,lamurde)Act 2 provision of cash-transfer/voucher to crisis-affected parents, guardians, caregivers for enrolment (school fees) of girls and boys.Act 3 Rehabilitation/construction of 10 classrooms/ TLS for provision of safe and inclusive education in Adamawa.Act4 provision of WASH services ( water points, sex-segregated blocks latrines, showers with pictorial images, locks inside, hygiene promotion ) to 7000 returnees as per standards in Adamawa for retention and reduce number of drop-out of adolescent girls during menstruation period).Act5 provision of teaching materials to 30 teachers (M/F) for delivery of quality education.Act6 Training of teachers (M/F) on PSS and right discipline to cope with effects of the insurgency and improve the outcomes of the returnees.Act7 distribution of sanitary pads (reuseable/disposable) to adolescent girls to ensure the retention and reduction of the number of drop-out of adolescent girls during menstruation period).
