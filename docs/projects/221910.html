Project Summary

In this project, Save A Life International will carry out an integrated water and sanitation (WASH) response in Melut County, in Upper Nile State targeting 2000 households (12,000 individuals). The beneficiaries are most vulnerable host communities, returnees and internally displaced persons. Collaborations will be done with nutrition and health partners to ensure sustainable access to safe water and sanitation promotion in communities, nutrition and health facilities to prevent WASH related epidemics. Rehabilitation of existing water boreholes, water quality testing and treatment will be conducted in flood prone or flood affected areas in the county. SLI will work closely with core-pipeline to respond swiftly to emergencies through distribution of WASH nonfood items and promotion of safe hygiene practices. To mitigate WASH related Gender Based Violence, SLI will conduct comprehensive safety audit prior and during the intervention. To ensure disability inclusion, SLI will work closely with chiefs, Relief and Rehabilitation Commission and community leaders to identify, register and carry out WASH interventions in locations where PWDs can easily and safely access the assistance. To ensure coordination and avoid duplication of efforts, SLI will submit 5Ws reports as well as participate in WASH meetings in Melut, Malakal and Juba, where it will receive and provide updates on the project. SLI will embed cluster’s approved accountability and quality assurance module in its monitoring framework to ensure compliance, enhance quality of outputs, outcomes and beneficiary satisfaction.

1. Needs

Due to out of a wetter than normal rainy season, communities in Melut County are grappling with a new wave of flooding during the current rainy season. The impact of the ongoing flooding compounded by high cost of living, inflow of returnees from Sudan and food insecurity may result in further deterioration of humanitarian outcomes for affected communities through 2024 and 2025. The current flooding crisis has occurred at a time when people in the county continue to face food insecurity and malnutrition. According to Integrated Phase Classification report, Melut is classified as an emergency (IPC 4) or worse food insecure. This means the flooding are happening at a time when the majority of people in the country were already experiencing diminishing coping mechanisms. Due to flooding, the water sources such as boreholes and hand-dug wells are submerged and contaminated leading the population to resort to drinking untreated surface water . Displaced households have lost their belongings to floods such as cooking utensils and water containers. The general hygiene condition in internally displaced sites and returnees transit points is worsening and there are potential risks for water borne diseases such as cholera, hepatitis A &E and intestinal worms. In locations with water pumps, lack of pump mechanics has made it impossible to repair damaged water pumps and boreholes triggering water scarcity. Further, dysfunctional markets, lack of WASH partners, absence of community based hygiene promoters and water management committees has negatively affected the capacity of the affected population to respond to flood related disaster. In light of this, there is a need to carry out WASH intervention in Melut in order to save lives and alleviate suffering of the most vulnerable people using cluster guidelines. SLI will conduct hygiene promotion at the community, nutrition and health facilities and distribute lifesaving WASH NFIs such as PUR sachets, filter cloth, aqua tabs, jerry cans, soap and buckets and menstrual hygiene kits for women and girls. The proposed project is in agreement with the WASH Cluster Floods Preparedness and Response Plan for South Sudan. Through this project, 4,000 individuals IDPs and 5,000 individuals host communities and 3000 returnees will directly receive assistance through the project.

2. Project activities

1.2: Selecting and training of 30 (15 F, 15 M) hygiene promotersSave A Life International (SLI) will work closely with chiefs, existing partners, existing water and sanitation committees, returnees, IDPs and host community leaders to select 30 hygiene promoters (15 Female, 15Male) with previous experience and knowledge of hygiene promotion and are based in their respective communities. The selected hygiene promoters will be engaged in hygiene messaging within their communities. A such, those selected must be versed with local culture and should demonstrate capacity to read, write and communicate either in English, Arabic and Local dialect to enable clear and widespread information dissemination. The training of hygiene promoters will be done in line with WASH cluster hygiene messaging and will be facilitated by SLI’ WASH Manager and WASH Officers. The content of the training will include safe water chain, safe excreta disposal, environmental hygiene, hand washing with clean water and soap/ash, how to make tippy taps, food hygiene, Menstrual Hygiene, cholera preventive messages as part of mainstreaming. 1.3: Conducting house to house hygiene promotion Save A Life International (SLI will conduct house-to-house hygiene promotion through the 30 (15 Females/ 15 males) trained hygiene promoters across seven (7) Payam’s in Melut. The aim of the activities being to develop good hygiene practices, discourage open defecation, prevent disease and encourage positive hygiene practices among returnees, IDPs and host communities. IEC materials such as brochures in local language will be used for hygiene promotion while the CHPs will put on t-shirts for easy identification during house-to-house hygiene promotion. 1.4: Conduct hygiene mass campaigns Save A Life International (SLI) will conduct hygiene mass campaigns in schools and at the community level through its 30 Community Hygiene Promoters. The campaign will be conducted in public spaces such as schools, markets, places of worship, water points, slaughter and fish landing sites. 1.5: Identification and registration of 5800 household beneficiaries for WASH NFIsSave A Life International (SLI) through local authorities and AAP Committee will identify 14 enumerators (7 males/ 7 females) who will be engaged in registration, distribution and post distribution monitoring. The enumerators will receive a two-day orientation and training on registration tools, standard operation procedures during distribution of WASH NFIs, Post Distribution Monitoring tools, data collection methods and analysis. Enumerators and SLI project team led by WASH Coordinator and 2 WASH Officers will jointly carry out verification of needs and selection of beneficiaries with the help of the Local authorities (RRC), AAP Committee and the Chiefs. Selection criteria will include households with elderly persons, caregivers of separated and unaccompanied children, unaccompanied minors, severely sick persons, individuals living with disability, individuals with no community links and women headed households and youths. 1.6. Provision of WASH NFIs to 5800 IDPs, Returnees and most vulnerable host householdsSLI will request for WASH NFIs from core pipeline such as jerry cans/ buckets without tap, aqua tab (for HHs using non-turbid water sources), PUR (for HHs using turbid water sources), soap and filter cloth. SLI will therefore collect the items from Malakal; thereafter transport them by boat and road to Melut for prepositioning. Distribution of the items to households will be conducted for a period of five (5) days through the fourteen (14) locally hired and trained enumerators. 1.7.Conduct WASH NFIs Post Distribution Monitoring PDMSLI’s selected enumerators with the assistance of project staff, AAPs Committee and Local authorities will conduct a post distribution monitoring 2-3 weeks after distribution of the WASH NFIs. 1.8: Rehabilitation of water boreholes. 1.9: Establish gender segregated latrines:
