Project Summary

The project “Hygiene, Nutrition and Winterization as Cash as well as Psychosocial Support for Conflict-Affected People in need along the Contact Line, Eastern Ukraine” (14 June 2019-13 June 2021) aims to improve food security, hygiene conditions, preparedness to winter and psychosocial well-being of conflict-affected populations in the 5-km zone along the 'contact line' in Eastern Ukraine (GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts).
Whereas the project’s food, hygiene and winterization components are implemented by ASB Country Office Ukraine, the psychosocial support (PSS) is provided by ASB’s local partner, Samaritan Union of Ukraine (SSU).
Within the framework of the WASH component of the project, vulnerable beneficiaries receive either cash or in-kind hygiene assistance. The vulnerable households in the 5-km zone near the ‘contact line’ will be provided with cash for hygiene to cover their basic hygiene needs (soap, washing powder, toothpaste, etc.). Moreover, people in need of adult diapers will be provided with hygiene kits for persons with special needs. The project activities contribute to the WASH Objective 2: “Provision of critical WASH-related supplies and information for the prevention of water- and sanitation-related diseases.”
The project is funded by the Federal Foreign Office of the Federal Republic of Germany.

1 - Needs

The conflict in Eastern Ukraine continues to have a negative impact on the socioeconomic situation in Ukraine, particularly in the areas adjacent to the ‘contact line’. According to the WASH Cluster Study 2019, funded by OFDA, 22% of households in the 20-km zone are in the poorest category, compared to 12% in GCA as a whole. In turn, the demographic profile in the 20-km zone is characterized by a higher number of older people (42% of the population and 67% of all HHs) when compared to the rest of GCA.Within the 20-km zone (GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts), 74% of households reported having at least one vulnerability, with the proportion being even higher (76%) for the 5-km zone (REACH Economic Security Assessment, March 2019). The households with overlapping vulnerabilities (e.g. older people with disabilities and/or chronic illnesses) may need additional assistance (REACH Protection Assessment of Isolated Settlements in Government-Controlled Areas Along the Contact Line, February 2019). Vulnerable households in Eastern Ukraine often prioritize food and heating expenditures over hygiene items. Moreover, according to the Joint Market Monitoring study, hygiene prices increased by 10% between September 2017 and February 2018. As a result, essential hygiene needs may remain uncovered, which poses a serious risk of water-borne diseases, especially in conditions of reduced water supply in the conflict area. In order to prevent/minimize health risks, conflict-affected people need clean water, adequate sanitation, hygiene items and hygiene promotion.According to WASH Cluster, over 3,2 million people in Donbas require WASH-related humanitarian interventions. As demonstrated by WASH Cluster Study 2019, 15,8% of people who live in the 20-km zone from the ‘contact line’ (GCA) require hygiene-related assistance. The proportion of people in need of hygiene assistance is the highest among the households with persons with special needs (22,4%). To compare, 14,6% of households with children and 15,7% households with older people need hygiene assistance, with the average of 14,4% of all households in the GCA (WASH Cluster Study 2019).In the 20-km zone, 30% of people note that hygiene situation is worse than before the conflict, compared to 9,1% in all GCA. In the 20-km zone, 5% of households have no access to over 30% of hygiene items they require. Overall, people living in the areas adjacent to the ‘contact line’ tend to buy cheaper hygiene items, compared to the rest of GCA (WASH Cluster Study 2019). On average, households tend to spend 167 UAH a month on hygiene items, though the actual recommendation of the WASH Cluster is 300 UAH per person per month. The WASH Cluster recommends provision of cash vouchers, hygiene kits and, upon need, diapers. The profile of persons with special needs who were selected by ASB for the distribution of hygiene kits demonstrates significant vulnerabilities. Thus, out of 617 persons, 77% are women. Older people (60+) represent around 90% of the caseload, with 81% of the beneficiaries in this age category being women. Most people who need adult diapers (87%) are bed-ridden, and the majority of them (76%) are women. Since only 30% of the caseload have an officially registered disability, they cannot claim disability-related social payments from the state and thus are in great need of in-kind hygiene assistance. As for the availability of hygiene items, they are fully available in market sub-centers and semi-peripheral localities. However, in some periphery locations, hygiene items are either partially available (Lobacheve, Peredilske, Troitske) or unavailable (Bolotene). As demonstrated by all three rounds of market study, the least available commodity are diapers (Joint Market Monitoring, February 2018). Thus, during the 3rd round of market monitoring (February 2018), adult diapers were unavailable in 53% of monitored localities along the ‘contact line’ in the GCA of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Purpose: To meet hygiene needs of vulnerable conflict-affected populations in Eastern Ukraine.Output: vulnerable beneficiaries along the 'contact line' received hygiene assistance.Outcome: target beneficiaries improve their hygiene situation.Activity 1.1. Beneficiary identification Given the multisectoral nature of the project (food, hygiene, winterization), the beneficiary selection methodology is based specifically but not entirely on food security profile of the households. Since food insecure households usually prioritize their food needs over other expenses, selected households will receive both food and hygiene assistance. Simultaneously, specific hygiene needs of persons with special needs (e.g. adult diapers) will be taken into consideration. There will be two rounds of assessment for the food and hygiene component in 2020:1) January-February 2020 3) August-October 2020The beneficiary assessment will take place in Avdiivka, Toretsk, Bakhmutskyi and Yasynuvatskyj raions in Donetska oblast as well as in Novoaidarskyj, Popasnianskyi and Stanychno-Luhanskyi raions in Luhanska oblast. Beneficiary selection will be undertaken in coordination with WASH Cluster, local authorities and humanitarian agencies operating in the area. ASB will ensure that beneficiaries meet vulnerability criteria identified by HRP 2019-2020 (i.e. older persons, people with disabilities, female-headed households, IDPs).A hotline feedback mechanism will be available to ask questions, request assistance, provide feedback and lodge complaints. All complaints will be recorded in the Complaints Registration Database for follow-up. At the end of each distribution cycle (8 months of assistance), ASB will conduct the post-distribution monitoring (PDM). Households who were the most vulnerable during the initial stage of beneficiary selection, will be re-assessed and, if needed, included into the next caseload. This may eventually reduce the overall number of unique beneficiaries. All assessments, including PDM, are designed to allow age, gender and disability disaggregation.Activity 1.2. Distribution of cash to cover basic hygiene needs There will be 3 distribution cycles of cash assistance, each lasting for 8 months. In each caseload, 3000 beneficiaries will receive cash assistance to cover their food and hygiene needs:• Caseload 1: July 2019-February 2020 • Caseload 2: March 2020-October 2020• Caseload 3: November 2020-June 2021ASB follows current recommendations of the WASH Cluster (UAH 300/person/month). However, due to the exchange rate fluctuations, ASB may reduce number of beneficiaries and/or revise cash value. For the population in the 5-km zone near the line of contact (GCA of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts), the most preferred modes of delivery for cash assistance are bank account (48%) and post office (32%) (REACH Humanitarian Trend Analysis 2018). Thus, the modes of cash disbursement used by ASB (Oshchadbank and UkrPoshta) are in line with the preferences of the affected populations. Due to the home delivery of cash by Ukrposhta, older people and persons with disabilities would not need to travel to bank to withdraw their cash grants. Activity 1.3. Distribution of additional hygiene support to persons with special needsWithin the framework of the project, 617 beneficiaries will be provided with adult diapers and related items throughout the 2020 year, unless they no longer need them. In the current caseload of 3000 beneficiaries, up to 14% (406 persons) have special needs, thus requiring additional hygiene support (e.g. adult diapers). The rest of beneficiaries in need of adult diapers (211 persons) was identified by ASB based on referrals from authorities and other NGOs. The composition of hygiene kit for persons with special needs is in line with the recommendation of the WASH Cluster. It includes adult diapers as main commodity as well as accompanying items (e.g. wet towel wipes, gloves, absorbent pads, powder).

3 - Indicators & targets

The main indicators and targets are the following:• 85% of beneficiaries are satisfied with the aid provided;• 85% of beneficiaries report that the assistance was easily accessible;• 70% of beneficiaries report that they are aware of the complaint mechanisms.• 3000 individuals in each caseload are supported with cash assistance for hygiene;• 617 beneficiaries with special needs receive additional hygiene items;• 60% of program beneficiaries are female;• 30% of beneficiaries are older people;• 10% of beneficiaries are persons with disabilities and/or homebound.
