Project Summary

In response to the internal displacement caused by the civil conflict in the Lake Chad Basin (LCB), WV proposes an integrated WASH and Child Protection project to address urgent unmet needs for those vulnerable displaced communities, host communities and returnees. The project key activities will provide life-saving activities to remote displaced communities in the prefectures of Ngouboua and Liwa where 14,056 IDPs currently reside. The project’s targeted sectors will build off one another to decrease vulnerability as well as improve quality of life. The 12- month project will target a total of an estimated 7,400 direct beneficiaries (IDPs, Host Community members; 50% women/50% men).
The importance to respond to the needs across all groups to avoid creating division and conflict was repeatedly stressed to the assessment team. The needs in this region were significant before the displacement and movement of people occurred, and while the communities have generally been welcoming the IDP and refugees, it has stretched to the limit the availability of little resources they had.
The eleven IDP sites which are targeted in this project are situated in the prefectures of Bagasola (Dar Naihm, Kafia, Bourdaleroum, Kife, Boudou Moussarom, Bibi Hassane and Wole Mbodou Tanfou ) and Liwa ( Mahiala and Berakole) which show a big need in terms of WASH and child protection activities. Information collected from a UNICEF database show a rate of 18% in terms of water coverage and 0% in sanitation coverage in both areas. Women and children (girls and boys) are often the most frequent collectors of HH water. The platforms and the troughs on the boreholes will be shaped to ensure the steps are not excessively high, and reducing risk from harm, injury on aged and/or physically challenged users. Fences will deter animals from accessing the water point. This means that WASH and child protection remain high priorities in the three targeted sites.
Regarding Child protection sector, trainings and sensitizations will organized according to the age of the beneficiaries. Children, teenagers and young people as beneficiaries will be the agents of change in their communities. Parents and households will be used to facilitate sensitizations in the communities to ensure the sustainability of the interventions.
