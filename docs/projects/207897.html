Project Summary

The war that broke out in Sudan in April 2023, has increased the number of people migrating into South Sudan through the Renk border. Migrants who consist mainly of returnees, are moving from Renk into Malakal and then transported to different location in South Sudan including areas in Upper Nile such as Nasir and Ulang. The populations of incoming migrants are faced with challenges of food insecurity, malnutrition for children under the age of 5 years & pregnant and lactating women, poor water- sanitation & hygiene, limited shelter, disease outbreak, lack of education for children of school going age and natural disaster such as flooding. According to a report by IMC in August 2023, for women and children fleeing Sudan and host communities, conflict, displacement and rising costs are worsening malnutrition rates. Returnees and IDPs have lost their livelihoods due to movement to safer areas, forcing the populations to depend on humanitarian assistance. This is further aggravated by flooding in Nasir and Ulang which began in October 2023.
The Food Security Outlook report for South Sudan, published in July 2023, states that Emergency (IPC Phase 4) is expected to persist in Upper Nile State counties, with pockets of the population likely in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) pushing the communities into dire food insecurity situation. However, during the needs assessment conducted by ADRA in Ulang and Nasir, returnees and IDPs stated that all means of production and tools were lost during the flee from violence rendering them not able to produce food unless assisted. For the host community, access to traditional livelihoods such as livestock rearing and crop cultivation have decreased over recent years due to recurring shocks, including flooding, conflict, and displacement.
The project in Nasir and Ulang will target South Sudanese returnees who have fled from Sudan and Ethiopia to their country of origin due to the war and conflict which broke out in April 2024 in Sudan and sporadic conflicts in Ethiopia- Gambella, IDPs from within Upper Nile State and the Host community residing in the target areas. The main groups to be targeted will be children, women and youth and people with disabilities. According to the 2023 HRP Intersectoral Prioritization, Nasir is under priority 1 and Ulang under priority 2. ADRA has ongoing projects in these two locations and this project will be complementing the already existing activities and a layering approach will be employed. • The project will target pregnant and lactating mothers, widows and teenage mothers. Pregnant and lactating women are vulnerable due to their increased nutritional needs and susceptibility to the harmful consequences of deficiencies. Pregnant and lactating women have been exposed to stressful conditions during migration and high levels of food insecurity where those interviewed mentioned that they spent days travelling without eating. This group of women will be targeted for nutrition and livelihoods especially agriculture production. When war broke out in Sudan most women and children fled to South Sudan and men remained and this has increased the vulnerability of women who have become head of household within the returnees. Within the host community and IDPs women head of households will be targeted. Protection issues including GBV will be mainstreamed.
• Children that will be targeted in this project include; children with disability, unaccompanied minors, orphans and those not attending schools or children with manifesting risk behaviors, children who are neglected by parents.
• Youths –youths will be targeted for livelihoods options. Youths will also be involved in peace building activities – (peace dialogues and conferences), peace structures in the communities will include youths who usually are not consulted for during decision making process due to lack of representation.

1. Needs

The populations of incoming migrants are faced with challenges of food insecurity, malnutrition for children under the age of 5 years & pregnant and lactating women, poor water- sanitation & hygiene, limited shelter, disease outbreak, lack of education for children of school going age and natural disaster such as flooding. According to a report by IMC in August 2023, for women and children fleeing Sudan and host communities, conflict, displacement and rising costs are worsening malnutrition rates. Returnees and IDPs have lost their livelihoods due to movement to safer areas, forcing the populations to depend on humanitarian assistance. This is further aggravated by flooding in Nasir and Ulang which began in October 2023.Households will increasingly struggle to meet their basic needs, and the risk of a resumption in fighting between different groups (returnees, IDPs and host community) remains high. This is coupled by increase in market prices as a result of affected trading between Sudan and South Sudan and between Ethiopia and South Sudan. Reintegration has been a challenge due to high poverty levels within communities in Upper Nile leading to competition between the host community and returnees for humanitarian assistance. Nasir is food insecure and has undergone repeated bouts of severe flooding since 2019, and experience chronic safety and security challenges.Public health risks are likely to increase at transit sites and Malakal and Renk towns with the movement of returnees into South Sudan after the outbreak of Malaria and cholera in some part in Sudan. These health challenges are imported into the local areas like Nasir and Ulang leading to a congestion of the health centres and continued infections and reinfections within the communities. An assessment conducted by ADRA revealed that returnee women have been exposed to GBV during migration from Sudan, minors and un-accompanied children have also been prone to abuses. Host communities have experienced abused related to forced marriages of girls of school going age. Protection activities need to be escalated as the numbers of households increase in these two locations

2. Project activities

The project will work with the different target groups to build their capacity to respond to shocks and bounce back. The major shocks at the moment include loss of livelihoods and income activities for IDPs, Returnees and host community, conflicts and food insecurity. All target groups – returnees, refugee, IDPs and host community have lost livelihoods as a result of violent clashes, war and flooding. The project will build upon work already being done in the two locations to improve food security and bring about resilience. ADRA is distributing multipurpose cash to meet food and protection needs for the most vulnerable people, funded by ADRA International Office (NEMP), ADRA Denmark and UNHCR. ADRA is implementing a Climate Resilience Agriculture and Renewable Energy Solutions for Sustainable Livelihoods, which aims to improve the agricultural production in Ulang and Nasir by introducing cropping thorough the year and has introduced appropriate irrigation equipment. This proposed intervention will be linked to this project.Proposed Agriculture production: Households among the host community, IDPs and returnees suffered loss of livelihoods including crops, livestock and means of production/fishing due to flooding and conflicts. The project will promote introduction of production means, crops and livestock that can adapt to climate change effects for example use of irrigation equipment to produce during drier seasons, water harvesting and use of climate adaptable crop varieties like early maturing sorghum varieties, cassava and rice in flooding areas. Once off production inputs for agricultural production will be provide to individuals and groups for early recovery. As the project progresses, communities will only receive capacity building while providing their own inputs. Post harvest technologies will be promoted to enhance the capacity of communities to keep food for a longer period especially during the lean season. The project will train farmers in CSA involving production with minimal disturbance of the soil, producing climate adaptable crop & vegetable varieties, intercropping and mulching through setting up learning centres within the communities. Improved livestock husbandry will be achieved through training to be conducted by community animal health workers who are working with livestock farmers under the CARE project in Naisr and Ulang.Proposed Nutrition interventions: Mother Infant Young Child Nutrition will be promoted by training mothers/caregivers on age-appropriate feeding practices for infants and children under 5 and improving knowledge and access to affordable nutritious foods. Supplemental Nutrition Assistance will be promoted through provide life-saving support for children facing risk of acute malnutrition through community screening, case identification and referrals to health facility level for SAM treatment and voucher distribution for identified MAM cases.The project will work with the community to develop/strengthen local protection & peace structures, who will spearhead the peaceful coexistence initiatives in order to promote peaceful coexistence among the three groups. The different structures are able to advocate for policy change and promote social cohesion to push for change. Local structure can develop localize policies for peace and peaceful coexistence creating an opportunity to benefit from peace dividends
