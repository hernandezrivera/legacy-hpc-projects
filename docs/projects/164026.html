Project Summary

In most societies, women have limited space to meet, and public spaces are often inhabited largely by men. Traditionally, women’s responsibilities include taking care of children, cooking, carrying out household chores, and generally looking after the family. While these roles may change during crisis, where women may find themselves working or becoming the breadwinner, they remain responsible for the household nevertheless. For many girls the opportunity to move freely in the community becomes limited at the onset of puberty. Parents often keep their daughters inside the house, protected from any contact with males. This unofficial restriction on female mobility tends to persist throughout life.
In the Syrian context, women have become more isolated as a consequence of the crisis. Their mobility has been curbed significantly. Women and their family members reported having limited movement of women and girls outside the home due to fear of sexual violence, harassment, and indiscriminate attacks increased fear of sexual assault and harassment has placed even further restrictions on displaced women and girls,
The Safe spaces GAN seeks to build will helps to reduce risks and prevent further harm during acute emergency responses. These spaces provide women and girls with a safe entry point for services and a place to access information. Safe gathering points also offer them an opportunity to engage with each other, exchange information, and rebuild community networks and support. In this way, safe spaces can be a key way of building women and girls.

4 - Needs

The hostilities in northwest Syria continue to impact heavily on civilians who have endured nine years of crisis, violence, multiple displacements and economic downturn. The humanitarian situation, particularly in the Idleb de-escalation area, continues to deteriorate as a result of the hostilities that escalated in late 2019.Since 1 December 2019, almost 350,000 people fled from their homes, mainly from southern Idleb governorate, moving further north away from the hostilities.• Most of the recently displaced people moved to urban centres and IDP camps in northwestern Idleb. Tens of thousands have reportedly moved to areas such as Afrin and A’zaz in northern Aleppo governorate seeking safety and access to services.Women and children are estimated to make up for the 76 percent of this population (OCHA-Situation Report No. 6 )• With the ongoing displacement, concomitant lack of shelter options, and the sub-standard living conditions in areas of displacement, protection risks have increased, specifically related to gender-based violence (GBV), child protection (CP).In the households, women and girls have taken on additional roles traditionally associated with men, including as heads of households and principal breadwinners and although they have more responsibilities, this does not automatically translate into decision making power. This contributes to increased pressure and stress for women to provide for their families, often in absence of any other support from family members. Consequently, they are exposed to increased risks of violence against them within the family and at their workplace. Where gender-based violence services exist, women and girls have confirmed that they have a positive impact on their lives, with Women and Girls Safe Spaces serving as a place to seek safety and to receive essential life-saving services (health, psychosocial and legal support) and other needed services (e.g. vocational and skill training).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Given the sensitivity of addressing GBV in the country and the protection risks related to backlashes from sections of the community and on GBV messaging, GAN will continue to engage with the community to mitigate this risk and to build acceptance. By engaging with the community, GAN can ensure the GBV messaging is appropriate and sensitive to the context and culture. Although GAN’s strategy is primarily focused on empowering women and girls, men and boys will also be targeted. outreach activities focused on building trust with men and boys will be developed to minimize the risks of women living in male headed households being refrained from accessing the WGSS. The unequal power structures between men and women and notions of traditional gender roles in Syria means there is a risk GBV programming can create tensions within communities and families, especially between men and women.Risk can originate in family or community backlash towards women participating in recreational, vocational or empowerment activities, in the form of stigmatisation, reprisals and further trauma. to mitigate this risk, GAN conducts reassessments of activities, including safety audits and FGDs regarding Protection and GBV, Persons with disability have difficult accessing GBV services Provide transportation support for persons with disability Breach of confidentiality putting at risk SGBV survivors due to lack of capacity on information management of service providers Mitigating Measures: Training of service providers on principles of safety and confidentiality, adequate follow up of measures to keep confidential the information. Exploitation by service provider or by other actor in the community. Mitigating Measures: - Ensure beneficiaries know services where to disclose if violence occurs - Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- the risk of shelling by the Government of Syrian forces and its allies in the targeted locations or and infighting between different armed groups continues causing loss of access to the targeted locations inaccessible: Mitigation: GAN will assess several options like the temporary suspension of activities until the areas become more secure again, or move the project to a safe area taking into consideration the ethical closure of GBV activities.2- Communities and key stakeholders reject our project/plan; Mitigation: Include the local and IDP community in the design and the implementation of the activities of this project, conduct community mobilization ahead of the project promoting to its proposed activities and ensure the community acceptance In addition to the close coordination of relevant stakeholder. 3- A change of government policy/attitude in Turkey could lead to a closure of the organization and negatively impact on humanitarian access to Syria. Mitigation: As a registered NGO in Turkey, GAN is expecting that this legal framework will, at best, give the leeway to operate. If the general attitude towards cross-border humanitarian assistance should change, GAN will adapt their modus operandi to new requirements that may arise to ensure continued assistance, although GAN has its operational offices inside Syria that can continue following up all the needed activities with the remote management from GAN headquarters in the neighboring countries.
