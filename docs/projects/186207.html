Project Summary

The main aim of the Early recovery and livelihood sector is to improve living conditions of displaced persons, enhance their coping mechanisms and build their resilience in the new areas of return. The sector facilitates assistance and strengthen protection and dignity of the displaced as whole with beneficiaries to attain durable solutions. BOAID will continue to expand its existing Early recovery and livelihood sector services in Borno State, Northeast Nigeria which currently includes Camps, Host communities and Returnees in Jere, MMC, Konduga, Ngala, and Kala balge LGA. With the specific activities which includes; - Tailoring - Beads Making - Cap making and - Barbing saloon The proposed project will specifically contribute towards enhancing coping mechanisms, and increasing livelihood opportunities of the marginalized people in the above stated areas, taking into account the natural risks and disasters and ensuring communities’ long-term livelihood sustainability and coping mechanism. The project will examine disaster-prone areas, areas of return and, on the basis of participatory studies, it will build up strategic efforts to increase skills and knowledge of the targeted beneficiaries in planning income-generation activities, exploiting markets, establishing linkages, enhancing incomes and organizing community-based institutions which will help in increasing their self reliance and dignity.

2 - Activities

List of activities and target beneficiaries: - Tailoring (90) - Cap making (316) - Beads making (294) - Barbing saloon (130) - Cash-for-work (914). The above mentioned activities were choosing based on the needs of the various communities. We have chosen to train the beneficiaries on tailoring, cap making, beads making, and cash-for-work activities (cleaning of drainage systems). The decision was made due to the aforementioned needs from the assessment conducted.Tailoring Fashion design is one the things that people do to earn a living which is why we choose tailoring as one the acquisition skills that will be given to the vulnerable groups in MMC, Jere, Konduga, Ngala and Kala balge. We will be targeting, 90 beneficiaries across the five selected local government areas and the training exercise will be conducted using two different methods. The first method will be applied in MMC and Jere were the target beneficiaries will be distributed to tailors in batches. Two beneficiaries will be assign to a single tailor and will be train for a period of four months and each beneficiary will be provided with training materials which will include scissors, yard, tape among others. The second method is to train all the beneficiaries of a particular location in one place with a single instructor. The beneficiaries will be grouped in to three different batches and the trainings will be scheduled one batch at a time. Cap Making: In this aspect we are targeting 316 beneficiaries across the five targeted locations. The training will be in batch of 30 beneficiaries per set. The beneficiaries will be provided with training materials such as niddles, trade, malti among others. Each set of 30 beneficiaries will have 3 instructors and the training will last for 3 months. Cash-for-work project for waste management: This intervention will be implemented with the goal of supporting the waste management emergency response for IDPs and host communities in Borno state. The objective of the project is to provide early recovery support to the most vulnerable through emergency employment. Priority will be given to cleaning off of waste accumulation in dumping sites and drainage systems in selected communities and promoting sustainable durable solutions within the humanitarian response framework. This program was design to address waste accumulations and illegal dump sites which, when cleaned, will reduce vector breathing sites and minimize the health risk in the host communities while providing a source of livelihood. In this context we are targeting 914 beneficiaries across the five selected local government areas.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Our work often put BOAID personnel and associates in positions of power or influence concerning the communities we work with. As a humanitarian aid organization, we are committed to creating and maintaining an environment that promotes its core values of human dignity and prevent the abuse and exploitation of its employees, partner and beneficiaries. BOAID has a duty of care towards the POCs and must not abuse power and influence that might have over the lives and well beings of the beneficiaries of its program. We will sensitize the communities on BOAID core principles relating to prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. The following core principles of behavior applies to all BOAID personnel including staff, volunteers, community member, interns and consultants while under contract with BOAID and at all times, this includes both within working hours and outside standard working hours. 1. Sexual exploitation and abuse by any staff: in BOAID constitute acts of gross misconduct and are therefore ground for termination of appointment. 2. Sexual activity with children (person under the age of 18) is prohibited 3. Exchange of money, employment, goods or services for sex, including sexual favors or other forms of humiliating or degrading behavior is prohibited. 4. Any sexual relationship between those providing assistance and protection from BOAID with the person receiving assistance is prohibited and will attract a penalty of dismissal. BOAID personnel are obliged to adhere to this standard of behavior, any violation of this standard constitute serious misconduct. Failure to comply with these standard or failure to report sexual exploitations and abuse is grounds for disciplinary action. This action may include termination of contract or dismissal. This project will provide an appropriate channels of communicating and reporting GBV and protection concerns. We will ensure that protection is mainstreamed in the whole program cycle.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Our project will ensure that feedback in complaints mechanisms is in place with necessary requirements: a shift of power, sharing power, listening, talking, adopting, been responsible and providing better quality services. We are committed to respond to and balance the needs of stakeholders in our decision, we holds ourselves accountable to both whom we seek to assist and those from whom we accept resources. We received complaints and feedbacks to provide people access to vital knowledge and information. We give our clients a chance to send their formal expressions of dissatisfaction or misconduct with something, that requires a response. The complaints may be about: 1. Quality of the project work 2. Staff or volunteer behavior: this may include disrespect towards community members, allegations of corruption, nepotism, favoritism or discrimination or allegation of sexual harassment, abuse and exploitation. We explain and sensitize our clients on the options of receiving complaints such as face to face, complaint desk, social media handles, suggestion boxes, dedicated phone/text, volunteer community visit. We always train our staff and volunteers on CFM and remind them of the dos and don'ts while receiving complaints.
