Project Summary

The objective of the project is to enhance the protective environment for displacement affected communities esp. women and children through response and remedial action, as well as environment building. Special focus will be put on support for GBV and child rights violation survivors through case management and case referral using a survivor-centered approach. 6000 GBV survivors and 1000 children will benefit from case management and FTR. 2000 most vulnerable families, that were worst affected by unlawful eviction will receive cash support to reestablish their shelters, making sure this does not trigger an artificial inflation of 'self-evictions'.
To strengthen resilience, existing community structures will be strengthened through capacity building to strengthen community - based protection - 1200 representatives will be trained and follow up support to community protection activities will be provided. DRC will also conduct protection mainstreaming trainings for non-protection actors - NGO field staff, local authorities. 3000 boys and girls will benefit from Child Friendly Spaces which will be also an entry point to share information about other available protection-focused services.
DRC will engage in advocacy for HLP rights and prevention of forced evictions and will facilitate discussions on title deeds and rental agreements between land owners, government and displaced communities.
Lastly, DRC will work with other PC partners on set up and update of referral pathways and will lead the roll out of the Protection Monitoring System to support regular monitoring of protection risks and rights abuses for evidence based advocacy and adaptive response management.

Needs

Decades of insecurity combined with intensified occurrence of natural disasters result in very limited rule of law and extremely high levels of poverty. Many Somali women, men, boys and girls are remain vulnerable to protection risks and have low positive coping capacities: 4.6 mln of them are food insecure, and 2.6 mln are displaced. Majority live in spontaneously established IDP sites, with limited and interrupted access to services, in shelters which do not meet any safety standards. The state building progress remains slow and many families live in areas outside of the governments control. Ongoing counter-terrorism offensive triggers new displacement especially into Afgoye corridor. Many HH have not recovered from the last drought. In the north mixed migration flows from Ethiopia and Yemen put people on the move at risk of multiple abuses and exploitation with almost none response services available. Necessity to share limited resources results in violent conflicts between host and displaced communities.GBV affecting women and girls remains underreported but widespread, with IDPs remaining extra vulnerable (76 per cent of people accessing GBV service were IDPs in 2016). GBV occurs while in transit to IDP sites, during water collection after dark or in shelters that offer little physical protection. IDPs from marginalized clans are reported to be more frequently affected, with limited access to justice as perpetrators often come from the host communities. Gaps exist in basic service provision for survivors oth medical and PSS.Forced evictions are increasing. 485 forced evictions occurred since January 2018, with the largest prevalence in Banaadir, Bay, Lower Juba and Mudug. Out of 39,311 evicted HH only ca 2350 HH received assistance. Many of the HH are heading to areas further away from the city, where prices of land and risk of eviction is lower, but the security situation and access of humanitarian actors is worse due to significant presence of AS.

Durable solutions/Resilience marker

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project will partly contribute to durable solutions, recognizing that emergency programing in the complex and dynamically changing context of Somalia can only have a limited contribution to durable solutions.Working from a rights-based approach DRC will ensure that relevant duty-bearers are an accountable part of the protective environment and whenever possible are supported, rather than substituted. Referral pathways will be established in coordination with local authorities, with their level of engagement corresponding to current capacity. Duty bearers will also be part of the quarterly protection monitoring joint analysis workshops. In collaboration with the HLP sub-cluster DRC will monitor eviction risks and ensure securing HLP rights is part of both protection and shelter activities implemented. The project will aim at addressing all 3 dimensions of safety, key to the achievement of durable solutions – physical, material and legal. Material safety will be addressed through integration with DRC response in other sectors in which protection will be mainstreamed. Social cohesion and peaceful coexistence will be supported analysing how targeting can minimize inter-community conflicts.DRC will be working through existing community structures, rather than establishing new ones, however their inclusivity will be evaluated and expanded to ensure all groups have access to representation. Trainings and capacity building will target members of those structures.DRC will strongly seek for complementarity of the response with the DRC-led Durable Solutions Program, a multiyear program focused on building systems, to provide a sustainable exit strategy, whenever possible. HLP rights and social accountability of formal and informal leaders will be of particular importance.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The IDP status is often strongly correlated with extreme economic vulnerabilities and insufficient social network, where the extended family members and relatives are unable to provide sufficient support. Majority of IDPs residing in spontaneous settlements come from marginalized clans with unequal access to resources. Weak clan protection and lack of resources to support positive coping strategies, makes them vulnerable to secondary exclusion, abuse and extortion from power-holders with interest to commercialize aid. DRC will consult DACs during both project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation, through existing, well-functioning community structure, where needed improving their inclusivity, to give voice also to traditionally marginalized groups, women, members of marginalized clans, youth. DRC will ensure all beneficiaries are aware of DRC feedback/complain mechanism. Being aware of the power relation between displaced and settlement managers, consultation will be done both with community leaders as well as regular residents, to increase multitude of perspectives on needs and priorities. Where appropriate DRC will adopt an area-based approach, ensuring the intervention benefits the whole community, regardless of displacement status and duration, and supports social cohesion, rather than further divisions. During mobilization sessions DRC filed staff with sound understanding of the community dynamics will provide detailed information on vulnerability-based selection criteria. DRC has strong framework for advocacy opportunities, on the issue of evictions from IDP settlements and collective centers; and promotion of land tenure security for IDPs at the onset of an emergency. For the case of returnees and IDPs in protracted situation, DRC will engage with both clan elders, regional and federal governments to provide land with title deeds to enable this category of DACs access durable solutions.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Women and youth remain especially vulnerable to protection risks and harmful cultural practice, especially GBV, child recruitment, hazardous child labor and early marriage. Their ability to access justice and remedies and enjoy rights is often limited and further weakened in case of displacement. Furthermore, stigma related to abuses (esp. SGBV) often deters survivors from accessing assistance or reporting incidents. Short-term funding and rapidly changing context often requires that established referral mechanisms need to be updated frequently, while risk of service provision interruption especially outside of main urban areas remains high.To address this DRC will ensure that specific needs of women, children and elderly are included when designing and implementing the project. DRC will seek ways to increase the space for female and youth leadership, recognizing their role within communities, as an enabler for protection outcomes. This will be done both through community awareness as well as activity design ensuring the physical access is safe (central location of infrastructure, installation of solar lights, access routes, timing of activities). Focus will also be put on HLP rights for women, whenever title deeds or land rent agreements are signed. Any response will be implemented using the survivors-centred approach and in line with principles of safety, confidentiality, respect and non-discrimination. Protection of personal data will be key. Daily field presence of well-trained and gender-balanced DRC protection staff, high quality of services will support building a strong relation of trust. Using the rights-based approach DRC will ensure that all activities build on protection standards but also leverage national frameworks - Action Plans between the FGS and UN to prevent and respond to grave child rights violations; Sexual Offence Bill.DRC will ensure proper coordination, especially on referrals to maximize quality use of already limited resources.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

DRC is aware that, abuses against civilians, including wide spread sexual and gender-based violence (GBV), recruitment and use of children, indiscriminate and disproportionate attacks on civilian areas and infrastructure, explosive hazards and forced displacement, remain a pervasive feature of the conflict in Somalia. Additionally, natural disasters further affect the already fragile coping capacities and slow recovery process. Most affected groups include women, children, youth and marginalized communities, especially the most vulnerable (older people, persons living with disabilities, female-headed households etc.) Through the Protection Cluster DRC is spearheading the protection monitoring initiative – establishing a Somali-wide PM system that will monitor key protection concerns across Somalia to identify trends and patterns of protection risks. Based on this, DRC will further engage in evidence – based joint advocacy with other protection actors, as well as the international community to address these protection concerns, breaching both IHL and human rights. DRC will support survivors of abuses esp. GBV through case referral and case management and provision of psycho-social support. Child Friendly Spaces will be established to provide a safe space to children affected by conflict. DRC will also support community-based protection mechanism and leverage existing positive coping strategies.
