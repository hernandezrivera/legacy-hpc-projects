Needs

In 2018 and 2019, the information centres/hubs have worked as a catalyst in the humanitarian response to the Rohingya crisis by not only ensuring that people had access to vital information and communication sources but also enabling a space for people to share their inputs, give feedback and lodge complaints. In JRP 2019 midterm review, it was reported that 92% of refugees say that they have adequate information to make decisions about their daily lives, compared to 23% in late 2017 and 84% in July 2018. This is indeed an impressive progress where the CwC information centres have played a vital role as part of the overall humanitarian response. However, the information and communications needs are constantly changing due to the changes occuring in the context of the crisis such as repatriation and relocation discssions and also due to other factors such as heavy rain/monsoon, breakout of AWD and so on. Considering the poor literacy and comprehension skills, Action Against Hunger will continue to develop context specific easy-to-understand audio-visual communication contents to disseminate vital messages in person and through centre based and outreach activities. Observing the growing tensions, low levels of social cohesion and interaction between the Rohingya and host communities, Communication with Communities (CwC) including behavior change initiatives are very much needed to avoid potential conflict and bring social cohesion among new refugees, old refugees and host community. This project will directly address information needs by implementing CwC information centres for both Rohinya and Host communities. Empowering Rohingya refugees and host communities to provide feedback is key to enable a relevant, conflict-sensitive and localised response. Using established feedback systems supplemented with additional feedback through targeted outreach and engagement, such as perception surveys and focus group discussions, will help provide data that can be consolidated and shared with CwC Working Group to support the sectors’ decision-making. This project will also undertake research activities to understand different kinds of barriers that still remain and influence affected communities to access information services or provide their feedback/complaints, especially since almost a quarter of refugee households reported that that their opinion is rarely or never taken into account. . Action Against Hunger remains flexible about the research design and objectives as the CwC needs and context are dynamics. Action Against Hunger will consult with the CwC working group members during the process of research activities to maximise the quality and impact.Action Against Hunger will continue to collect disaggregate data by sex, age and disability; ensure gender balanced teams (staff and volunteers); use mechanisms and create opportunities to engage community members and ensure equal access of women, girls, boys and men in the design, implementation and evaluation of Action Against Hunger’s integrated programs. The information centres will also raise awareness on nutrition, health, livelihoods, mental health, gender, gender based violence, protection and SRH.

Activities or outputs

Community Engagement:• Increased access to information- awareness and knowledge of rights for both refugee and host populations;• Opportunities provided for women and adolescent girls to co-design protection, WASH and nutrition programs and services;• Strengthen mechanisms through Communication with Communities (CwC) initiatives to systematically gather input from women and adolescent girls regarding their needs and proposed solutions;• Collaborate with women and adolescent girls to co-design programs and services that respond to their self-identified needs and priorities;• Ensure SADD- sex, age and disability disaggregated data;• Provide information and support related to Gender, GBV, Protection and SRHR through women and adolescent-friendly spaces, spaces for elderlies, home visits (for persons with special needs, eg: PLWs and persons with disability), Mens’ Forums, school-based sessions;• Achieve and ensure 100% gender balance in information centres/hubs staffing (including volunteers);• Outreach activities including community mobilisation on disaster preparedness (DRR); Information and feedback service centers/facilities:• Use advance data management and monitoring system including case-management (DHIS2-PDA software) to analyse progress on integrated program and will be shared and dicussed through consultation with/as per guidance from CwC WG;• Strengthen complaint and feedback mechanisms;• Strenthen referral mechanisms to coordinate among sectors and working groups; Communication Resources:• Develop information content on Gender, GBV, Protection and SRHR in addition to other integrated programme content (eg. Nutrition, Health, WaSH, Mental Health, Care Practices);• Active participation in coordination meetings and sector initiatives (eg. for advocacy, communication). Capacity Developement:• Capacity building of staffs on Gender Equality and GBV for ACF and it’s partners;• Training and skills development of staff and volunteers; Assessment/Research/Studies:• Undertake research and conduct analysis to inform strategic planning for CwC initiatives. The research design will be defined in consultation with the CwC Working Group to address the changing needs and context of the CwC sector;
