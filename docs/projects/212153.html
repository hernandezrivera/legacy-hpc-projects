Project Summary

The FSA 2024 Syria HNO indicates worsening food security, impacting 15.5 million people (65% of the population), based on a country-wide assessment of 42,000 HHs. This marks an increase from last year, with 12.9 million needing food assistance and 2.6 million at risk. Concentrated in areas like Hassakeh, Ar-Raqqa, and Deir-ez-Zor, food insecurity is exacerbated by displacement, with 73.4% of IDPs outside camps affected. Recurring disease outbreaks, drought, and economic instability escalate mortality, morbidity, and humanitarian needs. Economic challenges, including currency depreciation, spur population movements. Agricultural constraints, such as climate change and limited inputs, strain vulnerable farmers. In response and aligned with FSL, ERL sectors' strategic outcomes; and the IRC's 2024 ERD strategy; IRC targets economic recovery and development through enhancing livelihood opportunities, climate-smart agriculture, and market systems reinforcement. Prioritizing vulnerable groups in Hassakah and Raqqa, through the provision of vocational training, SME grants, and agriculture inputs for income generation. Cash for work activities support community infrastructure rehab while providing cash injections. IRC establishes VSLA groups for financial services, FFSs, and supports agricultural communities through skill-building and improved technologies. Gender equity is mainstreamed, ensuring equal participation, with affirmative action for female beneficiaries and SADD information collected and reported. Community tension due to perceived inequality and discrimination poses risks for staff. Mitigation includes establishing area-based coordination groups, ongoing engagement with community leaders, and clear communication. Selection criteria based on vulnerabilities and collaboration with protection service providers ensure targeted assistance. Continuous M&E with feedback mechanisms, clear complaint channels, community participation, and staff training on humanitarian standards and PSEA are crucial. The medium likelihood and high impact of beneficiary safety risks and exploitation are mitigated through collaborative planning, coordination, and adjusting project plans. Integrating protection priorities, staff adherence to IRC policies, conflict sensitivity, and risk assessment tools ensure safety and accessibility. GBV mitigation measures aligned with international guidelines reduce risks in project locations. FSL and livelihoods team, supported by the protection team, will promote "Do No Harm" principles and PSEA. Working with community leaders, representatives, and elders, IRC raises stakeholder awareness about rights and responsibilities. Clients are informed of entitlements and support procedures. Stakeholders and staff are sensitized to uphold humanitarian principles. IRC has trained all staff, including field staff, in PSEA. Confidential reporting mechanisms are available to staff and clients, encouraging use of IRC's feedback system. IRC will integrate program activities with the protection sector to assist survivors and ensure referral pathways during beneficiary selection, data sharing, and follow-up. IRC ensures marginalized groups review selection criteria, conducts FGDs, and engages authorities to clarify services and expectations. Pre-project security assessments address conflict risks, and clear complaint and feedback mechanisms mitigate impartiality or inequality risks. Protection Risk Analysis identifies tension in the community due to perceived inequality, addressed through robust selection criteria mechanisms and ongoing engagement with community leaders. Medium likelihood risks, like physical safety of clients, Taxation, and exploitation/abuse, are mitigated through collaborative implementation plans, amendments to project plans, and awareness of complaint mechanisms among clients.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The proposed initiatives target beneficiaries facing financial shocks prioritizing vulnerable groups from both the IDP and host communities to improve their livelihoods status through integrated early recovery and livelihood activities support and by providing access to income-generating opportunities in Hassakah, and Raqqa governorates. Almost 408 households in 2024 are protected and/or restored income generating through appropriate market skills/inputs/services including vocational training, apprenticeship, SME grants, and business start-up kits, as well 260 households with appropriate agriculture inputs (seeds, fertilizer, tractor plowing hours) and farming tools needed to increase the high-quality wheat seeds production both for domestic consumption and for income generating household’s level. In addition, 200 households in 2024 will be engaged in temporary employment through cash for work involving rehabilitating prioritized community infrastructure. Cash for work activities are intended to provide households with much-needed cash injections to meet their basic needs while rehabilitating community assets and infrastructure that are vital to sustaining and restoring the livelihoods of communities. Additionally, IRC will work to strengthen economic citizenship and improve community mobilization by establishing 11 VSLA groups, totaling 270 members in 2024 to provide simple savings, and loan facilities and provide a form of self-insurance to members in a community that does not have access to formal financial services. The IRC will support the agriculture community through skill-building via improving agriculture extension and veterinary service activities which play a crucial role in boosting agricultural productivity, increasing food security, and improving rural livelihoods by ensuring that 1000 farmers and livestock breeders in 2024 have access to improved agriculture technologies. Agriculture extension and veterinary services activities will cover a broad area including improved crop varieties, livestock management, improved water management, modern irrigation methods, and control of weeds, pests, and diseases through the mobile veterinary team, training, visuals posters, and brochures, electronic media (WhatsApp app groups), learning by experimentation, field trips, and exchanges, and Field Farmer Schools (FFS). The IRC will mainstream gender equity and promote gender inclusion, allowing the most vulnerable including disadvantaged women, girls, boys, PWDs and men to have equal and meaningful participation in the ERD program and to realize benefits for their own well-being. A participatory beneficiary identification and selection includes agreeing on affirmative action to register at least 50% female beneficiaries.
