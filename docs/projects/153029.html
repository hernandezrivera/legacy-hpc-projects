Project Summary

The IRC has 25 years of experience working in South Sudan which uniquely positions IRC to continue to provide assistance in established field sites. IRC has field offices located in Aweil East, Aweil South, Panyijar, Rumbek and Pamir and has longstanding relationships with the government, local authorities and communities in these geographic areas. Furthermore, IRC provides health and nutrition programming in each of these states, offering a foundation upon which to build strengthened nutrition service
This project will address life-saving humanitarian needs in Aweil East and Aweil South Counties, which are some of the counties with chronically high GAM rates of above 20%. SMART surveys conducted in Aweil East and Aweil South in June and August 2018, respectively, reported high prevalence of malnutrition in the two counties. The survey findings indicated that the nutritional status in Aweil East was very critical by GAM prevalence WHZ 21.2% %, which would require integrated strategies to control the situation. In Aweil South, the GAM rates were reported at 16.3 %. In Aweil East, the survey indicated high prevalence of acute malnutrition among children in the youngest age groups 6 -23 months with GAM 27.1% as compared to children aged 24-59 months with GAM of 16.4%. In Panyijiar County, GAM levels have consistently been above 15% with insecurity and influx of IDPs from Leer and Mayendit counties as aggravating factors. In Rumbek East, the coverage of the community management of malnutrition (CMAM) program is quite low and requires scale up of nutrition interventions to address needs. According to the FSNMS round 20 (at state level), in Lakes State, the prevalence of MAM and SAM in children under five years of age at 21.7% and 5.9 % respectively. The FSNMS also estimated the prevalence of PLW with MAM as 31.4% in Rumbek East. Aweil East is one of the counties with a high GAM rate and IPC of level 4 on the Nutrition Cluster strategy paper severity ranking matrix. The project will be integrated the health, child protection, WASH and food security sectors supported by UNICEF, WFP, OFDA and HPF in the counties.
The main components of this project are:
1) Management of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) among children under five years of age
2) Management of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) among children under five years of age
3) Management of malnutrition among Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW)
4) Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergency (IYCFE)
Treatment will directly target children under 5 years of age (without discrimination between boys and girls) and PLWs. The project will ensure inter-sectoral collaboration with the health sector for provision of stabilization kits for SAM with medical complications. Nutrition Cluster partners will provide malaria screening and referral/treatment for SAM and MAM cases. The WASH Cluster will support partners in the county to ensure the provision of clean and safe water for OTP/TSFP services, including appetite tests, hand washing, and appropriate ablution facilities. FSL Cluster partners in the county shall conduct general food distribution sessions that target households with SAM and MAM beneficiaries. Throughout the project cycle, community outreach and mobilization will be conducted to ensure early detection and improved coverage of the nutrition services in the targeted areas by the mobile and outreach teams. Needs

Based on the September 2018 Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis famine is still possible in certain areas of South Sudan. According to the report, 6.1 million people (59% of the total population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at the peak of the lean season (July – August 2018), of whom 47,000 were in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5 - famine) and 1.7 million were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). The IPC forecasts that an anticipated earlier than normal start of the lean season will result in an estimated 5.2 million (49% of the total population) people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity between January and March 2019, with 36,000 in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5 - famine). These estimates are in the presence of planned humanitarian food assistance. The level of acute malnutrition is attributed to severe food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition services, high morbidity, extremely poor diets and poor sanitation and hygiene. Persistent insecurity and armed conflict have disrupted livelihood activities, affected market functionality and limited physical access to markets. A number of coping mechanisms are being used when households do not have enough food, including reduced meal size, skipping meals; taking one meal a day and selling assets in order to generate money to buy food. Of 22 SMART surveys conducted between September 2017 and January 2018, 8 surveys showed Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates above the 15% WHO threshold. All counties in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (except Aweil Centre), showed Critical levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM 15.0%-29.9%). A SMART survey conducted in August 2018 in Aweil South and in June 2018 Aweil East reported high prevalence of malnutrition in the two counties. The survey findings classified Aweil East nutritional status as very critical by GAM prevalence WHZ 21.2% % and this calls for more integrated strategies to control the situation. In Aweil South, the GAM rates were reported at 16.3 %. The survey in Aweil East noted high prevalence of acute malnutrition among children in the youngest age groups 6 -23 months with GAM 27.1% as compared to children aged 24-59 months with GAM of 16.4% In the September 2018 IPC release, Aweil East was the only county in NBeG classified as IPC 4. The FSNMS round 20 at state level estimated the prevalence of MAM and SAM in children under five years of age at 21.7% and 5.9 % respectively in Lakes State. Similarly, the FSNMS estimates the prevalence of PLW with MAM as 31.4% in Rumbek East. Due to its geographical position and climatic changes, Greater Lakes State continues to be exposed to seasonal flooding which has destroyed crops and livelihoods. Food insecure populations sharply increased also due to the reduced harvest, disruption of trade flows, high prices and reduced households’ purchasing power. During the lean season, the communities mainly depend on wild food collection, fishing, selling grass, firewood and local poles with support received from relatives and friends. Throughout 2017 and 2018 Panyijiar county in southern Unity state has experienced food insecurity ranging from IPC 3 to IPC 4. Famine was declared in Unity State in February 2017 after months of a deteriorating food security situation across South Sudan. This led to concerted efforts by the humanitarian community to contain the famine. The September 2018 IPC reported lingering risk of famine in some part of Unity State during the lean season.

Activities

• Transportation of nutrition supplies: This will involve prepositioning of nutrition supplies in hard to reach areas• Formation of mother to mother support groups: This will involve training and establishment of mother to mother support groups (MtMSG) at the health facility and for community outreach• Screening/testing and treatment of malaria and other illnesses (URTI, UTI) in outpatient therapeutic feeding programs (OTPs) among children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM)• Training of health workers on CMAM and maternal infant and young child feeding (MIYCN): All the facility (stabilization center, OTP, and targeted supplementary feeding program) workers will be trained on the new South Sudan CMAM and MIYCN guidelines• Training of community nutrition volunteers (CNVs) on screening, detection and referral of malnutrition• Provision of soap for handwashing: Caretakers with children in OTPs will be provided with handwashing soap and will be oriented on handwashing and safe preparation of food• Initiation of breastfeeding and provision of nutrition education in health facilities/maternity wards• Cooking demonstrations: This will involve demonstrations on cooking and consumption of nutritious foods during pregnancy and complementary feeding • Establishment of kitchen gardens: The program will establish kitchen gardens at OTPs with hand pumps as part of demonstrating to caregivers how to cultivate nutritious foods• Procurement of Stabilization Centre (SC) infection prevention and control supplies. This includes detergents and other supplies in infection control at the SC.• Conduct of health and nutrition campaigns: This includes national immunization days and nutrition calendar events • Train all nutrition staffs on GBV and establish referral system and linkage between nutrition and GBV program • Provide ECD training for all SC staff and provide ECD counseling for SAM children and caretakers

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

IRC will provide beneficiaries with information through health committees, community health workers and through community dialogue sessions planned within the community.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Information, education and communication materials developed will be translated in the local language and target specific age group and gender. Hence messages will be tailored to target women, men, boys and girls.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

All staff will undergo an induction on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse and will be required to sign a code of conduct that mandates they report any PSEA cases

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Consultations have been conducted with community volunteers and community health workers who are community members. Further consultations have been done with targeted groups within the communities, including women and men.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The IRC implements and monitors programs through the inclusion of clients and other stakeholders in the feedback mechanism on its delivery performance. Room will be given to clients to report any IRC related misuse of resources, lack of effectiveness, timeliness or responsiveness. A communication structure is in place in collaboration with a Complaints Response Mechanism and includes guidance on how to handle complaints and report them to the IRC Management. The community health committees established will serve as forums where feedback on program implementation can be shared and ideas on further improving activities generated.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Health committee members will be involved during quarterly supportive supervision of this project.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Internally displaced persons and vulnerable groups lack high quality health care because of their poor socioeconomic status, long travel distances, lack of a proper public transport systems, insecurity and marginalization as a result of local clan dynamics. IRC will ensure services are provided to IDPs and vulnerable groups impartially and at no cost.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The main barriers to accessing health and nutrition care in these areas include lack of quality health services including lack of emergency obstetric and neonatal care and minimal staff capacity. In addition to the above, the recurrent conflicts have disrupted routine health programs leaving a large sector of the population without access to health services. Hence, IRC will recruit and engage qualified service providers to provide services and outreach responses that create more access to health and nutrition services.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Women and children remain the most vulnerable to health and nutrition shocks because of their multifaceted physiological needs. IRC’s interventions are tailored in such a way that they will address specific needs of women and children in this project.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The proposed project supports the gender theme in that it promotes nutrition service provision to all sections of the population including men, women, girls and boys, without discrimination.
