Project Summary

In 2022, PIB will continue providing support to improve access to quality, gender-responsive and inclusive learning opportunities in a safe and protective environment for Rohingya refugees and host community children, adolescents and youth (aged 3 to 24 years). PIB will continue education interventions in camps and host communities through direct implementation and local implementing partners. PIB will sustain its community-based learning facilities (CBLF) modality and establish and refurbish new ECD, LCFA, Myanmar Curriculum and Youth Functional Literacy facilities. Project learning facilities will benefit from improved WASH facilities through setting up hand washing stations, distribution of basic hygienic , MHM kits and COVID19 preventing materials. Facilitators and learners for the project LF will be jointly identified with community members and financially supported through 2022. This project includes an evident disability inclusion focus, aiming to identify and assess disability status of learners attending learning facilities and cater individualized support for those most vulnerable, including conditional cash transfer to reduce learners drop out. In 2022, PIB will assure the quality of the project’s learning opportunities through updating and upgrading teaching and learning modules for our various education levels, ensuring inclusion, gender sensitiveness and SEL is mainstreamed across our learning facilities. PIB will build the capacities of SBK, LCFA, MC ,FLN, host primary and secondary school teachers/facilitators and sector education partners on gender responsive pedagogy, inclusive education, theme-based training, crisis sensitive education and planning to become better teachers and improve learning outcomes of refugee and host communities’ learners. PIB will also identify and build the capacities of Rohingya women to become Myanmar Curriculum teachers and create a pool of female facilitators. In 2022, PIB is planning to incorporate technology-based teaching and learning approaches in a sample of learning facilities across the various educational levels to assess its impact and benefits on students' learning outcomes. To ensure students learning progress is assessed and tracked, PIB will conduct regular learning assessment and an individualizes progress tracking system put in place. In this JRP 22, PIB will continue to support the community engagement and capacity strengthening of Education Sector partners and affected communities to enhance their participation in the education of children, adolescents and youth. Key community stakeholders, parents and members of the learning facilities committees will be trained on gender, girls education, disability inclusion, child protection and safeguarding. PIB will also train LF management committees on DRR to jointly design emergency preparedness and response plans to equip LF and communities with required skills to respond to potential emergencies. Furthermore, PIB education partners will be benefiting from capacity building on gender responsive pedagogy, SEL, inclusive education and crisis sensitive education planning and management.

1 - Identified Needs

Through this proposal, PIB is aiming to address the main barriers and needs of Rohingya refugees and host community children, especially girls and children with disability, to access quality inclusive education. • High percentage of children not regularly attending school or learning centres even before the COVID-19 crisis. In Cox’s Bazar, prior to COVID-19 related school and learning centre closures, the number of students not attending classes in the previous 30 days ranged from 24% for children aged 3 to 10 in host communities, rising to 48% for children aged 15 to 18. In the camps the non-attendance figures were approximately 12.5% for children aged 3 to 10 (13% for girls), increasing by age to reach 77% for children aged 15 to 18 (93% for girls). • Facilities location and safety issues for children on the way to school have also been reported as key barriers in host communities, especially for girls. In addition, an increased demand for teachers has led many to seek higher-paid employment in the camps with humanitarian organizations, adversely impacting the availability and quality of education in the host communities. These are the main needs identified through the MYRP's strategy development;• In the camps, caregivers and teachers have reported various challenges, including a lack of adequate teaching and learning materials. Teacher absenteeism, teacher misbehavior and physical punishment practices have also been reported as challenges by students. Appropriate space to teach and learn in crowded camps is a further pervasive barrier, impacting all children but particularally impacting on access for girls due to the importance of gender segregated classrooms and latrines, especially adolescent girls. • Educational attendance of adolescent girls remains extremely limited in both the camps and host communities. In the camps, among those aged 11 to 14, girls are less likely than boys to attend education facilities, with this trend increasing with age, resulting in the highest proportion of children not in school being Rohingya adolescent girls. Similar challenges are faced by adolescent girls in host communities. This trend is enhanced by social and religious barriers that restrict mobility of girls in public once they reach adolescence, as well as the prevalence of early marriage. This contributes to social isolation and exclusion, low levels of foundational literacy, psychological distress, exploitation, and abuse. It has, however, been found that Parents are more likely to send their girls to education facilities if there are female teachers and sex-separated classrooms.• In the Camps there has been some success at recruiting a high percentage of female teachers, with 56% of teachers being female. However, the majority of female teachers are from the host community and are Bangladeshi, with female Rohingya teachers only comprising 25% of the total. This presents a challenge in the roll out of the Myanmar curriculum, with greater numbers of female teachers with sufficient Myanmar language skills needing to be recruited and trained. • Significant challenges also exist for children with disabilities, who attend learning centers at much lower rates than their peers across all age groups for both girls and boys. This is contributed to by insufficient skills and training of the educators, and poor awareness, negative attitudes and stigma related to children with disabilities attending the same learning centres as children without disabilities. Learning facilities also commonly lack ramps and accessible seating arrangements, toilets, and water facilities, combined with surrounding terrain that is often steep and rough and difficult to navigate, and there is poor availability of assistive devices. A lack of inclusive learning and teaching materials, including teaching materials suitable for children with learning difficulties, is an additional barrier.
