Project Summary

Conflicts, climate change, locust invasion and the COVID-19 pandemic have been drivers of vulnerability, which have led to access to basic social services being extremely low in Somalia. This has left communities with socio-economic vulnerabilities behind, negatively affecting their children’s access to education services. Vulnerable girls, boys and adolescents have consequently been denied access to inclusive quality education in a safe and protective learning environment.
The 2018-2019 EMIS shows the national Gross Enrolment Rate (GER) for primary education is at 30% for primary level and 18% in rural areas. The 2019 Somalia Joint Multi Cluster Needs Assessment indicates 58% of girls in host communities and 75% of IDPs girls were not attending schools. The dropout rate in host communities is around 30% and among IDPs around 17%. The disparities between access and attendance to school in urban and rural communities are also significant, with marginalized groups such as girls, IDPs, children with disabilities, children in hard-to-reach areas being the most affected.
There is major concern of high number of children not accessing schools and increased number of children at risk of dropping out of school. The shortage in educational opportunities exacerbates protection risks, putting children at great risk of harmful practices, including child marriage, child labor and recruitment by armed groups, as well as exposing them to other vices.
The COVID-19 pandemic led to 5-months school closure. The secondary impacts of COVID-19 has exposed children to higher risks of abuse and increased psychosocial distress as a result of confinements. The COVID-19 pandemic has indirect impacts on human capital formation through deteriorated educational outcomes as it affects enrolment, age-appropriate attendance and educational grade achievements. This impact will have long term effects on the delivery of educational services. The 2020 GBV/FGM Assessment and 2020 UNICEF survey underscore that protection risks have been further compounded by the school closure due to the COVID-19 outbreak. The prolonged closure of schools was not only a loss of education but also many children’s main source of sustainable access to food, clean drinking water, social care and a safe haven for play and interaction.
The UNICEF survey indicated an increase in child labor with 12% of children engaged in forced labour without pay, while 35% of children experiencing violence at home. 20% of boys and girls faced sexual abuse and harassment and many vulnerable children still face challenges that work against their re-entry to school, including financial struggles. The secondary impact of COVID-19 on already impoverished families has been severe placing them at greater risk of deepening poverty and long-term negative impacts on their social and health situations. 46% of parents are unable to provide basic needs for their children as well as support their access to education services, due to the diminished economic opportunities and the weakened social institutions (Save the Children survey 2020). It highlights 20% of children reporting negative impact on children’s psychosocial well-being due to being out of school.
UNICEF’s Emergency Education project will work closely with Child Protection and WASH to promote an integrated approach. Attention will be laid on enhancing safe and protective learning environment that are inclusive, and which meet the needs of vulnerable girls, women, boys and men, including those of persons with disabilities, for both displaced and host communities. UNICEF’s education response will focus on mitigating the impact of emergencies on education by ensuring improved access to education, retaining children in schools, and building the government’s capacity education in emergencies. 160,000 (80,000 girls) most vulnerable and marginalized children with 16,000 (8,000 girls) being children with disabilities will be targeted.

Mitigation Measures

Protection of displaced civilians: Centrality of protection will be the key area this response focuses on, to ensure children have access to inclusive learning opportunities in safe and protective environments. The interventions will be designed in a way that addresses protection risks related to the impacts of conflict and natural disasters. To ensure the protection of children, the project will include an integrated Education – Child Protection response, in line with the Education – Child Protection Response Framework. The interventions will consequently ensure improved safe and protective learning environments with access to protection services to reduce vulnerabilities and strengthen the safety of learners. In line with the Education Cluster Response Plan, the project’s strategy will have a two-pronged approach; a) prioritizing education support to children at the place where they primarily live, and b) support education to children at place where they have moved due to displacement, to ensure they have continued access to education and protection services. Mitigating risks related to exclusion from humanitarian services: UNICEF and the implementing partners will ensure community consultations and discussions take place during the assessment, planning, implementation and monitoring phases of the project. This will ensure the views and needs of the communities taken into consideration, as the stakeholders themselves are best placed to find the most suitable solutions to address their own problems. Key stakeholders, such as Community Education Committee (CECs), community leaders, parents, teachers and all learners will be engaged, to raise awareness on child rights and influence behaviors and practices that enhance overall enabling learning environments for children. At the school level, CtCs will support peer to peer support, rights awareness and will take part in schools' decision making, to ensure children's voices are heard. The project will be designed to address the specific needs of girls, in recognition of the specific challenges they face, including the provision of sanitary kits to adolescent girls to ensure regular school attendance. This will enhance girls’ access to learning opportunities. Protection mainstreaming: The community will be engaged, to ensure improved safety at and on the way to and from schools. In addition, the communities will be involved in the establishment of safe and violence-free environments with improved infrastructure, such as gender responsive learning and sanitation facilities. Teachers will be trained in psychosocial support and provide referral pathways for children in need of specialized child protection services, such as counselling and case management. Schools will identify a male and female teacher to be the child protection focal point of the school, who will support learners in need of protection support.

Please provide further explanation

he project will be anchored and aligned to key priorities of the Somali Government’s 2017-2021 Education Sector Strategic Plan (ESSP). This will provide opportunities for humanitarian-development linkages. The response will continue to support the strengthening of the Government’s systems, to ensure children benefit from increased access to education services. Engagement of strategic partnerships between civil society, UN agencies, donors and the Government, including the regional authorities, will be strengthened to benefiting from each stakeholder’s comparative advantage as well as to ensure complementarity and synergies between humanitarian and development actors. With the ongoing effort to improve policy environment, strengthening systems for planning provide an opportunity that will complement education in emergencies initiatives. UNICEF will work closely with the Ministry of Education at all levels to strengthen their capacity, to enhance effective governance, management, coordination and monitoring, thus contributing to a more effective and efficient education system that is responsive to the learning needs of children – whether in emergency or as part of the long term development context. This is with a vision to eventually support the Government to fully take up responsibilities embedding education in emergencies in the mainstream education. This will further enhance long term sustainability, improve governance and enhance capacity to reinforce quality delivery of services. Extensive engagement of communities and local authorities in taking the lead in school governance will foster local ownership and greater parental and community buy-in.
