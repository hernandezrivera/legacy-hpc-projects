Project Summary

Northern Mozambique, especially Cabo Delgado province has been affected by armed conflict, violence and insecurity caused by non-state armed groups (NSAG) from October 2017, resulting in the destruction of civilian infrastructure, disruption of local economies and agriculture-based livelihoods. The conflict is complex and multi-dimensional, with several endogenous and exogenous factors and dynamics contributing to its persistence. Whilst the NSAGs have driven violence, the conflict is rooted, amongst other causes, in poverty and inequality. In the endogenous dimension, the main factors identified include phenomena of socioeconomic inequalities and expectations related to the exploitation of natural resources, especially among local youth. This adds to factors of perceived political exclusion, limited participation, and few economic opportunities, affecting young people. Northern Mozambique is in fact one of the country's areas with higher poverty indicators, levels of unemployment, illiteracy, and limited access to basic services such as healthcare and education. The provinces of Niassa, Cabo Delgado and Nampula host 36% of the Mozambican population, with a significantly higher levels of poverty than the central and southern regions. Cabo Delgado has a poverty prevalence rates of around 50%. One-third of children and youth aged 5 to 24 are not enrolled in school; the average secondary school enrollment rate is 13.3% in Cabo Delgado, while the national level is 23.9%. Gender parity in primary education is also below the national average.
In Cabo Delgado Province, the armed conflict has disrupted the lives and livelihoods of 1.28 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs). Many IDPs have fled conflict multiple times — forcing them to abandon their personal belongings, assets, livelihoods and means of survival with each displacement. Despite continued fighting, according to the DTM data of August 20237, there were a total of 627,846 IDPs still leaving in resettlement sites or in host communities, while 540,958 IDPs have returned to their home district and the number of IDPs have reduced from over a million, prompted by the pursuit for better livelihood opportunities.8 Returning IDPs require both humanitarian assistance and resilience building interventions to meet their basic needs and to successfully rebuild their livelihoods for self-reliance. Mozambique is also prone to extreme climatic shocks (cyclone, floods, drought) which further decapitalize already vulnerable households.
Currently, FAO assists IDPs, host communities and returnees to produce their own food and build resilience, actions that guarantee the livelihood of the population as well as improve their self-sufficiency. In line with the current human mobility tendency, FAO intends to expand its emergency and resilience assistance to returnees and will work to restore the agricultural capacity of returnee families, while continuing to assist IDPs and host community families in displacement sites. This approach is shared by the government, which has requested urgent livelihoods support for returnees.
With a total ask of USD 17 207 008.00 FAO can support 430 175 IDPs, host community members and returnees in 22 districts of Cabo Delgado (17), Niassa (3) and Nampula (2) to rebuild and restore agriculture-based livelihoods (crops, livestock and, fisheries) and improve households' food and nutrition security, laying the foundation for resilient and people-centered agrifood systems. These interventions will be combined with FAO’s technical assistance and training on good agricultural and fisheries practices, animal husbandry and health and crop post-harvest and post-catch losses management/reduction to maximize production and food availability.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

For Mozambique Humanitarian Response Plan 2024 FAO has targeted 430 175 people need - IDPs, host community members and returnees - in 17 districts of Cabo Delgado, three in Niassa and two in Nampula to rebuild and restore agriculture-based livelihoods (crops, livestock and, fisheries) and improve households' food and nutrition security, laying the foundation for resilient and people-centered agrifood systems. These interventions will be combined with FAO’s technical assistance and training on good agricultural and fisheries practices, animal husbandry and health and crop post-harvest and post-catch losses management/reduction to maximize production and food availability. FAO will strengthen government and local institutions and will work with key partners to support the interventions in hard-to-reach areas of northern Mozambique to facilitate a comprehensive and people-centered approach that delivers as one through local partnerships.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Crops production and LivestockProvision of agriculture inputs (seeds and tools) to plant at least 0.5 ha of land and restore returnee households’ crop production during the short agriculture cycle and the main agriculture planting season.Distribution and multiplication of small livestock (four chicken and two goats by household) through community existing structures. The community will create a revolving system of distributing small livestock to other community members even after the project closure through coordination by the agriculture Government authorities.Post-harvest inputs and equipment for cereal and pulse conservation will be provided to the beneficiaries with proper training for use and maintenance for sustainability. Trainings on Good and Smart Agriculture Practices including basic care, husbandry, feeding and health of small livestock (chicken and goats) will be carried out through the Government extension services involving lead farmers, local radiomen for dissemination of technologies. In addition to that community vaccinators which are volunteers will be trained on chicken vaccination against Newcastle Disease (ND) and goats deworming.Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) to assess the effectiveness and outcome of the interventions, as well as the beneficiaries’ level of satisfaction will be carried out. Fisheries based livelihoodsPurchase and delivery of equipment – fisher boats, gear kits, cool boxes, and solar refrigerators. Restoration and strengthening of the cold chain to reduce post-harvest losses and increase the added value of the production, including rehabilitation of fish markets, and retail marketsTraining to fishermen on fish handling and food safety, processing, fish marketing, gear assembling will be carried out to reduce post-harvest losses and improve fisheries practices: Restoration of the fisheries management system - electronic registration of fishers, vessels and gears; revitalization of co-management system, with focus on the restructuring of the Fisheries Community Councils (CCP). Government institutions and community-based organizations strengtheningCommunity mobilization and Training of Trainers (ToT) on preparedness measures to minimize impacts of disasters on agriculture (including fisheries) livelihoods. Training of community-based organizations on anticipatory actions, preparedness and response to climate induced hazards. Training on early warning systems to empower returnee communities to prevent and act in a timely and appropriate manner when natural hazards occur.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Possible attacks from non-state armed groups can continue provoking continuous movement of IDPs and lead to further displacement of returnees, destroying productive assets and disturbing agriculture, fisheries and livestock-based livelihood activities and this can cause project delays and make people lose their productive assets. This will be mitigated by harmonizing the ADIN strategic plan and Cabo Delgado reconstruction plan to ensure IDPs and returnees are included. The project team will continue working with UNDSS to map relatively safe areas for implementation of agricultural and fisheries activities. Degeneration of operational conditions in the rainy season or due to extreme climatic events. To minimize its impact FAO will prioritize calendarization and detailed implementation planning of field activities, especially linked with agricultural campaigns, ecosystem-based adaptation, and water, considering the rainy season, accessibility conditions and potential impacts.Extreme climatic events: cyclones, floods, El Nino, and drought may happen and negatively affect agricultural activities and beneficiaries' priorities. Integrating agroforestry; early warning systems in place; prepositioning of agricultural kits (short cycle) and implementation of hydroponic vegetable production can help to minimize the negative impact of this climatic hazard.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

FAO in straight coordination with the Government and local authorities will apply gender sensitive participatory methodologies so that women can be part of the decision-making process which will enable them to play an important role in their transition from the humanitarian phase to recovery and self-reliance. This will ensure the design of participatory, displacement and gender responsive action plans focusing on priorities to address peacebuilding needs.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

While the project does not primarily target people with disabilities (PwD), consideration will be made to ensure inclusivity, leaving no one behind. PwD will be prioritized during beneficiary selection process including ensuring their participation in the decision-making process, including post implementation monitoring phase. FAO will coordinate with other UN agencies and agriculture-based livelihoods partners to ensure that the needs of PwD are taken into consideration in all the project cycle implementation period.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The opening of new production blocks (machambas) for the IDPs in host districts for crops production can contribute to deforestation and erosion if proper measures. Integrated agroforestry. This can exacerbate the negative impact of climate change on food production and productivity. Extreme temperatures ause increased incidents of pests and wilting which result in the loss of yields for both food and cash crops. Cabo Delgado province agriculture is rainfed, so the climate change can exacerbate the negative effect in agriculture. Agroforestry can be used as practice to reduce the negative effect of extreme temperatures by planting vegetable crops and/or pulses under the canopy of shade trees to minimize the impact of extreme temperatures. Other important benefits of agroforestry are: climate change mitigation through carbon sequestration, bioddiversity conservation, soil health enrichment and water quality improvement.
