Needs

The health status of the population in need has improved since the beginning of the influx but remains fragile. UNHCR is currently supporting 20 primary health care facilities through its partners. Key morbidities are upper respiratory tract infections (22%), gastritis (12%), fever of unknown origin (10%), skin diseases (7%), lower respiratory tract infections (7%) and diarrhoea (6%) (source: UNHCR HIS). Access to non-communicable disease (NCD) diagnostics and treatment remains constraint with only 2% of consultations provided for NCDs. Mental health and psychosocial support services are provided through 9 facilities as well as at community levels through 105 volunteers. Nevertheless, efforts to raise awareness and enable access need to be intensified. Skilled delivery uptake remains constraint with more than 60% of deliveries being conducted at home. Similarly, only 43% of women completed the recommended minimum 4 Antenatal Care visits (source: UNHCR HIS) and new-born care is poor. Access to SGBV services including clinical management of rape has increased but access for male survivors is constraint. Health awareness remains low, additional community based activities are needed to engage communities in disease prevention and addressing major health concerns. Key concerns raised by the community with regards to accessing health services relate to cultural, physical access and language barriers as well as staff behaviour. To address those, UNHCR will include measures to enhance access through community based referral systems and engage with refugee groups through regular feedback mechanisms. The risk of communicable disease outbreaks remains high with low EPI coverage and poor WASH services. There is an urgent need to support host community health facilities. The current influx of refugees adds an additional burden to the already overstretched health system. The main referral hospital in Cox’s Bazar has a capacity of 250 beds but provides services to 500 to 600 inpatients daily, including an average of 100 refugees. Ukhia and Teknaf health complex are similarly overburdened.

Activities or outputs

"UNHCR’s proposed project will provide comprehensive primary health services for men, women, boys and girls in line with the essential package of health services (ESP) recommended by the government and health sector. UNHCR will implement project activities in partnership with seven health partners. The proposed project will employ a strategic approach of building partnerships and capacity building including with the Ministry of Health, with a view towards sustainable solutions and inclusion of host communities. 1. Provision of comprehensive primary health care services through 21 health facilities (Objective 1)• Provision of curative, preventive and promotive health care to women, men, girls and boys in line with the ESP through 10 health centres and 11 health posts • Provision of essential medicines and equipment including NCD and psychotropic medication• Staff capacity building • Establishment of feedback committees to enhance community engagement and dialog • Improve data collection to monitor quality of care, access and utilisation through an improved Health Information System 2. Support access to sexual and reproductive health (SRH) and HIV services. (Objective 1)• Provision of SRH service to women, men and adolescents through the existing cooperation with UNFPA and implementing partners • Provision of Clinical Management of Rape for female/ male survivors, linkages to other supportive protection, legal and social services• Enable access to HIV prevention and treatment services with special emphasis on PMTCT• Provision of counselling, SRH and protection services to key populations (male/ female sex worker, LGBTIQ) through Drop-In Centres. 3. Enhance existing referral system and access to secondary/ tertiary health care services for host community and refugees (Objective 1)• Establish community based referral system through dedicated porters • Maintenance of 10 ambulances for emergency medical referrals • Support patients with translation, obtaining services and conduct payment of hospital referral charges through dedicated referral worker • Construction of outpatient department in Sadar hospital, provision of human resources to Sadar hospital 4. Integrate mental health component in the primary health and protection services (Objective 1)• Train medical staff in 20 health facilities in mental health (MH) using mhGAP-HIG. • Maintaining psychologists and psychosocial counsellors at facilities and community-based points of access and care including in community centres and client homes.• Maintain continuous supervision and monitoring of MH trained and newly recruited Volunteers of health and protection partners to assist in identification and referral of refugees with complex needs • Train 100 refugee lay counsellors to provide peer counselling and standard therapies 5. Building community capacity for healthy living through a community-based health promotion and surveillance programme (Objective 1,3)• Recruitment, facilitation and training of 300 existing and 100 new female/ male community health workers (CHWs)• Awareness raising through home visits incl. neonatal care visits, community gatherings and other methods • Ensure availability of IEC material through, developing printing and disseminating proper awareness messages. 6. Support health sector coordination and information management efforts (Objective 4) • Continue active contribution and engagement in health sector coordination, strategic advisory group and working groups• In support of the health sector and in close cooperation with relevant partners, chair Community Health working group• In support of the health sector, strengthen field level health coordination through dedicated health coordination focal points"
