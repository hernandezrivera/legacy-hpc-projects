Project Summary

In 2021, the ETS will continue to provide overall coordination through developing and implementing plans, reporting activities, and identifying needs as the situation evolves. The ETS will hold regular Working Group meetings to ensure plans, activities and gaps are shared and discussed with partners and will act as a forum where partners can exchange information, best practices, access technical expertise and support. The ETS will also continue to publish and share regular, accurate operational Information Management products with partners and relevant stakeholders to support decision-making, avoid duplication of efforts and strengthen advocacy.
The humanitarian community’s demand for reliable communications services remains a priority. To address this, the ETS will continue to provide security communications and data connectivity services across all common operational areas to support the humanitarian and enable the provision of digital assistance to affected communities. The ETS plans to install cyclone-resilient towers which will significantly improve the radio communications capacity (multiple simultaneous talk channels) but also the access to data connectivity services, in collaboration with the Bangladesh Telecommunications Regulatory Commission (BTRC). The ETS also plans to install back up solar power solutions in all sites to ensure the full operability and sustainability of its services.
In addition, the ETS will engage with the “Communication with Communities” initiative to assess and possibly support them with ICT requirements.
To strengthen local emergency communications expertise and skills on the ground, the ETS will continue to assess the needs of staff on the ground and conduct capacity building exercises tailored to those needs. One specific skillset that requires further capacity is the implementation and maintenance of VHF and HF radio infrastructure and networks in line with UN standards. This will also ensure the sustainability of services in Bangladesh in the future.

1 - Identified Needs

The present mobile network services in the Cox’s Bazar district are sporadic and limited in coverage. This has been identified as of one the main impediments to humanitarian operations and staff safety in the field. As a service sector, the ETS mandate is to provide common security communications and technology services to support the overall humanitarian response and will therefore be pursuing this effort in 2021 for the overall humanitarian response. Following regular assessment in the camps and engagement with humanitarian organizations, the ETS identified the need to continue and expand access to data connectivity in several camps’ locations as well as the need for continue providing an independent security communications network.

2 - Activities or outputs

The ability for humanitarian responders to communicate in an emergency is paramount. Effective communication not only facilitates the response and ensures humanitarian assistance reaches those who need it most, it also ensures the safety and security of humanitarian workers. In 2021, the Emergency Telecommunications Sector (ETS) will continue to ensure a smooth response by providing overall coordination through developing and implementing plans, reporting activities, and identifying needs as the situation evolves. The ETS will hold regular Working Group meetings to ensure plans, activities and gaps are shared and discussed with partners and will act as a forum where partners can exchange information, best practices, access technical expertise and support. The ETS will also continue to publish and share regular, accurate operational Information Management products with partners and relevant stakeholders to support decision-making, avoid duplication of efforts and strengthen advocacy. The humanitarian community’s demand for reliable communications services to enable them to carry out their lifesaving work quickly and effectively remains a priority. To address this, the ETS will continue to provide security communications and data connectivity services across all common operational areas in Cox’s Bazar to support the humanitarian response as well as enabling the provision of digital assistance to affected communities. The ETS plans to install cyclone-resilient towers which will not only significantly improve the radio communications capacity (multiple simultaneous talk channels) but also the access to data connectivity services in order to meet the humanitarian responders’ needs, in collaboration with the Bangladesh Telecommunications Regulatory Commission (BTRC). The ETS also plans to install back up solar power solutions in all sites to ensure the full operability and sustainability of its services. In addition, the ETS will engage with the “Communication with Communities” initiative to assess and possibly support them with ICT requirements. To strengthen local emergency communications expertise and skills on the ground, the ETS will continue to assess the needs of staff on the ground and conduct capacity building exercises tailored to those needs. One specific skillset that requires further capacity is the implementation and maintenance of VHF and HF radio infrastructure and networks in line with UN standards. This will also ensure the sustainability of services in Bangladesh in the future.
