Project Summary

International Medical Corps aims to provide integrated lifesaving nutrition responses to conflict affected resident and displaced populations in four locations: Juba PoC; Malakal town, Malakal PoC and Nyal Panyijar County. The protracted crisis and continuous critical malnutrition situation in the selected areas requires extended services to meet the current need and prevent the incidence of the cases in future. According to the assessments conducted in the proposed areas, the undernutrition situation is classified as critical with the prevalence of GAM rates 18.5% and 19.6% for Malakal PoC and Panijiyar respectively. The presence of underlining causes of malnutrition inflates the prevalence and deteriorate the nutritional status of the targeted population.

IMC will respond to the reduction of mortality, morbidity and impact of poor growth development, by treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition among children (boys and girls) aged 0-59 month pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition through management of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), at OTP/TSFP nutrition facilities and provision of support to maternal infant young child nutrition (MIYCN) focusing more on the first 1000 days, of a child life and the mother as well through increasing awareness, encouraging adoption of optimal Maternal, Infant and Young Child feeding and hygiene practices among caregivers and household members. Mother support groups will be supported to reach caregivers/mothers.
IMC with already established Nutrition surveillance team, will undertake nutrition assessments that provide robust information on the nutrition situation of a given population, this information will be key in responding effectively to nutrition needs in the project area, as well the team will work closely with the nutrition cluster Information working group to identify key priority areas with no or limited surveillance information to conduct appropriate assessment. Capacity building of the nutrition team, MoH staff, to equip them to respond to nutrition emergency on timely management of SAM and MAM cases.
Through the proposed humanitarian response plan, IMC will reach 14,664 children under five for the treatment of SAM and MAM prevalence and 4036 PLW beneficiaries with acute malnutrition by provision of supplementary foods and creating awareness on preventing measures. PLW will form support groups and will support in raising awareness through MIYCN key messages, and sessions on nutrition package. The project will provide lifesaving nutrition services focusing on the most vulnerable, especially PLW and children under five. Similarly, following the CMAM guideline IMC will identify and target the older people (>60 years) for the TSFP program and provide the supplements according to the protocol under WFP FLA. All the elderly vulnerable beneficiaries will be provided by fortified blended food (FBF) also locally known as CSB++.
IMC will engage the affected population to participate fully in the project design and implementation to ensure acceptance, quality services and accountability, through formation of Quality, Accountability of Affected population committees in each site in Juba PoC; Malakal town, Malakal PoC and Nyal Panyijar County. Community consultative sessions will be organized on a quarterly basis by field nutrition managers, site managers and field M&E officers, to receive feedback and draw an action plan on areas to improve program implementation.
IMC has already started the process of 2019 Field level agreement with WFP for TSFP supplies in managing moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) cases and PCA with UNICEF to support severe acute malnutrition (SAM) cases covering in Juba PoC; Malakal town, Malakal PoC and Nyal Panyijar County

Needs

The 2018 FSNMS results depicted a concerning nutrition situation in most parts of the country. About two-thirds of the 78 counties reported serious and critical levels of acute malnutrition, of which 23 (about 30%) of the counties reported a critical nutrition situation. Levels of acute malnutrition are expected to deteriorate in the first quarter of 2019 with early onset of the lean season as most households are expected to deplete food stocks from their own production. The Greater Upper Nile and Unity states continues to have the highest prevalence of acute malnutrition in the country for the last four years. The Nutrition and food security of this country remain critical and in great need for humanitarian assistance, food insecurity leading to nutrition situation deterioration mainly is due to continued conflict, economy instability and lack of access for flow of goods and services in most parts of the country. This situation has not spared Juba PoC; Malakal town, Malakal PoC and Nyal Panyijar County. In July 2018, International Medical Corps conducted a SMART survey in Malakal with results indicating GAM rates of 18.5% (15.1-22.4 95% CI). When comparing the GAM prevalence of July 2018 with July 2017 GAM prevalence of 15.2% (11.2 ↔ 20.2, 95% C.I.), the worsening nutrition status and food security situation can be seen as an area of concern requiring some additional continues assistance to mitigate the aggravating factor of undernutrition. According to the SMART Survey’s findings, 41.9% (40.5% as moderate and 1.4% as severe) of the households fall under moderate and severe hunger scale while almost 64% (22.4% as low dietary diversity (DD) and 41.4% as medium DD) of the population has either medium or low dietary diversity. In addition to that, 12.2% of the households are engaged in emergency livelihood coping strategies and 44.2% were categorized as using stress livelihood coping strategies. Communities living in Malakal PoC depends mainly on humanitarian assistance and fishing, making them especially vulnerable to changes in the security situation. Based on the September 2018 IPC analysis, Panyijiar was classified to be in Phase 4 (Emergency). Furthermore, the 2018 FSNMS results depicted a concerning nutrition situation with GAM prevalence of 19.6%, SAM prevalence of 2.4%, and Prevalence of Maternal malnutrition (MUAC <23cm) of 26.9%. According to WHO classification the nutrition situation remains critical, above 15%. The last SMART survey conducted in Sept 2017 in Nyal Panyijar indicated that 85.8% of the households have experienced moderate hunger as per household hunger scale, in line with the similar finding large number of households have practiced coping strategies to meet the needs of food intake. Limiting portion sizes of meals and borrowing food from relatives or neighbors were the major coping strategies used by the population 59.7% and 50.9% respectively Contributing factors leading to higher burden of malnutrition in those project locations include poor accessibility to health and nutrition services, unprecedented levels of food insecurity, high morbidity, outbreaks of diarrhea and other illnesses, sub-optimal IYCF practices, and poor WASH practices. Displacement due to insecurity as well as higher food prices caused by rising inflation and depreciation of the local currency are further increasing the risk of under nutrition and associated mortality among children 0-59 months and women of reproductive age. Through the proposed response plan IMC will continue the provision of life saving nutrition interventions through implementation of prioritized and integrated multi-sectoral responses. The ability to meet these needs will depend on humanitarian assistance. In line with nutrition cluster strategy, IMC nutrition program will mainly focus on treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable and at-risk under-five children, pregnant and lactating women and older people (>60 years old)

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

IMC has policy on preventing and Sexual exploitation and abuse cases, At the same time provides training on PSEA to the staff members, and also ensures that humanitarian workers do not use the project material, services provided to the affected population as a bait for sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). All IMC employee contracts, include a policy to ensure that employees adhere to PSEA policy and protect affected population against sexual exploitation and will conduct awareness at community level.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Community consultation has been a key aspect of the design of this proposed project with community leaders being consulted during the ongoing projects as well as during the assessments. IMC will continue to work closely with the community, building the capacity of community health committees to encourage and increase participation. If community participation decreases during the project implementation period IMC will find advice from community leaders to seek alternative ways of engaging the community. Consultation meetings involves community leaders , religious groups, Mother support groups, target groups (Adult) and other key stakeholders in the target locations

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

IMC is having a community based feedback and complaint mechanism, where, community leaders, beneficiaries and stakeholders can use various means to provide feedback and launch complaints. All the complaints and feedback have been looked after by designated staff members and feedback has been provided for all the actions taken to adapt the feedback or resolve the conflict. The mechanism system uses all the available means like suggestion box, QAAP committees, a direct communication to staff member at office or site, and a direct global communication line to provide maximum access to the community for providing feedback or launch a complaint. IMC ensures to keep the system unbiased and uninfluenced by any person or organization.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

IMC ensures services are accessible to the targeted population by establishing sites easy and safe to access. In case of any insecurity in reaching sites, IMC establishes mobile sites for the beneficiaries for continuous services. Additionally, IMC conducts assessments like SQUEAC and bottleneck analysis (BNA) to identify the barriers to access to the services in terms of availability of services, accessibility to the services, lack of resources (financial and physical), gap in supply chain and capacity building of the staff and partners. Similarly, IMC aims to identify and provide alternate innovative approaches to switch the barriers into the boosters and enhance the quality of the services. IMC will be following the recent conducted bottleneck analysis, and work on the identified barriers. IMC will focus to identify the additional barriers by daily routine data and supervision visits.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

IMC with its PSEA policy practices zero tolerance on protection threats from the staff members and ensures to address the inequalities by gender analysis of the program, and targeting the most vulnerable people with in the community. To mitigate the protection threats in community IMC focuses on integration of the projects with available services like GBV, WASH, MIYCN, FSL and child protection. Community engagement being the key factor for the acceptability of the services and advancing the knowledge of the community, IMC forms support groups and village committees to discuss the issues and find the local solutions. IMC will work on identifying the practices creating protection threats or gender inequalities within the community and will work on molding the attitude of the practitioners. IMC will continue empowering the community through engaging and linking them with programs and develop their attitude towards lifesaving and improving life quality practices to ensure the self-sustainability.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

To involve men, women, boys and girls in the project; targets are based on the gender equality analysis and most vulnerable are selected as per the criteria. Project activities involve awareness sessions on MIYCN and WASH for the community on improvement of nutritional status, engaging communities through support groups and village committees to improve collective decision-making power and working on attitude to develop better practices to meet the needs towards quality of life. The support groups and committees are tools to involve men to develop the understanding on importance of mother and child nutrition. The involvement of men in these issues will provide the psychological support to mother and quality time to child. Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

IMC conducts studies like SAM bottleneck analysis, and SQUEAC surveys to assess the accessibility of the beneficiaries to the service delivery points and produce the recommendations to optimize the uptake of the services being provided. Additionally measures access through routine monitoring systems.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

IMC works closely with local authorities and other humanitarian actors to get security information regarding safety and access to the outreach nutrition sites. Nutrition team reaches the nutrition sites early, provides services to ensure mothers are able to travel back home early enough. In case of insecurity, leading to suspension of the activities beneficiaries are informed through their community leaders, the same is done on return of services. In case of long distance to sites, IMC ensures community based services by establishing either nearby sites or mobile sites. The measures IMC ensures that the service delivery points are child friendly, easy to access, and away from any conflicting zone like military compounds or operational areas.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

This project will prioritize safety and dignity of beneficiaries mainly women and girls through linking them to the provision of psych-social and case management services, group psycho-social activities at the women centers management and support by IMC GBV team. In addition this project will provide access to assistance and services without discrimination of sex or age and also ensuring that most vulnerable groups and groups with special needs are reached and assisted with appropriate confidentiality. Through advocacy, empowerment of targeted groups as well as use of community based committees, this project will enhance population’s ability to reclaim their rights. Increased awareness on consequences of acute malnutrition, importance of early case detection, referral and treatment will enhance utilization of available services for women, girls and boys affected by conflict. All beneficiaries' information and records are kept confidential and are not shared with irrelevant person or organization.
