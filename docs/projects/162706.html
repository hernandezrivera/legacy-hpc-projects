Project Summary

This project is aligning with cluster objectives that aiming at increase equitable access to humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining health services for those most vulnerable and in need, strengthen health sector capacity to prepare and deliver timely response to affected population by conflict, natural emergencies, and disease outbreaks, and strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, and enhance risk mitigation measures. The project is targeting 480,035(112904 boys, 122313 girls, 117513 men & 127503 women) individuals in need living in 20 localities in 8 SCI priority states including Blue Nile, Central Darfur, North Darfur, West Darfur, South Kordofan, West Kordofan, North Kordofan and Red Sea. The project will deliver package of primary health care services including the treatment of communicable and non- communicable diseases, ensuring the availability of essential medicines, provision of MIPS for RH to support reproductive, neonatal, and child health, strengthening the expanded program of immunization EPI, infection prevention, health education, and mental- psychosocial support in addition to supporting referral to the secondary care services. The project will contribute to strengthening surveillance system and responding to disease outbreaks, including training and supporting Rapid Response Teams (RRTs), ensure weekly surveillance reports, supporting CTUs in the areas of high risk of cholera outbreaks. Drinking water in the targeted facilities will be chlorinated and monitored regularly. The project will contribute to building capacity of health care workers by conducting trainings to strengthening skills of staff to implement quality services. The project will be planned, implemented and monitored jointly with targeted communities and Ministry of Health. The project will be implemented in 12 months with estimated budget at $3,000,000.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) 2020 reported, 8.6 million people in need for humanitarian health assistance residing in high risk areas, and lacking the basic lifesaving and life sustaining health services across Sudan. Over 175,000 people in need are facing a catastrophic health situation (severity 5), approximately 2.5 million people in extreme in need for health assistance (severity 4), and 5 million people in areas of severe need of health lifesaving activities (severity 3). Out of the total figure, around 3.5 million children under 5 years of age are in need of Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI) programs for early diagnosis and treatment of life-threatening health conditions. Additionally, the vulnerability of these children is increased by low immunization coverage (EPI), which makes them at risk of future outbreaks of vaccine preventable diseases. The report indicated that pregnant women live in areas where provision of maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health services is suboptimal, in addition, the overall lack of adequate emergency obstetric care services, safe blood banking services, and referral services contribute to high maternal mortality. Out of the total population in need of humanitarian assistance, there are currently 1,360,000 pregnant and women of reproductive age in need of sexual and reproductive health services.The overall number of people targeted by the health sector are 5 M among them the vulnerable group are 2M pregnant women, 860,000 U5, 100,000 elderlies, 1,300,000 IDPs. 15 states in Sudan have been affected by the floods. According to HNO by September 2019, over 320, 000 people were directly affected of which 7,648 are pregnant women in need of safe delivery services. With thousands of houses, latrines, and public facilities destroyed or damaged, the most affected areas were, White Nile, Khartoum, Kassala, North Kordofan, and North Darfur. In these states, existing health systems capacities are stretched and needs are high in trauma services, referral systems, mobile health services, and community-based interventions; water services are disrupted and the consumption of unsafe water is predominant among the affected population. As a result, people are exposed to water-born diarrheal diseases with a projected 40 percent increase in Dysentery and typhoid cases compared to last year, malaria cases breaching the epidemic threshold in several states, and an overall number of people vulnerable or directly affected by floods double the number reported in 2018. The Federal Ministry of Health (FMOH) announced an outbreak of Cholera in Blue Nile state in 8th of September 2019, later cases were reported from Sinnar in 13th of December, Khartoum in 19th of October and Aljazeera in 7th of November and as per 23rd of November cholera cases were reported with 11 deaths (CFR3.3). Dengue fever outbreak was reported from Kassala, Red Sea, Gedaref, North, South, West and East Darfur, North Kordofan and Sennar states. According to WHO and as of 23rd of November 2872 dengue cases were reported with 9 deaths (CFR 0.3).Rift Valley Fever outbreak was reported as well from River Nile and Red Sea states in 28th of September. In 23rd of November WHO and FMoH reported 206 cases with 7 deaths (CFR 3.4).CHIKUNGUNYA cases were reported South, West and East Darfur, Kassala, Sennar and White Nile. By 23 Nov 2019 total of 177 cases were reported with 4 deaths (CFR2).

2 - Activities / Outputs

The project will respond to the basic needs of IDPs, returnees and host communities while increasing their self-reliance and improving their health, status and resilience. The planned activities to be implemented under this project will include the treatment of communicable and non- communicable diseases, ensuring the availability of essential medicines, provision of MIPS for RH to support reproductive, neonatal, and child health, strengthening the expanded program of immunization EPI, infection prevention, health education, and mental- psychosocial support in addition to supporting referral to the secondary care services. The project will contribute to strengthening surveillance system and responding to disease outbreaks, including training and supporting Rapid Response Teams (RRTs), ensure weekly surveillance reports, supporting CTUs in the areas of high risk of cholera outbreaks. Drinking water in the targeted facilities will be chlorinated and monitored regularly. The project will contribute to building capacity of health care workers by conducting trainings to strengthening skills of staff to implement quality services. The planned activities will be implemented in equitable manner to ensure access to services by all targeted population mainly the most vulnerable like women, children, the elderly, and people living with disability.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environment will be considered as an integral component for this project, and a number of factors will be essential for improving the humanitarian response such as coordination with different actors at the communities, prioritization and awareness raising amongst practitioners, and effective policy frame work. The plan mitigation will through effectiveness of existing disposal mechanism as preventive measure for disease caused by insufficient or unsafe waste disposal. Plan the location of emergency waste disposal sites with local authorities to avoid contamination of water sources, disease vectors and odors. Healthcare and other forms of hazardous waste will be disposed of using appropriate methods, safe and hygienic disposal of needles and other medical supplies, safe management of solid and human waste to prevent spread of communicable diseases.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Awareness raising for targeted beneficiaries will be conducted on availability of GBV services and importance of timely access to the service. GBV, survivors will have safe access to health facilities, and health providers will be equipped to offer survivor-centered, right-based, non-discriminatory, quality health services. Health staff will be trained on first line GBV management and CMR will be conducted in coordination with the protection and GBV AoR. Further, information sharing of violence, IDP movements, and GBV incidents will be shared among sectors to ensure timely response.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The preposition complaint and feedback mechanisms will be used to gauge the satisfaction level of beneficiaries against services provided by SC. Accountability phone is standing by to receive feedback and complaints from the project beneficiaries and stakeholders, for beneficiaries without phones they report to a community focal point. The call-in number will be printed on posters and displayed at service outlet centers to be easily access to callers when interested to deliver their feedback. The MEAL team alongside with program staff will conduct focus group discussions and community meetings to share project information with direct and indirect project beneficiaries and collect their views. All feedback and complaints received will be logged into the Complaints and Response system, analyzed and utilized for project management and planning. To ensure accountability to the affected community, SC will consult communities, government authorities and other relevant partners in the design of this project. The consultation with communities will involve both women and men as well as children. The accountability mechanism will provide opportunity to the affected population to raise their concerns and claim their rights. The project considers conflict sensitivity by understanding the situation, examining how each activity may influence the conflict and work to maximize the positive and minimize the negative impacts of the proposed interventions.
