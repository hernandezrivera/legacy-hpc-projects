Project Summary

Integrated WASH service delivery is essential in alleviating the WASH needs of the vulnerable and affected populations in South Sudan. The most affected population are the IDPS, returnees and vulnerable host communities who suffer lack of WASH services and that can be associated with flooding and adverse climatic conditions in some counties in South Sudan. The influx of returnees and refugees from Sudan and Ethiopia has also put pressure on already constrained WASH resources including limited water supply and sanitation facilities.
In Uror county of Jonglei state and Terekeka County of Central Equatoria State there are immense WASH needs and the population are in dire need of urgent WASH services to alleviate their suffering. According to the IPC classification (April to July 2023, Uror county is categorized in IPC 4 while Terekeka county in IPC 3 and therefore require urgent WASH intervention to ensure that the vulnerable and food insecure populations have access to safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene.
Access to WASH services will improve the livelihood of the vulnerable communities in these counties thus reducing the food insecurity associated with malnutrition and other communicable diseases. Access to safe water, adequate sanitation and good hygiene practice will prevent children and lactating mothers from sliding into SAM status thus there is need to break the WASH-related diseases transmission cycle and improve access to safe water and promote safe hygiene practices. Livelihoods are further affected by lack of adequate WASH services, as women who bear the rigor of collecting water for domestic use spend more time at functional boreholes, giving them less time for key core functions such as farming, breast feeding, income generating activities and self-development and further exposing them to protection issues within the community.
SLDA aims to implement an integrated WASH intervention targeting 11,111 beneficiaries in Uror county of Jonglei state and Terekeka County of Central Equatoria State to ensure that they are able to get enough water for drinking and domestic use as per the sphere standards through rehabilitation of boreholes and provision of WASH NFIs like jerry cans, soaps, water treatments items (PUR and Aqua tabs), etc. The project will also construct temporary latrines and rehabilitate existing sanitation facilities that are safe and accessible by all group of people including those with disabilities. Women and girls within the reproductive ages will be supported with dignified and safe menstrual hygiene management that will allow them stay comfortable and carry out their normal household duties during their menstrual period. Hygiene promotion will be carried out in these counties which will be geared towards total behavioral change in regards to hygiene

1. Needs

South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2023) estimated that the standard of living and well-being of 6.1 million people will be affected as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. WASH conditions were found to be most severe in most counties within Jonglei state including Uror county together with the high prevalence of malnutrition. Displacement, insecurity and economic downturn have compounded the impact of historically low coverage of water infrastructure in South Sudan. The economic downturn has exacerbated the disruption of water services, limiting the resources available for the maintenance of wells and boreholes. In the areas hosting displaced people and returnees from Sudan crisis, above-capacity demand results in water points being unable to supply enough drinking water for the people. Insecurity has also affected access to water. Protection concerns are also present, especially for women and girls as they collect water at the water points. With the influx of returnees from Sudan crisis, there is huge pressure on the limited WASH infrastructure. As of 20 July 2023, 183,854 individuals from 42,103 households have arrived in South Sudan, with the numbers expected to rise. At the transit points to the locations they settle, many of them rely on river water or untreated sources, exposing themselves to potential waterborne diseases and health risks. The situation is particularly dire for women and children crossing the border into South Sudan, as they appear visibly malnourished and urgently in need of life-saving nutrition assistance. (OCHA situation Report No.5.) According to the IPC classification (April to July 2023), Uror county is categorized in IPC 4 while Terekeka county in IPC 3 with high level of malnutrition among children under 5 years and pregnant and lactating mothers. Provision of adequate safe and clean water is key in reducing incidences of child related morbidity and mortality attributed to water related diseases that exacerbate malnutrition caused by gaps in food consumption amongst the host, returnees and IDP populations. IPC Report (April to July 2023) shows about 1.4 million children under five years were expected to suffer from acute malnutrition by June 2023. Of these, 345,893 children were likely to suffer from severe acute malnutrition. The childhood diseases recorded include Diarrhea, fever, and cough. These diseases are among the most common and fatal diseases among children and cause rapid deterioration in health. Poor sanitation conditions are likely contributing to the high prevalence of illness, with open defecation as high as 50% of households in 56 counties. Limited access to WASH infrastructure coverage alone, such as water points and latrines, poses multi-sectoral impacts. Poor access to WASH services and goods, combined with high levels of food insecurity, has a detrimental impact on the health of the most vulnerable people, as evidenced by the high prevalence of malnutrition in the country. At the national level, the GAM prevalence for children between 0 and 59 months is at 16 per cent, while SAM was at 5.9 per cent according to the preliminary FSNMS and 2022 SMART surveys. The presence of water-borne diseases such as diarrhea in children under age 5 is also indicative of poor WASH conditions. SLDA aims to implement an integrated WASH intervention in Uror county of Jonglei state and Terekeka County of Central Equatoria State to ensure that the vulnerable conflict and food insecure populations in these counties are able to get enough water for drinking and domestic use as per the sphere standards, and that they can access sanitation facilities while improving on their hygiene practices and behaviors by end of 2024.

2. Project activities

The activities to be done will include:1. Conduct project introductory sessions including Focussed Group Discussions with women/ girls on mapping of safe areas of implementation.2. Rehabilitation of flood resilient water point after a GBV risk analysis3. Construction of flood resilient temporary latrines and rehabilitation of existing household and communal latrine facilities and equip them with hand washing kits, doors and locks in addition to ensuring secure locations agreed after a GBV risk analysis 4. Mobilize, train and deploy Community Hygiene Promoters/ volunteers to spearhead hygiene promotion campaigns including referrals for GBV Case reporting, carry out mass hygiene promotion campaigns among targeted people. 5. Distribute assorted WASH NFIs - water collection and storage containers, Soap, jerry cans/buckets, filter clothes, pump spare kits etc,6. Conduct project monitoring missions including community feedback sessions in the target counties. 7. Conduct a rapid assessment on Knowledge, Attitudes and Practices on WASH to determine uptake and utilization of hygiene promotion interventions.8. Mobilize, train and support Water User Committees (WUCs) the target locations on basic borehole operations and management while ensuring more participation of women and girls.
