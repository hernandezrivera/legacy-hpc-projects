Project Summary

The project responds to the Shelter/NFIs Cluster’s Objective n. 1 “Displaced populations are provided support that mitigates immediate harm and ensures adequate and safe temporary shelter solutions”, This is linked to HRP 2022 SO.1; “The rights of Palestinians living under occupation, including those living under the blockade, and other restrictions, are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with International Humanitarian Law (IHL), and International Human Rights Law (IHRL), while duty-bearers are increasingly held to account”.
The overall project objective is to ensure that 150 most vulnerable HHs (855 individuals) from Gaza, North Gaza and middle area Governorates evicted or at immediate risk of eviction from rented shelters or facing imprisonment due to rental debts are protected.
The proposed response will be tailored on the HHs’ needs and will be including the provision of:
1) Cash-for-rent and legal support to 150 vulnerable HHs in rented accommodation who according to Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) , Gaza Protection consortium (GPC) and Protection Cluster database are repeatedly exposed to the risk of eviction or already evicted due to their inability to pay the rental costs. This includes provision of cash-for- debt re-payment for cases identified, or at high risk of imminent imprisonment due to being forced to sign promissory note. The cash for rent will cover 4-months’ rent fees including electricity and water bills and will be provided through phased cash payments.
2) Provision of case-by-case restricted cash assistance for essential household items. The support will be tailored according to needs and may include items such as bedding items, gas stoves, heaters with gas cylinders and fuel, emergency lighting system with batteries. Following the Do No Harm principles in case of heater provision all necessary safety measures will be applied and required items such as fire extinguisher, fire blanket awareness raising, and training will be provided.
3) Provision of hygiene kits and hygiene awareness material for the targeted families throughout the project cycle.
This project is designed in consultations and coordination with NRC local partner, the Shelter Cluster, GPC, Protection Cluster and the MoSD, the Ministry of Public Work and Housing (MoPWH) and coordinated with active shelter partners to avoid duplication and ensure fair distribution of the limited humanitarian funds among affected communities across The Gaza Strip.

1 - Needs

150 HHs (50F, 100M) with total 855 individuals are prioritized for temperory shelter support due to being at immediate risk of eviction a, including 45HH with female –headed, elderly or disabled breadwinner. During May- August 2022, NRC conducted a needs assessment and a the caseload was identified. The assessment covered 284 vulnerable households at immediate risk of eviction. This caseload was originally received from MoSD or referred to NRC by the Gaza Protection Consortium, shelter cluster or other INGOs. The analysis of the NRC eviction database indicates that 44% of total individuals are children, 16% of assessed households were under protection case management. 99% of all assessed tenants are forced to resort to at least one negative coping mechanism like accumulating debts, seeking family social assistance and selling household assets. 60% are unemployed and 83% of tenants have outstanding rental debts with an average of ILS 40,000 (USD 1,200), out of them 75% were previously evicted from their rented housing units, 25% of assessed households are living in overcrowded conditions lacking the minimum level of privacy for changing clothes, sleeping. 50% of assessed households reported not feeling comfortable inside their rental housing units. Most tenant-rental housing units (52%) have no furniture or own furniture in poor conditions. (63%) lack the basic household equipment like refrigerators and washing machines, or the equipment mentioned is in poor condition. Moreover, the families (38%) are lacking basic kitchen items; (40%) bedding sets; 29% signed payable bonds for their accumulated rental debt despite knowing that they will not be able to pay, which led to 18% receiving imprisonment orders. Furthermore, the assessed HHs reported that 31% of their children’s academic achievement have negatively been affected. 22% of women and girls feel unsafe and unprotected due to the frequent movement from place to another. From NRC staff’s observational remarks during the needs’ assessment, 70% of assessed households suffer from poor personal hygiene, 30% have no soap in their housing unit, 75% have poor hygiene conditions in bathrooms and kitchens. 30% of assessed households’ floors did not appear clean. 30% reported they are only using water to clean their homes (without any detergent), while 54% are using water and soap to clean their home and 16% are using broom to clean their home. In 36% of the assessed cases, at least one household member suffered from diarrhea in the two weeks prior to the assessment. 50% of households reported that at least one household member got sick with coronavirus. 77% of assessed households did not regularly buy soap, with 96% considering it a financial burden.

2 - Activities

To quickly restore temporary access to adequate shelter for the most vulnerable HHs already evicted or at risk of eviction or displacement, NRC will provide 150 families with temporary shelter support for a period of up to 4 months; the provided support will include: a) provision of legal support for direct beneficiaries through case-by-case legal awareness and counselling sessions. b) Provide restricted cash-for-rent support for the families through phased cash transfer (two instalments) covering the cost of rent fees, electricity, and water bills. c) Provision of cash-for-essential HH items for identified HHs based on tailored need assessment and considering the HH priorities including the specific needs of males and females of all ages in the HH, d) provision of legal support and counselling in case of any disputes, e) provision of monitoring field visit to the targeted HHs to ensure the commitment of the agreements and approve release cash payments. NRC in coordination with the local partner will be providing case-by-case restricted cash assistance for essential household items. The support will be tailored according to needs and may include items such as bedding items, gas stoves, heaters with gas cylinders and fuel, emergency lighting system with batteries. Following the Do No Harm principles in case of heater provision all necessary safety measures will be applied and required items such as fire extinguisher, fire blanket awareness raising, and training will be provided. Furthermore, hygiene kits and hygiene awareness material for all the targeted families will be provided throughout the project cycle. All NRC staff and the local partners staff will receive an information on the code of conduct, PSEA and CFM. NRC will also include binding conditions in all procurement documents and procedures with suppliers to ensure their commitment. In addition, NRC protection team will develop and disseminate PSEA awareness raising material. NRC has zero tolerance of PSEA and all new project personnel and implementing partners are mandatory to attend a series of PSEA sessions as well as refreshing sessions to existing trained personnel ahead of community engagement as well as during the implementation of the project It is highlighted that NRC will provide case-management services to the cases identified with protection risks/concerns through the NRC protection team or will be referred to suitable partners in case of more specialized needs such as trauma healing or GBV.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Cash is the only modality suitable for rent support due to varying rent fees and risk of eviction placed upon tenants who cannot meet their deadline to pay off their rent. In this action, the cash will be transferred in two instalments to the landlords, the tenant will be secured through two types of legal contracts, active monitoring, and legal support.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Through the Complaints and Feedback mechanism, NRC is committed to strict standards of transparency and accountability across all core competencies where everyone has the right to file a complaint and receive a response. NRC’s CFM focal points in West bank and Gaza respond to all requests/complaints directly or within 14 days as per SOP. The complaints and feedback received by NRC is confidential and closely monitored to be used to improve our working methods making our programs more responsive, effective, and appropriate to the needs of the affected populations. Across core competencies, the CFM team, based on the feedback or complaint received, have the responsibility to inform program teams to respond and act to bring positive change, and continuously inform the community on the appropriate actions taken to respond to the feedback or complaint. NRC CFM contributes to organizational learning, and continuous improvement of accountability and quality of NRC’s work to be gender-sensitive, safe, and confidential, avoiding any risk, harm, or retaliation to our affected population. CFM and M&E units compile, and analyze data collected through various learning exercises and then share the learning and findings with program management. This includes findings and instances of learning from all M&E exercises, field staff observations, outcome surveys, recommendations from evaluations, and recommendations from complaints or feedback received. Program management are accountable to ensure that the learning takes place, and recommendations and findings are incorporated into projects as much as possible.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

To minimize identified protection risks due to evictions especially for women, boys and girls, the following activities are tailored to meet the identified gender and protection related needs; Shelter field staff are trained to identify and refer cases with protection risks such as GBV, child marriage, child abuse, violence against children. Boys and girls displaying behavioral problems due to having frequently moved residencies or out of fear of being evicted at any time will be referred to NRC educational team to receive adequate psychosocial support outside school. NRC will ensure that rental contracts are signed with both husband and wife and that males and females are provided with house land and property (HLP) information relevant to their rights and responsibilities as renters but also to understand rights and responsibilities of the landlord. It may also include negotiating an agreement with landlords to redeem bonds securing previous debt resulting from unpaid rent. This is ensured through NRC’s Information, Counselling, and Legal Assistance Program.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

NRC identified the most vulnerable households based on a socioeconomic household vulnerability assessment. The factors reflecting socioeconomic vulnerability includes socio-demographic and socio-economic data, such as characteristics of the head of household especially female/widow/elderly/PWD, income and dependency ratio, poverty, debt and the use of other negative coping mechanisms, the status of household assets. The scoring system is designed to prioritize female/elderly/disabled headed households, heads of households with high dependency rates, large family size and overcrowded housing. During the design phase of the project, male and female households of vulnerable and affected communities especially youth and elderly as well as PWDs were consulted to reflect Shelter & NFI needs including gender, age, protection, and special needs. At the start of the project, essential project information including selection criteria and access to NRC’s feedback and complaint mechanism is communicated to the target communities. NRC engages male and female beneficiaries of all ages in designing the most suitable response to each renter households’ specific gender/age/protection concern. Integrate gender and age analysis in project monitoring and reporting. NFI support considers gender/age specific needs and concerns. NRC will provide substantial technical support to beneficiaries including 1) advice on the prioritization of shelter needs raised by male and female household members 2) advice on how to best respond to the identified personal concerns of the household members including protection concerns by suggesting adequate durable shelter solutions. The supported populations will be empowered to request information, raise concerns, and provide feedback related to project activities, which will strengthen the capacity of affected populations to claim their rights.
