Project Summary

Considering the scale and continued rise of needs, in alignment with HRP 2022-2023 objectives, the Ministry of Social Affairs and Labor (MoSAL) national strategy as well as the ERL sector priorities, the Action aims at supporting community recovery and livelihoods and to preserve social cohesion ensuring equitable access to services and mitigating protection risks linked to low income families. For 2023 planning, INTERSOS aims at fully integrating livelihood into protection (see HSYR23-PRO-197346-1), health (HSYR23-HEA-197010-1) and education (HSYR23-EDU-196630-1) responses, preventing and mitigating negative coping mechanisms such as school drop out, child labor, early marriage and GBV as a consequence of protracted crisis in the country and of dire economic conditions faced by Syrian families. The Action will be implemented in the Governorates of Hama, Rural Damascus and Idleb targeting newly accessible- hard to reach-rural areas. Clear criteria for BNFs selection are developed based on the vulnerability level of the individuals prioritizing Female Headed Households, families with Persons and Children with Disabilities and IDPs but also HHs that are unable to cover the cost of minimum expenditure resorting to negative coping mechanisms. BNFs are chosen through Education, Protection and Health interventions in the same target areas, promoting integration and population resilience. Based on in-country experience and lessons learnt the intervention builds upon Labor Market Assessments (LMA) carried out in the targeted locations to fully link Vocational Training provided to the characteristics of the labor market to support productive sectors and provide employable skills, taking into consideration particular vulnerabilities of the target populations (e.g. persons with disabilities - PwDs). INTERSOS aims at conducting a specific context analysis of the communities served through the engagement of different layers of society. This will be carried out through KIIs and FGDs involving local entrepreneurs, local authorities’ representatives and vulnerable individuals from the same targeted community (identified through INTERSOS protection-education intervention). The analysis will investigate key barriers in accessing the labor market and livelihood in specific targeted areas, the main services available and referral pathways in place, the soft skills and practical capacities needed by the beneficiaries. Such analysis will be linked to the evaluation of the demand of the labor market defining possible pathways for dignified and capacitated access to it. To ensure gender balance and empowerment of girls and women entering into the labor market consultations with communities and focus groups discussion will be carried out enhancing women's active participation in the choice of the training topics. Hence, the action encompasses the following key components: 1. LMA directed at the analysis of demand and supply sides of the local labor market 2. provision of technical and vocational training to build and strengthen beneficiaries’ skills 3. delivery of business and marketing skills workshops to transfer critical skills in communication and marketing to better connect with customers, promote local products and boost business activities, 4. provision of start-up kits to trainees to foster self-employment and broaden income generating activities 5. support to micro and small enterprises in the agri-business sector by provision of assets which are necessary to increase production. INTERSOS will conduct rehabilitation works in Protection Community Centers/Rural Development Centers (MoSAL) aimed at enhancing accessibility for PwDs. INTERSOS staff and partners (MoSAL) are provided with briefing sessions on core concepts and policies related to humanitarian principles, Child Protection and PSEA. Awareness-raising sessions are delivered on hazard and related preventive and mitigation measures for the most common risks in the area.

3 - Needs

A general deterioration in the access to livelihoods, ability to meet basic needs, availability and affordability of infrastructures and services are reported countrywide. INTERSOS continued identifying issues with segments of the population being excluded from income generating opportunities in rural areas in RD, Hama and Idleb (INTERSOS RMNA, May/June 2022). Women and older women face difficulties in accessing basic services and income generating activities, especially single female headed-HHs. Women and girls in the communities are often engaged in activities that help to support their families, hence the need for lifeskills and vocational training that can generate income. Protection concerns are also raised by increasing numbers of out-of-school children who need to work to support their families. In order to generate additional income or reduce household expenses, families are increasingly adopting negative coping mechanisms which increase protection risks, particularly for children and adolescents. Child labour, early marriage and child separation are reported across locations, school-drop-out rates and child labour are commonly cited as recurrent practice especially during the harvesting season, also leading to increase landmines and ERW related injuries. Early marriage - partly driven by families trying to ease their financial burden- is a prevalent form of gender based violence overwhelmingly affecting young and adolescents girls.

4 - Implementation of activities

Promoting an integrated intervention with Protection, Health and Education sectors, INTERSOS will implement the proposed action, in coordination with MoSAL (sub-MoU signed in August 2022). INTERSOS will mainstream protection and PSEA, conducting preliminary briefing and awareness-raising sessions on protection principles, CP and PSEA targeting MoSAL staff/volunteers and suppliers. MoV will include attendance sheets, training reports, pre&post tests, and BNF satisfaction surveys. Proposed activities are: 1. LMA: appraisal of supply-demand of the local economic ecosystem to identify stakeholders, constraints and main drivers. 2. Delivery of tailored vocational and technical training for unskilled and semi-skilled FHH, youth who dropped out from school in underserved areas, PwDs, low income families at risk of adopting negative coping mechanisms selected against a score system of vulnerabilities from caregivers and youth engaged in Education and Protection activities. 3. Provision of business and marketing skills workshops and life skills training comprehensively promoting beneficiaries’ engagement in the labor market. 4. Distribution of start-up kits to foster self-employment: VT Trainees receive start-up kits to initiate self-employment initiatives in relevant sub-sectors to the needs of the labor market. 5. Support to rural enterprises and entrepreneurs: provision of productive assets to rural micro-enterprises of the agri-business sector (specific focus on women and youth).

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1: Risk of discrimination, perceptions of discrimination/exclusion of vulnerable groups (women and girls) from male dominated productive/employement sectors. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation: Comprehensive consultations undertaken with communities, needs-tailored and adapted intervention; Productive/employment sectors selected by ensuring participation of all target groups; Adopt a lean and effective Complaint Response and Feedback Mechanisms. 2:Exclusion (physical access) of PwDs - poor infrastructure/lack of facilitation. L: High; I: High. M: training halls with available access for PwDs; Consultations and engagement of PwDs throughout the implementation; awareness-raising/briefings with MoSAL and trainers on measures to be taken to properly address PwDs needs. 3: Increase of tensions between returnees and HC due to unequal assistance provided. L: Low-medium; I: High. Mitigation: Secure a thorough mapping of affected communities and conflict sensitivity analysis performed at the onset of the project; Consultations and transparent communication to foster awareness, cooperation and social cohesion; Opportunities for assistance distributed evenly and according to severity of needs. 4: Exposure or exploitation of Child Labor. L: Medium; I: High. Mitigation: clear BNF selection criteria and regular monitoring; Inception training with MoSAL on CP; protection mainstreaming.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks: INTERSOS staff, MoSAL staff, suppliers (etc.) have prior conviction for SEA related offenses; INTERSOS/MoSAL staff, suppliers, any other actors involved in the Action do not comply/accept PSEA policy when participating in project activities; MoSAL does not have a strong PSEA system in place; SEA perpetrated by staff/service providers; PSEA allegations are not timely/effectively addressed; survivors have limited access to safe assistance/remedies; lack of PSEA reporting mechanisms; communities not aware of PSEA risks/reporting channels. M: INTERSOS has a Global PSEA/CP Policy that applies to all personnel in addition to a country level strategy to ensure SEA risks/mitigation strategies/procedures are tailored to the context. INTERSOS staff must sign a PSEA/CP Policy Declaration, attend a PSEA/CP Policy induction and one refresher training per year. 1 PSEA Referent and 4 PSEA Focal Points are appointed to receive complaints/reports about alleged acts of SEA and follow up in compliance with relevant guiding principles, ensuring safety of survivors. Complaints are referred to the in-country PSEA Referent and are subject to investigation. An assessment about MoSAL commitment and capacity to prevent/respond to SEA is conducted. Training is offered to partners to strengthen the identification of SEA risks/adoption of related SEA mitigation measures; when possible awareness raising on PSEA risks/reporting channels is conducted at community level.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

INTERSOS has developed a Contingency Planning Process (CPP) with risk analysis and related mitigation measures: Reputational Risk: unable to deliver on-time, breach of sanctions and/or do not harm principle. Procurement protocols in place, thorough market research/due diligence conducted for verification of quality standards/legal requirements, reporting mechanisms and robust monitoring strategy followed; Security and Access: unable to reach and implement in targeted areas. Internal security assessment and protocols, combined with coordination with OCHA, SARC, other INGOs and community networks in the field; Financial Risk and Accountability: exchange rates fluctuations affect achievement of targets; lack of transparency; diversion or misuse of project financial resources. Internal system and SOPs to secure compliance and accountability respected. Systematic and rigorous monitoring of project activities and spending carried out; financial controlling rules and processes in place; regular communication with the donor in case of economic shock and re-alignment of targets. Health risks-COVID-19 pandemic: staff and BNF exposed to health hazards and risks of contagion. INTERSOS takes into account precautionary measures related to COVID 19.
