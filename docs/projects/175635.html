Project Summary

South Sudan has been engulfed by conflict from 2013 to 2016, where armed conflict has caused un-bearing suffering of her people. Millions have been displaced internally and externally. In 2020, the country received large amounts of rainfall that caused massive flooding in many parts of the country leading to the destruction of farmland and crops, increased risk of water-borne diseases, flooding and destruction of schools and other public utilities. Sub national conflicts continue to displace more people causing death, looting and destruction of properties. The COVID 19 pandemic that hit the world in early March 2020 led to school closures, the collapse of the economy making life unbearable to IDPs and the host communities. The combined effects of flooding, inter-ethnic conflicts and the COVID 19 pandemic has hit the education sector hardest, destroying school infrastructure, school closures and the collapse of livelihoods increasing the vulnerability of parents, teachers and children.
This project is intended to support the resumption of learning, provide safe protective learning spaces, improve learning condition by supporting the learners and teachers with education materials, build the capacity of teachers and the school community to support safe learning and improve coping mechanism and resilience. The project targets 3000 learners in 5 most affected counties of Ulang, Nasir and Maiwut of Upper Nile state and Gogrial East and Tonj North counties of Warrap State. The establishment of classrooms will provide a safe and protective environment for learners. This will launch resumption of learning in the project targeted areas and protect children from harmful association like drug abuse, early marriages and recruitment to armed groups. The establishment of WASH facilities including hand washing will provide a safe environment from water-borne diseases and improve COVID 19 compliance. Integrated capacity building of teachers in pedagogy, PSS, protection/GBV would help them to give lifesaving skills support to learners so that they can be able to protect themselves and improve resilience by the youths. The SMC/PTAs would be trained in the management of schools in emergencies to support education. Education supplies provision will support a faster resumption of learning in schools.
This intervention intends to cover 3 primary schools and 3 ALP centers in Ulang, Nasir, and Maiwut in Upper Nile state and 2 primary schools and 2 ALP centers in Gogrial East and Tonj North of Warrap state to provide a quality emergency learning environment to the affected IDPs, Returnees and host community children.
TADO intends to target 1750 Boys, 1250 Girls, 90 Men, and 50 Women as direct beneficiaries among the affected communities of IDPs and host communities’ children, youth, and adults including people with disabilities who have limited or no access to education services.
The project will establish temporary learning spaces(TLS) and classrooms, establish gender-segregated latrines with water points and hygiene promotion, support school feeding program, schools supplies, and hygiene kits, recreational materials, train teachers on EiE pedagogical skills, life skills to improve teaching-learning, train teachers and learners on PSS and the impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanisms, Train teachers and SMC/PTA on protection/GBV, COVID-19, health, nutrition, and governance.
TADO has a static presence, experience and good relations local education officials and other education stake holders .

1 - Needs

According the South Sudan humanitarian overview (HNO) Report 2021, an estimated 3.4 million (51 per cent girls) school-aged children have lack of or inadequate access to education services in 2021. This is a slight increase from the 3.1 million girls and boys in need in 2020. 18,000 children are out of school. The negative impact of COVID-19 and subsequent school closures have resulted in an increased number of children in need of education support according to the humanitarian needs overview (HNO) report released this year Children and youth, aged between 3 and 17, represent 44 per cent of South Sudan’s population. Education in the country has been severely impacted by successive years of conflict, economic crisis instability, displacement and widespread flooding. The capacity of the Ministry of General Education and Instruction (MoGEI) to respond to the needs of children remains limited. Displaced people, refugees and returnees are identified as vulnerable groups and the education needs of children in these groups are particularly dire in Upper Nile and Warrap where a high percentage of children do not have access to an education. The closure of schools and all learning institutions followed by COVID-19 had devastating effects on the education of all children including IDPs, returnees and hos community in the counties of Ulang, Nasir and Maiwut and Gogrial East and Tonj North which has jeopardized critical gains made over the years. It also exposed them to different forms of gender-based violence including forced marriage and teenage pregnancies. Education needs remain high in the two states to spontaneous returnees and IDPs. Since July 2020 flooding has significantly impacted on education infrastructure, particularly in Upper Nile and Warrap of which Ulang, Nasir Maiwut, Gogrial East and Tonj North are the priority location targeted by this intervention. Many flood-affected people took shelter in schools. This may affect the first phase reopening of schools as announced by the MoGEI.According to the south Sudan IPC report published in November 2020 on the food insecure counties, 5% of the population in Tonj North, Greater Ulang and Nasir appeared in IPC phase 4 and 5 (catastrophe) and the situation is projected to worsen in the first and second projection while 10% of the population in Tonj North was in IPC phase 5 and is projected to remain like that or worsen in the first and second projection. According to the same report, the drivers of this crisis is food insecurity due to destruction of farm land and harvests, death of livestock and lack of access to markets leading to increased food prices, communal conflicts that has led to displacements, majorly Tonj North with poor access to food and COVID-19 pandemic that has increased travel restrictions, transportation of food commodities to the markets hence increased food prices and scaled down education and food security interventions and poor WASH facilities hence unsafe water consumption by the households and inadequate water storage containers that lead to inadequate water supply and this exposes children to frequent diarrhea diseases . According to Ulang county UN-OCHA lead IRNA report October 2020 which TADO participated, Great Ulang County is faced with severe food insecurity due to flooding resulting in to the displacement of 25,626 Individuals and 23,190 People have been displaced in the three counties of Tonj north namely Awul ,Kirik and Rualber Payam according to UNOCHA IRNA report August 2020 . Which has affected mostly children and Women with dire needs to basic education and health services. TADO will utilize its operational and static presence in Nasir and Gogrial east with field office in Ulang where the organization is implementing and coordination UNICEF funded education in emergency (EiE) project to support this project in Maiwut and Tonj north .

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

TADO commits to ensuring protection mainstreaming in all its humanitarian responses. Throughout this project, TADO will ensure affected populations are protected against harmful practices. IASC principles, and guidance by the GBV sub Cluster have been adhered to in designing the project and will be practiced during implementation period. Protection risks will be mitigated by introducing efficient GBV awareness sessions and control measures. Targeted beneficiaries will be informed about the program- process, safe learning spaces will be established or rehabilitated with gender segregated WASH facilities for boys, girls and children with disabilities. This will emphasize the Do No Harm principle in a humanitarian setting. Equal opportunity shall be given to all beneficiaries, to emphasize impartial access to assistance, with considerations set up for Children with Special needs in collaboration with protection actors. adolescent girls and young girls have been identified as the most marginalized and neglected group of the affected population requiring urgent interventions. TADO will further strengthen communication channels with IDP communities, Host Communities and Returnees. Through PSS and Life Skills trainings, self-protection capacities will be built among children. TADO plans to provide a multi – sector response utilizing schools as platforms for humanitarian interventions. TADO has provided new measures for prevention and handling staff misconduct in its HR policy with all staff signing a new code of conduct to reduce abuse, exploitation, harassment of beneficiaries etc. Additionally, TADO has carried out staff inductions to acquaint staff with AAP standards.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During the assessment phase of the project, beneficiary discussions will be held with community leaders, elders, school children, youth, Men, women and the disabled persons. Affected populations will be fully involved and participate in project planning, design and engagement. Local and preferred languages will be used to affected populations on organizational procedure and manuals that have been incorporated in AAP standards. TADO will conduct focus group discussion, interviews community meetings, use notice boards and install suggestion boxes at all TADO offices/ bases and supported schools to capture feedback and complaints from communities we work in. Weekly sampling is done and information shared up to the information management officer who discusses it with Juba Admin/programs team to advance decision making/ action. Transparency will be upheld. TADO will involve key community authorities including the education directors in the project design with key decisions and expectations laid out. As a procedure to enhance Do No Harm principles, TADO has provided new measures for prevention and handling staff misconduct in its HR policy with all staff signing a new code of conduct to reduce abuse, exploitation, harassment of beneficiaries etc. Additionally, TADO has carried out staff inductions to acquaint staff with AAP standards. SMCs, PTAs and Community leaders will also be involved in community sensitization efforts focusing on access to education services, school WASH and protection messages, and prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) with the aim to increase access to education for boys and girls as well as preventing and reducing protection risks for children on their way to and from school. Community members will be involved in the design of TLS, identification of a safe location for construction of TLS and monitoring teachers and pupils’ attendance through the PTA. Setting-up a simple, safe and accessible Community Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) for SEA and protection of school children will be enhanced and strengthened. A community based approach for protection of schools and children will also add efficiency to strengthen reporting mechanism by incorporating local solutions into the overall SEA reporting system. A successful complaints mechanism will ensure the full participation of the community, through integrating formal and/or informal community structures.
