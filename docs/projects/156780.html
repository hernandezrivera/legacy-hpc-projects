Project Summary

This action aims at improving the food security of conflict-affected households in Da’ra governorate, Southern Syria. This will be achieved by providing in-kind or vouchers kitchen garden kits (one for each agricultural season) to 1,200 households who have access to a small piece of land or backyard garden for cultivating. Targeted households will also take part in theoretical and practical training on climate-smart agriculture, planting, harvest, composting. Ultimately, it is expected that the households who will benefit from this project will be able to improve their food security and nutritional status through a self-sufficient and diversified cultivation. 6,000 individuals are expected to benefit from the project.

Needs

In 2019, the food security severity ranking remains high in South Syria. In 2018, the food security situation in Dar’a governorate was classified as severe in 11 out of the 17 sub-districts, revealing widespread consumption gaps but also a significant depletion of livelihoods assets by the population, in order to meet their food consumption needs. (Food security situation in Syria, Expanded version of the Food security sector Humanitarian Needs overview 2018). In 2019, the whole governorate of Dar’a is characterized as having a major food security severity level. Out of the 984,057 households living in the three districts in Dar’a governorate (Da’ra, As-Sanamayin, Izra) in August 2018, 355,693 were estimated to be food insecure, while 189,943 were considered at risk. 40% of the population is represented by internally displaced persons (IDPs). (HNO PIN Severity 2019). Prolonged displacement, lack of financial and physical access to food, loss and lack of livelihood and reduced production capacity remain the key factors of people’s food insecurity. A nation-wide assessment conducted in 2018 has found that an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategies (Food security sector narrative – HNO version). In Dar’a governorate, the situation was aggravated by the instability faced in summer 2018, when the Government of Syria regained control on the area previously under opposition control. As a result of the military operations, 270,000 people were displaced between June and July 2018, and this represented the largest displacement in the area since the beginning of the Syrian crisis in 2011 (United Nations). Consequently, food security needs are particularly high for host communities, returnees and IDPs. They are in urgent need of assistance in order to be able to improve their nutritional status through a self-sufficient food production. This will contribute to reduce their negative coping mechanisms, including skipping meals and relying on debt.

Implementation of activities

This project will benefit 1,000 vulnerable households in Dar’a governorate will be able to meet their basic food security needs through kitchen gardens. Vulnerable host community members but also IDPs households will have the opportunity to enrol in this project. Households will be selected based on eligibility criteria (low HH income, with priority given to beneficiaries having one or less household members generating an income), but also taking into account their ability and willingness to benefit from the project. Standard vulnerability criteria will also apply, with priority given to female and elderly-headed households, households with persons living with a disability, or with over 6 households’ members. Each beneficiary household will receive two rounds of gardening inputs per year – both a winter and a summer season kit. The modality (in kind or voucher) will be determined based on beneficiaries’ preferences and on the results of the market assessments. Prior to each distribution, beneficiaries will take part in practical trainings where they will learn about agricultural techniques specific to kitchen gardening and to nutritional practices and dietary diversity.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: interference with the beneficiaries targeting Likelihood: lowImpact: highMitigation measure: In order to reduce this risk, a strong process of beneficiaries’ selection is in place. This includes a combination of self-application and reliance on list provided by the local authorities. A1 100% verification by Monitoring and Evaluation Department will ensure only eligible households will receive assistance. A Complaints and Response mechanism will also be available for community members to raise a complaint or ask for clarification. Risk: aid diversionLikelihood: mediumImpact: high Mitigation measure: In order to mitigate this risk, a strong community mobilisation will be in place. The project will be carefully explained to community leaders, local authorities and residents. In addition, distribution will be monitored by the independent Monitoring and Evaluation Unit, which will also conduct a Post Distribution Monitoring. Through the Complaints and Response mechanism, beneficiaries will be able to give their feedback and report any alleged attempt of interference. Risk: sexual exploitation and violence during distribution Likelihood: mediumImpact: high Mitigation measure: Distributions to be conducted through locally set-up distribution points. One dedicated female distribution kiosk will be staffed by at least one female staff member to ensure women feel comfortable when receiving the vouchers or kits, and are able to ask questions on the project. Sites will be identified in a full participatory manner with men and women from the communities. For households with too severe movement restraints or protection concerns related to coming to distribution points, distributions will be conducted individually at a household level.
