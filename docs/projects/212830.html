Project Summary

Vulnerable families in Northeast Syria are grappling with persistent challenges in securing safe and adequate shelters, a consequence of the decade-long conflict that has severely impacted housing availability and displaced millions. According to REACH November HSOS, over one third of the population resides in substandard, damaged, or inadequate shelters, with 31% of assessed communities reporting occupied shelters with major damage. Finding suitable housing is critical, especially during the winter months, and households renting properties face challenges due to unaffordability. Security incidents in 2023, such as tensions between tribal leaders and authorities or Turkish airstrikes, increase the risk of conflict escalation and further displacement. In Raqqa, 84% of households experienced shelter inadequacies from October 2023 to January 2024, with issues like lack of lighting and a decline in daily electricity hours. Eviction risk, coupled with the prevailing security and economic challenges, has led to increased mobility, particularly in the Al-Hassakeh governorate, where IDPs and host communities face notable disparities in shelter conditions and livelihoods, impacting their living situations.

The organization will provide winterization support to vulnerable households in Ar-Raqqa and Al Hassakeh governorates, aiming to reach 5,640 individuals from November 2024 to the end of February 2025. Focusing on informal settlements as a priority, the organization will utilize experience gained from the LEARN consortium and employ a needs-based approach. In Ar-Raqqa, the organization plans blanket distributions in informal settlements based on SNFI WG rankings and will conduct needs assessments considering winterization criteria. In Al Hassakeh, 410 IDP households in neglected villages near collective centers will be targeted with vulnerability-based assistance. the organization aims for flexibility in responding to unforeseen emergencies, conducting market and risk assessments to determine the appropriate modality (vouchers, in-kind, cash, or mixed). Tailored approaches for persons with special needs will be implemented, and distributions will be accompanied by sensitization campaigns. The winterization kits' contents will be adapted based on SNFI WG guidelines and ongoing dialogue with other actors, with an average planned distribution value of 190 USD per household.
In the context of safe programming, field teams, comprising both male and female workers, prioritize gender balance and meaningful access for all groups. Gender-segregated consultations continue through various methods, and data analysis for household selection is done by project managers with IM department support. To mitigate safety and diversion risks in cash distribution, the organization communicates distribution details discreetly and implements protective measures during the process, including separating recipients by gender and prioritizing vulnerable individuals. A secure feedback mechanism and monitoring of cash usage are established. Recognizing evolving gender dynamics exacerbated by the crisis, the organization implements gender-sensitive approaches in cash programming, challenging traditional norms by considering voucher alternatives to empower women. Additionally, addressing the intersection of gender-based violence and shelter concerns, the organization emphasizes strategic shelter design, community engagement, and providing gender-specific spaces. For age and disability considerations, the organization acknowledges the marginalized status of elderly and disabled individuals, emphasizing the importance of transportation services and accessible premises but recognizing persistent challenges and discrimination, particularly for displaced populations.
