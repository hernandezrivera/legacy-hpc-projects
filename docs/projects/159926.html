Project Summary

The project aims to improve access to basic WASH services and address protection
concerns of Internally displaced Persons affected by armed conflict and violence and
destitutions triggered by climatic shocks. Vulnerable groups that including; people with
disability, people with medical conditions, children, the elderly and lactating mothers will
be supported to access services. Protection will be mainstreamed in the delivery of WASH
services to ensure that the populations in need targeted for this intervention are safe and
their dignity upheld in observance of their human rights and rights to basic services.
NCA and partners will implement temporary emergency. Sustainable WASH solutions
through water trucking, bore hole system with solar power and water poins, desalinization
of water, rehabilitation and expansion of water supplies systems, improve subsurface
dams/berkards. Gender-balanced WASH committees will be established and capacitated
to maintain WASH facilities functionality. Water jerricans and household treatment
options given ensure safe collection/storage as well consumption of water. Adequate
sanitation facilities, gender segregated and lockable from inside will be rehabilitated/
constructed where scarce to reduce morbidity and mortality. Hygiene promotion will be
conducted emphasising hand washing at critical times. People with incontinence will also
benefit from services to improve their dignity.
Projects will be implemented in line with the SPHERE and WASH/MoEWR guidelines/ To
reduce risk of violence upon facility users, special attention will be given to reducing
distance from households to WASH facilities as per the SPHERE standards and ensuring
that outputs are gender and disability sensitive. PHAST and CHAST approaches will seek
to promote an understanding of the connection between hygiene and health, as well as
the means to mitigate the impact of poor hygiene on health. MHM sessions for women
and girls will accompany distribution of hygiene kits.

GBV survivors will receive comprehensive GBV services including; Medical, Psycho-social,
referral, Legal support and livelihood assistance through provision of dignity kits and by
offering life skills/ vocational skills training for economic empowerment and
independence. Capacities of service providers will be strengthened on case management,
Clinical Management of Rape, MHPSS and on survivor-centred approaches to enable
SGBV survivors access quality, professional services appropriate to their needs.
Community members including community leaders, men and boys will be engaged to
challenge and advocate against harmful norms that perpetuate GBV and hold
perpetrators accountable for their actions. The community leaders, women and youth
groups will also be engaged to sensitize the community members on available services
for GBV survivors and complaint mechanism. Confidentiality, respect, safety and nondiscrimination
will be highly observed in this intervention.
Delivery of emergency services will prioritize inclusion, participation of and accountability
to affected populations as well as observe the Core Humanitarian Standards( CHS) an Do
No Harm principles. Integrated programming will provide holistic aid that can respond
rapidly to emerging needs, and this response will increase affected communities’
resilience to future shocks. Responses will prioritize needs of men, women, boys and girls,
and vulnerable people, include environmental safeguards to prevent degradation from
humanitarian operations (i.e. provision of energy efficient stoves), and afford affected
people dignity and choice through Cash and Vouchers Assistance (CVA), i.e. cash-for-work
(CFW) for WASH installations and restricted CVA for hygiene/dignity items
The projects will be implemented in Garbaharey , Belet Hawa, and Dollow districts in Gedo
region and Dangorayo, Garowe , and Eyl districts in Puntland state, Banadir administration
and Marka district in Lower Shabelle regio

Project Activities

NCA’s rapid assessment in Gedo, Banadir and Puntland (Sept 2019) revealed many IDPS, pastoralists and host communities lack nearby water sources; over half buy water/trek to other villages, often over 30 minutes. Crocodile attacks are a risk while collecting from rivers in Gedo. Majority of HHs lack storage containers. Most report limited sanitation facilities majorly communal and not gender-segregated, lack locks and handwashing facilities and are shared by as many as 20 HHs in Banadir. Many latrines, particularly in Gedo, do not function. Less than 10% of respondents wash hands with water and soap. Respondents report need for jerrycans, underwear, sanitary pads, laundry soap, bathing soap, buckets and chlorine. On GBV female IDPs are at risk of SGBV due to poor security and living conditions in settlements and risky livelihood practices. Camp leaders reported attacks during firewood collection (95%), water collection (65%), traveling to market (62%), at latrines (47%), and at home (47%). The few service provision centres lack trained medical CRM staff, provision of psychosocial support & legal personnel. NCA’s mid-term report (April 2019) found most SGBV cases go unreported. 1b. Joint program planning , implementation is central to this intervention. Theinvolvement of the communities strengthens their ability to manage the project andensure that needs prioritized locally are responded to. Empowering/capacitydevelopment of the different committees will ensure increased management andsustained access to WASH and protection services. Through the partnerships, thedifferent and complementary roles of local authorities, committees and localpopulation will be strengthened. This together with building the capacity of thecommittees will enhance community development in a manner that communities areresponsible of their own needs. At the end of the project, community capacities andcoping mechanisms are expected to be strengthened on different fronts,constructed/rehabilitated infrastructure will remain in place, and hopefully, capacitiesfor peace and stability will be enhanced 2a. Include gender sensitivity analysis in allassessments; integrate gender sensitive perspectives into NCA's staffing policies androutines; include gender considerations in partner assessments - in terms of bothpartners' capacity and routines; engage all stakeholders in target intervention areas toensure women's and girls’ inclusion in the initiative; include women and girls in ameaningful way into NCA and partners activities; establish necessary mechanisms toavoid increased exposure to discrimination or exclusion based on gender. NCA has agood standing with communities where we work. On the basis of this, NCA has a fairunderstanding of the current sensitivities surrounding ‘conflict prevention’ in Somalia.Recognizing these complexities and sensitivities, NCA has developed risk mitigationstrategies that have been utilized to enable project implementation. First, NCA employsnational staff and works with partners who understand the local context and are wellknown and respected by local authorities. NCA national staff can access locationsthat non-Somali nationals cannot access, hence ensuring continuous projectimplementation and monitoring even in challenging contexts 2b. NCA also works withlocally based partners who are located within the communities we serve. Further, NCAalso employs remote monitoring systems to follow up and monitor projects wherephysical access is a challenge. Second is a deliberate approach to project branding:selecting language that will explain the project fairly and accurately, in a way that willbe acceptable to stakeholders. Third, is an approach of open, ongoing stakeholderengagement involving NCA/partners, local communities and government. Regularsecurity updates will be sought from International NGO safety organization, NCA andpartners’ security teams to ensure that staff are well informed on security situation fornecessary precautions and arrangements. 3a. NCA also has a complains handling mechanism in place that will give beneficiaries the freedom to raise concerns they may have on the project. DO no Harm considerations are also put in place to ensure that no further harm is targeted on the already vulnerable groups being supported as part of our interventions

This project takes cognizance of the reality of climate, climate change andneeds for putting in place some adaptation mechanism. While this project is notspecifically addressing environment issues in Somalia, it will ensure thatresponses targeted have no or minimal impact on the environment. Thedistribution of solar lamps done in past projects is an example of such efforts.The design of the WASH facilities has taken into consideration theenvironmental impact of the project. Excavations done will ensure that soilexcavated is well disposed of to limit effect on environment. The facilities willdrain water into soak pits to ensure that water collection surfaces do not turninto breeding ground for insects, a vector of diseases. In the event of anynegative unforeseen impacts, mitigation measures will be put in place to reducethe negative consequences. An overall impact assessment at both governmentand partner level is yet to be conducted but location specific needsassessments are done to inform on project needs and response requirementsthat can help factor in preventive measures. NCA will engage rights holders andstakeholders at all levels and stages of project implementation to ensure theirparticipation. During community mobilization and sensitization, NCA willprovide information to the targeted communities about their entitlement/benefits. NCA will conduct consultative meetings with the relevant governmentofficials and work closely with existing community leaders and representativebodies (Village Committees) especially when selecting project beneficiaries andthroughout the project cycle. Government disaster management units includinggovernment line ministries will remain instrumental in guiding our work toensure it is aligned to government strategies and that it responds to the criticalneeds in project target locations. For enhanced sustainability, the projectintends to build capacities of WASH committees who will be linked to lineministries. . In areas where PPP structures have been established, the projectwill make effort to create linkages between WASH committees in project areasto PPP’s to coordinate and enhance management of the water facilities. Inlocations with strategic water supplies, NCA envisions expansion of thesystems to reach more vulnerable groups. The rehabilitated/constructedcommunal assets will be handed back to community management committeewith supervision of local government to ensure that the assets are accessed byall community groups (women, children and youths). The cash for workbeneficiaries will be linked to the private sectors and Civil society Organizationsin the regions to access new construction job opportunities. NCA will engage allkey stakeholders on project implementation to ensure that they all understandthe goal, objectives and activities.
