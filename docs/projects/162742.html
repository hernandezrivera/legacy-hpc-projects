Project Summary

The project is to Improve access to safe water and promote sanitation & hygiene services among IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in Central Darfur State which is 1 year project. This project will target 8,000 people (male, female, elderly, boys and girls) as follows returnees, IDPs and resident communities in Um Dukhun and Rekoro localities of Central Darfur State.
The 8,000 targeted people will receive water services, provided sanitation services and as well they will receive hygiene services within and outside the camp. The project will focus on rehabilitation of non-functional hand pumps, up-grading of the hand pumps to mini water yards, training of hand pumps
mechanics, refresher training of water committee, drilling of new bore holes and equipping and provision of water facilities & campaign awareness. There will be provision of sanitary kits including towels for 1000 (women and adolescents girls in and out of schools) through core pipelines in the 2 localities of Central Darfur State each 500 beneficiaries per locality.

1 - Needs

HNO 2019 indicates that the impact of the conflict in Darfur, South Kordofan and Blue Nile States has been widespread displacement with some 1.86 million IDPs (male 911400 and female 948600). In Darfur over 624000 people(305760 male and 318240 female)returned to their place of origin since when conflict started in 2013 inter-communal conflict particularly in parts of Darfur report by UNAMID 2018 also decreased in intensity and conflicts between farmers and agro-pastoralists communities over access to and use of resources primary land and water.It is estimated that out of 2.8 million people in 11 states need humanitarian WASH assistance which includes people without access to potable water, sanitation and hand washing facilities.These were caused by factors such as the current economic situation, malnutrition, diseases outbreak and limited water resources and natural disaster such as a drought and flood in 2019 . Across Sudan, WASH remains an urgent humanitarian priority.Surveys done at IDPs camps indicate that water service varies from 7.5 to more than 20 liters per person per day with an important number of them are still below the global SPHERE standards. This involves prioritization of water use within the household and resorting to use of unsafe drinkable water. There are humanitarian needs for construction of new and rehabilitation of households, public and communal latrines which are gender sensitive and consider disability and age in the same localities mention blow. The hygiene promotion was identified as a priority for targets with at least 1 hygiene promoter for 500 persons. This project will target 8000 people (Disability 15%, Adult 55%, elderly 5% and Children 40%) in Central Darfur State.The project target in Rekoro 4000 people(male 49% and female 51%) and 4000 people(male 49% and female 51%) in Um Dukhun localities each will receive water, sanitation and hygiene services collective. SORC will work in coordination with UNICEF, IRW, TGH, NEF, CRS and IOM in the targeted localities. Women and children are the primarily the ones who fetches water among IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in Central Darfur state. Long distances to water points expose them to potential physical and sexual abuse within this context of instability. Lack of latrines at the household and community level has resulted in increased risk of across contamination and the spread of illnesses, special consideration in design of water sources for disable, children, elderly and lactating women for easy access. The loss of trees affects soil fertility, contributes to erosion and reduced groundwater level which resulted to degradation in the environment.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Cutting of tress for construction of pit latrines at household's level affects the environment where loss of trees affects soil fertility, contributes to erosion and reduces underground water level which resulted to degradation in the environment. Upgrading of water sources to water yards requires power. SORC will mitigate by introducing technology of using solar panels for energy to pump water avoid air pollution, disseminate environmental messages to raise awareness during training and campaign on benefits tosupport sustainable use and maintenance WASH facilities. SORC further will encourage use of environmental friendly materials for constructions and rehabilitation latrines where compensation will be done by planting additional trees. Campign of cut one tree plant 5 seedlings will be key message to restore the environment.
