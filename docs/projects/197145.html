Project Summary

The proposed WASH project for 2023 will contribute to the achievement of HRP 2023 objectives through triple nexus approach. The Project will be achieved by increasing access and sustainable use of gender sensitive and safe emergency WASH services such as water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion sessions, ensuring that conflict affected populations receive immediate and sufficient assistance according to their needs. The project will respond to WASH needs in IDP camps and communities as well as in health facilities and schools. It will ensure free and equitable access to safe drinking water and latrine in secure places, enhance their knowledge on WASH practices, prevent the spread of COVID 19, reduce prevalence of diarrhea and ultimately also lead towards improved nutrition security. This will be achieved through a combination of interventions such as construction/rehabilitation of WASH facilities, Risk communication and community engagement (RCCE), Social behavioral change (SBC), formation and training of gender balance WASHCOMs with 40-50% female and building capacity of government authorities to effectively provide lifesaving assistance and have improved capacity to meet the immediate needs of newly displaced or returning populations. Throughout the course of the project, the active participation of the stakeholders and affected persons including women and marginalize group in the design and implementation to ensure the sustainability of the interventions while protection needs of women and girls will be identified and integrated into programming.

1 - Needs

Nigeria experienced its worst flooding in recent history with more than 3.2 million affected and with over 600 fatalities with majority of the death cases in Borno and Yobe state. Over 1.4 million people are displaced. Thirty-four of the country’s 36 states have been affected. According to the joint interagency need assessment report (October 2022), heavy rainfall from July through September 2022, flash flooding and thunderstorms devastated the lives of over 160,000 people (27,000 households) across the 17 LGAs in Yobe State. At least 74 people died, and 91 others were injured. It is estimated that over 26,290.6 hectares of land in the state had extensive flooding in the period from 1 August 2022 to 20 September 2022 and additional 7,622 hectares were severely flooded during the same period. In Borno 4,645 HH were affected severely affected by the flood and rainstorm leaving 16,393 individuals in needs of emergency assistance while 68 casualties were recorded in 7 LGAs within this period. Acute watery diarrhea/Cholera continued to spread across the states, Borno recorded 10,922 cases with 247 deaths having case fatality rate (CFR) of 2.3% Yobe a cumulative total of 1,890 AWD/cholera cases, including 59 deaths in 10 LGAs, were recorded by the State Ministry of Health in week 39. This number increased to 2423 in week 41 with 69 fatalities (Epidemiological Data). Most of the cases (69%) were concentrated in three LGAs of Gujba, Gulani and Fika and 26% of the cases were recorded in three other LGAs of Damaturu, Potiskum and Fune. While the number is relatively lower in 2022 compared to reported cases in the same week in 2021, the total case fatality rate was higher at 2.8% compared to 2.5% same week in 2021. This can be attributed to several factors, including but not limited to limited community awareness of the outbreak and preventive measures, lack of medical supplies in hotspots locations, limited case management drugs as reported by the State Rapid Response Team led by the Ministry of Health at the weekly coordination meetings, lack of isolation units in some communities where high AWD/cholera cases were reported, poor environmental sanitation and hygiene in affected communities, access challenges to remote affected communities to deliver urgently needed health service and for the affected people to access service in other areas due to the flooding and security concerns, difficulty in conducting preventive and control (WASH) measures due to the flooding, funding, and road accessibility and late presentation to seek medical care. The awareness level of what constitutes safe water is very low. It is imperative that state authorities and partners intensify their efforts to improve water quality. A significant amount of investment is required at various points along the water supply chain, both in rural and urban areas, to treat and test water quality. Since the respondents indicated that 60% of their water containers were lost during the flooding, they are forced to the remaining 40% of available containers. This is an indication that the households may not be consuming the required quantity of water since they lack the containers to collect and store water at the household level.

2 - Activities

Activity 01: Improved protected access to safe water supply for affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno states. In close coordination with the Government, RRT, RRM working group and the WASH sector-working group, the response will be deployed, integrating protection concerns and adopting a gender sensitive approach to adequately determine the needs of the male, female, boys and girls. Populations will be provided with access to a safe water supply (water trucking /quick repair of water points with chlorination, new drilling and rehabilitation of boreholes with solar pumping system/hand pumps) that prioritizes first distribution to vulnerable groups (women, children and elderly people). Water quality testing including free residual chlorine will be tested for all water supply to ensure the quality of the water. The IDP camps will be supported with operation and maintenance of water points to ensure continues safe water supply. In communities, WASHCOM will be formed and trained with 40-50% of female in the committee to ensure the needs of the women are considered during the implementation and LGA level revolving fund mechanism will be establish to improve the sustainability of water points. Activity 02: Improved safe access to sanitation service for affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno state. Multi-unit communal gender separated latrines will be constructed in IDP camps in safe places with light to ensure the safe access. Male and female Hygiene and sanitation volunteers will be trained and supported to monitor the cleaning and maintenance of latrines. Solid waste management will be conducted through distribution of waste bins, trainings and coordinating with BOSEPA for waste collection. In communities where returnees, host communities and IDPs have been displaced for extended periods, the project will focus on household latrines. Following a transect walk focused on the negative aspects of open defecation, the Community Lead Total Sanitation approach will be launched. The approach will ensure that community mobilizers and project staff integrate gender-related dimensions to ensure women and girl’s participation while developing community action plans. Beneficiaries will commit their own resources, including locally available materials to construct latrines. Local artisans and skilled technicians will be trained to build standard structurally sound latrines. The design will ensure that pour flush/VIP latrines for health facilities and schools are accessible to people with disabilities and take into consideration the needs of women, children and elderly. The structure will include ramps, be child-friendly, have hand railings, be lockable, gender segregated and have containers for menstrual hygiene management. Activity 03: Improved safe access to hygiene practices within affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno state. Hygiene promotion activities will be delivered with different approaches in emergency phases and resilience phases. In emergency, rapid hygiene session conducted by hygiene promotes and volunteers through group session and HH sessions. WASH EM approach will be implemented to improve the hand washing practices. In resilience building phase the beneficiaries will be reached through mix approaches of PHAST, CHAST, CLTS and sanitation marketing to ensure sustainability of the practices. BABY WASH approaches will be implemented together with health and nutrition staff members to improve the WASH practices and prevent the malnutrition. In addition to that the use of IECs and other communications materials will be adopted in sensitizing the beneficiaries through house-to-house visits, FGDs, campaigns to be conducted by a trained and equipped gender balance community hygiene volunteers and CHIPs. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The safety of beneficiaries will be prioritised in the delivery of WASH infrastructures such as water points, latrines and showers. With open defecation being practiced of both women and men in target area. Following an environmental analysis, ACF will prioritise the construction of latrines that are proximate to households and with adequate lighting. As 87% of women and girls are partially or totally responsible for collecting water, water points will be located in places convenient for women and girls. ACF will work to increase women’s voice and leadership by working with the state and community structures. We would ensure women are active participants in project decision-making and monitoring by conducting household surveys.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

ACF has a well-established accountability system with a feedback and complaint response mechanism within the MEAL Department, which aims at improving the quality of interventions and promoting Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in line with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) commitment on AAP, and ACF’s goal of institutionalizing the Mission's Accountability Framework (Including Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) in all functions and operations alongside system level cohesion, coordination and learning. To ensure accountability to affected population in this project, ACF will ensure that participation, feedback and complaints mechanisms are integrated in all aspects of this project to provide a range of rapid and accessible channels of communication to the affected populations. These channels will facilitate reception of feedback and complaints and to inform affected populations about procedures and processes that affect them so that they are able to make informed decisions and choices. ACF will capitalize on the result of Humanitarian Accountability Assessment (HAA) to determine the existing systems and structures of complaints referral as well as to determine the communities’ preference in terms of channels to put in place to enable them provide feedback/complaints relating to the intervention, while maintaining do-no-harm principle. Through the established channels, ACF will ensure that all formal and informal communications from persons of concern (both positive and negative) are analyzed to inform protection, assistance and solutions to the programming, and corrective action is taken as appropriate. Furthermore, ACF will ensure constant sensitization of the affected population on the established channels and how to use them for earing their views and opinion regarding the intervention.
