Project Summary

This project is designed to target South Sudanese Refugees and Ethiopian refugees in Khartoum, Gadaref, White Nile, West Kordofan and East Darfur state, through provision of WASH services for a number of 57,000 individuals as WASH beneficiaries, disaggregated to 41% male, 59% female, 8% elderly, 63% children and 29 adults. The intended project aims to delivered saving lives.
The main WASH activities will be delivered by HOPE are: Rehabilitate of hand pumps; Rehabilitate of water yards; Operate and maintain water sources (includes water quality treatment and monitoring); Construction of communal latrines; Rehabilitate of shared communal latrines; Provision of soap, Jerry can and Personal Hygiene Materials; Do hygiene campaigns; Dispose Solid waste and support its management; Do vector control activities; Operate and maintain water sources including water quality treatment and monitoring); Rehabilitation of shower blocks/ bathing facilities.
HOPE will consider accountability to affected people through full community participation, strengthen the current community mechanism/ committee such as WASH committees, hygiene promotors and IGAs as well as do follow up for all benefit targeted separately to assess the project, identify challenges and provide recommendations to improve the project outcomes.

1 - Needs

Clashes between SAF and RSF have been ongoing since 15 April, particularly in and around residential areas in Khartoum, Darfur stats except East Darfur as well as a north part of West Kordofan state and capital city of North Kordofan. As of 13 May, the Federal Ministry of Health reports that at least 700 people have been killed and over 5,200 injured since the onset of the fighting. The number of casualties is likely to be higher, as many individuals were reportedly unable to reach hospitals, services facilities and markets due to movement constraints. Several cities have faced heavy bombardments and clashes, and associated unexploded ordnance, causing hundreds of civilian deaths and casualties, as well as damage to critical water, health care and power infrastructure. Looting and destruction of health facilities, Ongoing violence has caused extensive damage to infrastructure, leading to water shortages, blackouts, communication disruptions and incidents of looting. Based on WASH gabs and findings collected by HOPE in the targeted localities in Sep-Oct 2022 and updated in mid of May 2023, in these target localities, compared to a gap, the WASH interventions are very weak including a shortage of clean water drinking, deterioration of sanitation & hygiene; no latrines in most villages; open defecation is widely practiced increasing, drinking water is suspected to be contaminated meanly in the rainy season, no vector control was in place; increasing of flies and mosquitos was reported. In WN, the new arrival from South Sudan Refugees that fled from Khartoum are about 139,000 which put additional pressure on the available limited services.Sudan hosts one of the largest refugee populations in Africa. South Sudanese make up the majority. Many others fled violence and persecution in neighboring countries, including Eritrea, the Central African Republic, Ethiopia, and Chad, but also the wars in Syria and Yemen pushed people to seek safety in Sudan. Most refugees live in out-of-camp settlements, host communities and urban areas, while others stay in 24 camps (10 at the East Sudan, 1 at Blue Nile State 10 at White Nile State, 2 at East Darfur State and 1 at the Central Darfur State). Sudan continues to generously host and receive additional asylum-seekers. As of 31st January 2022 a total refugees and asylum-seekers in Sudan, 1,127,247 individuals, (70%) are women and children. This integrated project will target a number of 45,200 individuals includes 35,700 individuals as WASH beneficiaries, and 9,500 individuals (1,900 HHs) as FSL beneficiaries (disaggregated to 41% male, 59% female, 8% elderly, 63% children and 29 adults) in Khartoum, White Nile, Gadaref, East and West Kordofan states. Refugees and asylum seekers often lack adequate support to satisfy their basic needs such as Education, WASH, health and livelihoods opportunities. Normally people who move from home countries to another country of asylum are characterized by a number of traumatic experiences, such as abandon of places of origin and correlated networks, risk of becoming a victim of violence, abuse and lack of adequate services. The response to their needs, including medical, psychological, social, economic and legal has been identified as a top priority intervention. Based on WASH assessment findings conducted by HOPE in the targeted localities in Sep-Oct 2022, as well as observations during visits to Gadaref, in these target localities, compared to a gap, the WASH interventions are very weak including a shortage of clean water drinking, deterioration of sanitation & hygiene; no latrines in most villages; open defecation is widely practiced increasing, drinking water is suspected to be contaminated meanly in the rainy season, no vector control was in place; increasing of flies and mosquitos was reported. Due to the above situation, HOPE looks for improving access to WASH and FSL services.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In recruitment process HOPE will continue to recruited project staff from targeted communities, the staff involved and fully dedicated to implement and follow up on .Project staff will be oriented on Code of conduct to avoid potential risks of violence, exploitation and abused of the beneficiaries of the project. The integrated project will adopt community based approach that promotes the active engagement and participation of all community segments in design and implementation phases to ensure the community by-in and sense of ownership. HOPE team confirmed that the Community management committee and water committees compositions considers balanced representation of gender, age, social status and minority to improve the decision making and allow the voices for these groups. Following the participatory and community based approaches, targeted communities will be involved in discussion, consultations and decision regarding planned activities. Through this, the project intends to ensure full participation and strengthen the ownership of communities. This will contribute to provide the target communities a concrete plate forum to identify their concerns, needs, challenges and needed actions to improve the protective environment. APP is core activity, Additionally applying Do No Harm principle to clearly done in this project through involvement and inclusion of the communities and hosting communities with equal access to direct assistance based on vulnerability criteria. HOPE will consider the dignity and confidentiality of beneficiaries by using and follow Safety, Access Desegregated Date (SADD), in monitoring and data collection and will strongly prioritize the best interest of the beneficiary by following protocols and standards. As part of the Do No Harm approach, HOPE organization will provide training to all project team members on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and code of conduct. This will help to mitigate the risk of harassment, abuse and other violation to be committed by staff members against the beneficiaries. In addition, the project team will conduct regular consultation with community leaders/ members and local authorities and receive their feedback about the project delivered service through complaint mechanism. This will help to identify any negative consequences that might be caused by the intervention as well as the necessary corrective/mitigation actionDuring design, HOPE assessed the safety, security and risk for target areas as well as types of GBV that facing women. During implementation, HOPE will support the capacities of committee members and assist to know their rights, concepts of GBV, Child Rights, Child Protection, and PSEA. HOPE has put in place for better understanding among the beneficiaries for self-protection on various programmatic matters. HOPE also will develop a system to collect feedback from beneficiaries to measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.
