Project Summary

IOM will continue supporting refugees aiming at reaching 96,321 households living in 17 IOM Are of Responsibility (AOR) camps with Shelter and Non-Food Items (NFI) including Liquefied Petroleum Gas related (LPG) interventions.
Under the Objective 1: Provide life-saving emergency Shelter/NFI support to households affected by disasters and other sudden onset events, IOM plans the implementation of cyclone prepositioning (covering all 17 camps as per the catchment area arrangement), emergency LPG distribution (in 16 camps), emergency NFI distribution (17 camps), Rapid Damage Verification (RDV) and shelter material distribution for emergency cases (15 camps) and tie down kits with technical supports (10 camps).
Under the objective 2: Provide safe and decent living conditions to reduce suffering, IOM plans to implement gender and inclusion activities (17 camps), Second phase of Shelter Upgrade and Maintenance (SUM) (13 camps), repair of Mid-Term Shelters (MTS) (4 camps), construction of new MTSs, NFI distribution (5 camps), and solar lamp distribution (8 camps) and LPG related activities (16 camps).
For the emergency NFI and shelter materials distribution, SUM and NFI distribution, IOM plans to implement directly and through Pipeline Partners. IOM will ensure to provide the required shelter and NFI materials to Pipeline Partners. For emergency NFI and shelter materials distribution based on RDV, Camp 13 and 19 will be implemented through Caritas and Camp 14, 15 and 16 will be implemented in coordination with HEKS. For the implementation of SUM, Camp 8E will be covered by NRC and Camp 14, 15 and 16 will be covered by HEKS utilizing the materials provided by IOM through Pipeline. For the NFI voucher programme, Camp 14 and 15 will be covered by HEKS through the Pipeline.
On the cyclone prepositioning, IOM will ensure the prepositioning of the emergency shelter kits at containers located at the strategic locations within camps covering the needs by 30 percent for camps in Ukhia, and 100 percent for camps in Teknaf. All emergency NFI and shelter material distribution will be based on the RDV and IOM will continue to be the Shelter Focal Point to conduct and assist as per the verification in Camp 8E, 8W, 9, 10, 18, 20, 20 Extension, 22, 24 and 25. Cyclone and monsoon preparedness will be conducted in these camps on top-of the ongoing SUM implementation and through technical guidance and message dissemination on key elements to strengthen. After the completion of the first phase of the SUM, the second phase of the SUM will be implemented to upgrade and maintain the existing shelters. Due to the temporary nature of the shelter materials used, the provision of shelter materials is required at least once a year to ensure the safe and decent living condition. SUM will be implemented according to the Shelter/NFI Sector’s technical guidance. IOM will continue its effort to repair the MTS in Camp 20 and 20 Extension, also start the repairs of the MTS constructed during 2021 after the fire in Camp 8E and 9 covering the caseloads implemented by IOM and Pipeline Partner. The new construction of the MTS will be implemented only when fire and other disaster occurred causing complete destruction of the shelters in large scale in coordination with the Office of the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner and Shelter/NFI Sector. NFI distribution will be implemented utilizing the vouchers. In 2023, IOM plans to prioritize to implement NFI distributions in Camps 10, 13, 14, 15 and 19. For the solar lamps, IOM plans to cover Camp 8E, 8W, 9, 10, 13, 19, 20 and 20 Extension. Prior to the distribution, the orientation on how to use and maintain the solar lamp will be conducted and repair and maintenance centre will be established at the IOM’s Distribution Points. LPG activities will be implemented in Camp 8E, 8W, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19, 20, 20 Extension, 22 and 24 directly by IOM, to address the need for cooking fuel.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

In August 2021, the Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment found that 74 per cent of refugees prioritized their need for shelter materials over food and self-reliance activities, an 11 percent increase from 2020. Considering that the situation in the camp is unchanged or worsening due to the funding decrease across the sector, the dire needs of not only shelter materials but also NFI continue to be among the top priority needs in 2023. Many of the shelter materials such as bamboo poles, tarpaulins and ropes require replacement, considering the limited lifespans and difficult weather conditions with monsoons and cyclones. The requirement for regular replacement is therefore inevitable to ensure decent living conditions. Provision of the shelter assistant through emergency shelter response, SUM, repair and construction of the MTS require to continue in order to minimize the biggest gap among the affected populations, and these interventions will also directly contribute to ensure protection, security, privacy and health of the beneficiaries. The 2022 Shelter/NFI Sector assessment showed that the average number of portable solar lamps per households is 0.35 which is far below the minimum standard which is 2 portable solar lamp per households, according to the Energy and Environment Technical Working Group’s solar lighting guidelines. The provision of solar lamps coupled with the repair and maintenance activities is required to continue to ensure safety during the night within and outside of shelters. On the other NFIs, the 2022 Shelter/NFI assessment also clarifies that all the key items for affected populations are not met sufficiently even after five years since the influx. Rather than providing random in-kind NFIs, the use of voucher approach to ensure the freedom of choice and different needs of the beneficiaries is required in the camps where it is identified as a gap by the Shelter/NFI Sector. In order to minimize the use of firewood and other items such as plastic as a cooking fuel, the continued provision of the LPG refills to all refugee households in camps is crucial. This is not only to prevent deforestation but also avert respiratory illness, improve mental health, increase spending on food and time for care and study, reduce inter-group and domestic violence. It is critical that sustained access to LPG cooking fuel continues without interruption to meet the essential needs of refugees and avoid renewed degradation of recently restored landscapes, especially given current restrictions on refugees’ access to stable income to afford the cost of LPG themselves. Acknowledging the donor fatigue, further research on alternative energy sources, and strengthening the training of efficient cooking techniques will be required to continue.Involvement of and assistance towards vulnerable host communities is another element that is necessary when providing support to the populations in the camps. The operations of the IOM’s Bamboo Treatment Facility will not only contribute for providing the durable materials to beneficiaries by treating and prolonging the lifespan of the bamboo poles but also contribute to capacitate host communities’ skills for operating such facility. Continuous provision of the LPG refills is also contributing to mitigate the conflict with among refugees and host communities over already limited resources around camps which will lead to better social cohesion in the area.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

IOM will focus its interventions in the camps that are categorized as IOM Area of Responsibility (AOR). Among the 17 IOM AOR camps, based on the discussion within the Shelter/NFI Sector, the focal point agency for each camp is designated and has been implemented. IOM will continue to be a Shelter Focal Point agency on covering Shelter/NFI for Camp 8 East, 8 West, 9, 10, 18, 20, 20 Extension, 22, 24 and 25. And other camps are designated to other Shelter partners namely BDRCS in Camp 11 and 12, Caritas in Camp 13 and 19 and HEKS in Camp 14, 15 and 16 in consultation with Shelter/NFI Sector. IOM will still intervene in other camps namely Camp 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 and 19 supporting the Shelter/NFI Focal Point Agencies with the provision of materials through Pipeline to respond emergency Shelter/NFI cases, implementation of Shelter Upgrade and Maintenance (SUM) and provision of LPG refills to address the cooking fuel needs. IOM’s Pipeline Partners in this regard that are planned in 2023 are HEKS (Camp 14, 15 and 16), Caritas (Camp 13 and 19) and NRC (tentatively at Camp 8E and Camp 25). For LPG specific intervention in Camp 25, it will be covered by Nabolok. All Shelter/NFI activities implemented in each camp will be done according to the Shelter/NFI Sector’s guidance and all activities are reported accordingly. Consultation with CiCs and SM actors will be ensured for the integrated service provision. IOM will ensure its involvement and play a leadership role in the Shelter/NFI sector to implement the continued support incorporating the Shelter/NFI Sector’s multi-year strategy. IOM will commit to strengthen the capacity building of other Shelter/NFI actors so that expansion of the Pipeline system is sought for more efficient intervention with coherent approach and quality of materials and items provided to the beneficiaries. IOM will coordinate all shelter interventions in IOM AOR camps to ensure the implementation of rationalized Shelter/NFI intervention.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

IOM will ensure protection is mainstreamed in all activities for implementing Shelter, NFI including LPG programmes. IOM will ensure that referrals from protection actors are prioritized and upon verifications to assist immediately. Periodic orientations with Protection and Site Management actors on the process for verification and shelter support will be conducted and in case there are any changes to the responsible staff in each camp,the information will be shared to ensure smooth referrals.All extremely vulnerable individuals (EVIs) will be supported with porter support to carry materials from the distribution point to their shelters as well as construction support with received materials.For LPG, the both way between EVI’s shelter and distribution point, the porter support will be provided. All staff including third party contracted staff and suppliers’ staff are trained on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and refresher training is conducted at least once a year.

3.2 - Gender

Gender is mainstreamed in all activities within IOM’s Shelter/NFI activities. Team are consisted gender-balanced, so that female beneficiaries are assisted with the female staff and likewise for males. All the distributions will be done at the Distribution Points that are designed to prevent and mitigate gender-based violence (GBV) by ensuring the separate lines for male, female and extremely vulnerable individuals, seating arrangements, breast feeding room for lactating mothers and complaints and feedback desks to address the issues and record their feedback immediately and bring changes to the programme as necessary. All trainings will be provided not only for the head of households but also second representatives to ensure that the capacity building efforts are provided for multiple genders. IOM will strengthen its effort to capacitate the females and youth as carpenters to increase their involvement in support for repair and maintenance of shelters.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

At the time of data collection, IOM ensures that age and sex desegregated data are collected so that different needs depending on age are captured and responded accordingly. At the time of implementation of shelter activities, engagement with the youth will be ensured so that they are trained to support the shelter upgrade and maintenance activities among their community. IOM will ensure to have works that are suitable for older person to take part to ensure their participation to the cash for work activities to support the Shelter/NFI interventions. The child labor is prohibited at all level of implementation of the project including the work done by supplier. In order to avoid the situation where children carrying the relief items, community meetings will be conducted prior to the distribution to identify the vulnerable households to provide porter support.

3.4 - Diversity

The involvement of the persons of disability and LGBTQIA+ personnels/communities will be ensured for the cash for work activities to engage in our implementation. The needs will be assessed during the community meetings so that additional supports are provided. FGD will be conducted to prior and after the project implementation to capture their needs and feedback in each activity. IOM ensures that all distribution points are accessible for the persons with disability and necessary support is provided to ensure the accessibility. In coordination with Site Management and Protection actors, IOM will ensure vulnerable ethnic and religious groups are supported equally and provide additional supports as required.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

At the time of designing stage of the project, the consultation with beneficiaries through focus group discussion (FGD) and key informants’ interview (KII) will be conducted. The orientation and trainings will be provided to beneficiaries for all programmes to explain the programme so that eligibility and process are clarified, and beneficiaries are trained to utilize the materials provided properly. The complaints and feedback desks will be set at all IOM’s distribution points. The strong communication channels with Protection actors for protection referrals and Site Management actors on the referrals from the Common Feedback and Response Mechanism continues to be ensured. Post distribution Monitoring (PDM) through household level surveys, FGD and KII will be conducted to capture the beneficiaries’ feedback and effectiveness of the implemented programme and reflect the required change in future implementation.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

IOM ensures that all borak bamboo poles used in all shelter interventions are treated to prevent the poles infected with incents and rotten due to the moistures and through treatment to prolong the lifespan of the poles and decrease the frequency of the replacement that subsequently contributes to less usage of the resources. The strict quality control measures are applied at the time of procurement of borak bamboo poles and muli bamboo poles to avoid suppliers harvesting premature bamboo poles that do not fit for the usage in shelter programme. The provision of LPG prevents deforestation by switching the energy source from firewood.Annually it prevents extraction of 330,600 tons of firewood from around 6,000 hectares of forest around the camps and avoid emission of 407,000 tons of carbon dioxide.IOM will also ensures that plastic packing materials are not used and alter with organic materials such as jute bag and bamboo basket that could be used continuously after distribution.
