Project Summary

WART is proposing the FSL response plan which seeks to address the food security and livelihood needs of 120,500 vulnerable individuals (78,000 women, and 42,500 men) in IPC phase 3+ within Aweil North, Aweil South, Kapoeta North, Ayod, Duk, Canal /Pigi, Rubkona, Mayom, Abeimhnom counties of South Sudan through delivery of humanitarian food assistance, Emergency livelihood support and resilience sustenance Northern Bahre ghazel, Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei, and Unity states.
WART envisages that implementation of this project interventions will prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and improve coping strategies for the target beneficiaries in IPC phase 3, 4 & 5 counties.
In line with FSL cluster objectives and HRP strategy of 2025, the project seeks to enhance emergency food assistance and production and contribute to the reduction in dependency on food and agricultural inputs.
WART will apply multiple intervention strategies including unconditional and General Food Distribution, cash transfers, complementary livelihood support, Sustainability plan through enhanced capacity building for instance on modern agronomic practices, Integrated Pest Management, Natural Resource Management, farming value chains through an investment of post-harvest handling equipment, climate resistance agriculture, disaster risk reduction among others; and resilience building at community and household level through microgrant support to the identified vulnerable individuals.
WART aims to target host communities, returnees, and IDPs vulnerable people whose livelihoods will be protected under this response plan by providing livelihood support during the main planting and dry season (crop and vegetable seeds, tools, swing kits), support food security information, analysis and coordination and strengthen absorptive capacity and build resilience at household and community level.
This project will further complement WART’s ongoing FSL community resilient programming to provide immediate and quick impact FSL response activities while protecting the gains of resilience programming for sustainability in South Sudan.
Given the limited resources and yet greater need for impact, WART will implement complementary interventions (Education, Health, Nutrition, WASH, and Protection) from different donors and in the targeted locations, while maintaining coordination and complementarity with the efforts of other humanitarian actors and in alignment with line ministries; and incorporate a Do No Harm approach as a mechanism for safeguarding the beneficiaries as well as accountability to the affected population. To reach the target beneficiaries with the prioritized FSL cluster objectives, WART is seeking to raise a total of $500,000 for its 2025 FSL project program budget. The proposed FSL response in Aweil North, Aweil South, Ayod, Duk, Canal /Pigi, Rubkona, Mayom and Abeimhnom will be implemented for 12 months while that in Kapoeta North will be implemented for 6 months in line with county prioritization matrix of the 2025 HRP.

1. Needs

The current IPC analysis report (November 2023) reinstates a persistent food insecurity in South Sudan with a projected 7.7 million people likely to face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) at the peak of the lean season in 2025 (SS IPC Nov 2024). The food insecurity situation will remain high in Unity state where 65% of the population will likely face severe acute food insecurity, followed by Jonglei with 62%, Upper Nile (59%), Lakes (57%) and Northern Bahr El Ghazal (62%) with the highest levels of severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) levels at the peak during the peak of the lean season in 2025. The longterm effects of conflict, reoccurrences of sub national violence mostly in Unity, Jonglei, and Upper Nile continue to drive population displacements in various counties resulting into IDPs and further threatening the community and household livelihoods, trade, undermine market functionality, and hindering delivery of humanitarian assistance to the affected populations. This has been exacerbated by the high influx of south Sudanese returnees fleeing the sudan/Khartoum crisis who are the brink of catastrophic conditions. In addition, the effects of the current economic downturn has resulted into hyperinflation with sky rocketing cost of food commodities on the local markets compounded by substantial cereal deficits, inflated prices of staple foods due to a shortage in market supply, deteriorating road conditions that impede market access continue to negatively impact the food security situation in South Sudan.In addition, the erratic climate change which resulted into severe flooding also continues to impact agriculture and livestock production which is one of the most climate sensitive economic sectors. As of November 2024, the abnormal rain pattern had affected crop yields in most parts of the country in general including localities targeted by WART for this FSL response plan. Flooding in most parts of the country continue to limit the flow of both commercial and humanitarian goods and services while sub-national violence is expected to resume in many locations during the lean season, with flooding seeming to be a “new normal” resulting into population displacement, damaging infrastructural facilities (schools, health and nutrition facilities, and crop farms and thus affecting agricultural livelihoods and households ‘ability to produce their own food with the added effect of water logging and water borne disease on livestock and human health. While other locations experience periodic localized drought at critical times of crop growth. The resultant impact is a severe impact on child health and driving acute malnutrition among the vulnerable women and children under the age of five. Still in Urban centers, high food prices despite the fiscal reforms continue to threaten people’s food security because most of the small towns where growing numbers of the population are entirely dependent on local market to access the food resulting in heightened food insecurity and poor nutritional status even in the agro ecological calendar. Given the current food insecurity situation and driving factors at hand, there is need to respond the food security gaps of the vulnerable population including the south Sudanese returnees. WART seeks to address these gaps in Northern Bahre ghazel, Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei, and Unity states with food assistance, emergency food production through support of agricultural livelihood inputs, and to enhance households’ ability to absorb shocks. WART in partnership with different donor partners; FAO, World Bank, Concern World SSHF anticipates to continue implementing Food, Livelihood support, and Cash programming in the target locations.

2. Project activities

WART is proposing FSL activities in accordance with the Food security and livelihood cluster objectives CO1, CO2 and CO3 and 2025 HRP strategy objective so as to address the priority humanitarian needs in South Sudan. CO1: WART will implement the following activities; µ Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations. µ Conduct General food distribution; Cash and voucher distribution (Conditional and unconditional); Conditional food transfer (i.e., FFA) and GFD+; µ Conduct Post distribution monitoring (PDM) CO2 µ Provide emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support. WV will implement the following activities: µ Conduct distribution of emergency agricultural livelihood kits; Main season agriculture seeds and tool distribution; fish, vegetable and crop kits; Emergency food & livelihood support; Fishing kits/ vegetable kits; µ Provide Livestock vaccination; disease prevention &treatment; Vaccination campaign support; Small ruminants restocking; CAHWs refresher training and vet kits support. CO3 In line with resilience building initiative, WART will attempt to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs by supporting and strengthening households’ ability to absorb stressors: µ WART will organize seed fairs using locally sourced seed; µ Support market functionality and support value chain addition; µ Conduct Training &capacity building; Adaptive and absorptive capacities; On-farm production (farmer field schools, demonstration plots); Saving groups, Income generation (business skills), vocational training; Multisectoral models; Livelihoods, nutrition sensitive food security, hygiene, sanitation reducing pest and crop disease; Post-harvest losses management including introduction of appropriate technologies; Village saving & loan schemes & micro finance; Peace building Activities; Establish, train & support peace committees; Enable conflict related meetings to strengthen local peace structures WART uses a result-based management approach to deliver project objectives. µ WART will ensure that the project outcomes are monitored through a monitoring and evaluation framework which consist of Indicator Tracking Tables (ITT), Detailed Implementation Plan (DIP), and project tracker. Tracking of the project outcomes will be done regularly on monthly, quarterly, and annually basis. WART anticipates to measure the following indicators; µ Number of beneficiaries receiving food, non-food items, cash transfers and vouchers µ Number of people receiving seeds and tools µ Number of people receiving fishing kits µ Number of people trained in improved agronomic practicesµ Number of livestock vaccinated and treated µ Proportion of people with improved dietary diversity µ Percentage of increase in food consumption/incomeµ Proportion of people with low Food Insecurity Experience Scale (FIES) During reporting, the project outputs and outcomes [PDM and Annual Outcome Monitoring (AOM)] will be measured based on the above indicators whose values will be tracked on a rolling basis.
