Project Summary

This project will focus on returnees from the Sudan crisis, IDPs, and resident communities in priority 1 counties (Rubkona and Leer) and priority 2 counties (Mayendit, Koch, Guit, and Panyijiar). As of November 12, 2023, UNHCR and IOM recorded 373,094 people crossing the border from Sudan into South Sudan. In the month of November alone, 19,314 people arrived, with an average of 1,609 people arriving daily (Humanitarian Updates Note, November 15, 2023). This situation is expected to worsen with the continued escalation of Crisis in Sudan.
A recent assessment conducted by CH in October 2023 in Rotriak further revealed that among the thousands fleeing the conflict in Sudan are South Sudanese nationals returning through Panakuach, Unity state. Most of these returnees have settled in Rotriak because many villages in Unity are still flooded. Other returnees lacked the means for onward movement to other parts of the country, forcing them to settle in Rotriak with more than 90% currently seeking shelter beneath trees, with only a few having been integrated into IDP communities. Many of the makeshift shelters of IDP households are already totally or partially damaged, and some are sleeping in the open. Therefore, returnees settling in Rotriak without shelters have added to the number of people sleeping/living in the open, ultimately increasing the level of vulnerability in the community. Living conditions are continually deteriorating, particularly during this rainy season, when these temporary structures are further damaged. The absence of suitable shelter and essential non-food items, such as mosquito nets, continues to expose residents to diseases associated with harsh weather conditions like malaria.
Coalition for Humanity (CH) will provide in-kind support for the construction of new shelters and the repair or rehabilitation of damaged shelters for returnees, IDPs, and residents living in camp-like settings. In locations with functioning markets, CH will conduct rapid market assessments and gather information from the Cash Working Group and the joint market monitoring initiatives (JMMI) led by REACH to determine appropriate cash-based modalities for those locations and determining the multi-sector survival minimum expenditure basket (MSSMEB).
CH anticipates a shift in its shelter programming approach and plans to incorporate locally sourced shelter solutions, with a particular consideration on environmental conservation. To mitigate potential housing, land, and property (HLP) cases, CH will conduct thorough due diligence, collaborate with relevant stakeholders, and integrate HLP training for returnees, IDPs, and resident communities in partnership with the appropriate authorities.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face a multitude of challenges, including food insecurity, floods, and inter-communal conflicts. These issues have a significant impact on the lives of many South Sudanese people. According to the latest published IPC report (November 6, 2023), an estimated 14,000 South Sudanese returnees are classified as being in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) with the most food insecure states between September and November 2023 being Jonglei State (61%), Unity State (58%), Upper Nile State (56%), and Lakes State (53%), with more than 50% of their populations facing IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse), with key drivers of this situation being economic decline, conflict and insecurity, climatic shocks, and low agricultural production. An estimated 8.5 million people will require humanitarian assistance in 2024. Of these, 2.1 million people will urgently need shelter and non-food items, with half of the affected population being internally displaced persons (IDPs) (ISNA 2023). The Shelter Cluster has identified 1.4 million people out of the 2.1 million who need humanitarian shelter assistance as their primary target population. These individuals are primarily located in the three states of Upper Nile, Jonglei, and Unity, which are frequently affected by communal violence, natural disasters (primarily floods), and food insecurity. In addition to the existing challenges, the ongoing Sudan crisis and the planned 2024 presidential elections are likely to influence the scale and type of violence as well as displacement patterns of populations (SNFI cluster HRP inputs for 2024). As of November 12, 2023, UNHCR and IOM recorded 373,094 people crossing the border from Sudan into South Sudan. In the month of November alone, 19,314 people arrived, with an average of 1,609 people arriving daily (Humanitarian Updates Note, November 15, 2023). This situation is expected to worsen with the continued escalation of Crisis in Sudan. Transit centers are already overwhelmed with the average cost of the Multi-Sectoral Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (MSSMEB) in South Sudan increasing by 28 per cent since April 2023, as reported by the Cash Working Group. According to Coalition for Humanity assessment conducted in October 2023 in Rotriak Plot 17, among the thousands fleeing conflict in Sudan are South Sudanese nationals returning through Panakuach. Most of these returnees have settled in Rotriak because many villages in Unity are still flooded. Otherreturnees lacked the means for onward movement to other parts of the country, forcing them to settle in Rotriak with more than 90% currently seeking shelter beneath trees, with only a few having been integrated into IDP communities. Many of the makeshift shelters of IDP households are already totally or partially damaged, and some are sleeping in the open. Therefore, returnees settling in Rotriak without shelters have added to the number of people sleeping/living in the open, ultimately increasing the level of vulnerability in the community. Living conditions are continually deteriorating, particularly during this rainy season, when these temporary structures are further damaged. The absence of suitable shelter and essential non-food items, such as mosquito nets, continues to expose residents to diseases associated with harsh weather conditions like malaria. CH will support the reconstruction and rehabilitation of shelters and the replacement of essential NFIs (non-food items) through a combination of in-kind support and cash vouchers (CVAs) in functional markets. This approach will address the needs of 30% of the target population through CVAs and 70% through in-kind support.

2. Project activities

Coalition for Humanity (CH) has identified the following key response priorities and activities for this project: Provision of Emergency Shelter and Non-Food Items: CH will distribute in-kind emergency standard shelter and life-saving non-food items to internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and resident community members affected by both new and protracted displacements in the target project locations. Flood Response: CH will distribute flood kits to flood-affected populations in the target counties and conduct essential emergency shelter upgrades where necessary. Cash and vouchers for essential emergency shelter upgrades, reconstruction, and rehabilitation will be utilized in areas where market assessments determine that markets are functional. Households will be encouraged to construct their own shelters using locally available materials. Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) Training: CH will integrate HLP training and awareness into its response to ensure that beneficiaries gain general information and knowledge about land rights, ownership, and tenure arrangements. CH will achieve this through collaboration with IOM/UNHCR/NRC and the government ministries in the various counties.
