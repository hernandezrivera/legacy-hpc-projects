Project Summary

The project aims at providing Emergency healthcare and nutrition services to respond to the current conflict while ensuring continuity of services in the existing areas of operations to IDPs, Returnees, pastoralist and most vulnerable communities in 6 states of Sudan, namely: Khartoum, Central Darfur, Blue Nile, South Kordofan and West Darfur where World Relief (WR) has been operating in previous years. In addition, for this ongoing conflict, WR plans to starts soon operations in the state of Jazeera to provide lifesaving interventions for IDPs who fled from Khartoum.
As a result of this project, morbidity and mortality attributable to high malnutrition rate and burden of common diseases in the targeted communities will be reduced significantly.
The targeted localities are the following; Azoum, Golo, Nertiti, Rokero, Zalingei, Bindisi,
Mukjar & Umdukun (8 localities of Central Darfur State); Geneina, Kerenik, Habila, Beida
and Sirba (5 localities of West Darfur); Bahri, Karari, Khartoum, Omdurman, Sharg En Neel,
Um Bada and Jebel Awlia (7 localities of Khartoum); Demazine, Kurmuk, Bau and
Rosaries (4 localities of Blue Nile states) and 1 locality of South Kordofan (Kadugli); Sharg al Jazeera and Medani (2 localities in Jazeera).
It is estimated that 832,217 (F:432,753; M:399,464) people; among which 141,477 are children under five years old and 34,838 with disability) will benefit from this project. The beneficiaries are mostly IDPs in the conflict affected states such as Darfur states, South Kordofan and Khartoum while Jazeera, and Blue Nile states, they are most scattered IDPs and refugees who are integrated in the communities and poor families.
The project will ensure that children under five years, women and especially those pregnant or lactating (PLWs) are prioritized as part of people with specific needs and vulnerable to diseases and malnutrition. Thus, the services are in line with the needs of the specific and general population and considering also the services package of the Primary Healthcare (PHC). In addition, the project will focus on the life-saving activities especially in Darfur and Khartoum states while initiating those strengthening and contributing to resilience of the health systems in targeted localities.
Under Health program, the activities will range from the promotional, preventive to curativeof common diseases. Immunization campaign for ,1 year old children will be organized,IEC on different health topics at PHC and community level, maternal health services, ANC,PNC, delivery and newborn care; strengthening the PHC functionality through running cost and establishment of health committees as part of governance & leadership component of the PHC; supplies of essential medicines as per WHO and FMoH guidelines, early warning system activation for disease outbreak or any relevant emergency; support with incentives for the health cadres especially in hard-to-reach areas; capacity building of health cadres, etc.
While the nutrition programing will focus on lifesaving preventive activities such as, support protection and promotion of breastfeeding; Maternal and Young Child Nutrition (MYCN) Counseling; micronutrient supplementation for under-twos and pregnant women; early detection using Mother/family MUAC. In addition, it will support capacity building of both community volunteers & Mother support groups (MSGs) and nutrition cadres at the health facilities; curative activities focusing on CMAM; for Outpatient management and TSFPs.
WR has been implementing health and nutrition programing since 2005 in Darfur and other states of Sudan using an integrated approach where the center of focus will be the patient. The project design is to engage the community from the identification of the needs and their participation in the implementation of the project as part of accountability of WR to beneficiaries.
WR will ensure that mitigation plan is in place for related to ongoing insecurity and other risks.

1 - Needs

According to the OCHA, “humanitarian snapshot of Sep 2022”, rains and floods have destroyed at least 24,860 houses and damaged another 48,250 in 16 out of the 18 states. In addition, more than 211,100 people were newly displaced as a result of localized conflict and insecurity in parts of Darfur and Kordofan regions, as well as Blue Nile State since the beginning of 2022. In WR areas of operations, field staff reported similar emergencies, such as floods especially in Khartoum states; clashes between communities in West Darfur (WD) and Blue Nile; drought in Central Darfur (CD) following the floods in Mukjar and Bindisi localities. All these disasters had left many people in a humanitarian crisis that needs to be responded to. For instance, in Bindisi and Mukjar, WR supported around 20,040 affected families with emergency primary healthcare services, WASH facilities, NFIs, etc. According to S3M II, 2020 version; the nutrition indicators are still alarming with regards to prevalent acute malnutrition in most of the targeted states. For instance, South Kordofan global acute malnutrition (GAM) is 10.21%, while Central Darfur and West Darfur are at 8.8% and 7.5 % respectively. The regular nutrition surveillance bulletin has reported for Q2 of 2022 that the trends of the overall trend for Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was estimated at (4%) in all Sudan while for Khartoum state high SAM proportion (14%) was observed. Access to primary healthcare services is still lower, taking into consideration the vaccination coverage for the 3rd dose of pentavalent vaccine was reported to be at 48% in WD while the ANC 4th visit and more is still lower at 42.2% in CD while the highest in the targeted states is 57% in Khartoum. (S3MII, revised). Thus, the overall project health and nutrition sectors target people in need (PIN), will increase compared to 2022. It is estimated that the project will support provision of healthcare and nutrition services to 517,765(M: 222,639; F: 295.1126) as total population in the five states and 25 localities of project locations. Under nutrition sector, among the under five children in need in the 25 localities, WR will support about 30% of them, who will need treatment and prevention for acute malnutrition; including a total of 23,570 as SAM cases and 38,715 as MAM cases, while 19,760 PLWs are expected to be treated as MAM cases in TSFPs. In addition, among the SAM cases 3,535 children will have medical complications that need referral support and inpatient services at hospitals. In 2023, a total of 175,143 women of reproductive age will be in need of maternal health services ranging from family planning education to facility-based delivery and postnatal care services. While 284,771children under five years old will be in need of basic healthcare services targeting common early childhood illnesses. In general, in the WR operations area, the health system is still weak and needs to be improved at all aspects or building blocks, for instance human resources for health that are in scarcity in some remote areas and where there are, they need to be trained in IMCIs, diseases surveillance and response; etc. In addition, most of the time, PHCs centers are in poor conditions and need to be rehabilitated, equipped with essential medical and non-medical equipment for optimal delivery of services to vulnerable people. Regular Rapid Response Kits (RRK) don’t have enough drugs to respond the demand of clients/patients that ever keeps increasing to seek services in PHCs, especially for anti-malaria drugs or anti-biotics to cure most infections (e.g., Acute respiratory infections, skin infections, etc.)

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Civilians have suffered from years of conflict, exposing them to protection threatsincluding gender-based violence, targeted attacks, and violations of basic humanrights. Freedom of movement, especially in conflict-affected areas such as Darfur,remains restricted. This greatly impacts communities’ ability to engage in livelihoods,undermining opportunities to support self-reliance and preventing durable solutions todisplacement. It also restricts people’s access to basic services. More importantly, thecauses of the conflict particularly in Darfur remain unresolved. Long-standinggrievances between communities continue to give rise to clashes which areaggravated by the involvement of heavily armed tribal militias and the proliferation ofsmall arms and light weapons. Sexual violence particularly affects women and girls indisplaced persons’ camps and in rural communities, where they are often preyed uponby armed men, especially when they engage in livelihood activities like collecting wateror firewood or working in fields. Protection will be mainstreamed across this project, tomitigate any risk, harm, abuse, or exploitation towards the targeted population.Minimizing the distances that people have to travel to receive services minimizes therisk of beneficiaries being targeted by armed groups. Therefore, locations of healthfacilities, nutrition feeding centers, and agricultural demonstration plots are managedas safe spaces that are centrally located and are accessible to large numbers of thepopulation. The locations also minimize the distance that distributed items need to betransported to the home and mitigate the risk of theft or harassment duringtransportation. All project staff will undergo training on protection against sexualexploitation and abuse (SEA) and be sensitized on the risks faced by many of thewomen and children accessing services. The various partners and stakeholdersmentioned throughout the proposal, as well as the beneficiaries themselves, will beasked to participate throughout all stages of the process from beneficiary selection tobuilding the work plan, to scheduling and designing trainings, to monitoring andevaluation. Additionally, WRS will create a number of reporting mechanisms includinga hotline, comment boxes, trained community volunteers, and mechanisms at projectsites through which community members can report incidents. Please refer to theattached document, Accountability to Affected Populations, for more information.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

WRS believes that the organization is accountable to the donor, line ministries, HAC,local authorities where the organization operates & beneficiary communities. WRS alsomaintains that beneficiaries have the right to know and be involved in projectsdesigned to provide service to them. As a result, WRS promotes communityparticipation in all stages of the project life-cycle by working with the communitiesfrom the initial identification of needs to the program phase-out. WRS's M&E systeminvolves all stakeholders for timely information sharing and direct participation inimplementation and M&E at all levels. In addition to ensuring openness andtransparency, this will also ensure best use of the resources according to the scope ofthe project within the budget and priority areas of the beneficiary communities. To thisend, WRS has established community level committees in its 0perational areas andthey are responsible to oversee the implementation and monitoring of project activitiesin their respective areas. To further ensure the communities' engagement, WRstrengthens community level management staff. The community level management isled by Area Coordinators (ACs) based in deep field who work closely with communityleaders. Regularly the ACs review project activities and progress of the project withcommunity committees and share pertinent information. The outcome of communityreview meetings will be reported to the Nutrition coordinators, Health and NutritionManager, Area Manager and the Program director. The information obtained from ACswill be discussed during monthly review meetings. Besides, the Health and NutritionManager, Nutrition Coordinator and Officers, Field Operations Officer and M&ECoordinator regularly visit the beneficiary communities to ensure the accountabilitymeasures are functioning properly. WRS uses suggestion boxes to be placed at eachfeeding center to get the feedback from beneficiary communities on health facilitiesservices. The beneficiary community will be informed to put their feedback anytime inthe suggestion box and WRS's Nutrition and health Coordinator will collect informationfrom the suggestion box on quarterly basis and discuss with Nutrition manager, AreaManager and Program director on improvements of some of the beneficiarycomplaints and also keep up the good side of our services. Also the findings from thebeneficiaries comment will be shared with SMoH for taking collective action. TheNutrition and health coordinators compile project information and share pertinentinformation at the state level with close supervision from the Health and NutritionManager and get feedback from partners to further strengthen the projectimplementation and information dissemination. Being cognizant of the ‘do-no-harm’principles, the project is designed to address nutrition needs of the communities whilemitigating unintended social, economic, and environmental consequences.
