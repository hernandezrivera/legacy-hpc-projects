Project Summary

The proposed project aims reduce WASH related suffering (including morbidity, mortality, and insecurity), to integrate WASH and Nutrition to prevent malnutrition, WASH and Gender Based Violence (GBV) to mitigate risks associated with GBV of conflict affected community through timely, sustainable and equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene services including distribution of WASH NFIs and Menstrual hygiene management for IDPs, host communities and returnees. The proposed project is inline with objectives as there are outlined by the WASH Cluster for South Sudan 2020. This project will be targeting about 130,000 individuals in Unity (100,000) and Mundri West and East (30,000).
The main components of the program will be:
Timely provision of adequate safe and equitable water supply to households, community, health, nutrition and protection structures through ensuring access to water infrastructures that include rehabilitation of broken/damaged boreholes, regular water quality monitoring and capacity building pump mechanics and water management committees.
Provision of dignified sanitation infrastructure using community participation strategy to host communities and returnees and construction of semi-permanent structures for IDPs with tailored designs for people with disabilities/older adults and children.
Extended, all-inclusive hygiene promotion with a focus on behavioral change interventions and menstrual hygiene and NFIs distributions that address acute basic needs and facilitate individual ownership of personal and community health and well-being.
Health, nutrition, food security, and WASH integration to mitigate malnutrition, gender based risks, WASH-related illness and morbidity through referral systems and integrated outbreak response planning. Coordination with Health, Nutrition and Protection clusters to ensure an integrated timely response to any need. Mainstreaming of Menstrual Hygiene Management among school going children with an aim to increase school enrolment among girls.

1 - Needs

Unity state is one of the most conflict affected states of South Sudan specifically the proposed counties under this project – Rubkona, Koch and Panyijar. Mercy corps rapid assessment in April to May 2019 in Boaw -Koch, identified that WASH infrastructure severally damaged during the conflict and inter communal clashes. there report also noted that line ministry does not have capacity to renovate the damaged structures thus community heavy relying on the humanitarian assistance to sustain their lives. In the target communities are in dire need of water, sanitation and hygiene services in Unity state. Lack of safe drinking water, sanitation facilities and poor hygiene practices has left large proportion of communities at risk of preventable WASH-related diseases including diarrhea and Hepatitis E. All efforts towards improving access to safe drinking water, hygiene, and sanitation should be enhanced to prevent the spread of WASH related diseases. The WASH needs for the communities of Unity state are but not limited to: Need of adequate safe water supply infrastructures in the state most of it was damaged and vandalized during the conflict and cattle raids. The existing water points need regular water quality monitoring to ensure the quality of water is still meeting the minimum standards. Communities lack access to dignified sanitary facilities. MC rapid assessment found that there is insufficient basic WASH services and facilities at schools, nutrition and health centres which put population lives at risk missing school days, malnutrition and WASH related diseases. People with disabilities do not have access and dignified WASH services because of limited WASH services in Unity State. Adolescent girls are missing schools for lack of dignified menstrual management resources. Insecurity makes most people not to access WASH NFIs to enhance their hygiene practices like water containers, hand washing facilities. As malnutrition and food insecurity increases, communities become more prone to WASH-related diseases. WESTERN EQUATORIA Mundri West and East are of the areas that have severely affected by the conflict in Western Equatoria since the conflict broke out in 2016. Insecurity in Mundri west and East has severely hampered humanitarian efforts. The prolonged conflict deteriorated the infrastructures in Mundri West and East and the communities have been lacking basic services and they have been exposed to diseases outbreak. According to the inter agency (including Mercy Corps) assessment conducted in June 2019, about 4,878 individuals fled during the 2016 conflict were in Mundri town. Following the signing of Revitalized Peace Agreement in September 2018, the security situation in Kedi’ba and Wandi Payam had improved to such an extent that Kedi’ba IDPs in Mundri have expressed a willingness to return to their place of origin in order to resume normal livelihood activities. but, the basic series are not in placed to support the returnees to run their routine activities and support themselves. A Kedi’ba Payam was one of the areas reached during the assessment, only 3 (30%) out of 10 community water supply facilities, boreholes/hand pumps were functioning, open defecation was prevalent and the awareness and the capacity of the communities were very low to support themselves. According to Mercy Corps rapid assessment conducted in Mundri West and East in June 2019, out of 29 reached community water supply facilities, boreholes/hand pumps 18 (62%) of them were not functioning. Among 10 schools visited during the assessment 3 of them didn’t have latrines, 3 of them had but broken and required repairs, and 4 of them hand latrines but were not functioning due to lack of cleanness. Generally, institutional WASH situation was poor and open defecation was prevailing. Humanitarian support in those locations is indispensable.

2 - Activities

Mercy corps proposed activities includes but not limited to:• provision of safe, adequate and equitable water supply to the vulnerable population through rehabilitation/repairs of the non-functional water supply facilities, • regular water quality monitoring, distribution of point-of use household water treatment chemicals with water containers, training of pump mechanics and water management committees (at least 50% females) on operation and maintenance of community water supply facilities and promotion of safe water chain. • Provision of appropriate and safe sanitation services considering people with special needs including people with disabilities, women and girls through construction of communal latrines at the IDP sites, supporting and promoting household latrine constructions, construction of institutional latrines for schools, nutrition (OTP/SC) centers and health facilities• Hygiene and Sanitation promotion intervention to enable the affected population to prevent themselves form WASH related communicable diseases though intensified and targeted awareness raising activities. In the hygiene and Sanitation promotion intervention all segments of the population particularly people with special needs will be involved. Mercy Corps will conduct hygiene and Sanitation promotion activities using different approaches, including: Selection, trainings, support and follow up of Volunteer Community Hygiene Promoters (VCHPs), at least 50% females, house-to-house visits, focus group discussions, and mass campaigns at water collection sites and various public gathering areas focusing on safe human excreta disposal including children’s feces, proper hand washing, household safe water handling, food hygiene, the use of WASH facilities and solid waste management, design/adaption, production and distribution of IEC materials, establishing, training and support school WASH/Hygiene clubs and Comfee clubs for Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM), distribution of WASH NFIs including dignity/MHM kits to adolescent girls and trainings of different stakeholders including community leaders and food vendors on basic hygiene. • Supporting the health and nutrition programs through provision of water, sanitation and hygiene services to health and OTP/SC sites; rehabilitation/protection of water sources, construction of semi-permanent latrines and solid waste disposal facilities, and using Mercy Corps referral forms, trained VCHPs will refer identified cases with danger signs of diarrhea and malnutrition to health and nutrition facilities respectively.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

MC uses the Community Accountability Response Mechanism (CARM) feedback mechanisms, used across MC globally, to ensure that the needs of the most vulnerable are met and their risks are minimized, CARM involves the community in problem solving and ensures their feedback is taken into account in the response. Besides,, MC will:: • Continuously inform community members (in written/oral language, pictures etc) how to provide feedback on problems and achievements with the services rendered and support them to monitor them jointly. • Install a secure box in various, accessible locations where feedback can be lodged • Hold informal gatherings alongside activities where community members can confidentially provide feedback • In Rubkona beneficiaries can call, SMS or visit the MC Office to provide feedback, About PSEA, MC reinforced its global Integrity Hotline and response to complaints.MC has a dedicated independent Ethics Team staffed by trained, professional investigators to monitor the hotline and oversee investigations. An in-house team works to identify options for stronger processes and procedures for vetting new team members such as background-checking mechanisms, safeguarding-screening questions. Another team works to find legal ways to ensure anyone terminated for sexual misconduct at MC cannot find work in the NGO sector again. On the ground, MC will ensure that its field staff is trained on PSEA; messages on PSEA are posted in offices and public spaces, as well as discussed during meetings with program participants, to ensure that communities receive the information.Overall, MC will monitor the various feedback mechanisms to ensure that they can be accessed by the most vulnerable (incl those who are illiterate, live with disabilities, women,girls etc) and, if not, propose recommendations to adapt mechanisms accordingly. MC will evaluate and analyse feedback collected and take the needed actions.
