Project Summary

Needs:
The 2024 HNO Summary states that 15.4M people are in need of Food Security and Agriculture (FSA) assistance. Furthermore, there are 124 subdistricts categorized as severity score 4 or 5 in terms of FSA needs. ZOA has also conducted their own needs assessment, which confirms the massive depletion of livelihoods infrastructure, high levels of unemployment and poverty coupled with high food prices, sanctions and inflation. All these factors have contributed to food insecurity, as households struggle to meet their daily food needs. Due to devaluation of the Syrian Pound and loss of jobs due to the impacts of the earthquake, as well as COVID and Cholera epidemics, including those in agricultural production, it is likely that food insecurity, malnutrition & adoption of undesirable coping mechanisms will increase, exacerbating existing substantial humanitarian needs across the country.
Intervention, implementation of activities:
ZOA is planning an intervention for 5M USD in 2024, benefitting an estimated 175.000 persons in Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Hama and Quneitra governorates. Based on community-based needs assessments & sector guidance, ZOA will target the most insecure HHs with an emphasis on those in areas with high severity. Activities are aimed at improving the food security & nutrition status of assessed food insecure HHs through life sustaining food assistance. According to needs, market assessments, Ministry of Agriculture & Agrarian Reform (MoAAR) as well as sector guidance & beneficiary preference, appropriate modalities that include vouchers, cash assistance, grants & other support to small-medium farmers will be used. ZOA will build on their previous interventions and experience, and is planning to implement the following activities, in line with the sector indicators:
1) Distribution of agricultural inputs (including seeds, fertilizer, pesticides and equipment, coupled with related training, e.g. on good agricultural practices and climate-smart agriculture). This activity will benefit around 1,400 households.
2) Light rehabilitation of bakeries, targeting two bakeries which will benefit around 20,000 people.
3) Light rehabilitation of irrigation systems, irrigation canals, wells, water and systems, aiming to benefit around 5,000 people.
ZOA’s MEAL is the responsibility of the country-based Manager of Program Quality, supervising MEAL officers in-country. ZOA will monitor its interventions based on the logical framework indicators and activities with sex, age and disability disaggregated. Field level monitoring will be key in quality assurance, tracking progress, improved accountability, and demonstrating impact.
Please note that the project activities are covering a period of two years (2024-2025), but the targets and budget in this intervention only covers 2024. Additionally, for 2025, ZOA aims to target an estimated 200,000 persons with a budget of roughly 5M USD.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Risks, assumptions and mitigation measures:1. Changes in exchange rate & increasing inflation: ZOA closely monitors market prices, and contractors are committed to their offers for a specific period to avoid big changes in pricing due to exchange rate changes or inflation.2. Lack of fuel availability: ZOA has contracts with several transportation service providers, allowing us to conduct the necessary trips without major constraints.3. Lack of skilled labor: ZOA already has contracts with experienced local skilled laborers.4. Delayed approvals: ZOA has an MOU with MOWR to facilitate faster approvals.5. Security risks affecting implementation in the field: ZOA continually assesses the situation in the field and coordinates with INSO and UNDSS.6. Climate shocks or bad weather conditions: ZOA is coordinating with local stakeholders to plan for required mitigation measures to minimize these risks and to plan interventions at the right time in the year. Protection Risk Analysis: The project will adopt an inclusive and non-discriminatory approach so that all groups can safely and equitably access assistance. Target populations are at the center of decision-making processes throughout the project, for example consulting on the eligibility for assistance, which are based on context specific needs, preferences and vulnerabilities. This project considers the safety and dignity of beneficiaries to avoid creating or exacerbating protection risks for individuals, households or communities. (i.e. safety of beneficiaries, social tensions, intra-household dynamics). Project related protection risks include potential abuse, exploitation or other unacceptable behaviors by contractors, suppliers or staff within the community during the implementation of the works or during distributions. Therefore, all contractors, suppliers and ZOA staff/ volunteers will sign ZOA’s Code of Conduct. Also, contractors are required to provide a safe and secure work environment for their workers. Multi-channel feedback mechanisms are in place and ZOA staff knows how to deal with different types of feedback, including referrals for psychological and protection services and support. All staff including volunteers are regularly trained on protection, PSEAH, ZOA’s CoC and child protection. PSEA Strategy: All ZOA staff, volunteers and contractors sign a Code of Conduct informing them of their obligations as well as consequences of involvement in sexual exploitation and abuse. Staff is regularly trained on PSEA and knows how to make safe referrals to relevant services for people identified to be in need of specialized protection services in coordination with the Protection Sector. The project will put in place and inform beneficiaries about several accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries, staff and partners, that include a variety of confidential channels, eg. hotlines, social media, complaints box.
