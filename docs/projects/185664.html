Project Summary

The ETS will continue to provide overall coordination and will improve communications services to the humanitarian community. ETS services and activities to be provided include coordination, information management, data connectivity, security telecommunications, customer support, and capacity development. The ETS plans to expand and improve each of these services while maintaining current sites provided with ETS services.
The humanitarian community’s demand for reliable communications services remains a priority. To address this, the ETS will continue to provide security telecommunications and data connectivity services across all common operational areas to support the humanitarian community and to enable the provision of digital assistance to affected communities.
The ETS security telecommunications network currently provides VHF radio communications to users from 11 UN agencies. In 2022, the network will be expanded to more areas of the camps in collaboration with UNDSS.
The ETS data connectivity network will continue providing connectivity across the operational areas, to both UN agencies and NGOs as needed, enabling these organizations to carry-out their life-saving activities. In 2022, the network will be expanded to more sites, more organizations, and more users across the three operational areas, with a goal of 70 total sites by the end of 2022.
To strengthen local emergency communications expertise and skills on the ground, the ETS will continue to assess the needs of humanitarians and conduct capacity building exercises tailored to those needs. Activities will include trainings telecommunications and radio communications procedures, trainings on the use of data connectivity services, and other requested capacity development programs where gaps in knowledge or skills are assessed.
A new activity in 2022 for ETS will be the Services for Communities project, aiming to increase engagement with the affected communities and provide an opportunity for ETS to support them through technology. This new activity - a part of the global ETC strategy - will evaluate the possibility of implementing communications services directly to the affected populations by providing a range of services such as access to information, common feedback mechanisms, etc.

1 - Identified Needs

From regular assessments in the camps and engagement with humanitarian organizations, the ETS identified the need to continue and expand access to data connectivity in several camps’ locations as well as the need to upgrade the independent security communications network. Among reported challenges when at camps locations, humanitarians cannot send out daily reports nor liaise with staff/main office nor uploading & synchronizing data.The ETS conducted its annual User Satisfaction Survey, collecting responses over the course of three weeks from 28 October to 18 November 2021. The survey was conducted to assess the performance of ETS services and activities, including internet connectivity, security communications, customer support, coordination, and information management (IM) activities for humanitarians in the three operational areas where the ETS provides services: Cox’s Bazar, Ukhia, and Teknaf.The survey also aimed to identify areas of improvement in line with evolving needs on the ground. The results confirmed that the present mobile network services in the Cox’s Bazar district remain sporadic and limited in coverage, revealing the need for an expanded data connectivity network in the camps. This has been identified as of one the main hindrances to humanitarian operations and staff safety.

2 - Activities or outputs

Throughout 2022, the ETS will:1) Continue the provision of coordination and information management services. The ETS will participate in relevant sector meetings and will organize dedicated local ETS working group meetings in Cox’s Bazar. Updated ETS information management products, including dashboards, situation reports, infographics, operational documentation, meeting minutes, etc. will be produced, timely disseminated, and posted on the Bangladesh emergency page on the etcluster.org website. 2) Expand and maintain the provision of security telecommunications services to UN agencies in the response. In 2021, the ETS security telecommunications network provided VHF radio communication to approximately 1,000 users from 11 UN agencies. In 2022, the network will be expanded to more areas of the camps in partnership with UNDSS.3) Expand and maintain data connectivity services across the humanitarian response in all operational areas. In 2021, ETS provided data connectivity services to approximately 500 users from 5 UN agencies and 11 I/NGOs across 40 sites, including e-voucher outlets, logistics and residential hubs, Severe Acute Respiratory Infection (SARI)/ Infection Treatment Centres (ITC), relay sites from where ETS data connectivity signals are retransmitted, and other UN agency, NGO, and government locations. Regular assessment, installation and maintenance activities will continue to ensure the full operability of these services. In 2022, the network will be expanded to more sites, more organizations, and more users across the three operational areas, with a goal of 70 total sites by the end of 2022.4) Conduct capacity development exercises to strengthen the expertise and skills of humanitarian staff and ensure sustainability of services. The ETS will provide training activities on security telecommunications procedures for humanitarians on a demand basis for an efficient use of the ETS security telecommunications services. Training sessions for the use of the data connectivity, and other requested capacity development programs where gaps in knowledge or skills are assessed.The ETS also plans to install back up solar power solutions at the ETS infrastructure sites for continuous provision of ETS services to ensure the full operability and sustainability of its services. In addition, the ETS will engage with the “Communication with Communities” initiative to assess and possibly support them with ICT requirements. The ETS will assess the communications needs of the affected populations to enable them to make informed decisions and will evaluate how to fulfil those gaps.
