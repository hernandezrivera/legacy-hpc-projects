Project Summary

This WASH intervention by CARE is designed to deliver a comprehensive WASH package to IDPs, returnees and host communities in South Darfur, East Darfur, Kassala, Gedaref, and South Kordofan. The overall objective of this intervention states “Decreased WASH related morbidity and mortality through provision of improved and sustained access to water supply, sanitation and hygiene promotion services among IDPs, returnees and host communities”. This objective is developed in line with the humanitarian needs overview 2022 and WASH sector priorities. The total number of beneficiaries in this intervention is 650,000 (127397 men,132600 women,198903 girls, 191100 boys).
Due to high WASH need in response to current conflict, CARE will reach out to additional 1,013,710 individuals ( 29,8031 boys, 31,0200 girls, 198,683 men, 206,797 women).

1 - Needs

Although some WASH sub-sector coverages considered acceptable and within sphere minimum standards, still sustainable service flow is a major issue not yet addressed. The motorized water sources and hand pumps are regularly broken down in a situation neither communities nor the local authorities have the required capacity to sustain the operational of the facilities. In 2022, the MSNA reported 74% of the population have access to water service but in a non-sustained manner, due to situation communities are solely reliant on humanitarian partners to handle the operations cost, ageing nonfunctional facilities, new and ongoing conflicts/displacements, inadequate institutional structures, and poor governance arrangements, all are limiting long-term service flow. Among the WASH sub-sectors, the sanitation is deteriorating with compounded effects comprised in high operation and replacement cost of latrines beside the flooding which caused massive collapse. Therefore, its estimated that only over 30% of the population have access to latrines service, and this poor access reflected in a result that 33.3% of the population practice open defecation and that made Sudan to be classified as one of the worst countries in the MENA region in relation to open defecation. Open defecation is a major health risk that make communities prone to contract sanitation related disease and increased morbidity and mortality. Hygiene coverage is remained a gap in 2022, the hand washing practice indicator reported that only 14.3% practicing hand washing with soap, in this situation the possibility on increasing hygiene related diseases is high because of poor practices. Regarding WASH in schools, 46% of schools reported children have no access to drinking water, 71% of schools lack access to handwashing facilities as well as an average of 132 students per latrine. Poor access to WASH services turned school students prone to health risks, communicable diseases which spreads in a fast manner among children who are usually enrolled in a congested classrooms and in direct contact. Since WASH and health are mutually impacting each other, in a situation of unsafe water, inadequate sanitation and poor personal and environmental health and hygiene, risks of WASH related diseases reported a significant increase mainly acute watery diarrhea (AWD), cholera, dysentery, hepatitis E, typhoid, vector-borne diseases including malaria, Dengue Fever, Rift Valley Fever, Chikungunya Fever and acute respiratory infections.With current crisis started on April 14th , WASH needs have been increased specifically in Khartoum state where the bulk water treatment and supply systems that can reach a million people are not functional. WASH cluster highlights that people in need has been increased drastically from 10.9 Million to 14.1 Million people while with strained resources and challenges due to current situation with limited access to war affected areas, banks not functional, high fuel prices, the overall target has been increased from 4.8 M to 6.05 M in hotspot areas like Kharotum, Nayala and Al Genina. The priority activities will be life saving so that people in need can be served with limited available resurces.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Water Activities: Activities in this action will be mainstreamed towards ensuring IDPs, returnees and host communities have improved access to sufficient and quality potable water effectively contributes into enhancing health status and wellbeing among target communities. The target activities will be implemented from sustaining and expansion platforms. the target activities as follows:-Water Trucking at hotspots and IDP's and Refugees camps and gathering points -Operation and maintenance of motorized and hybrid (Solar/motorized) systems.- Rehabilitation and repairing of water distribution points and systems, installation of elevated water tanks to increase water storage.- Repairs and rehabilitation (in place of new construction) of Water treatment and supply systems(plants) in urban centres like Khartoum and Nyala.-Daily and periodically water quality testing, analysis, and improvement. Water treatment at the household level by use of aquatabs and Pure ( household treatment tabs) - Repairing and rehabilitation of handpumps.Sanitation Activities: Sanitation activities designed to reduce sanitation related health risks and enabling dignified access and protection especially among vulnerable women, girls, people with chronic illness, and people with disability. CARE will replace collapsed latrines facilities and expand access through new construction of new latrines for IDPs, returnees and host communities. Referenced to the situation of increasing floods ratio and high displacements due to current conflict many latrines collapse in the operational areas, CARE is planning to adopt an improved latrine design to resist flooding and reduce latrine’s operation cost which is costive and heavy burden on communities and operational agencies. Drainage systems and flood control channels in the target camps will be improved jointly with communities who will be provided with tools and labor incentives to carry-out the work, current poor drainage systems cause stagnant water pools at camps which results in latrines collapse and form breeding sites for mosquitos and insects that compromises community health status. Community led-solid wastes campaigns will be implemented coupled with distribution of solid waste containers, tools, soap as in-kind incentive, and fuels for transportation wastes to the destination.CARE will build communal latrines and showers at newly displaced population gathering points, House hold latrines will be rehabilitated / reconstructed as per needs. CARE will focus on building emergency latrines to ensure timely provision of services. This will be build keeping in mind the separate blocks for women and men and elderly. Hygiene Promotion: Result-base hygiene promotion approach will be adopted in this intervention. Hygiene promotion messages will be designed to address the most concern morbidity cases reported through the nearby clinics. Community Hygiene Promoters (CHPs) will be trained in topics related to personal hygiene, solid waste management, vector control, WASH related disease beside Covid-19, menstrual hygiene management, and coordination with nearby clinics to collect morbidity reports and contributes into designing result-base hygiene messages. To link the awareness knowledge through messaging to practice among IDPs, returnees and host communities, CARE will distribute hygiene facilities such as soap, jerry cans, and hygiene kits including menstrual management items, vulnerable people with limited chances to access hygiene facilities such poor households led by females, people with disability, and people with chronic illness will be given the priority in these distributions.
