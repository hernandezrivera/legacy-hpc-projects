Project Summary

This project is designed to create access to basic education and protection services for vulnerable children affected by crises an emergency in Middle Shabelle Region, Hirshabelle State. The target population of this project is 2500 children (1000 girls and 1500 boys) either affected by floods due to River Shabelle breaking its banks or affected by droughts or the ongoing conflicts in the country.
The purpose of this project is to use school attendance as important factor for the prevention of child protection risks, particularly the girl-child. The project will employ interventions such as provision of teachers incentives, training of teachers on child-friendly pedagogy, training of teachers on basic child protection skills and counseling, construction of gender sensitive latrines for schools, provision of safe drinking water, the establishment Child-to-Child clubs, provision of teaching and learning materials-including recreational materials, capacity building of the Community Education Committees to ensure sustainability of schools and creation of child protection and GBV awareness sessions for women groups and men in the target villages and conducting routine hygiene promotion to raise public awareness on the prevention of Acute Watery Diarrhea/Cholera. These interventions are envisaged to prevent and protect children and women from abuse, exploitation and violence such Gender Based Violence, sexual exploitation, early marriages, child labor and recruitment of children into the armed groups.

Needs

Somalia endured civil war for nearly three decades; a conflict that ravaged basic infrastructures; school system being one of the most devastated. Around 30% of children are enrolled to primary schools, 44% girls and nearly 50 % drop out by grade five (UNICEF Annual Report, 2017). Food security is an issue, despite the rainfalls in April-June, 2018, which improved food security, yet, acute food insecurity is still prevalent; between August-December 2018 an estimated 294,200 children will likely face acute malnutrition, including 55,200 who are likely to be severely malnourished (FSNAU FEWS-NET, 2018). South Central Somalia is the most challenged; education receives minimal support from the national budget, lacks safe spaces for learning (security), has insufficient teachers (both qualified and unqualified), has limited oversight and outreach by MoE, and no teacher training institutes, etc. These gave rise to a wide variety of players offering education outside the jurisdiction and control of the government (Somalia Education Cluster, Annual Report, 2016). Middle Shabelle Region the target region for this project, has the lowest enrollment rate (19.4%) in Somalia (UNFPA, 2016). Children, especially girls, need educational development, psychosocial support, protection from risks, as parents afford school fees due to the low community resilience. According INTERSOS 2016, the main threats to girls’ education (49.5%) are financial challenges.This project targets 2500 learners (1000 girls) of whom 326 IDPs (40% girls) in Middle Shabelle, to enable them access to education. To realize that, there is a need to (1) provide TLMSs and recreational materials, 2) WASH facilities, 3) safe drinking water, and 4) school furniture for a comfortable learning environment; (5) train teachers, CECs, and CtC Mentors/child protection focal points (6) promote hygiene practices, child protection, GBV, etc.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The target children for this project have limited access to education, because of incessant insecurity and natural calamities, community resilience is low and parents unable to pay school fees; because of food insecurity parents have more pressing priority than education; feeding their children. The project aim is to solve parent’s worries (school fees) and put children in school. Once children access education, parents will have some cash to spare for the other pressing needs. It is in this juncture, the project will use sensitization, to initiate of discussions on the sustainability of schools after the end of the project. The Community Education Committees will be sensitized at first, to ensure community leadership understanding of the project purposes. Then, the community will be engaged in the discussion on how to sustain the schools. On the teacher incentive, Aid Vision will ensure the selection of quality teachers and provision of incentives for a specified period. In the meantime, the project staff will discuss with the community role on teacher incentives and how to raise the required incentives - in cash, in kind or through services done for the teacher. Dialogue on sustaining teacher incentives will be centered on the following scenarios; the community contributing one chicken per child/term or one goat/year per child; or community providing weekly cash to the teachers, - like in Madrasa; or helping the teacher with the farm activities while s/he is teaching children at school. Moreover, this project will promote girl-child education at all levels, and will ensure nearly 50% of the enrolled students are girls. The parents will be enlightened about children’s rights and child protection, and the importance of sending girls to school. Stories of well-known female role models will be cited and used to encourage parents to educate girls. The CtC Mentors will be trained as village Child Protection Focal Points, they will facilitate learners to access psychosocial support and train on child-led disaster risk reduction, that will enable children to get their voices heard at school; as a result, children will be able identify risks affecting children at school. The increased children’s knowledge on child rights and child protection will play a vital role in the prevention and reporting of child protection and GBV risks. Likewise, the CECs will double up as Community Based Child Protection Committees and sensitize the community to gain more awareness on the prevention, reporting and referral of cases of child protection and GBV. Considering the repeated outbreaks of AWD/Cholera, this project will increase people KAP, to reduce the incidence of outbreaks through regular hygiene awareness campaigns that will enable the community get the necessary hygiene and sanitation skills to prevent the periodic outbreaks AWD/Cholera

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

IDPs children specially the girls may be at risk of exclusion from access to education, likewise, children with special needs and the elderly may also be at risk of exclusion. Aid vision, through this project aims to advocate for and enforce the equal access to education and protection services, regardless of gender, social status, disability, or economic situation. The expected results from this project is inclusiveness, to achieve that, Aid Vision will negotiating with the local authority and the host community leaders ways to ensure children’s equitable access to education, especially girls. Finally, this project will secure that girl-child and women participation is visible and active from the start, that children from the IDPs and minority communities have access to education and all other school activities. Again, the project will ensure that women, youths and IDP representatives of minority groups are enabled to participate in the different committees and capacity building sessions.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The few available female teachers may be excluded from the teaching workforce1) Girls may face risk of exclusion as parents may neglect the girls-child rights to education. 2) Females may be denied of membership in community committees3) Elderly female mainly excluded from joining committees or capacity building sessions. Girls and women may be the most marginalized when it comes to education and other community services. To enforce equality, Aid vision will enforce recruitment of female teachers, and will ensure that all possible options were exhausted before a female quota is taken by a male counterpart. Furthermore, Aid Vision will ensure, that all girls are in school, to verify that, the recruited community committees, the CECs, and the CtC Clubs will be engaged to conduct house to house search for girls not attending school. Once a once a girl is found or reported to be at home the committee will embark on persuading and sensitizing the focus parent. This exercise will ensure that close to 50% of the enrolled school children are girls. To secure that gender exclusion is not an issue, Aid Vision will negotiate in advance with the local authority and the community leaders in order to respect and fill the 50% allocation of women in the Community Committee and prevent male replacing a female member.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The risk of conflict and violence at school may start from two students, flare up to parents and then to clans; thus disrupting children learning environment. To mitigate effects of conflict and violence at school, Aid Vision will train thoroughly the Community Education Committees (CECs), apart from school management and resource mobilization; the committee will be trained on conflict resolution, child protection and mediation. The CEC will have at 3 members on standby at school every day in order to mitigate conflicts. Two teachers from each school will be trained as child to child mentors; they will promote peace, provide psychosocial support to children and put down student conflict through counseling. The teacher counselors will train children on child led disaster risk reduction, (CLDRR); enabling children to identify risk and use pressure peer to mitigate student’s conflicts. Moreover, the child to child (CtC) clubs will be involved in mitigating violence, if unable, they will report to the teacher counselors to provide counseling and mediate between and conflicting students.
