Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Somalia continues to worsen with the recurrence of disasters, conflicts, and shocks in the country. The protection situation remains at the core of the humanitarian crisis with a diverse range of issues, including violations of human rights. The situation in the internally displaced persons (IDP) settlements continues to deteriorate and worsened by the protracted nature of displacement in the country. The IDP returns to their home areas of origin are extremely limited as many displaced families have lost livelihoods and are reliant on humanitarian services. Majority of IDPs settle in informal and unplanned settlements where the conditions are very poor and forced eviction is a common threat, and where newly displaced people join those whose displacement has become protracted. The settlements are often ungoverned or governed by gatekeepers, overcrowded and displaced people have limited access to protective shelter and other basic needs, with limited accountability and little adherence to minimum standards. There is also poor coordination of the camps by the village relief committees or the camp leaders; they lack the capacity to managing the population in the camp thus making some of the residents not getting better services. the vulnerability situation is even worsened particularly among women, children, the elderly, persons with disabilities, and marginalized communities.
DKH and Implementing Partners (IPs) based in Somalia target camp coordination and camp management (CCCM) in the IDP settlements in Banadir, Hiraan, Galgaduud, and Mudugud regions in the South-Central part of Somalia where the effects of the humanitarian crisis are dire. The aim sof the action is to improve the living conditions and protection of IDPs in the settlements ensuring equitable access to services and assistance by the vulnerable and poor host communities affected by the humanitarian crisis. In addition, the action will contribute to attaining durable solutions to the displacement situation. The planned action will target the most vulnerable people a affected, especially the settlements with newly arrived IDPs living in the target locations. DKH and IPs will select the target settlements based on extensive consultations with other humanitarian actors and cluster members in the areas, in order to target the most underserved areas and fill existing gaps in terms of humanitarian intervention. The CCCM activities such as the formation and strengthening capacities of Camp-Management Committees (CMC), including community feedback mechanisms (CFM), services mapping and improved coordination provision of humanitarian assistance in IDP camps, site planning, preparation, coordination and management, community sensitization/mobilization and promotion of community participation of vulnerable groups (women, girls, youth and those living with disability), and additional surveys/assessments as necessary and agreed upon with the cluster. DKH and IPs will select 55 sites, targeting 92,511 people, based on the needs and gaps reported by the cluster, and in coordination with other CCCM partners already present in the locations. The action aims for an effective and efficient coordinated humanitarian response in situations where IDP and host communities are forced to seek refuge in camp; and seeks to reduce barriers to assistance, ensure access to basic services, enhance the accountability to affected populations, mitigate risks of SGBV, fire, and flooding, through consistent engagement with the camp management committees. In addition, the project will enhance coordination with the existing authorities to negotiate for land tenure for the locations where evictions are rampant. DKH has coordinated with the CCCM Cluster in the project sites to ensure that activities address cluster identified needs and gaps. Throughout the project, DKH will document progress, challenges, and lessons learnt with other stakeholders on a regular basis.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project will use cash for work for the maintenance of the informal settlements ensuring hygiene. The strategy is to target vulnerable households to participate in this activity so that while ensuring a clean and safe living environment, they are also able to access livelihoods. Use of cash enhances decision-making and dignity of the beneficiary as they are able to make an independent choice, with the flexibility provided by cash, to purchase what they need. While cash enhances the beneficiaries' income level, it also contributes to strengthening local market functionality with the injection of the cash into the local markets.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project has considered market functionality through conducting assessments, including market and seasonality analysis. The cash will be provided based on the minimum expenditure basket, ensuring that while the beneficiaries purchasing power is increased they will be able to get access to livelihoods from the existing markets. The results of the seasonality analysis will inform the appropriate time of the year to implement the interventions, to ensure that the markets function as normal and beneficiaries are able to access goods and services from the markets. Besides, the amount of cash being injected will be little and may not have a negative effect on the prices of goods in the local market. During the implementation of the project, the team will liaise with other stakeholders to monitor the price behaviour and supplies for commodities to inform the effects the cash intervention is having on the local economy. Decisions will be made appropriately to ensure the cash is used for the right purpose, with no or little effect in the market functionality.

Please provide further explanation

The project is designed with a view to ensuring emergency lifesaving support, especially for the vulnerable internally displaced person and host communities. The project enhances coordination mechanisms within the settlements and also with external authorities to ensure that the people affected are able to access services, and further enhance stability. The project strengthens community structures, especially the community management committees (CMCs), in leadership and resource management, and these skills will contribute to enhancing the capacities of the CMCs. The project also strengthens beneficiary access to information, thereby strengthening their ability to contribute to the decision-making processes for the project delivery in the settlements.
