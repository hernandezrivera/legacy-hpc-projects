Project Summary

The economic deterioration in Lebanon is expected to worsen this year amidst continued COVID-19 lockdowns and the impacts of the Beirut port explosion. A contraction of the Lebanese GDP per capita and high inflation will undoubtedly exacerbate the crisis and lead to further loss of employment and decline in real purchasing power . Access to quality education is hindered by these circumstances, and based on a recent World Bank report, Lebanese schools were closed for more than 75 percent of the school year between January 2020 and February 2021. It is also estimated that only about half of public-school students benefited from online learning during the 2019–20 academic year (MEHE 2020) .
In the proposed project, IRC will serve 800 children with ECD programming (500 vulnerable Lebanese and 300 migrant children) and 500 children (350 vulnerable Lebanese and 150 migrant children) with retention support programming and 1,300 caregivers. ECD programs include children aged 3-5 while retention support targets school-aged children. Activities will be implemented in Bekaa both directly and through local partners. IRC proposes to provide ECD learning support and retention support incorporating social-emotional learning (SEL), as well as parental support sessions, through remote modalities.
IRC’s ECD and retention support programs conduct needs assessments, raise awareness, and receive referrals to enroll children. IRC ECD team will implement remote ECD learning support for 3–5-year-old through Preschool Healing classrooms (PSHC) integrating Ahlan Simsim (a partnership between Sesame Workshop and the IRC) that aims to address the devastating impacts of crisis and displacement by delivering early learning and nurturing care to children and caregivers. PSHC is designed to take an inclusive approach to education where all children are welcomed and supported, regardless of gender, nationality, religion, ethnic identity, or interests. PSHC supports children by providing key elements needed for healthy development: play-based learning environment, sense of control, sense of belonging, sense of pride and positive social relationships.

Education team will support the retention of children (aged 6-17) enrolled in public schools who are at a high risk of dropping out of public school. Support will focus on literacy, numeracy, second language, and social emotional skills and will be delivered primarily through IRC’s implementing partner NGO. Retention Support will meet core Education standards by using INEE and Education in Emergencies guidelines and standards.
IRC ECD team will also implement social and emotional (SEL) activities that are designed for children to acquire self-management, self-awareness, relationship skills and responsible decision making thus acquiring the knowledge, attitudes and skills that help an individual succeed in life. These skills enable individuals (children, youth and adults) to manage information without being distracted, use their working memory, control impulses and emotions, solve problems and persevere.
Caregiver support will promote children’s holistic development, early learning and wellbeing through playful parent-child interaction, improving parents’ stress management skills, positive parenting practices, and strategies for supporting young children’s physical, social-emotional, and cognitive development. IRC’s partner NGO will deliver targeted support to parents with children aged 6-17 years to help improve stress management skills, and positive parenting practices. Training topics will include the importance of education, school attendance, the role of positive parenting in children’s learning, positive communications with children, understanding and managing parents’ stress.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

SO1 Direct Community Support Measures to most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant children: IRC's Early Childhood Development (ECD) and Education (EDU) teams will conduct one needs assessment to identify out of education Lebanese and migrant children aged 3-5 years and 6 to 17 through a household survey, including dropouts from impact of socio-economic crisis. IRC team will implement one remote awareness sessions for 800 caregivers whose children aged 3-5 years and 500 caregivers who children aged 6-17 years and will refer 300 families to other services. Based on this needs assessment, IRC will outreach vulnerable children aged 3-5 years and 6 to 17 along with their caregivers to receive the education services and awareness sessions. To measure the improvement of the children’s performance, IRC will use three tools (IDELA, MEHE assessment and caregiver survey) throughout the implementation. Social and emotional learning activities will be shared during remote implementation twice a week, where children receive resources with social and emotional learning components.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

The IRC ensures that all programming is gender sensitive and integrates protection and social inclusion. All staff are informed of their responsibility to comply with the IRC code of conduct and Child Safeguarding Policy.IRC takes a zero-tolerance approach to safeguarding violations committed by its staff, consultants, contractors, volunteers, interns, suppliers, and vendors, and commits to taking action to prevent and respond to safeguarding violations suspected or reported. IRC’s education and ECD programs utilize approaches that ensure boys’ and girls’ have equal access to safe, nurturing spaces and ensures that male and female caregivers have equal opportunities to take part in the services. All program staff receive sensitization training on Gender Based Violence Core Concepts, Identification and Safe Referrals, Anti-Sexual Harassment (ASH) code of conduct, Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and safe reporting and referral lines.
