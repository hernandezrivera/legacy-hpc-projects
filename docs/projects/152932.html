Project Summary

Concern’s SNFI response will provide time-critical and life-saving assistance to populations facing greatest needs due to displacement through i) improved access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and life-saving non-food items to newly displaced or vulnerable populations in secondary displacement and ii) improved living conditions of highly vulnerable IDPs in protracted displacement, returnees and host communities unable to meet their S/NFI needs. The response will be targeted based on population needs and context. Concern has been providing SNFI assistance to conflict affected populations since the conflict began in December 2013 in Juba and Bentiu PoCs. This response will continue to support to populations in Juba and Bentiu PoCs with repair and replacement materials, construction of shelters in case of influx, as the main SNFI partner and with strong relations with the CHCs allowing for collaboration in planning and designing shelters. Concern was one of the first organisations to respond outside of Bentiu as part of BBR in 2016. As part of the BBR, Concern SNFI team will continue providing assistance to hard to reach and underserved populations in Rubkona, Guit and Koch Counties through mobile response. Also, IDPs have resettled in Bentiu and Rubkona towns due to congestion in Bentiu PoC and from more insecure locations in Unity. As Bentiu and Rubkona towns were destroyed during the conflict, rehousing opportunities remain low and SNFI needs are high. Concern’s market assessments in Bentiu and Rubkona have found that suppliers can meet some demands for shelter materials but as the markets are still recovering other materials will be provided in-kind. Concern piloted CBIs in Bentiu and Rubkona towns and will continue this approach in 2019. Concern will also implement a SNFI response in Juba to support IDPs resettling from Juba PoC and in Yei, with a combination of in-kind material and cash-based intervention, building on Concern’s experience in BBR to quickly set up SNFI operations in new locations. Concern aims to focus more on cash-based interventions in locations were security is more stable with functioning and accessible markets. This will give beneficiaries more flexibility in meeting needs and also contribute to market recovery. Concern mobile teams based in Juba and Bentiu hubs will be implementing the mobile responses. Concern will consult with beneficiaries to prior to distributions to ensure that shelter and NFI materials are as per social and cultural norms.

The targeted beneficiaries will be 14,092 households (47,988 individuals, 47,388 women and girls, 42,065 men and boys) in Bentiu PoC; 13,257 households (32,133 individuals15,540 women and girls, 16,573 men and boys) in need of reinforcement of shelters and new arrivals. 17,500 newly displaced and protracted IDPs in Juba and Yei Counties in Central Equatoria and Rubkona, Guit and Koch Counties in Unity will be targeted for access to shelter and NFIs. To ensure adherence to Do No Harm and to reduce community tensions, the most vulnerable host community or returnee populations will also be selected as vulnerability is not exclusive to IDPs. Beneficiaries will be selected based on strict criteria identified with communities to ensure needs are properly identified. The most vulnerable will be prioritised including PLW, households with the largest number of under 5 children, child-headed households, disabled, elderly, widows, orphans, and vulnerable female-headed households.
Concern as the Unity State SNFI Cluster focal point and Juba PoC site focal point is in a position coordinate with other partners including sharing assessment information, identifying gaps in Unity and Central Equatoria to ensure coverage of needs as well as coordinating with the national SNFI Cluster. Despite a revitalised peace agreement, security remains unpredictable along with displacement, thus coordination with partners is essential to improve efficiency of responses.

Needs

By mid-2018, on-going conflict has resulted in 7 million people in need. Despite a recent revitalised peace agreement, 7.1 million people will have humanitarian needs in South Sudan due to conflict since 2013. Around 1.96 million people in South Sudan are internally displaced, with over 203,000 IDPs in PoCs and around 2.47 million refugees in neighbouring countries (OCHA South Sudan Humanitarian Dashboard 30 September 2018). While the intensity of conflict may have reduced, there continues to be localised clashes, particularly in Unity and southern Central Equatoria. In Unity, the humanitarian and security situation remains unpredictable and fragile with communities experiencing violence and large scale displacement in the first half of 2018 in central/southern Unity. Unity is one of the worst conflict-affected areas and currently hosts the highest number of IDPs in the country with 116,417 individuals. By May, Rubkona and Bentiu Town had 1,765 IDPs living in collective centres (source: CCCM). REACH conducted assessments in May 2018 highlighting the need to S/NFI assistance in Rubkona and Guit but due to insecurities in Koch, they could not conduct an assessment. Their findings were: • Rubkona: 19% of settlements assessed showed IDPs living in the open• Rubkona: 25% of settlements assessed found damaged shelters due to conflict, the 6th highest record in South Sudan during this period.• Guit: 20% of settlements showed that the majority of shelters were damaged by conflict, the 3rd highest in South Sudan during this period. Concern conducted a needs assessment in February 2018 in Juba PoC and found that plastic sheeting was identified as the most important NFI need and is linked to the rainy season and the need for protection and privacy followed by sleeping mats as most families slept on the ground. Cooking sets and water containers was a third priority given that most items were burned and looted and families were unable to carry items as they fled. Following intercommunal violence in Juba PoC 3 in August 2018, 3,500 households were relocated to land allocated by the government and Concern responded to shelter needs. Although it is likely despite the recent peace agreement that IDPs will remain in PoCs due to the unstable security situation, there may be further relocations of IDPs to around Juba due to congestion in the PoCs or further tensions. Concern will maintain its response capacity in Juba urban areas with mobile shelter teams. Concern conducted an assessment in Yei town in May 2018 focusing on security, logistics and humanitarian needs to collect information for establishing a humanitarian response in the town and surrounding areas. The assessment involved meeting local authorities, national and international NGOs, local leaders. It was identified that most of the organisations implementing programmes are development focused with limited emergency response capacity in high insecurity context. Yei town is more stable than surrounding areas and there has been an influx of IDPs from more insecure areas into the town as well as returnees from neighbouring countries of Uganda and DRC. Due to local authorities being keen to avoid camps, IDPs have been settling among host communities placing a strain on host community households who are mostly poor and extreme poor socio-economic groups. While tensions seem not to be a problem during Concern’s assessment, it was felt that larger influxes of IDPs and/or returnees could risk increasing tensions. It is estimated that 2,000 IDPs have recently arrived into Yei town. And 2018 saw continuous movements of IDPs in that area. Yei being still a hotspot of the conflict with fragmented groups, this trend and related needs is likely to continue in 2019.

Activities

The project is in line with the SNFI Cluster objectives to Improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and life-saving NFIs to newly displaced or vulnerable populations in secondary displacement and the living conditions of highly vulnerable IDPs in protracted displacement, returnees and host communities unable to meet their SNFI needs. The activities planned as per of out-of-camp settings in Bentiu, Rubkona, Juba and Yei are feeding into the cluster objective of supporting the most vulnerable returnees, host communities and IDPs in secondary displacement rebuild lives through coordinated, integrated and targeted interventions. The following activities will be implemented:Provide access to emergency shelter and life-saving NFIs (in-kind or cash modality): i) through a combination in-kind and cash-based interventions to implement in Bentiu and Yei towns; ii) voucher exchange for shelter materials in Bentiu PoC and Rubkona County. Shelter materials will be procured locally where possible. NFIs will be identified based on assessment of most acute needs in coordination with other sector needs (WASH, health, etc). Progress will be tracked based on the number of households receiving shelter assistance and/or life-saving NFIs per modality (in kind or cash) and the timeline of assistance delivery (objective of delivering the assistance within 23 days of the first alert). Concern will utilise its experience to strengthen NNGOs in Yei for SNFI responses. Based on Concern’s partner assessment, training needs will be identified. Concern’s Finance and Systems staff will focus on improving procurement and financial procedures to improve efficiency. Concern’s Juba SNFI staff will support technical aspects including planning of settlements, shelter design and monitoring.Maintain or upgrade shelters for IDPs inside PoCs: Concern will continue to respond to the emergency shelter needs of vulnerable IDPs in the 2 PoCs as main shelter partner. Assistance will be targeted based on needs assessment to support the most vulnerable IDPs improve their living conditions and ensure privacy and dignity. Shelter assistance in the PoC will be implemented using the existing CRM of each PoC and conducting needs assessment for each complaint received. Based on needs assessment results, distribution of shelter materials will be conducted and the most vulnerable beneficiaries will receive additional workforce support to rehabilitate their shelters when they are unable to do so themselves. Engage community that stimulates active participation: Concern will conduct assessments with support from local and displaced community leaders. Concern will involve female and male and specific groups such as PSNs in needs assessments. The results of the needs assessment will be compiled and explained to targeted communities, providing details on targeting, the CRM. A CRM will be implemented in all stages through the PoC system or through Concern’s own CRM outside of PoCs.Monitoring of quality: Depending on security, PDMs will be conducted 2-4 weeks after distributions or in the event of insecurity rapid response monitoring will be done the day of distributions. PDM tools will include questions on the satisfaction levels of beneficiaries about the assistance received. All data will be collected and analysed using gender disaggregation. Results will be communicated to the SNFI Cluster and partners in the areas.Mainstreaming of HLP and conflict sensitivity: Concern will use the standard cluster tools which include HLP concerns. Concern receives quarterly visits from a humanitarian advisor working specifically on conflict sensitivity. The advisor’s support include review of all programmes to ensure conflict sensitivity is considered at all stages. Concern is a partner with CSRF who visited programmes to conduct a review of Concern’s programmes through a conflict sensitivity lens. Mainstream GBV: See more details in GBV mainstreaming in the protection sections.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

During needs assessments, Concern will consult with POC community committees, local authorities and community leaders to prioritise locations. Information on actual needs from communities will be collected to ensure that shelter and NFI materials provided correspond to needs as well as appropriate to social and cultural norms. Before the beginning of the project Concern will provide sensitisations explaining details of the programme, community involvement in the targeting process, what assistance beneficiaries are entitled to and the CRM. Following beneficiary selection, beneficiaries will be consulted on design of shelters and NFI kits to ensure materials are appropriate to the needs. Before distributions, Concern will conduct sensitisations through community messaging again to reinforce understanding on beneficiary entitlements, CRM and explain the distribution process. PDMs are also as a way for beneficiaries to provide feedback so the project can be adjusted. Information on distribution days will be on a “need to know” basis to minimise information circulation and thus protection risks.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Information is context specific as beneficiary entitlements will be different in each targeted location. Shelter and NFI assistance inside the PoCs is focused on repair and reinforcing shelter structures as well as replacing damaged NFIs. Outside the PoCs, focus will be on entitlements for newly displaced and resettling IDPs where assistance is to support a more permanent shelter and recovering from displacement. Information will also be on the voucher fair process. Given security risks, information will be on a “need to know” basis to minimise information circulating such as on distribution dates or value of vouchers provided.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

There are considerable protection risks in the project locations including GBV and protection needs to be considered at all stages of the programme cycle. For the mobile responses based on displacement patterns, prior to assessments, security will be assessed and information triangulated to make decisions on whether a response will put beneficiaries at risk or not. Concern’s staff, have been trained by Concern’s Protection Advisor earlier in 2018. All Concern staff members and stakeholders must sign Concern’s Code of Conduct, Programme Participant Protection Policy (P4), Child Safeguarding Policy and Anti-Trafficking in Persons Policy which are mandatory for all those involved in Concern’s programme including staff, partners, suppliers, contractors and traders. Included in these policies is mandatory reporting of abuse or exploitation of beneficiaries. To minimise risks, beneficiaries prior to distributions will be informed of risk reduction measures that they can take such as travelling in mixed groups and during daylight hours. Distribution sites will be identified in safe locations such as church grounds or health centres in consultation with communities who have strong local knowledge of the safest locations. Considering women and children are often responsible to collect shelter materials in wilderness areas, they are at a greater risk of violence; provision of shelter materials at safe distribution sites will reduce protection risks. Helpdesks during distribution days and the CRM will also be used to identify protection cases and subsequent referrals will be made to Protection partners such as Protection Partners such as DRC, UNHCR, the Red Cross movement or MSF in Unity and Terre des Hommes, UNHCR or Voice for Change in Juba and Yei.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Concern’s evaluation of its recently completed multi-sectorial programme consulted directly with beneficiaries of the shelter interventions and PDMs of the pilot CBI collected satisfaction levels of the voucher fair to inform design of this programme. Given the security situation in the programme locations, Concern considers Do No Harm in the design for this project. Concern in April and October 2018 conducted a conflict sensitivity assessment of it programmes as part of the Conflict Sensitivity Resource Facility programme. This included consultations with communities to understand the conflict dynamics and ensure the programme design will not impact negatively on the conflict. These include participation in programme design to understand conflict dynamics and take appropriate risk reduction measures; and ensuring clear understanding of the selection process of beneficiaries and entitlements to assistance to minimise misinterpretations and thus communal tensions.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

There are considerable protection risks the programme locations and protection will be considered at all stages of the programme cycle. South Sudan ranks 187/189 countries in the 2017 HDI and the country loses 36.3% when the value is discounted for inequality (HDI reduced from 0.388 to 0.247 ). This figure reflects a strong patriarchal society and culture in South Sudan; and traditional gender roles subject women to play secondary roles in the community. This place them at a disadvantage with household decision-making and this disempowerment exposes women and girls to several risks including early/forced marriage and sexual and domestic violence. While the laws in South Sudan provide for the protection of women and girls against discrimination, abuse and GBV, they are often misinterpreted or ignored. In 2017, the Protection Cluster reported 3,585 GBV cases (95% female, 19% children); an increase of 35% compared to 2016. This is in particular in locations affected by conflict. In Juba PoC, 34% of women had been exposed to intimate partner violence with 70% of these not seeking formal help, and 33% of women being survivors of sexual violence (No Safe Place, 2017. In PoCs, overcrowding, often up to 3 families living in the same shelter, can lead to increased tension and violence along with lack of privacy for women and children increasing the risk of GBV. Concern will consider privacy in shelter materials provision and will include metal sheeting to beneficiaries as required. During displacements, families often get separated especially female members who often flee to avoid sexual exploitation and male members to avoid recruitment into armed groups. Thus there are often large numbers of female headed households where women have to take additional roles and their needs will need to be considered such as whether as per social norms they can access land for shelter constructions.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Despite the revitalised peace agreement in August 2018, recent trends show high risks of insecurity in the programme locations such as in Unity and Yei. This can place considerable risks of beneficiaries accessing shelter distributions. Following an alert of displacement, Concern will assess security in the locations prior to assessment. Concern will also consult communities to identify distribution sites in safe locations such as churches and health facilities that reduce travel times for beneficiaries. Distributions will also be placed in central location as close as possible to the targeted beneficiaries to reduce risks with travel. The rainy season also impairs greatly access in the rural and hard to reach locations of Unity. Concern may request support from logistics cluster and access the location by air when roads are impassable. Women as per social and cultural norms have limited decision-making roles as decisions are made by men. This includes women’s rights to property as they cannot own the property or land they work and can impact on where they can relocate to in event of displacement or resettling. Disabled have limited means to access shelter and NFI materials as they lack mobility and means for shelter construction. In addition, women with disability are excluded from decision-making processes, 48% of women with disabilities reported never having access to decision-making process through community participation and engagement mechanisms (IOM, 2018). Concern will ensure that specific needs of vulnerable groups are identified by including representatives of those groups in the discussions held around the project implementation. Specific FGDs will be organised to ensure freedom to speak and raise concerns by those vulnerability groups.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Concern will assess security and information triangulated to make decisions whether a response would put beneficiaries at risk or not implementing the Do No Harm principle. Concern’s staff are responsible to implement Concern’s Code of Conduct, Programme Participant Protection Policy (P4), Child Safeguarding Policy and Anti-trafficking in Persons Policy. Included is mandatory reporting of exploitation or abuse of beneficiaries. Project design and approach will be reviewed by Concern Equality Adviser based in HQ. During assessments, specific needs of women, men, girls and boys as well as PSNs will be considered to ensure composition of shelter and NFI kits meets all needs. During distributions of vouchers and kits, households will nominate one member to receive assistance, usually a senior female as this can encourage joint decision making. To reduce protection risks, beneficiaries will be sensitised on measures they can take such as travelling in mixed groups and in daylight hours. Distribution sites will be in safe locations such as church grounds or health facilities and the mobile nature of the response will allow for the sites to be located reducing travel times. Helpdesks on distribution days and the CRM will be used to identify protection cases and make referrals to Protection partners. PDMs will also collect information on protection risks and the information will be used to inform future distributions. Concern will coordinate with Protection partners during targeting to ensure specific groups such as PSNs are selected. Concern emphasises mainstreaming protection and recognises that households with unique needs require special assistance in shelter construction. Equal access to assistance will be prioritised during distributions, communities will be informed that people with mobility or other PSNs will be served first and assisted by casual labourers for shelter construction.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

2-4 weeks following distributions, PDMs will collect gender disaggregated data to measure impacts on women, men, girls and boys as well as specific data on the impacts of PSNs. Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

PDMs will be used to collect information on protection and security from beneficiaries. In addition, FGDs with separate female and male groups will allow for women and men to share information on any protection or security risks they encountered during the programme. This information will inform planning of future programmes and will be shared with the SNFI and Protection Clusters. Concern is harmonising its tools with the SNFI Cluster’s PDM tool which cover collecting information on protection and safety of beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Concern will collect during its PDMs satisfaction levels on the programme from beneficiaries which will include questions on beneficiaries’ perceptions on how the assistance impacted on their households’ dignity. Separate FGDs with women and men will allow them to safely express their opinions on how the assistance positively or negatively impacted on their dignity. Concern will also harmonise its tools with the SNFI Cluster’s one which cover questions on dignity.
