Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to improve food security, economic and social well-being of vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host families in the target areas.
NRC Cameroon’s Livelihood and Food Security (LFS) programme will target displacement-affected people based on vulnerability criteria and not their displacement status (refugees, IDPs, returnees (former IDPs, and host communities). NRC will apply social and economic vulnerability criteria to identify vulnerable households most at risk to food insecurity. The social vulnerability criteria applied will include female-headed households, child-headed households; households, pregnant and breastfeeding women; elderly people (60 and above); households with a person with physical or mental disability or debilitating chronic disease. For the economic vulnerability criteria, NRC will use as a reference: low income households under the poverty line (28 000/months), poor or limited food consumption score; no food stock, number of meals per day, no productive assets. For agricultural restoration activities, NRC will also include the following criteria: having at least one able-bodied person in the household who can farm land. NRC’s LFS programmes will also target as far as possible vulnerable youth and women for vocational training and economic empowerment.
To address the intermediate needs of affected populations NRC will use cash, vouchers or in-kind assistance based on the results of market analysis and cash feasibility studies. To maximise the chances of success in this activity, NRC will as far as possible couple livelihoods interventions with food assistance to meet basic food needs. Moreover, NRC will implement complementary activities such as training and awareness raising sessions to support target households. Programme activities will gradually scale down and phase out so that by the end of the projects, communities have the skills and resources needed to be more resilient to shocks and crises in the medium and long term. NRC will work in coordination with local partners such as local technical and administrative structures and will collaborate closely with them so they can provide technical support during programme implementation and continued follow-up after the programme ends.
The LFS programme will include cash assistance, livelihoods promotion and enhancement applying a market-based approach (MBA) that refers to local traders so to improve access to income and revive/strengthen the local economy. The intervention will be geared towards supporting the most vulnerable displacement-affected households and at the same time building their capacity in food production, food transformation and marketing.

1 - Besoins / Needs

People forced to flee because of the crisis have often lost their productive assets (seeds stocks, agricultural tools, livestock) thus hindering their capacity to restart their main livelihood activity. Moreover, they have also lost access or have limited access to farmlands due to enduring insecurity. Some productive assets may have been sold as part of coping mechanisms, thus further decreasing their capacity to meet their basic needs. According to an assessment conducted by NRC, less than 30% of respondents have sufficient income to cover their basic needs. The data also shows also more almost three out of four households spend more than 80% of their income on food expenses. Conflict negatively affects livelihoods in several localities of the departments of Logone-et-Chari, Mayo-Sava and Mayo-Tsanaga. Agricultural production has been affected by the following shocks: insecurity, flooding late rains and drought, plant diseases and higher prices for agricultural inputs due to COVID-19. In the region, livestock activities were also affected by the crisis with a significant number of livestock being stolen or killed. Animal production also suffers from drought and unusual diseases and climatic variation. Some epizootics have resurfaced because of the interruption of vaccination and animal disease prevention activities, due to the insecurity created by Boko Haram. Furthermore, the combined insecurity situation and the structural poverty jeopardise the take-off of local economic initiatives. In Logone-et-Chari and Mayo Tsanaga divisions, the acquisition of food in markets represents a great challenge for those who lost their livelihood during displacement. Food SecurityTo respond to the immediate needs NRC will provide immediate food assistance to save lives and reduce suffering of households unable to meet basic food needs (Phase 3 and above). Three types of activities will be implemented;- Food assistance through cash/vouchers (for people recently affected by the crisis)- Awareness raising on food hygiene, food diversity, food storage and food preparation - Sensitisation on COVID-19 prevention measuresNRC will ensure the immediate food needs of people recently affected by the crisis, and other vulnerable groups in the same geographical locations, via the provision of food assistance through cash-based transfers/vouchers or inkind based on market analysis. NRC will choose the modality based on the security and local context, protection risks, market analysis, and needs assessment. To ensure the safety of staff and beneficiaries, all standard security SOPs will be followed. Livelihoods / Early Recovery NRC’s LFS programme will focus on restoring and/or promoting livelihoods activities for people affected by the crisis. The main livelihoods options in the targeted area will be agriculture, livestock production, aquaculture, income generating activities (IGA) and entrepreneurship. As part of this component, NRC will take advantage of its memorandum of understanding signed at the regional level with FAO to provide a joint response to vulnerable people by drawing on the expertise of each of the two organizations in the Far North in order to increase the impact for vulnerable people.To maximise the chances of success in this activity, NRC will as far as possible couple livelihoods interventions with food assistance to meet basic food needs.
