Project Summary

Purpose: Provide safe and clean water, adequate sanitation facilities; ensure access to quality primary health care facilities; preventive and curative nutritional interventions and income earning opportunities.
The project will work in an integrated manner to address the WASH, health, nutrition and FSL needs of the affected community to save lives and reduce unnecessary human suffering through reduced morbidity and mortality and life free from violence.
CIS with its national NGO partners and sectoral ministries will support disaster affected and vulnerable people in the targeted areas of East Darfur, South Darfur, Kassala, South and West Kordofan. The aim of integrated sectors is to contribute to the immediate life-saving needs for adequate and safe services and reduction of morbidity and mortality among IDPs, returnees and affected host communities of women, girls, men and boys. This will be done through improving water and sanitation services, linking to health and nutrition facilities for synergistic impact through mobilizing communities for individual and common efforts.
WASH activities will mainly focus ensure adequate and safe water supply, provision of acceptable sanitation facilities through rehabilitation and construction of facilities as decided by the community and sector partners. The WASH activities will be integrated to nutritional prevention and curative actions and community based health response mechanisms. Health activities will include delivery of basic health packages of primary health care services, medical supplies, expand and maintain disease surveillance, reproductive health and emergency obstetrics. Nutritional interventions will include the full package of CMAM and IYCF services in 20 CMAM sites for provision of CMAM and SAM services. FSL interventions will include support to targeted families that experience malnutrition, food insecurity and have special needs and also capability to engage in income earning opportunities to enable families’ resilience. Agricultural and livestock based and off farm income generating activities are planned by the project.

Needs

As per the CARE’s multi-sectoral needs assessment, November/December 2018, availability of safe water, access to nutritional and health facilities still remains a challenge requiring huge attention in East and South Darfur and Kassla states. In IDPs camps and affected communities of East and South Darfur states are getting assistance through CARE and are currently receiving 15 l/p/d, in line with SPHERE standards. However there is a need to sustain the provision of these services through further construction, rehabilitation and maintenance of facilities. In the new targeted areas, such a Shataya the average is less than 10 l/p/d and comes from unprotected sources. FGD participants in Hashaba Tamad location reported lack of latrines. Hygiene practices were lacking – most people are not using soap. South and West Kordofans share similar risks and vulnerability while humanitarian response level in West Kordofan is very low.The same multi-sectoral needs assessment has indicated that adequate water supply is one of the biggest challenges for IDPs and affected host communities including Kassala as not receiving safe and sustained access to water. In refugee camps and host communities, FGD participants reported that the water is pumped only in the morning and in the evening, which limits their access to adequate supply. 50% of the host community members still remain without latrines and similar needs exist for South Sudan Refugees. An interagency assessment conducted by UNHCR in 2017 found alarming rates of refugee children out of school (almost 99%). Access to potable water remains critical in Kassla, especially in Dabat Aisha and neighboring villages. The only source of water here is Atbara River, which is far from the village (2 km) and does not have a filtration system. The per capita water consumption is estimated at 4 l/p/d – far below the SPHERE standard. The health and nutritional needs also remain serious with deteriorating economic situation and limited assistance. There has been a dramatic deterioration in the economic situation in Sudan in the past year, with a rate of inflation at 68%. The on-going fuel crisis and bread shortage have compounded the humanitarian needs of the already vulnerable populations. HHs are unable to cover their basic needs due to increased food prices as well as other basic commodities. The deteriorating macroeconomic conditions, continue to erode household purchasing power in Sudan. The impact on household’s heath and nutritional condition exacerbated the already weak situation of the affected population in the targeted states. The continued support need for preventive and curative nutritional response for affected population remain high including treatment of CMAM and SAM for PLW and children under five. Furthermore, health facilities remain in dire need of continued support through primary health care facilities, diseases treatment and prevention, SRH and other risks. Some of SS Refugees living out camps.

Activities

WASH activities include water supply facility construction/rehabilitation/maintenance chlorination, water distribution point construction/rehabilitation/maintenance, latrine & shower construction/rehabilitation; hygiene promotion and awareness campaigns (open defecation/AWD); personal hygiene kits (women's menstrual hygiene); building community capacity through training of Water User Committees (WUCs) and other structures, water voucher system implementation in the camps (no free water' approach); promote community-based approaches to O&M. CBI is limited to cash payment for latrine construction, maintenance and related activities carried by community groups. CARE plans to carry market survey. Health interventions include deliver basic package of primary health care services (disease treatment and prevention); supply health facilities with medical supplies and equipment (provision of essential and live saving medicines) and supplies (medicine for chronic illness); expand and maintain disease EWARS; Support Reproductive Health services including Emergency Obstetric, new born care; Support routine/outreach EPI, Nutrition interventions will include supporting stabilization centers services in East Darfur (Eddaein SC), South Darfur (Kass SC), and Kassala (Kassala SC). Provision of CMAM services to treat sever acute malnutrition to children under five, provision and Pregnant, Lactating women. Interventions under FSL will include vet services, seeds distribution, training in improved ag and animal health, self-employment and entrepreneurship skills. The health and Nutrition facilities targeted will be located nearby to communities, with gender segregated latrines; project staffs and community volunteers will be trained in Genders. CIS will ensure proper feedback mechanism at all Project areas. CIS project activities will be implemented in close Coordination with all relevant stockholders, SMoH, WES, MoAG, INGOs, NNGOs and UN Agencies.

Impact on environment/natural resources

CARE recognizes that water and sanitation, health and nutrition response programs and livelihoods diversification interventions do have impact on the health of the public, environment and natural resources. Interventions to preserve and protect the environment will be included from the design phase, implementation and monitoring of each program intervention. The mitigation measures could include but not limited to: analysis of the potential impact of key interventions and discussion with community leaders and sector experts; regular joint water and sanitation facilities monitoring and supervision; emphasis on environmental education and capacity building for increased environmental awareness among beneficiaries and implementing partners; solid and liquid waste management; actions to avoid water pollution due to excavation, backfilling and concreating, hauling and damping of earth materials; controlling formation of stagnant water pool to avoid soil erosion and breeding mosquitoes. Health and nutrition facilities measures include cleaning campaigns, quality and monitored incinerators, and refresher training to health professionals, WASH experts and community volunteers. Youth, women, men and others will be organized into water users committees, hygiene promoters, natural resource management groups, health committees, nutrition volunteers, and VSLAs where appropriate and tailored training will be provided to contribute positively to the management of natural resources and peaceful co-existence. Environmental impact assessment will be carried out if interventions require to do so and will be decided with sector ministries.
