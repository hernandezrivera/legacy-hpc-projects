Project Summary

It is estimated that around 3,500 women and adolescent girls will be reached with a comprehensive package of GBV services (including case management, focused PSS and legal assistance). Among those 800 will also receive emergency cash assistance. 9500 will benefit of community outreach initiatives, non-focused PSS activities, including life skills sessions.

UNFPA will focus on the following interventions:
- GBV Service Delivery
1) Support access to survivor centered multi-sectoral services for women and girls at risk and survivors of GBV through Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS). UNFPA will continue to support the operations of existing women and girls’ safe spaces. . The key objectives of a safe space are to provide an area where women and girls can: socialize and re-build their social networks; receive social support; acquire contextually relevant skills; access safe and non-stigmatizing multi-sectorial GBV response services (psychosocial, legal, medical); receive information on issues relating to women’s rights, health, and services)
2) Provide psychosocial support to survivors of GBV and individuals at risk including psychological counselling, case management, focused and non-focused group psychosocial support
3) Provide legal counselling and representation for survivors of GBV (including survivors of domestic violence and survivor of sexual harassment and exploitation)
4) Provide technical support to existing target safe shelters to meet minimum standards for optimal care of survivors in needs of safe shelter options. Shelters for survivors of violence are an essential component of a comprehensive and coordinated response and are globally recognized as a useful option for women escaping violence. This is because shelters provide survivors with the opportunity to distance themselves from abusive relationships and consider their choices for a life free from violence
5) Provide emergency protection Cash Assistance to survivors of GBV and individuals at risk of violence and imminent harm. GBV Emergency Cash Assistance (ECA) aims at addressing a situation that exposes an individual to immediate serious GBV harm /risk and/or addressed its consequences. The GBV Emergency cash assistance’s goal is to address the consequence of GBV (such as treatment for injuries) and/or to mitigate the risk of gender-based the violence to occur (e.g. to flee an abusive situation). It can be used to access services that are not provided free of charge. The provision of ECA is a one-time intervention intended to address the critical needs of a GBV survivors. The Recurrent Cash Assistance (RCA) aims at ceasing or mitigating risk of gender-based violence, including to sexual harassment, exploitation, or abuse on the longer term. It might include support to pay for safe accommodations for survivors leaving abusive situations.
- Outreach and GBV Risk mitigation:
1) Engage community members in safety audits exercises to identify GBV risks and mitigation measures and disseminate the GBV referral pathways. Promote the peer-to-peer approach
2) Organize non-focused psychosocial support activities tailored to the different needs of vulnerable and marginalized groups.
3) Promote employability and access to labor market for women and girls by supporting life skills and financial training opportunities for women and girls. These initiatives are designed to help women and girls generate income while contributing to the mitigation of GBV risks by providing women and girls with information on SRH and GBV services and support along the training process.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Activities will be tailored to the specific needs of women and girls of different age, nationalities, cultural background, sexual and gender orientation. Needs of women and girls with disabilities will factored into the project to extend the access to services to all the segments of the vulnerable target, marginalized and discriminated. Safety audits and community consultations will be regularly held to identify risks within targeted communities and to engage women, girls, men and boys in identifying plans to respond to the identified risks. Key messages about what constitutes PSEA and how to report allegations will be mainstreamed in all the outreach initiatives and community-based activities. Monitoring activities will include a section on protection risks and PSEA that will aim at maintaining a close relation with communities supported and regular collect their feedback.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

UNFPA operates according to the Five Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations (Leadership and governance, Transparency, Feedback and complaints, Participation, Design, monitoring and evaluation). Women and girls are key actors in their own protection and it is critical that they are active partners in identifying protection risks and solutions. UNFPA will facilitate their active involvement in the design, implementation and monitoring of this action by consulting with women and girls on GBV risks and constraints to their participation in and access to aid delivery, GBV and SRH services. UNFPA will also work with women and girls to identify those who are most marginalized and at high risk and design approaches to ensure their participation. UNFPA will engage with women, girls, boys, and men from the affected communities to explain protection activities, facilitate community acceptance and address barriers to women's and girls' attendance.
