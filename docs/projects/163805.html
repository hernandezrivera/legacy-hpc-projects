Project Summary

The IRC will provide an immediate response to vulnerable people in need of protection services – including Internally Displaced People, refugees, migrants, returnees, and vulnerable non-displaced Libyans, with a specific focus on women and girls, children and people with mental health-related issues in urban areas and in detention. Building on IRC’s successful integrated model, the IRC will use its Reproductive Health teams as an entry point to identify and create trust with women and girls, thus creating a space of trust for disclosure of potential GBV incidents in Tripoli, Misrata (and surroudings), Ghat and Ubari. IRC will do so in PHCs, women and girls' safe spaces, or through local CSOs.
With the MHPSS component that bridges from Health to Protection the IRC will ensure the continuity of service provision across all sectors, with specialized GBV and Case Management capacity, in addition to General Practitioners trained on mhGap, and clinical psychiatrists available in multiple PHCs.
Similarly, the availability of pediatricians in the teams will be an excellent entry point for child-protection activities. IRC teams will have child protection and facilitators who will carry out recreational and PSS activities with children while their family members are in the PHCs, while visiting local schools and communities, or in children safe spaces.
In addition to the integrated approach with Health, the Protection teams will also maintain a stand-alone capacity by working with local CSOs, local authorities (MOSA, and schools) and in children and women safe spaces that will target exclusively children and women and girls respectively.
Mobile Teams activities will be complemented by robust community outreach activities to raise awareness on availability of services. This strong link between the community and the public facilities allows for better follow up of cases, case management, and referrals.
The teams activities will include protection led GBV and psychosocial support services, creating a service package of case management, mental health and psychosocial support services to survivors of SGBV. Additionally, SGBV case workers will be trained alongside the health staff on clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS) and mental health gap action plan (mhGAP) to promote comprehensive care for survivors.
The IRC will work to strengthen its existing referral systems with local and international actors to maximize the number of people who receive appropriate next levels of protection care.
The IRC will provide technical trainings and assistance to local actors, including local CSOs, public stakeholders, community leaders and community volunteers to effectively respond to emerging needs and improve the urban and community based protection space. Working with targeted communities of migrants, the IRC will support the development of CBP action plans, and fund identify actions to strengthen community based resilience.

1 - Needs

The 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview estimates 897,000 people in Libya are considered to be in need of humanitarian assistance, especially Internally Displaced People (219,000), refugees (48,000), migrants (299,000), returnees (74,000), and vulnerable non-displaced Libyans (256,000). With 34% of these people being women, 30% children, and 15% people with disability, it is paramount that any humanitarian intervention is designed with a people-centred approach, that builds on the protection mainstreaming principle. Indeed, the Humanitarian Response Plan anticipates 448,848 individuals are in urgent need of protection-specific assistance with Tripoli, Misrata and Sirt mantikas showing a severity score of 4, and Ubari and Ghat a severity score of 3. Migrants and Refugees in detention and urban areas:Data from the Libya Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) reports that 63% of migrants in Libya come from Sub-Saharan Africa and 29% from North Africa. The latest Mixed Migration Centre report shows the number of migrants and refugees in Libya (Jul-Sep 2019) increased compared to previous quarter, most of whom (20%) are estimated to be in urban Tripoli, and 4,000 in detention, including over 3,000 in conflict-affected areas around Tripoli. Migrants are at risk of kidnapping, exploitation, forced recruitment, arbitrary arrest, detention, sexual abuse, and rape in conditions described as “abysmal”, “unacceptable” and “unimaginable” by UN and civil society observers. The current conflict has exacerbated these vulnerabilities. People from Sub-Saharan countries are most at risk of detention and ill treatment as anti-black racism, which is endemic in Libya. Findings of SARA 2017 are particularly alarming when they confirm that from 15 to 42% of health facilities are either partially operational or closed, in addition to facing a critical lack of medicines and staffing shortages, particularly for reproductive and mental health. These shortages are affecting more than proportionally migrants and refugees, who indeed report the lack of access to free of charge primary health services as being the biggest challenge they face in Libya. Women and Girls:Numerous reports on protection and gender profile in Libya show that GBV is rampant, mostly against women and girls and in particular in geographical areas run by militias. Although women and girls are not the only population at risk to GBV they are a particularly vulnerable group in the ongoing conflict in Libya, especially those in situation of displacement, detention and women heads of household.A recent UNFPA survey indicates that 87% of female respondents need permission and at times an escort from a male family member, spouse or parents to access health services. Other data suggests women don’t access health services out of fear of stigma, gossip, or lack of confidentiality. An estimated 70% of respondents were not aware of any community based organization providing services in their community. Lack of specialised and quality services for GBV survivors along with social norms condoning violence against women discourage reporting of GBV accidents. Children: Children have been among the most negatively impacted by the ongoing conflict. The psycho-social impact on children has been devastating and is further aggravated by the death of relatives or friends, the continuous exposure to violence and by their experience of forced displacement. Children have been the victims of human trafficking; forced recruitment by armed groups, particularly in the southern region; abduction and torture by armed groups and in detention facilities; and of collateral damage from airstrikes and other targeted attacks. The presence and control of militia within communities in which children live increase their risk of association with armed groups. Cases of violence against children, if reported, face limited capacity for professional follow up.

2 - Activities

CBP Approach: The IRC will apply its comprehensive community profiling methodology, including protection risk analysis, stakeholder analysis and a do no harm assessments, to expand its successfully established CBP approach to additional communities in Tripoli and new communities in Misrata and Ubari. The IRC will use KIIs and FGDs to assess and assess protection risks using community action plan funds. These will promote safety measures in each community to improve the sense of well-being and empowerment for the most vulnerable. The IRC will also train community members, including community leaders and mobilisers and local CSOs to use feedback boxes and direct communication with IRC staff to strengthen the Accountability systems. GBV and CP Response: IRC will ensure availability of GBV and CP specialized services both in a stand-alone capacity through the identification of women and children safe spaces, and in an integrated fashion with the medical teams in primary health facilities. Case management will be conducted in the PHC or in the existing safe space located in the premised of Libyan CSOs or facilities made available by MOSA local branches. In 2020 the IRC has secured funding to recruit an Inter-agency Case Management specialist to train INGOs and local CSOs on GBV Case Management standards. GBV and CP awareness and capacity building: The IRC will organize awareness sessions on GBV and CP for adolescent girls,boys, teachers and parents in Schools and CSOs venues. The awareness sessions will be conducted both with CSOs and public stakeholders identified through service mapping exercises in Tripoli and Misrata. Protection Mainstreaming: In order to provide safe, dignified and meaningful access to services in Tripoli, Misrata and Ubari, the IRC will develop a protection mainstreaming risk assessment which will enable the development of a protection mainstreaming action plan. The IRC will use a version of the Global Cluster Mainstreaming training modules.

4 - Protection risk analysis

Access and availability of services and staff: In 2019 the rapidly evolving conflict, exacerbated by increasing numbers of migrants and internally displaced people have led to mounting needs, exacerbated by attacks on healthcare responders. As of December 2019, 75 people had been killed and 49 had been injured in 60 attacks. The continued fighting has also restricted the movements of humanitarian workers and people’s access to service provision. Gender Based Violence (GBV): The protection crisis in Libya is characterized by violations of international humanitarian and human rights law, restricted access to safety and freedom of movement, forced displacement, and the severity and pervasiveness of gender-based violence. Due to the stigma and the fear of the retaliation, GBV is widely underreported in Libya and it is believed that the vast majority of GBV survivors do not receive adequate assistance. Underreporting of GBV is also associated to the lack of trust in the formal judicial system, weak reporting structures, lack of specialized staff, collapsed infrastructure including health facilities, and the fragile security situation for legal justice. This has immensely contributed to very little data collected on GBV to inform humanitarian actors of the magnitude of the problem.Mental Health and Psychosocial Support: The number of people with mental health and psychosocial problems increased substantially as a result of the conflict, including severe psychological distress for the conflict affected population coupled with the contamination from explosive hazards affecting human security and access. Due to the volatile security and access limitations, the majority of international humanitarian actors continue their involvement in Libya with remote management with national and local actors as well as key official stakeholders providing services.

5 - Mitigation measures

Access and availability of services and staff: The IRC will provide comprehensive integrated health and protection services through mobile teams, local CSOs, and referrals to local and national authorities, and INGOs. The IRC will work with the MoH and partners on technical and operational capacity development, including HR processes, to increase skills and create a pool of qualified applicants who can fill gaps in the project. Gender Based Violence (GBV): The IRC Mobile Teams will offer embedded and integrated Clinical Care for Sexual Assault Survivor (CCSAS) services through GBV case workers. Survivors will receive clinical care and PSS support through trained health and protection staff. In addition, both health and protection staff will be trained on CCSAS multi-media training tools. Health facilities will be supplied with necessary supplies and equipment, including post-rape kits, as per IRC global GBV facility checklist to ensure services provided with dignity, confidentiality and privacy. The IRC will use the service mapping produced by the protection sector to refer and receive GBV cases to/from other actors.Mental Health and Psychosocial Support: The IRC will recruit a full-time psychologist and part-time psychiatrist as part of the Mobile Teams to provide MHPSS. General Practitioners will receive mhGAP training to provide a wide range of MH treatments together with the psychologist. The Psychiatrist will provide supervision to the trained GP and the psychologist in addition to consulting on complicated MH cases. All health facilities will be provided by psychotropic medications. The IRC will establish referral pathway between health and protection to refer cases requiring specialized PSS

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

The IRC maintains a conflict-sensitive approach and provides services across statuses, including refugees, migrants, displaced Libyans, and returnees. In 2020, the IRC will focus its intervention on remote, hard-to-reach populations. The IRC’s 2020 strategy also prioritizes a needs-based, client-informed humanitarian response. This approach includes soliciting feedback from clients, and adapting projects to meet client-identified and evidence-based needs. At the onset of the Action, the IRC will conduct a series of KIIs and FGD to gather primary data about needs and access constraints for the general population and for acutely vulnerable groups such as women, people with disabilities, migrants, and ethnic minorities. IRC will conduct client exit interviews with beneficiaries, solicit feedback on services and medical teams’ behaviors, and create an open forum for additional feedback. IRC will also continue to use the feedback/complaint boxes, and feedback forms. Feedback will be analyzed and course-corrective measures taken. All new staff involved will receive training on confidentiality and information protection. Facility safety audit assessments will be conducted, and interviews and focus group discussions held with women and girls to identify and mitigate access barriers.
