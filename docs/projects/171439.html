Project Summary

The COVID-19 spread implications affected all life aspects in oPt, particularly education, which overwhelmed the education system with extra burden in addition to challenges facing education in oPt over the last decades. In Gaza escalations and blockade is continuing for 13 years, increasing poverty and unemployment rates, lack of resources and protection concerns. In WB and Jerusalem, checkpoints and the Barrier impede access to education for children living in the “Seam Zone” and in East Jerusalem communities isolated by the Barrier. In addition, recent years witnessed an increased number of Israeli attacks on schools located in Area C and Hebron H2. All of these factors put children at huge risk of losing access to education with increased possibilities of drop-out and be left behind for the marginalized groups of children mainly CWDs and children living under poor conditions. Therefore, SCI will focus on supporting Safe Back to School actions by supporting 10,000 vulnerable and underserved children and 450 teachers through supporting their continued access to quality education, psychosocial support, improved emergency and adaptive infrastructure in schools, and support MoE and UNRWA teachers on remote learning aspects. Furthermore, it will support conflict-affected students with disability with assistive devices to ensure their equal access to inclusive quality learning opportunities, in addition to supporting 300 parents on self-care, stress release and supporting children in times if emergencies.

1 - Needs

According to SC “Danger is our reality report 2020”, more than 40% of children reported that Israeli military or settler violence affects their ability to learn. Parents reported being constantly concerned for their children’s mental wellbeing that might lead parents to restrain from sending the child to school. According to the Education Cluster, education-related incidents increasing by almost 60% between 2018 and 2019. The total number of loss school time due to firing tear gas on schools and/or students is around 143 classes, including cases in which students lost full school days after evacuating the school premises due to heavy tear gas firing. The double and triple shift system applied by UNRWA schools (70%) and Ministry of Education schools (63%) further limits the time available for studying to four hours a day and impedes remedial education, co- and extracurricular activities. The deterioration in the humanitarian situation in Gaza has pushed more families into poverty, making basic education supplies and transportation costs unaffordable for many and increasing the risk of school dropouts. SC’s HH Assessment on Out of School Children reported higher rates of functional limitations than in-school children, signifying a need for access modifications to reach the most vulnerable, especially those currently excluded from education. The average age of CWD dropping out of school is 11.85-directly linked to stigmas, poorly equipped schools, a lack of specialized materials, and a lack of economic means to support transportation. Schools across opt suffer from poor learning environments. Per ECW Baseline report 2020, 52% of schools have no internet connection and 23% have no playgrounds at all, and around 150 schools serving around 30,000 children lack adequate WASH facilities in Gaza and West Bank. UNDP East Jerusalem schools survey 2020 revealed that almost 59% of schools in East Jerusalem have no playgrounds, libraries or science labs. Around 40% of schools in East Jerusalem lack the adequate number of functioning drinking water fountains. Children with disabilities, including children injured during the Great March of Return demonstrations, are the most affected by the deteriorating situation. According to UNICEF, an estimated 30% of 10-15 year olds with disabilities are excluded from school in oPt. Self-care and vision (9.51% AND 7.69%) were seen as statistically significant functional limitations for children. UNDP East Jerusalem schools survey 2020 indicated that schools can also be risky for students with other disabilities, such as those with visual impairments. Close to 35% of the assessed vulnerable schools need additional toilets, and 45 % are in need for adaptations for CWDs according to ECW Baseline report 2020. Following the full closure in March 2020, following COVID-19, MoE has prepared response plans, and created different strategies to support the continuity of education. With the move from classroom to virtual teaching, and now back to the blended approach, where half of the surveyed teachers have stated that they felt “exploited” and 68% of teachers reported that they spend more time preparing for the lessons and materials. 44% stated that they have to self-learn and do their own research with limited support. Families faced double challenges related to the unavailability of devices, poor internet connectivity and power cuts. Children with learning difficulties and children with disabilities suffered as well. Just over 70% of households in Gaza have at least one child not accessing any distance education programme as per the The impact of COVID-19 on education in Palestine, Lebanon and Jordan: Reflections from teachers, students and parents” Study by the centre of Lebanese studies. NRC rapid assessment on COVID revealed that 62 % of these households in Gaza and 13% in West Bank have reported no internet access or availability of the necessary devices to follow online distance classes available at home. Almost half (46%) of the households have reported that their children were feeling anxious or stressed about the situation. SCI, in partnership with DCI and with the support of the child-led groups conducted a survey during COVID 19 where 50% indicated their need for guidance to relieve boredom and stress, during this period, 41% of children affirmed that psychological and material damage affected all groups.

2 - Activities

The project will center around four main components in direct support of the strategic and cluster objectives of the 2021 HRP. All actions are designed to ensure Safe Back to School interventions for the most vulnerable children. The project will target 15 schools (7 boy, 8 girl schools) located along the ARAs and pre-identified and used as shelters and/or UNRWA schools that will be closed down as a result of downsizing will be targeted. 15 schools in WB Area C will be chosen and in reference with ECW baseline report that has identified the most vulnerable schools. It will target a total of 10,000 children (5000 girls and 5000 boys) whose learning outcomes are affected by high level of distress and low level of motivation resulting from the violence, and poor infrastructure overcrowding in schools and COVID implications. The intervention will also include training 450 teachers on different issues related to supporting children on remote learning, conduct home learning assessment on children learning outcome, Psychological first aid, and sessions to support teachers’ wellbeing and self-care. 300 Parents will also be supported on how to deal with children during emergencies, and stress release activities. 150 students (80girls, 70 boys) with sensorial and physical disabilities will receive assistive devices to attend schools. School adaptation will be implemented to support CWDs. Schools in need will also be supported with improved emergency rehabilitation to support children’s learning environment, especially in light of COVID 19 increased protection risks.Referral pathways, for identified/disclosed protection concerned to service providers (health, case management, mental health/pss, security/legal etc), will ensure that protection needs identified for specific children are addressed by relevant service providers. This will ensure a protection-sensitive approach to project and service delivery. Teachers/counsellors will support on referral. Girls and boys will have equal access to project activities and gender-specific needs and risks will be paid special attention to. All this aims to provide tailored support to vulnerable children by addressing their distinct barriers of being at risk of drop out. Safe Back to School responsive activities will include:1) Provision of remedial education and learning support to vulnerable school aged children, either remote or face to face. Remedial activities will include • Provision of remedial education to 850 vulnerable school aged children. In addition to Social Emotional Learning classes (SEL). As suggested by global research, these skills set, essential for children’s development, are directly linked to improved learning outcomes, reducing toxic stress and mitigating interpersonal violence in classrooms. In support of children’s well-being.• Teachers will be trained on Child Safeguarding, Social Emotional Learning (SEL) approach and Inclusive Education. The whole process of teachers’ selection will ensure that identified teachers are properly equipped to support the learning needs of targeted children including CwDs. In addition, teachers will be supported on remote learning processes such as home based assessments to measure learning outcomes. Teachers will provided with needed materials and academic training with focus on either English, Arabic, math, or science. Remedial Support Classes will be supplemented by SC’s SEL sessions for children. The SEL focuses on critical social emotional skill development in the areas of determination, positive communication, responsible decision making, self-awareness, self-regulation, and social awareness. Action will also conduct back to school activities including recreational, extracurricular, fun days and trips. 2) Provision of essential and basic emergency rehabilitation to schools to ensure that school environments are appropriate and safe for students and teachers. School staff and children will map out key needs, this will be followed with specialized engineering assessment, inter-sectorial coordination between education, protection and infrastructure units will be carried out to highlight main interventions priorities within the intervention scope, with involvement of children and school staff to agree upon final interventions to improve school environment. 3) Support school-aged children with disabilities with devices and adaptations to ensure their continued access to education. The will also include assistive devices 150 children with disabilities or multiple disabilities to facilitate their retention and transitions into formal school or specialized, certified service providers. Targeted schools will be offered adaptation services to ensure inclusiveness and ability of CWDs to have protective and supportive learning environment. Beside the normal procedures of conducting the detailed engineering assessment, CWDs will be consulted and actively involved of the assessment and design of the planned adaptation to properly address and respond to their needs. 4) Provision of school-based psychosocial support to school staff, students and parents affected by the education-related violations and COVID-19 pandemic either Face to Face or remotely• The Action will also conduct back to school activities including recreational, extracurricular, fun days and trips• Teacher’s wellbeing sessions on self-care, PFA and stress release• Parents sessions on dealing with children during emergencies and self-care. SC with identified partners and stakeholders will conduct a rapid assessment which will focus on identifying the most vulnerable boys and girls particularly those at risk of school dropout due to education and protection vulnerability. Community locations with high numbers of children exposed to protection risks will be identified through a vulnerability matrix. SC will cooperate with UNRWA, CBOs, and CPCs to identify the most vulnerable children in these areas.

The protection from sexual exploitation and abuse of children is dealt with under our Child Safeguarding policy. This policy is specifically concerned with the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) of adults. This includes direct or indirect beneficiaries of our programming and adults in the wider communities in which we work. For anyone under 18, refer to the Child Safeguarding Policy. For issues in relation to staff refer to the Global Anti - Harassment policy which covers sexual harassment, SEA and bullying of SCI staff. The PSEA policy contains essential principles and rules that reflect the behaviours and standards expected from employees, trustees, volunteers, interns, trainees, consultants, partners, and other representatives working for, or contracted on behalf of SCI in any capacity and covers our international development and humanitarian work.SCI is committed to reducing the opportunities for abuse and exploitation to happen through effective prevention, ensuring that survivors are not exposed to further harm through good reporting and responses, and ensuring that survivors receive appropriate care. Effective prevention and providing safe responses requires all staff, volunteers, interns, partners and other representatives to be clear about their individual accountabilities and responsibilities. It also requires all senior staff and managers to ensure that their working environments minimise the opportunities for sexual exploitation and abuse, and to ensure that reporting and response do not expose survivors or reporters to any further harm. Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) is a gross breach of both standards of aid, and of the rights of those in need23. The PSEA policy applies 24 hours a day, 7 days a week and 365 days a year. Any breach of the policy will be taken extremely seriously and responded to. Breaches of the policy may be grounds for termination of engagement with SCI. SCI takes the position that early reporting of any concern supports both prevention of further abuse and safe response through the hotline available 24/7 (1800-724-726). There must be increased awareness about reporting, including the importance of reporting of any and all concerns, however apparently minor. SCI’s commitment to Zero Tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse means all programme design and planning must include a gender power analysis, which also looks at the roles and structure of SCI staff, volunteers, interns and partners and a safeguarding risk assessment SC has a PSEA Focal Point at the country level and the field sites, who is responsible for driving awareness of the policy and on how to report, inviting, receiving and collating all causes for concern. When these collected pieces of information generate a picture of a site, or a programme, or a particular person, the PSEA Focal Point must escalate the matter to trigger further inquiry and/ or investigation.
