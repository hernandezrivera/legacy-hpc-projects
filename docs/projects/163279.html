Project Summary

Based on the need analysis of the food security and nutrition needs identified by WFP during the EFSA, stunting rates were high at 36%(NW) and 28%(SW). Rates of exclusive breastfeeding practices were 42.5% and 58.3% in NW and SW regions respectively. Nutrition data collected through EFSA 2019 indicate low proxy GAM of 4.4 % in NW and 5.6 % in SW. SHUMAS intervention will be designed to meet the emergency nutritional need of children 6-23months and PLW through micronutrient supplementation and ready to use therapeutic food. The main objective of this project is to protect the nutritional status of children and women in the North west region of Cameroon.
And the specific objectives and activities are;
 Screening, referrals and treatment of un- complicated severe acute malnutrition (SAM) of children 6-23months and PLW.
 Capacity building of SHUMAS staff and community health actors on Screening, Severe and Acute Malnutrition (SAM) management, IYCF-E and breast milk monitors and screening.
 Screening and referral of acute malnutrition of children 6-59months and PLW
 Sensitization of IYCF-E and monitoring of breast milk substitutes
 Provide complimentary package of services (Vitamin A, deworming medicines) in children 6-59months and PLW
 Blanket supplementary feeding program through provision of super cereals in children 6-23months and PLW
 Participate in cluster coordination mechanism as a Member of SAG for nutrition cluster
We will target 5 health districts (Nkambe, Jakiri, Ndu, Wum, Bambalang and Benakuma). 286 Community actors (166 women, 120 CHW) will be recruited, and beneficiaries will be 19,298 children less than 2 years, 25,000 children aged 6-59 months, 16,081 PLW and 50,000 men and women sensitized on infant and young child feeding practices in emergencies. We are working with WFP on BSFP in Donga Mantung.

Besoins / Needs

Based on the need analysis of the food security and nutrition needs identified by WFP during the EFSA, stunting rates were high at 36%(NW) and 28%(SW). Rates of exclusive breastfeeding practices were 42.5% and 58.3% in NW and SW regions respectively. Nutrition data collected through EFSA 2019 indicate low proxy GAM of 4.4 % in NW and 5.6 % in SW. SHUMAS intervention will be designed to meet the emergency nutritional need of children 6-23months and PLW through micronutrient supplementation and ready to use therapeutic food. It is likely that the situation already has a serious impact on nutritional status of the most vulnerable groups, mainly children under five and women.Historically NW and SW regions had low levels of GAM – 1.5% in NW and 2.2% in SW (MICS 2014). The prevalence of underweight was also reported to be low at 4.7% in NW and 5.9% in SW. However, access a major issue à possibility of higher nutritional vulnerability in hard-to reach areas Mobile Vulnerability Analysis and Mapping (mVAM) results reveal that 18.2% and 6.9% of children 6-23 months in the NW and SW regions respectively met the minimum acceptable diet (MAD). About 30% of the women surveyed are achieving minimum dietary diversity (MDD-W) - 23 % in NW and 32% in SW. MDD-W findings show that majority of the women have low micronutrient intakes.Low technical capacity of local implementing partners to implement nutrition interventions both at facility and community level. A number of areas with high vulnerabilities could be left out. Lack of a comprehensive picture of the nutrition needs leading to less prioritization of the nutrition response – Low funding. There is urgent need to protect the nutritional status of children and women in the two regions to prevent excess morbidity and mortality.
