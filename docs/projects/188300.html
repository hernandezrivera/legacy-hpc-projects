Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to improve sanitary and hygienic conditions of internally displaced persons (IDPs) in northwest Syria, and consequently reduce their risks to Covid-19 infection. Hygiene kits will be distributed to approximately 8760 IDPs. Water trucking and hygiene promotion will be also provided. The targeted area will be overcrowded camps in Idleb Governorate in northwest Syria. Fund is unsecured.

4 - Needs

An estimate of 3.3 million people in northwest Syria live at high risk of COVID-19 infections; yet, due to the vulnerabilities of their natures, IDPs are considered to be exposed to further high risk of COVID-19. In northwest Syria, 2.8 million people; up to 65% of total population is IDPs. Among them, 1.5 million people are still forced to live in over1,380 camps. IDPs living in camps faced more vulnerabilities due to various restrictions resulting from COVID-19. Moreover, high density and shared lives in camps make their infection risks higher. In 2021, insufficient funds make sectoral efforts to keep WASH systems at a minimal level of functionality very challenging, for instance only around 50% of targeted people have been reached in 2021 through light rehabilitations of water systems. A further decline of funds could lead to further widespread breakdown of WASH systems functionality. Considering the on-going COVID-19 pandemic and deepening economic crisis it would have dramatic public health and protection-related consequences. The highest needs exist in Dana and Ma’arrat Tamsrin Sub-districts as they have more than 57% of the total number of camps and IDPs in NW Syria. More than 0.7 million people requires hygiene kits assistance. For these reasons, AAR Japan aims to improve hygiene situation and prevent COVID-19 outbreak through distribution of hygiene kits, water trucking, and hygiene promotion in IDP camps in Dana Sub-district and Ma’arrat Tamsrin Sub-districts.

5 - Implementation of activities

1st, Area selection: in coordination with the WASH Cluster, CCCM, and local councils, AAR Japan, with HIH, shall collect information on IDP camps in Dana and Ma’arrat Tamsrin Subdistricts; and HIH will conduct assessments of potential target areas. Based on the results, target areas will be selected considering hygiene needs and gaps. To avoid overlaps, AAR Japan shall coordinate with the relevant clusters and organizations. 2nd, verification: AAR Japan shall prepare the beneficiaries' list based on the information from local councils and camp managers. HIH shall verify the list via house-to-house visits. 3rd, procurement: the content of hygiene kit is determined based on Sphere Standards, WASH Cluster recommendations. They will be procured in Turkey and shipped to NW Syria. 4th, distribution: HIH shall deliver the packages door-to-door. Each household shall receive hygiene kit(s) once sufficient to serve its family members for 30 days. Hygiene promotion leaflets will also be distributed and staff will share messages to improve beneficiaries’ hygiene awareness and practices regarding covid-19. 5th, PDM will be approx. 20 days after distribution. Also water trucking will take place in the same camps. Vendors will be contracted. They will provide water on daily basis to the selected camps. Quantity of water will be according to WASH Cluster recommendation. AAR Japan shall create a list of samples for the survey considering location, family size, and gender.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

AAR Japan and its partner uphold the 9 commitments of the CHS throughout the project, putting the Code of Conduct in practice and fulfilling its essential responsibilities as humanitarian organizations. To avoid conflict with communities due to misinformation of the services, pre-coordination with local councils shall take place to provide detailed information of the services to the beneficiaries, to ensure that beneficiaries understand their rights. Paying particular attention to women, children, PWDs, and other vulnerable groups, measures such as allocating a female staff in the field team will be taken to facilitate communication with communities and people affected, and to ensure that their feedbacks are properly reflected. Measures will be in place to ensure beneficiaries have safe access to handle their complaints and requests. Leaflets specifying the contact point for complaints will also be handed to each beneficiary receiving the service. All complaints will be addressed in a confidential and appropriate manner.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AAR has a zero-tolerance policy towards SEAH (Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Sexual Harassment) and a responsibility to ensure that all people are treated with dignity and respect. Every staff member working for AAR and our partner organizations must always uphold the highest standards of behavior and accountability with everyone we encounter at all times. AAR ensures that all AAR staff and associates are aware of the high standards of behavior and adopt exemplary behavior to create, strengthen, and maintain healthy environments and minimize the risks of SEAH. This includes, but is not limited to, promoting awareness among AAR staff and associates and people in the communities, conducting safe staff recruitment, building safe projects and programs and ensuring good practice when using media. In case complaints and tip-offs regarding sexual abuse or exploitations are received, the Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Officer shall intervene and respond to the case; an investigation team, under the direct chain of command of the Chief Executive Officer, shall report to the PSEA committee, and verify the facts on the case. HIH MEAL team shall report misconducts in the field in accordance with policies on preventing such acts.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

As a precondition for the project to be implemented, HIH staff members must be able to safely operate in the selected target areas; however, these risks are assumed: deterioration in security situation, intervention by armed groups, closure of border between Turkey and Syria, restriction of humanitarian activities within Syria by Turkish or Syrian governments, and the outbreak of COVID-19. Depending on the situation in the areas of operations, the project will be suspended until safe operations for HIH staff members can be ensured. In case no signs of recovery in security situation or improvement through coordination with relevant actors can be seen, AAR Japan shall consider changing access routes and target areas, or ceasing the project. Security assessment will take place before any field activity to check the security situation in the planned area of operation. To mitigate the risk of the spread of covid-19 in NW Syria (93050.000 As of 23 January 2022), both AAR Japan and HIH have adopted their own policies to prevent Covid-19 infections among the staff members and beneficiaries. All staff members are required to abide by polices and restrictions imposed by the organizations and the local authorities, wear masks and gloves, wash hands on regular basis, measure body temperature, and take other precautious measures as necessary. Field activities shall also incorporate safety guidance developed by the relevant clusters.
