Project Summary

This project is aimed at addressing acute water shortage and sanitation and hygiene needs among disaster affected communities in Somalia through the provision of access to safe water and improved sanitation and hygiene practice. Access to safe water will be delivered by rehabilitation of strategic boreholes and equipping them with elevated water tanks, distribution systems, water kiosks and where possible hybrid energy systems. Access to sanitation will be provided by rehabilitation/construction of improved shared household sanitation facilities with hand washing stations. WASH services will be delivered using a strong protection lens including ensuring latrines are gender sensitive, provided with lockable doors. Distance to water point will be reduced to SHERE standards. Women and girls will be consulted on the appropriate locations and designs of water facilities and latrines. Water facilities will also be designed to ensure equal access by all gender and appropriate to physically impaired persons.
Provision of Emergency WASH services to address emergencies will include water trucking, provision of household water treatment/purification tablets, desludging of pit latrines, hygiene promotion and provision of appropriate temporary water storage facilities.

Needs

About 2.9M people need urgent WASH assistance in Somalia in 2019. Unreliable access to a temporary water source and unprotected water is an important feature of the WASH humanitarian landscape in Somalia. Nationwide, access to an improved water source remains below 40% and water prices remain high in most parts of the country. Assessment by CARE revealed increased water prices by about 49%-from $0.2 per twenty (20) liters to $0.5 in Godobjiran (Eyl district) from Dec 2017-Feb 2018, posing additional economic stress. In Mudug, the cost was approximately US$3-4 for a 200-litre barrel; too high especially for IDP and other vulnerable HHs in both the main towns and rural areas. In Nugaal region, the price of water increased by an average of 47% from Jan- Feb 2018. This means communities have to search for water from distant areas and this is a challenges to vulnerable groups such as disabled persons, elderly, or young children. Rapid WASH Assessment of May 2018 in Galmudug State indicated that up to 3,005 HHs in the region have no sustainable access to safe water. In addition, a joint needs assessment in Oct 2017 revealed inadequate hygiene practices. Only 24%, 11%, and 10% of surveyed HHs in Nugal, Mudug, and Galgadud regions treat their drinking water; only 17% in Dhusamareb wash their hands after defecation. Over 77,000 cholera cases and over 1,590 deaths were reported across the country. Needs assessment conducted in Sanaag, Sool, and Togdheer by CARE revealed that about 80% of households assessed did not wash hands with soap at critical times and were not taking regular baths. Acute water shortages exposes vulnerable women, girls, boys and men to consumption of unsafe water. Poor hygiene knowledge and practices put them at risk of contracting water-borne diseases including cholera/AWD. Lack of latrines, exposes women and girls to protection risks. Open defecation notably undertaken after dark, exposes them to Gender Based Violence

Activities

1. Rehabilitation of strategic water points. The project will rehabilitate strategic water points in consultation with the different sectors of the affected communities. 2. Establish and train gender inclusive Water management committees and equip them with tools and materials to operate and maintain water supply systems, for local ownership and sustainable management of the facilities. 3. Rehabilitation/construction of shared latrines for the affected communities in the target locations. This will be done through a consultative process that will involve women, men, girls and boys in the selection of locations and designs of all facilities so as to conform to their culture and preferences. Separate and lockable (from the inside) latrines will be constructed for males and females indicated by pictograms. 4. Establish and train gender inclusive hygiene mobilizers to improve hygiene practices among target communities. All hygiene promotion activities will target both men and women to ensure equal access and will be conducted at household level in local vernacular language. 5. Purchase/distribute water collection/storage containers (water bladders and portable water storage tanks).This will be placed at placed strategic points where large number of affected communities with no water storage facilities will congregate. 6. Purchase and provision of appropriate water treatment and purification tablets to affected households in consultation with the women and men so as to appeal to their test and preferences. 7. Emergency water trucking will be done as the last resort for the hardest hit communities and institutions that are facing acute water shortages. This will be done in close collaboration and consultation with regional WASH cluster Coordinators. 8. Purchase /distribution of hygiene kits and NFIs to disaster affected households. 9. WASH in Schools-Provision of emergency water, sanitation services and hygiene promotion activities to schools affected by disasters

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed target locations are sufficiently safe and stable. CARE has a risk management strategy which identified potential risks in target areas. This strategy has an elaborate appropriate risk management matrix and mitigation measures to minimize the risks. Regular reviews are done to ensure the risk matrix remains relevant to the changing dynamics. The project is linked to the government priorities as CARE has strong and cordial relationships with the government and local community leaders. CARE coordinates and collaborates its interventions with key government agencies both at federal and State levels. The program teams collaborate and involve local community leaders in identification of target villages based on needs identified through CARE’s assessment and FSNAU reports. Gender-inclusive village committees support CARE in identification, selection and verification of beneficiaries using agreed-upon criteria. The project will build on CARE’s experience and cordial relations at grassroots level across Somalia. The proposed interventions, are expected to meet the immediate humanitarian needs of the affected population. These will also be link up with other ongoing activities in the development projects aimed at reducing risks and vulnerabilities and building resilience to future shocks. This will promote complementary/synergy and cushion beneficiaries from slipping back to crisis. In order to increase disaster risk reduction strategies, CARE will ensure timely, coordinated and high quality assistance to communities in target areas where disaster losses pose a threat to people’s life and livelihoods. This coordination will involve building capacities local state agencies in disaster responses in providing early warning systems of alert before the onset of droughts, floods, cyclone etc. The project has inbuilt sustainability as it will establish and train gender inclusive Water management committees and equip them with tools and materials to operate and maintain water supply systems. The rehabilitated water of points will be handed over to Water Management Committees (WMCs) for local ownership and sustainability. All WMCs members will be trained on operation and maintenance and equipped with basic tools. The respective ministry of water in each state will also be involved in the handover of the water facilities to the WMCs and they will sign a tripartite agreement (CARE, Ministry of water and WMCs) to ensure continuity after the project. CARE will facilitate accountability to communities people affected by crisis, and improve the quality of services provided by monitoring quality of output using the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) as a framework. Conformity to CHS will be verified and internal processes assessed to establish if they meet the actions and organizational responsibilities set out within the CHS.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The main risks facing the targeted population based on displacement include scenarios where displaced and vulnerable groups are excluded from the resources including humanitarian aid by the host communities. This can be mitigated by the following;1. Having an agreed set of beneficiaries selection criteria that looks at vulnerabilities of the target population.2. Establishing gender inclusive relief committees that are inclusive of both displaced and host communities.3. Inducting the relief committees on the need of transparency, neutrality and impartiality of humanitarian Aid.4. verification of identified beneficiaries at public forum (community gathering) prior to registration.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The main risks facing the targeted population based on gender and age are as follows;1. exclusion women and girls from accessing water2. exclusion women and girls from accessing sanitation facilities either by location of the facility or by design of the facility. Sanitation facilities far away from the homestead or in poorly lit areas cannot be properly accessed by women. latrines with that are not lockable from inside are also not secure for women and girls thus not well suited for themMitigation measures include;1. Consult all gender and age groups when selecting the site for water and Sanitation facilities.2. Locate water and sanitation facilities in sites that are close proximity to the homestead.3. Locate latrines in properly lit areas and with good view from all sites. 4. Male and female should have separate latrines and they should be lockable from inside.5. Hand washing stations should be gender and age appropriate

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

There are high potential risks of armed conflict resulting from resource based clan conflict. This will be mitigated by using a "do no harm approach" where CARE will ensure the intervention do no trigger conflict among the target populations. Also, there is high risk of armed conflict between the state government and militia. CARE has a way of monitoring the security situation in the target location through security field teams who are well placed to advice the organization and project staff on any impending risk. The organization has a functional risk matrix with appropriate mitigations measures.
