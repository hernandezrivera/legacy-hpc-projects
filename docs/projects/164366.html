Project Summary

Support the Resilience of Syrian Women Farmers in Aleppo by Distributing Inputs:
The project design is based on the following:
Increasing the food security and enhancing the livelihoods of IDPs and Host Communities (65% IDPs, 35 % host communities) in Aleppo governorat (Jarablus District) as following:
1. (400 HHs) in (Jarablus, Tal Elamara, Zoghra, Lower Um Rotha, Jamel, Upper Um Rotha, Lower Jarablus, Marma Elhajar) will receive vouchers of (vegetables seeds, vegetables fertilizers, vegetables pesticides , irrigation fuel, irrigation lines , and costs of tools )
To enable them to cultivate 2000 dunum vegetables as total .
2. (100 HHs) in Ghandorah will receive vouchers of (vegetables seeds, vegetables fertilizers, vegetables pesticides, irrigation fuel, irrigation lines , and costs of tools )To enable them to cultivate 500 dunum vegetables as total.
WSA needs assessments have identified significant need for both IDPs and host families who have lost productive crop agriculture due to ongoing conflict or displacement, and as a result have reduced purchasing power and incomes that hinder food security. It is critical to support these households to begin to restore lost crop agriculture livelihoods, thus enabling them to have increased food availability through personal production, as well as a small income for increased food access.
Key interventions will include:
1- Provision vouchers to buy agricultural inputs, accompanied by related extension services (use of fertilizers+ Irrigation + Harvest).
2- Capacity Building targeting women farmers and local community members.
3- Follow-up the project during the production cycle of the crops.
WSA is planning to implement the project through providing (4 vegetables seeds vouchers, 3 types of fertilizer vouchers, 7 types of pesticide vouchers, fuel vouchers, tools vouchers), to:
WSA is planning to implement the project through providing (4 vegetables seeds vouchers, 3 types of fertilizer vouchers, 7 types of pesticide vouchers, 1 fuel vouchers,1 lines vouchers, 1 tools vouchers), to:
1. 400 HHs in Jarablus and surrounding villages (1085 female and 915 male), approximately (375 men, 525 women, 560 girls, and 540 boys) (the women and men farmers and their children).
2. 100 HHs in Ghandorah (283 female and 217 male), approximately (82 men, 142 women, 141 girls, and 135 boys). (the women and men farmers and their children).
WSA will identify, verify and profile target beneficiaries through community leader, Volunteer and local council (where the long list will be prepared). Then the short list will be identified by the criteria of selecting beneficiaries, considering vulnerable people, including; IDPs, hosting families, and female headed HHs as priority.
The draft distribution of vegetable seeds, fertilizers, pesticides and irrigation lines will be early of March 2020 to prepare agriculture early April. In addition, the project will conduct surveillance after planting and the provision of technical advice and periodic follow-up necessary by WSA an agronomist
WSA will hold awareness sessions with the traders and all beneficiaries to explain the voucher redeeming mechanisms and to sensitize them to the list of authorized agricultural inputs.
Throughout the duration of the project a market monitoring system will be put in place in all targeted areas. To ensure that the project does not disrupt or affect the markets systems and balance.
WSA monitors will collect information for the market monitoring during spot checks monitoring visits at the local shops involved in the project. Traders will be warned ahead of the project start that spot checks can be conducted by WSA so as to discourage any forms of corruption.
WSA will also establish a complaint mechanism (hotline), and a participatory monitoring system through working with local authorities, beneficiary representatives. in Jarablus and Ghandorah, All these measures to mitigate the risks of fraud and to assure the gender sensitivity.

4 - Needs

Based on results from a countrywide household assessment, an estimated 7.9 million people in Syria are food insecure and an additional 1.9 million people are at risk of food insecurity. life.” Some 70 per cent of the assessed households reported that food accounted for more than 65 per cent of their expenditure. More than 37 per cent of food insecure households reported having outstanding debt and 57 per cent of food insecure people had adopted negative coping strategies. Almost a decade into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are conflict and protracted displacement, loss or lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity and economic sanctions on the country. These factors have resulted in limited physical and financial access to food, soaring prices and inflation, contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous livelihood depletion for the most vulnerable. About 216,000 hectares of wheat and other crops such as barley, fruit trees, vegetables and grazing areas were affected by a number of shocks including floods, fires, hailstorms, high temperatures and plant diseases and pests specifically yellow rust. (HNO 2020)Through the Discussion with the local councils, host community and IDPs in the project areas the results showed that project areas are considered ideal areas for planting vegetables, large agricultural areas are available, irrigation sources are available in the area. However, the continuation of the Syrian crisis and the security threats in the region, in addition to the high prices of agricultural inputs (seeds, fertilizer, fuel) have led to the reluctance of farmers to cultivate their lands and abandonment.add the results showed It is also feasible to support at least 5 dunums to 500 HHs in Jarablus and surrounding villages, approximately (457 men, 667 women,701 girls, and 675 boys) (the women and men farmers and their children),to encourage farmers to planting and sell inputs And achieve a good income.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will be directly implemented by WSA in coordination and cooperation with the local councils of the selected target districts. The community mobilizers will be in charge of beneficiary selection from both IDPs and host community, based on the following criteria: most vulnerable households, households headed by women, household with elderly, disabled, households with sick care takers. WSA will implement full market assessment and identify traders and evaluate their capacity and reputation, to sign the MOU with selected traders. many awareness sessions will implement with traders and beneficiaries to identify vouchers and how benefices redeem it. Voucher will provide with many information (beneficiary name,ID, code,signed of field manager, .. etc) to avoid fraud. The beneficiaries will be verified and their ownership of agricultural lands or rent agreements will be verified and registered in local councils to avoid fraud.The project will integrate IDPs with the host community through the establishment of clear mechanisms in cooperation with the local councils to rent agricultural land from their owners during the project period by paid money or part of the production at the end of the project and the establishment of mechanisms for monitoring and supervision by coordination with local authorities.The project will be managed by a qualified FSL technical adviser and oversee the local staff by providing technical advice and guidance The project will have four M & E staff (2male and 2female). WSA will integrate gender-based violence prevention activities into project activities through the development of an action plan that will incorporate the principles of protection during project implementation stages. Throughout the WGSS : Women and Girls Safe Space which WSA is managing as sustainable community center in Jarablus, it is called SALAM center 5 total project beneficiaries in this sector will be 59 % female and 41 % men.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Distinguishing between residents and displaced citizens Risk Level: Average /Medial Mitigation Measure : Full survey of the percentage of the displaced people in the region and implementing external activities for them and inviting them to the center. Risk 2: Distinguishing between residents of the city and the residents of the neighborhood areas Risk Level: High Mitigation Measure: Increase the external activities in the neighborhood villages more and more.Risk 3: The inability to meet the needs of all women in the region that we work in. In terms we are the lonely center that is specialized in the service GBV Risk Level : Average / Medial Mitigation Measure : Prioritizing the women according to their needs and provide them the services they need. Risk 4: Fear of the inability to keep the conﬁdential and secret documents safe due to the emergency security incidents. Risk Level: low. Mitigation Measure: Archiving all the documents on confidential electronic system and destroying the paper documents periodically as soon as they are completed and done.. Risk 5 :violence from beneficiaries among themselves . Risk Level: Average / Medial Mitigation Measure, Ensure distribution is well-organized (e.g. identification of sites/environment first; set up several distribution spots on sites; do joint distribution with another partner)

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A- IEDs/VBIEDs/PBIEDs/ Can be direct or indirect. It is one of the major threats in Syria. Solution/ Mitigation measures: -Choose centers far from military locations -Avoiding the crowding of beneﬁciaries during activates. -Commitment to all humanitarian principles, especially neutrality and impartiality to avoid Targeting. -Continuing coordination with the Security and Safety department during staff movement B-The intervention of Armed groups in humanitarian action/ Its common risk, to obstruct humanitarian action (blackmail) Solution.Mitigation measures: -The organization's obligation not to provide any information about the donor to any military faction interventions and other related information. -WSA policy is clear, do not to make a concession. -Coordination with civilian local authorities and Turkish government. C-Fluctuation of Dollar rate and issues of money transfer, Mitigation: 1-WSA is using OWNDA &QUICK BOOKS tools of cash monitoring and follow-up. 2-Sending money to Syria by PTT (Turkish government postal and telegraph organization)D- Lost of Access: The areas of project are located under the control and administration of Turkish Army Force ,and WSA has full access and full approval from the related directorates , in case of change of the controlling authorities , the alternative plans will depend on the phase of the project reached , and the action plan implemented and achieved,all beneficiaries (the women farmers) will be trained and oriented on all S&S procedures and mitigation measures (how to choose the location of land , how to set the routs of transportation and materials distribution , how to act in cases of bombs or shelling or any military action. H-Project start late for planting date:the mitigation measures will be replacing the crop with other types of strategic crops that have not yet been completed and thus ensure that farmers receive the appropriate agricultural support for them.
