Project Summary

This project is seeking to enhance IDPs residents' life-threatening conditions by providing essential, high-quality, and specialised humanitarian lifesaving services to the IDP population in fives of highly risk target states with twelve localities through a comprehensive and holistic approach that integrates health, nutrition & WASH services.
This project will continue at states which are interventions by the GAH in cooperation and coordination with other partners over past years. This project will target: west Kordofan (alidia, lagawa , alnohod, alsonot & abyie)south Kordofan(rashad, abujebaha ,habial & talodi) east Darfur at aldein locality, south Darfur (tulus &shataya )and Khartoum state at gabal-awlia locality.
The Health interventions include the provision of primary health care (PHC), reproductive health (RH) through support health facilities in the target locations with needed medical supplies and equipment. The interventions will also focus on increase the health capacity, support to the health referral and support the community’s health by mobile clinic. The nutrition interventions include provision of CMAM services for all the target populations and treatment of SAM cases, with or without complications for children under 5 and MAM cases for CU5 and PLWs. The project further intends to improve the public health of these targeted communities by providing WASH services for these target communities through promotion of hygiene, and construction/rehabilitation of communal and institutional latrines (in target HFs, OTPs, SCs and schools). In addition to improving the access of the target population to safe drinking water to minimize the risk of contracting infections.
Through these integrated interventions the IDPs will be targeted with the services and improve their accessibility to the services. All while ensuring that gender, protection and disability mainstreaming is an integral part of implementation, and working on strengthening the resilience and integrating the IDPs and returnees within the target communities.
This project will also take into account the environmental factors and risks that may be influenced by the proposed activities and devise the appropriate mitigation methods to minimize the effects on the environment.

1 - Needs

SSudan remains a large protracted humanitarian crisis with a significant impact on the already fragile health systems’ capacity to respond to several emergencies and peoples’ needs. The health needs are constantly exacerbated by a deteriorating economy, episodes of conflict, civil unrest, floods, and disease outbreaks.Despite the ongoing efforts made by the Government entities and nutrition sector partners to scale up treatment and preventive nutrition responses the prevalence of risk factors (Health, WASH, Food security, feeding practices) show no signs of significant improvements.According to the health cluster in Sudan, around 1.5 million women of reproductive age lack the access to both essential obstetric care, basic emergency obstetric and newborn services Bmonc and comprehensive emergency obstetric & newborn care services Cmonc765,000 IDPs living in areas with a severe lack of health services are specifically in need for support due to their complete dependency on humanitarian aid & lack o service coverage by health institutions. According 2021 multisectoral need assessment (MSNA) only 13.4 &% of women across the country had their deliveries in health facilities, during 2021 and with 40% of the home deliveries were not attended by qualified health care services. According to the Nutrition sector HNO 2022, a 25% increase in displacement due to floods or inter-communal clashes in 2022 compared to 2021, continue to be one of the main drivers of malnutrition as it disrupts affected communities’, access to health and wash services and therefore increasing risks to disease outbreaks and food insecurity in limited access to farming land, availability, and utilizations of foodHNO 2022 also stated that food insecure population (IPC 3 and above) are projected to increase by 34% from 7.2 in 2021 to 9.8 million in 2022 of which, 7 million people are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) while 2.7 million are critically food insecure classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and need humanitarian assistance. In 2020 15 out of the 18 states had high global acute malnutrition rates. Around 95 localities reported extremely high levels of malnutrition. The people in need for WASH services are 9.1M across Sudan including 2.2M IDPs, in accordance to HNO 2021, Sudan protracted crises continues to create WASH needs for vulnerable people. Around 39% of the population in Sudan have access to limited sanitation services and 13% have access to basic hand washing facilities. In addition, access to WASH in public institution such as schools and PHCs is limited for instance two thirds of the existing schools do not have improved sanitation facilities & a quarter of them do not even have access to clean water. In health facilities the lack of gender segregated facilities is a challenge & menstrual hygiene management is not often prioritized impacting girls negatively. For the 4 targeted states that were chosen according the severity of needs and vulnerability of their populations as per the 2021 HNO. In West Kordofan state that has an overall severity ranking of 4, from the total of 43081 IDPs; 99% of them are considered as people in need. while 26% of them are women and girls at child bearing age. In East Darfur that has that has the overall severity ranking of 3, 68,350 persons of the total population which accounts to about 73% of the total IDPs population are in need. For the case of SK 100% of the total population of the 83187 IDPs are in need with an all severity ranking of 4 according to the 2021 HNO. In Khartoum state, the project is targeting Gabal awliya locality that has the total of 263,092 IDPs in need. Of which 50%, 85% and 70% of them in need of nutrition, health and WASH interventions respectively.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In this project, mainstreaming of protection and GBV is through analysis (data gathering and analysis using sex and age disaggregated data collection tools), targeting and adaptation to the diversity of needs, identifying potential negative effects, and introducing prevention or mitigating measures, community engagement, information sharing, feedback mechanisms, and stakeholder coordination. Data of beneficiaries supported will be disaggregated by sex and age and disability.Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs) established in the target communities and seek to enhance beneficiaries’ rights to accountability. GAH considers ways to minimize tension and conflict between beneficiaries, by ensuring that accurate information reaches all relevant community groups, including children, adolescents, older persons, and persons with disabilities, using different means of communication and dissemination of AGD appropriate information. GAH will ensure that special arrangements (including but not exclusive to community outreach and volunteers) are made to provide meaningful access to services for those who may not be able to reach such as older persons and persons with disabilities.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs), at a minimum including Complaint Boxes, focal persons and physical monitoring will be established in the target communities to enhance beneficiaries’ rights to accountability. GAH considers ways to minimize tension and conflict between beneficiaries, by ensuring that accurate information reaches all relevant community groups, including children, adolescents, older persons, and persons with disabilities, using different means of communication and dissemination of AGD appropriate information. GAH will hold general and refresher training on protection mainstreaming, GBV, and PSEA prevention for its staff at HQ and field level, as well as the relevant community workers. Specifically tailored gender mainstreaming processes will be performed for each intervention and reports will be shared with the relevant sectors, and Protection sector and GBV subsector, and included in the reporting of the project. AGD disaggregated data will be collected for reporting purposes in all stages of the project. GAH is providing a response plan for the targeted locations in Education and Protection as well and will ensure that necessary links exist between all components and the GBV response, GAH will make sure to distribute flyers and posters in all Health and Nutrition services centres to inform the community about the services provided and who can benefit as well as the feedback collecting mechanisms and the working hours in a clear and easy language, and all sector's project staff and key stakeholders are trained on PSEA and will provide targeted communities with information regarding the SEA, and how to report and access services including health, legal and psychosocial support services, and GAH existing internal mechanisms for PSEA and SEA reporting and investigation, to ensure access and information of participants of these concepts and mechanisms. GAH will have an active presence in the national PSEA working groups and mechanisms to ensure all information is up to date in the cycle of sharing and reflect any possible case of SEA to the relevant mechanisms. Information sharing with communities will be conducted in a way that is suitable for the communities, this will be decided in the preliminary focus group discussions that GAH conducts throughout the project cycle to ensure meaningful participation and information of the communities in the intervention and promote AAP. Participants in these FGDs will be selected with attention to Age, Gender and diversity to ensure equal and inclusive participation.
