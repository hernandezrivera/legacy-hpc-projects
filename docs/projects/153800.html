Project Summary

The project aims at Strengthening Mechanisms for Protecting HLP Rights in Areas Expecting High Return. It contributes to the HRP objective SO 2&3, to enable durable solutions for displaced persons and other affected population in the project locations. These will be achieved through; (1) conducting HLP assessment to ascertain HLP needs of the displaced persons, refugee returnees and host communities existing mechanisms for HLP disputes resolution, (2) Raising awareness on HLP and the law, on spot monitoring and response to assist IDPs reclaim their land and properties, provision of free legal aid and (4) Establishment of innovative and sustainable community based alternative dispute resolution mechanism. Due to the complex nature of HLP issues and limited information on HLP issues in the project locations, the SSLS intends to carry out assessment, establish innovative and sustainable community mediation groups and provide continuous legal aid services. These interventions require a period of at least nine (6) months to implement and coordinate HLP program. The project targets areas of potential return of displaced person of Malakal, Bentiu, Bor and Juba.

Needs

Widespread destruction of houses and properties, degradation of housing due to displacement, secondary occupation, and discrimination in access to land, loss of documentation and title deeds, and disputes over customary land tenure are all features of the humanitarian crisis in South Sudan. Awareness of HLP rights is often low among local authorities and affected populations, in particular women and child-headed households. These HLP issues are more prevalent in urban centers compared to peri-urban areas and this problem has been aggravated by lack of legal information, assistance and litigation, limited access to courts, alternative dispute resolution or grievance management mechanisms where people may claim their rights. Based on the SSLS assessment, Magwi, Torit, Yei and Wau have high priority needs for HLP programming. However, Malakal, Bentiu, Juba and Bor also fall under high priority needs for HLP programming because of massive destruction, displacement which make these locations high areas of potential return. HLP issues constitute a major challenge to creating conducive conditions for return and reintegration of displaced populations. As a result of signing of the R-ARCSS, HLP needs are expected to increase during return, reintegration and resettlement of displaced persons in the project locations particularly during the fourth coming dry season. One of the key issues affecting the IDPs is the limited access to legal information, counseling and litigation. Most of the IDPs don’t understand their rights, to land, housing and property, their documents destroyed and their houses occupied. Available protection, monitoring and response mechanisms with regards to land, housing and property is lacking in most of protection programing. Strengthening HLP Legal frameworks provides an opportunity to enhance the protections afforded to women, vulnerable groups, and communities, promote transparent land governance, expedite the peaceful resolution of HLP-related disputes, and remove barriers and obstacles that currently impede the displaced population’s access to justice.Therefore, this project seeks to improve and strengthen protecting housing, land and property right of displaced person, returnees and host communities in the project locations. These will be achieved through, mapping of secondary occupation of displaced persons housing and land, on spot legal information, counseling and litigation, free legal aid and innovative and sustainable alternative dispute resolution mechanism.

Activities

Activities under Objective 1.• Conduct mapping exercise in Malakal, Bor, Juba and Bentiu to establish the number of illicit occupation of displaced persons land and housing. • The SSLS will conduct one stakeholders’ briefing with the protection cluster and one with the relevant government institutions at the national level. • The SSLS will also conduct stakeholders’ briefings to share the findings of the mapping in each of the locations. Activities under objective 2 • Establishment of legal aid clinics: The SSLS will establish one stop- shop legal aid clinic in the location where SSLS doesn’t have physical presence. • Provision of legal advice, counseling and litigation in courts: The SSLS will assist and provide legal services to about 1500 IDPs and other affected persons to reclaim their properties. • Conduct mobile legal aid days. The SSLS will conduct regular visits and assessments to the rule of law institutions and protection of civilians camps to provide onsite legal services and counseling to indigent IDPs, especially single headed female families, women and vulnerable persons targeting 12000 persons.Activities under objective 3: • The SSLS will conduct trainings on Housing, Land and Property (HLP) rights to community leaders and traditional authorities targeting 30 participants in each of the project location. • The SSLS will establish HLP Community Mediation Group (CMG) in each of the project locations. The CMG will consist of 11 members (6 female and 5 male). The CMG will mediate about 1000 HLP cases. • The SSLS will develop comprehensive paralegal training manual to aid in training of paralegals by SSLS and other NNGOs. • SSLS will also conduct training for paralegals for other NNGOs to widen the scope of legal provision and enhance access to justice. • The SSLS will rejuvenate the HLP coordination working group. The SSLS will work jointly with NRC to establish the working group under the protection cluster. Activities under Objective 4• Conduct 16 legal awareness outreach• Development of IEC materials on HLP rights

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project targets two categories of beneficiaries; (1) host communities including women, men boys and girls and (2) Displaced persons- IDPs and Refugees Returnees- including women, men, girls and boys.Among the target beneficiaries, single headed women families and children headed families are the most vulnerable groups affected by HLP issues. Women and girls have been particularly affected as the majority displaced population is women as women are often targeted by sexual and gender based violence. Women who remain in their housing, land and property continue to face sexual and gender based violence. These two categories of beneficiaries often face even more HLP protection issues owing to their vulnerability due to discriminative cultures and economic constraints. Men have been intentionally targeted on suspicion of political affiliation and many have been brutally killed. This has led to displacement of men in great numbers leaving behind single female headed families and families headed by minors. The beneficiaries are selected based on the level of vulnerability, capacity and those directly affected by the conflict. However this project targets beneficiaries who are within the host communities are faced with acute HLP related cases. Whereas the project intends to raise legal awareness on HLP related issues in the project locations, SSLS will develop selection criteria which prioritize the following: Vulnerability, financial capacity, HLP cases related to the conflicts, SGBV cases and other family related cases that undermine the right of women and girls.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

SSLS has formalised system of soliciting, receiving, processing and responding to the feedback and complaints we receive. The complain and feedback mechanisms provide a safe, non-threatening and easily accessible mechanism that enables even the most powerless to make a suggestion or complaint. On the part of SSLS, this helps us to address and respond to all complaints, and to be timely and transparent in our decisions and actions. The SSLS has two layers of complain accountability mechanisms at the head office. The Board of Trustees represented by the secretary General operates as mechanisms for detecting; registering complains from beneficiaries on the performance of the executive secretariat and undertakes measures to address them. At executive secretariat level the program management council composed of all heads of departments and the executive director serves as mechanisms for addressing accountability concerns regarding the performance of the program officers including field officers. SSLS has complain and suggestions box fixed in conspicuous placeas depository for complains and suggestions.The fields offices are equally fitted with complain and suggestion boxes and hotline to the head office as mechanisms for registering complains and feedbacks.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Risks: 1- The assessment team may be denied permission to conduct assessment due the sensitivity of the issues.2-Insecurity in the project location may disrupt the assessment exercise.3-Mobility of Legal Awareness team may be affected by heavy rains. 4- Negative cultural barriers may hinder women access to HLP. Mitigation:1- The SSLS will inform, engage and collaborate with relevant government authorities at national and sub-national level to have their buy-in and obtain necessary permission.2- SSLS will conduct regular situation monitoring and liaise with protection partners in the project locations to advice on safe and security. 3-SSLS will carefully plan to conduct Legal Awareness days during dry season.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Weak HLP dispute resolution MechanismsThe authorities’ ability to resolve HLP issues and competing claims will be compromised by the fact that land cadastres have been destroyed in some regions. Also, returns will take place to disputed areas where there is ambiguity about which body has the legal authority to take binding decisions on HLP issues.Ethnic dividesAuthorities may wish to favour one ethnic group in a territory over another in order to solidify their political control over the territory. Having experienced terrible violence, some communities may wish to live in geographic isolation from other groups. These trends are likely to lead to discriminatory patterns in returnees’ access to their property, since groups that are an unwanted minority may not be allowed to return at all, or upon return may find that their property has been destroyed or occupied. Discriminatory cultures against women and girls provide barriers to women right to housing, land and property. Single female headed families are more vulnerable to losing HLP rights in the context of culture that do not recognise women's rights to HLP.
