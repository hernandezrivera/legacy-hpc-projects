Project Summary

The project aims to increase the resilience of IDP Households who had to leave their houses due to the deterioration of the security status, as well as targeting a percentage of the host communities who match IYD’s selection criteria. The project will provide food rations on monthly basis for 12 months to 62,500 families in camps of Dana Sub-district in Idleb province. The same camps' beneficiaries will benefit from bread distribution every other day as a part of the general food assistance, which covers the minimum need.
Furthermore, multi-round cash distributions of $ 60 will be provided to 32,500 HHs in Aleppo and Idleb divided as follow: 5000 vulnerable families in Al-Bab district, 5000 vulnerable families in Jarablus district, 5000 Jebel Saman sub-district, 2500 in vulnerable families in Afrin sub-district, and 15,000 vulnerable families in Idlib district, 50% are from the host communities. Here we should mention that beneficiaries benefiting from cash in Idleb are not the same targeted with food rations.
In addition to that, the project aims to improve food security for the newly displaced people households and most vulnerable families by providing emergency cash for food of $100 value for a one-off to 5,000 vulnerable households within the project targeted sub-districts (1,000 HHs in Al-Bab, 1,000 HHs in Jarablus, 1,000 HHs in Harim, 1,000 HHs in Jebel Saman, and 1,000 HHs in Idleb). In addition, the project will distribute bread to beneficiaries in camps every other day as a part of the general food assistance, which covers the minimum need.
The IDPs are wholly dependent on humanitarian assistance for their daily needs, and the host communities in both provinces Aleppo and Idleb, are agricultural communities with very limited financial and livelihood resources, and the IDPs became a liability on the host communities because of this lack of livelihood, this issue is clearer in the areas where a limited number of NGOs provide humanitarian support.
IYD's MEAL plan will be done by monitoring the implemented activities and evaluating the objective achievements, to ensure that the project meets the required quality and time frame, to tackle challenges faced and summarize lessons learned. The IYD’s MEAL team will prepare a post-distribution monitoring report, and the data analysis and issue the report with key findings.
IYD will share the 5Ws report to appropriate clusters in order to find the gap if any and coordinate more on the cluster level. Also, IYD will prepare a final narrative and financial report.
IYD will coordinate with the camps' management committee in Idleb only, and with local councils in Aleppo. This coordination will facilitate IYD’s work especially in terms of data collection and verification, assist in solving any disputes that might happen during the distribution.
Approximately 80 % of this project fund is secured

4 - Needs

Syria remains one of the world’s most complex humanitarian emergencies characterized by ongoing hostilities which have killed hundreds of thousands of people, triggered one of the worst displacement crises of our time, and led to the widespread destruction of civilian and agricultural infrastructure, including homes, schools, health facilities, water supply, and irrigation systems. Today, 13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance – 21 % increase compared to 2020 – with needs increasingly being exacerbated by an economic decline (HNO 2021). According to IYD's needs assessment covering all the project areas. The first three needs; 32% is cash, 26% is food, and 18% is livelihood. Most of the respondents stated that the main method of coping is mainly purchasing food on credit and relying on less preferred and less expensive foods. 56% of respondents stated that no cash assistance has been provided in their communities during the last month. While 34% said that there has been cash assistance provided. 92% of respondents stated that the most urgent food intervention required is cash assistance, while 89% stated food baskets. According to the needs assessment, the studied HHs has reported that 86% of the families have no food stock for more than one month. The coping strategies of the studied families vary between relying on less preferred and less expensive food items, reducing the number of daily meals, buying food items on credit, etc.

5 - Implementation of activities

IYD will distribute General Food Assistant on a monthly basis to 62,500 HHs for 12 months in Dana Sub-district camps in Idleb and multi-round cash distributions of $60 for 32,500 HHs in Aleppo and Idleb. In addition to emergency cash for food of $100 for a one-off to 5,000 new IDPs•Registration & Verification: IYD team will visit the potential beneficiaries and collect their data, and according to criteria, IYD team will select the project beneficiaries•Contracting the money transfer agent and retailers, selecting the supplier must be carried out according to IYD policies•A Market Assessment will be conducted by IYD in the suggested area to check the Market’s functionality. •DistributionFood Rations: IYD team will coordinate with local authorities to determine a food distribution point “FDP”. The beneficiaries must provide an ID to prove their ID. After that, IYD team will deliver the ration. Elderly and pregnant women will be given priority. Vouchers: Vouchers will be distributed by the IYD field staff to selected beneficiaries in a pre-determined distribution site at the community level. Vouchers will be redeemed to verify the receipts against the distribution records using the beneficiary’s name and voucher serial numbers. •Post Distribution monitoring: IYD MEAL team will conduct PDM with beneficiaries to verify that the activities are done according to IYD and donor policies. •IYD will contract bakeries to bake and deliver 62,500 KG of bread every other day in camps

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

•IYD has adopted the inter-agency Code of Conduct Which condemns the SEA ( Endorsed by the Humanitarian Liaison Group - 2020) for the Syria cross-border response, which is intended for all staff and representatives of humanitarian organizations as well as their associated contractors, and volunteers•IYD is an active member of the PSEA network, and it is already familiar with the network’s monthly reporting tool where IYD reports its PSEA monthly activities such as (the number of beneficiaries reached by the distribution of awareness-raising materials, the number of beneficiaries reached by providing PSEA awareness-raising sessions, the number of field staff with direct interaction with beneficiaries who trained on PSEA)•IYD has established an internal SEA reporting and investigative mechanism which will not tolerate an atmosphere of impunity toward SEA. Zero tolerance means that active measures will be introduced to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse by IYD and that appropriate disciplinary action will be taken against all persons who are found to have violated the IYD standards of conduct•Complete PSEA Checklists: The organizational checklist was be completed by the management and the HR unit in communication with the CEO. Also, the Field Observation Checklist for PSEA Mainstreaming will be completed by IYD's program managers who will utilize these tools in all our interventions during the implementation and monitoring stages to identify gaps and risks related to PSEA


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Institutional: Low collaboration between IYD and the actors involved in the project leading to a slowdown in the implementation of activities. However, IYD’s strong presence and good name will enable it to overcome this challenge • Operational: Limited capacity of service providers leading to a delay in the supply process. To mitigate this, IYD will keep following up the service providers day by day, to ensure their commitment to the contracts signed with them. • Social risks: From a social standpoint, this project presents no risk to the IYD and local communities. Although women are still underrepresented in decision making bodies, their concerns have over time been taken into account, and this project will seek to ensure their better presence and representation. •Security Risks: Interference from the military controlling groups or the local councils in the targeted areas, and deterioration of the security situation. IYD has a strong relationship with neutral communities’ leaders and will highly coordinate with them for the prevention of any interference attempts in the project activities or IYD’s work as a whole. IYD will coordinate with the active actors on the ground to select the safest area to implement this project; additionally, IYD’s safety and security team will keep following up the security situation and send updates to the field team to ensure their safety •
