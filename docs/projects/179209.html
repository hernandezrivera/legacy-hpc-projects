Project Summary

Mercy Corps (MC) will respond to priority needs with life-saving and life-sustaining WASH assistance across the governorates of Al-Hasakeh, Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor and Idleb.
MC will work on improving access to basic Water and sanitation services through the delivery of water trucking, SWM, Desludging and the rehabilitation of water supply infrastructure in IDP locations and areas with a high number of IDPs and returnees, and areas we anticipate significant inward displacement (Ar-Raqqa and DEZ, Dana, Daret Azza and Atareb in Idleb and north Aleppo). In NW, MC will continue providing a full WASH package camps and IDP sites, which includes COVID related interventions, Water Quality monitoring and treatment and hygiene education. Hygiene education, using carefully tailored messages, and the distribution of hygiene and COVID kits will complement the other WASH interventions. Through ongoing Coordination with WASH cluster, working groups, local councils and other agencies, MC will be coordinating its response in relation to the gaps identified and associated needs. In 2021, a special focus will be given by MC to seek solutions for operation and maintenance (O&M), through support to local committees and water authorities directly after the completion of water supply rehabilitation. MC will work actively to identify and implement water trucking sustainable solutions and cost efficient sanitation management in the targeted IDP locations. Internal water supply networks, water tanks and toilets will complement more comprehensive HH shelter activities, providing safe and dignified accommodation standards for displaced and for those who have chosen to return. MC will also distribute multi-purpose cash assistance to conflict-affected households based on regional SMEB values, which include hygiene items.

4 - Needs

MC's 2020 MSNA highlighted that 47% of interviewed HHs in Al Hasakeh and Ar-Raqqa governorates primarily rely on water trucking provided through private vendors. An unregulated private sector has filled the gap left by the absence of a centralized water system but is inadequate, unaffordable and often low quality. A total of 10% of the HHs’ income is spent on purchasing water. Inadequate storage capacity was reported by 61% of the interviewed HHs living in collective accommodation, unfinished/abandoned buildings and informal settlements/camps. Overall, 42% of assessed population reported that waste collection frequency in their communities either happens once a week or on irregular basis. Issues related to adequate sanitation and access to latrines, were reported to be a major concern by 60% of the respondents in the assessed areas. Only 17% of the overall sample of HHs reported having receiving any awareness information or materials regarding prevention and safeguarding measures related to COVID-19. MC assessments revealed severe damage to pumps, generators and piped infrastructure impacting water quality, quantity, and availability. With public water systems operating at less than their pre-conflict capacity and unable to ensure the same water quality. High needs of life-saving supplies and services were reported in Aleppo governorate and Idleb governorates. In both formal and informal camps there is a huge need to increase the water supply and manage drinking water safety to ensure the displaced people stay away from potential diarrheal diseases. Similarly, there is an increasing need for desludging, as the septic tanks and pit latrines are flooding causing contamination to nearby water sources.

5 - Implementation of activities

1 Repair and rehabilitation of water systems: MC will identify critical service gaps and needs for rehab of water stations and networks. Technical assessments in consultation with the water directorates and local councils will inform BoQs design. Staff will also facilitate FGDs with men, women, boys, and girls to identify protection risks faced by each in accessing WASH sites, which will further inform the design. 2 Support to water quality assurance and operation and maintenance of water supply systems: MC will provide capacity development and O&M support for municipal water service team through provision of monthly maintenance costs and necessary materials and equipment, including generators, pumps, fuel tanks, diesel, disinfectants, and spare parts for periodic maintenance. 3 Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems: MC will provide garbage removal services and provide communal bins in informal settlements, camps and urban settings. 4 Distribution of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotion including COVID related messages: MC will conduct hygiene promotion activities to raise awareness of safe hygiene behavior and practices and distribute hygiene kits. MC will conduct a Knowledge, Attitudes and Practices (KAP) survey to identify KAP gaps in hygiene, water and sanitation. 5 Improved access to lifesaving/ emergency WASH facilities and services: Mercy Corps will provide a comprehensive WASH package including water trucking, Sanitation services, and SWM with the implementation of low cost and sustainable solutions according to WASH WoS minimum standards. Full water quality analysis will be conducted with the water supplier in advance.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Mercy Corps recognizes the importance of protection, gender and inclusion mainstreaming into all its WASH programming, as it will ensure participants’ safety and dignity, equal access and meaningful participation. Mercy Corps will conduct protection, disability and gender mainstreaming and conflict sensitive refresher training for staff and partners prior to the WASH implementation. Mercy Corps will also complete a WASH specific protection risk analysis (PRA) at the start of its projects, which will be reviewed throughout project implementation. Mercy Corps will further deliver community awareness sessions on rights and entitlements, information on available services, selection criteria, complaints and feedback mechanisms and Mercy Corps’ Code of Conduct prior to the implementation of field activities. Data collected in WASH programming will be disaggregated by sex, age, and disability. The following illustrates the main protection risks associated with this program, and mitigating measures that will be taken by Mercy Corps. Mercy Corps acknowledges that there are a significant number of protection risks that individuals and communities can face as a result of poor programming or programming in a difficult environment. This does not capture them all. 1. Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers/partners: Mercy Corps monitoring and evaluation systems include accountability frameworks, and Mercy Corps has a beneficiary complaints mechanism. 2. Programming exacerbates community tensions due to perceived discrimination/favouritism: Mercy Corps upholds a conflict sensitive and DNH approach throughout programming.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Safety and security of program participants and staff could be negatively affected: The ongoing conflict in Syria presents a constant threat to the safety and security of individuals and groups across Syria, including prospective project participants and staff members. To mitigate this risk. Mercy Corps will conduct ongoing context analysis including security and risk assessments to inform decisions on location, routes and timing of program activities, ensuring such decisions are in the best interest of the safety and security of participants and staff. Social and cultural norms of targeted communities will be observed during all phases of the program implementation to ensure safety risks associated with real or perceived violation of norms are mitigated. Project staff will complete a mandatory safety and security training; and participants will also be sensitized on issues of safety and security so that they are able to recognize threats to their own safety and security and take action to protect themselves from such threats. Implementation delays due to COVID 19: Program implementation could experience significant delays due to the COVID-19 pandemic, occasioned by lockdowns and curfews. Mercy Corps will draw on experiences adapting program implementation over the last one year to ensure that as many activities as possible can be implemented. Simultaneously, opportunities for pursuing and strengthening remote program implementation capacities will be explored and harnessed. Loss of access to target communities: Shifts in territorial control or major security incidents could temporarily or permanently impede access of Mercy Corps organization to project participants. Mercy Corps will continuously monitor the situation
