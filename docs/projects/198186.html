Project Summary

CDHI will implement 12-month emergency GBV response that improves access to justice and specialized GBV services to affected population in crisis affected communities of Michika and Madagali LGAs targeting IDPs, Returnees and Vulnerable women, men, boys, and girls living in the host communities. The project will provide GBV specialized services and access to legal and justice services to women, men, boys and girls, amongst IDPs, Returnees and Hosting populations. The project will leverage on CDHI’s long standing presence in community-based driven initiatives in GBV, WASH, and Nutrition to deliver quality GBV response that meet the needs of vulnerable people living in the host communities. The GBV project will focus on actions that will increase access to prevention and response services for GBV survivors and individuals at risk of GBV. GBV survivors will receive survivor centered case management services including referrals for special services, including access to justice through formal or informal channels. Through this project formal and informal institutions will be strengthened to facilitate uptake of access and utilization of the justice systems. CDHI will construct Women and girls friendly spaces across communities of Michika and Madagali LGAs. CDHI will strengthened existing health facilities through training of health workers on clinical management of Rape, provision of supplies including contraceptives', PEP - Kits, delivery kits and facilitation of transportation to survivors for timely uptake of services. The project will establish and/or support existing family support networks to reduce the social isolation of families and increase social support for women and girls. This will increase parent’s ability to provide social. Similarly, CDHI will facilitate the training of formal institutions including the NBA, International Federation of Female Lawyers – FIDA, State security architecture, Human Right commission to support facilitation and access to justice for beneficiaries wishing to access justice through the formal system. Additionally, activities to improve resilience and coping mechanisms of women, girls, men, and boys affected by GBV through provision of livelihood support through income generating activities - IGA and fostering social cohesion targeting vulnerable individuals through community-based psychosocial support, provided in newly established safe spaces that will be supported by the project. CDHI through this project targets to provide case management services to identified GBV survivors.
Similarly, women, men, boys, and girls will benefit from life skills and positive parenting sessions using the adolescent life skill curriculum and positive parenting manual. CDHI will increase local capacity by strengthening community-based mechanisms, through training of community leaders, religious leaders, health workers, security personnel’s on GBV core concept and referral pathway. The project will also increase awareness on the impact of GBV and sexual reproductive health right - SRHR through sensitization during health facility based ante-natal care services and also through outreach to individuals and will work with communities to identify GBV risks in the targeted communities and develop action plans to mitigate these risks.
Trained facilitators will deliver tailored age, gender, and culture sensitive, appropriate GBV/SRH messages using the provided sensitization guide to members of the communities. Similarly, CDHI will provide and distribute dignity kits accessed from the GBV core pipeline to targeted vulnerable women and girls who meet the vulnerability criteria. Men and boys will also be engaged at the community level through peer discussions to raise awareness and reduce stigma towards women and girls during menstruation. All communication packages will be adopted from the existing key messages and IEC materials developed by the sector.

1 - Needs

Yes, as part of CDHI’s policy to ensure that humanitarian response is informed by needs, CDHI conducted rapid multi-sectoral assessment covering GBV and WASH as well as community consultation and desk review of existing evidence of GBV assessments to determine the GBV needs in these locations.The conflict stemming from the insurgency of non-state armed groups (NSAGs) in north-east Nigeria continues as intensely as ever. The attacks and insecurity have displaced millions of people, devastated livelihoods, cut off essential services, including GBV, and health and caused a crisis of protection. According to the 2023 Humanitarian needs overview and GBV sub sector response plan and target, a total of 1,317,980 million people including IDPs, returnees and Host communities are in need of GBV response. out of which about 301,706 women, men, boys and girls including people with disability are targeted for specialized GBV services including case management, mental and psycho-social support, medical care, legal assistance and safety and security support. In Michika LGA, about 4,980 individuals are targeted to recieve GBV specialized services. Government camp closure in Borno state continue to stretch limited resources at LGAs level and significantly impacting on the severity of the needs of returnees. The joint inter-sectoral Analysis framework (JIAF) forecast shows a significant increase (by 68%) in the number of returnees with extreme severity of needs, from 600,000 to more than 1 million, and with almost 500,000 returnees with a severe level of intersectoral needs. Many of the returnees (70%) with extreme severity of needs reside in Adamawa State in six Local Government Areas (LGAs) including Michika and Madagali. Adamawa has proportionately more returnees overall because insecurity is less severe in much of the state, compared to Borno. Many of the Adamawa returnees returned to their homes several years ago, though, in many cases, their needs are still extreme. Multi-sectoral GBV and WASH assessment conducted by CDHI in Kirchinga and Jingilanbu ward of Madagali and Michika LGAs respectively also revealed that SGBV such as sexual violence, rape and domestic violence assault are high yet under reported. Focus group discussion with the participants involved in the assessment suggests that the cases of SGBV are many and very complex to handle that even after mediation the fighting between couples continues. The community also report such cases to Civilian Joint Task Force (CJTF) for mediation, but the women mentioned that the CJTF, usually support the men and in circumstances where the men is held responsible, the CJTF, seriously physically torture the men perpetrators posing additional risks to the woman who have reported. The major cause of these violence is lack of money because of no means of livelihood. The men feel frustrated with their inability to provide food for their families and this in turn forces them to get violent with their wives. Another challenge is lack of sex between couples. The women complain of being tired because of them working all day on their farmlands and at night, the men will want to have conjugal activities with them and when they refuse, they get beaten. They are most times ashamed to report or discuss these issues with others. Similarly, female headed households were identified to be more at risks, because they don’t not have husband to provide for them. Madagali and Michika is also one of the locations with critical humanitarian needs but limited partner presence. CDHI is currently the only humanitarian partner providing live saving interventions through a multi-sectoral approach in Madagali LGAs.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CDHI will ensure that all its staff and other stakeholders involved in the delivery of this project are well trained on the four key pillars of protection mainstreaming to ensure that these elements are adequately integrated thought out the project implementation. This will include.- Prioritizing safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm: CDHI will ensure to prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the planned activities which can increase affected population's vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks- Meaningful Access: Planned activities will be implemented in a way that is accessible to the target beneficiaries and in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g., discrimination). CDHI will ensure to pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. – Accountability: CDHI will ensure to set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints through a robust complaint and feedback mechanism.– Participation and empowerment: CDHI will support communities through the development of self-protection, capacities and assist targeted beneficiaries to claim their rights, including basic human rights, rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education and specialized GBV services including access to justice. The training will seek to increase knowledge and consciousness of staff and stakeholders in ensuring these principles are part and parcel of design and implementation of activities. A key outcome of these trainings is the identification of protection risks within the frame of planned activities and how to mitigate these risks. There will be a protection mainstreaming budget line which will remain flexible to address any form of protection related issue identified. CDHI staff have been specifically trained to identify and support extremely vulnerable persons and ensure they are supported appropriately throughout the project.CDHI will integrated in its sensitization messages mechanism to report issues related to sexual exploitation and Abuse. CDHI have and internal system of PSEA. Those systems and mechanism will be made available to the communities. Community members will also have the complaint box, and a dedicated community level help desk which could be utilized in raising concerns of SEA or suggestions on how to prevent SEA. As this planned project is informed by needs; CDHI will continually conducts assessments using qualitative approaches such as focus group discussions, key informant interviews and in-depth community discussions and feedback sessions to remain informed on the needs of the affected population and ensure to realign planned activities to meet these needs in program design and response. In addition to this, CDHI is guided by the principle of neutrality in responding to needs and ensuring communities understand that CDHI staff are neutral and trustworthy bound by strict principles of confidentiality and integrity. The consultative approach employed in the design and implementation of this project will ensure feedback from the communities will continue to inform the programme implementation thereon. CDHI protection staff (funded by this project) sole’s responsibility will be to ensure the highest level of protection and gender is well mainstreamed in the project implementation
