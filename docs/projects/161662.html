Project Summary

Mercy Corps’ Recovery and Assistance Initiative program is a continuation of the on-going emergency humanitarian response currently implemented in four LGAs (Damboa, Dikwa, Ngala and Gwoza) in Borno State. The program extension focus on addressing immediate lifesaving humanitarian needs especially those needs faced by new arrivals and people living in overcrowded and undeserved camps and host communities using an integrated approach with Shelter, WASH, Protection and nutrition interventions.
The nutrition component focus on strengthening the quality and scale of preventative nutrition services through promotion of appropriate infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices and supporting timely community level case identification and referral of acutely malnourished cases through role out of Mother Led MUAC approach.
Infant and Young Child Feeding promotion targets to reach 15,000 individuals in the four LGAs and it will be achieved through enhanced social behavior change frameworks and approaches using a four pronged approach i.e. use of nutrition promoters, Mother Support Groups, Male Engagement and Mass Sensitization.
In addition to IYCF promotion, Mercy Corps will also train/ sensitize and equip 10,800 mothers on how to regularly measure the Mid Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) of their children and checking for oedema. This will enhance their knowledge about malnutrition, improve coverage, and increase timely case identification and referral of malnourished cases to SAM/MAM treatment sites supported by other Nutrition actors.

1 - Needs

The conflict in some parts of North East continues to have a direct impact on people’s nutrition status. The crisis has also exacerbated pre-existing conditions of malnutrition, driven by a number of interrelated factors, including: weak health infrastructures; poor infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices; limited access to safe water and health and sanitation services; prevalence of water-borne diseases; poor hygiene conditions; and seasonal food insecurity. The 2019 HNO, reported that about 2.8 million people, including 1.7 million children under five and 1.1 million women of reproductive age within Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States, need life-saving nutrition services. In Borno alone, 1.7 million people are in need of nutrition services, out of these, 1.2 million are children. Out of the 1.7 million children across the three conflict affected states in the Northeast who are in need of nutrition services, 21 percent are suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) while 18 percent from Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). Consequently, 9.1 percent of children with Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) are from Borno state. The 2019 Round 8, NENFSS reported a GAM rate of 8.1 percent (based on Weight for Height Z-Scores), showing a slight reduction however, still poor according to WHO classification and requires continued strengthening and scale up of on-going nutrition services to ensure improved quality, coverage and timely response.

2 - Activities

Promotion of appropriate maternal, infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices among 15,000 individuals. This will be achieved through enhanced social behavior change frameworks and approaches using a four pronged approach i.e. use of nutrition promoters, Mother Support Groups, Male Engagement and Mass Sensitization. Nutrition Promoters: Mercy Corps will support recruitment of 80 nutrition promoters (20 per targeted Local Government Area) to promote better maternal and IYCF practices. They will be trained on IYCF using sector approved tools and manuals, equipped with Information, Education and Communication (IECs) materials and supported to conduct at least two monthly visits to each targeted household. They will receive a monthly stipend and quarterly refresher training to enhance their knowledge and skills. Community leaders and other key stakeholders will also be trained to reinforce Nutrition Promoters’ messages. Mother Support Groups: Secondly, Mercy Corps will coordinate with local community leaders and structures to form 40 Mother Support Groups from households within the program locations. These groups will serve as a safe space where women feel a sense of respect and acceptance, share information and feel emotionally connected. These groups will be targeted with messages on maternal and child nutrition and receive IEC materials to facilitate discussions among themselves. Mercy Corps also plans to involve grandmothers to provide support to the young mothers on positive IYCF practices. The MSGs with also conduct hand washing and food preparation/cooking demonstration sessions to enhance knowledge and improved dietary practices. Mass Sensitization: Thirdly, Mercy Corps will distribute handbills, banners, leaflets and other supporting materials to schools, markets, and health centers (including OTP/stabilization sites) in targeted catchment areas to support proper IYCF and promote its awareness and uptake. Male Engagement and Sensitization: Finally, Considering the role of men in decision making and household financial support to improve the acceptance of the program and also the utilization of health and nutrition services by women and children, Mercy Corps will conduct male engagement meetings targeting men across the program locations with positive nutrition information. Mercy Corps will also support community level active case finding and referral using a Mother Led MUAC approach. This will involve training/ sensitizing and equipping 10,800 mothers on how to regularly measure the Mid Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) of their children and checking for oedema. This will enhance their knowledge about malnutrition, improve coverage, and increase timely case identification and referral of malnourished cases to SAM/MAM treatment sites supported by other Nutrition actors.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Mercy Corps has developed and maintains Community Relief Committees (CRC) as its primary community mobilization mechanism, with representatives of diverse age, gender, ethnic, religious, and socio-economic groups, including displaced and host communities, so that their concerns are heard and addressed and to avoid community tensions. CRCs have at least two female members, helping to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse of people seeking shelter and services. Mercy Corps’ Community Accountability Reporting Mechanism (CARM) will be continued in the RAI Borno 3 project to enable a safe and effective feedback/complaint system for beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. The CARM includes a community-based feedback mechanism developed by Mercy Corps in Gombe in 2014 that allows people (in particular non-literate beneficiaries) to give feedback verbally and with discretion. Complaints will also be gathered through designated community focal points and through the use of a logbook updated by the focal point within each CRC, with electronic recording, analysis and follow up of all complaints within a specified timeline by Mercy Corps’ M&E staff. CARM will also be used to monitor protection issues, and issues will also be monitored under the Protection-sector specific programming described below; that information will be used to reduce existing and newly identified risks. The program will monitor the security situation to adjust its implementation approach if the security situation deteriorates.
