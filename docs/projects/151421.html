Project Summary

The proposed intervention targets the Internally Displaced People (IDPs) in Benadir, Bay, Bakool, Hiran, Galgadud, Gedo, Middle and Lower Shabelle regions of Somalia who face deplorable living conditions in overcrowded settlements. By delivering a number of interrelated activities to enhance information gathering at site level, stakeholder coordination, camp management, settlement planning and structure, the project will significantly enhance the living conditions of the IDPs and foster durable solutions to their plight.

Needs

Somalia continues to face huge internal displacement crisis. According to a recent UNHCR bulletin, there was an estimated 2.6 million Internally Displaced Persons in Somalia by end of August 2018. The number continues to grow due to conflict, forced evictions and natural disasters. In August 2018 alone, up to 53,000 people were newly displaced, over half of whom came to Banadir from Lower Shabelle (UNHCR Brief, 2018). Data from the JMCNA that was collected from 54 districts in Somalia in 2018 reveals that 80% of the IDPs live in makeshift shelters, temporary emergency shelters, tents, or under trees which indicate significant levels of vulnerability. The displaced, most of them women and children, often live in informal and un-planned settlements where living conditions are extremely poor and forced eviction is a common threat. Poorly constructed shelters expose IDPs to inclement weather conditions, health hazards and gender-based violence (GBV). The latter is particularly happening at alarming rate whereby 76% of recorded GBV cases countrywide have happened in IDP settlements (Protection Cluster Mid-year Review 2018 report). Lack of adequate information at site level also results in poor prioritization and targeting of beneficiaries that limit the effectiveness of humanitarian response. While multiple actors continue to provide services to IDP settlements, coordination levels are weak resulting in duplication of effort and lost opportunities for complementarity. In this context and to effectively support the IDPs with timely and relevant support, AVORD presents this initiative to setup effective camp coordination structures where necessary, enhance coordination among humanitarian actors providing response and gather/disseminate data for effective programming.

Activities

This project seeks to enhance the living conditions and access to service for IDPs in Benadir, Bay, Bakool, Hiran, Galgadud, Gedo, Middle and Lower Shabelle regions of Somalia where they are concentrated. This will be achieved through a number of complementary activities including setting up of camp coordination structures where necessary, strengthening of the same where they exist and by conducting continuous detailed site assessments. Compiling of a master list of sites and mapping of both accessible infrastructure and partners operating in sites will be undertaken regularly to enhance service delivery and coordination. An integral part of the proposed intervention is to support community led site maintenance activities through cash vouchers to ensure upkeep of sites. AVORD will also implement emergency site improvement projects to minimize protection risks and ensure safety. In the implementation of the proposed package, AVORD will closely coordinate with different levels of local authorities to advocate for local reintegration and permanent sheltering to support durable solution. AVORD will adapt a systematic approach to site selection with a clear criterion to prioritize those hosting the largest number of vulnerable IDPs such as unaccompanied children, older Persons, persons with disability, single women, pregnant and lactating mothers, single headed-households, persons with chronic illness or psychological condition, families with babies, toddlers or children attending school and persons at risk as identified by protection officers. Data collected to inform site selection will be desegregated by gender to ensure that no group is left-out. The effectiveness of the aid package will be evaluated through ongoing monitoring and evaluation processes thus ensuring that beneficiaries access their services safely and in a dignified manner.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

This proposal is humanitarian in nature focusing on the coordination of life-saving assistance to IDPs in humanitarian crisis. However, it also contributes to building the resilience of targeted IDPs in a number of ways. On the one hand, strengthening of information sharing, camp management and coordination among humanitarian actors contributes to effective service delivery at site level which will translate into overall improvement in the living conditions of IDPs and their chances to access livelihoods. Multi-sectoral interventions are often more effective when coordinated, leaving IDPs more resilience to future shocks and able to cope with the grim reality of displacement. On the other hand, the project advocates the local integration of IDPs and enhancing living conditions in their places of origin, for those willing to return, thus providing durable solutions to the protracted IDP crisis in Somalia. AVORD will also support communities in IDP sites towards self-reliance in locations where prospects of obtaining land rights is achievable while advocating with local authorities for public land for displaced people.To maximize impact and sustain project outcome, AVORD will seek integration with other clusters providing response in areas where proposed activities will be implemented. Details pertaining to project activities will be shared through relevant clusters and 4W reporting matrix to avoid duplication of effort. Local communities will play a key role in different phases of the project to create sense of ownership which will contribute to sustaining of project outcomes. AVORD will strive to build local capacities thus ensuring that various community leaders and committees are able to sustainably maintain resources following project completion and handover.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

IDPs may face exclusion from gate-keepers who try to hamper their access to the assistance provided by humanitarian actors. Besides, certain power dynamics within IDP settlements may sometimes lead to the exclusion of IDP from marginalized communities. AVORD will prevent this by undertaking direct identification and registration of beneficiaries without use of middle men or gate keepers. AVORD will also apply systematic beneficiary identification and collection of adequate household profile before the intervention to ensure that only the most vulnerable and marginalized IDPs are registered for assistance. Forced eviction remains a constant threat facing the IDPs in Somalia. To mitigate this, AVORD will advocate for the construction of permanent shelters only in public land or in areas where land tenure is secured for a minimum of 10 years and obtain relevant legal documents e.g. land titles. AVORD will also work closely with local authorities to address the impact of eviction on IDPs while identifying the groups who could be more prone to eviction during need assessments.Finally, there is a likelihood of tensions among the IDP and host community tensions resulting from the intervention. This will be mitigated by being transparent with the target communities and providing them with timely information on the selection criteria, organisational procedures and processes that affect them. Joint IDP-host community committees will be established to participate in project design and implementation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Exclusion based on gender and age could happen in IDP settings if adequate preventive measures are not taken. For example, in an environment of male dominance, women and girls could be easily left-out or disproportionately registered during beneficiary identification and selection processes. To prevent that, AVORD will collect and use data dis-aggregated by sex, age and diversity to inform beneficiary selection and maintain gender parity. Activities will also be designed in a manner that can address the unique needs of women, men, boys, girls and those with special needs.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Repetitive flare-up of armed conflicts in Somalia may hinder access to areas targeted by this project. AVORD will mitigate that by continuously assessing local dynamics and making plans accordingly. Where necessary, relief items will be pre-positioned in strategic locations where feasible to respond to humanitarian emergencies in the event of restricted access. To prevent exposing beneficiaries to risk, AVORD will ensure that access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly and safe distribution. The Do-No-Harm principals will be observed at all times to prevent un-intended negative consequences.
