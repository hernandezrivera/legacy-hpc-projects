Project Summary

Youth development and empowerment are vital stages in life for building the human capital that allows young people to avoid poverty and lead better, and possibly have a more fulfilling life. The human capital formed in youth is thus an important determinant of long-term growth that a nation can invest in.
International organizations and governments have invested in the youth sector over the past decades, Where it has a good impact on the political and social stability of societies.
It also contributes to the end of the conflict and peace, whether as a result of external wars or internal conflicts.
In the areas where refugees and IDPs are hosted, Inside and outside Syria, the economic empowerment of youth contributes to reducing the financial burden of receiving refugees and IDPs and reducing tensions with the host community as a result of the depletion of existing resources, especially in economically vulnerable areas. Therefore, based on the difficulty of the situation of young Syrian refugees to find jobs because of the legal conditions and policies imposed in the host country to work with any official party or the establishment of private work, as well as young people displaced within Syria under conditions of war, and the waste of the ability of young people in work in the poor jobs that consume their energy. The QRCS has developed the following proposal to support a range of youth initiatives for internally displaced persons in Syria.
Through the provision of grants that enable them to establish innovative, income-generating pilot projects to support themselves and their families. As well as providing them with the skills and experience necessary

4 - Needs

Despite the reduced level of hostilities and violence in many key densely populated areasof the country by the end of 2019, millions of Syrians continue to face huge challenges inaccessing basic services, due to widespread infrastructure destruction, erosion of theproductive base, scarce resources, and massive internal displacement. The social fabricof the country has been severely damaged. According HNO 2020 report, there are 9.9 million people inside Syria requiring Early Recovery and Livelihoods support given the cumulative impact of crisis on the population and broader macro-economic developments ,. While areas exposed to active hostilities have shrunk over time, populations continue to experience poor living standards 25 per cent of basic and social infrastructure is destroyed.approximately 60 per cent of males and females who are part of the workforce are without sustained access to employment due to lack of livelihood and income-earning opportunities as well as vocational skills. Such high rates of lack of sustained access to employment is a result of years of crisis with women, female-headed households, people with disability and elderly being particularly vulnerable. The local context analyses indicate that poor economic conditions, physical destruction of infrastructure, and large-scale displacement have led to competition over scarce livelihood opportunities and overburdening of available social services. A combination of these factors is also responsible for posing a significant hindrance to some of the displaced people wanting to voluntarily return to the places of their origin, forcing them to live in a state of constant uncertainty and to remain dependent upon humanitarian assistance for surviva

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Access restrictions to the geo area due to active fighting and/or shifting of front lines would prevent the humanitarian actors from providing timely emergency relief to the affected population. Likelihood: High Mitigation: 1. Constantly monitor the security situation on the ground, 2. Develop contingency planning. 3. Set-up multiple back-up distribution centers, 4. Liaison with the local authorities, 5. Explore different distribution modalities, 6. Elaborate on different plans for flexible deliveries in areas that are inaccessible. Monitoring: 1. Monitor the security situation on the ground & hire security staff and specialists, 2. Liaise with local authorities and focal points in the field, 3. Keep communication channels open with civilian authorities, stakeholders, and NGOs. 4. Monitor and identify as needs arise new distribution points, 5. Develop and maintain monitored contingency plans. Risk 2: Eviction from the shelter due to lack of approval from the property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitated Likelihood: High Mitigation: 1. Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster, 2. Do not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP, 3. Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed, 4. Have an HLP section in the M&E assessment form and verify the documents available. Monitoring: 1. Monitor the existence of the documentation and conduct periodic visits to the rehabilitated Shelters to assess the condition of the beneficiaries and verify that they have not been unlawfully evicted 2.
