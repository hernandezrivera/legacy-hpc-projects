Project Summary

Context and background/history
On 25 August 2017, violence erupted in Rakhine State, Myanmar, triggering a massive and swift refugee influx into Bangladesh. Many of the refugees who arrived required critical medical treatment for physical injuries including gunshot wounds and burns, they also needed preventive treatment to mitigate against communicable diseases, antenatal care, emergency obstetric care services, reproductive health and GBV case management including clinical management of rape.
Needs Assessment
CARE conducted a Rapid Gender Analysis 19-23 September 2017 noted the community’s history of poor family planning practices, recognizing that most married women had between three and seven children. It also identified the social stigma attached to menstruation management. Adolescents in the camps especially those without one or more parents are now even more vulnerable to sexual harassment, forced or child marriage, trafficking and other forms of gender-based violence including rape. Many married women were already pregnant but have never received any reproductive health support. In the upheaval, countless women were raped as they fled their homes and tried to cross the border.
Strategic orientations/goals
To provide SRH and GBV services along with PHC services through outreach doorstep activities and static health facilities across the people living in the camps including the host community with special focused on women girls and children.
Description of activities and implementation Modalities:
Based on the context/background and the needs CARE recognized the need for static health care clinics in November 2017, and in December opened 4 clinics to provide SRH and basic health services in Camp 12, Camp 13 and Camp 14. In opening the facilities, they recognize the gender divide in patients and therefore instituted an outreach system, which enlisted healthcare practitioners and volunteers to visit shelters to actively seek out women and offer them support. Therefore, CARE seeks to utilize these resources to continue funding the clinics and strengthen their activities. In this phase of the project, we will also further incorporate GBV support and SRH education.
Beneficiaries of the project
There are approximately 50,000 who will be targeted. This represents 11,500 women, 9,500 men, 14,500 girls, and boys. Among these there are 2,000 pregnant and 3500 lactating women.
