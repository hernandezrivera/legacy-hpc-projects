Project Summary

For the 12 consecutive years of the non-stop conflict in Syria, the scale, severity and complexity of humanitarian needs of people in Syria remain widespread. The conflict is ongoing in localized areas, new and protracted displacement, increased self-organized returns and the sustained erosion of communities’ resilience the conflict continues to take a devastating toll on the lives of the Syrian people, this is added to a rapidly worsening economy leading to higher food prices and the Covid-19 pandemic and recently the new outbreak of Cholera confirmed by the Ministry of health by September 2022.
The political and security scene remains complex since fighting and insecurity are continuing which will cause further civilian movement. The situation is leading to an increase in the number of spontaneous returns by IDPs.
Aleppo Governorate was one of the most populous Syrian governorates and was known as the beating heart of the industry and commerce, that before the civil war that occurred and destructed cities and displaced the majority of its population.
Aleppo is one of the governorates in Syria where Dorcas started working in relief since 2016 and has several ongoing projects including the livelihood projects that were implemented since 2018.
In Aleppo, the situation is more or less stable although, it is targeted by rockets from time to time because it is impacted by the situation of Idleb.
The situation continues to get worse financially for the households, and the burden of daily-life expenses was the main challenge, in addition to the displacement movement from many rural-Aleppo areas and other cities to Aleppo. Job opportunities and financial capacities were not enough to cover all the increased needs especially with the unstable exchange rate, the increase of US sanctions. The UN currently estimates that on average, almost 10,000 individuals are displaced each day, mainly from Aleppo and Idlib Provinces. Those who are displaced are facing a protection and poverty crisis, Based on HNO 2022 report, there are 14,6 million people in need in Syria, and 2.7 million people in need in Aleppo.
Based on the above and adding the successful Dorcas intervention and the good impact and feedback of the Livelihood project from the previous years, Dorcas continued its Livelihood Project activities in Aleppo thus to reach more vulnerable families through providing empowerment, professional skills, and supporting and developing various enterprises.
The project will help to re-establish both urban and rural livelihoods for conflict-affected households in Aleppo to revitalize local markets devastated by the conflict. It will also provide awareness sessions for the entrepreneurs, farmers and livestock owners on protection topics such as awareness on the gendered discriminatory practices, laws or cultural norms, etc.
The project will support business owners to scale up their business, it will be through providing new equipment / materials, raw materials, maintenance for their shops, maintenance of equipment, etc.. .
The project aims to create a positive effect within the communities served, leading to more purchasing power, and the potential to kickstart the micro-economy within the area by rebuilding businesses. The project will help beneficiaries supplement their household incomes and make their livelihoods more resilient against shocks and crises by supporting livelihood diversification and strengthening.
Dorcas reached beneficiaries through different strategies, including but not limited, Dorcas’ community centers, direct contacts and visits to the market and shops, churches and local NGOs,… and as a result of the 5 years’ livelihood work in the area, the needs are still enormous, and the difficulties are huger beside the challenges that occur unexpectedly (cholera outbreak/ Ukraine War...)

3 - Needs

According to the 2022 HNO report, there is 14,6 million people in need in Syria, and 2.7 million people in need in Aleppo- with needs increasingly being exacerbated by economic decline. The decade-long crisis has inflicted immense suffering on the civilian population who have experienced massive and systematic violations of international humanitarian and human rights law, for the past 3 years, it has been coupled with a global pandemic the COVID-19 adding the Cholera outbreak (September2022) that amplified the needs sparked by the ongoing conflict and has brought the need of accessible and quality living conditions.The situation continues to worsen financially & economically, the daily-life expenses is a burden, in addition to the displacement movement from many rural-Aleppo areas and other cities to Aleppo. Job opportunities and financial capacities were not enough to cover all the increased needs. Based on all of the mentioned above, the continuous monitoring of the market, the instable rate of the Syrian pound versus $, the sanctions, the decrease of the export & import activities, the unstable availability of raw materials, Dorcas long waiting lists of beneficiaries requesting support to their businesses, stakeholders, communities committees, the increase of prices in addition to Dorcas participation to the sector meeting... reveal that the needs are still huge and insufficient to support entrepreneurs and allow them to generate income and to live with dignity.

4 - Implementation of activities

The implementation always start with a set of criteria: The beneficiaries of this project are working men & women in Aleppo Syria, trying to survive the Syrian economic crisis and civil war by working to generate incomes in order to support themselves and their dependents, they are equally men & women, married, single, widowed or divorced aged between 18 & 64 years, preferably having (or a member of the dependent) medical conditions, willing to work and have sufficient skills in the business.The data is collected from Dorcas community centers, stakeholders, partners, outreach, communities' committees.. followed by individual interviews, field visits, studies of the social files, 1st selection, feasibility studies and beneficiaries' knowledge and skills, offers. Furthermore, evaluation of the projects by the livelihood team jointly with the beneficiaries, followed by the evaluation of an impartial committee to ensure the transparency and the objectivity. The execution phase is done jointly by Dorcas livelihood team with the beneficiary keeping a critical eye on the market and prices to ensure the maximum benefit of the beneficiaries. The execution is not the final step in the implementation activities, it is followed by a 6 months follow-up by Dorcas expert team to ensure the continuity of the small business, without neglecting the continuous presence of the monitoring and evaluation team to ensure the proper follow-up of the process and the respect of CHS.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

While planning and carrying out activities, special attention will be paid to ensure that gender, age and diversity are mainstreamed throughout the project. Dorcas maintains a gender- and conflict-sensitive perspective in designing activities, and will identify beneficiaries in a participatory approach through: consultations with beneficiaries, internal discussions and capacity building of staff. All Dorcas staff will be trained on the internal Code of Conduct and asked to sign it and be compliant to it in addition to the CP and PSEA policies. Beneficiary data will be collected and segregated by age, sex and gender whenever possible. Male and female protection staff will be involved throughout the project cycle to ensure that urgent protection issues are addressed and to provide beneficiaries the opportunity to raise any concerns. Dorcas has a strong experience in delivering protection programs and its team is well trained on Case Management SOPs and minimum standards.Dorcas' interventions will be guided by the non-discrimination principle to ensure direct access to services for the most vulnerable groups that will be identified through rigorous assessment.Internal mechanisms to report misconduct will be established


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Dorcas and the partner staff are well trained and continuously monitored. Policies and procedures in addition to a complaint mechanism with complaint box, hotline, flyers, pamphlets, etc.. are in place to ensure that the beneficiaries are not exposed to any risk and in order to handle any complaint confidently.Dorcas/partner are well-known & trusted; individuals and families understand that Dorcas has the ability to provide a multitude of protection services, as well as many humanitarian services like health and others. Working in conversation with the community, Dorcas develops many proposals and activities based on information received from its feedback systems and the needs of the community expressed by those coming into the center. Dorcas’ two-way beneficiary feedback system was established to ensure that beneficiary populations have a voice in the planning, activity implementation, and evaluation of the project. Feedback and suggestions are listened to as part of the protection risk monitoring and subsequently included in the implementation. This also includes a process to confidentially handle allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse in an effort to ensure that beneficiaries are protected against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Dorcas’ long term presence within the community also ensures that the most vulnerable, such as the elderly, children, PWD, and widows are reached through its services.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Operational risks: 1.) Covid-19 and Cholera outbreaks could impact the project. Mitigations: Dorcas has standardized SOPs in case of a confirmed case of Covid-19/ Cholera in project locations and will continue to closely monitor all reports and recommendations from WHO, and other stakeholder in addition to the precautionary measures taken by Dorcas/partners to prevent risks through provision of PPE materials to staff and beneficiaries but also awareness campaign, posters..Security risks: The security situation in Aleppo is almost stable Mitigations: Dorcas will follow the ongoing protocols it has been using for the past years by working hand-in-hand with local partners to gain access and stay up to date regarding any changes to the security environment and will follow the protocols setFinancial risks: - Fluctuation in the exchange rate of the SYP seriously impacts budget expenditure and planning, - Fraud, bribery - Movement of cash at risk of theft.Mitigations: -Dorcas is monitoring the fluctuations and making sure, as much as possible, that will not affect the implementation-Internal financial audit is continuous beside the staff trainings-Minimize the cash as much as possible - The participation and involvement of the beneficiaries in all the projects phases and steps.
