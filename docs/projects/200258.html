Project Summary

It is estimated that around 7,500 women and adolescent girls will be reached with a comprehensive package of GBV response services (including case management, focused PSS and legal assistance and better access to shelter, emergency, and recurrent cash assistance). 38,000 will benefit of community outreach initiatives, non-focused PSS activities, including life skills sessions, vocational training, and dignity kits distribution.
UNFPA will focus on the following interventions:
- GBV Service Delivery
1) Support access to survivor centered multi-sectoral services for women and girls at risk and survivors of GBV through Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS). UNFPA will continue to support the operations of existing women and girls’ safe spaces and support GBV service provision integrated into primary health care centers. UNFPA has a cost-effective model of GBV and SRH integration and will support partners in ensuring comprehensive provision of GBV services out of PHCs
2) Provide psychosocial support to survivors of GBV and individuals at risk including
psychological counselling, case management, focused and non-focused group psychosocial support.
3) Provide legal counselling and representation for survivors of GBV (including survivors of domestic violence and survivor of sexual harassment and exploitation)
4) Provide operational (rent, furniture) and technical support to existing safe shelters (NABAA, ABAAD) and/or establish new ones that meet minimum standards for optimal care of survivors in need of safe shelters. Shelters for survivors are an essential component of a comprehensive and coordinated GBV response. They provide survivors with the opportunity to distance themselves from abusive relationships and consider their choices for a life free from violence. The budget required to support the shelter is 130,000 USD (80,000 USD operational and 50,000 USD technical)
5) Provide emergency protection Cash Assistance to survivors of GBV and individuals at risk of violence and imminent harm. GBV Emergency Cash Assistance aims at addressing a situation that exposes an individual to immediate serious GBV harm /risk and/or addressed its consequences. It can be used to access services that are not provided free of charge. The provision of ECA is a one-time intervention intended to address the critical needs of a GBV survivor. The Recurrent Cash Assistance aims at ceasing or mitigating risk of gender-based violence, including to sexual harassment, exploitation, or abuse on the longer term.
6) Administer Client Feedback surveys for individuals receiving case management interventions. The client feedback surveys are a keyway for us to know how survivors experienced the case management service. This can help us understand what is being done well, what needs to be improved and what the challenges are. Although specifically designed for case management, if needed, the tool can be slightly adapted and administered with beneficiaries receiving counseling services as well. Client feedback surveys are usually implemented at the end of a session or at the closing of a case.
- Outreach and GBV prevention and risk mitigation:
1) Engage community members in safety audits exercises to identify GBV risks and mitigation measures and disseminate the GBV referral pathways.
2) Organize activities tailored to the different needs of vulnerable and marginalized groups including empowerment life skills activities and vocational training with a focus on adolescent girls.
3) Engage community in outreach activities and information sessions to raise awareness on GBV services, risks, women’s rights, gender related legal frameworks as well as on menstrual hygiene management addressing period poverty through distribution of dignity kits.
- Capacity development:
1) Train GBV service providers staff on GBV prevention and response along with mentoring sessions, Train non –specialized GBV staff on GBV risk mitigation.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Activities will be tailored to the specific needs of women and girls of different age, nationalities, cultural background, sexual and gender orientation. Needs of women and girls with disabilities will be factored into the project to extend the access to services to all the segments of the vulnerable targets. Safety audits and community consultations will be regularly held to identify risks within targeted communities and to engage women, girls, men, and boys in identifying plans to respond to the identified risks. UNFPA will conduct regular beneficiary satisfaction surveys to continuously monitor and ensure quality of services and community receptive to the services. Monitoring activities will include a section on protection risks and PSEA that will aim at maintaining a close relation with communities supported and regular collect their feedback. Moreover, the cash component has a solid Post distribution monitoring that assess protection outcomes of the intervention.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

UNFPA operates according to the Five Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations (Leadership and governance, Transparency, Feedback and complaints, Participation, Design, monitoring and evaluation). UNFPA’s integrated, participatory approach relies on the engagement of local actors to ensure appropriate implementation of all interventions. For instance, UNFPA and its implementing partners will ensure that all members of the targeted communities are involved in the various interventions related to prevention and response activities. Women and girls are key actors in their own protection, and it is critical that they are active partners in identifying protection risks and solutions. UNFPA will engage with women, girls, boys, and men from the affected communities to explain protection activities, facilitate community acceptance and address barriers to women's and girls’ attendance, and facilitate strengthening of community based complaints mechanisms including for PSEA.
