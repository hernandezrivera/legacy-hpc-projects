Project Summary

HOPE is planning to work on improving access to WASH services for the Vulnerable population in localities of Um Dukhun (CD), Assalaya (ED), As Sunta (SD), Al Fashaga (Gadaref), and Abu Hujar (Sennar) that will contribute to 3 HRP WASH sector objectives for 2021.
In this project, HOPE will focus to achieve the objectives of (1) 36,000 the crisis-affected population will have access to basic water services, (2) 14,86 crisis-affected populations will have access to adequate (basic or limited), protected, and gender-sensitive sanitation services, and (3) 40,000 crisis-affected populations will have access to hygiene promotion services, water quality, and environmental health interventions.
HOPE will consider accountability to affected people through full community participation, strengthen the current community mechanism/ committee of water committees and hygiene promotors as well as do follow up for all benefit targeted separately to assess the project, identify challenges and provide give recommendations to improve the project outcomes.

1 - Needs

In target localities, compared to a gap, the WASH interventions are very weak including a shortage of clean water drinking, deterioration of sanitation & hygiene; no latrines in most villages; open defecation is widely practiced increasing, drinking water is suspected to be contaminated meanly in the rainy season, no vector control was in place; increasing of flies and mosquitos was reported. The WASH severity for 5 localities is 4 or 5, the People In Need (PIN) are 103,779 in Um Dukhun (CD), 39,995 in Assalaya (ED), 7,386 in As Sunta (SD), 5,732 in Al Fashaga (Gadaref), and 20,355 in Abu Hujar (Sennar) including 62,788 IDPs in Um Dukhun and 14,158 in Assalaya. The PIN/ target disaggregated to 41% male, 59% female, 8% elderly, 63% children, and 29 adults.The nu of appropriate hand-washing events identified out of 5 times is 2.21 in Um Dukhun, 1.78 in Assalaya, 2.02 in Al Fashaga, 2.21 Abu Hujar, and 3.20 in As Sunta, 4 out of 5 targeted localities the numbers are less to 50% this is a critical situation will be l to health deterioration for community people. Percentage of Uses basic drinking water sources (within 30 minutes) is 68.76 in Um Dukhun, 24.83 in Assalaya, 67.70 in Al Fashaga, 53.60 Abu Hujar, and 12.19 in As Sunta, so As Sunta, Assalaya and Abu Hujar localities classified are most in need to support in providing basic drinking water. Percentage of Open defecThe main communicable diseases and outbreaks that related to the deterioration of WASH services of vector control, solid waste management, and contamination of drinking water in the target locality are Malaria, AWD, Fever, and Schistosomiasis as well as all localities at risk for COVID-19 due to the bad health system, lack of communities practices in term of handwashing and wearing face-masks in a crowded area such as a market or when visit health facilities. In targeted localities of Darfur, there is a high risk to expand the last outbreaks of Chikungunya, and Dengue Fever that is happening in the border states of South and West Darfur.In Darfur, the inter-communal, inter-tribal conflicts, and violence that is happened in the last years which are affected the safety and security of people exacerbating the risk of sexual violence especially when displaced. Women and girls continue to face the risk of gender-based violence including sexual violence when they leave the villages. Fear of reprisals from attackers often prevents rape survivors from seeking medical help. The conflict in Darfur has also increased the number of women who are the sole providers for their families. Al Fashaga of Gadaref is suffering from poor WASH services and a deterioration in security due to the frequent attacks of the Ethiopian Shifta armed gangs, especially in the agricultural season, and in October 2020 the arrival of Ethiopian refugees fleeing the fighting in Ethiopia began, as well as hosted more than 15,000 refugees and expected increasing the new refugee's arrival, so there is an urgent need to improve the WASH services of the host communities. Abu Hujar of Sennar is also suffering from poor WASH services, in last year, registered the highest cases of Cholera and AWD due to a deterioration of WASH services, lack of sanitation, and hygiene practices by community people.Sudan also faces issues with more chronic levels of malnutrition with one in every three children under the age of five years affected. These children will suffer from more long term developmental, WASH impact resulting in both a shorter stature than non-affected peers as well as a significant impact on the development of the brain resulting in long term impact on IQ, learning potential, educational outcomes as well as poorer WASH in the immediate and longer-term future. As a result, these children will have lower lifetime earnings and lower productivity as adults, influencing the economic growth and potential of the entire country. Due to the above situation, HOPE looks for improving access to WASH services.

6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

During design, HOPE assessed the safety and security for target areas as well as types of GBV that facing women. During implementation, HOPE will support the capacities of committee members and assist to know their rights to WASH. All essential drugs at the pharmacy will be handled with the utmost care and storage - warehouses will be clean to avoid contamination. This project will include psycho-social stimulation activities for infants and young children will also be provided with psychosocial support to help mothers deal with their own distress and improve care for their children. Child protection messages on prevention and response as well as referral mechanisms will be included in activities related to WASH outreach and awareness-raising. All WASH volunteers will be trained on referral procedures and mechanisms linking patients directly to experts from other agencies and the government is already supporting the WASH sector. Children and women will be given priorities. Privacy for HH shared latrines will be considering. Awareness-raising during community mobilization on WASH, education a key component that HOPE has put in place for better understanding among the beneficiaries for self-protection on various programmatic matters. HOPE also will develop a system to collect feedback from beneficiaries to measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.
