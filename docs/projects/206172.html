Project Summary

An estimated 7.8 million people are likely to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2023
lean season (April – July).The acute food insecurity in South Sudan remains worrying with the latest data showing that approximately 5.83 million people – almost half of South Sudan’s population (46 percent) – are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) between September to November 2023 . while 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in South Sudan are also classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). A further 1.64 million people are in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). The main drivers of FSL needs include covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks such as floods and dry spells, inflation and rising food prices, continued disruption of livelihoods due to conflict and insecurity and household-level idiosyncratic stressors, like death of the breadwinner, GBV, morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene and the lack of access to essential basic services. Food insecurity is greatest when these shocks and stressors are compounded by the persistent high levels of vulnerability and poverty associated with the protracted crisis since December 2013. The situation progressively worsened over time, reaching its peak in 2022 and projected to continue into 2023. With heavy local presence, with ongoing projects and as static in these Locations; TITI Foundation aims to target 24,000 most vulnerable HH with Nutrition, Food Security and Livelihood response to increase agricultural production and build community resilience for the vulnerable food insecure households through a multi-sector integrated response to prevent catastrophic consequences. TITI Foundation in response to the ongoing emergency will improve the general availability of food and strengthen vulnerable communities’ ability to withstand future threats associated with massive displacements. The project will continue to promote alternative farming techniques through the capacity building of contact farmers. working with CAD (County Agricultural Departments, training of VAC (Village agricultural Committees), farmers groups on conservation agriculture and value chain addition to increase crop production coverage and minimize production costs. Farmers' groups will be organized and supported to continue developing community food banks, gunny bags gardening, kitchen gardens and post-harvest handling practices to enhance food security for vulnerable households. This will assist them to have the capacity to process farm produce and identify potential markets for their farm produce. TITI Foundation will work with strategic partners to provide inputs including early maturing vegetable seeds and tools to farmers in the target locations with an aim to restore food security, production of vegetable variety, fishing to enhance nutritional diversification and production capacity while maintaining high levels of accountability to affected persons through participatory engagement. In addition, TITI Foundation will support mechanized agricultural practices.
Value addition to increasing employment among the youths and women; improve agronomics practices; integrate fishing and apiary practices; Provide food assistance, this will limit household food expenses and ease the burden. TITI Foundation will also ease pressure on the elderly, women, Girls, PWDs to acquire Psychosocial support, child-headed household protection and GBV. During the project implementation, TITI Foundation (MEAL) teams will conduct field visits, PDMs after every distribution and progress reports in the selected locations. Strengthen Complaint and Feedback Mechanism to enhance accountability for the affected communities. This will involve key informants' interviews in the various communities and focus group discussions to determine the efficiency and effectiveness of the intervention.

1. Needs

An estimated 7.8 million people Area are likely to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2023 lean season (April – July). The acute food insecurity in South Sudan remains worrying with the latest data showing that approximately 5.83 million people – almost half of South Sudan’s population (46 percent) – are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse) between September to November 2023. while 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in South Sudan are also classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). Food insecurity is highest in Jonglei, where 79 per cent of the population will likely face severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) at the peak of the lean season in 2023, followed by Unity (75 per cent), Warrap (70 per cent), Northern Bahr El Ghazal (66 per cent), Lakes (61 per cent) and Upper Nile (60 per cent). The main drivers of FSL needs include covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks such as floods and dry spells, inflation and rising food prices, continued disruption of livelihoods due to conflict and insecurity and household-level idiosyncratic stressors, like death of the breadwinner, GBV, morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene and the lack of access to essential basic services. Food insecurity is greatest when these shocks and stressors are compounded by the persistent high levels of vulnerability and poverty associated with the protracted crisis since December 2013. The situation progressively worsened over time, reaching its peak in 2023. Food consumption gaps are extreme, and people have largely exhausted emergency coping strategies. In 2023, the precarious situation will be further exacerbated by the country’s increasing humanitarian caseload, the expected increase in the duration of needs in the most affected areas, and the projected further reduction in humanitarian assistance across the country. (HNO 2023). The Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) sectoral analysis identified an increase in the severity and extent of food insecurity through the use of Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System Plus (FSNMS+) outcome indicator data, FSL assessment reports (including over 100 pieces of evidence), and other information and analysis by colleagues from WFP, FAO, FEWSNET, REACH, IRNA's and the Food Security Cluster. In response to the needs, beneficiary selection and registration will be done in participation with displaced & host communities and will be carried out by TF in joint committees to strengthen feedback and accountability mechanisms. This will be verified, and information provided to the community at every point. TF will work closely with the financial service provider (FSP) to carry out the distributions; procurement of tools and seeds; carry out agronomics and mechanized agricultural practices; establish a complaints desk to analyze all risks carefully prior to, during and after distributions; conduct post-distribution monitoring exercises. TF will mainstream and strengthen support for PSS, protection and GBV services, FES (fuel-efficient stoves and briquettes), extension services, VDCs, CAD and referral pathways. Titi will also coordinate its activities and collaborate with partners in the ground such as IOM, SAADO, CCOSS and ACFwho will extend services of WASH, S/NFI and nutrition to our beneficiaries to complement our FSL activities.
