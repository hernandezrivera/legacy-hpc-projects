Project Summary

The Project aims to provide in-kind food assistance to the vulnerable CAR refugees households through unconditional food distributions. The project will support targeted 61,200 CAR refugees to have access to adequate and nutritious food, therefore prevent them from adopting negative coping practices. Food assistance will be provided for 12 months The full ration of the in-kind distributions will provide 2,100 kcal energy per day per person for the beneficiaries who rely entirely on humanitarian food assistance. the food rations comprise 350g cereals 100g pulses 35g vegetable oil and 5g salt.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The socio-political crisis in CAR since March 2003 has forced many Central Africans to seek refuge in in Cameroon. As Cameroon is sharing about 800 km of border with CAR and also the cross-border ethnic affinities between the two countries, Thousands of refugees fled to Cameroon. According to UNHCR (November 2019), 292,863 CAR refugees from different arrival waves (2003, 2008 and 2014) are settled in the East, Adamawa, and North regions, mainly in the rural areas. About 30% of these refugees live in 7 refugee sites (Ngarissingo, Lolo, Mbile, Timangolo, Gado, Borgop and Ngam,) while the remaining 70% live in more than 100 localities across 23 councils in these three regions. This refugee population is largely made up of children, young people and women, from various ethnic groups including Mbororo, Gbaya, Haoussa, Kolé, Kako, Foulbé, Mbimo, Mvon-Mvon and Yanguéré. The demographic pressure resulting form the presence of the CAR refugees has disrupted the economic and social order of the three regions, and put additional pressure on already scarce resources, resulting in increased vulnerabilities, and humanitarian-peace-development challenges. The JAM conducted from November 2018 to May 2019 revealed that 81% of refugees are highly vulnerable about 58.9% of refugees are in need for immediate food assistance, out of which 38.9% need livelihood support. Overall food consumption is acceptable among refugees (63 percent) and host communities (79 percent), with distributed food consumption patterns across regions. Adamawa is the worst-off region (35.7 percent of households consume inadequate diets) followed by North (33.8 percent) and East (31.2 percent). Despite this stability, the refugee households rely on unstable food sources, mainly food assistance, humanitarian cash transfers and gifts. Consumption from own production is low-conversely, dependence on markets is high for both refugees and host communities, which raises questions on the purchasing power of these households and their capacity to access to food.The presence of CAR refugees has greatly increased pressures on natural resources and basic social infrastructure that were already precarious. Competition over scarce resources and the consequent tensions between refugees and host communities, farmers and herders have led to an increase in food needs, as well as a deterioration in the provision of basic social services and severe environmental degradation. Despite the achievements on the ground, the multifaceted assistance provided by the Government of Cameroon and humanitarian actors is still far from optimal to ensure sustainable food security and basic needs for this population. This is due to several factors, including the persistence of insecurity in CAR (resulting in continuous arrivals of new refugees) and the worldwide multiple crises which have shifted donors’ interest from the situation in CAR. This has led to a decline in resources for CAR refugees in Cameroon.
