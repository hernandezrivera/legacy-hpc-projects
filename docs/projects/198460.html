Project Summary

Provision of Emergency Shelters and Non-Food Items to the Most Vulnerable and Crisis-Affected People in camps and host communities in Jere and Maiduguri LGAs of Borno state, has taken a new phase in its implementation through planned activities from month 1st January to 31st December 2023. In designing the work plan, the team had to take a number of factors into consideration including the security situation on ground, arrangement of logistical support and acute time pressure in implementation of activities.

1 - Needs

As the Borno state government began the closure of some camps in Maiduguri and with the preannouncement of total closure by 31st Dec 2021. The influx of new arrivals into already overcrowded camps and communities has increased the risk of illness spreading, particularly cholera and COVID-19. CCCM and shelter partners are struggling to secure additional land to expand and decongest the camps. Shelter and NFI kits stockpiled across field locations have been exhausted. This has impacted the capacity for timely response, especially for sudden needs. Due to funding shortfalls, Sector partners are struggling to meet the increasing shelter and NFI needs, particularly in hard-to-reach locations.The monthly CCCM/Shelter/NFI SITREP report for past months – June to Oct 2022 already indicated the huge gap in emergency shelters and NFIs.Identified needs and gaps Within the period of January – March 2022 according to DTM it is estimated that 327,993 HHs need shelter and NFI assistance. In addition, June to October 2022 report of 5W Matrix, Site Tracker DTM, FTS, Nigeria also shows that 2,945,848 are in dare need of shelter and NFI.The assessment has it that the cause of the gaps is diverse but includes; increase as a result of new arrivals, fire outbreaks in some camps, and recent rain/windstorms during the rainy season that just finished.During the reporting period, the number of people requiring shelter assistance has increased. This rise is driven by the destruction and the deterioration of shelter conditions in camps, camp-like settings, and host communities and compounded by the scale of new displacement, protracted displacement, return movements, and a minimal shelter response across Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe state. These conditions increase protection and public health risks, particularly when combined with overcrowding and inadequate access to Shelter and NFI responses across all need locations. These shelters and the NFI situation will continue to deteriorate due to living in unsustainable settlements with poor access to basic services. A total of 107,903 HHs according to the sector site tracker and DTM data are in dire need of an adequate shelter solution. In addition, ­fire outbreaks occurred in 8 LGAs, damaging 3,853 shelters in total and resulting in a significant loss of properties for the affected IDPs, and immediate shelter and NFI assistance are required.The gap is most likely to increase as camp closure in Maiduguri commences and the movement into the already congested shelters and damage of NFIs while on transit to their new locations.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

GREENCODE will continue to ensure that protection is mainstreamed into all its activities as noted in the previous sections. The mandate of our humanitarian intervention is rooted in the 'Do NO HARM’ Principle, also GREENCODE approach to protection is governed by the SPHERE Standards and Protection Principles, the Core Humanitarian Standards.Protection concerns are considered in the planning of all Shelter/NFIs activities in order to ensure that any risk of harm, abuse, and sexual exploitation faced by the target population is mitigated. Furthermore, GREENCODE has a protection officer that will be working alongside another project officer in monitoring the gender and protection markers as well as handling all Protection related activities.As part of achieving this protection mainstreaming, we have planned for community outreach using FGDs, IEC, and a large gathering of community members to talk about protection, GREENCODE will closely with the Protection sector and other partners to closely monitor the identification of protection cases in order to adequately serve groups at risk, such as youth, female¬ headed households, people living with disability and the most vulnerable. Through protection referral systems, GREENCODE will continue to conduct special NFI distributions for, especially vulnerable people. On days of distributions provision for shades, mate, biscuits of local snacks and drinking water will be provided to women and children.In addition, protection/mine awareness sessions will be organized for large numbers of community members on a monthly basis, while about 5% of the project budget will go into protection activities from the start of the project to the end.All activities will be decentralized so that protection concerns voiced by women related to the risk of GBV in the displacement sites are taken into consideration and referred to other actors with expertise in GBV. Women, men, girls, boys, and persons with specific needs will be consulted at all stages of the Shelter/NFIs intervention.In addition, GREENCODE will adopt and deploy all Sector COVID-19 strategies in the implementation of this project to protect Target beneficiaries from Corona virus. Some of those protection strategies will include the inclusion of COVID-19 prevention kits among the NFI package, provision of handwashing stations during all the project activities, use of hand sanitizers for both staff and beneficiaries, use of face masks, maintaining social/physical distancing, as well as door to door distribution, etc.

GREENCODE places a high premium on Accountability, especially to the affected population. In GREENCODE we conduct and value two ways of accountability (the Upward accountability and the Downward accountability) - Upward from target beneficiaries to GREENCODE and to the Donor, while in downward accountability from Donor to GREENCODE and to target beneficiaries and other stakeholders.In guarantying accountability, GREENCODE will undertake a conscious effort to share project information with stakeholders, also project beneficiaries (women, men, girls, and boys, the aged, and persons with physical disabilities within target communities) will be at the heart of all the project activities and participate too.GREENCODE is fundamentally committed to ensuring accountability to communities and installing a rigorous beneficiaries and other stakeholders feedback mechanism, which is part of the program collected through Focus Group Discussion and also linking with other clusters who have set up desks in the biggest sites. GREENCODE has placed high value and premium on humanitarian accountability. The proposed project is highly gender and age¬ sensitive, considering gender equality for effective programming with assurance that all affected members of the target population have equal access to services and the targeted actions is based on gender and age analysisBeneficiary selection criteria will be jointly agreed upon by the target groups, while distribution standards through a Banner or billboard will be made accessible to the community. A help desk will be set up at the distribution point, which will be able to provide more background and contact information of GREENCODE.Participation: Discussions with beneficiaries will be done during project monitoring and evaluation to make sure that their voices and feedback are heard. Also, they will be involved in project activities such as land clearing, mapping/layout, and selection criteria, as well as provision of security,
