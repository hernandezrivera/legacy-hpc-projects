Project Summary

The general context in South Sudan is summarized, based on the Real Time Review (RTR) for the South Sudan Joint Response 4 report 4th October 2018, as that the humanitarian crisis has continued to intensify and expand. As a result of the compounding effects of widespread conflict and violence and a deteriorating economy, 7 million people – more than one in two across the country – need sustained humanitarian assistance. For most of the counties in Northern Bahr El Ghazal, according to Fews Net (http://fews.net/east-africa/south-sudan), the IPC level in June-September 2018 is 4, indicating emergency levels of food insecurity. Based on the recent FSNMS round 20 conducted in 2017 inducted the GAM rate of 22%, SAM rate at 7.7%, MAM rate at 14.3%and PLW prevalence at 28.4% for Aweil North and Aweil West. A significant increase in the provision of humanitarian assistance, above planned levels, is needed to prevent more households from falling in to Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). This is particularly the case in North- and West Aweil areas. This situation is attributed to, among others, to difficult access especially in the rainy season. Save the Children reported in the Gurtong newspaper on 18th October 2018 that more than six million people currently need urgent food assistance, including more than one million children. Dorcas nutrition intervention will assist the suffering people in the two counties. The objectives for Nutrition: Deliver timely, life-saving management of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable and at risk, SAM/MAM U5 children, PLW. Awareness raising on SAM/MAM and IYCF/MIYCN best practices. Missing details in this proposal will be filled in during the full proposal stage when the security situation allows.

Needs

Based on the recent Integrated Food Security Phase Classification IPC analysis (September 2018-March 2019), Most of the counties Northern Bahr el Ghazal State reported critical levels of acute malnutrition (GAM (WHZ) 15.0-29.9%), which is above the World Health Organization (WHO) emergency threshold of 15 per cent.The state is classified as IPC level 4. The humanitarian crisis has continued to intensify and expand. As a result of the compounding effects of widespread conflict and violence and a deteriorating economy, 7 million people – more than one in two across the country – need sustained humanitarian assistance. Hunger and malnutrition have reached record levels, and 1.75 million people are now on the brink of a catastrophe. More than 1.1 million children under the age of five were forecast to be malnourished in 2018, including nearly 300,000 severely malnourished and at a heightened riskof death. This makes 2018 the toughest year on record. This is particularly the case in North- and West Aweil areas. This situation is attributed to, among others, to difficult access, especially in the rainy season. As a consequence, little assistance is reaching the vulnerable population. Save the Children reported (Gurtong on 18th October 2018, Posted in: Home, Humanitarian) that more than six million people currently need urgent food assistance, including more than one million children. As the lean season (when food stocks are low) begins, 270,000 children in South Sudan are severely malnourished (SAM) and at risk of starvation. Some 20,000 could be expected to die from extreme hunger before the end of 2018. “Malnourished children have substantially reduced immune systems and are at least three times more likely to contract and die from diseases like cholera and pneumonia than healthy children,” says Deidre Keogh, Save the Children’s Country Director in South Sudan. “Without urgent funding to increase and maintain humanitarian services, many children are in danger of dying.” The revitalized peace agreement signed in September provides hope for millions of children to be protected and thrive if implemented effectively. The level of emergency malnutrition is in addition to insecurity and related limited access, understood to be attributed to severe food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition services, high morbidity, extremely poor diets and poor sanitation and hygiene. Due to the prevailing access situation and few (I) NGO’s working in Aweil North and Aweil West little precise information is available on these Counties. During the full proposal writing stage, Dorcas will assess in detail the context the status for each proposed sector

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project activities will be implemented to in a manner that addresses inequalities or protection concerns, our staff will be trained on the do no harm approach to ensure they understand the concept in implementation to avoid causing harm to beneficiaries. The involvement of project management committees in the implementation will ensure that beneficiaries are given voices and in the implementation through joint planning and reviews they can raise concern on activities that are a threat or causes threat. An internal audit by the project on protection concerns will be conducted by the monitoring and evaluation team on a quarterly basis to help address any concerns that may arise during the implementation.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project plan monitoring measures on access of beneficiaries to provided services and will make sure that all people are having same access potential. In some situations where the beneficiaries have access challenges the project will seek to move services closer to the beneficiaries for example having mobile screening and provision of nutrition services closer to the people rather than having it a fixed place where access or reach may be difficult. A detailed location and distance mapping will be established to know the far distance beneficiaries come from and being children who are the main beneficiaries including lactating and pregnant mothers knowledge of their access is important for effective implementation.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project will monitor safety of beneficiaries through village committees and meeting with them. Individual cases will be followed up and solutions proposed. The project management committees and community leaders will be helpful to ensure that community safety is ensured and appropriate measures are taken by the community and the project to ensure safety of project beneficiaries.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Specific monitoring will be conducted on monthly basis to find out the measure of project implementation dignity to beneficiaries is perceived by the beneficiaries themselves the findings will inform the project implementation to incorporate any changes. Through the feedback mechanism and also discussions during meetings with mothers and caretakers feedback in regard to dignity will be found out and any issues will be addressed there and then. Otherwise the project will ensure that dignity of beneficiaries is respected and adhered to in implementation of this project and particularly working with children and mothers (Pregnant and lactating) will require absolute support for upholding dignity of beneficiaries to the maximum standard.
