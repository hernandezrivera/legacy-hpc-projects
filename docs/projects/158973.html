Project Summary

The intervention will improve access to drinking water for social and economically vulnerable Households (HHs) and improve hygienic conditions and practices at poor HHs in 6 communities in the Gaza strip. The interventions will respond to the shortage of household drinking water through improving drinking water storage capacity for 1,000 vulnerable households. Moreover, the proposed interventions aim to improve access to hygiene services through rehabilitation of sanitation facilities inside 300 homes and 40 KGs, including adaptation of WASH HH sanitation facilities for PWDs. In order to ensure the best use of the interventions and ensure the sustainability and the logic of the whole project, the aforementioned interventions will be complemented through the hygiene promotion activities such as awareness activities and distribution of hygiene kits to the most vulnerable girls, boys, women, men, elders and people with disabilities. The hygiene promotion activities will prioritize girls and women needs, including menstrual hygiene management (MHM).
The project will target the vulnerable HHs in Bani sohailah, Al Qarara/Khan Younis, Al Bayuk/Rafah, Dair Albalah/Middle Area, Al Qararh and waadi Gaza/Gaza. The proposed intervention will directly target 14400 persons (6981 children and 7419 adults - 7073 F and 7327 M).

1 - Needs

The WASH situation in Gaza continues to deteriorate. The severe electricity crisis, the Israeli blockade and repeated offensive and the lack of maintenance for public WASH facilities led to significant shortage of WASH services and increase the environmental risk for the entire population of Gaza. Moreover, the increasing of poverty rate (53% FSS-HNO-2020) contributed to undermining the ability of poor to secure their basic needs including drinking water and hygienic materials (including menstrual hygiene management). As part of its emergency intervention, SCI has conducted a socio-economic assessment in North Gaza and eastern Khan Younis for 1,995 poor households (HHs) The assessment revealed that the average household income is NIS 618 (US$177) per month in North Gaza and only NIS 495 (US$141) per month in Eastern Khan Younis. The low HHs income in the surveyed areas together with the shortage in water leads to poor hygienic conditions of the HHs and increased their vulnerability to health risk. A SCI gender and WASH assessment (2019) as part of the current emergency WASH intervention in 8 communities in eastern Khan Younis, Rafah, Wadi Gaza and Dair Albalah found that limited public services, economic vulnerability and limited hygiene awareness negatively affect household ability to meet basic hygiene needs. FGDs conducted with male and female adult beneficiaries (31 F; 34 M) identified a lack for potable water, limited access to water and sanitation facilities and hygiene materials (household and MHM materials) as main concerns. Families do not have access to soap and clean water, resorting to using lemons and sand to clean their hands. Limited access has health implications, with reports from caregivers of increases in water-borne illnesses such as diarrhoea and giardiasis. Women and girls must use alternative materials, such as diapers, to manage menstruation due to the cost associated with MHM items. Limited clean supplies and water can contribute to gynaecological health issues. The results of Emergency Repairs of Essential Infrastructure Utilities in the Gaza Strip conducted by WASH cluster in August 2019 revealed that 55% of participants in FGD (57% men and 42% women) stated that they noted unusual disease symptoms such as skin, diarrhoea, and giardiasis that affected their children under the age of 5 years in the previous six weeks, especially in Khan Younis and Rafah areas. 33% noticed frequent diseases in adults and women at their homes in the previous 4 weeks.Water collection has an added child protection concerns, with young boys primarily tasked to walk long distances, along busy roads to collect water. Caregivers and children expressed concern that they would be hit by vehicles, attacked by animals or injured in altercations at water collection points. Moreover, children in KGs are more vulnerable to adverse health effects from lack of adequate sanitation facilities and face difficulties to use kindergartens’ toilets that are inadequate and do not provide privacy. IRPAL needs assessment in July 2019 highlighted the need for improving health environment for kindergartens and maintaining bathrooms, adapt them to include facilities for PWDs and separate male bathrooms from female bathrooms as priority to improve the educational environment in KGs.The results of the multi-sectoral assessment conducted by SCI- WFP-UNICEF in 2018 showed that 25% of the surveyed HHs do not have sufficient cleaning and hygienic materials. The home visits confirmed the results of FGDs as the SCI outreach workers report that sanitation services inside 70% of the 1,200 homes they visited are far below the minimum humanitarian standards.

2 - Activities

The project will directly support WASH CLUSTER OBJECTIVE 1: Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient WASH services with dignity for the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank through ensuring basic WASH services for conflict-affected, un- or under-served and vulnerable populations and institutions in the Gaza Strip. The proposed project will support people with inadequate access to sanitation and hygiene services, and people who have limited access to safe drinking water services. It will target six vulnerable communities with a total population of over 167,600 people. The project will complement SCI effort through current WASH intervention for improving access to water and Improving access of vulnerable women and girls to menstrual hygiene management (MHM) supplies and gender responsive hygiene information to meet their basic needs. Five of the proposed six communities are among the eight communities targeted through current WASH project. SCI designed the project to increase the impact and sustainability of the current intervention through providing improved access to essential sanitation services, in parallel with hygiene promotion activities through conducting the following activities: 1) Rehabilitation/upgrading the HH’s sanitation facilities in 250 homes. This includes maintenance of toilets, kitchens and HH water and sewage connections. 2) Continuation and expansion of the hygiene promotion activities to target more families. The hygiene promotion activities will include a series of awareness sessions and distribution of hygiene and dignity kits for 2000vulnerable HHs in the targeted communities. 3) Adapt 50 HHs sanitation facilities for people with disabilities. 4) Rehabilitation of WASH facilities in 40 KGs.5) Supply 1000 HH drinking water tanks (200 litres) to improve HHs drinking water storage capacity for 1,000 HHs. The intervention will help the most vulnerable group to enhance their ability to survive, prevent or alleviate the risk of deteriorating their health and dignity. The interventions will be carried out in partnership with local implementing partners SYFS and Bayader Society. The project will select the beneficiaries based on their vulnerability through socio-economic and protection assessment. Beneficiary selection will prioritize vulnerable groups such as female-headed households, and HHs with dependent(s) suffering from chronic disease, injury or disability, malnourished children, children suffering from multiple child protection concerns and those receiving case management services.Implementing partners will conduct a pre-intervention survey to identify health and hygiene needs to analyse the intervention plan with community-based organizations and active community members. SCI, Bayader and SYFS will also procure and distribute hygiene kits to households. In integration with other SCI programming, the local partners, and in coordination with SCI will coordinate the intervention with the two existing Community Protection Committees (CPC), composed of 50% female members, and CBOs. The CPCs and CBOs will facilitate coordination with the community, and facilitate raising the target communities concerns and urgent needs. To ensure full integration with other WASH actors, SCI will closely coordinate with the WASH cluster and area focal points as well as hygiene promotion working group Gaza strip. The Education Cluster will nominate KGs and SCI will identify the KGs that need rehabilitation through technical assessment of the nominated KGs. The rehabilitation work in KGs and homes will be supervised by SCI engineers. The project builds on the results of the needs assessments conducted in the current intervention. SCI will continue the coordination with WASH Cluster, other WASH actors who have similar intervention, and the Hygiene Promotion WG in designing the awareness messages and campaigns
