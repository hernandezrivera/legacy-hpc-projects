Project Summary

The IRC Bangladesh is one of the important health partners of Health Sector in Cox’s Bazar in Rohingya response and will be providing essential health services focusing Maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health (MNCAH), Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) including adolescent SRH, Non-Communicable Diseases (NCD) including MHPSS and Communicable Diseases (CD) from 04 Primary Health Care Centers (PHC) (camp 6, 11, 22 and 25), 01 BEmONC Center (camp 2E PHC) with Inpatient Department (IPD) services, 13 Referral Hubs, and community health components in different camps especially. A NCD clinic will be established in camp 11 to cover the NCD needs in this camp as well as in the surrounding few camps.
The 24/7 PHC services from 4 PHCs will be guided by health sector approved Minimum Package of Essential Health Services (MPEHS) for Rohingya people. In camp 6 and 11 PHCs, a coordinate approach will be considered with SRC/BDRCS. In camp 2E PHC, the IRC will work in coordination with BDRCS/SRC to fill the comprehensive SRH services (ANC, PNC, FP, CMR, STI, MR, PAC) including normal vaginal delivery (NVD) services and 24/7 IPD services. All these health facilities will have adequate clinical & support staff (staffing norms stipulated in the MPEHS) and logistic support to ensure free health care services for the targeted beneficiaries and these facilities will be backed up by comprehensive diagnostic laboratories to ensure proper diagnosis to prevent unnecessary referrals to secondary/tertiary level care facilities. An adequate supply of essential medicines and quality of medical commodities will be ensured with temperature-controlled warehouse/storage management for ensuring pharma qualities. Under the guidance of the Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR), the IRC will continue to maintain 4 surge Mobile Medical Teams (MMTs) in camp 6, 11 and 25. An additional MMT will be formed in camp 22 based on the gaps and need in this camp. Adequate Emergency Preparedness for health facilities and required capacity building of MMTs will be targeting natural disasters and artificial hazards in Cox’s Bazar (Fire, Monsoon rain, Cyclone, Floods) and epidemic-prone Infectious/communicable disease through training and simulations. Reporting of health services to DHIS2, 4Ws and Early Warning, Alert and Response System (EWARS) will be done regularly and on time.
The IRC supported Referral Hubs (RH) in 13 camps (4, 4E, 6, 7, 8E, 8W, 12, 13, 19, 20, 20E, 26 and 27) is to ensure referral and transportation support for patients with emergency obstetric conditions and other medical emergencies to access to PHC/BEmONC facilities through community health volunteers and deployed ambulances. These hubs will work closely in coordination with PHCs and BEmONC facilities and CHWs in these respective camps. The IRC will expand community health component in coordination with community health working group (CHWG) within Rohingya camps especially in camp 11 and 25. The aim of this intervention is to enhance the capacity of Rohingya people in terms of increasing knowledge on health seeking behaviors especially on MNCH, SRH, NCD and to work on gender norms to influence this behaviour. Male engagement activities in MNCH, family planning (FP) and SRH will be periodized. Additionally, needs of children, adolescents, youth and old in all their diversity (gender, age, disability, ethnicity) will also be addressed. IRC will focus on Sex, Age and Disability Disaggregated Data (SADDD) for program quality improvement.
The IRC will coordinate with BBC Media Action to mainstreaming AAP by building capacities of project staff members, producing community facing contents, also assist partner agencies to establish CFRM and analysis of feedback for programme development. The insights from rigorous research are shared among the organization so that aid worker can take necessary measure in effective community engagement and accountability.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Around 902,798 Rohingya refugees are living in the Cox’s Bazar district in the 33 extremely congested refugee camps across and the total people in need is 1.4 million (UNHCR 2022) including affected host communities. Among the Rohingyas, 11% of them (10% male and 13% female) had a health problem and were needing to access health care (J-MSAN 2021). A total of 3.9 million OPD consultations were provided- equivalent to a utilization rate of 2-3 consultations/person/year in 2021. The major morbidities among Rohingyas are skin diseases (27.44%), acute respiratory infection (18.68%), AWD (3.45%) and other acute conditions (15.63%) (HSB#18). A recent study reported that 32.2% of the adult Rohingya were newly diagnosed as hypertensive and 44.5% of them were confirmed stage I & II hypertension. It also reported NCD risk factors that 22.9% smoke, 16.9% overweight and 90% are physically inactive (Rahman A., et al. 2022). A Systematic Review found a varied pattern of psychological symptoms with a wide range, such as the prevalence of depression is 12% to 89%, PTSD 3.7% - 61%, and anxiety 9% - 70%. (Jyoti D, et al., 2022). A total of 117,999 patients sought care for NCDs and 9500 individuals received individual-level psychosocial support in 1st Quarter of 2022 (Jan-March) (HSB#18).Generally, communities are unaware about the STI (Sexually Transmitted Infection) and their effect in reproductive health. It is reported an increase uptake of short-acting contraceptives (oral pills and injectable) over long-acting and reversible contraceptives (LARC). Though the LARC uptake has been improved, the rate remained low at only 2% (1.3% IUD and .7% Implant) in 2021 (SRH WG 2021). The preferred family planning method was the oral contraceptive pill (56%) followed by injectable contraceptives (33.6%) and condoms (8.7%). A recent study reported that 60% participants were unaware that there was no physical harm brought by using LARC, 40% were ashamed and afraid to discuss family planning matters with their husbands and 58% had the opinion that a couple should continue bearing children until a son is born (Azad MAK, et al., 2022). Additionally, the child marriage is quite prevalent in the Rohingya camps and it is primarily rooted in socio-cultural and religious beliefs around readiness for marriage (Melnikas AJ, et al., 2020) which has further consequences on maternal morbidity and mortality. Additionally, it reported that 76% women and girls feared being identified as survivors of SGBV when seeking services. it is also mentioned that rape including marital rape is exacerbating the increase in unwanted pregnancies and unsafe abortions resulting in disastrous emotional and physical consequences on survivors (IRC safety audit, 2021).There is an urgent concern about increased maternal and new-born deaths due to preventable causes. The MPMSR Annual report (2020) showed that the majority of maternal fatalities in the camps (47%) were related to obstetric haemorrhage and hypertension in pregnancy (eclampsia and preeclampsia). The estimated neonatal mortality and stillbirth was 27 and 15.2 per 1000 live births, the majority of neonatal deaths (76.3%) and stillbirths (72.1%) occurred at home a positive correlation existed between the camp population size and number of health facilities inside the camp (Amsalu R, et al., 2022). Availability of emergency transportation from home to nearby health facilities remain a challenge, especially at night time, to reduce maternal and neonatal mortalities and to address other medical emergencies (Barua M. et al., 2020).Refugee populations have cited ongoing gaps in information for complex topics; women are especially underserved by current information and community engagement provision. The community feedback needs to assess and generate findings and recommendations for all in Rohingya response for integrated approach on AAP. It will help to address community concerns and to enhance quality of humanitarian assistance.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The PHC health facilities of IRC (camp 6, 11 and 25) has already been recommended by the health sector based on recent rationalization of health facilities. The IRC will be taking over another PHC in camp 22 from MSF which is also a recommended PHC though the rationalization. In camp 2E PHC, the IRC will be providing the BEmONC and IPD services in coordination with BDRCS/SRC. These health facilities have already integrated comprehensive GBV and EPR services and camp 25 PHCC has integrated with rehabilitation services with Humanity & Inclusion (HI). The eye care is integrated in camp 6 PHCC with BDRCS/SRC and will be integrated in camp 11 PHC. The IRC will be looking further opportunities to integrate specialized health services like Eye care, Rehabilitation Care and Oral Health in all of these PHCs. Inter-sector integration with Early Childhood Development (ECD) will also be piloted to understand the feasibility (the IRC will appeal ECD component though Education sector). The IRC has confirmed funds for these health facilities, and it is highly expected that the donors for these interventions will further continue their funding in the year 23 and beyond. The funding for Referral Hubs in 13 camps are confirmed till early 2023 and it is high likely to be continued for next few years. These hubs are well integrated with EPR and integration with GBV referrals are in the process. The support for government hospitals (Teknaf and Ukhia UHCs) will also be provided as per gaps identified in these MoH hospitals. BBC Media Action has been working on accountability mainstreaming among the consortium partners by producing minimum standard guideline for AAP and support implementing the tools and techniques. The learning will be shared with other humanitarian agencies as a best practice to follow. BBC Media Action intends to work closely with the sectoral AAP focal in order to provide technical support in mainstreaming AAP within the sector.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The proposed project will focus on integrated protection intervention. The IRC will ensure access to protection mechanisms to increase capacity to stay safe, exercise basic rights, and access essential services. IRC will ensure integration within the protection (CP, GP, and GBV) sector. Particular attention should be dedicated to all mechanisms to protect refugees from Sexual Abuse and Exploitation by from anyone including humanitarian workers. The IRC will ensure staff are trained on humanitarian code of conduct, Child Safeguarding, and Prevention of PSEAH. All part of the health facilities will be cover with adequate lighting with required security guards to make sure all parts of the health facilities are safe for every client and the staff working in these health facilities. There will be separate space (e.g. rooms and screening process) to isolate any client of contagious diseases to reduce the transmission of CD diseases to other clients as pert of IRC’s “No Harm” policy.

3.2 - Gender

The patriarchal representation of masculinity is very strong in Rohingya, and host communities, men & boys are leading decision, bread earner for the family and protector for any emergency. Women & girls’ roles in patriarchal femineity are mostly defined based on the concept of sexual purity and chastity, women are expected to fully obey the decision of men and elderly member of the family. The participation, mobility and decision making of women are very limited. Even issues related to their own body, sexuality and rights are controlled by men. Social & cultural norms support men in all aspects, confirming physical, psychological, or sexual harassment to women & girls in a name of disciplining. Transforming this situation will need a normative shift, therefore the project will rigorously analyze the power inequalities looking through the intersectional lens and place corrective measures to support all. There will be gender segregated separate consultation and toilets for women & men

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The project analyzes the risk and needs of the clients in-terms of age diversity. The services are designed to address the specific needs, like person above 40 are supported with multiple diagnose to have a comprehensive assessment, children aged 0 to 5 are taken care with the highest level of sensitivity, adolescent boys and girls are provided with separate content and services being aware of the issue of cultural sensitivity. Child safeguarding will be dealt with highest organizational priority, this falls under mandatory reporting protocol. Both preventive and response mechanism are in place to ensures safeguarding awareness (robust session with staff/incentive worker who directly contact with clients on regular basis), reporting in-terms of clear flowchart and anonymous reporting process in every facility where IRC operate, response includes immediate support to the survivor including functioning referral pathways. Child safeguarding risks will be assessed regularly

3.4 - Diversity

Acknowledging the fact that neither women and girls, nor boys and men are homogenous group and have an equal hold on power. Therefore, the project aims building lens to the staff to interrogate racial, ethnic, class, physical ability, age, sexuality, & gender disparities and to contest existing ways of looking at structures of inequality. The intersectional power analysis would be a key approach to address the issues of diversity and reaching to the most at risk, especially persons with disability, transgender communities, women, and child headed family, single mother, separated children and women, elderly member or person carrying life threatening disease who are at higher risk of abuse. The IRC facilities are adequately designed to have disability friendly toilets and ramps to enable friendly access for PWDs. The IRC has coordinated with HI to incorporate more disability inclusiveness in the health facility and provided training to health care providers on disability inclusiveness

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The IRC is committed to placing the people we serve, our clients, at the center of humanitarian response and being responsive to their aspirations and perspectives. This makes our services and assistance more effective, relevant, and appropriate, and our decisions and actions more accountable to our clients, affected communities and other stakeholders. As part of AAP, IRC will continue operating Help Desk at PHCCs in different camps. Besides, IRC has installed Suggestion Boxes, Hotline Numbers for receiving complaints and feedbacks and will conduct one to one discussion, FGDs, post distribution monitoring and ‘client satisfaction survey’ to understand the level of satisfaction of the community. Alongside, BBC Media Action will offering capacity building, producing standard guidelines and support implementing a sectoral AAP mainstreaming. Also, analyze the community feedback data to produce insights will keep continue to make sure that the community are at the centre of the response

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Cox’s Bazar is vulnerable to cyclones, floods, landslides, and heavy rainfall. Considering context-specific natural hazards and previous disaster impacts, DRM has been incorporated to reduce the risk of negative effects and adverse impacts on life and the environment; the project has established an information management system to ensure early warning dissemination, multi-hazard preparedness messages to increase awareness of target population, enhance knowledge and skills of frontline emergency health service providers, volunteers, and community people to implement disaster risk reduction interventions effectively and identify the risk of natural hazard through health facility assessment, community health risk assessment periodically by developing an emergency response plan, to do advocacy with the relevant actors and also to provide emergency health services during a disaster in line with ISCG Multi-Hazard Response Plan with sector-specific preparedness and response guidance
