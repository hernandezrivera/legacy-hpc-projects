Project Summary

This project will scale up nutrition services in Fangak and Nyirol Counties of Jonglei State with a focus on the populations recently displaced by conflict and severe flooding. In both counties, needs analysis shows the impact of the unprecedented level of intercommunal conflict and severe flooding has driven food insecurity levels to Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and to Critical (IPC AMN Phase 4) for acute malnutrition. The expectation is that the situation will deteriorate as the lean season of 2021 approaches (IPC South Sudan Key Messages Oct 2020 – July 2021, 18 Dec 2020, pg 8-10).
To effectively respond to this emergency, this project will scale-up life-saving treatment services targeting the most vulnerable children U5, PLW, elderly and the disabled reaching locations where displaced populations are already experiencing acute malnutrition. In addition, the project will enhance prevention activities through expanding MIYCN services and integration with co-located health, WASH and FSL services provided by CMA and other humanitarian agencies in order to address the key drivers of chronic malnutrition. The project will also enhance nutrition monitoring, analysis and utilization of early warning information for timely coordination of response decision making. The project will reach four payams in Fangak - Manajang, Paguir, Mareang and Phom with estimated population of 112,927, and five payams in Nyirol - Chuil, Pultruk, Nyambor, Pading and Waat with estimated population of 112,353.
The project will use a combination of response approaches - RRM/ICRM, static, outreach and mobile approaches with nutrition survival kits to ensure timely response to emergencies. Services will be delivered from 23 static OTP-TSFP sites, and these sites will support mobile outreach services to cover recently displace populations. The project will adhere to new COVID-19 protocols in delivery of nutrition services. The response activities will cover (1) case-finding screening using the Family-MUAC method from static units and through mobile outreaches to IDP locations; (2) admission U5 SAM and MAM patients and PLW MAM patients to SC, OTP and TSFP services using expanded criteria where needed including malaria testing and SAM with medical complications will be referred to PHCC and/or SC services; (3) deliver MIYCN services to PLW and care-givers of children U23 months through counselling MtoM support groups, including vitamin A supplements and de-worming; (4) fully integrate nutrition with health, WASH and Protection services.
In order to improve this project’s responsiveness to evolving needs of the affected population, CMA will adopt a people-centered approach by mainstreaming protection and disability inclusion as well as emphasis on gender and age considerations. In order to improve sustainability, this project will seek to strengthen local capacity, including CHDs, project staff and community nutrition volunteers through job-relevant training. To address insecurity risks for PLW and U5s, CMA in collaboration with the targeted communities will implement protection practices to reduce risk of SGBV. By applying IASC guidance, measures to identify specific needs of men, women, boys and girls will be identified and addressed through a gender mainstreaming approach.
In relation to Accountability to Affected Population, this project will provide a mechanism through which affected populations can receive the information they need to make informed decisions on accessing nutrition services, provide feedback on the nutrition interventions, share concerns and submit complaints. CMA will work closely with local authorities, community leaders and beneficiaries, to ensure their inclusion in program decision-making and participation in planning, implementation and monitoring the delivery of nutrition services.

1 - Needs

The emergency this project will address is acute malnutrition (GAM ≥15%) in Fangak and Nyirol counties of Jonglei State in the context of the COVID-19 threat. In Fangak, the payams of Manajang, Paguir, Mareang and Phom will be served, and in Nyirol, the payams of Chuil, Pultruk, Nyambor, Pading and Waat will be served. The 18 Dec 2020 IPC report indicates both Fangak and Nyirol counties are currently experiencing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and classified as Critical (IPC AMN Phase 4) acute malnutrition (IPC South Sudan Key Messages Report Oct 2020 – July 2021, 18 Dec 2020, pg 8-10). Caseload data for Jonglei shows 19.1% of U5 children are suffering SAM and 63.8% are suffering MAM (IPC Report, 18 Dec 2020 pg 7). The January 2021 HNO shows targeted counties rated as Severity Phase Extreme for overall humanitarian conditions with estimated 18.2% of total population expected to face the Severity Phase Catastrophic rate in Fangak and 19% in Nyirol (2021 HNO, pg 7). The crisis is driven by an unprecedented level of inter-communal conflict and severe flooding. COVID-19 presents a new threat with 9 cases confirmed in Nyirol County. Severe flooding has caused an increase in displacement, loss of food-crop, restricted access to agricultural land, disruption in access and damage to nutrition facilities (see South Sudan: Flooding Situation Report, ICCG-UNOCHA, 18 Nov 20). The South Sudan Key Context Update (Jan 26, 2021, pg 3) indicates most of Fangak remains flooded and worst flood affected payams are in Mareang and Manajang. Worst flood affected payams are Waat, Pading and Chuil in Nyirol. Intercommunal conflict has caused loss of access to agricultural land, impeded access to nutrition facilities, looting of facility assets (Nyadin) and displacement. Worst affected locations are Waat (19 wounded plus 9 deaths), Pading (as recent as February 2021) and Mareang (Nyadin looting). Six conflict events were recorded in Nyirol County during the first half of 2020 (South Sudan Food Security Outlook Update FEWS NET, August 2020 pg 2). The “2020 harvest will provide only marginal relief for most households” (FEWS NET, August 2020 pg 6). Projecting into mid-year 2021, the IPC report indicates the situation is likely to deteriorate further. Where conflict and flooding intersect as in Nyirol and Fangak, the situation could rapidly deteriorate. Conflict and flooding has forced frequent displacements. Every household reports hosting IDPs in these counties, which exacerbates host household’s risk of acute malnutrition. In addition, displacement forces people to move away from locations with nutrition services. There is need utilize mobile approaches to reach these IDPs – vehicle, boat, motorcycle, bicycle, canoe and walking, to link affected populations to surveillance and treatment services. In the context of COVID-19, there is need to continue training on guidelines for nutrition service delivery for infection prevention and control and training mothers/caretakers to determine the nutrition status of their own children by conducting Family-MUAC screening and self-referrals. The needs analysis presented above clearly demonstrates the severity of malnutrition which is expected to worsen. In catchment are covered by this project, the caseload data for SAM and MAM treatment services shows the most at risk and in need populations are children U5 (33,795), pregnant and lactating women (11,559), the elderly and disabled (6,803). Treatment alone will not end malnutrition. MIYCN services with vitamin A supplementation needs to be coupled with treatment services. There is also need to strengthen nutrition monitoring to achieve early detection and emergency response. The project will use a combination of responses with nutrition survival kits to ensure timely response in an emergency.

2 - Project activities

To directly contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality from acute malnutrition in Fangak and Nyirol counties, this project will focus on four critical objectives. These objectives and the activities to achieve output targets are defined below. Objective 1. To increase equitable access and utilization to quality preventative nutrition specific services for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations.Planned activities:1.1 Prevention of malnutrition through behaviour change communication with focus on caregivers of children 0-23 months;1.2 Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies through vitamin A supplementation focused on children 6-59 months; 1.3 Training nutrition workers and community health workers on the MIYCN counselling package including new COVID-19 protocols for prevention of infection. Objective 2. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for the early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under 5 years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations. Planned activities:2.1 Establishing and maintaining SCs, OTPs and TSFP facilities for delivery of quality and timely nutrition services;2.2 Early detection and treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition:2.3 Training health and nutrition workers on the Family-MUAC screening approach for self-referrals;2.4 Training health and nutrition workers of OTP/TSFP sites on management of SAM and MAM to attain very high quality in management of SAM and MAM cases;2.5 Conducting monitoring visits to nutrition sites to assess performance and impact of the project;2.6 Conducting KIIs, FGDs and patient exit interviews to evaluate their satisfaction with services received;2.7 Promoting community participation for responsive nutrition service delivery by raising awareness on beneficiary rights/entitlement in the nutrition sites and by establishing a community complaints and feedback mechanism in OTP/TSFP sites. Objective 3. To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information.Planned activities:3.1 Through monthly and quarterly meetings, conduct ongoing consultation, review and evaluation of nutrition program performance with local authorities, co-located humanitarian actors, community leaders and affected beneficiaries;3.2 Improve facility-based data collection and management with training nutrition staff on preparation and analysis of monthly NIS reports including analysis of impacts on affected populations and disseminate reports to stakeholders in a timely manner. Objective 4. To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations.Planned activities:4.1 Prevention, testing and treating for malaria and provide LLITNs for children under 5 years of age with acute malnutrition;4.2 Provide WASH NFI kits to children with SAM in nutrition sites;4.3 Conduct safety audit/assessments at nutrition sites at least twice per year to identify and address women and children’s protection/safety needs;4.4 Establishing and maintaining demonstration kitchen garden at OTP/TSFP sites for training caregivers of children 0-<23 months and MtoMSGs;4.5 Integration of child stimulation and ECD activities in the provision of care for children treated for SAM;4.6 Mainstreaming child protection and GBV risk mitigation through training of health, nutrition and WASH workers on SGBV and the referral pathways for clinical management of rape and psychosocial support.

3 - Project indicators

The project success will be measured during implementation and after. The project success will be measured for learning or lessons learned with a view to improving on quality. The project will also be assessed for effectiveness. This will be done by evaluating key performance areas such as schedule to determine whether activities are being implemented within time and as per the work plan. The project success will also be measured against costs to ascertain if the project is being implemented within budget.To measure and report success against objective 1, indicators and targets the following methods will be used:• Tracking food distribution per beneficiary reported with NIS reports.• OTP records on counselling (age, gender), content of counseling to be reported quarterly.• OTP records on number, age and gender of participants to be reported quarterly.• OTP records reported quarterly. For objective 2, indicators and targets the following methods will be used to measure and report success:• NIS weekly and monthly reports.• Quarterly review by Nutrition Manager with beneficiary exit interviews and planned vs actual achievement analysis.• Monitoring reports by Nutrition Manager.• Monitoring report by Nutrition Manager with beneficiary exit interviews, KIIs and FGDs.• OTP quarter reports with evidence of program adjustments as result of feedback. For objective 3, indicators and targets the following methods will be used to measure and report success:• Monthly NIS reports For objective 4, indicators and targets the following methods will be used to measure and report success:• OTP records reported quarterly.• OTP records on counselling caregivers (age, gender), content of counselling, reported quarterly.• Community surveys with KIIs, water quality checks.• Safety audit reports (2 per year per site) with evidence of installation of safety improvements and implementation of safety practices.• OTP records reported quarterly with clinical reports on services provided. Monitoring Implementation: CMA will use the following tools to monitor implementation of project activities: 1) Monthly reports from project sites providing data focused on output achievement comparing actual with planned achievement; 2) Quarterly activity reports from project sites providing data activities including on trainings, community meetings and other potential progress impeding contextual details; 3) Regular meetings (monthly/quarterly) and consultations with affected populations (women, men, girls and boys) to ensure their direct participation in monitoring project progress and on community access to services, the effectiveness of services, the safety of service delivery and the utilization and effectiveness of the complaints mechanism; 4) Quarterly monitoring reports completed by CMA’s county and Juba-based monitoring and technical management team. CMA’s monitoring will collect information for the measurement of success and inform decision-making regarding needed adjustments and modifications in approach as the project progresses. Evaluation: From the planning and implementation monitoring, CMA will derive the main data for evaluating the success of this project and to ensure accountability to affected populations. This foundation of evaluative data will be complemented with information directly obtained from affected populations and the beneficiaries of the project. CMA will use participatory tools including FGDs, KII and community meetings, and the input from local authorities and county-based sector department M&E personnel to complete the information for internal project evaluation. These qualitative evaluation tools will help CMA assess and learn of the impacts of awareness and behavior change communication, issues related to GBV and protection, effectiveness of outreach and mobile approaches and the resiliency of project results when delivered in the integrated and cross-sector approaches.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CMA has completed the rollout of its revised PSEA policy with training of all management at Juba and County levels and the Facility lead officers at the facility level. The County level officers and the Facility lead officers are now skilled to implement the PSEA training at the facility level which includes training of facility-based, mobile team and outreach workers on: (1) gender, age and disability appropriate methods and content for disseminating information about PSEA; (2) available channels for reporting abuse; (3) confidentiality rules in reporting abuse cases; and (4) the mechanisms and locations available for victims of abuse to access support and treatment.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CMA’s on-ground personnel undertake focus group discussions (FGD) and key informant interviews (KII) as part of the planning and monitoring processes to ensure ongoing effective feedback. CMA’s Facility lead officers ensure that the most vulnerable disabled, illiterate, women and children are included. At all sites, the affected population groups that CMA consults in the planning and feedback processes are as follows: (1) local women and men leaders, focus groups and key informant women and men; (2) Mother to Mother Support Groups, other social groups representing the most vulnerable including individuals living with disabilities and (3) discharged patients women, men, girls and boys including individuals living with disabilities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Needs assessment on entry to new locations and on an ongoing basis thereafter ensures that project activities are tailored to community needs. Information is disaggregated in respect of gender, age and the needs of different population groups to improve suitability of planned interventions. In locations already served, CMA has established mechanisms to ensure accountability to affected populations. In new locations, CMA will apply its established strategy and implementation model to ensure accountability to affected populations. The structures and mechanisms established enable affected populations to participate actively in planning services, receive information they need to make informed decisions on accessing services, provide feedback on interventions, voice and submit complaints and participate in program decision-making. CMA’s programs are implemented in collaboration with local authorities and leaders of host communities and IDPs. At all locations, monthly meetings with leaders, Mother-to-Mother Support Groups and other key social groups are conducted to report on project output and obtain community feedback. The Facility lead officers, (typically a Clinical Officer or CMAM Supervisor) conduct discharge interviews with a sample of the patients to obtain feedback from those who receive services. These consultations provide the most vulnerable of affected populations full awareness and opportunity to utilize the complaints mechanism, the opportunity to actively participate in planning and monitoring the project, learn their entitlements for accessing project services and render complaints. CMA’s on-ground teams will ensure that affected populations are aware and able to utilize the complaints mechanism by practicing the principles of transparency, communication and provision of information linked to the complaints mechanism, and engaging the target populations to actively seek out their feedback.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The Facility lead officer will be responsible for coordinating the engagement with communities and actively seeking their feedback. The consultation activities will be recorded and reported monthly to the County Officers and the project management team, highlighting what new actions (if any) need to be taken. These measures will ensure that an effective system of representation of affected populations is in place for the purpose of improving project results. Further, the Project Manager will visit and supervise the project (once per quarter) to conduct monitoring and evaluation so that regular assessment of beneficiary knowledge on project progress and their satisfaction with project performance in conducted, and new measure put in place in response to beneficiary feedback. Further, the Project Manager will conduct meetings with local leaders of host and IDP communities and local authorities to ensure effective implementation and robust monitoring of the complaints mechanism is in place so as to achieve effective accountability to the populations being served.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

• 8.1: High Risk: Mitigation Measures: To address the reality that many beneficiaries face exposure to violence while accessing facility-based services because of long distances to locations where project services will be centered, and to mitigate this risk, CMA will employ mobile and outreach approaches to go to the locations where at-risk populations live in order to reach beneficiaries in a safe way. Further, the project will promote community-based strategies and practices among affected populations to provide protection for the most vulnerable community members (children, PLW, especially IDPs and disabled). The project will engage men and women leaders of affected populations to take responsibility for mobilizing protection for disadvantaged and vulnerable individuals, so that they have unimpeded access to nutrition services. To adhere to the principles of “Do-No-Harm”, the project will strive to deliver services in a balanced manner so that IDP and host community populations, regardless of ethnicity or population group, will have equal access to project services. To achieve this balance, CMA will implement a strong awareness campaign to engage both IDP and host community leaders.• 8.2:High Risk :Mitigation Measures: To address the reality that women, girls and boy with and without disabilities feel unsafe and are at risk of sexual violence and abuse, CMA will implement full engagement of the leaders of affected populations and host communities in community-based assessments for delivery of static, mobile and outreaches services, and apply the “do-no-harm” approach to reduce the risk sexual of exploitation and abuse. The project will support the implementation of safety audits at all static, mobile and outreach sites, and implement all recommended safety audit measures to improve safe access to nutrition services. The safety audits will be led by CMA Safety Audit Focal Person, and implemented by female staff members in respect of commonly practiced protection protocols. These measures will ensure access and minimize exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to project assistance.• 8.4:High Risk:Mitigation Measures: To address the need for available and effective protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents, all of CMA’s project sites will be linked to health services where CRM support will be provided for victims of SGBV and psychological support and treatment for the trauma experienced by child survivors of abuse and conflict and the survivors of SGBV. Through public promotion and community awareness sessions both those receiving treatment and the wider community will be made aware of the appropriate referral pathways for the services that survivors need. Further, local authorities and community leaders will be engaged on issues around child protection and protection of the most vulnerable elderly and disabled and made aware of available protection assistance and the GBV referral pathway for CMR and psychological support for survivors of SGBV. • 8.5:High Risk :Mitigation Measures: Exclusion of marginalized community’s/minority groups from consultations and decision making on project approaches and interventions will be mitigated by adopting strategies and mechanisms through which affected populations can receive (1) the information they need to make informed decisions on accessing project services, (2) provide feedback on the project interventions, share concerns and submit complaints, and (3) by working directly with leaders of affected beneficiaries and through local authorities and community leaders. As women, girls, boys, the disabled and elderly are primary targets of this project, and to ensure they are all included in consultations and decision-making, CMA will organize specific forums (Key Informants, Focus Group Discussions) and meetings to engage these vulnerable groups.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The nutrition project will be implemented along with Health services in both Fangak and Nyirol counties to ensure timely and inclusive medical care, mental health and psychosocial support are available to target populations especially at risk women and GBV survivors. Where Protection partners are co-located within the county and operating in the vicinity of this project’s catchment area, CMA will seek the assistance of these partners to support women at risk and GBV survivors with legal matters. Where Protection partners are not available to assist in a direct way, CMA will refer women who need protection services to capable service providers and assist these vulnerable women and adolescent girls to access required legal, mental health and psychosocial services.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Fangak and Nyirol counties are classified in IPC 4 Emergency for food insecurity. Further, UNOCH estimates that 18.2% of the population of Fangak and 19.0% of population in Nyirol will face IPC 5 Catastrophic by April 2021 (South Sudan HNO 2021, pg 34). Toward decreasing the proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5, the project will serve an estimated population of 17,936 with SAM and MAM treatments of children under age 5 (34% of county caseloads) and 6,130 pregnant and lactating women, including women with disabilities, with MAM treatments (33% of county caseloads). These population targets include those facing IPC 5 Catastrophic conditions

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The project will be implemented along with Health and WASH sector programs in an integrated approach providing the core set of basic services and with a focus on reaching the most vulnerable (women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons) through the combination of static service, outreach from static PHCC based service and the mobile service approach where required. Further, the project will link with with FSL and Protection partners where they are co-located with CMA. Even where FSL and Protection partners are not co-located within the vicinity of this project’s catchment area, CMA will support food security with kitchen gardens, support protection with safety audits, other basic protection services and community-based protection approaches to enable access to nutrition services. Through these measures, CMA will ensure that the complete spectrum essential cross-sectoral basic services reach the populations targeted in this project.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Fangak and Nyirol counties are classified as severity level 4 with and in IPC 4 Critical for malnutrtion. Nutrition Cluster data (16 Feb 21) shows the proxy GAM rate is 20.27% in Fangak, and 23.12% in Nyirol. Toward decreasing the global acute malnutrition among children under age 5, project estimates show that in Fangak, it will reach 3,474 children under age 5 with SAM treatments and 4,345 with MAM treatment (25% of county case load). In Nyirol, the project will reach 3,963 children under age 5 with SAM treatments and 6,154 with MAM treatment (45% of county case load).
