Project Summary

PRIDE-Initiative’s proposed intervention will aim to strengthen displace affected Women and Children Right to Land and Property HLP assistance Legal assistance, Gash for Rent, Capacity development, Counselling and support in proviso of title documents to the displacement affected communities The PRIDE will extend urgent need for support to the vulnerable groups among other activities. The application of protection services to the displaced persons in out of camps is greatly determined by access to the IDPs. In Banki and Ngala LGAs insecurity is the major hinderance to access of humanitarian access, however for out Camp Protection Monitoring, in addition to insecurity, state government guidelines as well need to be considered. This strategy outlines the respective recommended implementation modalities on the basis of access to the IDPs

1 - Needs

The crisis in Northeast Nigeria is now in its 11th year, with over 1.8 million people displaced across the three states of Borno, Yobe, and Adamawa (BAY states). Across the three crisis-affected BAY states, 7.9 million people out of a total of 13 million – more than one in two people – needed humanitarian assistance in 2020. This is an 11 per cent increase from the 7.1 million people who were in need of some form of assistance in 2019, resulting mainly from rising violence and insecurity. Over 80 per cent of IDPs are in Borno State, the epicenter of the crisis. One in four internally displaced people are under five, and 79 per cent are women and children. Over 60 per cent of IDPs are living in host communities, making it harder to access them with assistance and putting additional pressure on the already stretched resources of these communities. In total, over 75 per cent of people living in Borno State were in need of humanitarian assistance in 2020. Additionally, UNOCHA estimates that 1.2 million individuals remain isolated in areas inaccessible to the humanitarian response, with close to 1 million residing in Borno State. These individuals have limited access to basic services such as health care Housing, Land and Property and education. Specific conditions and needs of the population in Hard to Reach (H2R) areas remain unknown, and population figures, as well as demographic composition and status (non-displaced, IDP, returnee), are formulated with little actual knowledge of the situation. Given the potential for critical levels of humanitarian needs in H2R areas highlighted by REACH’s assessments, it is evident that the lack of regular, reliable and predictable information on the scale of population, their needs and conditions in these areas is limiting the capacity of humanitarian organizations to both advocate for access and appropriately plan a response. To be able to properly deliver aid in accessible displacement locations to serve the needs of the newly displaced, a better understanding of the needs of arriving populations, as well as the potential scale and timing of influxes of new arrivals, is needed. This strengthened evidence base will also be critical to inform appropriate planning of initial emergency response to newly accessible areas. In (Banki) and Ngala towns represent islands of relative safety and basic service provision, within Local Government Areas (LGAs) that are otherwise entirely controlled by Armed Opposition Groups (AOGs) and remain inaccessible to humanitarian workers and civil authorities.

2 - Activities

The proposed Action has been designed according to the needs identified in Nigeria by the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), multi-sector needs assessment, and through communication with OCHA, sector leads and donors. This section of the proposal will be divided into the purposes outlined above: Purpose One: To mitigate and respond to the harm caused to persons who have suffered violence, coercion, exploitation, serious neglect or discrimination, and to restore their capacity to live safe and dignified lives. Purpose Two: To strengthen targeted protection services to address the needs and vulnerability of persons at heightened risks of violence, exploitation, serious neglect or discrimination; enhance their capacities and reduce the risk of resorting to negative coping mechanisms. Purpose Three: Strengthened community resilience and systems/institutions that prevent and respond to gender-based violence including harmful practices

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Risks and security issues related to this program include possible child abuse, sexual harassment and exploitation of women and girls, acts of theft including defrauding of illiterate members or other forms or coercion of beneficiaries. PRIDE will ensure safe implementation of activities through strict adherence and commitment to uphold the principle of “Do No Harm” in its activities. Suitable sites will be chosen for any activities including meetings, focus group discussions PRIDE and key informant interviews of beneficiaries with community leaders. Secure sites that are near beneficiaries will be given priority to ensure that people can return home before dark to minimize risks. The program implementation team staff will be provided refresher training on child safeguarding, accountability, the Inter-Agency Standing Committee guidelines on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse of beneficiaries and Green Concern for Development’s Code of Conduct. All new PRIDE employees are oriented to and required to sign the PRIDE Child Safeguarding Policy and the Code of Conduct as part of their contractual package. PRIDE mainstreams protection across all programming. The protection team identifies unaccompanied and separated minors and identifies suitable caregivers. Caregivers are referred to the food assistance program as vulnerable households with minors. This reduces the burden for caregivers and the need for negative coping strategies such as begging and child labor (street hawking). The protection team conducts regular follow-up in cases of abuse and neglected children for proper referral pathway
