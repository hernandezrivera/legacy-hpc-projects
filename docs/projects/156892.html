Project Summary

Rural Aleppo witnessed increased rates of child recruitment and exploitation. Boys and girls are more vulnerable and at risk of sexual gender based violence due to forced displacement, family separation, lack of basic structural and social protections and limited availability of access to services
The project will address the issues above as the following :
Section 1: Increase the equitable access of 3500 children and adolescents to good child protection interventions in 5 large schools in 5 communities in Jebel Saman district(Babka – Jeineh – Kafr Karmin – Abin Samaan – Maaret Atarib) in accordance with Minimum Standards for the Protection of Children in Humanitarian Law Through the establishment of child protection committees and psychosocial support activities in every school where adolescents can participate actively to make the schools source of love , peace , tranquility and spaces where children feel secure and stable, express their views, learn and achieve their dreams with a bright future without any forms of violence and this will also be done through awareness sessions for students' parents about the risks of protection to prevent them and to meet the needs, wishes and desires of children at the home.
Section II: Enhancing the capacity of humanitarian actors and community key service providers to assess, analyze the needs and prevent protection risks by training teachers(60 males & 60 females) in the targeted schools on codes of conduct to help them improve the classroom environment and education and consequently make the schools spaces of safety and hope contributing in building the bright future of children and reducing the risks of protection at the level of the schools and surrounding community through the establishment of a community-based protection networks including the actors from local councils, school principals, medical points and heads of education committees in addition to existing protection filed program coordinators to better understand the challenges facing children who dropped out of school and their needs .In order to ensure that protection standards are applied in programs provided by organizations in other sectors, 100 staff members(50 males & 50 females) in other sectors(non-protection)will be trained to integrate protection and reduce violations of protection and thus we have formed community protection committees and networks at the level of each community in the targeted locations within the project.

Needs

According to the needs assessment that Saed Charity conducted in 30 schools in the five communities targeted within the project((Babka – Jeineh – Kafr Karmin – Abin Samaan – Maaret Atarib)in Jebel Saman district it was found that:- 50% of the children and adolescents (5-17 years old) are being harassed by teachers in the schools that were assessed- 60% of the children are displaced more than once- 75% of the children lived in scenes of horror and bombardment - 18 % became orphans due to the loss of one of their parents in the shelling- 40% of the children suffer from psychological deterioration and lack of interest in the house.- Half of the children interviewed said" They do not feel safe at school or when they play outside- Some children without safe care arrangements and emphasize the need to strengthen the role of the community and families in the care and building protection committees of community to protect children from the risks they face and the maintenance of their rights in accordance with the principles and standards of protection.

Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served.Risk that existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles (e.g. child rights messaging is perceive as a threat1. Including access barriers for those with injuries/disabilities and communication mechanisms for persons with visual/hearing impairments.3. Liaise with "neutral" parties as much as possible and triangulate all information from all sources prior to decision making.4. Utilize sector agreed key messages to ensure consistency and inform sector of discrepancies or if new messaging is required.5. Ensure safety audits for access are conducted for each fixed site or mobile service location.Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups.Risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups / individuals at the exclusion of other groups/individuals.1. Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. 2. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Changes in the security situation, particularly in project target areas, affects the safety/mobility/ displacement of beneficiaries/staff and results in being unable to participate in project. This includes the risk of targeted attacks and bombing of schools/ communities and the risks of mines, airstrikes and cross fire. Mitigation : Closely monitor the security situation in coordination with EDs and relevant stakeholders and conduct a security assessments. They will ensure that staff members and teachers are trained in safety and security best practices, including mine risk education. Risk 2 : Attacks on schools Mitigation : Alternative locations (community centers, homes, etc…) and methodologies of education should be considered to avoid attack-related risks. Risk 3: Interference of armed groups, including confiscation of sensitive materials (laptops, phones); Mitigation :In order to ensure that armed groups do not get involved in programming, education partners will engage with relevant stakeholders and make sure that the local councils and local authorities are aware of the projects and have provided adequate access and space to conduct activities.
