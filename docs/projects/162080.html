Project Summary

The project will support 5000 beneficiaries (both Rohingya and host) who are at risk of natural disasters with cash transfers that sufficiently enable them to meet their basic food requirements. It will improve and increase the availability of mitigation infrastructure and reduce the scale and severity of damage that occurred as a result of monsoon and disaster and provision of Cash to (cash-for-work) beneficiaries allows them to meet their most pressing needs for food and non-food items among others. DCA will purchase the materials (Cement, brick, sand, bamboo, jute bag, GI ware, roof, drum sheet) through a tendering process where intensive labor engagement by direct cash as per sector guidelines. More importantly, the project will improve gender equality and women’s empowerment by involving women in cash for work. DCA will coordinate with camp level site management agency as well as the site management sector at Cox's Bazar level. The project will be implemented by a contractor if cash for work is banned. DCA will involve other program staff in this project and their salary will be charged to DCA's own fund.

Needs

The Rohingya face challenges to move around the camp safely and access life saving services (such as food distribution) because of damage to roads, pathways, and bridges. Poor drainage systems across much of the camps mean that the opportunity is lost to channel water in a path that would limit destruction to infrastructure, further impacting the Rohingya ability to move around the camps in the lengthy monsoon season. Slopes that have been destabilized by deforestation pose a pertinent threat to life, and landslides are common. Interventions are required to decrease these risks, and improve the camp infrastructure; particularly in terms of construction of access routes, tree plantation to stabilize slopes and reduce the risk of landslide, and drainage system development to ensure that further damage is not caused by monsoon flooding. Currently, 47% of households report that these structural improvements are ongoing and create positive change in the camps, highlighting a successful intervention approach that should be scaled up. More than two years after seeking shelter in Bangladesh, most of the Rohingya remain largely dependant on aid and relief items. 95% of the Rohingya currently settled in Cox Bazar report engaging in negative coping strategies to obtain cash to meet basic needs, including borrowing money (69%), selling non-food items (41%), and exchanging food items distributed by WFP (35%). Increasingly, households are getting into debt to access cash to meet their basic needs, with the number of people reporting to be in debt rising from 35% in July 2018, to 69% in August 2019. The Rohingya are not permitted to access employment opportunities outside of the camps, however, at this time the Government of Bangladesh does allow volunteer incentive rates to be paid to Rohingya volunteers on a daily basis.
