Project Summary

This project is planned to target conflict affected girls and boys and their families in North and Central Darfur; who have fled from the outbreak of violence in the Jebel Marra (JM) area which started in Jan 2016. The JM areas lies in the center of the Darfur region, bordering the state divisions of Central, South and North Darfur. The region is mainly inhabited by the Fur tribe and has, since 2003, been the primary stronghold of the Sudan Liberation Army (SLA), a Fur-dominated group. It is the only place in Darfur where an armed opposition group maintains prolonged control over territory and the only area in Darfur to which humanitarian organisations have had no access since 2011.
This will be done through the delivery of quality preventative and remedial interventions to improve response to emergency humanitarian child protection needs in communities affected by conflict. Save the Children (SC) will ensure the provision of timely and rights based child protection services to the most deprived and marginalized girls and boys. These services will include psychosocial support, prevention and response to the needs of child victims of Sexual and Gender Based violence (SGBV), family Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR) for the Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC), as well as assistance to child victims of landmines.
To prevent and mitigate child protection risks in communities affected by conflict,; SC will strengthen community based child protection mechanisms in their capacity to prevent and raise awareness around key child protection risks, such as family separation, gender based violence, psychosocial distress, children’s association with armed forces or groups and landmines. SC will invest in capacity building of CP Sub-sector members including the National Council on for Child Welfare and other SC humanitarian partners to ensure the quality of child protection interventions.
In order to reinforce the resilience of communities and strengthen capacity of child protection institutions,; SC will support community based child protection mechanisms and civil society organisations to disseminate information on relevant services and contribute to advocacy and the referral of child protection cases. As part of SGBV (Sexual and Gender Based Violence) eradication programming, SC will engage with men and boys in gender focused prevention work, addressing root causes and changing social norms around gender relations and masculinity.
Gender power relations restricting women's and girls’ possibilities to participate were analyzed in programme activities as domestic workloads, social norms and/or threats of sexual violence restrict their mobility. Accordingly, gender sensitive measures will be adopted to mitigate these programmatic risks including adapting schedules and meeting venues to ensure equitable participation, using single sex discussion groups at the CFS, promoting a girl-friendly space once a week to ensure that adolescent girls are able to participate, adapting case management to the different types of violence faced by boys and girls. It will also include using quotas to ensure equitable participation in the child clubs and CBCPN.
To mainstream gender in this project, the IASC Guidelines for integrating Gender-based Violence interventions in Humanitarian Settings were adopted as follows: there will be a reference document for ensuring quality in planning and coordination and staff will be trained on the guidelines. A SGBV screening of any alternative care facilities to be used for UASC will also be included as a first measure. The project activities were trailered to cater for the needs of girls and boys between the age 5 and 17 years old, after conducting a thorough analysis to the power relation, gender dynamics and accessibility in the selected communities. Children and communities should be consulted to identify the appropriate locations for the construction of the CFSs.

Needs

Children affected by the fighting in the Jebel Mara, particularly those displaced, remain exposed to various protection risks such as exposure to violence (including SGBV), family separation, deteriorating living conditions and recruitment by armed actors. These risks have a devastating impact on their well-being, physical security and future prospects with girls bearing the heaviest brunt of the conflict. There is an urgent need to support children and families in Central Darfur (Zalingie, Golo and Nertiti) as well as North Darfur (Elfasher, Tawilla and Kabkabiya). 2.6 million are in need of protection services; of these, 1.2 million people are targeted with 0.73 million in IDPs. In this third year of the MYHS 2017-2019, the overall goal remains to protect and save lives. Assistance will be needs based and protection, livelihoods, reinforcing community assets and services, partnerships and social cohesion will be at the centre of programming. It is aligned with both our Country Strategic Plan as well as the SCI global priority on Protecting Children in Armed Conflict/ Stop War on Children Strategy 2019-21 regarding the Children Affected by Armed Conflict in Sudan.As set in the MYHS, the scope and priorities of the 2019 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) are defined around saving lives, providing basic services and increasing self-reliance and improving nutrition and resilience of affected populations. Save the Children will continue to build on and consolidate its long experience and learning gained through other ongoing projects with similar objectives with projects funded by a group of donors including Sida Hum, SHF, UNHCR, and Protection of Children in Conflict (PCiC) pooled fund from SCI headquarters. This is carried out in order to strengthen its programme, advocacy and interventions to protect the children in conflict. The ongoing insecurity in this area has impeded humanitarian access, leading to gaps in protection and saving life and other interventions. Without access to inner Jebel Marra until recently, there has been no consistent provision of basic humanitarian services, including child protection in IDPs camps and most affected communities’ settlements. SC and other partners has started running protection and lifesaving services for IDPs in Jabel Marra, a conflict Zone neglected for 15 years from basic services due to ongoing conflict and restriction of access is very new. OCHA estimates that 95,000 people are in inner Jebel Marra.Assessments conducted last year confirmed that in newly accessible areas, such as Jebel Marra, social services are not available or have very limited capacity to care for vulnerable people. Daily attacks occur between rebel groups, government, and the peace-signatory militias are reported. Tribal clashes among Arab and non-Arab tribes are still taking place.

Impact on environment/natural resources

SC will work on neutral impact on environment with mitigation or enhancement to ensure that the proposed activities will have no negative impact on the environment. Materials will be procured locally at state level to avoid extra charges of transport such as doing bulk procurement at country office and transport to project location. If there is an existing structure to accommodate the CFS, the structure should be inspected for its appropriateness before usage. Moreover, all prevention activities including Family Tracing and reunification (FTR), training, awareness and strengthening of community mechanisms are expected to positively affect the ongoing and any future conflicts, through restoring family links and reduce further separation. The project will support the effort of minimizing energy use and instead use renewable sources of energy through orientation sessions at community level to influence the mind-set of the local people on how to positively deal with their local environment and avoid negative actions contributing to damaging/destroying their environment. The project will contribute to resilience and a sustainable use of natural resources through involving and encouraging children and local communities to be part of planning mitigation measures and better environmental preservation. The environmental/climate change impact will be monitored during the implementation of the project through updating the section of environment on CRSA and including it in the yearly reporting of project implementation. Most of the project activities will be focused on knowledge dissemination and capacity building of different categories of community groups, the construction is limited to child friendly spaces where environmentally friendly local materials will be used such as mud and solid blocks as well as tents made from cloth for shading. The CBCPNs will attempt to intervene in other community issues including response to potential land conflict, plans for education, training and common activities in local environmental issues as part of the training plans.
