Project Summary

This project is expected to provide emergency health services for 16, 667 IDPs, Host communities & returnees in Kajo Keji, Yei, Morobo, Magwi and Yirol East counties. Based on cluster determined cost per beneficiary of $60 per person, the cost of this project will be $1,000,000. Four of the locations targeted (i.e. Kajo Keji, Yei, Morobo and Magwi) are mapped as Ebola risk counties due to the outbreak of Ebola in Neighboring Congo and the presence of unofficial crossing points. Magwi and Yirol counties are prone to Cholera and Rift valley Fever. The gaps in health service delivery are similar across the greater Yei river state and include; low EPI coverage below 1%, high burden of diseases such as malaria at 33% and RTI at 26%. Diarrhea accounts for 10% of the cases and 11% Urinary tract infections (UTIs) and other cases such as sexual transmitted infections (STIs) intestinal parasites, skin infections and trauma account for the remaining 19% (TRI-SS, EWARS & IDSR, 2019).
In 2019, TRI-SS projected a budget of 984,129 to reach 16,402 beneficiaries at the cost of $60. Through the support of the Health cluster, Rapid Response fund and UNICEF, TRI-SS secured 529,000 US dollars and reached 50,986 people. Although the funding secured was 43% less than the projected amount, the number of beneficiaries reached were thrice more than the target. The average intervention cost for the year was about $10 per beneficiary.
The intervention will use the MoH strategy to deliver services applying a mix of static and mobile approaches to reach the target population. The clinical packages planned to reach the target population are 8 PHCUs, 6 PHCCs & 4 Mobile teams. TRI-SS will implement the following activities to deliver the project;

1 - Needs

The populations of Kajo Keji, Morobo and Yei continue to experience the effects of the protracted war despite peace being signed in 2018. While displacements continue in Some parts of these counties, there is also a significant return. A June 2019 Interagency Assessment (IRNA) show that Kajo Keji still host 36,559 IDPS settled in Korijo, Keriwa and Pure IDP sites. In the same report, the number of returnees is estimate to have reached 50,194 people. A public Health situation analysis of the situation (PHSA) done by TRI-SS on behalf of the health cluster in April 2019 identified significant gaps in health service delivery including low EPI coverage below 1%, high burden of diseases such as malaria at 33% and 26% RTI, 10% diarrhea 11% Urinary tract infections (UTIs) and other cases such as sexual transmitted infections (STIs) intestinal parasites, skin infections and trauma account for the remaining 19% (TRI-SS, EWARS & IDSR, 2019). high risk of disease outbreaks (Cholera /AWD, Measles, meningitis, Hep.E, Polio and EVD. Poor WASH and WASH in facilities especially lack of water. Destroyed/damaged health facilities with >90% damaged, lack of humanitarian support for health facilities (3 facilities fully functional supported through SSHF funding), low ANC2 and skilled delivery <50%, lack of secondary health care and limited referral facilities and a high risk of HIV transmission with no ART services. In Yei, the situation is more tense at the outskirts with active fighting still being reported causing displacements. Despite this situation, a small population is coming in to Yei town from neighboring Uganda and Congo. According to IOM Event tracking report of April 2019, the number of displaced persons (IDPs) in Yei county reached 26,454 as of April 2019. The number of returnees reported at the same time were 213. Major public health threat remains the risk of Ebola Viral Disease (EVD) crossing over to Yei. Other health service delivery gaps such as inadequate service delivery points with about 66% of health facilities functional remain a key challenge. Morobo county is more proximal to Congo with a busy road linking the two countries. This puts them at a direct risk of EVD transmission. A reported outbreak of Ebola in Ariwara (Congo) about 70KM from Morobo raised a lot of concerns considering an already weak public health system in the area. County health department records show that only 3 health facilities are functional supported through HPF. In Yirol East, communities are at greater health risks due to the onset of the rainy season, poor crop harvests and lack of food and inter-communal conflicts that have caused displacements. The situation overview report (Situation Overview: Awerial, Yirol East and Yirol West Counties, Lakes, South Sudan, January-March 2019) indicates that malaria was the main health concern with 22% morbidity. 46% of the assessed settlements reported that most people had to walk for a full day to access health services. According to WFP/FAO (Special Report, FAO/WFP Crop and Food Security Assessment Mission to South Sudan, March 2019), the total estimated population of Yirol East as of Mid-2018 was 110,778 with a total of 14,746 households while IOM’s displacement tracking matrix (Mobility Tracking Round 3, Focus on: Lakes, July-August 2018) put the number of IDPs at 30,290 and returnees at 1,146 as of august 2018.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes:The project has a focus on displaced populations, returnees and host communities. IDPS and returnees are targeted 100% and flexible implementation modalities will be used such as the use of mobile teams to reach more population. TRI-SS will use UNICEF designed emergency cold chain apparatus (ARKTEK) to ensure children under five years and women of child bearing age are vaccinated in emergency situations.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes:The response is linked to the South Sudan health sector development plan 2019/20 to improve on health service delivery and access through improved coverage of delivery of full basic package of Health and nutrition services (BHNS). This response plan seeks to improve service delivery through static and mobile response mechanisms including, 8 PHCUs, 6 PHCCs & 4 Mobile teams reaching 25000 IDPS, returnees and host community.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes:This project is linked with the South Sudan health cluster strategic plan and its cluster objectives drawn from national policy documents and guidelines including the South Sudan health sector development plan 2019/20 and the stabilization and recovery of the health system document 2019.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

No but a risk management plan (mitigation measures) will be put in place. The protection environment remains fluid. The government of national unity as per the revitalized peace agreement has not being formed and there are concerns around contentment of forces. Many security analysts at the United Nations Mission in South Sudan think that areas around contentment sites are likely to face some security issues related to misconduct of the forces and lack of payment of salaries. Any collapse in the implementation of the revitalized peace agreement will worsen the protection environment displacing more people with restricted delivery of humanitarian aid and increased violence against women. TRI-SS takes the security of its staff and beneficiaries seriously. We work closely with UNOCHA, INSO and South Sudan NGO forum to monitor the security situation and follow operation recommendations. We will develop a risk management plan specific to this project to ensure timely identification of potential risks and adequate response.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes:As defined in the national humanitarian/development nexus, this response plan has in place sustainability and hand over plan. A strong coordination will be established with government line ministries and departments such as the National ministry of Health and the county health departments. This coordination will involve regular meetings, sharing of plans and tracking of activities such that at the end of the project, all actors are aware, involved and jointly figured out how to take over or fill gaps.Principally, the humanitarian actor will hand over to the county health department and its development partners.
