Project Summary

Provision of school meals to 970,000 number of children in 80 localities for 180 days, ensuring that children coming from poor and vulnerable households are able to access education. The project will incentivize learning for children who often have to walk long distances and learn on empty stomachs, gravely affecting their health and cognition abilities. School meals programmes are designed to support children coming from poor and vulnerable households, often facing critical household food insecurity and malnutrition. This leads in many parts to poor attention in class, ill-health and subsequent absenteeism and drop out.
Emerging evidence is seeing nutritious school meals as a necessary component of health for school children. In UK, the medical sector is calling for an expanded Free School Meals to all children in households that live on Universal Credit, arguing that it is a first step to improve children's nutrition and protect their health.
In Sudan, the HNO notes that, 4.5 million school-aged children (ages 6-18) are experiencing crisis, emergency, or catastrophic levels of food insecurity. Most of these children are not attending school; those who are, are at a high risk of dropping out or having their educational attainment curtailed if they are not adequately supported through a full package of educational interventions, including school feeding. Food insecurity, driven by many factors including a protracted conflict and disasters is expected to increase in 2023. This is against a background of two years of the Covid 19 pandemic whose impacts are still felt in many sectors, the Ukraine-Russian war which has affected food and oil supply chains driving up the cost of food. The additional factors of teachers' strikes, floods, closures of schools all greatly affect the children's ability to catch up with their counterparts in other countries.
The school meals will address both hunger at school and enable consistent class attendance for the children which will positively contribute to their academic performance. In addition, nutrition awareness will be carried out to both PTAs and children on the benefits of healthy diets.

1 - Needs

By the end of the 2021-22 academic year, Sudan had an estimated 6.9million school-aged children (SAC) who are out of school. Low literacy and numeracy levels characterise the Sudan Education sector. Additionally, 4.5million of SAC are experiencing a harsh crisis of food insecurity and deteriorating social economic conditions. High levels of malnutrition and anemia among children of school age are a common factor. Floods have damaged schools' infrastructure. The impact of the Covid 19 which still linger on, are compounded by the regular political demonstrations and teachers' strikes. All these factors negatively impact the learning opportunities of the school children, ultimately reducing their education potential. 3.7million school-age children (SAC) of ages 3-18 years have been affected by conflict and natural disasters. 664,834 of these are internally displaced while 209,384 are refugees and 207,194 children have returned to Sudan after seeking refugee elsewhere but remain vulnerable. A total of 2.6 million SAC non-displaced children are vulnerable. The most affected regions are the Darfurs, Kordofans, Blue Nile and Kassala states. However, the rest of the country is facing social and economic difficulties, making poorer households more vulnerable, with children being the most affected.Children of Sudan face multiple challenges, which is worse for girl children, those living in conflict areas and the disabled. Early/child marriages and pregnancies are not uncommon. Withdrawal of children from school amidst an economic meltdown tends to be one of the immediate responses by families, fueling further the inter-generational illiteracy, malnutrition and poverty. Anemia or iron deficiency is particularly linked to poor cognition in children, leading to lower IQ and overall poor academic performance. The impact of this on a country's human capital is unimaginable. low-income countries are said to lose economic potential of between 50 and 70% in the long term due to low IQ. The Education Cluster as a collective body target to support 2.4million children with a package of interventions to reduce their vulnerability. Among these interventions is provision of nutrition sensitive school meals to address both poor nutrition and hunger to enable quality learning for the vulnerable children.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Based on the IPC data, WFP proposes to implement school feeding in selected vulnerable localities in where use of school meals will motivate children to go to school, and keep them there to learn; and also, the provision of nutritious meals will be used to address hunger and poor nutrition among the school children.Based on the Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) rankings, localities falling under 3 and above will be targeted for school feeding.Schools assessments to determine their eligibility will be conducted. As much as possible, all eligible schools will be put on the programme to lessen 'student migration' because of school meals. Evidence indicates that when school feeding is provided to only some of the schools in a locality, schools without meals experience pupil migration, distorting the real increase in enrolment. When all schools participate, it is easier to observe the actual increase based on out of school children being motivated to go to school.All PTAs will be trained and oriented in effective school feeding, roles and responsibilities to ensure consistent meals to the children.Nutrition education through SBCC will be conducted to teachers, children and communities through PTAs on healthy diets, basic information on food groups to contribute towards improved nutrition and health among schoolchildren;Cooks, store managers and school feeding focal points will receive more detailed training on menu development, food safety and quality and ensure that to the extent possible, in case schools can buy fresh foods to complement the food basket from local farmers. In addition, non-food items in form of pots, plates, cups, measuring spoons and weighing scales may have to be procured for new schools.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Under the Climate Fund, WFP is working towards improving cooking energy to reduce on consumption of firewood, which is the predominant fuel used in school meals. Fuel efficient stoves is being evaluated for possible use. A study conducted in Rwanda recently on school feeding and cooking indicates very high use of firewood which increases the cost meals and exposes women to open fires. WFP is studying the recommendations to ascertain which ones are feasible for Sudan.

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In alignment with the WFP Humanitarian Protection Policy, WFP remains committed to ensure that the assistance does no harm to the safety, dignity and integrity of the people it serves. In the design phase, WFP conducts in-depth consultations to key stakeholders including schoolteachers for needs collection and inform beneficiaries, school children and other relevant individuals of their entitlements (e.g. what to be served at what time) to ensure that everyone is aware of their entitlement. In the implementation phase, regular visits to and monitoring at project sites will take place throughout the project period by dedicated staff including senior gender and protection associates who are based in Area Offices with support from four officers in gender and protection team based in Khartoum.

WFP is part of the Sector Advisory Group and Education Cluster at Area Office levels. WFP will at the beginning of the project convene other stakeholders to highlight the project objectives and modus operandi. Quarterly updates will be provided to the Education Clusters and other sectoral groups to ensure that issues emerging from communities, schools and being experienced elsewhere that may hurt the project integrity are addressed. The project will also work closely with other projects focusing on child protection to ensure that the rights of children are preserved and protected.In addition, inter-agency collaboration in Community Feedback Mechanism has been established with other UN agencies including UNHCR, which enables to ensure wider coverage and a smooth coordination. Also, WFP has a dedicated PSEA advisor and actively contributes to inter-agency fora such as PSEA network at national and state levels. Regular participation in these forums enhanced collaboration and coordination to strengthen WFP’s approach to PSEA throughout the project.
