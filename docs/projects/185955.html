Project Summary

Since late 2017, increased insecurity, violence and loss of lives have forced thousands of families to flee their homes. Civilians have been killed and displacement continues to have serious consequences on livelihoods and living conditions of the affected populations. Vulnerability has been further compounded by limited access to education for children due to a ban and attacks on schools by armed groups. Insecurity in the affected regions remains high, with continuing armed attacks and confrontations between the military and armed groups. The violence has uprooted 711,025 people according to UNHCR Operational Data Portal 2021, from their homes. The majority of the displaced are women and children. Food, shelter and civilian protection are the principal humanitarian needs, although across all sectors needs are high. Many of the conflict-affected people are growing more vulnerable as the violence persists and humanitarian aid remains inadequate. The people who flee the war in the North West and South West were mostly women and youth abandoning their livelihoods and education. Increased IDP population strains resources including living homes, create conflicts as they try to integrate into their new environment and increased certain negative coping strategies such as prostitution, manipulation and exploitation of women and youths. Housses of hosts in Yaounde have been overcrowded by relatives fleeing the war with as many as 15 persons in a home and sometimes 6 persons in a single room, coupled with scarcity and high price rates on food items, with people sleeping on the bare floor because of overcrowded beds and lack of extra mattresses.
This project aims at strengthening the economic capacity of 220 vulnerable IDPs and households in Nfoundi Division in the Centre Region) by building strong micro-enterprises that will empower them focusing on environmentally sustainable recovery activities. It will also set up and strengthen institutions, community and individual capacity to enhance social cohesion between IDPs and their host communities, improve resilience and promote risk prevention and management.
Livelihoods: IDPs households’ (HHs) livelihoods are insufficient to meet their basic needs. An assessment conducted reveals that, 83% of vulnerable IDPs HHs have earnings less than 25,000 XAF per month. Half of the population has insufficient food. Although their host families have similar rates of food insufficiency, they cope by accepting other options. IDPs have limited skills and assets to grow or diversify their livelihood and do not have enough access to financial institutions. They face limited options when seeking loans to invest, or options to save money since what they have as income is very little to provide for adequate feeding. With limited opportunities, IDP HHs practice negative coping strategies. Women have engaged in transactional sex and prostitution; children move around to work in people’s homes where they are vulnerable to recruitment into prostitution or exploitation.
Assets: Challenges are reflected in IDP asset stock. Most women who flee the war were engage in agriculture while youth are either drop outs, graduates or school children who stayed out of school for over three years due to lockdowns. IDPs are trying to restore their livelihoods in a depressed local economy with limited experience in the dominant livelihoods activity (agriculture) but will not have access to land and even those who want to engage in petty trading do not have startup capital while youths may show interest in vocational training as a means to come out of poverty.
Protection and Social Cohesion Risks: Despite facing limited livelihoods opportunities, IDPs face difficulties in either integrating with their host or their immediate environment especially as they our coming from a different cultural and linguistic backgrounds. Suspicion, fear and conflicts on available local resources now exists.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Assessment conducted in the seven sub divisions in Nfoundi (1-7) shows that IDP women and youth are interested in small enterprise development. It is hoped that 30% of the beneficiaries will be trained on marketing skills and each receives a startup capital of 100.000FCFC to begin a business, while 70% will undergo vocational training for 4 – 6 months on vocational trainings based on their motivation. In order to enable these vulnerable IDPs to be effective in the various trades they have chosen, training kits will be provided based on the type of trade selected and demand. A startup capital will also be provided to enable all who have graduated training to set up and manage their own enterprises.Objective 1 : Women and young adults generate income. Activity 1. Stimulate entrepreneurship and Small Businesses: WARDA will capitalize on the entrepreneurial spirit of the IDPs by providing small grants to help traders, identify potential small business beneficiaries and will conduct initial assessments and trainings to design business plans and will base the cash grant value according to the plan and support the 66 entrepreneurs with business grants worth approximately 100,000 FCFA (70% of the grants for female while 30% for male). Upon completion of the course, the beneficiaries will have the opportunity to present their business plans for consideration for a cash grant. Activity 2) WARDA will form and train 14 CIGs groups to encourage women to participate in gender-integrated groups that will help in mobilizing internal financial resources through the establishment of savings and loan schemes. Activity 3). WARDA will engage unemployed women and youth who have expressed interest in trades and technical training by supporting a system of apprenticeships practiced in the area of masonry, carpentry, shoe making, and hair dressing, restaurant, embroidery, tailoring. In collaboration with the technical departments in the Ministry of Employment and professional (vocational) training. WARDA will support this system by paying 154 IDP women/youth monthly training costs ranging from 25000 CFA per month to the trainer, per trainee. Training materials will be provided based on the training identified by the trainee. Upon completion WARDA will provide the apprentices with a start-up kit with basic tools to start their own trade. It will adopt 70% women and 30% men as one of the criteria Objective 2 - Protection through Social Cohesion: WARDA will combine the social cohesion approach and appreciative inquiry aiming to encourage conflicting parties to think and act positively. Seven workshops targeting divided groups (IDPs and host community members) will be organized that will help participants visualize conflict and build shared vision of social cohesion. Result 2.1: IDPs and host communities participate in the training activities. Conflict Mitigation: This project will increase tolerance, develop peacebuilding skills, and strengthen relationships among IDPs and host communities. To do this, the program will: 1) build capacities of IDPs and local populations to identify, prevent and mitigate conflict, and 2) support community development initiatives that foster solidarity and understanding. Key protection activities are: 1) Trauma awareness training for potential trauma healers.2) Training on trauma healing, social cohesion, and peaceful conflict resolution techniques for persons who will be leading peacebuilding efforts and discussions in their communities. 3) Training on social-cohesion techniques for men and women who are leaders and key stakeholders in conflicts management 4) Establishing or reinforcing 14 mixed-group community social cohesion committees (CSCCs), where members can discuss issues in the community and collectively find durable solutions in a peaceful manner.
