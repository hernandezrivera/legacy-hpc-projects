Project Summary

According to HNO 2021, 13.4 m people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance - a 21% increase compared to 2020. Between 2020-2021, the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in NWSyria has increased from 2.8 to 3.4 m people. The overall humanitarian situation remains dire for 4.2 m people living in Idleb and Afrin as well as A’zaz to Jarablus areas in NWSyria. Almost 12,000 children have been killed or injured since2011. With around half of Syria’s children having known nothing but a lifetime of crisis - 2.45 m of whom were estimated to be out of school in 2020 alone. 22% of assessed communities report child labor as frequently occurring, while child marriage of young and adolescent girls (12-17 years) is reported by 18% of assessed communities as a very common issue. Grave violations against children continue to be documented, including the use and recruitment of children and sexual violence against girls and boys. The type and scale of needs faced by people and families in NW Syria is pervasive and multifaceted due to a convergence of factors arising from prolonged and multiple displacement and lingering conflict, a sharp reduction in purchasing power, the loss of essential livelihoods and income, mounting food insecurity, limited access to basic services as well as the immediate and longer-term effects of the COVID-19. This project works on child protection and general protection and protection and prevention against GBV. SDI aims at protecting and empowering children &adolescents. Interventions will include conducting assessments/researches, communal awareness & dissemination of information on available child protection services, training of social workers & other stakeholders, opening safe spaces for children, enhance the educational status of children, increase the accessibility of children & adolescents, especially girls & CwDs to education, support case referrals, optimizing children protection through engagement with other sectors, like education, provide training to teachers on how to engage with GBV survivors and make referrals, training for teachers, students, parents, and community on health (including Covid-19), child protection and GBV issues, empowering adolescents by enhancing their psychological, educational, & vocational status and raising their awareness, raise the awareness of communities to participate in creating a more enabling environment for adolescents, participate in narrowing the gap between stakeholders, and local authorities and the adolescents in future programming. and, promoting positive relations between the host community & IDPs. Moreover, SDI will support women &adolescent girls empowerment & protection and work on implementing GBV PPMs (protection and prevention mechanisms) through conducting assessments/research, distributing dignity kits, conducting recreational activities, raising awareness campaigns on GBV &covid-19, skills/ vocational & income generating trainings/activities, support promoting women and young girls resilience and livelihood opportunities, provide financial/in-kind support for women, dissemination of information concerning GBV services for survivors and referrals, provide training for counselors, and training on GBV basic concepts, case management, PSS, and Covid-19, support women’s accessibility to women-related services. Open women-safe spaces, provide specialized GBV services. And, we will work to implement PPMs against PSEA by conducting assessments/research, and applying PSEA mitigation measures, for example. Beneficiaries, 70% of IDPs & 30% of host communities, and adolescents and youth will form at least 50% of beneficiaries. Beneficiaries will be of men, women, boys, girls and PWDs& older persons with prioritization for the of the most vulnerable. Females will form at least 50% of beneficiaries. M&E is part of all activities

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, 13.4 m people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance. The overall humanitarian situation remains dire for 4.2 m people living in Idleb and Afrin as well as A’zaz to Jarablus areas in NWSyria. Almost 12,000 children have been killed/injured since2011. 2.45 m of children were estimated to be out of school in 2020 alone. Facing deteriorated living standards, families are adopting harmful coping mechanisms. 22% of assessed communities report child labor as frequently occurring, while child marriage of girls of 12-17 years is reported by 18% of them as a very common issue. Grave violations against children continue to be documented, including the use and recruitment of children (813 verified cases in 2020) & sexual violence against girls and boys. 287 related violations verified in 2020. More than 1.5 m of the 2.7 m IDPs in the NW currently shelter in over 1,200 “last resort” sites. Women &children represent 80 % of this caseload while more than 22,000 IDPs are reported to be persons with specific needs. Besides, SDI has recently conduct need assessment that showed the deep need in the following: The communities targeted by this project in Syria are in need for Safe access to humanitarian assistance/services with dignity. Protection from discrimination, violence, abuse, exploitation, deliberate deprivation or neglect of vulnerable individuals/groups, like women & children, SGBV related risks, child labor. And According to the data collected on adolescent, it was found that the most important interventions needed were:1. Raising the educational level. 2. Increasing awareness among adolescents. 3. Vocational training. 4. psychological support.

5 - Implementation of activities

SDI offices in Syria & Turkey will coordinate to get the best result. Procurement & recruitment will be done according to SDI’s procedures & policies. Beneficiaries will be selected without discrimination. SDI with its experience & wide network will opt at reaching the most vulnerable categories, like PwDs, and they will be included in all stages of the project cycle. The implementation will take into consideration the specific needs of each category which will grants the inclusion of the most vulnerable depending on IASC guidances. SDI will mainstream protection in all activities and take risk mitigation measures while implementation. M&E will be present at all implementation stages; continuous monitoring of the project activities and outputs will be conducted. M&E will use the tools provided by CPSC and GBVSC to monitor the different activities of this project, like the Case management tool. Moreover, beneficiaries’ satisfaction will be measured. M&E will identify best practices and lessons learned. Implementation of all activities will take into consideration IASC guidances, like, GBVSC SOPs, Guidance on strengthening disability inclusion in Humanitarian Response, the Awareness Raising tools. reporting, like, 4Ws, will be key component throughout the implementation of the project. Implementation activities will be done in coordination with local authority/ community leaders. Accountability will be placed allowing the reception of beneficiaries’ feedback & complaints. Employees will read and sign the CoC, and will be trained on GBV, PSEA, and Covid-19. SDI’s vendors/contractors in touch with the beneficiaries will sign CoC and receive sensitization on GBV&PSEA. PSEA mitigation measure & Covid-19 will be mainstreamed.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Risk of leaving harmful impact on children in friendly spaces if they were mistreated. Level: low. Impact: High. Mitigation: conduct sensitization training for all staff dealing with children& regular visits by M&E & well-announced complaint receiving mechanisms. 2. Risk of children receiving/referred to specialized PSS/psychological assistance feeling ashamed/stigmatized. Risk: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: Staff trained on referring children in a manner that protect their privacy&security. 3. Risk of women receiving/referred to specialized PSS/psychological assistance feeling ashamed/stigmatized. Risk: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: Staff trained on referring women in a manner that protect their privacy&security. 4. Risk of women in safe spaces or children in friendly spaces got harassed/assaulted. Risk: low Impact: high Mitigation: safe & friendly spaces being guarded, strong doors lucks & locations chosen carefully. 5. Risk of women & girls getting harassed/exploited/feel ashamed during distribution of dignity kits. Level: Medium Impact: high Mitigation: choosing a place that is safe as the distribution point, complaint box in place, employing women staff for distribution, M&E shall be present there. 6. Risk of women getting harassed/exploited during income generating activities or skill/vocational training. level: low, Impact: High Mitigation: Choosing a place that is safe for women, complaint box, M&E make regular visit to the place, and having female staff members to deal with women.7. Risk of new outbreak of Covid-19. Level: high, Impact: Medium, Mitigation: SDI is member in the health cluster& field staff is trained on the preventative measures. WHO directions will be followed for mitigation measures.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

SDI is a member of the PSEA network. Beneficiaries are at risk on being exposed to any sexual misconduct by the humanitarian workers and they might not report the misconduct due to the following reason: 1. lack of knowledge of reporting mechanisms, especially for people with disabilities and women living in far away areas. 2. feeling afraid of retaliation. 3. fear of stigma. 4. fear of deprivation of assistance. 5. being unable to understand written banners for being illiterate and/or for having a visual disability. 6. for children, language might be complicated To mitigate these risks, SDI will :1. aim at clearly announcing the hotline number and email specialized for PSEA of SDI and UN numbers. through many means, like, banners, social media, and messages to beneficiaries phones through telegram channel to ensure reaching to the biggest number of beneficiaries including women living in far away areas and people with disabilities. 2. Conduct PSEA training for all staff members and contractors/suppliers dealing with beneficiaries and make them read and sign CoC that state Zero Tolerance towards SEA violations. 3. Conduct awareness raising sessions on PSEA for beneficiaries to encourage them to report any violations and stress on the confidentiality and on eliminating any other fears that they might have.4. Conduct awareness raising sessions for children in an appropriate language. 5. complaint boxes6. Using PSEA field and organizational checklists tools.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Risk of lacking required permission that imped opening friendly spaces for children or safe spaces for women. Level: low, Impact: high Mitigation: working in location were our organization has good relation and strong contacts to acquire permission from local authorities and/or community leaders. 2. Shelling and worsening of security situation are continuous risks for the overall project, Risk: medium Impact: high Mitigation: choosing locations that are safe and training our staff on retreat and evacuation plans and how to act during emergencies. 3. Risk of local authorities/communities impeding activities implementation. Level: low Impact: high Mitigation: working in location were our organization has good relation and strong contacts to acquire permission from local authorities. 4. Risk of local authorities/communities impeding awareness campaigns or dissemination of information on the protection of women and children especially against GBV risks. Level: low Impact: high Mitigation: working in location were our organization has good relation and strong contacts to acquire permission from local authorities and were communities are open to such campaigns. 4. Risk: Closure of Border Crossing Points, Likelihood: High; Impact: Low.Mitigation: This will have a low impact as all needed materials are available in Syria and will be procured in Syria according to SDI's experience. SDI will keep informing humanitarian actors and advocating through advocacy channels to continue the response in Northwest Syria.
