Project Summary

Since the health and protection-related needs are among the most prominent within Jisr-Ash-shugur district with 23062 and 35569 individuals are in need of health and protection assistance respectively, Al Resala Foundation RF seeks to address the most urgent needs across the aforementioned sectors of people with disabilities within Jisr-Ash-shugur district. RF plans to address those needs through establishment of this facility that will be providing physical rehabilitation and psychosocial support services for the targeted individuals. Such intervention shall address the shortages in terms of specialized health and protection services currently suffered across Jisr-Ash-shugur district.

Needs

Given that the Syrian conflict being the biggest crisis of the 21st century since the world war II, The consequences of economic decline, deteriorating living conditions and social fragmentation have had a deep and far-reaching impact on people in Syria.According to the humanitarian needs overview data for Syria 2018, Within the overall 13.1 million people in need, some 2.9 million Syrian people are now treated as people with disabilities were such individuals would subsequently undergo specific needs that cut across multiple sectors with their disabilities limiting their access to services fundamental to their wellbeing and survival and exposing them to different forms of violence , what would further aggregate their life conditions and render them requiring special consideration in the response planning and prioritization.In the same context, the multi sectorial needs assessment conducted on August 2017 to inform the HNO for Syria 2018 showed that needs for specialized services for people with disabilities have been reported in 72% of assessed communities.Jisr-Ash-shugur district being not an exception to the Syrian context, is also lacking provision of services that address humanitarian needs of persons with disabilities, what would highly inform the need for further intervention to address those needs

Implementation of activities

Early in the project implementation phase, the facility-based staff are planned to be provided with capacity building sessions including and not limited to psychological first aid PFA, psychosocial support PSS and child safeguarding mainstreaming, were the skills achieved through those training sessions will be potentially utilized by the staff while providing similar quality services beyond the duration of the proposed project.Also RF plans to equip a facility with the required items for conducting both physical rehabilitation and psychosocial support servicesIn addition, The project's logical framework with its set indicators, the activity work plan and the detailed budget are the foundation of the monitoring process. Project data is collected on a monthly basis from the field, compiled by RF Turkey office in its HMIS and budget control software and reviewed by technical and administrative staff to ensure quality control, adherence to the project proposal, financial and administrative guidelines and for financial control. Photo documentation will complement the monitoring process.The Health Program Coordinator will conduct on-site field visits for review of the project implementation according to the project proposal, supporting the field with problem solving and technical support as neededRF and will conduct a mid-term and final review. Standard monitoring and evaluation techniques are applied throughout the project phase to ensure progress and achievement of the set objectives, outputs and targets and for transparency

Protection Risk Analysis

The list of the key protection risks includes and not limited to the followings:(Abbreviations: HFs: Healthcare facilities/ LCs: Local councils/ HDs: Health directorates/ L: Low/ M: Mediu m/H: High)1. Beneficiaries are affected by conflict related incidences while seeking healthcare at the HFs.• Likelihood: L/ Impact: H• Mitigation: HFs are based in areas included in the de escalation zones agreement at adequate distances from gatherings of armed groups. Establishment of MoUs between RF and local authorities (Civilian LCs and HDs) to ensure exclusion of the HFs from any possible clashes. Technical support by RF safety and security team. Training of staff on contingency plans. Establishment of emergency exits. 2. While at HFs, Beneficiaries are abused, harassed, undergo compromised privacy or dignity.• Likelihood: L/ Impact: H• Mitigation: Ensure individuals of vulnerable groups (children, women, elderly and people with disabilities) are accompanied while at the HFs. Child safeguarding training provided for all staff. Ensure adequate female staff representation. Application of privacy screens. Separation of medical examination theaters. Establishment of breast feeding corners. Separation of the bathrooms by sex. Label tags to be demonstrated within the HFs. Establishment of adequate waiting areas. 3. Confidentiality of the information provided by the Beneficiaries is compromised.• Likelihood: L/ Impact: H• Mitigation: staff of the HFs adapt RF code of conduct. Tags about confidentiality of patient records and information demonstrated at the HFs. Maintaining patient records through well secured and strictly monitored modalities inaccessible to public. Accountability leaflets disseminated among targeted communities. Complaint mechanisms are offered for the beneficiaries. 4. Communities are affected by medical wastes produced at the HFs.Likelihood: L/ Impact: H• Mitigation: Securing the medical wastes prior to disposal. Application of medical waste disposal systems at all HFs.5. Staff/ beneficiaries acquire healthcare associated infections.• Likelihood: M/ Impact: M to H• Mitigation: programmed use of disinfection measures. Programmed microbiological screening of the HFs. Establishment of clean and well ventilated waiting areas. Proper handling of medical wastes. Use of personal protective equipment when indicated
