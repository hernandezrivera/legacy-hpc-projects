Project Summary

Lebanon was hit by unprecedented ascending multiple crises at a time, which is negatively impacting the lives of boys and girls living on its soil. More of 80% of the Lebanese, PRL and migrant populations are under the poverty line, leading to exposing children to child labor in its worst forms as schools are closed, or became inaccessible. Lebanese children are more and more in need of an adequate response as other response plans failed to compensate the emerging need, since targeted areas were linked to the presence of Syrian refugees, leaving the host and other cohorts in other areas underserved.
All proposed activities are long awaited by the affected populations, given the fact that the needs are exponentially increasing since the onset of the financial crisis. himaya first suggests implementing an integrated package of community based and focused psychosocial activities, addressed to both children and parents as well as case management services in Akkar, specifically in the most vulnerable areas of Wadi Khaled. According to interviews conducted with several decision makers in the region, some villages in Wadi Khaled didn’t get any support from NGOs since 2016. Furthermore, as per Unicef field focal point and the CP sector coordinator in the North, there is a big gap in child protection services in the region, especially in Hnaider, Amayer, Kneiseh and Majdal, knowing that these villages are listed in the high priority localities to implement an integrated approach. Community members identified the stateless people, especially children as the most vulnerable and marginalized groups in Wadi Khaled society. Children are deprived from their basic rights: Their right to play and the right for a descent education. Community members mentioned that a huge number of children are exposed to different kind of abuse: Neglect (Children left in the streets with no safety measures taken), sexual harassment (for boys and girls), Child Labor, Child Marriage, and Violent discipline. Furthermore, a big tension is highlighted between the Lebanese and Syrian children.
Another intervention is proposed in Ras Baalback, a forgotten area calling for himaya's help, via its community based groups such as the scouts. Few children were recently referred to himaya's office in Kaa for case management, and the absence of local actors is a concern. Parents are in distress, as their monthly salaries are currently worth less than 40 usd and they cannot endure severe conditions anymore. Finally, an extremely important component is to cover in both Bekaa and Baalbek- Hermel governorates, which is the judicial protection of children as himaya is mandated to cover this service.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

In line with the sector objective and with its first output, himaya will provide specialized case management entailing of an intervention of both a social worker and a psychologist to protect the children at medium to high risk while working with their parents and the environment to decrease the risk on the child and ensure a sustainable care plan. This activity will target 120 children in Wadi Khaled during a period of 11 months, and another 27 children in Ras Baalbek during 7 months. 190 high risk children in both Bekaa and Baalback- Hermel governorates will be judicially protected, not to forget providing all children in imminent danger with alternative care solutions and referral to adequate service providers. 190 children will also benefit from focused psychosocial intervention especially under certain thematic such as violence and child labor to address the risk. Himaya will continue adopting its community based approach under objective 2 through strengthening the child protection networks in the area, outreaching, then providing community based PSS to both children (1150) and caregivers (150) who will benefit from life skills to overcome the hardship and its impact on the protection risks. Himaya's well establish community groups will also detect and refer cases in need of more focused or specialized intervention. 3 initiatives will be led with the participation of the community to strengthen the CP networks in both governorates , including judicial protection.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

All himaya services address PSEA by providing information/awareness to beneficiaries (children, caregivers, community groups, trainees and volunteers) and reporting mechanisms/ pathways. All himaya curricula and care plans consist directly of responding to protection needs through informing, responding, detecting and referring children and caregivers to service providers with whom himaya will collaborate with on a need based approach. All himaya content, data segregation, intervention strategies and programming will be designed in respect to gender, disability and social inclusion

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

AAP is mainstreamed throughout himaya's strategy and programs and will be reflected in the project deliverables: himaya ensures feedback and complaint mechanisms to all of its beneficiaries (through complaint boxes and visuals present in all of its service delivery locations in addition to other pathways such as social media, and hotline). Feedback are tracked, taken seriously and a proper action is taken. All beneficiaries are consulted before any care plan is set or any service is delivered so that they decide on their need and actively participate in the process. Localization is prioritized via the capacity building that is ensured to the CBOs, the adapted material and language in respect to the locals, and the exit strategy that ensures sustainability of the action beyond the project. Monitoring and Evaluation tools will accompany the project via data management, analysis, accountability and learning. All resources will be optimize to reach the most vulnerable
