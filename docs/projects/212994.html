Project Summary

The protracted crisis in Syria continues to affect millions of lives. The security situation in parts of
the country remains unpredictable, and the economic situation is increasingly dire. In Syria, an
estimated 90 per cent of the population lives in poverty, 12.9 million are food insecure, and 6.8
million are internally displaced. Having to contend with multi-faceted crises, 16.7 million people
need humanitarian assistance, a 9 per cent increase from the previous year. More than six million children, including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse and exploitation, and need critical child protection services. The ongoing hostilities, protracted displacement and limited access to basic services continue to have a significant effect on the physical, mental and social well-being of children and caregivers.
Insecurity and economic hardship exacerbate child protection concerns and fuel harmful coping mechanisms. 96% of children live in communities where child labour is reported.8 Another 84% of children live in communities where child marriage is an issue for girls (15 -17 years) and 52% of people live in communities where family separation is reported.
Amal will provide CP prevention and response services in three different cities in NW Syria (Idelb, Harim and Suran) through the centers currently open in Idleb and Harim cities and the center that will be established in Suran to provide safe protection services for children and facilitate their access to the needed services, and enhance equitable access to quality CP services, including for children with disabilities, through two main priorities:
1) improving the quality of community-based CP through behavior change and awareness raising on specific risks and psychosocial support.
2) improving the reach and quality of specialized services (Case Management) for child survivors of violence, exploitation, neglect, and abuse (including child laborers, separated children and GBV child survivors).
Amal will provide the key activities below:
1. Provision of three levels of Child Protection Case Management supporting children facing different types of child protection risks including child labor, family separation, mental health and psychosocial distress and sexual and Gender Based Violence. This will target 500 children (300 male, 200 female). Some of those cases and based on the action plan developed will be supported also with cash funds in the framework of case management as following:
High Risk Cases: Maximum amount of 300 USD
Medium risk: Maximum amount of 200 USD
Low Risk: Maximum of 75 Euro USD
2. Provision of structured and semi structured PSS activities for children. Children will be divided into two main age groups (3-8) & (9-17) Total target is 500 (250 boys and 250 girls)
3. Provision of recreational activities inside the CFS. Total target is 2500 (1250 boys and 1250 girls)
4. Facilitating parenting skills sessions for caregivers. Total target is 300 (100 male and 200 female)
5. Outreach and Identification Teams.
PRA: Risk that existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles (e.g. child rights messaging is perceive as a threat). Low possibility and consultations with group of male and female community members as well as community leaders were conducted in the design phase and will continue during the period of implementation to mitigate this risk.
Risk of creating or perpetuating stigma for girls, boys and their families receiving specialized services. Possibility is medium and to mitigate this risk all staff will be trained on CPMS and confidentiality.
PSEA: the risk of PSEA is always one of the risks of the humanitarian interventions. The possibility is low as all our teams are trained on PSEA, CoC is available and signed by all staff and contractors, complaint mechanism is available, confidential and easy to access for all beneficiaries including complaint boxes, whatsapp and email.
