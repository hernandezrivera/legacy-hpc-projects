Project Summary

In 2023, UNICEF will reach 90,251.2 girls, boys, women and men, including persons with disabilities and gender orientation, with GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response in the camps and host community of Cox's Bazar (80 percent will be in host community and 20 percent in camps).
UNICEF will strengthen GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response, and ensure preparedness for emergencies and resilience to shocks. UNICEF will continue to work with Government Ministries, especially the Ministry of Women's and Children's Affairs, to strengthen the overarching system to address GBV in a durable and sustainable manner. This will be done through capacity development and technical support to, inter alia, the social service workforce, law enforcement personnel, probation officers and others to improve the access of children and women to quality GBV services. This also comprises strengthening GBV referral services and providing a more robust continuum of care between ministerial and departmental jurisdictions, and NGO service providers, for survivors and those at risk. UNICEF will train and mentor 60 social workers and caseworkers to lead response to GBV through case management. Services will be provided through 19 safe spaces for women and girls - providing a one-stop facility that aim minimize stigma for those who access them - inclusive of Psychosocial support and skills development actvities in a confidential space. Advocacy for equitable access to justice and social services wil be stepped up.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya refugee crisis is set within the broader context of a district that lags markedly in Bangladesh in terms of key child protection and human development indicators. Approximately 917,486 people are estimated to be living in Rohingya refugee camps, with 52 percent being children (GoB/UNHCR Fact Sheet 31-Oct-2021). The district at large has a population of approximately 2,650,000 of whom 541,000 reside in the host communities of Ukhiya and Teknaf. The situation of children and women is delicate, with many protection concerns arising out of gender-scripted social norms and attitudes. Girls and women are especially exposed to GBV, with child- and female-headed households and persons with disabilities particularly vulnerable. Child marriage and child trafficking are prevalent. Protection risks are compounded by cultural mores that restricts the movement of girls and women and can deny them access to services and support networks. The population inside and outside the Rohingya camps has been affected by the protracted refugee crisis. The context has exacerbated GBV concerns with a particular increase in “intimate partner violence” reported. The August 2020 analysis of the GBV Information Management System showed that intimate partner violence constituted 84.4% of all reported GBV incidents compared to 72% before the Covid19 pandemic. Such dynamics within the household have a terrible impact on the well-being of children and adolescents exposed to it. An assessment carried out by UNICEF and the Child Protection Sub-Sector in October 2021 found that respondents in the camps most frequently expressed concern about were child labour (64% of respondents), child neglect (59%), child marriage (57%), child trafficking (32%), depression (27%), separation of children (25%), and physical abuse (23%). A joint UNICEF and Government of Bangladesh assessment on basic services reported that 35% of respondents believe marriage below the age 18 is acceptable, slightly higher than the national average (31%). According to J-MSNA September 2021, 65 percent of girls and women reported a need for protection services. However, social norms often have the effect of limiting their freedom to access support. Furthermore, although girls and women are disproportionately affected by GBV, studies have also shown that boys and men are also affected by sexual violence (Women’s Refugee Commission, 2018). While robust GBV interventions to prevent and respond exist, there are still huge gaps to be addressed, to improve the quality of services. The gaps include limited access to specialized services such as Health, PSS, and legal assistance, lack of social service workforce capacity, cultural barriers that hinder disclosure, the normalization of GBV, and lack of knowledge of available services (GBVSS 2020).

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The Project will ensure that Rohingya refugees have equitable access to GBV services in a predictable, efficient, timely, transparent and accountable manner. The Project is in line with the one camp approach and will ensure common strategies, systems, protocols, and tools wherever possible. UNICEF and implementing partners provide a consolidated package of services in the camps and host communities thus ensuring equity in service provision. The Project will work with the Government, UN, NGOs and donor community together with the relevant sectors and working groups to further avoid gaps and overlaps.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The Project incorporates protection principles and promotes meaningful access, safety and dignity to children and women in the Rohingya refugee camps and host communities. Through service delivery nodes (esp. Safe Spaces for Women and Girls) and ensuring robust outreach to the most vulnerable, the Project will ensure access to assistance and services without discrimination or barrier. Embedded within project design is an information and feedback mechanism through which the affected population can demand accountability for the adequacy of interventions, and for UNICEF and implementing partners to address concerns and complaints.

3.2 - Gender

The Project has been tailored to meet the needs of girls and women. Gender equity has been considered in all facets of project design including recognition of the particular protection concerns facing girls and women, such as the high levels of GBV that prevail. Girls and women will benefit from dedicated Safe Spaces for Women and Girls providing bespoke case management, Psychosocial support and skills development services. Adolescents will benefit from Adolescent Sexual and Reproductive Health guidance, pre-vocational preparedness recreation activities. The support to adolescents will recognize their capacities as agents of positive change in their families and communities. Mothers and fathers will benefit from positive parenting support. Overall,the Project will respond to all forms of abuse, exploitation, violence (including GBV) adopting a socio-ecological approach that recognizes the roles of children, families and communities in the protective environment.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The Project aims to prevent and respond to GBV. Age-specific interventions are provided throughout, including Psychosocial support activities and adolescent life skills-based learning. UNICEF has stringent standards and protocols related to Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Implementing partners are assessed for rigor in this regard and are required to be rated as low risk. UNICEF is a key member of the PSEA Network.

3.4 - Diversity

The Project aims to provide equitable access to prevention and response services across the full diversity of the target population. While the Project does not have a specific focus on encouraging diversity (e.g. sexual orientation), all actvities are undertaken free of discrimination or judgement.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Embedded within project design is an information and feedback mechanism through which the affected population can demand accountability for the adequacy of interventions, and for UNICEF and implementing partners to address concerns and complaints. Ensure Accountability of the Affected Population (AAP) i) ensure women, men, boys, girls and persons with disabilities and diversities, participate in assessment, decision making and programming, and ii) enhance UNICEF information feedback Centres (IFCs) and community-based groups in camps and host community, to receive and send feedback to and from the affected populations on their concerns and services.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

A central aim of the Project is to ensure sustainability and resilience in GBV programming. Key to this is the systems-strengthening approach together with community-based protection - both of which compliment and mutually reinforce each other - and aim to reduce reliance on humanitarian service provision over time. In this way, the Project aims to make children and women more resilient to shocks, and thus contribute to DRR with a GBV lens. Further, ensure adequate preparedness and response plans in place to address the impacts of natural disasters including, monsoon, cyclone, and other emergencies (Pre-position 10,000 Dignity kits and strengthen the capacity of implementing partners for emergency GBV responses among women, girls) in camps and host community.
