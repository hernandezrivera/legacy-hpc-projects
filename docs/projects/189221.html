Project Summary

UNICEF will provide women, men, girls, and boys affected by multiple shocks and hazards
in South Sudan with critical water, sanitation, and hygiene services, items, and facilities.
The objective of this project is to provide lifesaving emergency water supply, sanitation
and hygiene promotion services for con􀂧ict affected people, vulnerable host communities
and people in disease prone (high GAM famine and outbreak related) areas including
protection related actions to mitigate GBV, strengthening of communities’ resilience to
cope with the impact of emergencies and recurrent shocks. This emergency WASH project
will comprise of situation assessments, preparedness, transitional programs, frontline
emergency WASH response, strengthening of partners’ capacity, and cluster coordination
including sector information management. UNICEF will deliver emergency lifesaving
support by providing access to WASH services as per Sphere and WASH Cluster
standards.
The project will focus on addressing the following:
- Effective Cluster coordination mechanism in the country
- Provide timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs/host
communities and returnees, including transitional programming
- WASH in Nutrition for famine prevention with a minimum package of interventions.
- WASH in Health for outbreak control at wider community level (i.e. Ebola, Cholera, etc.)
- While implementing projects a special attention will be given to mitigate WASH-related
GBV.
The project will also support expansion of partnerships with local and international NGOs
and CBOs in highly affected areas to ensure basic services are provided and long-term
capacity built. The activities will be implemented in complementarity with other sectors
such as Health, Nutrition, Protection and where possible Education projects including
disaster risk reduction (DRR) focusing on strengthening community coping mechanisms.

1 - Needs

According to the 2022 HNO, only 41 percent of households reported having access to animproved water source and 10 percent have access to safe sanitation facilities. Limited access to appropriate and dignified sanitation and hand washing locations, in particular in urban and peri-urban settings, increases the risk of cholera and other diarrheal or vector-borne diseases. Water-borne disease remain the third largest cause of morbidity in South Sudan. Poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continue to have adetrimental impact on the health of children, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutritionand water-borne diseases. Counties reporting high GAM rates have also high WASH needs.Mothers and care takers of malnourished children lack basic hygiene knowledge and access tosafe water at domicile jeopardizing the benefits gained in nutrition programs. Women and girls who are traditionally responsible for water collection face an increased risk ofharassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water far from their homes. High open defecation rates also expose women, children and people living with disabilities at higher risk of violence anddiscrimination, particularly in IDP sites and other crowded settlements. The absence, presenceor placement of WASH facilities is a driver of population movement and conflict over land andproperty in some areas and requires careful management to ensure a conflict-sensitive approachthat strengthens social cohesion.

2 - Project activities

UNICEF continues to provide coordination services at the national and sub-national levels throughout the country, including facilitating the WASH severity classification exercise. To reduce the prevalence of malnutrition UNICEF will establish new water points, rehabilitate water points, establish WASH committees to sustain infrastructure, promote safe hygiene behaviors and disease prevention, distribute hygiene and water treatment NFIs to caretakers/mothers of malnourished children, and install or rehabilitate sanitation infrastructure in nutrition facilities. These activities will be done in collaboration with the Nutrition Cluster. Similar activities in addition to emergency sanitation facilities in displacement sites will be undertaken to respond to shocks and for disease prevention. Safety audits will accompany response interventions to inform programs.
