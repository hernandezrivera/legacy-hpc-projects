Project Summary

The project is focussed on accountability mainstreaming. It will enhance the capacity of humanitarian actors in community engagement and accountability, ensuring that the needs of the affected population are at the centre of every intervention. Additionally, projects will be implemented through local partners, with adequate capacity building and mentoring support. The project also aims to ensure the community’s access to timely and lifesaving information on different thematic areas and allow them to participate in decisions that have positive impacts on their lives through effective community engagement and community participation practices. To ensure social cohesion and peaceful coexistence between and within refugee and host communities, mobilization sessions will be continued with community people and religious leaders and/or other influential members of the community.
The project will also emphasise on adapting common feedback platform in 2022 and support the sector in promoting the mechanism of CFP across the response. While doing so, CAID will continue and contribute to the project through tracking Feedback, Complain and Rumour through it’s in house digital complaints, feedback mechanism called COMPASS. Relevant staffs will be capacitated on the digital channel (COMPASS) time to time that results on less error on collecting complaints & feedback from the beneficiaries. It will also lessen errors related to data processing. Capacity building of the staffs will also ensure their important role on sharing the information towards the beneficiaries.
CAID is an active member of the PSEA network and regularly receive trainings and learning materials from the network and accordingly disseminate these among Men, Women (including PWDs) groups through community engagement. Apart from this, CAID will also continue to disseminate the key messages among Male and Adolescent boys’ group to address GBV prevention and mitigation in the community. To address DRR issues, any lifesaving information, communication materials received from the sector will be disseminated among different gender and age group people of the community. Additionally, we adhere to our own safeguarding policies at all levels.
The project also seeks to draw learning from the response through different studies that will inform on community preferences for information and feedback, awareness of their rights, community participation and feedback mechanisms. From that point of view, accountability related questionnaires are tagged in all kind of surveys and interviews (e.g., PDM, FGD, Interview etc.). The findings from the surveys will inform the partners, sectors and the larger response on appropriate and relevant approaches and strengthen Community engagement and Accountability practice in the Rohingya and the host communities. The project will focus on mitigating the impacts of climate change through information sharing with community people. Group discussion will be facilitated on adverse effect of climate change and importance of preserving forest land as well as plantation of tree seedlings.
The project will advocate on policy level issues with different stakeholders from GoB (RRRC), Sectoral representatives and partner agencies. To facilitate the process CAID will publish booklet, info graphs, flyer and disseminate with the relevant stakeholders through meetings and seminars regularly.
Also, the project will disseminate audio visual messages through diversified digital channels and other creative mediums for the programme participants who face challenges with regards to mobility and accessibility. The project will engage Rohingya volunteers for disseminating messages in their own language. The project is targeting both Rohingya and host communities, to share lifesaving information and put them in the centre in designing the project intervention through the implementation of the principle of accountability to the affected people.

1 - Identified Needs

To ensure key messages are disseminated widely, partners need to engage with various stakeholders, including camp management authorities, other agencies, and non-formal structures through small community consultations to complement ongoing mass messaging interventions. Continuous technical inputs will be needed on developing messages and contents of various issues, including COVID and non-COVID issues. Engagement with local government, leaders, youth, and women groups and dissemination of basic hygiene and health messages were successful and need to continue in 2022 in all sectors. 74% Households (HHs) in Rohingya camps reported that they were unable to access enough clear information on the types of assistance available to them while 9% of HHs reported having faced challenges during providing feedback or complaints and 6% of HHs reported not consulted by the aid providers and their opinion is not considered. In the host community, the percentage is 99%, 36% and 35% accordingly [J-MSNA report 2021]. Strengthened capacity will lead to mainstreaming of accountability to the affected population in the project activities, ensuring that the communities are actively involved and at the centre of every intervention, and their needs and contributions are listened to be addressed. J-MSNA report-2020 claimed 25% of the Rohingya community and 50% of the host community were never consulted for the intervention. From J-MSNA report 2021, we see the scenario have changed slightly (Now the percentages are 6% and 35% accordingly) but still there are scopes to work. 91% respondents are aware where to go if they have any complaint or feedback- CAID Outcome Monitoring Report-2021. There is a need to ensure effective community engagement of women, men, elderly, boys, and girls and know their perception about the project intervention and to identify the gaps to modify the response accordingly. Engage the elderly to address their specific feedback with appropriate messages to explain the risk for elderly and how to care for them, especially in homecare with the help of family members, health care providers and caregivers. Limited access to timely and lifesaving information, especially for the vulnerable group (women, girls, elderly, PWDs) in camps and host community, due to social practices and poor mobile connection restricting Rohingya people to receive information (J-MSNA). In some cases, whilst information is available, literacy levels and language barrier making the problem to understand the information (J-MSNA). The project will consider the Special Needs of PWDs and disseminate audio/video and pictorial print information to be provided in various ways, relevant to the community context. Focused and strengthened technical support should provide to all sectors to ensure that activities on the ground are in line with the community needs especially the needs of a vulnerable population as part of accountability to affected populations. To engage the host community, gaps need to be addressed, and correct messages are disseminated through mass messaging tools and interpersonal communication channels. In both communities, preferred means of information-sharing are site management volunteers/Rohingya-speaking volunteers, NGO staff, community leaders, Mahjis, women leaders, block committee, mosque miking, banners, courtyard sessions, UP members, elderly citizens, mentioned in J-MSNA. CAID experienced some differences regarding means of information sharing. Beneficiaries are more comfortable to use official channels (help desk, hotline number, project staffs, government representatives etc.) as preferred means of information sharing than Majhis [CAID CAA Report-2020].
