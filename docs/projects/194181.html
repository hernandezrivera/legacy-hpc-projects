Project Summary

The project which is a continuation of activities from 2022 targets Ethiopian and South Sudanese refugee children who live in camps in Blue Nile and Central Darfur. Following the conflict in Tigray in late 2020, a number of Ethiopian refugees crossed into Sudan with some settling in Blue Nile. Ethiopian refugee children staying in camps are in high need of education services provided in safe spaces, to support the resolution of trauma, and facilitate learning which upholds children's rights and equips children with skills for life, whilst mitigating protection risks. Across Sudan, there are high unmet needs for refugee children's education and about 67% of primary-school aged refugee children remain out-of-school.
Save the Children will provide quality primary education service to all school-age refugee children in Camp 6, Blue Nile, and will ensure the provision of equivalent services to children from the Sudanese host population (Village 6) in order to also address their needs and mitigate tensions. Save the Children will construct classrooms, furnished and equipped with appropriate learning and recreational materials
and establish gender sensitive latrines and WASH facilities to support children's learning and protection. Moreover, teachers will receive training on teaching in crisis contexts and protection topics including psychosocial support and GBV, and will further be supported through incentive, and teacher kits to facilitate effective teaching. School uniforms will be provided to students in order to address key needs of refugee children and facilitate effective learning.
In Central Darfur, Save the Children will provide child protection activities to South Sudanese children which will include case management and referral services, family tracing and reunification, provision of child friendly spaces and capacity building of different stakeholders such as the community structures, Government and other NGO/local partner staff.

1 - Needs

According to the draft Sudan HNO 2023, there are over 900,000 refugees in Sudan with almost half being children. Due to conflict, displacement and COVID-19, thousands of children have lost access to, or are not able to enter to safe and quality education. Out of 23 million school aged children in Sudan, 7 million are out of school in 2022 compared to 2 million in 2018. Drivers include economic deterioration, conflict, lack of access, child labor and child marriage. This year 400 schools were destroyed or damaged by floods and heavy rains in Sudan. According to the National Learning Baseline Assessment 2015 by Federal Ministry of Education, only 40% of children in school are able to read and write by grade 6. 12 million children will have their school years heavily interrupted by a lack of sufficient teachers, infrastructure, and an enabling learning environment to make them reach their full potential. Refugee children are frequently unable to go to school due to discrimination, conflict and financial and legal barriers. Even when in school, many displaced children are not receiving a good-quality education, putting their development, learning and wellbeing at risk and leading to high dropout rates. Displaced girls also face heightened risk of trafficking, child marriage, early pregnancy and gender-based violence at basic level. Schools can provide a safe space for children affected by conflict and forced displacement, and mitigate against key protection risks, including risk of exploitation and abuse, as well as GBV and early marriage for young girls. Education needs remain high for refugee children in Sudan, with about 67% of primary-school aged refugee children remaining out of school, and higher rates in remote out-of-camp contexts; and over 90% of eligible children for secondary education are also out of school. Challenges to education provision include limited availability of schools in host communities, lack of basic resources and facilities, and lack of availability of teachers. User fees and costs, limited accessand under-resourced schools, lack of school feeding, and delivery of curriculum in Arabic are amongst the challenges limiting refugee children’s education in Sudan.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Save the Children will provide quality education to refugee children in Camp 6 in Blue Nile until March 2023, after which, the education component will be handed over to a partner, Windle Trust. Save the Children will work to ensure that the school environment is adequate with enough learning spaces, gender-sensitive latrines and all WASH facilities to support children protection and learning. Save the Children will provide of all learning and teaching materials that will reduces the burden on families encouraging school attendance and retention, particularly amongst girls. hygiene awareness activities will be integrated into learning Save the Children will equip the teachers with the necessary knowledge and skills to improve the quality of teaching. Teachers will receive comprehensive training including on “Teachers in Crisis Contexts” (TiCC), as well as on topics including COVID-19 school protocols and preparedness, Psychosocial Support and GBV, and positive discipline (avoiding physical and humiliating punishment [PHP]). They will further be supported through incentive, and teacher kits to facilitate effective teaching. School uniform and classroom kits will be provided to ensure children have the necessary basic items to encourage school attendance. The child protection interventions in Central Darfur will provide and strengthen preventive and responsive child protection services such as direct age-appropriate case management services and psychosocial support, as well as recreational activities for children at risk, running and maintenance of child friendly spaces, family tracing and reunification as well as capacity building of various actors who interact with children such as community structures, Government and other local actors.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Save the Children has an Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change (ESSCC) Policy, and is committed to minimizing the negative environmental impact of our programmes and operations, contributing positively to environmental sustainability and climate change action, and implementing interventions which reduce vulnerability to climatic shocks. Save the Children will include environmental considerations into all interventions. In particular, the sourcing (local, national, international) of all supplies and materials, especially those used for construction and rehabilitation of infrastructure, will be carefully considered to select the option with the least negative environmental impact, considering both local dynamics (e.g. deforestation, brick manufacturing processes) and contributions to global carbon emissions caused by freight and production processes. All construction of schools and associated WASH infrastructure will be conducted in a way to limit negative environmental impact on the soil and its surroundings. Furthermore, the project will contribute to resilience and a sustainable use of natural resources through involving and encouraging children and their communities to implement better practices which support and better environmental protection and a safe local environment. Recognizing children's roles as agents of change, school clubs will be established in all schools where children will be trained on how to keep their school and surrounding areas clean and green, including through hygiene and sanitation, appropriate management of resources (like water), and including activities such as tree planting within their schools as appropriate. Save the Children in Sudan will also communicate publicly about the impacts of climate change and environmental degradation.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Save the Children aims to increase safety, uphold dignity, and ensure equitable access to services, including through addressing the concerns of individuals and groups with specific needs. The programme design and implementation are informed by context analysis including local power and conflict dynamics. Save the Children will work and coordinate with multiple stakeholders (community members, government partners, local NGOs, and INGOs) which will be done through ensuring information sharing based on need, the aim is ensured relevant and related actors are informed and maximize the use of resources and sharing of best practices and to avoid unintended negative effects on individuals or exacerbation of community tensions, and will seek programming which safeguards, protects, and capacitates vulnerable and marginalized individuals, and reduces tensions. Save the Children will support and work with the CP and GBV AOR to provide regular updates and analysis on certain protection issues, shading lights on immediate issues that needs urgent response throughout the active monthly coordination meetings at both federal (Khartoum) and states levels and investing in strengthening humanitarian practitioners' capacities in child safeguarding to do no harm to children.Save the Children Sudan Protection staff support the effective mainstreaming of protection across all programming including though training on referral pathways, conducting protection audits, and providing technical advice. A Gender Equality Specialist supports with improving the gender sensitivity of programmes, and all projects are assessed through gender equality marker. Through community engagement activities, training of community-based structures, and training of service providers, information will be shared on protection risks and appropriate prevention and reporting actions.
