Project Summary

The project will meet protection needs which promote safety, dignity and resilience for women and girls, men and boys and PWD and the elderly. This protection intervention will build synergies and linkages with existing humanitarian and development/resilience and WASH projects across 5 districts in Zimbabwe in our Local Rights and Project Areas. Protection will be integrated in projects like Zimbabwe Agricultural Growth Programme, ZRBF, WFP Lean Season Assistance, Southern Africa Food Crisis Response and Partnership for Social Accountability. A total of 68 525 people will be targeted in the selected districts, with 50530 women and 18095 men. 4804 PWD will also be targeted. The project will leverage on existing projects to enhance protection and provide safe spaces for the vulnerable and build strategic partnerships for service provision. Our project will increase accessibility and availability of multi-sectoral protection services for GBV and Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) survivors. We will also strengthen capacity of most vulnerable women and girls, men and boys, to mitigate their risk of exposure to protection, GBV, SEA and Child Protection Violations. In addition, the project will enhance the capacity of protection and humanitarian actors to mitigate acts of GBV, SEA and CP violations

1 - Activities

The project seeks to support protection isstues in a bid to reduce GBV in target communities. This will include distribution of hygiene kits as well as creating safe spaces for women and girls to discuss GBV issues affecting them. Expanding and building on AAZ approach (Tuseme which we are already rolling out under our PPL Project. AAZ will therefore leverage on the already existing infrastructure and structure of this project to expand on this model. Tuseme (which means ‘let us speak out’) is a participatory theatre-based approach in schools and communities to create safe spaces for girls and boys to take action for gender equality and transform social norms, which centres on the empowerment of adolescent girls. The project will work in schools creating these safe spaces for girls in school during COVID-19 response programming to allow girls to speak up on GBV and protection issues that they will be facing. The project will also develop IEC material that is disability friendly utilizing audio, visual and braille communication strategies to ensure the inclusion of girls, women and young people with disabilities. Through the project, strategic partnerships for referrals like Child Line, Musasa, VFU, Legal Resources Foundation for health, legal, counselling services will be built and support to service providers that offer health services, legal support, psycho-social services, sexual reproductive health services and shelter in the form of finances (access to justice fund) as well as creating platforms for these service providers to interact with women and girls in the target communities and school. As part of Referral System strengthening, the project will facilitate a referral service mapping exercise and develop IEC material that may be used in the communities.In order to strengthen efforts by the project to empower women and girls in the target communities, there will be strengthening of community structures by facilitating Community Sensitization Meetings with opinion leaders, community leaders as well as men and boys. This will ensure that no one is left behind and the requisite support and buy-in is secured from the men and boys in the community who are usually the perpetrators of violence. Through these community engagements, community champions on GBV Prevention, CP and SEA will be identified and capacity built to provide further strengthening of Protection initiatives. • Trainings for staff, local leadership and community champions• Trainings on disability and age inclusion

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

The project was designed after conducting a Needs Assessment in our Local Rights Programme Areas (LRPs) and rapid assessments in project areas, which involved the communities and improved the targeting of assistance and influence the nature of interventions supported and the location of services provided. AAZ also undertakes continuous awareness raising on available complaints and feedback mechanisms in place from community to the level of the organisation and partners. This is done to ensure that people who have grievances can safely lodge a complaint and organisations engage a mechanism to redress wrongdoing. The organisation uses suggestion boxes and tollgates. In addition, we are consistently mapping existing partners and engaging in different coordination meetings and clusters. This will culminate the promotion of community participation and better information-sharing strategies and platforms. Coordination compliance to the CHS for accountability and quality assurance is key in our projects We also realise the importance of acting in a timely manner, and adapt our actions to the capacities and mandate of the organisation, as well as the phase and circumstances of the protection challenges we are faced with. Women, girls, PWD and the elderly know that they will get assistance best suited to their needs. The needs analysis also identified people with disabilities. This will influence the projects’targeting and ensuring of meaningful access to services for the disabled will be enhanced through a collective effort of collecting disaggregated data by age, gender, location etc and enabling easy access for the disabled and elderly and coordinating with other specialized agencies like Handicap International. The project will increase accountable participation and empowerment by consulting widely with all groups of people, involving all groups of people in planning and increasing participatory planning and providing confidential complaint mechanisms like suggestion boxes and toll-free lines. In addition, disability inclusion trainings will be conducted for project staff, community champions, traditional leaders and local authorities.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project will have minimal impact on the environment with the only environmental hazard being paused by supply of hygiene kits containing sanitaryware. To minimize negative impact on the environment, the project will provide reuseable sanitaryware that reduce carbon footprint and pollution. The project will also make use of digital fliers and posters to be circulated via social media to reduce use of paper. Distribution of Paper Posters, flyers and pamphlets may have a negative effect on the natural environment by contaminating groundwater and soil as people may tend to throw them away after reading.
