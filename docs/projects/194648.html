Project Summary

Stop Poverty Communal Initiative (SPOCI), proposes actions that will target 24,000 beneficiaries (4000 men, 4000 women, 8000 girls, 8000 boys), who have been grappling with flooding and cyclical waves of cattle raiding, inter/ intra communal skirmishes and subsequent multiple displacements, compounded by multiple needs, fourth consecutive year of flooding that has affected at least 1 million people at as November 2022, unprecedented food insecurity levels, high malnutrition levels, protection concerns including gender based violence and abuse, inadequate health and water, sanitation and hygiene and COVID 19 pandemic risks spread (IPC November 2022; HNO 2022; WASH Cluster Updates 2022).
Further knock-on effects of this insecurity often lead to protection risks, such as an increase in GBV cases, in relation to WASH needs in addition to vulnerability access constraints among people with disabilities. This is likely due to declining food security and access to WASH services as majority of women and girls are reported to be collecting water far away from their homes. This contributes to higher rates of malnutrition, increased WASH related diseases leading to high public health diseases prevalence, increased infant morbidity and mortality rates, and MAM and SAM incidents linked to high malnutrition the targeted counties (IPC November 2022; FSNMS 2022; Protection trends 2022).
Proposed actions will integrate the WASH response with nutrition and health. With an aim of reducing the burden of WASH-related diseases as aggravating factors for malnutrition in locations presenting an extreme WASH vulnerability and high prevalence of malnutrition with key actions aimed at breaking the transmission cycle of main communicable diseases (i.e. diarrhea), thus improving food intake of malnourished children and contributing to the sustainability of health and nutrition objectives. This will be accompanied by actions that address live-saving needs in disaster contexts in restoring immediately access to basic WASH services either temporarily or permanently while mainstreaming protection and mitigating WASH Gender Based Violence and finally support resilience building in locations that were affected by flooding in the past three years through community engagement in finding solutions and maintenance of facilities.
SPOCI recently accomplished implementing an SSHF funded projects in Western Bahr el Ghazal, and other donor funded actions in the proposed states/ counties and will scale up activities that will support SAM children/caretakers and PLW who will be provided with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion, support to nutrition facilities with improved access to sanitation services and improved access to safe water, support water treatment and good hygiene practices, support access to sanitation facilities and support women and girls with improved access to water and/or sanitation after a safety audit in addition to rehabilitation/ construction of water points and latrines with flood-resilient designs in flood prone areas.
SPOCI is a national NGO with over 7 years programmatic presence in proposed counties and is a member of National NGOs Steering Committee, member of the ICWG and AAP Working Group and M & E Working Group. In addition, SPOCI has fostered strong linkages with communities, state/county line ministries in addition to strong and competent team including staff redeployment to scale up interventions based on needs.
Activities will ensure integrated programming with Nutrition, Health, Protection etc to address the high level of food insecurity and malnutrition, coordinate with RRC among other partners. These different categories will be consulted and involved in implementation of the project including monitoring and evaluation; while taking into consideration people with disabilities, AAP, Gender Equity, Protection concerns including GBV and sound environmentally friendly approaches.

1 - Needs

According to the latest IPC November 2022, high levels of acute food insecurity in the country are driven by flooding, insecurity, population displacements, deteriorating economic crisis, the effects of COVID-19 pandemic with negative economic implications, limited access to basic services, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods (FSNMS 2022; IPC November 2022; WFP Market Price Bulletins 2022). Despite progress in the implementation of the revitalized peace agreement, which has been followed by the ongoing armed forces re-unification (OCHA, 2022); there have been an increase in numbers of returnees back into their original homes especially in Yei, Wau and Pibor (IOM DTM Updates, 2022). However, these will put much further strain of host communities, who are still reeling from severe flooding impacts and unprecedented food insecurity (WFP Market Bulletins, 2022). Seasonal scarcity of food, the flooding in 2020, 2021 and 2022, and the much lower than required humanitarian food assistance in the face of increased needs, will likely result in an increase of acute food insecurity during the projection periods, with at least 7.7 million people facing severe food insecurity in the lean season in 2023 with pockets of famine in Pibor. This is in addition to at least 1.4 million children under the age of 5, and 345,893 at risk of acute and severe malnourishment respectively (IPC, November 2022). Flooding extremes impacts have persisted to date compounded by renewed cyclical waves of inter-communal violence and age set fighting among youth (OCHA Updates, 2022). This is in addition to protection concerns which include destruction and looting of property including pastoralist – farmer aggravated violence in Yei and Jur River (Protection Trends, 2022; NGO Forum Updates, 2022; REACH Situation Overview Reports, 2022). This has been exacerbated by additional bureaucratic impediments, operational interference and access constraints and increased cases of GBV along major roads; including mines presence and increased cases of ambushes and attacks on humanitarian convoys and other commercial vehicles, further impacting delivery of critical supplies/ aid (MAG Updates, 2022; NGO Forum and REACH Updates 2022).Furthermore, flooding, cyclical waves of inter-communal violence and COVID-19 has limited humanitarian access, and therefore, more isolated areas have received significantly less services, including WASH services, and their resilience and ability to sustain coping mechanisms. Such reduced ability to cope has led to famine-like conditions in counties like flood prone counties like Pibor, which put heavy strain on water, nutrition and health facilities. These water resources are already limited due to being damaged in the floods, therefore repairing boreholes will aid in lessening potential strains on water resources and the effects of famine-like conditions (UNICEF Country Reports, 2022). These contribute negatively to the wellbeing/health and subsequent reduced livelihoods and incomes at household level. This creates a cyclical effect, and furthering the need for scaling up WASH activities to address unprecedented food insecurity levels. directly attributed to poor hygiene practices, lack of access to safe water and the rampant open defecation cases witnessed in Pibor, Jur River, Wau and Yei; almost 70% of population in these counties have no access to WASH services (South Sudan Flooding Situation Reports, 2022). Further knock-on effects of this insecurity often lead to protection risks, such as an increase in GBV cases, in relation to WASH needs in addition to vulnerability access constraints among people with disabilities.

2 - Project activities

• Conduct safety audits, sensitization and mobilization of beneficiaries and key stakeholders including mapping of OTP/CS/ nutrition/ Centres in consultation with nutrition partners• Provide targeted children admitted for SAM treatment with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion. • Map, assess dysnfunctional water points and rehabilitate water points at key strategic locations neighboring OTP/CS/ nutrition Centres• Mobilize and train hygiene promoters/Nutrition workers/volunteers trained on trained on core WASH and Nutrition competencies and WASH minimum package of activities• Map, assess and rehabilitate latrine facilities including provision of lockable doors/ locks in secure locations agreed after a GBV risk analysis through focus group discussions with women and girls, conducted by female staff • Procure and support target beneficiaries with household water treatment kits • Support water point and latrine rehabilitation targeting women and girls to improve their access to water and/or sanitation after a safety audit• Carry out rehabilitation /construction of water points with a flood-resilient design in flood zones• Carry out rehabilitation/ construction of latrines stances with a flood-resilient design in flood zones

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Implementation committees shall include other vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities, women, girls and men including illiterate ones who shall be guided and supported to ensure their views and concerns are taken into consideration. In addition, feedback sessions will be provided among all target beneficiaries through Boma level focal points/ help desks that will have contactdetails for reporting including hot lines and email addresses to support direct beneficiary feedback to the coordination office. Further, two way AAP will be ensured by working with people with disabilities and other vulnerabilities will have mobile focal points moving to their homes to support in beneficiary feedback missions.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Stop Poverty Community Initiative (SPOCI) will select and brief AAP committees who will be elected from the community and will include people with special needs. These committees will be mandated oversee implementation in addition offer means for communities to provide feedback on the project and to submit complaints, and to ensure that they receive a timely response; review howcomplaints are received and handled, consult with communities on how they could best submit complaints, and develop a plan for establishing mechanisms that shall be discussed and implemented in consultation with other key stakeholders.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SPOCI will educate target beneficiaries through individual and group sessions on its PSEA policies including using relevant IEC (information, Education and Communication) materials in regard to PSEA including their rights, reporting channels which shall include numbers and email addresses including having PSEA focal points at Boma levels to support in confidential reporting. SPOCIshall ensure that staff and contracted personnel shall refrain from, and shall take all reasonable and appropriate measures to prohibit its employees or other persons engaged and controlled by it from exchanging any money, goods, services, or other things of value, for sexual favors or activities, or from engaging in any sexual activities that are exploitative or degrading to any beneficiary. All SPOCI staff have received training on PSEA and have signed organization’s PSEA policy including newly recruited staff. SPOCI does not tolerate any form of discrimination against ethnic groups, clans, minorities or communities. SPOCI shall ensure that the selection of beneficiaries istransparent and conforms to established selection criteria and is applied objectively and without prejudice.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Stop Poverty Community Initiative (SPOCI) will select and brief AAP committees who will be elected from the community and will include people with special needs. These committees will be mandated oversee implementation in addition offer means for communities to provide feedback on the project and to submit complaints, and to ensure that they receive a timely response; review howcomplaints are received and handled, consult with communities on how they could best submit complaints, and develop a plan for establishing mechanisms that shall be discussed and implemented in consultation with other key stakeholders.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

SPOCI has policies and code of conduct relating to PSEA and will ensure appropriate awareness measures among beneficiaries in regard to their rights including on PSEA which shall be communicated through Boma level feedback and complaints desks, IEC materials that shall include hot lines and email addresses for reporting any cases of PSEA. In addition, key PSEA focal points will be identified within the community to support in confidential reporting of PSEA cases. SPOCI will in addition through its referall pathways, support referral of cases through relevant agencies which shallinclude protection partners, health partners among others. WASH actions especially areas such as latrines and water facilities use will be mapped in collaboration with protection actors after conducting safety audits including linkages with other agencies to support in provision of eg., torches at night and ensuring facilities are safe and accessible to all target beneficiaries – women, girls, men and boys; which shall be disaggregated to promote safety; in addition to people living with disabilities.SPOCI will ensure communities are consulted, sensitized and involved in beneficiary targeting andselection whose criteria will include both displaced and host communities, people with special needs etc with selection agreed upon to avoid/ mitigate potential conflicts in the community. Implementation will be conducted in nearby locations and will be selected in close consultation with women and local authorities including ensuring appropriate safety audits are conducted to ensure safety of beneficiaries particularly women and girls to mitigate potential protection concerns. Each distribution point/location will cover. Peace messaging, messaging on UXOs/ mines ect and coordination with government agencies including other focal points such as the NGO Forum, UNDSS among others.
