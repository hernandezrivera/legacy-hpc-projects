Project Summary

The IPC May 2023 report reveals alarming statistics, estimating that approximately 4.4 million children aged 0 – 59 months in northwest and northeast Nigeria are currently experiencing acute malnutrition. This includes 1.04 million cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 3.37 million cases of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). Furthermore, 585,000 pregnant or lactating women are acutely malnourished and require urgent attention.
In response to this critical situation, Pro-Wellness is launching a targeted intervention aimed at providing nutritional support to these vulnerable groups. The focus will be on delivering in-kind assistance to address the immediate nutritional needs of children, pregnant women, and lactating mothers. The project's overarching goal is to contribute to the reduction of acute malnutrition and promote sustainable community health and well-being.

1 - Needs

The IPC May 2023 report highlighted a distressing estimate of almost 4.4 million children aged 0 – 59 months facing acute malnutrition, with a substantial portion in the inaccessible areas of the BAY states, Pro-Wellness is set to address the identified gaps through targeted interventions.The project will primarily focus on responding to the nutrition needs of the vulnerable group through the provision of In-kind assistance. Specifically, the project aims to address the following key areas:1.Treatment of Acute Malnutrition: The project will target the treatment of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) for U-5 and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) U-5 without medical complications. This involves implementing an outpatient therapeutic program and Targeted supplementary feeding program to address the immediate nutritional needs of the affected children.2.Malnutrition Prevention: The project will undertake preventive measures through Maternal, Infant, and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practices. This includes educational initiatives and support to caregivers, promoting healthy nutrition practices to prevent malnutrition, through group and individual counselling.3.Social and Behavior Change Communication (SBCC): The project will employ SBCC strategies to raise awareness and promote positive behavioral changes related to nutrition. This involves engaging communities and individuals to adopt healthier practices for improved nutritional outcomes.4.Micro Gardening: In-kind assistance will extend to supporting micro-gardening initiatives, empowering communities to cultivate small-scale gardens. This approach contributes to enhanced food security and increased access to nutritious food, directly impacting malnutrition prevention.By strategically addressing these specific needs, the project aims to make a meaningful impact on the acute malnutrition crisis, especially in the inaccessible regions of the BAY states.

2 - Activities

Pro-Wellness will implement a comprehensive set of activities to address the identified nutrition needs. These activities include:Community-Based Screening and Referral:Screening and referral of children aged 0-59 months for acute malnutrition and feeding difficulties at the community level.Establishment of Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTPs):PROWI will Set up three OTPs in Madagali, Adamawa State, to provide essential treatment for severe acute malnutrition (SAM) cases.Capacity Building:Quarterly training sessions for 36 government health workers, 60 Community Nutrition Mobilizers (CNMs), and 8 nutrition staff to enhance their skills and knowledge.Management of Acute Malnutrition:PROWI will Provide management for acutely malnourished children without medical complications.SAM Treatment:Treatment of 11,714 SAM under 5 children through outpatient therapeutic programs.MAM Treatment:Treatment of 3,028 cases of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) among children under 5, utilizing the Tom Brown supplement approach.Caregiver Training:Training of 20,000 caregivers on Mother Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) for early detection of malnutrition.Skilled IYCF Counselling:Weekly skilled counseling on Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) to 12,200 Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) and caregivers.Formation of 180 IYCF care group models Distribution of Seedlings:Distributing seedlings to 2700 PLW and caregivers, supporting them in micro-gardening initiatives for improved food security and increased access to nutritious food, directly impacting malnutrition prevention.Through these targeted activities, Pro-Wellness aims to effectively address the nutritional needs of the vulnerable population, particularly focusing on early detection, treatment, and preventive measures for acute malnutrition.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Pro-Wellness is committed to the full integration of gender equality throughout every phase of the project. This commitment extends from targeted inclusive implementation processes to staff recruitment, ensuring equal opportunities for both men and women in project structures and evaluation processes. During project staff recruitment, Pro-Wellness prioritizes equal chances for women, actively encouraging them to apply for positions. Recognizing the vital role of women in successful community mobilization, the project aims to deploy as many female workers as possible.All program staff share accountability for achieving project gender equality and safe programming goals. In collaboration with the government, Pro-Wellness will conduct awareness and capacity-building sessions for community nutrition and health workers involved in the project. These sessions will address the effects of gender inequality, power dynamics, harmful traditional practices, and the specific impact on vulnerable groups, including girls and children with disabilities. Training activities are carefully designed to better meet the needs of these vulnerable groups based on their disability status and gender.Pro-Wellness adheres to the principle of "Do No Harm" in all project activities. This involves taking specific steps and measures to protect and minimize the risk of harm in intervention processes, including participatory beneficiary selection, community involvement, informed consent collection, risk assessment, and the establishment of a responsive feedback and complaint mechanism at the community level.The organization recognizes the importance of identifying gender-differentiated opportunities and constraints for better implementation during the project design phase. Sensitization on gender, child protection, and safeguarding concerns is integrated into all training and awareness-raising activities, particularly during the inception workshop. Mandatory onboarding includes the signature of the Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct by Pro-Wellness staff and associates. Gender-responsive safeguarding risk assessments are conducted before engagement to mitigate any concerns related to the risk of harm, reputational risk, or conflicts within beneficiary communities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Pro-Wellness is dedicated to upholding the principles of participation and downward accountability in its humanitarian efforts. A key element of this commitment is the establishment of a formalized Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM), ensuring a secure, confidential, accessible, transparent, and effective channel for beneficiaries and stakeholders to voice concerns, register complaints, request investigations, and offer suggestions. The organization diligently investigates and follows up on complaints in a timely manner.The purpose of the CRM, along with relevant contact information (hotline, suggestion boxes, email, physical address, etc.), is communicated to beneficiaries through complaint boxes, project activities, and distributed materials, as well as at distribution sites. Staff members actively explain the purpose of the CRM to beneficiaries. Complaints received are categorized based on sensitivity and severity levels, and responses adhere to Pro-Wellness's Standard Operating Procedures for Programmed-related Complaints Response and Feedback Mechanism.To maintain transparency, Pro-Wellness informs beneficiaries and the broader community about the selection criteria and distribution processes to prevent the creation of tension. The organization's Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) mechanisms establish a two-way communication channel, allowing beneficiaries and affected populations to provide feedback and complaints. This enables Pro-Wellness to adjust interventions promptly and address issues in a time-effective manner.Pro-Wellness is committed to ensuring its staff complies with its code of conduct, and serious measures will be taken if any violations occur during the project. Lastly, the organization adheres firmly to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence, and neutrality, aiming to implement a 'Do No Harm' approach throughout its activities.
