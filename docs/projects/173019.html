Project Summary

Given the dynamic and developing situation of Sudan in which the country is prone to
multiple crises, people often have multiple vulnerabilities. This project is specially
designed to respond to the urgent needs of people affected by crisis by provision of timely
gender sensitive lifesaving assistance through Cash and Vouchers while looking at
durable and sustainable livelihoods and food security actions applying nexus
programming in FSL. The project will target vulnerable people including children, people
with disabilities, women headed households, IDPs, Returnees, adolescents and youth girls
and elderly affected by crisis in North Darfur , North Kordofan, South Kordofan, White
Nile, Kassala and Khartoum state. The project designed to mainstream gender and
connect with durable solutions in humanitarian and development as it considers peace
building interventions as well.

1 - Needs

According to the recent update from Food Security and Livelihoods Sector, Food insecurityremains alarmingly high in Sudan with increased and protracted displacement, economicdecline and Inflation, and high food price hikes exacerbated by the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic and the high Inflation rates. In addition to this, White Nile, North Darfur, South Kordofan, Kassala and Khartoum states are under stress (IPC 2) and acute food insecurityfood crisis level (phase 3) and potential to shift to severe levels of hunger if additionalshocks arise. Around one million of the 7.2 million food insecure people are facingemergency levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4) (IPC report October -December2020 projection) Further to this, the target states are fragile and continue to be potential toannual seasonal floods disasters. Based on OCHA reports and HNO update, Sudanexperienced the worst floods in a hundred years. By October, close to 900,000 people wereaffected in all 18 states in Which White Nile, Khartoum, Kassala, North Kordofan andNorth Darfur were affected. According to the Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC), bymid-August, more than 380,000 people have been affected by torrential rains and floodingacross the country. More than 1,200 heads of livestock have been lost and approximately20,000 hectares of cropped land were affected in the middle of the agricultural season.The worst affected states are Gezira and Kassala, followed by the Red Sea, NorthKordofan, South Darfur, North Darfur, West Kordofan, and Northern states. (FEWS net,August 2020). As result of FAO rapid Assessment of Flood Adverse Effect 2020 in White nile state, It revealed that there were high damage in shelter, assets and resources (agriculture and livestock). About 5,977 HH have been displaced due to flood crisis, there were loses in crop production (Sorghum, Sesame, vegetables and groundnut), the area has been damage was 105,358 feddan. Besides lose in livestock. For these reasons it was badly needed to intervene in Food Security & livelihood aspects to support community recovery and survival. In addition to this multiple crisis situation (Inflation, acute food insecurity, floods and COVID-19 pandemic) that Sudan is facing, HRP Addendum revealed that, women and girls are more likely to experience a worsening of existing inequalities and disproportionate secondary impacts of floods and restrictions compared to men and boys. This effect is even worse for other marginalized groups such as persons with disabilities and those in extreme poverty. Projections indicate that the informal sector will be particularly impacted by the COVID-19 containment measures. As estimates show that women make up 50 percent of this workforce, the limiting of work opportunities will likely affect household income and have more severe and longer-term impact on women. While the effects of food insecurity are felt across communities, the impacts are particularly devastating for adolescent girls, who are being exposed to new risks while having their wellbeing and future prospects undermined. Particularly when food is scarce, girls ofteneat less and eat last. As families increasingly resort to negative coping mechanisms, pre-existing, child protection problems become more acute and widespread. Girls are morelikely than boys to be removed from school for economic reasons and are at heightenedrisk of child, early and forced marriage, and sexual exploitation.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Beside the environmentally adapted Agricultural interventions, the project has given a full attention to impact on environment and natural resources as descried in the needs analysis. the planned project is also introducing an early warning system to mitigate the risks of floods and bringing innovations for response. Resilience and preparedness are the first lines of defense for vulnerable communities in disaster-prone areas. To enhance community resilience, this program is combining two innovations; one, digital weather forecasting and risk modeling technologies early warning systems, and two, financial technologies that enable pre-disaster cash transfers. Based on a smart data flood early warning system (first innovation), local humanitarian actors can immediately and safely deliver cash prior to a disaster (second innovation) to vulnerable households who are in the path of floods. Vulnerable households will receive financial resources and capacity building before severe flooding strikes, allowing them to effectively prepare, protect themselves, and minimize the impact. The prepositioning of funds will allow beneficiaries to act quickly to ensure they secure the supplies they need before and immediately after the disaster strikes. If no flooding occurs, communities will still benefit from capacity building training, the establishment of community-based disaster response and preparedness committees, and improved community preparedness plans. Additionally, the targeted local government officials and humanitarian staff will benefit from the capacity building and trainings on disaster preparedness and cash programming. Communities, humanitarians, and government actors will be able to utilize these skills and preparedness plans in the event of future disasters.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Plan International Sudan is an active member of National FSL sector, INGOs forum and other forums coordinated by OCHA. the organization is leading the coordination of states emergency forums/committees in White Nile and North Kordofan states that are responding to people affected by crisis. In addition, Plan has a long experience in applying and establishing Community Feedback and Response Mechanisms which considers one of the accountability strengthening measure. Community Feedback & response Mechanism (CFRM) Plan International Sudan will ensure that target communities are fully oriented and consulted on establishing the CFRM; how they want to raise feedback and complaints, to interact with the organization, and to be acquainted with the purpose of this system. CFRM will ensure multiple layers or channels are available for affected populations to report, share their feedback, suggestions for improvement and report for any type of violations or abuse of resources. This will include Focus Group Discussions, high visibility of Plan staff e.g. community meetings, sharing of appropriate contact details with community members (hotlines and national hotline for PSEA), billboards and suggestion boxes. During activities banners/signposts with PIS details will be visible, especially during distributions. This will help the organization to avoid risks of serious harm of the organizations’ image and profile as well sustaining good relationship with the people it is trying to serve and impacting their lives. Plan International’s Global Policy on Safeguarding Children and Young People puts procedures in place to prevent and deal with the actions/behavior of our staff, associates, visitors or us as an organization that result in violence against a child or young person and/or places them at risk of the same. Plan staff are obligated to ensure children and young people we work with are aware of our responsibilities to prevent and respond to any harm against them arising from actions and behaviors of our staff, associates and visitors, and the routes for reporting such incidents. In addition to Plan’s global safeguarding policies, Plan International Sudan has committed to the Joint Framework for Action on PSEA under leadership OCHA and in partnership with other humanitarian actors.
