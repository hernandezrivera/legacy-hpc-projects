Project Summary

According to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview, the humanitarian crisis in the north-eastern Nigeria states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe, has persisted in 2022 as one of the largest and most severe in the world. The present project aims to provide emergency WASH assistance to IDPs, returnees and host communities in Borno State, Northeast Nigeria, given particular consideration to the plans of Government of Borno State to close camps in MMC by the end of December 2021 and open the road linking Damboa and MMC.
The project targets 10000 people in communities that have not attained the minimum WaSH standards through, including children, adolescents, women, men and other vulnerable members in Damboa LGA. Project activities: Construction of household latrines across Damboa host community with strong community participation to support cash-for-work hence direct labour in digging, masonry work, mud-bricks making etc; Intensive and sustained sensitization of beneficiaries on Covid-19 and cholera prevention in Damboa host community; Provision of intensive solid waste collection, transportation & disposal mechanism across Damboa community, Create and support hygiene promotion groups in host community with mass campaigns to boost sustainable KAP change; create monthly Community-led total sanitation (CLTS) activities across Damboa host community with sanitation ambassadors.

1 - Needs

According to the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview, the humanitarian crisis in the north-eastern Nigeria states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe, has persisted in 2021 as one of the largest and most severe in the world. The present project aims to provide emergency WASH assistance to IDPs, returnees and host communities in Borno State, Northeast Nigeria, given particular consideration to the plans of Government of Borno State to close camps in MMC by the end of December 2021 and open the road linking Damboa and MMC. According to the MSNA data, 1,363,002 million people, comprising 645,110 children (47%) and 243,288 women (17%) lack sufficient access to safe water, while 2,684,531 million children and 449,380 women have no access to sanitation and 2,684,531 million 1,170,552 children and 449,380 women have no access to a hand washing facilities with running water and soap in the region. Data available on the WaSH Sector website indicates that in Damboa, more than 20 people share a latrine in IDPs Camps. As a consequence, open defecation occurs in camps and within host communities, which has contributed to health concerns including the Cholera outbreak. This situation is quite likely to worsen with new displacement/influx and mass movement of people out of MMC camps based on the policy of Borno State government to close all camps in the city by the end of December 2021. The lifespan of latrine designs has become a major problem too. The impact of adverse weather and high dislodging needs accelerate the wear and tear of VIP latrine facilities which have been the approved design for many years until 2021.As a consequence, thousands of sanitation facilities across camps throughout the BAY states have been in abeyance, needing replacement.

2 - Activities

The project will focus primarily on sanitation and hygiene, especially within the Damboa Host community with an emphasis on Shuwari and Hausari areas. Under this project, 2,000 affected people have safe and dignified access to improved sanitation facilities, as per sector’s standard. Furthermore, 5,000 affected people benefit from community-tailored gender- and age-sensitive hygiene messages aiming at hygienic behaviour and practices as per sector’s standard, and 5,000 affected people benefit from basic gender- and age-sensitive hygiene items including top-ups as per sector’s standard. In addition, the project shall commemorate WASH days, with special hygiene promotion activities. The project shall also constitute WASH groups (WASHCOMs, Hygiene Promoters, and Sanitation teams) and train them towards boosting the resilience and wellbeing of the community, improving knowledge, attitudes and practices, and so on. The cash-for-work dimension of the project shall ensure that beneficiaries are employed in construction tasks in activities such as flood control, masonry and digging during construction of sanitation facilities, and monthly stipends for hygiene promoters, SATCOMs,a dn WASHCOms.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project activities are designed such that nothing is to the detriment of girls', boys', or other beneficiaries' physical or mental health, education, moral or social-emotional development. Gibran Society shall ensure that mechanisms are put in place to prevent situations of manipulation, misuse, abuse, victimization, oppression or ill-treatment. The project recognizes that sexual exploitation and abuse is the abuse of a position of vulnerability, differential power, or trust for sexual purposes; this includes profiting monetarily, socially or politically from the exploitation of another as well as personal sexual gratification. Gibran Society PSEA manual clearly provides measures for guarding against these occurrences and actions to adopt in the event of occurrence, following UN guidelines. Beneficiaries shall be involved in key decisions pertaining the project, from needs within their immediate context and throughout implementation and monitoring. A Women's Beneficiaries Committee shall be constituted to work closely with the Gibran Society Gender Focal Officer on the project. Project personnel including volunteers, teachers, and WASHCOMs and other WASH Committees shall receive training on protection with emphasis on GBV, PSEA, and UXO. Proper feedback mechanisms shall be established to ensure that beneficiaries are able to make complaints in a proper fashion and such that speedy action can be taken to address any arising protection issues. Beneficiaries shall be properly sensitized on their rights under the project during project inception. GBVS shall ensure to communicate the proper channels of reporting cases to all stakeholders in the community.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GBVS considers protection of all persons affected and at risk when planning humanitarian response and protection is central in GBVS’s preparedness and life-saving activities throughout the duration of project. The population at risk would be identified considering the specific vulnerabilities that underlie these risks, including those experienced by men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as ethnic and religious minorities, older persons, persons with disabilities, and pregnant/lactating women. Primary consideration will be given to GBVS’s accountability to affected populations, to identify, understand and support their own protection measures. GBVS’s Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) guidelines shall be fully implemented throughout the intervention, and all beneficiaries, including teachers, volunteers, pupils, other key stakeholders and constituted committees shall receive psychosocial support/training. In a dedicated project gathering and other meetings, GBVS will take time to explain its PSEA policy, emphasizing the right of the community to report any SEA cases and the available reporting mechanisms. The beneficiary committee members, IDPs Community Service Committees and other key stakeholders will receive refresher training on PSEA and the available referral pathways. GBVS will work closely with protection cluster/SGBV working groups/Human Rights Advocate to provide support and referral of PSEA/SGBV victims.
