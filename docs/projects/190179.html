Project Summary : This project will improve the Provision of life-saving ESNFI and NFI assistance to vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities most at risk due to the escalating conflict in Benishangul Gumuz Region for 72,112 (50% girls) emergency-affected population in Dibate and Wombera Woredas of Benishangul Gumuz region. The project will be implemented by Plan International Ethiopia. PIE has ample years of experience implementing ESNFIs and other emergency programs in Ethiopia. In line with the draft 2022 Ethiopia Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), the project mainly aimed to Provide safety and security for women, children to improve the beneficiary’s safety and security. It will also support ESNFI service delivery systems by strengthening their institutional and technical capacities, capacity building, coordination and information management support.
To meet these objectives and provide Provide safety and security for women, children to improve the beneficiary’s safety and security. PIE will capitalize on best experiences in their effort to deliver effective and sustainable program for IDPs in targeted areas. Plan International will implement the project in Wombera and Dibate. Plan International will reach around 36,912 IDPs through this project.
This project is designed in consultation with the ESNFI cluster. The main project activities includes
Provision of Emergency Shelter and NFI that consider the needs of women, children to improve the beneficiary’s safety and security. The items will be provided to each family and individuals according to Shelter and NFIs Cluster Ethiopia standards (ESNFI- V1 for lowland), Provision of ESNFIs to the persons with disabilities, older people and persons with chronic illness. The items will be provided to each family and individuals according to Shelter and NFIs Cluster Ethiopia standards (ESNFI- V1, Disable for lowland), Provision of cash for Rent assistance for physical protection and to reduce overcrowding. The Cash will be provided to each family and individuals according to Shelter and NFIs Cluster Ethiopia standards (Cash for Rent), Provision of Non-food items to reduce the likelihood of health and protection consequences. The items will be provided to each family and individuals according to Shelter and NFIs Cluster Ethiopia standards (NFI- V2, for lowland), Provision of NFI Kits to the persons with disabilities, older people and persons with chronic illness. The items will be provided to each family and individuals according to Shelter and NFIs Cluster Ethiopia standards (NFI- V2, Disable for lowland). Moreover, to ensure Protection mainstreaming approaches and strategies like; Gender mainstreaming, AAP and PSEA activities are considered. People With Disability, older people and persons with chronic illness are considered by Disability and Inclusion NFI Kits.
For sustainable and strong ESNFI in Emergency system, the project will actively engage and support Shelter and NFIs service delivery systems and local NGO partners by strengthening their institutional and technical capacities. The project will provide capacity building opportunity for local NGOs, regional, Woreda, IDP/school Shelter experts, and other related sectors/partners including local/IDP administrators and IDP community leaders to enhance their capacity to conduct need assessment and effectively Support ESNFI in emergency response.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

PIE will hold ourselves and the project fully accountable to the beneficiary population in each community. At its core, AAP is about systematically and meaningfully engaging the beneficiaries of aid into all stages of the humanitarian programming cycle, ensuring they have a voice and a hand in the decisions that affect their lives. At the project’s start, PIE will agree on and commit to a shared AAP policy and set of concrete practices. This will not be limited simply to seeking feedback on our performance for us to assimilate as we deem appropriate. Rather, PIE will actively collaborate with the project’s beneficiaries—including government stakeholders, communities and other local authorities, and IDP structures—to co-create the program from the start. Setting the broad parameters for the project’s technical dimensions, the project will give the “affected population” broad discretion in many of the actual design details and implementation. The project design will serve as a roadmap, but it will place the many different actors on roads, not rails, to reach the ultimate destination. They will also be profoundly involved in the monitoring and evaluation of the many activities that affect them and in reacting to shortcomings. Concretely, PIE will invite different committees organized in the IDP setting to support the monitoring and evaluation process and review findings and to identify and implement relevant actions to improve results.As an AAP strategy, this will not just make PIE accountable to the beneficiaries. It will, importantly, also make them accountable to themselves and to each other. PIE see this dynamic as important equally for the conduct and results of the various interventions and for long-term sustainability. Many mechanisms will support the AAP agenda, including illustratively: the organization of reflection sessions; the complaint box; regular internal meetings; regular meetings with different structures, communities, women and children and with other partners and the collection of data for the analysis and dissemination of key project indicators.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Plan International Ethiopia and its partners follow the principle of “Do No Harm” and accountability to affected populations which will be considered in all activities, which requires the organization and partners to take specific steps and measures to protect and reduce the risk of harm from all intervention processes and activities, such as participatory beneficiary selection, involvement of community members throughout implementation, informing them about the intervention’s aims and activities, informed consent collection, conducting a risk assessment prior to engagement, as well as ensuring the existence of functioning and responsive complaint and feedback mechanisms at the community level that allows timely action to address issues of concern that may cause or put beneficiaries at risk of harm, reputational risk or create conflict in the beneficiary communities. During the reporting period, the affected persons or the target beneficiaries participated in each and every activity of the project. Before implementation of a given activity, consultation with IDPs is conducted and necessary inputs are taken from them. For instance, before NFI distribution, IDPs through sample individual interview and FGD their need was identified and based on the need materials procured. In addition, the selection of beneficiaries made with them to reach the most vulnerable. Community-based compliant and feedback management committee organized and active at the IDP site. The Accountability to Affected people officer employed by Plan International is also monitoring and ensuring accountability and participation of the affected population during the implementation of all project activities.
