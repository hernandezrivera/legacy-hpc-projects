Project Summary

Titi Foundation (TF) will provide integrated life-saving Food Security and Livelihood assistance to the most vulnerable internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and vulnerable host communities in Kajokeji, Yei, Lainya and Morobo counties in the Central Equatoria State; Magwi, Budi, Ikotos and Lafon in Eastern Equatoria State; and Cueibet, Yirol East and Yirol West in Lake State through emergency crop, vegetable and fishing kits distribution, establishment of farmers and fisher fork groups, training on integrated farming practices , equipping fisher forks with skills and knowledge on sustainable fishing practices. By providing crop, vegetable and fishing kits and training on improved farming and sustainable fishing methods, beneficiaries will be expected to diversify their diet, increase their food production for home consumption and for sale. FSL project staffs will support and participate in FSL assessments initiated by the FSL cluster and Ministry of Agriculture and Fisheries. Also, the staffs will monitor crop disease and pest outbreaks and make referrals to FSL cluster and MAF in target locations. The project will mainstream protection in order to reduce risks and exposure to Gender Based Violence. Most importantly, the project will support the existing coping mechanisms of the local community and will build resilience towards other shocks that have direct effect (such as the inflation and flooding) through integrating the FSL response to NFIs, Wash, Nutrition, Education, Health responses in target locations. Titi is an FSL partner in Kajokeji and Yei, NFIs partner in Yei, Cueibet, Kajokeji and Juba, GBV partner in Kajokeji, Yei and Juba and Livelihood Partner in Cueibet, Yei, Lainya, Juba and Kajokeji. Beneficiaries in this FSL project will be linked to other Wash, NFIs, Shelter, Health, Protection and Education interventions currently happening in proposed location to holistically and comprehensively address most pressing needs of affected communities. The intervention will also be characterized by a focus on community engagement activities such as monitoring, awareness-raising and risk mitigation achieved through a strong system of community leaders and chiefs whereby Titi will provide basic training and engage community members in specific techniques for contextualized approaches to strengthen an enabling and protective environment for beneficiaries. Titi will ensure activities are tailored to the specific gender and age of target population as detailed in the needs analysis section. Titi will also strengthen the capacity of farmers and fisher forks through thematic workshops and trainings as per the needs identified in each micro-community. Farmers groups (Trainer of Trainers) will be trained on integrated farming, disease and pest control methods in their demonstration gardens and community outreach. Topics on nutrition, food wastage, harvest management and storage will also feature prominently during the training. Fisher Fork Groups will be trained on sustainable fishing methods, preservation techniques, and hygienic ways to handle and package fishing, value addition and fish marketing. The project will last for 12 months and directly support 79725 (48608 female) individuals target counties. Beneficiaries per target location being distributed as follows; Kajokeji (10800), Yei (5500), Lainya (4800) Morobo (5900) Magwi (9600) Lafon (6100) Cueibet (6400), Yirol East (8850) and Yirol West (8900) Budi (6410),and Ikotos (6465).Of the targeted counties, Yei, Lainya, Morobo, Magwi, Lafon, Cueibet, Yirol East, Yirol West and Ikotos are projected to remain in IPC3 (Crisis) food insecure or worse from September (2019) to April (2020). On the other hand, Budi County will be in category IPC 3 and expected to slide to IPC4 (Emergency) in the first quarter of 2019. Magwi, Budi, Lafon and Yirol West are classified as IPC4 for acute malnutrition.

1 - Needs

The UNOCHA situation report for August 2019 estimate that South Sudan has over 1.9 Million Internally Displaced Persons; 6.95 Million Severely Food Insecure Persons; 7.2 Million people in great need of humanitarian assistance and 2.5 Million refugees residing in camps in neighbouring Countries. According to Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) report covering September to December 2019, an estimated 4.54 million people (39% of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity or worse. From January to April 2020, the food security situation will deteriorate with an estimated 5.5.million people (47%) of the total population likely to face crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. In Central Equatorial State, 345,000 persons faced IPC3 in August 2019 across Kajokeji, Yei, Lainya and Morobo Counties. In the same month, an estimated 55,000 individuals faced emergency (IPC4) in the same counties. Between September and December 2019, the number of persons expected to remain in IPC3 totals 305,000 with an estimated 10,000 in IPC4. During the last quarter of 2019, Kajokeji and Morobo will face stressed (IPC2) food insecurity or worse while Yei and Lainya will remain in crisis (IPC3). Food Security situation is expected to worsen in the first quarter of 2020 with Central Equatorial state projected to have 345000 individuals in IPC3 and 35,000 persons in IPC4 with Kajokeji, Yei, Morobo, and Lainya counties facing IPC 3 or worse food insecurity. In Eastern Equatorial State, 410,000 persons faced IPC 3 (Crisis) in August 2019 while an estimated 55,000 individuals were classified in IPC4 (Emergency). In the same period, the IPC analysis report noted Magwi (IPC3), Lafon (IPC3), Ikotos (IPC3) and Budi (IPC4) food insecurity or worse. During the last quarter of 2019 (September to December), Eastern Equatorial State is projected to have 320,000 individuals in IPC3 and 60,000 persons in IPC 4. Magwi, Lafon and Ikotos will remain in IPC3 during the same period while Budi County will be in IPC 4. In the months of January to April 2020, food insecurity in the state will escalate with IPC3 cases rising to 400,000 and IPC4 numbers reaching 100,000. Magwi, Lafon, Ikotos counties will face IPC3 food insecurity or worse while Budi County is projected to remain in IPC4. In the former Lake States, an estimated 430,000 persons remained in IPC3 while 220000 were classified as being in IPC 4 in the month of August 2019. Yirol East, Yirol West and Cueibet Counties were among food insecure counties and classified in IPC4 (emergency) food insecurity or worse. In the last quarter of 2019 and first quarter of 2020, Yirol East, Yirol West and Cueibet Counties are projected to remain in IPC3 or worse food insecurity. The number of IPC3 cases will rise from 365,000 (last quarter of 2019) to 425,000 (first quarter of 2020). During the same period, IPC4 cases will escalate from 105,000 (last quarter of 2019) to 150,000 (first quarter of 2020). Drivers of food insecurity across the counties indicated include disruption of farming activities due to fighting, delayed rainfall, lack of functional markets where communities can buy seed and tools, runaway inflation, household food shortages that is typical during lean season, time poverty, lack of access to productive assets, high dependence on agriculture, limited asset ownership and poor diets, rain induced deterioration of road networks that disrupt supply routes and markets, and incidences of Fall Armyworm (FAW). Over and above, the rainy season has resulted in increased cases of Malaria and acute diarrhea which often lead to reduced productivity and depletion of savings and family assets for family members seeking treatment.

2 - Activities

OUTPUT 1.1: 79,725 beneficiaries comprised of 15000 Host Community, 49725 Internally Displaced and 15000 Returnees Households provided with emergency vegetable and crop Kits and Training. ACTIVITY 1.1.1: Conduct community communication, consultation and sensitization meetings on the FSL project with local authorities, community leaders and members to provide project information and the role of the community in the implementation process. ACTIVITY 1.1.2: Community Mobilization, Registration, verification and selection of beneficiaries with the participation of community members, leaders, and local authorities, and beneficiary’s representatives. ACTIVITY 1.1.3: Prepositioning of vegetable, crop and fishing kits items from UNFAO core pipeline .ACTIVITY 1.1.4: Conduct distribution of vegetable and crop to 79725 (48608 female) using UNFAO criteria in target counties .ACTIVITY 1.1.5: Formation and training of 55 vegetable and crop groups (Trainer of Trainers), each composed of 25 individuals. Topics to include; establishing sunken and raised beds, nursery management, planting methods (row planting and number of seedlings per row), seedling care management, thin/transplanting, mulching, soil and water conservation, integrated pest and disease management, and harvesting and harvest preservation. ACTIVITY 1.1.6: Conduct joint field monitoring visits to farmers and farmers groups with project staffs, community members, local authorities and officials from MAF. Joint monitoring meant to obtain information on project progress, challenges faced and to seek continued participation and support from target communities. ACTIVITY 1.1.7: Conduct farming stakeholder’s forum to obtain information on challenges faced by farmers. ACTIVITY 1.1.8: Conduct PDMs on crop and vegetable kits distribution with 15% beneficiaries randomly selected to ascertain use, appropriateness and benefits of items distributed. ACTIVITY 1.1.9: Conduct a joint Post Harvest Survey with farmers, Officials from MAF and local authorities to obtain data on post-harvest food harvest, losses and waste. Report to be shared with farmers, local authorities, and officials from MAF during stakeholder’s forum in target locations. ACTIVITY 1.1.10: Conduct environment education and awareness targeting farmers and fisher fork groups to mitigate effects of Global Warming. ACTIVITY 2.1.3 Formation and training of 33 Fisher Folks Groups each composed of 15 members. Training topics to include benefits of fish diet, fish preservation, sustainable fishing, net making, fish packaging, fish handling and marketing. A total of 6600 individuals comprising 4200 males and 2400 females to be trained through the groups: ACTIVITY 2.1.4: Conduct PDM on fishing kits with 15% of fisher forks randomly selected among beneficiaries to ascertain use, appropriateness and benefits of items distributed. ACTIVITY 2.1.5: Conduct joint Post Harvest Assessment to obtain data on post-harvest losses and waste. Report to be shared with fishermen, MAF, local authorities during stakeholder’s forum. ACTIVITY 2.1.7: Conduct joint field monitoring visits for accountability purposes to fishermen and fisher fork groups with project staffs, community members, local authorities and officials from the ministry of agriculture and fisheries. Joint monitoring meant to obtain information on project progress, challenges faced and to seek continued participation and support from target communities.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The guiding principle of the FSL intervention is “Do no harm’’ and it prioritizes the protection, safety, dignity and rights of beneficiaries while delivering services and assistance. Distribution of inputs and trainings will be provided in adequate scale, within safe and easy to reach known affected Returnees, Internally Displaced Persons and vulnerable host communities according to needs and risks devoid of any form of discrimination based on age, gender, ethnic allegiance, or religion. The FSL project ensures maximum inclusion and will seek to build the capacity and assist affected individuals to claim their rights. Also, activities proposed will abide by principle of humanitarian protection by ensuring women, girls and children are protected from all forms of sexual gender based violence and exploitation. Platforms such as open community dialogues, complaint and feedback mechanisms, community consultations, Focus Group Discussions and meetings are embedded in the project to ensure community members views, suggestions, ideas and concerns are received, acknowledged and attended to on time to safeguard their rights and entitlements.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The FSL intervention is designed based on information obtained from returnees, Internally Displaced Persons and Vulnerable host communities during IRNA, FSL Cluster and Multiagency assessments. Also, the project refers to data developed through Integrated Phase Classification for 2019-2020, Acute Malnutrition Integrated Phase Classification for 2019, FEWSNET and CFSM reports and analysis. Assessments and Reports noted are referenced in the FSL project to justify the intervention and were compiled after extensive Interviews, Focus Group Discussions, Observation and household visits to obtain information from affected persons. Titi Foundation has reached a minimum of 153 individuals (73 Females) while designing the FSL projects. Their needs, priorities, concerns and suggestions are embedded in the project activities. As such, the project is a product of assessed, expressed and prioritized needs of the affected communities and will ably meet the specific needs of the affected populations.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Due to years of conflict, Government and Community Structures and Networks collapsed in targeted locations. However, as a coping mechanisms, communities in target locations have come up with their own systems of addressing challenges they face as well as in disaster risk reduction. In locations with or without Governance and community structures, the FSL project assumes participatory approach seeking to involve community members in project activities at every stage to generate project buy in, legitimacy, acceptability, sustainability, accountability and to allow the community members to contribute their talent, ideas and other resources to complement the intervention. Coordinating project activities with the local authorities, community leadership diminishes chances of overlap or duplication of effort. Also, coordination allows the project also to benefit from technical knowledge and capacities at the community and government agencies. The FSL project staffs will actively participate in meetings, field visits and assessments organised by the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry throughout the period of the intervention. Also, the intervention will seek to develop existing referral pathway for effective disease and pest control monitoring and reporting. Increased participation of community members through existing networks in the community allows for strengthening community networks and systems for better response to risks and disasters.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

South Sudan Strategic plan on Food Security seeks to enhance food production for rural and urban communities with special focus on provision of inputs and training on improved and sustainable food production methods and practices. On the other hand, the FSL intervention anticipates to enhance food production for conflict affected and food insecure households through capacity building of farmers, farmers groups and fisher forks. In light of this, the intervention is aligned to National Objectives on food security for South Sudan and will complement the efforts of the Government in the food security sector.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Whereas project locations were immensely affected by the conflict that erupted in 2013 and escalated in 2016, they have enjoyed relative calm after the signing of Revitalized Peace Agreement in September 2018. Accessibility to locations and beneficiaries is by road and by air with the local authorities allowing unhindered access to beneficiaries and delivery of humanitarian aid. Assessments conducted in the locations by the Protection Cluster have noted improved security with no much risk posed to humanitarian agencies and supplies. Noting that security situation is fragile across South Sudan, Titi Foundation will conduct safety audits in target locations to map existing and potential risks and possible impact on project activities. The findings of the audit report will be embedded in project activities and used to advocate for protection of staffs, beneficiaries, project supplies and assets. Further, Titi Foundation will rely on protection cluster, FSL Cluster and UNOCHA for security updates. Local authorities will be informed of staff’s movement, project activities well in advance in order for them to provide security. To alleviate resource driven conflict during project implementation, the FSL project will ensure protection for environment with special attention being placed on avoiding depletion of natural resources i.e. ensuring trees are not cut, avoiding conducting project activities next to watering points, animal migration corridors, and religious and cultural shrines.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The FSL project will seek to equip farmers, farmers groups and fisher forks with skills and knowledge they can utilize to increased food production for own use and for sale. All the Trainer of Trainers will be drawn from the local community and will be required to train other fishermen and farmers of improved and sustainable food production including fishing. Training the TOTs and community members will generate a pool of men and women equipped with farming and fishing skills and knowledge they will utilize long after the project has come to an end. Rather than just distribute aid, the Intervention seeks to build the capacity of targeted beneficiaries meaning they can utilize the skills by their own to help themselves recover, restore their productive capacity and that of their community. Titi Foundation will also continue to fund raise to support FSL activities in targeted locations after the 12 months’ period through WFP, UNFAO and other agencies who are already willing to continue supporting FSL interventions. Finally, by building the resilience of farmers, farmers groups and fisher forks, the intervention will strengthen the resilience of households’ thus expanding household production capacity. Enhancing food security of households is likely to extend to economic empowerment of households and by extension have long term impact at community level.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

61% of displaced persons in South Sudan conflict are women and girls. Negative gendered norms, safety concerns and domestic chores can exclude women and girls from accessing humanitarian assistance. To ensure equal participation of women and girls in this project, project consultations and planning meetings will be conducted in locations that women and girls can easily and safely access and during time of day they can attend. Women and girls will be engaged in project monitoring to embed their views and concerns in project activities. To mitigate occurrence of GBV, Titi Foundation will deploy a protection officer who will provide guidance to project staffs on existing and potential GBV risks to ensure activities do not pose additional risks to women and girls. To mitigate exposure to violence during registration and distribution exercises, FSL staffs will conduct consultation with the community members and leaders to provide information on the project including selection criteria, number of beneficiaries per location, distribution dates and locations. Also, registration and distribution exercises will be conducted in collaboration with community leaders, local authorities, beneficiaries’ representatives to ensure fairness. To support impartial and conflict sensitive intervention, community leaders, members and local authorities will be engaged in decision making regarding the project. All complaints received on the project will be safely, impartially and swiftly acted on with complainants being provided with feedback within the shortest time possible. Prior to commencing project activities, a safety audit will be conducted in each target location to map existing or potential protection concerns posed to beneficiaries, project staffs and FSL supplies. The Safety Audit report will be utilized by project staffs, community leaders and members in deciding registration, distribution and training sites, where beneficiaries can access easily and safely.
