Project Summary

The proposed project responds to immediate needs identified by SI and its partners in WASH, Shelter NFI and Food assistance for conflict-affected populations in Northwest Syria. Over a period of 12 months, the activities will be implemented by 3 NGOs (SI, AFH and Ihsan) in coordination with communities and local authorities. This project is aligned with USAID/BHA Supplemental Guidance for Partners in Syria, as well as with the priorities of the Humanitarian Needs Overview for Syria, and is justified by the following:
• There are still unmet needs as described in section 1, despite the implementation of various humanitarian responses by SI, AFH and Ihsan. Protracted displacement continues in Aleppo and Idlib governorates, and the capacity of IDPs to return to their areas of origin is limited, forcing many to remain in formal and informal settlements.
• The proposed project takes into account the crucial and unmet needs identified in Idlib where SI will extend its current humanitarian response with the new partner Ihsan focusing on districts which are not covered by other humanitarian actors. Therefore, the project creates an extra level of synergy by adding a new local partner, Ihsan, and developing a new operational area based on needs and with the potential for cross-sector integration.
The project approach follows USAID guidance on flexible programming and can shift between modalities and sectors and adapt to the evolving humanitarian context. Based on lessons learned from previous emergency interventions and taking into consideration new emerging needs, SI and its partners will address emergency needs and strengthen the resilience of conflict affected people in NWS through an integrated WASH, Shelter and Food Assistance response.
Providing a comprehensive response, which includes food assistance, will prevent beneficiaries from having to divert part of their assistance to cover other needs, such as using part of the winterization or
WASH assistance to cover food needs. After analyzing several scenarios, SI is opting for cash transfers as it is more flexible for beneficiaries and it will give a boost to the local economy. SI will carry out 3 rounds of cash assistance of 100 USD (covering the full SMEB as recommended by the food security cluster and CWG) to 3,291 individuals from
November 2022 to January 2023. As recommended by the CWG, SI will transfer a value of 100
USD and, at the same time, has budgeted 130 USD to rapidly adapt to potential shifts in the
market (see Annex 9). SI will conduct regular market analysis and couple the cash for food
assistance with the other distributions to protect the amount of cash initially dedicated to other
sectors:
 Round 1: Cash for hygiene kits;
 Round 2: Cash for hygiene kits, winterization and food assistance;
 Round 3: Cash for hygiene kits and food assistance.

4 - Needs

The proposed project is based on the extensive knowledge and expertise of Solidarités International (SI) coupled with evidence from previous projects (see Annexes 2, 3 and 4). Additionally, three Rapid Need Assessments were conducted to collect data on current needs in order to design this project (see Annexes 5, 6 and 8). According to the HNO, more than 12.4 M individuals are in need of food assistance (2.6M in Aleppo gov). According to the last updates from the Food Security Cluster, out of 13 sub-districts in A'zaz-Jarablus and Tell Abiad corridors, only 25% of all food needs in sub districts were covered. Food security partners in these areas started to implement food assistance to cover these needs but the severity of the needs combined with the decrease in agricultural production will result in increased food insecurity in the area. According to the FAO, the wheat production in 2021 is estimated at around 1.05 million tons, compared to 2.8 million tons in 2020. Crop deficits are related to insufficient and poorly distributed rainfall during 2020/21 agricultural season; several heat waves, the high cost of agricultural inputs; limited availability and high costs of irrigation water. The market analysis of the context in Al Bab shows the following: (i) the purchasing power of IDPs and host populations is low (ii), the markets are functional, and (iii) food is available in local markets.

5 - Implementation of activities

FOOD ASSISTANCE: SI is extending its sectors of intervention and integrating food assistance as part of the proposed project. SI’s observations in Al Bab district show the following top expenditure categories:1-debt (40%), 2-food (25%), 3 fuel for heating (22%), and as a result of lack of resource, people are adopting coping strategies, such as reducing expenses on health to buy food on credit. SI will provide food assistance through unconditional cash transfer to respond to the basic food needs of the most vulnerable households, selected amongst beneficiaries for winterization and hygiene kit distribution. The targeting process will consider the most food insecure households (according to the Food Consumption Score and Reduced Coping Strategy index), and households with people at risk, such as, women headed households, households with children under 5 or elderly. By ensuring their access to food, the project will enable people to cover their most vital needs, especially during winter as daily KCAL intake increases. Three rounds of cash distributions are included in the proposal. As recommended by the CWG, SI will transfer a value of 100 USD and, at the same time, has budgeted 130 USD to rapidly adapt to potential shifts in the market.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks include: • Inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across locations • Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees due to varied assistance packages • Abuse of power by staff distributing goods or delivering services to vulnerable populations. • Road accidents, physical harm • Restricted access (due to insecurity of COVID-19 lockdowns) • COVID-19 public health concerns.Mitigation measures for the identified risks are integrated in SI’s implementation strategy, starting with transparent selection criteria agreed upon in coordination with the relevant clusters. The risk of abuse of power is mitigated by thorough vetting of locally recruited staff, who all abide by the SI Code of Conduct. SI also has a Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanism (FCRM) in place that allows for real-time feedback from beneficiaries and other stakeholders. The FCRM is an accountability tool that simultaneously facilitates the collection of lessons learnt that will be used to improve future interventions.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks:Gender-based violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), tends to increase during times of crisis. Risks of this occurring further increase in situations of aid dependency and power imbalances. SI is cognizant of the inherent risks related to service delivery, notably for cash and in-kind distributions. To ensure that assistance delivered is safe, relevant and timely, SI has accountability systems and procedures in place.Mitigation and prevention measures:SI is determined to prevent the sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries by humanitarian workers and reaffirms its goal of achieving full implementation of the six core principles adopted by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee Task Force on Prevention and Response to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. SI's PSEA Policy complements the Code of Conduct and Protection Policy, both of which state that it is strictly forbidden for any employee i) to use its position to obtain any favor, including sexual favors, and ii) to have sexual intercourse with minors. SI’s contracts and partnership agreements include a standard clause requiring sub-contractors to adopt policies that prohibit SEA and to take adequate measures to prevent and respond when required. A Whistleblowing Policy and a Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanisms Framework are key pillars of SOLIDARITES INTERNATIONAL Policies package. SI has PSEA focal points to identify and address any PSEA concerns and implements a referral system when require


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:• Access to vulnerable population and vendors is not further reduced by additional border-crossings restrictions • Operations are not further limited by regulatory restrictions inside Syria • Safety and security of SI and partner staff is not compromised • SI is able to appropriately manage the risks of aid diversion to designated terrorist groups or through corruption and bribery • Exchange rates and material/service costs remain reasonably stable throughout implementation period of the project • Sufficient materials and skills required for the output are available in the targeted geographic areas• Construction companies with sufficient capacity are available inside Syria• Labour with sufficient technical skills is available inside Syria• Security situation allows regular sharing of information between stakeholders• Communication channels remain open and enable relevant monitoring methods• Payment mechanisms remain open for the reimbursement of suppliers• Inter-agency coordination mechanisms minimize the risk of duplication and variations in levels of assistance. Risks: Non-materialization of the assumptions mentioned aboveMitigation: • Use money transfer system (hawala) for operations in North Syria including for the payment of its staff and contractors • Cooperation with Turkish authorities and capacity to work Cross-Border from Turkey. • SI maintains a representation in Gaziantep in Turkey with its main coordination office in Amman, Jordan.
