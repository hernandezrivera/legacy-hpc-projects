Project Summary

DRC and partners will implement comprehensive GBV prevention and response services in the Bekaa, Baalbek, North, and Akkar, targeting the most vulnerable and hard-to-reach Lebanese and Palestinian Refugees in Lebanon (PRL). The compounded socioeconomic crisis in Lebanon has significantly deepened the vulnerability of aforementioned target populations, and resulting limited coping capacities have heightened exposure to GBV, disproportionally affecting vulnerable and marginalized groups, including female-headed households, older women, persons living with disabilities, and increasingly children. Through programming in 2022, DRC has identified significant increase in the adaptation of negative coping mechanisms to meet basic needs, with women and girls particularly being increasingly exposed to exploitation, violence and abuse when trying to meet their needs. Feelings of safety have starkly deteriorated amongst targeted communities, with increasing reports of robberies and harassment in public spaces. Proposed activities under the ERP are designed based on coordination with the GBV sector, and aim to complement services provided under the LCRP to allow for better and increased inclusion of PRL and Lebanese.
DRC will respond to increased needs for specialized GBV response interventions by providing comprehensive Case Management (CM) services following a survivor-centered approach to support informed decision making about how to address the GBV incident, risk, or violation, and understand which services are available and to facilitate access to these services in a holistic and dignified manner. Protection cash will be integrated into CM services where relevant and appropriate and to support the achievement of protection outcomes, and survivors will have access to individual psychosocial support as part of CM, including PFA, which will be complemented by ensuring access to Emotional Support Groups (ESG) to restore feelings of safety and dignity and to enhance available community support networks. For children, DRC will leverage its own and partners’ CP and GBV CM capacities to ensure a tailored response to child survivors following established GBV and CP guiding principles and approaches. Quality and outcomes of CM services will be monitored through satisfaction surveys, ensuring services can be continuously improved and any challenges addressed in a timely manner. DRC as part of GBV response services will also provide Focused Psychosocial Support services based on the “I Am Here” approach, developed to identify the most marginalized adolescent boys and girls through a door-to-door assessment methodology. Adolescents will participate in the design of participatory curricula whereby adolescents prioritize their own needs in terms of skills, knowledge and attitudes in pre-defined thematic areas related to GBV concerns. The FPSS curricula will also target caregivers and key male household members as decision-makers in adolescent’s life’s, requiring their engagement to empower adolescents and facilitate access to rights and services.
DRC will implement GBV prevention activities at community-level, including GBV sensitization activities, aiming to contribute to the reduction of violence against women and girls by addressing gender-inequality as root cause of gender-based violence, and to encourage help-seeking behavior of survivors of GBV. Dignity kits will be distributed as part of sensitization activities and to address a critical need for women and girls. Particular attention will be paid to engaging men in prevention activities, amongst others through delivering the Engaging Men in Accountable Practices (EMAP) curriculum aimed at engaging men in the reduction of violence against women and girls. Impact of prevention activities will be measured using tools from the GBV PEF to ensure robust learning. DRC will also build capacities of partners on GBV response and prevention, including on administering satisfaction surveys.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Case Management: DRC will provide holistic CM services following a survivor-centered approach in safe spaces. Individual PSS and Cash for Protection will be integrated into CM services. DRC will also conduct referrals to ensure access to complementary services.Emotional Support Groups: DRC will provide ESG sessions in a group format focusing on identifying and dealing with emotions and stress, rebuilding trust, developing self-protective capacities and building a support network. Focused Psychosocial Support: Focused PSS services will be implemented using curriculums developed to target adolescent girls and boys, their caregivers and key male household members to ensure GBV risks are addressed in a holistic manner. The “I am here” approach will be used to identify the most vulnerable adolescent boys and girls, using a door-to-door outreach methodology. Sensitization sessions: DRC and partner staff will provide sensitization and awareness raising activities on GBV core concepts, Safe Identification and Referrals (SIR), women’s rights, gender equality and PSEA. Dignity kits will also be distributed to participants of sensitization activities. Engaging Men in Accountable Practices aimed to prevent and end harmful attitudes, behaviors and social norms that can contribute to violence against women and girls through creation of women and men groups who receive tailored curriculums. DRC will provide capacity building for partners on GBV response and prevention.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Gender, diversity and inclusion has been integrated into the project design in line with the IA Standing Committee GMT. DRC will disaggregate all beneficiary data by sex, age and disability. DRC will protect the identity and data of survivors as per IA GBV data protection protocols. DRC maintains high level of confidentiality, ensuring services are provided discreetly, and never identifying survivors. DRC ensures that all staff are aware of the Secretary General Bulletin Special Measures for PSEA, and the internal reporting mechanisms for SEA cases, through mandatory trainings and the signature of the code of conduct. DRC will work on enhancing its awareness raising activities with communities to disseminate information on SEA key messages, available reporting channels and how the overall procedure functions, from the point of receiving the complaint, to investigation, resolution and feedback to communities on the outcomes of complaints and actions taken.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

DRC maintains a set of robust accountability and feedback mechanisms committed to participation of affected communities. DRC’s community feedback mechanism (CFM) ensures voices of beneficiaries are included, valued and heard. It systematically captures records, tracks, and follows up on the feedback it receives to improve elements of a response. Any person or community affected by DRC, or the DRC’s partners’ actions, decisions or policies can submit feedback and complaints through the CFM system, including through physical Feedback Boxes (FB), a hotline and email. Information on the availability and purpose of these channels is consistently disseminated by DRC staff through business cards. An information poster is installed next to each FB explaining its purpose. DRC staff are trained to explain the existence and importance of these channels. DRC ensures to close the feedback loops through communicating a response back to beneficiaries about the actions taken to address it.
