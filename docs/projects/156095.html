Project Summary

IOM will continue to provide and further expand its provision of integrated protection services to conflict-affected populations across NW Syria in dedicated community centres. Services include:
• Specialised child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) case management
• Psycho-social support (PSS)
• Protection monitoring (on-site and mobile teams)
• Legal support (civil status documentation and housing, land and property)
• Community mobilization and outreach
Overall, IOM will directly support 50,000 beneficiaries (10000 men, 17500 women, 12500 girls and 10000 boys) across NW Syria through the provision of integrated protection services including legal support, community-based and individually targeted protection interventions. Project locations are Al-Bab, Darkoush, Ariha, Maaret Elkhwan (IOM Planned Camp) and Afrin. The purpose of these interventions is to mitigate and reduce protection risks and vulnerabilities for crisis-affected populations, with a focus on CP and GBV case management and providing access to legal information and support with regards to civil status documentation and HLP. Specifically, activities comprise of:
• Provision of integrated protection activities in Darkoush community center which provides a space where men, women, boys and girls can gain access to specialized protection services, including GBV and CP case management, obtain specialized information/legal support on Civil Status Documentation (CSD)/Housing Land and Property (HLP), and receive information on services available in the wider area. The project, running from Sept 2018 - June 2019, will assist an overall 6,000 beneficiaries.
• Provision of protection services in Al-Bab, Aleppo Governorate, to ensure that a greater number of Syrians have access to information and protection services in their community. Services provided include GBV and CP case management, information/legal support on Civil Status Documentation (CSD)/Housing Land and Property (HLP), and community outreach on services available in the area. The project started in September 2018 and will end in December 2019 and assist an overall of 15,000 individuals.
• Provision of integrated protection services at IOM’s Planned Camp in Maaret ElKhwan to provide the full range of integrated protection services; namely PSS, CP and GBV case management, legal support and protection monitoring through in-site and mobile teams. These services will also be offered at Maarzaf community centre in Ariha. Both projects will run from March 2019 to December 2019 and assist 7,500 beneficiaries respectively.
• Provision of child protection services in two centres in Ariha and Taftanaz. Full CP case management, PFA and PSS will be provided, with a focus on children with special needs. Parenting session will be provided to both men and women to mainstream child protection concepts and contribute to reducing the risks of child abuse. Throughout 2019, the project will assist an overall of 14,000 beneficiaries.
In addition, HLP Due Diligence support across XB Clusters is ongoing and will be continued throughout 2019, including through the formulation of sector-specific guidance on how to minimize the risks of contributing to HLP grievances and appropriate trainings of humanitarian workers. This work will be closely aligned with the XB HLP Technical Working Group. In addition, IOM will conduct assessments on key HLP issues around early returns, risks and challenges in accessing HLP documentation in NW Syria and existing formal and alternative/complementary HLP dispute resolution mechanisms to identify best practices and recommendations for HLP programming, and development of key messages on HLP. The findings of these assessments will inform IOM’s ongoing Due Diligence activities as well as legal capacity-building for humanitarian workers and first line responders on HLP core concepts, providing much-needed sensitization of front line responders in HLP issues.

Needs

According to the HNO 2019, protection issues remain widespread across Syria, with more than 90% of assessed communities reporting the occurrence of at least one protection concern for one or more population groups. Eight years into the crisis, the magnitude of protection needs still exceed partners' response capacities. Grave violations of International Humanitarian and Human Rights Law continue to be reported, including targeting of civilians and indiscriminate attacks. In areas where a reduction of hostilities has been registered, civilians suffer the effects of eight years of conflict: breakdown of community structures, lack of safety nets and rule of law, the proliferation of weapons, continuous strain on resources, high levels of trauma and psychological distress, and adaptation of negative coping mechanisms. Forced and multiple displacements, coupled with family separation, have resulted in eroding coping strategies, weakened family and community support structures as well as an increasingly complex and high-risk protection environment. According to the Voices from Syria 2018 report, women and girls have been facing particular protection risks due to their gender and cultural constraints including early marriage and sexual exploitation and abuse. People with disability (PWD) were also identified as particularly affected by violence, including GBV. Furthermore, grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of being killed and injured, recruitment and use in hostilities, torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence, attacks on schools and hospitals and denial of humanitarian access by parties to the conflict. During the first 9 months of 2018, the UN verified 2,354 such violations against children, in 13 of 14 governorates in Syria, highlighting persistent trends of violence though not the full scope of conflict-related violence affecting children. As per the MSNA 2019, civil documentation, housing, land and property issues and freedom of movement remain major and countrywide protection issues, reported by respondents as occurring in 59%; 47% and 46% of assessed communities respectively. The MSNA 2019 also shows that lack/loss of civil documentation continues to be described as a protection issue affecting all population groups, with a higher prevalence among female respondents. This, in turn, compounds a series of existing protection risks and vulnerabilities in terms of freedom of movement and access to HLP rights. The issue is particularly pronounced in NW Syria, due to the absence of an internationally recognised legal system and concerns related to approaching government authorities. Regarding HLP issues, disputed ownership is described as a major concern, reported by 61% of the respondents in the assessed communities where HLP concerns were identified. Qualitative sources illustrate how HLP issues arise when shifts of control occur and also emphasize the complexity in finding available remedies, especially when lack/loss of civil documentation is also reported. Additional concerns emerged in several parts of the country, where the secondary occupation of vacant properties is considered as an emergency solution for newly displaced population. Civil documentation and HLP-related needs are likely to increase in areas witnessing returns and/or shifts in control. Finally, humanitarian assistance itself can create or further exacerbate existing protection risks if protection is not systemically considered and addressed. This is, inter alia, exemplified by findings which indicate that children in in-camp locations are perceived to be at greater risk of certain types of violence and exploitation (PMTF Quarterly Report, April-June 2018).

Implementation of activities

The project will be directly managed, in cooperation with the Protection Cluster Coordinator, by IOM Turkey cross-border Protection team based in Gaziantep with technical and administrative support from IOM Sub-office in Gaziantep and main office in Ankara, and IOM’s Syria Crisis Coordinator based in Geneva. For all activities, IOM will work in partnership with well-experienced NGOs in each intervention, selected from active members of the Protection Cluster. IOM will provide capacity support in all aspects of programming, in particular in the legal support components on CSD and HLP. Third-party monitoring mechanisms will be utilized to monitor and evaluate project activities. The disaggregated number of beneficiaries will be verified through regular reporting by cluster members through the 4W reporting format to the Protection Cluster and through IOM weekly and monthly progress reports. Protection risk analysis will be conducted on a regular basis to ensure any protection risks are duly identified and addressed and protection principles are adhered to throughout the project cycle. The activities are as follows: 1.1. Provision of integrated protection services (PSS, CP and GBV case management, protection monitoring and legal support on CSD and HLP) to crisis-affected populations in Aleppo and Idlib Governorate (Al-Bab, Darkoush, Ariha, Maaret Elkhwan and Afrin)Service provision includes psycho-social support, child protection and GBV case management, protection monitoring and legal support on civil documentation and housing, land and property. GBV and CP case management services will be provided by caseworkers for individuals requiring specialized case management. Project implementation will be guided by IOM’s Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV in Crises, and the IASC GBV Guidelines to ensure that gender-specific needs are addressed and measures are taken to mitigate risks of GBV throughout all activities. Protection monitoring will be conducted as an integral part of services, including through on-site and mobile teams, to identify protection risks through the collection, verification and analysis of information and informing effective responses through referrals, recommendations to partners, awareness raising for affected populations, and capacity building for humanitarian actors. 1.2 Provision of specialised Child Protection Services in Idlib Governorate (Ariha and Taftanaz)IOM will also provide specialized child protection case management, psychological first aid (PFA), awareness raising sessions for children in addition to psycho-social activities and parenting awareness sessions, with a focus on supporting children with disabilities. IOM will also conduct CP capacity building activities for humanitarian actors active in the area. Both centers have a mobile team comprising pf PSS workers and animators providing the same services in camps, schools and community centers in the region. 1.3 HLP support (Due Diligence and research)IOM will continue providing HLP Due Diligence Support across Clusters, including the provision of new HLP Due Diligence tools and guidance. In light of a rapidly changing legal landscape with regards to HLP, IOM will also increase its legal analysis of these new regulations and formulate recommendations to inform advocacy efforts and relevant project activities. In addition, IOM will conduct assessments on key HLP issues around early returns, current practices, risks and challenges in accessing HLP documentation in NW Syria and existing HLP dispute resolution mechanisms to identify best practices and recommendations for HLP programming, and development of key messages. Findings will inform IOM’s ongoing Due Diligence activities as well as legal capacity-building for humanitarian workers on HLP core concepts, providing much-needed sensitization of front line responders in HLP registration, procedures, risks and challenges in GoS and non-GoS areas.
