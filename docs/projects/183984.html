Project Summary

Education management responsibility in Sudan is shared between the federal, state and locality levels of government. According to Sudan Federal ministry of education, General education sectoral strategy plan and report 2018/19-22/23, It is estimated that pre-school GER increased by 6 percentage points from 37.1% in 2009 to 42.8% in 2016. Basic education GER remained stable at 72% between 2009 and 2016 before increasing by a single point to 73% in 2017. In secondary, the GER increased by 5 percentage points from 34% in 2009 to 39% in 2016 before dropping a point in 2017 to settle at 38%. There are more boys accessing basic schools compared to girls, the former recording a GER of 75% while the latter posted 71%. In majority of the states, the gap between boys and girls is considerably small but in some cases, the disparity is overwhelming. In secondary, girls have a coverage of 41% compared to 35% for boys, this pattern is evident in all the states expect Central Darfur, West Kordofan, Al Gadarif and West Darfur where boys have slightly higher GER. The National Learning assessment conducted in 2014 established that 40% of learners in grade 3 in Sudan could not read familiar words compared to an average of 20% from countries drawn from the Middle East and North Africa region. In terms of oral reading fluency, measured by the number of correct words read per minute by the learners, the assessment established an average of 14 words per minute. Significant number of children are still excluded from a conducive learning environment. Only two thirds of learners in government basic education schools are seated. 6% of learners in basic education do not have access to water supply and the ratio of learners to toilets is very high 1:102 for boys and 1:82 for girls. About half of learners in basic education are enrolled in schools that are not connected to any source of electricity.
This project will address strategic objective 2018/19-2022/23 Policy priorities 1 and 2 increasing access to pre-school and enhancing quality of delivery of pre-school. And, enhance retention in basic education and improve quality at basic education. Using the child-friendly school strategy, schools will be safe, protective spaces where children learn new behaviors and social norms related to protection. In addition to the construction and rehabilitation of primary schools, this project will build the capacity of teachers and PTAs to support an inclusive education agenda in 5 states (Central and West Darfur, Khartoum, Blue Nile, and South Kordofan). The initiative is conflict-sensitive to avoid risks of harm to vulnerable groups. Girls and other vulnerable populations will be given special focus for protection. Measures will be taken to identify and mitigate the most salient barriers to participation. Given the socio-cultural context and the risk asymmetries, female participation and voice in all aspects of education will be prioritized, thus improving inclusivity and access.
The project targets 177,986 people including 35,525Men, 36974 Women, 40599 Children 28,275 Adult and 3626 Elders

1 - Needs

According to UNICEF 2021 report, It’s estimated that over three million children here, aged 5-13 years, are not in the classroom. Seventy-six percent of primary age children attend school; in secondary that figure dips to 28 percent. At 43 percent, Blue Nile is the worst performing state for children who are out of primary school. Conflict, a lack of awareness about the importance of education and prolonged under-development all contribute to the poor schooling of boys and girls in Sudan. The inability to pay fees—even though school is free per government policy—prevents many poor families from sending children to school. Finally, cultural pressures and the traditional views of the role of women resulted a fewer girls attend, and remain in, school. In Sudan, more than 3.6 million children, or one-third of the school-aged population, do not go to school. There are disparities among this figure: girls, children affected by conflict, refugees, internally displaced persons (IDPs), children in rural areas, and children from poor households are most vulnerable to the lack of schooling.Poor access to schools is also a major issue in Sudan which contributes to low schooling rates. Furthermore, the quality of the education is most often poor, with few available teachers, the infrastructures of the schools are strikingly low-quality, and as such school environments are often unsafe. (Only 45.5% of the schools have access to clean water.) Missing out on school can have very detrimental consequences for the child’s physical and mental health: children are then more likely to suffer from child labor, poor mental health, and sexual violence. (UNICEF Sudan, 2021, “Education”). The literacy rate is particularly low: 45.2 percent of girls and women aged 15-24 are unable to read or write. Sexual violence and assault were also reported: women and girls in Darfur spoke out on the assaults they face when they are going to school. In addition, there is discrimination in class towards girls. In addition, one of the finding of WR protection monitoring in Nertiti was, there is no school in the area. Some of the families are sending their children to Thur village school. But, the distance from Boldong village to Thur village is very far away and insecure. In some areas, there is a school for children. But, it doesn’t have the facility it needs. Therefore, children will remain at home without education. In addition, adult education for women is another need mentioned in the discussion. Because of the cultural taboo before most women are illiterate that has an impact on their decision-making power. The continues conflict in in Central, West Darfur, Bule Nile, South Kordofan and Khartoum has resulted in displacement, livelihood disruption, and has contributed to high dropout rates in education. In addition, according to NHO 2022 report, all the localities in Central Darfur, West Darfur and Blue Nile and in South Kordofan and Khartoum leveled 4 in education program need. Furthermore, West Darfur state has one of the highest reported number of children in grade 3, 59%, who are not able to read. The low performance in West Darfur can partly be explained by the highest pupil-teacher ratio which leaves 84,907 children of school age 6- 13 out of school with only 18,529 children are provided education opportunities at Alternate Learning Program (ALP) centers. It is worth mentioning that 5 localities in west Darfur have a GER of less than 60% and 5 are affected with conflicts and humanitarian situations. As per the Ministry of Education records in the Blue Nile, the total number of children in Blue Nile State is 292,737 58% of those (121,770 children) are out of school because of the war between the Government and the SPLM-N, and due to inflation rates and family income (school fees). In all four states, the education facilities lack access to protective learning spaces, latrines, and water and school materials.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental factors may affect children’s access to education through protection issues. In areas where there is a higher risk of either illicit environmental economies or modern slavery related to environmental economies such as charcoal, mining, illicit crop production, sugar production and seed nurseries, children may lose access to education due to child labour, smuggling or trafficking. School environment: WR will address issues related to the school environment, possibly to add new/temporary classrooms to expand schools. To that end, the environmental footprint of school construction will be considered, to avoid construction waste becoming a site for vector propagation or an accident risk. WR will consult USAID sector environmental guidelines for primary and secondary schools and ensure than an environmental management plan is developed before expanding or modifying school facilities. Sustainably sourced construction materials will be a condition of tenders, regular removal of construction wastes, regular elimination of stagnant water and any food waste to protect students, and ensure the implementation of water and energy systems to reduce consumption. WR will consult the WASH sectors Where installing or rehabilitating WASH facilities in schools.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WR will ensuring that beneficiaries safely access their services, without causing harm, and for promoting meaningful access, accountability and participation of beneficiaries in the provision of education assistance. By mainstreaming protection into education programming, WR will maximize the positive impacts of education programs on people’s safety and dignity and support affected populations access and enjoy their rights. safety audit will be conducted to ensure the location of and access routes to schools, classrooms, teacher learning centers, playgrounds and child friendly spaces pose no safety risks to children and teachers: In addition, will avoid constructing education facilities near military installations and conflict frontlines. WR will make sure the timing of classes is safe for children to travel to and from school without exposure to further risk of harm. Classes should take place in daylight hours giving children sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. WR will provide separate, secure and private toilet/latrines for boys and girls and separate facilities for students and teachers. Ensure toilet/latrine design accounts for children (e.g. size of drop hole may present a safety risk for children). WR will identify groups of children who face difficulties accessing education (e.g. girls, children with disabilities, children from minority communities, children belonging to disadvantaged or marginalized groups) and work with children, teachers, community leaders and parents to support equal and safe access to education without discrimination. WR will create a system that is transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures and processes that affect them. In addition, set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box at learning centres, phone lines. Student, teacher, parent and school management committees are democratic and inclusive and members represent all groups within the community (e.g. gender, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, persons with disabilities.
