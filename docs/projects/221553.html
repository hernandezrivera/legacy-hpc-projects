Project Summary

Food insecurity continues to adversely affect South Sudan with 7.69 million people, including refugees projected to experience severe food insecurity in South Sudan in 2025 during the peak hunger season. Comparing with the year 2024 there is no improvement of the food security situation which remains worrying, with the most severely acute food insecure populations significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation and high food prices), conflict and insecurity – including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan – causing forced displacement, low agricultural production and a reduction in humanitarian assistance. The protracted conflict and steady economic decline have eroded the limited provision of public services and exacerbated individual vulnerabilities. South Sudan faces several social challenges as it attempts to navigate a revitalized peace agreement and a transition leading to elections in 2026. The food insecurity situation in Unity, Ruweng Administrative Area and Upper Nile state remains poor. Localized tensions compelled by Intra and inter community clashes, revenge killings, cattle rustling, burglary, theft continue to pose a great threat towards community livelihoods. Targeted individuals as a result of revenge killings are threatened to pursue livelihoods and economic opportunities and hence continue to remain vulnerable. To address the flood & conflict induced acute food insecurity situation as well as livelihood need of most vulnerable community, DRC intends to achieve following results –
1. Food Assistance: Address immediate household food security needs through provision of food assistance and prevention of malnutrition initiatives.
2. Agriculture and Food Security: Flood and conflict affected Agro-pastoral IDP & Host communities’ food security and livelihoods are protected and restored through the provision of emergency and early recovery and flood adaptive agriculture and fisheries practice for most vulnerable households
3. Economic Recovery and Market System: To ensure improved access to income & livelihood opportunities through Cash / Market based Livelihood interventions vulnerable Households. DRC will support value chain development and strengthening
The Danish Refugee Council (DRC) aims to ensure a dignified life for all individuals affected by displacement. To achieve this, DRC’s Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) initiatives are closely integrated with Protection activities, including addressing sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) and promoting peacebuilding efforts. Protection principles will be incorporated into all project activities, with tailored assessment tools and inclusion criteria developed to prioritize the most vulnerable households within targeted communities.
DRC will focus its interventions in Rubkona, Guit, Malakal Town, Malakal PoC, Kodok, Ajoung Thok, Jamjang, Pamir Counties, and other areas across South Sudan, using mobile responses to address acute emergency needs. The program will work to strengthen social cohesion by supporting vulnerable households to create, rehabilitate, and safeguard durable community assets, with a particular emphasis on empowering women and youth as key drivers of change. DRC will also ensure conflict sensitivity and integrate peacebuilding activities to address underlying causes of food and nutrition insecurity linked to conflict and displacement. Recognizing that social cohesion significantly impacts access to livelihoods, DRC will address these challenges as a prerequisite for effective FSL interventions. Efforts to improve the protective environment will include mainstreaming protection and GBV considerations across all programming. Additionally, DRC will conduct regular protection monitoring to guide its initiatives and ensure they respond effectively to community needs.

1. Needs

South Sudan faces severe humanitarian challenges worsened by the influx of individuals fleeing conflict in Sudan, which has intensified food insecurity, human rights violations, and hindered recovery from shocks. Acute food insecurity remains critical, with 7.69 million people (57% of the surveyed population) projected to face IPC Phase 3 or above between April and July 2025, driven by climate shocks, economic instability, conflict, and displacement. Malnutrition rates are alarming, with 2.1 million children and 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women expected to suffer acute malnutrition from July 2024 to June 2025, particularly in Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. The influx of refugees and returnees has strained limited resources, requiring urgent funding to meet food and nutrition needs. Gendered conflict analysis highlights restricted movement, livelihood disruptions, and increased vulnerabilities, especially for male youth unable to engage in traditional activities and women taking on primary economic roles while facing risks such as harassment. Addressing these intertwined challenges requires comprehensive, gender-sensitive, and sustained humanitarian interventions. The analysis further shows that male youth and men who have lost their properties and livestock due to conflict, has limited their ability to engage in traditional livelihoods activities. Low levels of education, limited marketable skills, and the lack of capital has made it difficult for these groups to get employment or start businesses. Currently, some men are engaged in other livelihoods activities such as burning and selling of charcoal. Some of the conflict-affected unemployed youths in the target locations have resorted to collecting items in the dumpsite to survive, while some are now part of gangs, reportedly engaging in criminality and causing insecurity. Women in many cases have become the primary breadwinners for their families, on top of their traditional domestic and child-care duties.

2. Project activities

Under this project, DRC proposes to implement following activities; CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 & 5) Under partnership with different donors, DRC will provide cash for work opportunities which is aimed at improving food security, income earning opportunities and while improving community infrastructure such as flood dykes and swallow wells to channel the water for irrigation and work on individual household asset creation and livelihoods. These assets would be identified by communities based on their needs. DRC also anticipates additional funding that will cover Multi-purpose cash that will complement to meeting the above mentioned objective f if successful in securing funding. DRC is aware that the complex conflict dynamics in South Sudan have the potential to both negatively impact or be negatively impacted by humanitarian activities. Proposed activities are designed in accordance with protection and Do No Harm principles and will follow a gendered conflict sensitive approach that actively seeks opportunities to promote social cohesion through decision-making processes. The conflict sensitivity and social cohesion component of this project will focus on strengthening community social cohesion through supporting local capacities and provision of durable community assets. A participatory conflict analysis shall be conducted and developed at the beginning of the implementation to ensure that conflict sensitivity is taken into consideration throughout the project cycle. DRC will work with community-based structures we have already established, on conflict analysis, training on conflict management and peacebuilding, and community dialogues. CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support; DRC will provide the emergency access to agricultural inputs to most vulnerable to increase the food production in Unity, Upper Nile and Ruweng Administrative Area. Households will be prioritized who rely on the agriculture, have lost the agriculture tools due to the displacement and or don’t have self -capacity to buy the seeds and inputs. DRC will also provide the training on best agronomic practices to increase the food production, reduce the post -harvest damages and building on seed multiplication approaches. The intervention will ultimately increase Households self-reliance capacity and will reduce the dependency on humanitarian assistance in coming time. DRC will also encompass efforts to increase the local seeds demand in the respective counties to develop the market systems. DRC will also use the Cash & Voucher assistance through seed vouchers to enable the local procurements of seeds. Both crop and vegetable seeds would be provided through seed vouchers so that beneficiaries can buy seeds on their preferences. CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks and & contextual changes; DRC will continue working with local agriculture extension workers to extend the services on promoting climate smart agriculture practices to increase the food production, promote local seeds and seed multiplications approaches, reduce post-harvest losses and increased crop diversification. As mentioned above in objective 02, DRC will promote to local procurement of seeds and support the seed fairs through seed vouchers enabling communities to buy their preferred seeds for both vegetables and crops.
