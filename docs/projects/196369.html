Project Summary

The proposed one-year project aims to improve livelihoods and self-reliance of the Rohingya residing in 5 Kutupalong-Balukhali settlement camps (3, 4, 4Ext., 6, 7) and host community in Raja Palong Union in Ukhiya, while enhancing environmental protection and social cohesion. The project takes a comprehensive and transformative approach integrating skills development with the utilization of skills to enhance livelihoods.
By providing vocational training and technical skills in line with UN/GoB Framework on Skills Development for Rohingya refugees, the project will develop portable skills of the Rohingya that will make them more self-reliant both during their stay and following possible repatriation in the future. To enable the utilization of skills and facilitation of wage-earning employment, the project will integrate innovative and tested approaches, such as the Local Service Provider Model to create market linkages, establish a multi-purpose learning hub and engage Rohingya youth as volunteers. With the comprehensive livelihood support at the community level, the project will support the targeted Rohingya population to initiate production interventions which will enable them to secure their day-to-day needs. By mainstreaming climate-smart solutions and environmental awareness throughout the whole project, the persons of concern develop and use their green skills and contribute to improved protection of shared natural resources and the environment. The Local Service Provider Model will also benefit the host community who will receive mentoring support to develop their commercial activities and create market linkages. Overall, the project will contribute to strengthen social cohesion within the Rohingya community as well as between Rohingya and host communities through the Local Service Provider Model that connects both communities, through the improvement of the shared environment and through soft skills development as well as youth exchange initiatives that will enhance awareness and mutual understanding.
The project aims to directly engage 1,100 Rohingya and 200 persons of the host community.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Refugees in Cox’s Bazar generally have no regular income opportunities and lack the skills that would allow them to adapt quickly to change in their situation. The fifth round of the Rohingya Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA 5) published in March 2022, revealed that 95% of all Rohingya households are moderately to highly vulnerable and remain entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance. Furthermore, the results revealed that half of the Rohingya refugee labour force was not engaged in any type of income-generating activity. Therefore, offering skill development opportunities in different trades that can be utilized in the camps and following possible repatriation as well as creating income opportunities is key to improve livelihoods and self-reliance. Scaling up vocational and skills training, and socio-economic empowerment and self-reliance activities with special attention to uneducated and unskilled persons, youth, and women in the camps and host communities, is critical to enhancing access to economic opportunities. A recent skills need assessment conducted by UCEP/UNHCR in 2022 showed that 76% of the Rohingya Youth has not received any skills training since they arrived in the camps. The needs assessment further showed that for female youth sewing machine operation/tailoring and for male youth mechanics and solar repair are among the main interests of the youth. Thus the proposed trade trainings for this skills development project have been selected considering and assessing the the UN/GoB framework, the demand by the Rohingya youth (see skills need assessment of UCEP/UNHCR 2022), the portability of skills to Myanmar, the needs as well as the possibility for volunteer engagement and market linkage within the camps. Moreover, as the experience of ongoing projects shows, there is not only a huge need and demand to offer vocational trainings, but to offer safe spaces for learning, especially for female members of the community. Furthermore, the rapid and massive increase of the refugee population, concentrated in the south of Cox’s Bazar district, has had an enormous impact on Bangladesh’s host communities’ food security, economic vulnerability, market access, and labor opportunities. The massive increase of refugees puts an immense strain on infrastructure and services. The host populations – who have been the first responders to this crisis were already suffering from food insecurity prior to the influx of refugees. Now, this situation has exacerbated poverty, and the situation is likely to worsen in the near future due to a combination of factors that include depletion of assets, price inflation for basic goods, or labor competition. COVID-19 has made the humanitarian crisis more complex and hit disadvantaged people (e.g. daily labour, women-headed households) hardest. Moreover, the needs for improving livelihoods and nutrition in both communities has been exacerbated by the Covid-19 pandemic. A study conducted by HELVETAS and Alliance2015 partners surveying 600 households of refugees and host communities in the district showed that 43% of those interviewed said they were eating less now when compared to the period before COVID-19. In 2022 particularly food prices have been rising that hugely affect the host communities food security.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Coordination with the relevant sectors, working groups and key stakeholders will be constant throughout the implementation of the project to identify gaps and adaption strategies, especially during the selection of the beneficiaries. The project specifically aligns with principle #3 of rationalisation as it integrates skills development with the creation of livelihood opportunities that increase food security and nutrition, gender considerations and DRR and environmental protection – offering a package of activities that mutually reinforce each other. Following the “one-camp approach” HELVETAS proposes to work in livelihoods in total of 5 camps that have been selected considering the needs, geographical closeness and existing structures and former experience. However, HELVETAS is open to implement the proposed project in other camps as per sectors recommendation. Cost-effectiveness will be ensured by a) coordination b) building on existing initiatives, capacity and existing infrastructure (such as existing community centers, capacitated Local Service Providers and volunteers) and c) a system of proper planning and monitoring of the project implementation. Moreover, the project aligns with principle #4 as it is designed to be expanded to multiple years. The past experience has shown that increasing self-reliance and ownership requires a continued project over time. The activities in this project are designed to build on each other to ensure sustainability of the intervention.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

HELVETAS will ensure that persons of concern and stakeholders receive information on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) along with complaints and feedback mechanisms to ensure that persons of concern and stakeholders can provide feedback or raise concerns or complaints on project implementation. All complaints will be treated confidentially by an independent M&E team and addressed in a prompt and appropriate manner, in order to close the feedback loop, and mitigate further damage related to potential risks. All staff members, contractors, volunteers, and any other associated individual will sign an organisational PSEA policy and will be held accountable to their obligations.

3.2 - Gender

HELVETAS mainstreams an age, gender, and diversity (AGD) approach throughout the planning, implementation, and reporting processes of the proposed project. To strengthen the assistance provided, HELVETAS will use the Inter-Agency Standing Committee’s (IASC) Gender with Age Marker (GAM). The project is designed considering the needs of different age and gender groups. To ensure an equal participation of women and girls (at least 50%), the project deliberately selected tailoring, handicraft and food processing as well as homestead gardening as a main activity as these are culturally accepted and areas of interest of Rohingya women and girls (see needs assessments). Furthermore, the LSP model has proven to strengthen the inclusion and empowerment of PwD and women, because the LSPs are able to offer need-based mentoring support at the doorstep.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

HELVETAS mainstreams an age, gender, and diversity (AGD) approach throughout the planning, implementation, and reporting processes of the proposed project. HELVETAS will ensure that persons of concern and stakeholders receive information on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), including child protection, along with complaints and feedback mechanisms to ensure that persons of concern and stakeholders can provide feedback or raise concerns or complaints on project implementation. HELVETAS organisational Code of Conduct, PSEA policy, Regulations on Child Protection are binding for all employees worldwide and all collaborators need to commit to the key principles of the CoC. Moreover, HELVETAS has standardized recruitment process. Reference checks are carried out as a mandatory part of this process. During the design and implementation of the project, HELVETAS participatory process (see AAP) and complaints and feedback mechanism contribute to age and child safeguarding.

3.4 - Diversity

HELVETAS mainstreams an age, gender, and diversity (AGD) approach throughout the planning, implementation, and reporting processes of the proposed project. We collect data disaggregated by age, sex and disability for both analysis and programming purposes. As a result, operations are able to account for the differing needs of persons of concern with respect to their age, sex and disability. The proposed project will engage a variety of different groups, notably women, PwD, faith leaders, caregivers, parents, youth and adolescent from both Rohingya and Host Community, encouraging widespread participation. Awareness and mutual understanding will be raised through joint activities, the improvement of the shared environment and soft skills development. People with disability will be given priority in the beneficiary selection.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To ensure AAP, HELVETAS will put in place a robust system of participation and complaints and feedback mechanism throughout the planning, implementation, and reporting processes of the proposed project. The selection of beneficiaries will be done transparently in consultation with the community, with the relevant local authorities and CiCs and Site Management agencies in the camps. For the implementation of the project, HELVETAS will put in place a complaints and feedback mechanism for all stakeholders. All complaints will be treated confidentially by an independent M&E team and addressed in a prompt and appropriate manner, in order to close the feedback loop, and mitigate further damage related to potential risks. HELVETAS will use tools for assessment, baseline and end-line survey, post distribution monitoring, and monitoring visits to track progress against the activities and indicators, constantly incorporating beneficiary feedback in the monitoring and implementation process.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The livelihoods activities will adopt the 3R (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle) approach wherever possible to make the project intervention environment-friendly and sustainable. Community-based livelihood interventions like homestead gardening will adopt climate-smart and soil conservation solutions. The project will promote “low-external -input sustainable agriculture (LEISA) practice by utilising ecosystem resources to protect environment and protect health hazards. LEISA practice is economically feasible and environmentally sound. To protect the soil from being eroded, tree-plantation will be done in the most vulnerable areas.
