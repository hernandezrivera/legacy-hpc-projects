Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) is committed to sustaining educational services for children and adolescents by providing secure, fair, inclusive, skill-focused, and developmentally suitable learning opportunities for the children of age group 3-18 years. This commitment extends to fostering a child-friendly and protective environment within the camps and host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf of Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh.
The initiatives will focus on maintaining, enhancing, and increasing additional Learning Centres (LCs) to accommodate learners in the Myanmar Curriculum (MC) under two shifts along with establishment of new Community-Based Learning Facilities (CBLF). Additionally, efforts will include the implementation of Peer Education Programs (PEP), Distance Education (DE), Learning Management System (LMS), Mobile Library and the capacity development of the Master Trainer of the Education Sector Partner on the Accelerated Learning Program (ALP).
Focused efforts are underway to empower Rohingya women, enhancing their skills to serve as proficient teachers. Save the Children (SC) is implementing targeted interventions, encompassing Early Childhood Development (ECD), the Myanmar Curriculum (MC), and Community-Based Learning Facilities (CBLF), catering to children aged 3-18 in both Rohingya and host communities. These interventions extend across multiple camps, featuring 147 Learning Centres in Refugee Camps (RR) and 418 CBLF-RR across various locations. Key camps include Camp 01E, 04, 7, 8W, 10, 13, C, E, F, G, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19 , 25 , 26 . Additionally, 3 Learning Centers in host communities, is in Jaliapolong Union, Ukhiya Upazila.
SC will continue to extend support to 20 Government Primary Schools in Ukhiya. The interventions also encompass empowering parents and caregivers with the knowledge and skills to actively contribute to their children's learning, development, and social well-being. There is a focus on enhancing teaching quality through continuous professional development support for all partners in the education sector of ALP. The training will cover inclusive elements such the integration of Mental-Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), gender-responsive approaches, child-sensitive psychosocial support, and considerations for disability and gender-friendly practices.
Efforts will be continue to promote integration across sectors, including livelihood, child protection and health, ensuring that activities are customized to address the diverse needs of girls and boys, thereby ensuring equitable benefits from the interventions including specialized training initiative spanning 8-10 months will be initiated within selected camps to equip targeted Rohingya women to become skill teacher.
Particular emphasis will be placed on enhancing the skills of relevant staff members in crucial teaching-learning methodologies aligned with the new Myanmar Curriculum (MC) and Accelerated Learning Program (ALP), as well as addressing Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) issues. Gender-segregated WASH points, including Menstrual Health Management (MHM) services, will be incorporated into all learning facilities. In the capacity of a sector co-lead, SC will maintain a pivotal role in advocating for, improving the quality of, and ensuring the sustainability of education services for displaced Rohingya and the neighboring host communities. Besides advocating for the continuation of education as an essential service and the permission of technology-based interventions in the camps, SC will also support lower-performing government primary schools to ensure quality education among learners.
The project and programmatic initiatives are and will be enhancing social cohesion within the host and Rohingya communities.
The project will reach a total of 38,095 learners out of which 46% are girls and 54% are boys.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Firewalled priorities within the education sector in Cox's Bazar, particularly in Rohingya camps and host communities, underscore critical areas demanding immediate attention. A crucial focus lies on Capacity Building, with a requirement to train 6,800 teachers in subject-specific learning, Burmese, and English, vital for the successful scale-up of the Myanmar Curriculum. However, the current funding for Capacity Building stands at 43%, emphasizing the urgency to secure additional resources to bridge this gap( Education Sector Firewalled activity). Furthermore, the Education sector is deeply committed to evaluating learning outcomes through comprehensive learners' assessments. This reflects a dedication to measuring the impact of teaching and learning, ensuring continuous improvement through both formative and summative assessments. Addressing the challenge of access to education for Children, specially in the Rohingya Camps is still needed. The J-MSNA,2023 (published in October, 2023) suggested that only 54% children between the age 3-4 years have attended the early education program. The same report (J-MSNA,2023) also showed that , 15% boys and 10% girls between the age range of 5-11 is still out of enrollment. But the Most staggering findings from the J-MSNA, 2023 report is regarding the adolescents (age 12-18) where only 51% boys and 21% girls of that age bracket are enrolled and attending in various education program. The impact is more very sever considering that there is total 493418 children between the age of 1-18 (GoB-UNHCR Population Factsheet; October, 2023). Where the reason behind this access issue is multifaced, lack of awareness among the parents regarding the education and lack of girl only shift/facilities are some of the core reason cited in the J-MSNA 2023 report. In addition, critical priorities center around the construction and rehabilitation of classrooms to accommodate the evolving needs under the Myanmar Curriculum. An estimated 800 additional classrooms are deemed essential for KG-G2, the Accelerated Learning Program, and dedicated girls-only classrooms for adolescents. This expansion necessitates an investment of USD 11.7M to address funding gaps and ensure the functionality and accessibility of learning centers, creating an environment conducive to quality education for all. Sustained investment in education infrastructure is emphasized as a cornerstone for the continuous progress of access to education in the region, securing a brighter future for the upcoming generation

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

In Cox's Bazar, Save the Children (SC) is at the forefront of protection mainstreaming, implementing comprehensive strategies to mitigate the risks of any kind of danger and risk for the children. SC has ingrained a zero-tolerance policy within its organizational culture, leaving no room for ambiguity regarding the prohibition of any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. This policy serves as the cornerstone of SC's protection mainstreaming efforts. The Centrality of Protection (CoP) is also the main pillar of any kind of SC programming. The organization conducts extensive training programs for its staff and volunteers, emphasizing the importance of upholding ethical standards and fostering a culture of accountability. Through community engagement initiatives, SC empowers local residents with knowledge and skills to recognize and report incidents of abuse, thereby creating a network of vigilant community members actively involved in safeguarding efforts. SC's protection mainstreaming extends to advocacy and policy development, where the organization collaborates with local authorities, international agencies, and other stakeholders to influence policies that prioritize the protection of vulnerable populations. By actively participating in shaping the broader humanitarian response, SC contributes to creating a safer and more secure environment for children and families in Cox's Bazar. Through a multifaceted approach that includes internal capacity building, community empowerment, and advocacy, Save the Children is actively working to mainstream protection measures in Cox's Bazar, ensuring a comprehensive and sustained effort to safeguard the rights and well-being of those affected by displacement.

3.3 - Age & Disability

In collaboration with local partners, SC implements inclusive programming that considers the diverse needs of different age groups and individuals with disabilities. This includes the provision of specialized support services, adaptive technologies, and inclusive spaces to facilitate their active participation in community life. Moreover, SC engages in community awareness initiatives to foster understanding and support for the rights and dignity of older adults and persons with disabilities. In all of the SC’s ongoing EiE projects, there is a specific target to include the children with disability to ensure their participation. To provide more holistic and specific solution for these disadvantaged groups, Individual action Plan, assessing through Globally recognized Washington Group Questioner to identify children with disability, provide awareness session for the parents and community and specific inclusive education focus captivity development initiative for the teacher are some of the key project interventions. Recognizing this as one of the crucial aspects for providing the service to the community, SC has also partnered with two specialized organisation (HI and CDD) who have a significant global and local expertise on disability and inclusion. By integrating age and disability considerations into its response efforts, Save the Children not only addresses immediate needs but also contributes to building a more inclusive and resilient community in Cox's Bazar. This approach aligns with the principles of the Joint Response Plan, ensuring that the most marginalized and vulnerable individuals are not only considered but actively included in the collective efforts to alleviate the impact of crises on displaced populations.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Save the Children’s (SC) Feedback and Reporting Mechanism (FRM) is a system for collecting and responding to feedback and complaints from children, communities, and stakeholders. It is designed to ensure accountability and transparency in SC's programs, and to maintain community dialogue and understanding. The FRM includes a variety of mechanisms, such as community meetings, exit interviews, hotlines, and helpdesk services. These mechanisms are accessible to even older persons and people with disabilities.SC processes and analyzes all feedback and complaints, and refers them to relevant sectors and external agencies as needed. Urgent matters, such as child safeguarding complaints, are responded to within 24 hours. SC uses the information collected through the FRM to improve its programs and services. The organization also reports on feedback and complaints through a monthly Accountability Snapshot and quarterly Accountability report.Key features of the FRMare Accountability: The FRM provides a mechanism for children, communities, and stakeholders to raise their voices and be heard. It also allows SC to identify areas where its programs and services can be improved, and to build trust and confidence with the people it serves.Community dialogue and understanding: The FRM provides a forum for children, communities, and stakeholders to share their views and experiences with SC. This helps SC to better understand the needs and concerns of the people it serves, and to develop programs and services that are more responsive to those needs and concerns.Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS): SC adheres to the CHS throughout its responses. The CHS are a set of nine standards that define the minimum levels of quality and accountability in humanitarian response. The FRM is aligned with the CHS, and helps SC to meet its commitments to accountability and transparency.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Save the Children's (SC) Education in Emergencies (EiE) intervention in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, includes a strong focus on disaster risk reduction (DRR) and environmental education. This is essential in a region that is highly vulnerable to natural disasters and climate change.All teachers and staff in SC's EiE programs receive basic DRR training. This training covers topics such as early warning systems, evacuation procedures, and disaster preparedness and response planning. In addition, there are regular discussions on DRR at different staff and teacher meeting platforms, such as Quality Learning Meetings and Teaching Learning Cycle meetings.The following are some specific examples of SC's DRR and environmental education interventions in Cox's Bazar:DRR training for teachers and staff: All Sc’s teache r and staff receive the training of DRR.This training covers topics such as early warning systems, evacuation procedures, and disaster preparedness and response planning.DRR drills and simulations: SC regularly conducts DRR drills and simulations with teachers, staff, and students. This helps to ensure that everyone is familiar with the evacuation procedures and knows what to do in the event of a disaster.Environmental education for students: SC's environmental education program is integrated into the curriculum and taught to all students in SC's EiE programs. The program covers topics such as waste management, water conservation, and tree planting.SC's DRR and environmental education interventions are helping to make the Rohingya refugees in Cox's Bazar more resilient to natural disasters and climate change. By educating teachers, staff, and students about DRR and environmental issues, SC is helping to build a safer and more sustainable future for the Rohingya community.
