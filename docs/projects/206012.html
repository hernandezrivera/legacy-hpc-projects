Project Summary

The project aims to provide timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for returnees, refugees and IDPs/host communities living in the hard-to-reach areas of Aweil East County of Northern Barhl El Ghazal State,South Sudan. It will target newly displaced people, returnees and host communities affected by conflict and flood. The project will focus on providing emergency WASH services to the displaced persons including most vulnerable host communities and also support the areas of returns. Focus will be on the repair and construction of Water points, training hand pumps mechanics, provision of hygiene and dignity kits and water treatment materials as well as hygiene promotion and messaging to increase awareness among the affected population and reduce the incidence water borne diseases. The affected population will also be supported in the construction of emergency latrines specifically targeting the most vulnerable and newly displaced people. The project will be delivered through a rapid response mechanism in project locations selected in line with the cluster prioritising process.

1. Needs

Access to safe drinking water: Shallow/hand-dug wells, or surface water (rivers or streams) are the main sources of water in South Sudan. Access to safe drinking water continues to be a challenge, and water-borne diseases have spread due to lack of clean water and poor sanitation. It is estimated that only 7% of the South Sudanese people have access to improved sanitation and only 41% t to safe drinking water. The effect of conflict, flooding and evolving of inter-communal fighting to new areas in 2023 aggravated the situation. As boreholes were damaged or made dysfunctional due to lack of repairs or effects of flooding in 2022. In Northern Bahr El Ghazal State, the monthly REACH assessment reports shows that while boreholes are the main source of water, their operation and maintenance and contamination by flood water during the rainy season are the main issues. Due to distance to the few existing limited infrastructure, access to safe water during dry season drops drastically. With increased population in some areas due to displacement and reduced amount of water during dry season, it is expected that water induced conflicts will arise at the water collection points. In South Sudan lack of access to safe drinking water and adequate sanitation pose protection risks for women, with the search to find a safe place to defecate and the trek to collect water, women are exposed to the threat of violence and harassment. Unsafe excreta disposal: Only 16% of the urban population and 4% of the rural population in South Sudan have access to adequate sanitation facilities, which is one of the lowest statistical coverage in the world. 50% and 79% of South Sudanese practice open defecation in cities and rural areas, respectively. Open defecation is therefore common and is one of the biggest causes of diarrhoea, due to the faecal-oral transmission of diarrhoea-causing organisms. Lack of awareness regarding safe hygiene practices: A higher percentage of the affected population often have very low ability to effectively utilise improved water and sanitation facilities to prevent diseases. It was noted that this was a result of using dirt water containers for collection and storage. Poor hygiene and sanitation conditions, as well as a lack of access to safe water, have resulted in an ongoing high watery diarrhoea disease. There is a dire need to raise awareness through promotional activities and training to enable effectiveness of water and sanitation improvements. Community capacity building for the management of water sources: There is a need for capacity building and support to communities to empower them to take responsibility over their water facilities and actively participate in their Operation and Maintenance in ways that are flexible and incorporate measures which deal with gender inequality and barriers to access.South Sudan continues to suffer complex shocks of floods, displacements, food insecurity, inflation, and lack of access to basic WASH and protection services. At least 6.1 million are in need of WASH assistance. Over 1 million people got affected by floods due to torrential rain and flooding across 36 counties of South Sudan further exacerbating the already dire living conditions of the population and propagation of communicable diseases, maternal mortality and neonatal health that continue to be the leading causes of morbidity and mortality in South Sudan and in particular Northern Barhl el Ghazal where access to basic services remain unmet.

CLWSH/CO3: WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation. (1,416,841). CLWSH/CO4: Community members exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures and infrastructure (Number of water points rehabilitated/constructed with a flood-resilient design-7,303; Number of latrine stances rehabilitated/constructed with a flood-resilient design-36,515; Number of disability inclusive sanitation facilities designed-5,287). Emergency Response and Development Initiative (ERDI) intervention strategy would align with strategic objective to contributing towards reducing WASH related morbidity and mortality in the priority counties and Providing access to basic WASH services in emergencies through WASH integrated programming with health and Nutrition subsequently allaying hunger reduction and climate change sensitive approach (Adhere to thresholds of environmental markers). Response to myriad of WASH needs will include increasing equitable access to improved water sources/ water treatment goods, increase inclusive access to sanitation facilities and improved hygiene practices both at institutional and community levels. WASH non-food item (NFI) distributions will be based on the need of the beneficiaries. WASH NFIs will be distributed through community and nutrition programs to optimize desired public health outcome. Community distributions will respond to acute needs, based on validated assessment or public health concerns that emanate from flooding or displacement. Distributions through the nutrition program will target children or pregnant lactating women (PLWs) enrolled in the program to provide a long-lasting supply for those families with intent to impact recovery and prevent relapse. Distributions will also prioritize extremely vulnerable households such as female-headed households, households with a member who is disabled, elderly, and pregnant or lactating women. Hygiene kits received from the core pipeline will be distributed in compliance with response strategy including the HeV WASH response strategy. Distributions will be standardized in each location, but based on the needs of beneficiaries in that context. WASH infrastructure rehabilitations, including water points and sanitation facilities will be provided in strict adherence to sphere and national cluster standards including water quality monitoring. In view to ensure ownership of the response across all spectrum of beneficiaries, ERDI will facilitate equitable formation of WASH resource groups (Water users Committees; Community handpump mechanics etc.) and provide targeted trainings and basic equipments for operation and maintenance of established WASH system, job aids will be provided to selected community members as volunteer community hygiene promoters for hygiene promotion, and who will also liaise with health and nutrition facility staff to target beneficiaries who seek health and nutrition services. Community Hygiene Promoters will also closely liaise health and nutrition extension workers such as community nutrition workers, Boma Health Workers (BHWs) to reinforce community-based outreaches including referrals for safe services. At community levels, door-to-door campaigns, clean water source campaigns, participation in global WASH events such as Global handwashing day; World Toilet day, menstrual hygiene management day and World Water day to accelerate awareness linking poor sanitation; poor water quantity & quality and poor hygiene practices to diseases outbreaks; morbid
