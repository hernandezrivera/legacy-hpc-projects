Project Summary

An Initial Rapids Needs Assessment(IRNA) conducted by humanitarian partners including HARECOD in Cueibet county between August 22-23, 2022(https://www.humanitarianresponse.info), found over 68,988(11,498 Households) vulnerable persons are in dire need of humanitarian assistance with Payams of Cueibet center, Pagor, and Achol Malek having almost 50 percent of the affected people(estimated 5664 Households or 33,984 people; 6Persons per Household).
Over 754,000 people are in dire need of immediate access to safe drinking water through household products or SWAT according to the latest OCHA Flood Response situation Report as of 31st October 2022(https://reliefweb.int).
The cumulative impact of recurrent flooding contributed to the destruction and damage to water facilities, leaving vulnerable people in urgent need of water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services, and intensified existing vulnerabilities of affected people, which include limited access to basic services, high prevalence of disease outbreaks, and widespread displacement. Covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks, as well as other household-level stressors, for example, gender-based violence (GBV) and poor water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services, are some of the key drivers of humanitarian needs in the country(See: South Sudan: Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022).
Protection concerns are present when accessing WASH facilities and services across all counties of South Sudan. 18 per cent of households reported feeling unsafe while collecting water (FSNMS, 2021). Of those, 12 per cent of women and girls reported avoiding water points due to GBV concerns (FSNMS, 2021).
HARECOD is proposing a project in line with the achievement of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals Number 6 which advocates for available and sustainable Water and sanitation for All worldwide. The project is also aligned to the implementation of the South Sudan WASH Cluster strategy 2022-2023 and aligned to the south Sudan WASH Cluster HRP 2023 Indicator framework under Specific Objective 2 and indicators 5,6 and 7 which translates to target of over 6,936,367 targeted people for access to improved water source and sanitation facilities, as well as people reached with hygiene promotion and soap.
The target county: Cueibet is a HRP 2023 priority county and is in severity classification 4(critical phase) and is a flood affected county experiencing conflicts leading to massive displacement of populations.
The purpose of this project is to achieve WASH Cluster strategic objective 2 under specific objectives 1 and 3 relating to reducing prevalence of malnutrition and providing access to basic WASH services in underserved locations affected by disaster.
This project will approach each specific objective by integrating with nutrition at community level, and Delivering WASH services through static partners like HARECOD and mobile emergency response mechanism (EPR).
This project aims to increase access to safe and dignified services leading to provision of portable safe water from improved water sources and improvement of sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion for households in Cueibet and for communities nearby to reduce the risk of water-borne diseases particularly flood-prone areas of Malou-pec, Abiriu, Mayath, Chitcok, and Pagor payams.
The capacity of hygiene promoters, Nutrition nurses, community mobilisers, and county health staff shall be improved to promote WASH key messages within the community; with WASH related promotional materials provided at all community and facility hot spots such as schools, markets, cattle camps, community centers. HARECOD will ensure these messages are translated to the local languages and Arabic for easy of understanding.

1 - Needs

Cueibet county is one of the 52 priority counties in south Sudan in Severity Classification 4(Emergency phase) and is likely lead to catastrophic phase 5 if priority is not given to this county which is severely destroyed by Floods and experiencing repeated intra-communal conflicts coupled with the continued displacements due to Floods, food insecurity, and conflicts. This has negatively impacted access to basic WASH services resulting to WASH Cluster target of 100,834 vulnerable persons according to Indicator 5, 6, and 7 included in the WASH Cluster HRP 2023 Indicator framework shared to all partners on south Sudan WASH Forum. The affected populations in Cueibet county represent 80.3 percent of the WASH HRP 2023 target in Lake’s state(WASH Cluster HRP2023 Indicator framework, partner reference). Over 11,498 households were reported to have been affected by Floods in Cueibet county according to a latest IRNA assessment conducted in August 22-23 by Humanitarian partners, including HARECOD(https://www.humanitarianresponse.info) leading the WASH Cluster as HRP 2022 partner during the assessment.HARECOD is targeting 38,142(6,357 Households, at 6persons per a Household) vulnerable persons representing 30.4 percent of WASH HRP target for Cueibet and 0.5 percent of the overall WASH target for 2022 countrywide. We are prioritizing 4,577 children under 5(8% Male and 4 % Females), 16,401 children within age range of 5-17 years (23% Male, 20% Female), we shall target 12,968 adults aged 18-60(18% Male, 17% female), and the elderly represent 11 percent of our HRP target translating to 4,196 adults aged 60 above.According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview, South Sudan 2022(see: South Sudan: Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 |(https://www.humanitarianResponse.info), floods regularly damage existing sanitation infrastructure and given their annual occurrence and paired with frequent displacement, there is great hesitation by communities to invest in, reconstruct, or repair sanitation facilities.Cueibet county has experienced annual heavy flooding and intra-communal conflicts leading to mass displacement and destruction of sanitation facilities. The massive flooding in Cueibet county in late 2022 led to the destruction of 11,498 households as found during a Rapid Needs Assessment in 2022(IRNA Report-Flood Assessment in Cueibet County Lakes State August 22-23 | https://reliefweb.int). The collapsed households were highest recorded in payams of Cueibet center, Pagor, Duony, Ngap, and Achol Malek. The affected populations were displaced and left behind necessary WASH materials such as hygiene kits making them vulnerable to WASH related diseases such as diarrhoea, cholera, and skin diseases.Limited access to WASH infrastructure coverage alone, for instance, water points and latrines has carry-on impacts when utilizing a multi-sectoral view. Poor access to WASH services and goods combined with high levels of food insecurity has a detrimental impact on the health of the most vulnerable people, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition. Cueibet county has seen latrines, water points including boreholes, and crops destroyed due to heavy flooding leading to displacement of people which has made them more vulnerable to water-borne diseases such as diarrhoea in children under 5 raising prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). All the four nutrition facilities in Cueibet county are in critical phase 4 and need urgent response, which HARECOD will integrate into its WASH services provision.

Activity 1: Carryout Market based assessment in all payams in Cueibet 2. Conduct WASH specific assessment and safety audit Integrated with WASH related GBV risk questions 3. Conduct extensive assessment of all Nutrition sites, and provide recommendations and proposals for scale up of services. 4. Improving access to sanitation facilities at all the four nutrition sites. 5. Scaling up provision of safe drinking water to key nutrition sites. 6. Conduct water quality testing, analysis and chlorination at all targeted water sources. 12. Repair or rehabilitate non-functional water facilities especially boreholes, hand dug wells, etc. 13. Distribute WASH hygiene kits including water treatment tablets to all targeted households in a safe manner. 15. Conduct End-Line and behavioral change Surveys and submit reports to WASH Cluster and donors.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HARECOD and Directorate of Water are always working to ensure the above mentioned Water Committees gender balanced and inclusive composition and attendance, making them as participatory spaces women and other vulnerable individuals can be part of, an empowerment driver, offering public engagement opportunity, usually very limited for certain groups, and making these latter aware of their own value, to be enhanced through a virtuous circle of increasing participation. Similarly, Review Meetings and community-based activities will be open to women, individuals living with disabilities(PWD), and other vulnerable groups.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information about the project will be driven through existing community consultation mechanism, to avoid overlapping and confusion and to contribute to strengthen them even further. The same mechanism will be used to get feedback and complaints, to make easier for the beneficiaries, already used to them, to give their contribute. HARECOD is used to maintain a constant dialogue with communities representatives, from those belonging to the Water facilities related Water User Committees to those from community based associations and nutrition sites, which are invited to take part to the Quarterly Review Meetings held at County level and which ensure a wider perspective on community needs and expectations and can report on services responsiveness and acceptability. The quarterly Client Satisfaction Survey offers the opportunity to get feedback on provided services by the direct beneficiaries.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HARECOD has its own PSEA policy. The related communication strategy consists in integrating PSEA information dissemination into the ongoing activities aimed to sensitize people on Gender related matters, including Gender Equality, Gender Empowerment, GBV. These activities includes all project staff and authorities training, community meetings, IEC materials production and events, activities which are part of HARECOD on going intervention and which will be included in the project as well, to reach a wider audience. Integrating PSEA information in this framework is expected to facilitate its understanding and to promote its acceptability. HARECOD PSEA policy is completed by a toolkit aimed to assist the field team in this process

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries feedback will be collected through the above-described community consultation mechanism, already existing and regularly used to get population perspective and perceptions on services responsiveness and acceptability. This consultation mechanism has been designed to get continuous inputs on the situation on ground and to be enabled doing timely adaptations of the proposed activities/supported services. As already used to do, HARECOD field team will assist the local health authorities in collecting, consolidating and analyzing the various feedback and to translate them into action points to be integrated into proposed project work plan and overarching strategy, making them more tailored and focused to respond to the needs and to address the issues beneficiaries perceived as priorities in that specific moment. This is also functional to ensure project constant update and alignment to the reality, narrowing the gap created by the time passed from project writing and project implementation.
