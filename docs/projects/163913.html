Project Summary

The project is designed to respond to the needs of the hosted people, The prioritization of the targeted beneficiaries would be based on cluster coordinated vulnerability and selection criteria, to the protracted needs of the existing IDPs and the hosting community by providing a reliable sources of drinking water and sanitation service and men, women, boys, and girls will benefit from the activities which aim to increase access to sustainable water, sanitation, and environmental hygiene solutions. Sustainability will be aimed at through the re-establishment of a cost recovery system. This project aims to cover the WASH services need of 310370 :( Men:56980, Women: 60990, Boys 94670, Girls: 97730 ) persons (162630 DPs, 141550 hosted people, and returnees: 6190, of the Persons with Disabilities: 2180) at Idleb and Aleppo governorates.
The planned quantity of water that will be provided to each person in the targeted communities that will benefit from water sector intervention is about 40-50 liter/day and 30-30liter /day for the people in the camps.
During the project SECD will perform the following activities:
-Rehabilitation of the four water stations in the targeted communities.
- The rehabilitation sewer system in the targeted communities.
-Establish a cost recovery system
- 1000 family Hygiene kits will distribute to the most valuable people in the targeted communities.
- Garbage bins 0.1m3 size for will be Provided and install.
-Water trucking for the people in the camps
-Fuel and chlorine will be supported by water stations.
-Solid waste management (collection and disposal) will be supported.
-Capacity building of the water unit in the city council to ensure that they have adequate operations and maintenance teams. These teams will be responsible for ensuring sustainable water services by activating a tariff system.
During the project, SECD will support and rehabilitate and operate about 30 water systems. During the project, SECD confirms and ensure that the drinkable water, public sewage system, and solid waste management are available for the health centers, Schools Bakeries, Child-friendly spaces, protection centers, and the SECD team take in their account that the sudden new displacement during the implementation period and new IDPs may arrive at the targeted locations, so the SECD team will give them the priority according to vulnerability criteria and provide them with hygiene kits and WASH services. During all stages of the project the local authorities/communities will be involved from need assessment until closing the project, so SECD will always conduct a meeting, FGD, questioners, MVR, workshops….with the key beneficiaries representative.
Through this project, we will be able to provide better access to basic necessities for IDPs and hosted people, including women and children, Water tank, WASH facilities, and hygiene kits will be provided to the beneficiaries, Also, populations, including women and children, will be less vulnerable to communicable diseases that are spread through unsanitary water. Furthermore, by providing WASH services to locations that IDPs are fleeing to, the IDPs will be welcomed into a more protective, well-serviced environment
The intervention is considering the provision of durable and cost-effective solution to reduce the operational costs of the water stations by using the hybrid power system to pump and supply water for household consumption since the solar power system can be applied in the targeted water stations due to its safe locations and the technical specifications of the water stations is suitable for such solution Through this project, SECD is planning to support the targeted communities with the following activities: -Installation of the solar power system to turn the operating system of the water stations when possible.
Also, SECD now studies the possibility of turning the solid waste to compost to reduce the amount of solid waste and recycle most of it.

4 - Needs

Comprehensive WASH assessments were conducted in June-August 2019 to inform the 2020 HNO, including a country-wide individual household-level survey at sub-district level (25,644 household level interviews from 268 sub-districts across Syria) and a household-level survey focused on IDP sites in north-west and north-east Syria (2,910 household level interviews in 280 IDP sites in 34 sub-districts). Both assessments included drinking water sampling for free residual chlorine (FRC) testing at the end-user level to determine water safety. The assessments revealed that, despite the overall slight improvement of WASH conditions in Syria, vast WASH-related needs continue to exist in many parts of Syria, affecting various population groups. Access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies remain a challenge for newly displaced populations, IDPs living in IDP sites, as well as other most vulnerable households with limited livelihood opportunities and access to basic WASH services. Newly displaced populations require a timely and quality response based on comprehensive preparedness, and smooth transition into regular programming, in particular in NWS where large scale movement of IDPs is continuously observed. IDPs informal sites and informal settlements are highly dependent on continuous humanitarian assistance. Disruptions of services in IDP sites and their quality will have a direct negative impact on people's physical and mental well-being as well as protection consequences, specifically for women and girls. After nine years of conflict, the WASH infrastructure and service provision in many parts of Syria require significant investments, repair and operational support. WASH systems have suffered from damages owing to years of functioning at high capacity, limited or no maintenance, destructions, displacement of technical staff and effects of climate change and poor water resources management.
