Project Summary

SI aims to provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable displaced populations living in camps and camp-like settings by providing winterization services. Despite humanitarian presence, most IDPs continue to face critical winterization needs. Findings from the CCCM Cluster confirm that, despite the humanitarian presence, winterization needs are still not fully covered in Azaz. In fact, according to Humanitarian Needs Assessment Programme (HNAP), 40% of IDPs in NWS report that water is their first priority. In terms of infrastructure, at least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated, while at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional and 29% of garbage is inappropriately disposed within communities (HNO 2021), with 64% of water stations not functional, according to the Syrian Water Resources Platform. SI assessments in the area of operation indicate that respondents rely heavily on NGO
supported water supply sources, primarily water trucking. SI seeks to address identified needs with a comprehensive (WASH and) winterization approach:
• Winterization assistance through one-off cash distribution of $150 to 2075 HHs in AlBab, Ghandorah and Aghtrin. (Target: 12.412 individuals)
Beneficiary selection criteria are always based on needs and vulnerabilities. Gender equality will be prioritized and beneficiary engagement throughout the project cycle will ensure transparency and awareness of project activities. SI will use the Washington Group questionnaire in the identification of barriers and risks of persons with disabilities in accessing water, sanitation, and hygiene services.

4 - Needs

According to the ACU report, 250,000 people are in need of a winter response in NWS. The priority needs are: fuel (53% for Azaz, 32% for Idlib, 57% for Jarablus) and heaters (25% for Azaz, 45% for Idlib and 37% for Jarablus), while in Al Bab the priority need is clothes for winter. Observation of SI teams in Aleppo and Idlib governorates confirmed that the three most needed items are: heating fuel, heaters and winter blankets.

5 - Implementation of activities

Shelter & Settlements objective: To provide critical, timely and appropriate winterization assistance to the most vulnerable in NWSSI seeks to contribute to the ongoing Shelter intervention in Northwest Syria, by supporting the Shelter/NFI cluster specific objective “Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelterand non-food item (NFI) support” by distributing winterization cash assistance with a one-off cash distribution of $150. Based on SI’s past experiences in NWS, cash distribution is the more suitable modality for winterization assistance. In Azaz, Jarablus and Al Bab, SI has recently finished cash distributions for winterization assistance, PDM beneficiaries’ feedback confirmed that cash modality is preferred compared to in-kind because it gives them more flexibility. Additionally, the local markets are functional in the target areas and offer the needed items in good quality. In line with Shelter and NFI cluster recommendation, SI will conduct one round of distribution of cash for winterization ($150) in Feb/March 2022; (and 2023 depending on Funding) to cover the full SMEB value (including heating fuel cost).

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks include: • Inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages acrosslocations • Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees due to variedassistance packages • Abuse of power by staff distributing goods or deliveringservices to vulnerable populations. • Road accidents, physical harm • Restricted access(due to insecurity of COVID-19 lockdowns) • COVID-19 public health concernsMitigation measures for the identified risks are integrated in SI’s implementationstrategy, starting with transparent selection criteria agreed upon in coordination withthe relevant clusters. The risk of abuse of power is mitigated by thorough vetting oflocally recruited staff, who all abide by the SI Code of Conduct. SI also has a Feedbackand Complaints Response Mechanism (FCRM) in place that allows for real-timefeedback from beneficiaries and other stakeholders. The FCRM is an accountabilitytool that simultaneously facilitates the collection of lessons learnt that will be used toimprove future interventions.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks: Gender-based violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), tends toincrease during times of crisis. Risks of this occurring further increase in situations ofaid dependency and power imbalances. SI is cognizant of the inherent risks related toservice delivery, notably for cash and in-kind distributions. To ensure that assistancedelivered is safe, relevant and timely, SI has accountability systems and procedures inplace. Mitigation and prevention measures: SI is determined to prevent the sexualexploitation and abuse of beneficiaries by humanitarian workers and reaffirms its goalof achieving full implementation of the 6 core principles adopted by the Inter-AgencyStanding Committee Task Force on Prevention and Response to Sexual Exploitationand Abuse. SI's PSEA Policy complements the Code of Conduct and Protection Policy,both of which state that it is strictly forbidden for any employee i) to use its position toobtain any favor, including sexual favors, and ii) to have sexual intercourse withminors. SI’s contracts and partnership agreements include a standard clause requiringsub-contractors to adopt policies that prohibit SEA and to take adequate measures toprevent and respond when required. A Whistleblowing Policy and a Feedback andComplaints Response Mechanisms Framework are key pillars of SOLIDARITESINTERNATIONAL Policies package. SI has PSEA focal points to identify and addressany PSEA concerns and implements a referral system when required.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: Absence or non-materialization of the assumptions mentionedabove Mitigation: • Use money transfer system (hawala) for operations in North Syriaincluding for the payment of its staff and contractors • Cooperation with Turkishauthorities and capacity to work Cross-Border from Turkey. • SI maintains arepresentation in Gaziantep in Turkey with its main coordination office in Amman
