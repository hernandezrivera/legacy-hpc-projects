Project Summary

The project will be implemented in Lainya, Kajo keji, Morobo and Yei counties of Central Equatoria State, Aweil East, Aweil South and Centre of Northern Bahr Ghazal State and Uror, Twic East and Pochalla of Jonglei State.
Poor food security in all locations, coupled with poor WASH practices, is leading to high rates of global acute malnutrition, over WHO’s critical level of 15%. The strong relationship between poor WASH infrastructure and practices, food insecurity and malnutrition is perpetuating high SAM and MAM rates. Use of unsafe drinking water and poor hygiene and sanitation practices are contributing to the high incidence of diarrhoea and water-borne diseases in vulnerable communities, sustaining the malnutrition-diarrhoea cycle and reducing the nutrient uptake in infants and young children.
The project will target over 141,000 individuals. The project will seek to improve water supply infrastructure to increase access to safe drinking water and facilitate hygiene promotion to encourage behaviour change relating to hygiene and sanitation practices by ensuring access to safe water through rehabilitation/ construction of water points, water quality testing, provision of WASH NFIs, ensuring access to safe sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion.
The project will be in line with WASH Cluster's objective of integrating WASH response with nutrition /food security and Health; addressing live-saving needs in disaster contexts in restoring immediate access to basic WASH services either temporarily or permanently; and building in locations that were affected by flooding in the past three years.
Tearfund will, (1) Improve water supply infrastructure to increase access to safe drinking water and (2) Facilitate hygiene promotion throughout the targeted communities to encourage behaviour change relating to hygiene and sanitation practices, (3) construct and installed hand washing facilities in Health, Nutrition facilities to encourage hand washing hence reducing Covid 19 transmissions. The proposed intervention will enable Tearfund to continue providing critical services addressing access to safe drinking water and increased sanitation facilities, and improved hygiene practices to vulnerable communities in Aweil East & Centre, Uror, Twic East, Pochalla, Kajo keji, Morobo as well expand activities to targeted communities in Yei and Lainya.
Tearfund has a static presence throughout Jonglei, Northern Bahr El Ghazal, and Central Equatoria. The proposed project is part of Tearfund’s wider programme, which integrates WASH, Nutrition and Food Security and Protection.

1 - Needs

Twic East has been prioritised by the WASH Cluster as one of the priority 1 counties presenting an extreme WASH vulnerability and high prevalence of malnutrition. According to the 2022 WASH Cluster Population in Need, the severity of the target locations will continue to be high. The findings from the 2021 FSNMS + analysis indicate that access to WASH facilities by women and girls, boys, persons with disabilities and elderly and services remains a challenge. Latrines were often overflowing, also creating the fear of disease.Severe flooding in 2022 resulted in the destruction and damage of water facilities, reduced access to sanitation and loss of hygiene items which resulted in the urgent need for water, sanitation and hygiene services. The proportion of the population with access to latrines remains very low in the majority of the country, resulting in high open defecation levels. Overall, 80 percent of households have no access to latrines (HNO 2022). The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. These shocks have led to the continued displacement of people and restricted their access to already limited and functional WASH infrastructure. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites and creates barriers for Tearfund (WASH partners) when implementing activities.Assessments conducted by Tearfund and other Consortium partners highlighted the need for humanitarian aid amongst the returnees and IDPs as the host communities are already poor and lack basic services. Surveyed households across the counties indicated that food, healthcare, shelter and household items are priority needs. Severe food and income insecurity, poor sanitation practices ( 70% reported not having access to a safe latrine), limited access to protection, WASH and health services are linked to widespread deaths, diseases and poor well-being of vulnerable people. Women, children and youth are disproportionately affected. Due to security concerns many people are settling in remote locations where provision of services is even worse than would be typical in South Sudan. The current COVID-19 pandemic and the threat of a more lethal wave in the region, has further hindered border communities’ ability to access basic services and means of livelihoods from neighbouring countries. The economic downturn has exacerbated the disruption of water services, limiting resources available for the maintenance of wells and boreholes. Community engagements revealed that threats to personal security for children and women (eg sexual and GBV, including rape, forced, early and child marriages), as well as limited access to GBV medical and psychosocial support services produce multiple layers of harmful inequalities. Protection-related services in all the target counties are very limited. The strong relationship between poor WASH infrastructure and practices, food insecurity and malnutrition is perpetuating high SAM and MAM rates. Use of unsafe drinking water and poor hygiene and sanitation practices are contributing to the high incidence of diarrhoea and water-borne diseases in vulnerable communities, sustaining the malnutrition-diarrhoea cycle and reducing the nutrient uptake in infants and young children.

2 - Project activities

The project is in line with the Cluster's HRP strategic objectives. The proposed project will carry out the following activities: Rehabilitation of boreholes: This rehabilitation will be carried out in health and nutrition facilities and in the community, prioritising villages with high rates of GAM and SAM. Tearfund will test the water quality to ensure that it remains safe from bacterial contaminants after activity completion. In order to prevent contamination of the borehole after rehabilitation, the WUCs (one at each borehole) will be trained and equipped to fence off the boreholes, in order to prevent animals from accessing them. Pump Mechanics at each rehabilitated borehole will receive technical training including on the construction of platforms and basic repairs in order to ensure they are well capacitated to maintain the boreholes in working order. By deepening their knowledge of borehole repair and rehabilitation, Tearfund is adding to the sustainability of boreholes in situations where overuse frequently causes breakdowns. Water quality testing carried out on boreholes in targeted areas: Tearfund will conduct water quality tests on each of the rehabilitated boreholes to ensure that SPHERE standards for water quality are met. Training and equipping hand pump mechanics: Tearfund has trained pump mechanics linked to WUCs for the last 6 years. This is becoming a sustainable structure for the maintenance of water points, with the pump mechanics embedded within the community (and therefore easily accessible) and WUCs collecting contributions from the community which are put towards the operation and maintenance of the water point. The added value of creating exchanges between new and experienced pump mechanics has become evident from previous/ current interventions. Through this exchange, Tearfund has been able to broaden the knowledge of experienced pump mechanics by introducing them to new topics during the training of the new mechanics. Tearfund will train new pump mechanics - targeting both females and males where possible in order to enlarge the pool of pump mechanics in the targeted area and to close the gender gap in WASH. Strengthening Water User Committees: All communities where borehole rehabilitation is undertaken will receive support for setting up WUCs and corresponding training. While additional selection criteria will be defined in consultation with the community, at a minimum, members will be permanent residents of the village, respectful of others and trustworthy. The selected community members will be trained by Tearfund and enlightened on their roles and who they report to and how. Training of WUCs will include the chief and Payam administrator. Training will be carried out based on a 14-step curriculum developed by Tearfund’s WASH Advisor, led across a 2-3 day training, based on Tearfund’s experience in Aweil.Construction of households and institutions latrines with hand-washing units addresses issues of open-defecation and the spread of oral-faecal disease. Gender and protection needs will be taken into consideration in the design of the latrines, ensuring safe access for women, girls, men and boys alike.Tearfund will sensitise targeted communities on 5 different areas of hygiene i.e. personal hygiene, food hygiene, water hygiene, domestic or environmental hygiene and safe excreta disposal. This will be conducted through Village Hygiene Promotion Resource Persons (VHRPs). A Participatory Hygiene and Sanitation Transformation (PHAST) approach will be applied to address the general hygiene and sanitation issues that affect the targeted area and which increase the risk of fecal oral diseases and, consequently, malnutrition.WASH NFIs will be distributed to households affected by floods to ensure access to basic WASH supplies, including aqua tabs, Menstrual management kits, water buckets, soap, etc, reducing the risk of waterborne diseases and malaria.

3 - Project indicators

The number of SAM children provided with standard hygiene kits paired with hygiene promotion and the number of nutrition facilities located in IPC 4/5 areas reached with access to safe water will be used to measure progress against this objective: WASH integrated intervention starting at nutrition facilities targeting the couple “mother/care-taker – severely malnourished child” until HH level aiming at breaking WASH-related diseases transmission cycle. The number of beneficiaries at risk of WASH-related diseases reached with safe access to semi- or permanent water, and the number of beneficiaries at risk of WASH-related diseases reached with hygiene promotion will be collected to measure the extent to which the project is progressing towards reducing prevalence of WASH-related diseases and risk of outbreak by transmission contexts upon epidemiological investigation. Other key indicators will include: Number of beneficiaries reached with safe access to temporary or semi-permanent water Number of beneficiaries reached with safe access to temporary or semi-permanent sanitation Number of beneficiaries reached with hygiene promotion Number of beneficiaries reached with the package of assistance ( conduct safety audit at site level) Number of beneficiaries regularly affected by flooding reached with sustained access to permanent water with flood-resilient infrastructures Number of beneficiaries in historically high food insecure communities reached with sustained access to permanent water with solid/strong infrastructures and sustained access to sanitation Number of beneficiaries in communities showing high prevalence of GAM and high WASH vulnerabilities reached with safe access to (semi-)permanent water Number of beneficiaries in communities showing high prevalence of GAM and high WASH vulnerabilities reached with hygiene promotion The project will target the most vulnerable groups in the target locations. Data will be disaggregated by age, gender and disability, and will include IDPs, returnees, hosts and chronically affected by floods and (pre-) famine conditions.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Carrying out need assessment will help us understand Information sharing and feedback mechanisms in order to adapt information to the literacy levels amongst specific vulnerable groups to avoid excluding vulnerable groups. As part of the program design, risk assessment will be conducted to ensure Tearfund work does not cause harm to vulnerable people. The information tools will be contextualised and explained to generate understanding and be relevant. The project will disaggregate data to match the project affected population or sub-groups where practical actions will be embedded into planning and delivery of WASH services to ensure inclusivity and needs of all gender groups. The engagement of the vulnerable groups will help us to understand expectations, involve them in the decision making process and enable us to receive timely feedback. The steps will help address the needs of the most vulnerable in reducing inequalities and help everyone benefit equally from the project.Tearfund will build and strengthen the capacity of staff, partners and relevant stakeholders to integrate gender and intersectionality into project design, planning, implementation and monitoring and review of project messages. Key messages on gender, disability, social inclusion will be developed and translated into the local language to guide staff as they implement activities. PMC will work closely with the local actors to identify the individuals with mobility needs and mentally handicapped to facilitate timely provision of support.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

To support better performance and WASH outcomes at the community, Tearfund and partners will ground their response in the needs, priorities and expectations of the beneficiaries through a number of approaches for example, by engaging in dialogue and joint analysis with beneficiaries on feedback and complaint mechanisms to empower the communities in ways to communicate their needs and concerns and provide the space to make decisions that affect them to enable beneficiaries to act on their own independently. At WASH facility centres, Tearfund will use notice boards with details about tearfund, contact persons, names and numbers of community representatives in committees, a summary of the project and a map of the project area. Communities will be included in the leadership of WASH response activities in designing facilities processes and community participation in monitoring and evaluating response programs. Complaint help desks will help in providing basic assistance, psychosocial support and counselling to returnees/IDPs/ host communities through the use of community volunteers at the water points and community centres and sites so that the communities can provide regular feedback. Additionally, referral pathways will provide the communities with information on the existing service providers, their services and locations and how to access them.The project will establish a Project Management Committee (PMC) who will be involved in the design phase of the project and throughout the implementation period. Mapping of existing mechanisms will identify challenges and best practices. The PMC will orient the communities on feedback and complaint mechanisms to ensure their timeliness and responsiveness.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Tearfund will strengthen staff capacity on PSEA and will share with beneficiaries information about protection from sexual exploitation and abuse during all project activities. In targeted locations, Tearfund will work with GBV actors and community based protection actors to spread awareness about the GBV referral pathway including a range of services for survivors. Tearfund will collaborate with protection cluster to provide information that seeks to prevent and respond to abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence against women, men, boys and girls, and ensure that a comprehensive complaint mechanism is in place and sagefuarding reports and concerns are investigated. Tearfund will develop Information Education Materials (IEC) and disseminate information to the beneficiaries at the host communities/IDPs/Returnees on the available service providers, locations and services available. The IEC materials will also convey knowledge and skills to young men to adopt gender equitable behaviour.Tearfund has safeguarding policies in place, the staff will be trained on PSEA to undertake safeguarding initiatives to strengthen local capacities and assure their full compliance and understanding on safeguarding policies and practices. PSEA messages together with entitlement cards will be translated into local language to ensure transparency and accountability. Tearfund will embed a robust and timely referral mechanism and form linkages with national child protection actors to refer any identified child protection case.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Tearfund will gather data about communications channels in communicating with communities and consider their rights and entitlements in order to identify feedback mechanisms to be adapted in delivering food assistance in emergencies. Tearfund will analyse information provided by the returnees/IDPs and host communities through the use of field staff and community volunteers to triangulate the information analysis for service improvement. Tearfund will look at other types of information such as, life saving information, where people can access support, people’s rights and entitlements, sexual exploitation and abuse to the communities, security risks, corruption etc to provide adjustments if necessary throughout the project. Information on people’s rights will take into consideration international humanitarian standards (Sphere, HAP, IASC GBV guidelines, Red cross code of conduct) in the provision of impartial and timely access to services and right to protection. Tearfund will ensure beneficiaries participate in decision-making concerning activities and to lead or support the implementation of activities.Tearfund will undertake feedback and review meetings with PMC on a regular basis to identify challenges and gaps to integrate learnings and improve project implementation.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will provide sustainable and high-quality standard services with a disaster risk reduction component. There is a need to provide climate-proofing WASH infrastructure, drainage systems, services, and behaviours to build community resilience against flood-related displacement. Through the hygiene promotion activities, Tearfund will enhance beneficiaries coping mechanisms and local individual measures to build resiliencies to the concurrent shocks through identifying and implementing various flood mitigation measures to reduce risk and exposure during flooding, working to create flood friendly environments, improve the community’s ability to respond during a flood, and identifying strategies for recovery after a flood. This project will address live-saving needs in disaster contexts in restoring immediate access to basic WASH services either temporarily or permanently. The project will also target locations that were affected by flooding in the past three years. It aims at addressing the needs of the most vulnerable with durable solutions and community engagement in finding solutions and maintenance of facilities.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Tearfund through this project will ensure that prior to boreholes rehabilitation safety analysis is conducted to mark the protection and appropriate measures are taken into eliminating the risk. Maintaining the sphere standard ratio of 1 borehole to 500 individuals, it will reduce queuing time. Prior to hand over of the rehabilitated borehole, water quality analysis will be carried out to ensure safe portable water is commissioned to the community for consumption.Tearfund approach embedded in the equity and inclusion as well as Accountability to the affected population WASH Cluster approach, Thus consultation and beneficiaries on the design, accessibility and locations for the implementation of the activities. borehole platforms and latrines design include access ramps for the disabled.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Providing safe access to potable drinking water and sanitation facilities including good hygiene practices, will improve the quality of food being consumed at household level, it as well reduces the prevalence of the acute watery diarrhoea case in Children. It gives the mother and caregivers an opportunity to spend more time doing food security and livelihood activities than looking for water. Thus lowers water related diseases, improves nutrition and decreases the IPC level.As poor access to WASH services have a high impact on the health of children, resulting in high malnutrition rate, providing safe drinking water to the community, dissemination of safe hygiene and sanitation practices to the mother/care givers will automatically reduce malnutrition and improve the health of children. Services provided will break the transmission cycle of main communicable diseases (i.e. diarrhea), thus improving food intake of malnourished children and contributing to the sustainability of health and nutrition objectives.
