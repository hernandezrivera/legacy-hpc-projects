Project Summary

CRADA is proposing to support to communities in Pibor IPC 5, Nyirol and Akobo counties in IPC 4 in meeting the FSL cluster’s strategic objective:
1. CO2 Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines)
2. CO3 Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19
CRADA will implement the project in Akobo, Nyirol, and Akobo County. Pibor County has population of 227,900(Humanitarian Needs Overview South Sudan, February 2022) with UNOCHA approximating that 216,500 people being in need of humanitarian assistance 95% of the total population,52% of this are women and 55% of these are children there are 57,000 people in catastrophic level and are in need of humanitarian assistance (HNO, February 2022, pg. 45-46) Nyirol county has a population of 142,200 with 128,000 persons in in need of humanitarian assistance,50% being women and 55% being children and 14,200 persons are in the catastrophic level (HNO, February 2022, pg. 45-46)Akobo County has a population of 226,500, the total number of people in need are 215,200, 51% of the population being women, 50% being children with 22,700 persons being in the catastrophic severity phase (HNO, February 2022, pg. 45-46).
CRADA will work with communities in the most marginalized areas to ensure that we meet the two cluster objectives stated above. In Akobo we will work in Walgak, Buong Diror, and Barmach. In Pibor we will work in Pibor, Verteth, Lekuangole, and Gumuruk. In Nyirol We will work in Tut, Pultruk, Thol, Nyambor, Pading, and Chuil.

1 - Needs

An estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May – July). This represents a 7 per cent increase from the 7.7 million people in need in 2021. The main drivers are: (1) covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks (such as floods and dry spells, inflation and rising food prices, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or fear of violence); and (2) household-level idiosyncratic stressors (such as death of breadwinner, gender-based violence, morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene, and lack of access to essential basic services) which are greatly amplified by the continuous effect of shocks (HNO, February 2022, pg.73). Food insecurity is greatest when these shocks and stressors are compounded by the persistent high levels of vulnerability associated with the protracted crisis that has existed since December 2013.Akobo County Akobo County is in IPC 4 with over 200,000 people in need humanitarian assistance. According to HNO, February 2022, pg. 62, Akobo County severity of needs is at extreme levels wit with women and children being most affected 30% of the entire population are in severe need of food security assistance. Akobo county is greatly affected by conflict both internal intercommunal clashes and between the various sub clans and extern between the communities in Pibor, Nasir and Pirguol in the boarder between South Sudan and Ethiopia. The conflict has claimed the lives of bread winners leaving behind women and children headed households, in December 2021, UNMISS approximated that more than 86 people were killed in one month alone in cattle raiding clashes in Akobo. HNO February 2022 pg. 65 puts Akobo County gender-based violence severity at Severe level. These has greatly contributed to low food security in the region.GPAAPibor in IPC 5 has faced serious challenges in Food Security, according to the HNO, February 2022, pg. 62, the county severity needs with respect to to FSL is in the catastrophic level and with over 200,000 people in need of humanitarian assistance, the situation in the county is worsened by ongoing age set conflict between the Lang’o and Kurenen and external conflict between Lou Nuer and Dinka from Duk and Bor. According to UNMISS, in August of 2021, three counties of GPAA had been captured by raiders from Duk and this led to a wide spread of IDPs who fled to Pibor town for safety. Bread winners were killed in the raids leaving behind vulnerable female spouses and children to take care of huge households of an average of 14 persons. Cases of GBV are still high with the HNO, February 2022, pg. 65 placing the stress levels of cases at a severe level. NyirolThe county has over 100,000 people in need of humanitarian assistance according to HNO, February 2022, pg.62. The severity levels of the county FSL needs are at a catastrophic level, The huge county was affected by flooding in 2021 that cut off access to Chuil and Pultruk Payams. Immediately after the floods, cattle rustlers from Pibor and Ayod attached more than 25 villages (Nyirol county Commissioner) leaving women and children headed house hold heads that are more prone to economic shocks. Cases of GBV are at a severe level and this has impacted negatively on women’s ability to contribute at the household levels to improve food security levels. The above summary shows the extent to which these three counties are in need of FSL humanitarian assistance. CRADA will support the objectives of the FSL cluster by Enhancing and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support and through initiatives that reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19.

2 - Project activities

CRADA's strategy is to ensure that in all our activities, our beneficiaries are protected from all forms of SEA, and inter communal clashes. We will do this by bringing our services as close as possible to our beneficiaries and not the other way round. CRADA will work closely with the UN, local authorities and other IP to get regular security updates so that we plan our activities when its most safe for our beneficiaries. CRADA will ensure that all our section processes are fair and that all vulnerable community members are given a fair chance to be part of our activities by involving grass root leader i.e., chiefs and headmen. CRADA will form AAP committees with 60% female representation in all our project locations and train them on PSEA, child protection and Disaster risk management. 7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries know what they want, they just don’t have the means of getting them. We shall understand the beneficiaries’ needs through stakeholder consultative meetings, focused group discussions, and community dialogues. The organization shall use chiefs & sultans as part of the volunteers program. These people best understand their community and have a say in whatever enters their area. Feedback on project implementation shall be received and analyzed. Communities are diverse and not everything works everywhere. Based on this, the project shall be contextualized and well adapted to the community needs as analyzed from the feedback All feedback got from the community shall be treated with utmost importance and used to inform and adapt the project design so as to meet their needs.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CRADA will conduct a training of the community leaders, youth leaders, women leaders on our zero-tolerance policy on SEA the training will mainly focus on equipping them with the knowledge of how they can identify a victim of sexual abuse and exploitation and the reporting channels they can use to report all forms of SEA. This training will be done using CRADA’s own resources. CRADA will also use its own resources to print PSEA materials with reporting hotline and distribute the print outs, banners and posters to all distribution centers, to local government offices and in market places. CRADA will also raise awareness of PSEA at water points, markets, and cattle camps using public address To add to that CRADA staff STRICTLY abide by the six core principles that seek to protect beneficiaries and CRADA staff too from all forms of SEA. The principles are as follows. 1. Sexual exploitation and abuse by CRADA workers constitute acts of gross misconduct and are therefore grounds for termination of employment. 2. Sexual activity with children (persons under the age of 18) is prohibited regardless of the age of majority or age of consent locally. Mistaken belief regarding the age of a child is not a defense.3. Exchange of money, employment, goods or services for sex including sexual favors or other forms of humiliating, degrading or exploitative behavior is prohibited. This includes exchange of assistance that is due to beneficiaries.4. Sexual relationships between CRADA workers and beneficiaries are strongly discouraged since they are based on inherently unequal power dynamics. Such relationships undermine the credibility and integrity of humanitarian aid worker. 5. Where a CRADA staff develops concerns or suspicions regarding sexual abuse or exploitation by a fellow staff whether in the same agency or not, he or she must report such concerns via established CRADA’s reporting mechanisms. 6. All CRADA’s staff are obliged to create and maintain an environment which prevents sexual exploitation and abuse and promotes the implementation of their code of conduct. Managers at all levels have particular responsibilities to support and develop systems which maintain this environment. As such, information on PSEA shall be provided by the Gender & Protection Officer whose main role is to act as the PSEA Focal Point for the project. The Officer is to ensure that staff are trained on PSEA and the community also gets to know about this. Posters & visual aids (IEC) materials shall be pinned at the sites for use. Through community dialogues and focused group discussions, the community shall be able to discuss issues around PSEA with concrete examples. The Annual 16 Days of Activism shall be used as an opportunity to pass out PSEA messages to the community through role plays, dramas & songs. The community shall be made aware of the PSEA Hotline Number for CRADA +211919067218 and email psea@crada.africa

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

We will hold a meeting with community leaders and various key stakeholders and community members who we will brief them about the projects objectives and get any questions from them as well. We will then form a beneficiary identification community that will have grass root leaders i.e., chiefs and headmen who will identify beneficiaries who have not received any assistance from other NGOs, after the committee gives us the names together with other FSL partners in the locations, we will do verification of the names and then we will proceed to register the beneficiaries. We will have suggestion boxes at all our project meetings and trainings and a hotline number for beneficiaries to provide information on SEA, fraud and corruption. All complaints received from our beneficiaries will be investigated and solutions provided within 48 hours of the report, in the event it involves a CRADA staff, an incident report will be required and proper disciplinary action will be followed. All our staff and suppliers will have to sign and adhere to a strict code of conduct.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

• Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services - CRADA will bring the services very close to the beneficiaries no beneficiary will have to walk more than 20 mins to the distribution points • Risks of women, girls and boys without and with disabilities exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance - CRADA will strictly enforce our code of conduct and PSEA policy for all our staff and suppliers• Risks that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA - All CRADA staff will under mandatory training on child safe guarding and PSEA.• Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.) - CRADA is an active member of the protection cluster at state and national level, hence we will refer any cases to the cluster so that beneficiaries can get referral services.• Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations ordecision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities - CRADA will ensure that 10% of all our beneficiaries are PWDs plus all CRADA staff engaged in this project will undergo mandatory training on disability mainstreaming • Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms - CRADA will provide at all our distribution points and meeting places suggestion boxes and toll free numbers for beneficiaries to all and raise complaints. CRADA will also work with AAP committees to ensure that the community members can raise their grievances to the people they trust. 60% of the AAP committees will be women. • Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries - CRADA will enforce our data protection policy • Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources - Selection of beneficiaries will be done by a panel of community leaders alongside NGOs working in the FSL cluster to ensure that we don't get duplication of services and award same persons over and over again.• Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area - CRADA will follow security protocols provided by UN and the local authorities, in the event of an insecurity in an area, CRADA will suspend activities temporarily until calm resumes.• Risk of explosive hazard contamination in project target area - CRADA will use guidance issued by UN agencies to avert this
