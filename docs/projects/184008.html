Project Summary

Sudan has a young population with the median age being 19 years half of them are below that age , about 47% of population lives on less than 1,25 USD; most of them in Darfur state which they considered as poorest group this is due to longest conflict and due to economic crisis in Sudan and high inflation since 2018 while COVID 19 containment measures prevented the speared of virus impacted in the services in urban areas ,however there other factors affected whether directly or in directly on Education instability in normal life due to protest or lack of qualified teacher specially for grade 7 ,typing new curriculum, COVID 19 and the economic crisis .More than 2.9 M of vulnerable children need life sustaining humanitarian support in education ,there are 1,8 M of children at school experiencing crisis emergency or sever levels of food insecurity these children are at high risk of dropping out of school and they are under severity risk 30% even women and girls continuous to face different types of GBV contributing to factors including low economic, lack of community awareness on women rights due to social norms and stigma.
So Al Massar NGO will ensure to enhance school capacity and school environment through teacher training to ensure qualified teacher are in schools through intensive training on new curriculum specially for grade 7 targeting 100 teachers in 10 school ,awareness camping targeting 10 school raising awareness about how to prevent sexual and harassment and protection issues ,provision of COVID 19 materials to enhance school environment through provision of soap ,sanitizers and hand washing facilities targeting 5 schools .

1 - Needs

The Education Sector has identified 2.9 million conflict and disaster-affected basic education (primary school) and secondary school-aged children (686,928 internally displaced, 244,830 refugee, 187,853 returnee, and 1,803,781 non-displaced vulnerable children) who will require humanitarian assistance to fulfil their right to education, of which 2.2 million children will be supported through the Education Sector with an additional 244,830 refugee children supported by UNHCR and its partners through the Refugee Coordination Forum. Since 2018, an estimated 4.2 million basic education and secondary school-aged children in Sudan have been out of school. Additionally, due to COVID-19 all schools in Sudan closed March 2020 and remained closed for an extended period with school reopening postponed until at least November, affecting approximately 9 million schoolchildren. It is expected that the most vulnerable children, particularly children living in areas affected by conflict, will not be able to return to school or to progress in school unless significant support is provided to mitigate against this long absence from school.
