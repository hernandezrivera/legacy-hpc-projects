Project Summary

The NGO aims to improve access to basic preventive and curative healthcare services for internally displaced and conflict-affected populations in targeted areas in NWS through the provision of life-saving emergency, primary and secondary healthcare services, including sexual and reproductive healthcare, physical rehabilitation and mental health services. Programs will be delivered through support to one maternity and child hospital, seven static primary healthcare clinics, 2 ambulances will also provide transportation for emergency cases and those in need of more specialized medical services. Three physical rehabilitation static centers and 4 rehabilitation mobile units will operate for physical rehabilitation services, integrated with MHPSS and protection services Centers will be supported through the provision of incentives, running costs, equipment, pharmaceuticals, and other needed supplies. Services will target girls, women, boys and men. In addition, program will operate outreach health education activities through 60 community health workers.
Medical: the supported PHCs will provide primary health care services include treatment of common acute and chronic illnesses and injuries, including local endemic diseases; the provision of essential medicines, including contraceptives and treatment for chronic diseases; management of communicable and non-communicable diseases and health education. The PHCs will also provide basic reproductive health services including antenatal care, post-natal care, family planning, and syndromic management of STIs. PHCs will operate a referral system for cases to secondary and tertiary service hospitals. Health activities will maintain and ensure sustaining and expanding essential, quality health services at community through primary and secondary health care to meet the needs of the most vulnerable groups to both IDPs and host communities, in addition the community health care workers will provide health education and referrals for a wide range of services, and provide support and assistance to communities, families and individuals with preventive health measures and gaining access to appropriate curative health and social services, The maternity and new-born care hospitals will be supported in Idleb city it will provide Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and New-born Care (CEmONC) services in addition to outpatient reproductive services including antenatal, postnatal care and family planning are also provided.
Physical rehabilitation: Rehab static centers will provide physical therapy services integrated with psychosocial support and protection services for people with disabilities (PWDs) and war injuries, including treatment plans, follow-up sessions and the provision of mobility and orthoses devices. Caregivers and family members will be provided with training to support the care of PWDs.
Mental health: The NGO will provide an integrated and holistic approach to MH service delivery for those with mental, neurological and substance abuse (MNS) conditions and/or living with acute and chronic psychological distress. This will include specialized and focused individual MHPSS services, including the assessment and psychotropic treatment via a psychiatrist and mhGAP-trained doctors.

4 - Needs

The health sector PiN has increased three per cent with the largest absolute change seen in Idleb Governorate where the PiN increased by more than 200k. Due to cumulative effects of concurrent crises, cost of health services; lack of medicines, supplies or health staff; and fear of exposure to COVID-19 have replaced distance, over-crowding and long wait times as the main self-reported barriers to health access with persons with disabilities disproportionately affected. Disrupted medication supply chains, particularly for psychotropic drugs and Noncommunicable Disease (NCD) medicines were previously identified as a key gap in the health at risk from COVID-19 and the economic situation on account of increased reliance on caregivers, cost of transport, and greater household vulnerability. In Syria, more than one in four individuals (28 percent) aged 2 and above have disabilities, the rate of persons with disabilities inside Syria is 10 percentage average of lower income countries and significantly higher than points higher than the 18 percent the global average of 15 percent. The organization conducted MSNA in Jan 2021 at NW of Syria, the findings indicate the causes of MH are resulting from the war situation in NWS and the stress of their displacement and uncertainty from the protracted conflict which still affecting peoples’ lives and psychological wellbeing in many ways, as well as persons who have mental disorders from before the onset of the conflict, In addition, MSNA indicate that 54.4% people from different communities reported the need for different health services. A total of 89.4% (329) of household respondents face challenges in receiving medicines and 26.4% of the HHs were not able to access basic SRH services.

5 - Implementation of activities

To respond to the needs, the NGO will provide support to 7 static PHCs and one Specialized Maternity and Child Health hospital which will be including: capacity-building through sustained technical support, provision of pharmaceuticals, consumables, and equipment; disbursal of staff incentives and funds to cover facility running costs. The supported PHCs will serve as PHC-Unit and centre, the NGO will ensure high quality of services provided to the beneficiaries by providing full package of primary health care services in accordance with WHO PHC standards/levels and will maintain integration with nutrition, MHPSS and GBV, in addition, Al Salam MCH will provide (CEmONC) services. Furthermore, all health facilities will be involved in referral system led by WHO to enhance the coordination among the actors as well as the coordination between deferent level of health services, the organization will implement outreach health activities through 60 community health workers across the program sites. As COVID-19 is still expected to be a threat, the organization will continue incorporating strict precaution measures in each health facilities. The medical activities are monitored by experienced Field Medical Officers (FMOs) who conduct regular supervisory field visits to the supported health facilities as well as Rehab centers on weekly basis using consolidated relevant checklists. Physical Rehabilitation: the INGO will support 3 static rehab centers and 4 mobile units in terms of running cost, equipment, assistive devices, incentives and training of the staff. MH Case Management will be provided through two integrated MHPSS clinics, one in Atmeh & the another in Idleb in integration with the PHCs at the health sector.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk: The population, including survivors, do not report allegations of SEA which results in the organization being unable to refer the allegation to investigation, hold personnel to account and assist survivors.Greater difficulties in accessing health services, as well as increased burdens and separation from caregivers (due to quarantines, or severe illness/death), may lead to SEA against children, in particular girls, including child/forced marriage Mitigation:• We have prepared a safeguarding action plan to promote and make sure Safeguarding measures are in place.• Safeguarding policy is being distributed for all staff to read and sign acknowledgement letters for.• We deliver MAST training which includes SEA training for new hires and as an annual refresher for all staff.• We have Safeguarding focal points in the fields for the staff to revert to in case of any concern, question, complaint.• We have Safeguarding Posters placed in all of our facilities along with the several complaining channels that staff or beneficiaries can use.• Protection team delivered training sessions for all staff for the Safeguarding and the referral pathways.• Protection team does a Safeguarding risk assessment for all facilities• We have the CBFRM in place along with the community awareness done in this regard.• Safeguarding measures are implemented in the Recruitment processes to make sure suitable candidates are brought to hired within our organization.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:• Security situation remains stable• NGO maintains access to targeted locations• Non-interference by groups in control of program locations• Guaranteed access for persons with disabilities to accessible Physical Rehab Centers, and through home visits• Different vendors have capacity to deliver items to the organization’s warehouse inside Syria• Adequate and competent staff are hired to provide services Risks:• Security situation is volatile where destabilization negatively impacts staff and beneficiaries• Change of control over implementation areas: • Interference and aid diversion by armed groups in implementation areas: • Risk of Aid diversion, corruption, fraud• Delays in procurement and delivery of essential pharmaceuticals and medical consumables and equipment essential for health facility rehabilitation and operations; leading to delay in start-up of services for beneficiaries or cause of stock outs and shortage of medicinesMitigation Measures:• The physical security of NGO facilities to be regularly assessed and upgraded as needed.• Maintain liaison with the UNOCHA access group and the health directorates in areas of implementation • Appropriate mitigation measures within procurement process, and anti-fraud, anti-corruption, code of conduct, humanitarian principles and other relevant trainings provided to all staff.• The NGO applies for appropriate certifications, licenses and permits to import and/or use bonded transportation and warehouses for humanitarian assistance• BIS screening for all staff, consultants, vendors, contractors and sub-contractors.
