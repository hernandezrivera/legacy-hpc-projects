Project Summary

The proposed action will be implemented through existing Government-MOH system to enhance sustainability through health system strengthening (HSS) interventions thus provide quality accessible, acceptable and affordable health services to the target populations; IDPs and host community during humanitarian crisis. The action will aim at enhancing sustainable impact through HSS and therefore deliver quality health services to the target populations. Target locations are Baki and Lughaya districts of Awdal region, Burco and Odweyne districts of Togdheer region both in Somaliland, Garoowe, Burtinle and Eyl districts of Nugaal region in Puntland and Baydhaba and Qansax Dheere districts of Bay region and Dollow and Luuq districts of Gedo region in South Central Somalia. The interventions will seek to improve emergency response during crisis-driven disease and outbreaks e.g. AWD/Cholera and measles through supporting the MoH prepare emergency response plans (ERPs) at regional health authorities level and have rapid response teams (RRTs) with capacity to promptly investigate rumors, confirm outbreaks, notify WHO and MoH, mount an aggressive response, and report promptly. The action will also ensure increased access, utilization and coverage of essential health services by supporting health facilities; making them functional to provide out-patient consultations, immunization services for vaccine-preventable diseases, maternal, new-born and child health services through; capacity building to the health care workers on clinical management of rape, BEmONC and CEmONC, EPI and cold-chain management, integrated disease surveillance and response (IDSR) and mass casualty management in order to improve their capacity to deliver quality health services; provision of essential medicines including vaccines thus avoiding stock outs and ensure availability and accessibility of supplies; strengthening emergency obstetric and new-born care through adequate staffing to provide BEmONC services in most Health Centers and CEmONC services at selected referral Health facilities, both services provided 24/7, improved case management and referral of sexual & gender-based violence survivors and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS/PFA); scale up of immunization services both routine and campaigns during disease outbreaks. Through this action also, mitigation of impact and response to shocks to the affected populations as a result of conflict and public health emergencies will be enhanced. Referral health facilities will be selected strategically and equipped to enable management of mass casualties during catastrophe and staff trained on mass casualty management. It is envisaged that through this interventions, target populations will have improved access to quality healthcare services during humanitarian crisis and therefore reduce excess mortality and morbidity.
The proposed action will reach 73,800 direct beneficiaries comprising of the following:
1. Out patient consultations due to disease outbreaks; AWD/Cholra, measles... and out patient consultations of SGBV survivors
2. Children under 5 years vaccinated against measles and Penta 3
3. Health care workers trained on the following:
- AWD/Cholera and measles rapid response
- Clinical management of rape (CMR) survivors
- Mental Health Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) PFA
- Nurses and midwives and clinicians trained on BEmONC and CEmONC in order to provide RH services 24/7
- Community Health resourse persons trained on community mobilisation and referral of cases to the Primary Health Facilities or treatment centres e.g. CTCs and CTUs
4. Health workers and CHWs supported with incentives to provide essential health services during emergency response

Mitigation Measures

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measuresPreventing risks of exclusion and marginalizationWomen, men, boys and girls, persons with disabilities, and marginalized communities are impacted differently by the crisis and displacement, having distinct humanitarian needs and facing specific protection concerns. Therefore, integration of age, gender and diversity principles in service delivery and assistance will be promoted. Protection risks in IDP sites include widespread GBV, child recruitment, physical attacks, forced early marriages, forced evictions, limited access to humanitarian assistance due to insecurity and/or discriminatory practices. The project will strengthen inclusion by instituting an accountability mechanism for effective protection of affected populations’ i.e. robust complaints and feedback mechanism for instance a toll free telephone line. Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age Gender segregated data will used throughout the project. The project will ensure that the health facilities are accessible and appropriate for all in the communities. This will take gender specific protection needs into account. This will include but not limited to making of availability of rooms for privacy, gender-segregated latrines and handwashing facilities in the health facility. The latrines will be equipped with locks. Hygiene kits will also be distributed to women and girls to address their menstruation need. While gender disparity in Somalia often exclude women from accessing information and making decision, the project will not only ensure health and hygiene promotion will include both men and women in the event, it will also intentionally include both male and female in the various health committee so that they can have equality opportunity to take part in decision making on health issues in the community. Mitigating the impact of external stakeholder interferance in beneficiary selection Using ‘Do no harm principles’, WV will seek to avoid physical or psychosocial risks that would be detrimental to project beneficiaries, and will serve all people irrespective of age, sex or clan. Given vulnerabilities associated with issues related to clan dynamics, WV has done a thorough analysis of the context and has conducted the Do-No-Harm Assessments for target districts. Competing over access to basic commodities and services are often one of the major causes of community conflict. To avoid provoking fraction among communities, the project will closely engage community throughout the project cycle, ensuring they are involved in site and community health committee selection, fully informed of project details, and making sure accountability system is in place to allow them voice their concerns and complaint. WV, throughout the project cycle, will ensure the safety of beneficiaries and staff during program. This will ensure timely provision of services to prevent late travels by the staff and beneficiaries which might expose them to security risks. Throughout the project duration, world vision will monitor the security situation and also collaborate with the local administration and the community to ensure that the beneficiaries and the staff are well aware. WV has the security team that closely monitors the security situation within the target areas, hence able to advice and prevent staff and also beneficiaries from being affected.
