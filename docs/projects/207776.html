Project Summary

In the Logone et Chari, Mayo-Sava and Mayo-Tsanaga divisions, 62 schools are still closed, and 50 others have been destroyed affecting over 35,000 students. Among the key factors impeding access to school are limited and poorly maintained infrastructures (including water and sanitation), shortage of qualified teachers as many have been displaced by the security situation, shortage of learning materials and unaffordable school fees. The number of girls and children with disabilities that are out of school in those divisions shows that the situation is disproportionally affecting them.
To respond to those needs, NRC and Plan International will scale up their ongoing education programme, in four subdivisions (Makary, logone Birni Kolofata and Mora) in the two most affected divisions (Logone et Chari, Mayo Sava) of the Far North region. The programme aims to meet the immediate needs of out-of-school children to access basic quality education, while building the capacity of duty-bearers and the communities to respond to the needs of both out-of-school children and those in formal school in the longer term. In coordination with MINEDUB, the programme will provide alternative education activities aligned with the ten key principles developed by the Global Accelerated Education Working Group (AEWG) as well as the recommendations included in the framework document for the implementation of the AEP in Cameroon, as defined by the MINEDUB. The action will mainly support out-of-school children’s return to learning through implementing accelerated education and catch-up classes, following up with support to ensure successful integration into the formal school systems.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

MINEDUB/DDEB reported that over 19,000 children have been impacted by school closures in Logone Birni and Kousseri sub-divisions 10, 2022. According to the Delegate of Basic Education for Logone et Chari Division, when classes resumed for the second semester of the 2022/2023 school year, on September 10, 2022, more than half of students in class were at school without school materials because they had lost them during the multiple crises. The parents who have the will to send their children to school have to overcome lack of school materials and clothing.About 20,000 school-aged children and youth are now in desperate need of educational support, either in danger of, or already missing out on their education. Communities highlighted the importance of education during these times of crises, yet education appeals receive less than 2% of humanitarian funding. have identified the following obstacles in accessing education:a) Cultural traditions hindering girls' enrolment in education;b) The cycle of poverty means families are unable to pay school kits and fees for their children;c) Early and forced marriages for girls;d) Early or unwanted pregnancies;e) Lack of a birth certificate means children are unable to sit exams and to continue their education;f) Insecurity in the region resulting in the displacement of families and in the increase of early/forced marriage for girls;g) Low number of qualified teaches available, with many teaching personnel fleeing the region due to insecurity – current teacher:pupil ratio is about 211 students per teacher in Mayo-Tsanaga, 191 in Logone et Chari and 121 in Mayo-Sava;h) Poor availability of classrooms, with nearly 80% of schools surveyed having an insufficient number of classrooms for enrolledstudents,i) Reinsertion of unschooled children into the formal system for those who are still of age; jk) Lack of teaching materials for teachers;The project seeks to promote safe access to quality education for children affected by conflict and displacement in targeted communities of the Far North region, Littoral and West. To achieve this, NRC and Plan will implement a range of activities in in line with the Humanitarian Response Plan and Education priorities• Provision of comprehensive community sensitisation on the benefits of AEP• Establishment of two Temporary Learning Space (TLS) for AEP centers • Provision of teacher professional development courses on subject knowledge and Accelerated Learning pedagogy • Identification and enrolment of target students for either AEP/CARED• Provision and monitoring of AEP classes to selected learners• Organisation of catch-up classes (CARED/remedial classes) to support 4000 children out of school for shorter periods and their reintegration into the formal system• Support the MINEDUB in the implementation of the National Accelerated Education Framework• Development of an Accelerated Education curriculum for anglophone sub system
