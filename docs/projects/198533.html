Project Summary

Young Africa (YA) Mozambique aims to implement a 12 month-project titled 'Empowered 2 Take Charge' targeting four districts of Cabo Delgado: Chiure, Montepuez. Metuge and Ancuabe. This initiative seeks to reduce and mitigate violence against Adolescent Girls and Young Women (AGYW) through providing economic empowerment-focused livelihood interventions. YA will reach a minimum of 1,000 AGYW between the ages of 15 and 24 across the four target districts. YA will deliver interventions through mobile Technical Vocational Education and Training (TVET). YA's 'integral' approach to vocational training incorporates holistic wellness (physical and mental health; sexual and reproductive health and rights - SRHR), entrepreneurship support and employment readiness, and life skills education. This initiative will also provide additional targeted economic inclusion services such as financial literacy, savings support, business development and start-up kits. Through the delivery of gender and adolescent-sensitive interventions aimed at enhancing AGYW agency and decision-making, as well as participation in employment and entrepreneurship readiness initiatives, YA will achieve the following results:
Result 1: Empowered and resilient AGYW demonstrate improved livelihoods, entrepreneurship skills, and employability readiness.
Result 2: Increased access to, awareness and use of comprehensive, adolescent-friendly and gender-sensitive GBV and SRHR services.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Our initiative will promote sustainable livelihoods, reduce vulnerability to GBV, and enhance the resilience of 1,000 AGYW aged 15 to 24 in four districts of Cabo Delgado through the provision of tailored economic empowerment and GBV awareness-building and mitigation interventions. Informed by a robust needs assessment, the services target youth will receive include mobile vocational training integrated with contextually-relevant life-skills education. Our goal is to equip the hardest-to-reach AGYW interested with the skills required for gainful and dignified employment. We envisage that a minimum of 700 AGYW will be trained via mobile TVET, which is 70% practical and 30% theoretical. TVET graduates will receive additional support training on savings and lending, and a minimum of 100 savings groups constituting 5-7 AGYW will be formed. Successful groups will be supported with start-up kits, including financial and business literacy training. Furthermore, 300 AGYW with existing small businesses will receive training on enhanced business management. This is designed to support business expansion and scale-up efforts while simultaneously building their financial literacy confidence and decision-making capacity. Within the target training sites, temporary wellness centers will be established and manage by qualified nurses with the support of peer educators. Wellness centers will provide services such as drop-in counseling sessions, designated safe spaces for youth groups, PSS for GBV survivors, and SRHR services. For AGYW who require specialized services, referrals will be made via the appropriate stakeholders and follow-up support will be provided until cases are adequately addressed. Within the wellness centres, a 24 hour hotline will be availed.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

YA is committed to providing appropriate and adequate learning opportunities to ensure safeguarding commitments are met. A key step in reducing risks to adolescents when delivering programmes is to increase awareness of risks and how to manage them. YA’s centre-based and mobile vocational training staff have a dedicated focal person responsible for ensuring adherence to our Safeguarding Policy. With recent refresher training delivered by REPSSI in early 2022- a YA partner with protection and safeguarding expertise - our vocational training staff are well equipped to identify, respond to and provide referral support for PSS and GBV services. Our policy applies to employees of all levels, management board and board of trustees, volunteers, consultants or anyone working on YA’s behalf. The focal person is the first point of contact for YA beneficiaries and staff, and ensuring that employees are trained to identify act on suspicions of violations, including confidentially receiving evidence of abuse, keeping the management informed of all violations - unless the management is subject of a complaint, and managing records and incident forms in accordance with Mozambican data protection laws. YA’s anonymous hotline will be accessible for staff and beneficiaries to report suspected corruption and all forms of violence. Existing SRHR, GBV prevention and economic inclusion programming in Cabo Delgado informs the project design. We will leverage our lessons learned and evaluation recommendations, as well as the results from our needs assessment, to inform the delivery of age-appropriate vocational training for economic empowerment and GBV prevention and awareness-raising activities. We will use YA-developed beneficiary screening tools to identify vulnerabilities specific to youth across our target age range of 15-24. We will use YA-developed beneficiary screening tools to identify vulnerabilities specific to youth across our target age range of 15-24. We will contract specialists trained on the appropriate collection of GBV, SRHR and disability-related data to identify the hardest-to-reach women and girls most likely to benefit from mobile vocational training for economic empowerment. YA’s staff include those with expertise in engaging youth with disabilities and can develop training materials that are responsive to their unique needs (i.e. braille resources and typewriters; sign-language speakers). We will target districts within Cabo Delgado with limited to no support for empowerment interventions for employment and entrepreneurship and where the prevalence of GBV and economic exclusion of women and girls is highest. YA will support beneficiaries to share feedback throughout implementation through our confidential hotline.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Young Africa will deliver age-appropriate interventions that prepare adolescent girls and women for success in life and livelihood. We target young mothers, nursing mothers, survivors of GBV, and women and girls involved in selling sex, prioritising internally displaced persons and youth living with disabilities. Working with youth and through community structures and leadership, we will identify their diverse needs to deliver contextually-relevant support. Through a robust needs assessment, we will determine how harmful practices undermine wellness and personal agency for youth of all ages. YA will use youth insights to inform needs for in-demand trades, understand the prevalence of GBV, and awareness of GBV and SRHR support services. Our training will focus on appreciating diverse identities, mobility, and individual autonomy to ensure a 'Do No Harm' approach and ensures we engage young people with a leave no one behind mindset. YA’s vocational training incorporates life skills and comprehensive SRHR education - considering youth's evolving needs and capacities as they age. Our other interventions - financial literacy and savings - are relevant for all ages; however, microfinance is more appropriate for older youth who are more likely to have the mobility, resources, and social support needed to benefit from these interventions.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

A provision of 3-5% of target beneficiaries will be for adolescent girls and young women living with disabilities. The project will engage local community-based organizations working with youth living with disabilities to refer beneficiaries who can participate in YA's proposed interventions. Guided by the expertise of local organizations working in the disability-development space in Cabo Delgado, YA will employ a more tailored approach to adequately and appropriately engage target beneficiaries living with disabilities on skills training and GBV prevention and awareness-building initiatives. The project will not take in those beneficiaries with severe disabilities. To ensure that beneficiaries are meaningfully and engaged in the project, trainers, project staff, and other stakeholders involved in the project will receive capacity training on disability-inclusive education. Young Africa anticipated that with the training, all stakeholders would be more sensitive to the needs of AGYW living with disabilities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

TVET plays an important role in helping the world make transitions to a low-carbon economy and climate-resilient society. YA recognises that it has an important role in promoting efforts to transition communities and young people towards greener and sustainable practices. In order to achieve this goal, the project will equip project beneficiaries with green skills and thus preparing them for emerging green jobs as well providing them with green competencies that will enable them to adapt to changing work processes and profiles. As part of the theory lessons, lessons or modules on sustainable development will be included and beneficiaries will be encouraged to be “green ambassadors” in their communities. Since the training programme will work with local businesses,an assessment will be undertaken at these workplaces to establish the environmental risk that these businesses may potentially have and recommendations will be offered. The project will monitor implementation progress towards the recommendations offered.
