Project Summary

December 2013 and signing of 2015 revitalization of peace agreement that saw the return of main opposition armed group SPLA-IO return to Juba was short lived as tensions continued to build among the two parties hence July 2016 conflict erupted which remain impeccable in the minds of the people of South Sudan and diverse communities that are operational in various fields such as business, humanitarian and government as they bring services closure to the people in difficulties of the distracted infrastructural systems as a result of continuous conflict. The conflict that broke between the ruling government SPLA and the incumbent SPLA-IO from Juba and quickly spread to the neighbouring states of; Western Bahr el Gazal, Upper Nile Jongle, Eastern Equatoria, Western Equatorial and other parts of Central Equatorial such as Yei, Morobo, Kajokeji of course not forgetting Juba which were battle grounds for quite a long period of time hence suffered profusely with impacts felt immensely on the community.
Gender Based Violence (GBV) is a term that has traversed South Sudan and the globe at large of which Central Equatorial-Yei, Morobo and Kakokeji have not been exceptional. It is not an error that GBV is ranked among one of the main protection threats that sabotages our daily progress limiting the full participation of human potential. SPEDP has over the years worked and still continues to work with the most vulnerable and GBV survivors who are the extreme poor according to the global definition of poverty. Unfortunately, the different forms of GBV which are encountered on their daily lives magnify the at-risk groups’ vulnerability. Case in point; internally displaced persons (IDPs) in Yei, Morobo and Kojokeji during Focus Group Discussion (FGD) were able to highlight key protection risks that continues to manifest in the camps as well as in integrated communities of which different forms of GBV: Sexual abuse; early/child marriages, forced marriages, rape, aggravated defilement; economic violence, psychological and physical violence with minimal structures in place to prevent, protect, mitigate and respond to GBV cases which SPEDP is looking forward to with support.
Gender based discrimination also inhibits women’s access to education and health services. Sexual and gender based violence remains a serious concern especially in areas of Yei, Morobo and Kajokeji- Central Equatorial in South Sudan which have been hit so hard by the Conflict, poverty and traditional cultural norms. The importance of improving women and girls’ access to basic services, economic opportunities, and human rights in South Sudan is an urgent priority for the new country especially through WGFS which creates a platform for the marginalized and affected to express themselves hence aiding recovery. Women are more likely to die in childbirth than in almost any other country in the world (2,054 deaths per 100,000 births). Only 10% of deliveries are attended by a skilled health professional, this compares to 46% across Sub-Saharan Africa. Teenage pregnancy is common with 40.7% of girls married before their 18th birthday. There are over 1 million children out-of-school of which 60% are girls. At primary level girls’ enrolment lags behind compared to their boys counter part by 14% according to Context Analysis and Programme Options in Central Equatorial which is a war torn location compromising of Yei, Kojokeji and Morobo in South Sudan conducted by SPEDP in April 2018. Yei for instance remains accessible 5-10KMs aware from the town and most women and girls experience abuses during the time of seeking alternative livelihoods, fetching water and collecting firewood from the nearby sources. The fairly safe times for travel remain limited yet the traditional women’s activity clock stands at an average of 16-20hours daily.

Needs

SPEDP has been implementing key protection projects surrounding community mobilization, empowerment of IDP communities and creation of peaceful environment towards coexistence between IDPs, returnees and host communities in Six former greater states of South Sudan. Central Equatoria is one of the key former States of SPEDP interventions. It has been extensively providing humanitarian assistance in Yei, Kajokeji and Morobo Counties. These Counties are known as the original base for SPEDP since 2011. However, despite the fact that the project has registered numerous successes since then, there are still existing gaps SPEDP wishes to fill as the project locations continue to receive more IDPs due to the inter-intra communal conflicts within the state and neighboring states of South Sudan. Natural disasters such as floods that continue to displace persons year in-out which subjects the beneficiaries at desperate situations of protection needs/threats and concerns. Similarly, negative cultural practices enrooted in the society that intensify protection risks. Gender based violence (GBV) remains a major concern and protection threat within the communities of Kajo-keji, Morobo and Yei Counties. This stems from; early/forced marriage, rape, defilement and physical assault. SPEDP has been implementing projects in the areas of food security and livelihoods (FSL), water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), health, education funded by DCA, DFID, UN Women, Christian Aid and UN-FAO. However, there is an urgent need to respond to the critical needs of survivors of GBV to access legal, psycho-social support and healthcare services based on their informed consent. SPEDP has is continually advocating for a GBV free society through awareness raising and prevention activities. SPEDP’s project coverage remains minimal due to financial constraint which limits delivery of services to specific project locations/beneficiaries thereby living other locations without critical life-saving services. As a result of the ongoing GBV awareness raising initiatives, SPEDP has noted the need for increased awareness among community members on GBV and need of services for survivors of GBV. Given that Yei, Kajokeji and Morobo have been directly affected by both 2013 and 2016 conflicts and other emerging armed groups which turn the locations as battle fields as the rivalry parties subsequently used GBV (rape) as a weapon of war for their selfish interest to inflict pain and suffering on communities. The need and demand for services in the communities has become intense as SPEDP remains the only partner on ground with active projects. SPEDP is currently providing minimal prevention services at the local level. The need to sustain essential GBV response services in Yei, Kajokeji and Morobo remains critical since most partners only provided emergency response due to fear and lack of security. SPEDP has the geographical advantage to respond to GBV needs and gaps in the above locations as its grassroots presence, demonstrated experience and technical expertise of staff. SPEDP seeks funding from Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) to ensure that critical GBV services are available for survivors of GBV and vulnerable women and girls. Through the proposed partnership, SPEDP intends to enhance GBV prevention, response and coordination services in Yei, Morobo and Kajokeji Counties of Central Equatorial State as with the signing and celebration of 31/Oct /2018 peace agreement which is likely to provide a conducive environment for both returnees with increased needs and paving way for implementation.

Activities

SO1: Provide immediate lifesaving assistance to address priority protection needs for the most vulnerable populations including those in hard-to-reach areas Act. 1.1: Strengthen and Establish GBV Referral Pathway in order to enhance access to essential GBV services and protection mechanisms in Yei, Morobo and Kajokeji. SPEDP will work with other partners to regularly review and update the GBV referral pathway with a functional hotline, which will be shared among beneficiaries and partners for incidents reporting, case fallow-up and referral. Act. 1.2: Train 60 SPEDP staff, Community Health Officers (CHOs), Community Health Workers (CHW) and community volunteers on GBV case management, psychosocial support (PSS) and psychological first aid (PFA): SPEDP will train project staff (GBV Officers/Case Workers) on GBV case management to provide individualized psychosocial support for survivors of GBV. Community volunteers including women leaders, women groups and social workers of other agencies will be selected and trained on PFA, which will enable them to identify GBV survivors and refer them for appropriate case management. The trained community volunteers will serve as referral entry points for survivors in their communities. SPEDP will ensure that training for staff and community volunteers on case management, PSS and PFA is conducted by external consultant with the appropriate competencies using the IRC/UNICEF case management/PSS/PFA resource materials. Act. 1.3: Provide individualized case management/Psychosocial support for survivors of GBV and vulnerable populations: Through trained staff (social workers/case workers), SPEDP will provide comprehensive compassionate case management and psychosocial services for survivors of GBV or referred to SPEDP for secondary GBV services. Based on survivors’ needs and their consents, SPEDP staff will also facilitate referrals for other services including healthcare, safety and legal to appropriate institutions. Act. 1.4: Train 60 healthcare providers on clinical management of rape (CMR): In collaboration with GBV sub-cluster, UNFPA and IRC, SPEDP will facilitate induction or refresher training on CMR for 60 healthcare providers including clinical officers, nurses, physician assistants and health officers. Following the CMR training, SPEDP staff will carry out monthly visits to key health facilities providing CMR services to assess their institutional capacity. SPEDP will liaise with GBV sub-cluster and UNFPA to ensure that CMR supplies and drugs are available to support survivors of GBV. CMR training will be conducted by external consultant with an appropriate expertise and competencies in CMR. SO2: Prevent, mitigate and respond to protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience Act. 2.1: Raise awareness on GBV and services available for survivors and vulnerable populations: SPEDP will use different communication means including radio talk show and group dialogues to reach community members in Yei, Morobo and Kojokeji with key GBV messages. SPEDP will also use different information, education and communication (IEC) materials including posters and t-shirts with different social messages to raise awareness on GBV and services and women’s rights. Act. 2.2: Train 60 key community leaders and service providers on GBV, GBV services and referral options: SPEDP will mobilize and train key community leaders and service providers including protection committee members, model men, police, social workers and religious leaders on basic GBV concepts, GBV consequences, available services and referral options. The proposed training will increase awareness among key community leaders and other service providers. Act. 2.3: Establish and strengthen Women and Girls Friendly Spaces in Morobo where women can have access to resources and information ensuring confidentiality.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Project information will be shared with beneficiaries during the baseline survey and inception meeting with stakeholders. Review meetings are part and partial of the project to enable beneficiaries understand and own the project, avoid over expectations and improve project performance. Beneficiaries have been involved at the onset of the project during needs analysis and data collection processes through community forums, focus group discussions (FGDs), open campaigns, household visits of survivors. SPEDP will continue to provide information in all aspects of project including inception meetings, review meetings and during joint monitoring and evaluation missions.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project targets Central Equatoria State in the Counties of Yei, Morobo and Kajokeji, which have literally been conflict affected zones. The other factors such as cultural, gender norms, values and beliefs will be taken into consideration while developing messages that target adults, women, youths and children. The project targets both sexes (male and female) with gender and age disaggregation. The project will integrate Age Gender Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM) tool into programming and implementation to promote quality outcomes.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Inline with the Secretary General’s bulleting of 57/306 on Secretary General’s bulleting of 57/306 15 April 2003 in 15 April 2003, SPEDP is committed to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), which has been incorporated into the SPEDP code of conduct. The project ensures both Programme, Participation, Protection, and Policy (P4) and PSEA policy are mandatory to all project staffs through provisioning trainings and orientations.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

SPEDP extensively carried out community awareness campaigns and conducted a series of meetings with relevant national, state and local governments and other implementing partners from different thematic areas before engaging with the communities. SPEDP applied FGDs, Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) and document reviews to ascertain the magnitude of needs in the target locations of Yei, Moroba and Kajokeji Counties.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

A complaint and feedback mechanism will be established at field in order to register complaints from community and give immediate feedback to the complainers if it is a case that can be handled at field level but if not, the case will be referred to Juba. The project will utilize Community Based Response Mechanism (CBRM) as a community management mechanism that delivers collaborative livelihoods and income generating opportunities as a means of building peace and stability. The CBRM will address issues that may arise in a credible manner. CBRM will also facilitate community-based dispute resolution process to receive feedback, deal with complaints or disputes. It is expected to receive and proactively respond to issues with the potential to impact community relationships.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

SPEDP will collaborate with M&E and other SPEDP programme departments and project beneficiaries to conduct periodic monitoring of project activities. SPEDP will utilize the Community Based Participatory Approach (CBPA) right from the onset of the project to include local communities in all processes of project formulation, implementation and monitoring and evaluation. This will foster community ownership, project reliability and sustainability where the beneficiaries and community engagement is part and partial of implementation through, baseline and end line surveys, Initial Rapid Needs Assessment (IRNA), review meetings, trainings, and project monitoring and evaluation.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project design and implementation is conflict sensitive in nature. It will not inflict more pain and harm to the most vulnerable and the at-risk vulnerable groups. IDPs, returnees, host communities and poor rural households in both conflict and negative cultural dynamics are vulnerable to both economic and social shocks which hamper their participation in project activities. Well-designed social protection programme can help to reduce both the risk and vulnerability by building resilience to shocks and stresses. Although the gender-specific challenges of women’s largely unequal involvement in growth and development due to cultural and protection threats that incriminates their full participation in activities are generally well-articulated. The social protection policy and programming have not adequately recognized the gendered experiences of poverty and vulnerability and the extent to which gender inequality affects both social protection programme design and outcomes.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

To maximize the linkages between social protection and livelihood growth, and to improve the effectiveness of both poverty reduction and protection, it is imperative that gender-sensitive measures are integrated into policy and programme design and implementation. The project will use IRNA analysis that revealed a number of important lessons which can be used to inform policy dialogues on protection initiatives in Central Equatoria context of South Sudan.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

It is believed that monitoring is the continuous assessment of the intervention and its environment with regard to the planned objectives, results, activities and means. SPEDP has robust monitoring system that has been developed to capture project impacts, create a cycle of lesson learning and accountability and assess security risks to the implementers and beneficiaries. The project develops and adopts a monitoring, evaluation and reporting (MER) framework to assess the project performance. At the start of the project a clear MER workshop will be organized involving SPEDP national and field staff. SPEDP Centre will conduct periodic monitoring visits to target communities; develop and submit narrative and financial reporting; and ensure procurement, monitoring and stock taking of commodities. Direct beneficiary reach will be monitored by Field Staffs during trainings, workshops and community meetings. Monitoring will be done through case studies or photo stories, process and progress monitoring during monitoring visits. The project team will track lessons learned and incorporate them into the project activities. The project will involve the beneficiaries monitor the project activities when they participate in its implementation.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

SPEDP has a remote management transfer plan. SPEDP has a potential advantage of being a NNGO with its operational base starting from Morobo County extending to Yei County and other parts of the Country. SPEDP through its security focal person will constantly monitor the security threats and developments in the Country and specifically operational areas to determine safety of beneficiaries and staff. The organization also has protection policy, PSEA policy and P4-Programme, participation, Protection, Policy which all staffs sign and adhere to.

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project will foster beneficiary accountability. It will promote the dignity of those affected by enabling them to influence decision-making and play a more active role in their own recovery. Project staff will ensure full respects of the dignity of beneficiaries. The project will adopt 5 standard practices: Participation, Transparency, Feedback and Complaints, Monitoring and Evaluation, and Staff Competencies and Attitudes which are all core values of SPEDP that guide programme implementation.
