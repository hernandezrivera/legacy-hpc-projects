Project Summary

Multiple shocks resulting from many years of wars and intercommunal conflicts, coupled with successive years of heavy floods has resulted into constant loss of livelihoods and key household assets including severe impact on building resilience for sustainable food production and thus leading to a gradual increase in food and livelihood insecurity situation in South Sudan.
Child Relief and Support Fund (CRSF), an active women-led national organization has been implementing a resilient food and livelihood security programme in three counties (Kapoeta South, Bor South and Ibba counties) since Jan 2020 to date. CRSF is designing this FSL project to meet the needs of vulnerable populations previously affected by war, inter communal conflicts, and floods, and are in hard to reach counties (Kapoeta South, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North, Bor South, Ibba and Nzara counties) with high food and livelihood insecurity, high morbidity due to preventable diseases, high GAM rates, depleted community assets.
In this proposed project, CRSF has targeted; Kapoeta South, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North (EES), Bor South (Jonglei state), Ibba and Nzara counties (WES) currently in IPC 4, 3 and 2 (IPC, Feb 2022) respectively
The FSL project will effectively contribute to the protection and rehabilitation of livelihoods of the most vulnerable and food insecure population in EES, Jonglei and WES and support them transit from food shocks through recovery and resilience building by enhancing their food production capacities and livelihood support services.
In line with 2023 FSL cluster objectives 2: CO2 Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock and objective 3, CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes, CRSF intends to reach out to the target population with the following activities; 1) Validating vulnerable households for their eligibility under the agreed criteria through FSNMS
2) Emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fishing, vegetable & crop kits; Emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fishing, vegetable & crop to enhance access to food production technologies.
3) Emergency food & livelihood support: fishing kits/ vegetable kits; rapid response kits (fish kit/ cow pea and varieties of fast maturing vegetables
4) Livestock treatment and vaccination; livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers Training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance.
CRSF is targeting to reach 12,050 households (Kapoeta Sout-2,500 HHs, Bor South-3,500 HHs, Kapoeta East-2,300, Kapoeta North 2,100 HHs, Ibba-700 HHs, and Nzara-950 HHs.
CRSF will target vulnerable household in consideration of gender and people living with disabilities while ensuring protection and Accountability to affected population practices. With support from CRSF technical FSL team, a total of 1,000 households will be trained on modern agronomic practices and 100 Community Animal Health Workers (CAWHs) will be recruited and trained for the livestock support. Under CRSF supported farmers groups on seed multiplication project in Ibba County, CRSF will use vouchers to support farmers in other locations during seed fair to access quality seed inputs for sustainable agriculture on food production. CRSF will further ensure that FSL program implementation conforms to the Do No Harm principle and transparency and accountability to the affected population principles is adhered to as a right for every targeted beneficiary. CRSF derives its strength in its physical and operational presence in the three states particularly in Kapoeta South, Bor South and Ibba counties where the current ongoing integrated FSL interventions is being supported funds from Care Plus Foundation International.

1 - Needs

According to the IPC analysis conducted in October 2022, an estimated 8.2 million people, including refugees and Abyei, are likely to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2023 lean season (May – July) (IPC, Octobre 2022). When compared with the FSL sectoral analysis (December 2021) and IPC analysis (March 2022), the food security needs in South Sudan remained large and severe. Jonglei state tops food insecurity with 79% of the population likely to face severe acute food insecurity while Unity (75%), and Warrap (70%) follows with the highest levels of severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) at the peak of the lean season 2023. A host of covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks (floods and dry spells, inflation and rising food prices, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to conflict and insecurity); and household-level idiosyncratic stressors (such as death of breadwinner, gender-based violence, morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene, and lack of access to essential basic services) which are greatly amplified by the compounded effect of shocks. Women and children continue to suffer the greatest proportion of the effects. The recent reductions in funding against increasing humanitarian needs has significantly compelled humanitarian actors to reduce entitlements and prioritize the most vulnerable households. Further, food insecurity continues to persist due to displacements caused by sub national conflicts, climatic shocks which resulted into heavy floods for the last three years, and effects of pests and diseases, slowed recovery from prolonged asset depletion due to the effects of civil wars, poor macro-economic conditions and sub-optimal market functionality (IPC Feb 2022). Further, Livestock significantly contributes to food security in rural areas in South Sudan, however, the lack of veterinary services, livestock disease outbreaks, conflicts and inter-communal cattle raiding and adverse weather conditions, availability of water, pasture and abundance of diseases continue to threaten the lives of millions of livestock (FSNMS round 24, 2022). There is a high need to respond to these emergencies.Cycles of sub national conflicts resulted in loss of life and assets, displacement, disruption and destruction of livelihoods as is the case for WES while cyclic floods continued to submerge human settlements, farmlands and pastureland, disrupted markets access and humanitarian assistance delivery to the flood affected populations.The greater Kapoeta emanates from a pastoralist setting characterized by cattle raiding and inter-communal violence with most vulnerable population currently in IPC Phase 3 and Phase 4. Food insecurity was expected to be severe among households whose food stocks will have run out and they will be relying on markets for food purchases (SS IPC, Oct 2022). Bor South county in Jonglei state emanates from a setting characterized by heavy floods, inter-communa violence, civil conflicts, market disruptions and inadequate food production with most vulnerable populations in IPC phase 3 and 4 while Ibba and Nzara counties emanates from a political conflict setting. Although Ibba and Nzara counties belong to the green belt livelihood zone, lack of organized farmers' groups has increased risk of food insecurity. CRSF intends to respond to the identified gaps by establishing farmers groups, seed multiplication groups, distribute emergency livelihood kits and build resilience of populations at risk through establishing VSLA groups, establish climate sensitive agriculture technology to increase food production and incomes at household level in the proposed project locations in line with 2023 FSL sectoral HRP objectives. CRSF secured integrated FSL and GBV protection funds from Care Plus Foundation International, the project is currently being implemented in Bor South, Kapoeta South and Ibba counties. Thus CRSF has capacity and gained sufficient experience overtime.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CRSF uses a community based approach to implement and reach its targeted beneficiaries. In this project, CRSF will form farmers groups whose composition will largely be illiterate groups of women and adolescent girls in the six counties. These will be trained on best and modern agronomic practices to empower their capacity in food production. Further, these groups of women and girls living with disabilities and other people living with disabilities will be engaged to participate in focus group discussions and consultative meetings to have their views incorporated during the project life.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During implementation of this project, CRSF will ensure that, six project committees are formed and established in the project locations. The project committees will be charged to oversee the implementation of the project strategy in line with indicator achievement, link with community beneficiaries and provide feedback to the FSL project technical staff who will synthesize the information and further provide feedback on the corrective measures undertaken. Further, CRSF will engage the community through holding quarterly focus group discussions in all the project locations where CRSF will be expected to hear from the response beneficiaries about the indicator achievement and consequently appropriate information will be passed to the beneficiaries while complaints will be heard from the beneficiaries. Further, through its technical program team, CRSF will establish complaints and feedback mechanisms tables where the wider community is able to bring and submit complaints at the desk and all this information will be analyzed and feedback provided to the beneficiaries.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

At Organization level, CRSF has a functional policy on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse that govern its programs. CRSF will ensure that FSL project staff are recruited and trained on protection and prevention against sexual exploitation and abuse policies and its implications. CRSF will mobilize beneficiaries and empower them to report any form of PSEA cases with in the community or locations where CRSF will implement this project. Additionally, CRSF will fit suggestion boxes at all distribution/project sites to obtain confidential information with regard to PSEA. All staff for this project will be sanctioned by CRSF's policy on PSEA by signing a copy of the PSEA policy in line with IASC guideline.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will use beneficiaries's feedback to change the project strategies and modalities that suits the interest of the affected population. The information or feedback will support to provide the FSL interventions in a way that fits the interest of beneficiaries. This will be done by the technical team in coordination with the FSL cluster and donor to ensure that beneficiaries are satisfied with the service. CRSF has FSL specialist and a project manager and the entire program team. These will be charged with addressing beneficiaries and community issues that are raised during community engagement meetings and focus group discussions. to input an doffer any support to the solutions made by the technical and program team and thus shall be adapted to suit beneficiaries adoption and use of the interventions.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

During the stakeholders consultative meeting with beneficiaries and local authorities, the project will consider the attendance to have 50% female, 50% youth, and 50% persons with a disability during consultation and decision-making processes. Beneficiaries will be informed about this criterion to ensure voices of female, youth, and persons with disability are heard in relation to project design and implementation. The beneficiaries will be provided with information about program activities before the commencement of the project to involve them in project management, create awareness and sense of ownership, and provide accountability mechanism. CRSF will ensure staff are trained on protection of persons of all age including child protection and GBV risks. CRSF recognizes the community engagement and therefore will ensure participation of community members inactivities at community level hence championing community protection mechanisms.CRSF will train community existing protection structures such as church leaders,school administrations, and local authorities on protection principles including GBVrisk and referral pathway existing in the community. CRSF will work closely with local leaders and authorities to create community awareness on the targeting and selection criteria. CRSF will engage some resident community and returnees’ members during the identification of project beneficiaries in order to build a strong relationship between returnees and resident community members. CRSF will consider all age group and gender in the project implementation. The confidential complain mechanism will be establish and all the issues that may arise will be addressed without discrimination.CRSF will position a complain desk and box in a visible area and place accountability to the affected population officer to receive complains. Complaints received will go through the case management process to generate appropriate solutions and feedback. All decisions will be generated through inclusive participation, harmonized agreements and decentralized problem-solving approach to concerns and needs; while ensuring accountability to the affected population through holding stakeholders’ meetings and focus group discussions with community members. CRSF will conductplanned focus group discussions to engage women, adolescents and people living with disabilities during implementation phase to review the success or failures of strategies, devise mechanisms to change if need arise in order to improve the program performance. CRSF will fit suggestion boxes to receive complaints from the community members at every nutrition site, and formation of mother support groups will empower them and adolescent girls to share the challenges, successes, what is not working well with the project and devise change of strategies to ensure achieve its objectives. CRSF will step up coordination with protection partners and ministry of gender to ensure that GBV is integrated into this project through conducting training of nutrition staff on the GBV related risks to identify, report and refer cases for management. Further, CRSF will ensure that FSL interventions are taken to the beneficiaries in hard to reach locations to prevent them from traveling long distances to access the services which may increase their risk to protection issues like rape and GBV issues. During the implementation, the technical team will coordinate with protection cluster and mine action partners like UN MAG to ensure that suspected explosives and equipment are deterred without affecting the beneficiaries and project staff.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

CRSF will ensure that climate smart agriculture techniques are used and adapted by the beneficiaries. CRSF will do this by recruiting competent agriculture teams such as agronomist who will work along with other FSL team ensure that farmers and beneficiaries employ farming techniques like tree planting and creating mass awareness as early warning information so that affected can adequately prepare to reduce the impact of floods. This way, CRSF anticipates to reduce displacements related to floods by less than 30% by the year 2025.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

CRSF will ensure that FSL staff are trained on GBV, reporting and referral mechanism of GBV cases in Kapoeta South, Bor South, Kapoeta North, Ibba and Nzara counties. Further, CRSF has technical GBV specialist who will mobilize beneficiaries and community members to create mass awareness programmes for the local to ensure that GBV reduction measures are enforced among the affected beneficiaries.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Since the project aims to significantly contribute to the livelihoods recovery of the affected population and building their resilience, farmers groups establishments, formation of seed multiplication groups and capacity building of extension workers will be stepped up to ensure that farmers adopt to sustainable mechanisms of food production and thus a likelihood of reducing the affected households in IPC phase 4 by less than 20%. Further, the project will establish VSLAs which will lead to establishment of small business and thus developing copying mechanisms towards eventual stressors that may impact food and livelihood security situation.
