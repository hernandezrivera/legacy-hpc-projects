Project Summary

War Child Holland has been implementing child protection, psychosocial support, case management and livelihood development projects in Jonglei, Eastern Equatoria, Upper Nile and Unity States targeting communities affected by conflict and violence including IDPs, host communities and people in PoC. Backed with its substantial experience in protection sector, War Child is proposing community base child protection, psychosocial support, case management and cash based programming in Jonglei (Canal), Uper Nile (Fashoda/Aburoch), Eastern Equatoria (Torit) and Unity (Koch) ) with the purpose of prevention, mitigation and response to protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience as per the protection strategic objective (SO). The purpose of interventions is to contribute to strategic objective of protection cluster through interventions designed to prevent and respond to violence, exploitation, abuses and neglect affecting children and promote resilience and recovery from the effects of conflict through psychosocial support and targeted cash based programming. The project envisages to reach a total of 18,200 boys, girls, men and women through interventions presented below. The organization is member of CPSC and relevant TWCs both at national and state levels. Hence, the interventions are aligned with cluster priorities and minimum standards and guidance. Summary of specific interventions planned in the proposed sites included but not limited to:
• Conduct protection risk assessment in collaboration with partners
• Identification and documentation of 550 children with protection concerns in Aburoch and Canal (Upper Nile), Koch (Unity State) and Torit (Eastern Equatoria).
• Support total of 550 Girls and Boys in vulnerable situations through case management and referrals to specialized services
• Identification and placement of 40 released children in alternative or group care
• Family tracing and reunification of 250 UASC
• Identification and placement of 50 UASC children in alternative care
• Provide unconditional cash support for 300 extremely vulnerable HHs caring for children using broader case management vulnerability criteria
• Train 12 CP staff on Child Protection case management
• Provide training and mentoring to 50 child protection volunteers and staff on child protection approaches
• Conduct capacity building training for 20 child protection workers and staff on CPiE
• Conduct capacity building training for 16 non-child protection workers (teachers) on CPiE
• Provide capacity building training for 40 government officers/social workers on CPiE in target 4 Counties
• Facilitate parenting skills session to 800 caregivers and community members based on existing tools in 4 Counties
• Provide structured community-based psychosocial support through 8 CFS for 9,916 boys and girls using both centre and mobile based approach
• Train and support 8 community-based child protection committees to engage in child protection activities in 4 target Counties
• Organize awareness raising sessions and campaigns on prevention and response to child protection issues in 8 communities to reach 18,200 children and adults
• Provide life skills and resilience programming to 600 adolescent girls and boys in 4 target Counties
• Support adolescents to organize peer support activities to further reach 3,600 boys and girls
• Mainstream Child Protection and psychosocial support in 16 schools through capacity building events
• Design and implement community based feedback/complaint mechanism
The aforementioned key activities give an overview of What War Child intends to implement in collaboration with community and government structures and in coordination with other cluster members in the 4 Counties. War Child programming is informed by inter-agency guidelines and standards in addition to internally developed approaches and tools which will contribute for protection and psychosocial well being of vulnerable children affected by conflict and violence.

Needs

Some 6.44 million people in South Sudan were identified as in need of protection response including IDPs, separated and unaccompanied children (UASC), GBV survivors, children associated with armed groups and forces (CAAFAG) and people living in counties frequently affected by violent incidents as per 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HRO). The proposed sites including Koch (Unity), Aburoch and Canal (Upper Nile) and Torit (Eastern Equatoria State) have been significantly affected by the ongoing crisis in the Country and the population experienced adverse effects of the violence and its negative consequences. The Counties host more than 600,000 people in need of protection response including people in PoC, IDPs and host communities. As a result of violence and worsening humanitarian situation, community members in general and children and women in particular has suffered from psychological distress and violation of protection rights. Population displacement and deterioration of traditional support mechanisms for children and other vulnerable community members resulted in lack of adequate care for children and other vulnerable community members who are often dependent on social support. As per HNO 2018, there are more than 19,000 children affected by recruitment, 1 out of 2 women or girls experienced GBV, 16,055 children were separated from their primary caregivers and 1.84 million people displaced across the country. The four proposed Counties are among the priorities of HRP 2019. Specific child protection and psychosocial support issues affecting children in proposed Counties included: - Increased risk of girls and boys to recruitment, sexual violence, school dropout, neglect, child marriage, child labour and mine risks.- Existence of large number of children affected by separation and neglect due to population movement and stretched caring capacity of families. - Psychological distress and self-harm among children and caregivers due to exposure to violence, displacements, targeted threats and livelihood disruption - Inadequacy of child protection and psychosocial support interventions in the proposed locations- Deterioration of traditional support mechanisms for children and other vulnerable community members due to conflict and displacement - Exposure of vulnerable children to risky activities including recruitment, robbery and street life - Significant technical capacity gaps of child protection and welfare focused ministries and community based structures and institutions in planning, coordination, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of child protection and psychosocial support interventions in the proposed locations War Child Holland has presence in Malakal, Aburoch, Bor and Nimule and recently in Koch and has established good working relationship with key stakeholders in the proposed states and locations. Hence, this will help to effectively implement additional protection response within the proposed Counties.

War Child , as a member of protection and CPSC in the locations, will coordinate with protection actors to avoid overlaps through mapping partners and dividing sites for efficient use of limited resources. Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

War Child Holland will organize project launching meeting with community representatives and local authority to discuss about the project design and inform key activities and target groups. In addition, consultation will be held with children and adult groups to communicate available supports and eligibility criteria.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Message development and communication for this project follows participatory approach whereby concerned community members including children and women will be consulted to come up with locally appropriate, gender and age sensitive messages. Messages can be further refined through feedback received from children and community members during implementation phase as well. In addition, War Child will hire community workers including female staff from target locations to ensure alignment of messages with local context and gender considerations. Messages specifically for children will use drawings, drama and art to ensure friendless and effectiveness in communications with them.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

WCH has safeguarding policy that outlines commitment and mechanisms to protect beneficiaries from sexual exploitation and abuse. The policy covers background checks during recruitment, assignment of focal points at field level, providing training/induction to staff, volunteers, visitors and contractors on PSEA and signing of code of conduct, setting up confidential reporting mechanisms at community level and measures including zero tolerance to any sexual violence or exploitation. The organization will strengthen the existing child safeguarding mechanisms to ensure protection of all beneficiaries from SEA by staff and other associates.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Consultation is done in one of four locations with participation of boys, girls and men and women. The discussion mainly focused on identification of protection risks and prevention and response measures. Consultations in the remaining locations and wider groups will be done afterwards. The project design is informed by prior experience in the locations, cluster specific assessments and other secondary sources.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

WCH has planned to design/adapt and use feedback/or complaint mechanism in the proposed project to ensure accountability to affected beneficiary. Key feedback mechanisms planned included use of feedback box, organizing discussion separately with children and adult groups , review meetings, assigning focal persons to receive complaints and designating hotline and email address to receive feedback from community members.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

WCH gives special focus on participatory program approaches that contribute for community leadership and ownership in all phases of its interventions including at monitoring and evaluation stages. Hence, both community representatives and beneficiaries will take part in assessments, review meetings, supportive supervision visits to ensure the concerns and voices of in the project design and adaptations.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The target population for the proposed project is mainly IDPs in camps and communities frequently affected by conflict and violence in recent times which implies the centrality of protection in identification of the locations from the beginning. WCH will do further protection risk assessment at the initial phase of the project to identify protection concerns and affected population groups which intern informs the local level planning and targeting process. Besides, WCH will ensure that tools and methodologies for interventions are designed with the protection and gender lens. For example, beneficiary selection and program activities will include do no harm and other protection frameworks and analyses. Specific attention will be given to extremely vulnerable individuals within the target population including female headed HHs, child headed HHs, children experienced violence or exploitation, women and girls and persons with disability and elderly people etc.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Access barriers exist in the proposed locations including challenges related to security , transport and bureaucratic impediments. WCH will closely work with other partners, local authorities and community representatives to address problems preventing access to project site and groups.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The protracted crisis in the proposed project locations aggravated the pre-existing gender inequalities and protection threats particularly affecting girls and women. Hence, the project is designed to address differential impacts of conflict and displacement on girls and women. The project involves women and girls from the assessment and planing phase to analyze context specific threats , violence, deprivations and neglect to better inform the planning and targeting for different interventions.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring indicators are designed to capture number of people participating in different activities disaggregated by age, gender, vulnerability and social status. Hence, the data measures access through documenting and analyzing the number of community members taking part in project activities including in training, awareness raising sessions, CFS and PSS activities and cash support. WCH will use beneficiary registration template and summary reporting tools.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring measure the safety of beneficiaries through discussions at the initial phase of the project and during implementation by asking a set of questions around safety and security as a in relation to their participation in project activities. Beneficiaries will be given a chance to reflect on safety issues and can propose measures they advice to further improve safety.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

WCH will capture issues related with dignity in its monitoring tools. Project activities and assistance will be done in a manner that respect local values and privacy and following protection principles and procedures for safety. Community members will be consulted to identify appropriate approaches and the agreed procedures will be measured in subsequent phases through discussions and feedback from community members and beneficiaries.
