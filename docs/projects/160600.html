Project Summary

The project aims to reduce the mortality and morbidity rate caused by high incidences of water borne diseases outbreak in the Prone and vulnerable communities in Yirol East, Awerial, Torit and Ikotos Counties through provision of lifesaving safe water provision through water point construction and rehabilitation (hand pumps, SWAT systems, water trucking, or provision of household water filters, depending on the needs); provision or rehabilitation of sanitation infrastructure (latrines, hand washing facilities and solid waste management) ; hygiene promotion (hand washing campaigns in communities and institutions, safe water chain and jerry can cleaning campaigns); capacity-building of community institutions (pump mechanics, water user committees, community hygiene promoters, school hygiene clubs), and distribution of WASH NFIs (soap, hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene kit, water containers, water purification filters, PUR or others).
Safe water supply (new boreholes drilling, repairs and rehabilitation), increased household rural water treatment using Pur/Aquatabs, hygiene promotion sensitization at household and institutional levels, improved safe sanitation practices awareness, and breaking the cholera transmission routes between victims and caretakers/community members by 'contact tracing', disinfection, sensitization, WASH NFIs support and monitoring the gradual progress in hygiene and safe sanitation practices.
The communities where this project will be implemented will be identified under WASH Cluster priorities in consultation with the local authorities and other stake holders on ground on the most vulnerable groups (IDPS, Women headed Household, child headed household, disabled, gender based violence (GBV) survivors and chronically sick) who will be prioritized in the selection process.

1 - Needs

South Sudan humanitarian crisis has continued to intensify and expand due to the compounding effects of the widespread violence and the deteriorating economic situation, the ongoing fighting and surges of violence has forced people to flee their homes, many of them multiple times. Violence and rights violence continue unchecked and have become a persistent reality for civilians as access to basic WASH services has eroded with insecurity and economic decline. Various types of gender based violence (GBV) are pervasive but go largely unreported, this aggression is faced mostly by women and girls in a situation where undertaking daily survival tasks such as collecting water and firewood places them under threat. Particularly vulnerable groups such as children, people with disabilities and elders suffer the most intense consequence of sustained displacement, violence and lack of access to services. Continued economic decline has undermined people’s access to basic resources. 2 - Activities

Activities • 18 pump mechanics given refresher training on hand pump maintenance and repair • Repair and rehabilitate 30 boreholes each with trained water management committee members-WMCs • Organize and conduct participatory hygiene promotion, and conduct training for 70 community Hygiene promoters • Supply and distribution of hand washing facilities in schools, health centers/markets, and other public places • Community mobilization and sensitization on good hygiene practices through a participatory approach through health education in health facilities, churches and markets on good behavior targeting 35,500people • Carry out sanitation education and awareness to enable vulnerable and affected population to practice safe sanitary and hygienic living through secure dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services, construction of 10 sanitation facilities in Nutrition/Health centres, schools, with consideration for gender separated latrines and provision of latrine digging tools to the affected community and training of the community on how to construct the HH latrine using available local materials as an exit strategy. Undertake Mainstreaming cross-cutting issues across all activities with particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to affected population and committing to improve effectiveness of cooperation with communities.Distribution of WASH NFIs, capacity building in terms of preparedness through (training of pump mechanics and provision of borehole spare parts, training of Water Management Committee for resilience) water quality tests, both at water points and community level. RUWASSA shall apply sustainable solutions, such as installing water sources propelled by solar power, where feasible. RUWASSA has its own drilling equipment and team with geologist to conduct geophysics survey before drilling. Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among affected population to prevent and mitigate WASH related diseases through promotion activities, particularly hand washing for displaced and vulnerable communities and institutions. RUWASSA will mostly focus on health workers, teachers, pupils, and women group leaders while conducting household visits, focus group discussions, training for Community Hygiene Promoters,. Post-distribution (PDM), post- intervention (PIM) monitoring will be done across the project done where accessible and secure. PDMs/PIMs gives clear view of whether beneficiaries have profited from the provided assistance and whether they are satisfied with the quality and safety of carried activities. These reports will provide clear lessons learnt and recommendations and will be used to improve overall project quality.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, rights relating to WASH and GBV issues shall be fully incorporated and implemented through this project. Safe drinking water and sanitation is recognized as a human right and its key aspects need to be met as they are a precondition for enjoying other rights like education, health, protection, life and housing. All human rights principles including 'Do No Harm' shall also be adhered to at all times during and after the implementation.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, IDPs and Returnees who are part of the displaced-affected people are targeted through this project and the most vulnerable affected identified population shall be targeted and reached with basic life saving WASH services including Gender protection issues related to WASH.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the project will be linked to government and community structures and mechanisms that communities use to interact, coordinate and deliver responses. RUWASSA will utilize the guidelines in the National Strategic Plan and Humanitarian principles on how to effectively coordinate with gobernemnet or community structures. Government institutions like RRC and County level authorities through which humanitarian services are rendered to the vulnerable communities shall be involved as stakeholders to aid in service delivery and safe lives of the needy populations. In addition, community structures like village chiefs, hand pump mechanics, school hygiene clubs, sanitation committees and others existing that compliment the projects will be utilized to effectively respond to community needs.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project is fully aligned to the National objective of saving lives through basic WASH service delivery to the most vulnerable conflict affected IDPs and Returnees in South Sudan.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the project would ensure the full protection of the environment in order to protect people from unknown environmental risks by ensuring that risk to public health like unsafe drinking water and poor sanitation are minimized. RUWASSA recognizes that diseases like diarrhoea, respiratory infections and noncommunicable diseases caused as a result of a contaminated environment would weigh heavily on household budgets as well as health services. RUWASSA would therefore follow Environmental Safety measures at all times and implement its interventions in a way that ensures environmental health protection through its basic environment services like safe drinking water and sanitation.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes sustainability is incorporated into the project and to enable its success and handover, RUWASSA will ensure sustainability is built in the project design where all stakeholders know from the onset that they would own the project after its closeout. Also the project would utilize local Staffs, communal and technical staff in key parts of the implementation for example workers shall be locally recruited and trained to offer tailored services like community sensitization and awareness in the best possible way using local languages where its needed. These approaches would enable RUWASSA to build trust within the community but also for the community to understand their role in moving the project forward on their own.
