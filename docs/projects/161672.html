Project Summary

The regions surrounding the Lake Chad basin have been embroiled in a decade-long conflict with the three North East Nigerian states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) bearing the brunt of the violence. The conflict has severely disrupted food production systems resulting in high levels of displacement and extensive human and socio-economic losses. According to the Recovery and Peacebuilding Assessment (RPBA) undertaken by the World Bank in 2016, the conflict has resulted in losses of USD 3.7 billion caused by the destruction of farmland, animals and infrastructure.
Acute food insecurity persists at alarming levels. Findings of the October 2019 Cadre Harmonisé analysis highlight that, between October and December 2019, approximately three million people in the BAY states will face acute levels of food insecurity (Phase 3 and above).Moreover, the prevalence of acute malnutrition is high, with GAM rates in Yobe (11.5 percent), Borno (8.1 percent) and Adamawa (7.2 percent) under pressure. As a result, the mortality rate in all three states is equally under pressure.
FAO has had a permanent presence in the BAY states since 2015, implementing lifesaving interventions in the form of emergency food security, nutrition and livelihoods support to IDPs, returnees and host communities affected by the crisis. Despite these ongoing efforts, without sustained and expanded humanitarian assistance the number of people experiencing acute food insecurity is projected to increase by up to 50 percent by the onset of the 2020 lean season (June – August).
The overall objective is to improve food security and nutrition among conflict-affected communities through the restoration, protection and promotion of sustainable and environmentally friendly Agro-Sylvo-Pastoral-based livelihoods. This will be achieved via the distribution of essential inputs/assets and capacity building targeting vulnerable households in both rural and urban / peri-urban communities; the creation of sustainable livelihood opportunities and decent employment in the agriculture, livestock and aquaculture sectors; and the promotion of value addition via the formation of cooperatives, group savings and loans, and market linkages. In addition, Safe Access to Fuel and Energy (SAFE) will be promoted
More specifically, the proposed intervention includes the following activities:
• Support to land preparation including distribution of rainy season and dry season kits (seeds and fertilizer) for crop production as well as essential inputs for backyard vegetable gardening to restore/protect agricultural livelihoods and promote household nutrition.
• Animal restocking targeting women and youth with goat, sheep and poultry packages for income generation and improved nutrition.
• Vaccination campaign to control animal diseases aimed at protecting livestock-based livelihoods; biosecurity/biocontainment training for disease control, food safety and animal welfare to key associations/groups of animal workers.
• Distribution of aquaculture kits (tanks, feed, fingerlings) to unemployed fisherfolk linked with the provision of fish processing assets (smoking kilns) and training to vulnerable women’s groups.
• Distribution of assets (bulls and ploughs) and training to unemployed rural youth to promote entrepreneurship in land development services.
• Establishment/upgrading of fuel-efficient stove production centres and distribution of fuel-efficient stoves to households facing heightened protection and health risks; mitigate poor energy access and adoption of negative coping strategies.
More Specifically the proposed intervention includes the following activities;
• Organization of beneficiaries into local agribusiness cooperatives supported with training and seed money towards establishing group savings and loans to promote upscaling and resilience building

1 - Needs

Acute food insecurity in the north east (BAY states) of Nigeria persist at alarming levels. Findings of the October 2019 Cadre Harmonisé analysis highlight that, between October and December 2019, approximately three million people in the BAY states will face acute levels of food insecurity (Phase 3 and above).Moreover, the prevalence of acute malnutrition is high, with GAM rates in Yobe (11.5 percent), Borno (8.1 percent) and Adamawa (7.2 percent) under pressure. As a result, the mortality rate in all three states is equally under pressure.The proposed project intervention will help address the following needs of the conflict affected population;•To restore and protect agricultural livelihoods decimated by the lack of access to essential agri-inputs as a result of the conflict. • To restore livestock-based livelihoods for households whose assets were lost through looting, destruction, disease or forced abandonment while fleeing attacks by armed insurgents.Furthermore, the project will generate alternative, sustainable livelihoods for vulnerable women and fisherfolk made redundant by the embargo on fishing in Lake Chad and generate livelihood opportunities for unemployed rural youth to support/promote the humanitarian-development-peace nexus. • Additionally, the project is aimed at mitigating conflict over limited fuel resources and protect vulnerable groups from risks associated with access to fuel and energy.

2 - Activities

FAO will collaborate with government institutions, the state ministry of agriculture, livestock, environment, RRR, research institutions to support the targeted households. The beneficiaries will include a minimum of 35% women and at least 60% youth, throughout the activities. Their specific needs in terms of training, resources, communication with communities and all aspects related to their successful participation in the project will be integrated. For animal restocking of goats and poultry, fish processing support, group savings and loans FAO will prioritize women-headed HHs to boost their income generation & nutrition, taking into account their limited access to land and other productive resources. FAO will target youth through its bull distributions primarily.In SAFE, FAO will build on its existing cook stove production centers in Borno to enhance expansion to other locations in Adamawa and Yobe states. This will also involve building capacities of young people in energy efficient stove production for employment generation.Specifically the proposed project will include the following activities;• Support to land preparation including distribution of rainy season and dry season kits (seeds and fertilizer) for crop production as well as essential inputs for backyard vegetable gardening to restore/protect agricultural livelihoods and promote household nutrition.• Animal restocking targeting women and youth with goat, sheep and poultry packages for income generation and improved nutrition.• Vaccination campaign to control animal diseases aimed at protecting livestock-based livelihoods; biosecurity/biocontainment training for disease control, food safety and animal welfare to key associations/groups of animal workers.• Distribution of aquaculture kits (tanks, feed, fingerlings) to unemployed fisherfolk linked with the provision of fish processing assets (smoking kilns) and training to vulnerable women’s groups.• Distribution of assets (bulls and ploughs) and training to unemployed rural youth to promote entrepreneurship in land development services.• Establishment/upgrading of fuel-efficient stove production centres and distribution of fuel-efficient stoves to households facing heightened protection and health risks; mitigate poor energy access and adoption of negative coping strategies. • Organization of beneficiaries into local agribusiness cooperatives supported with training and seed money towards establishing group savings and loans to promote upscaling and resilience buildingFAO will employ the following criteria in beneficiary selection for the activities mentioned; • Having secure access to land for planting, housing animals where relevant• Ability to physically participate in the productive activities.Priority households- Most vulnerable/food-insecure households among returnees, IDPs and host communities.- Households with high number of members.- Female-headed households.- Youth headed households- Households with children under five.- Households with malnourished children or at risk of malnutrition.- For crop production, households with access to land and ability to farm.*Protection*5 percent of the budget will be allocated for protection mainstreaming. Expenses will include the recruitment of a protection expert, training of stakeholders, the publication of communication materials and sensitization/advocacy.Protection mainstreaming will be reflected in the planning, implementation and monitoring of the project. FAO will support information sharing with communities on their entitlements and will implement complaint and feedback mechanisms. FAO will train all its staff and IPs on PSEA, as part of its already established PSEA action plan. FAO will undertake a protection risk assessment prior to the implementation of the activity. The Organization will also reinforce training of staff on GBV, PSEA, AAP and CwC. FAO undertook protection risk analysis in 2018 &findings will inform implementation.
