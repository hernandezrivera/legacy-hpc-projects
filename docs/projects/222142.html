Project Summary

The location of this project is Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North Counties. it is will cover 4 payams in Kapoeta East and 6 Payams in Kapoeta North. This project has been designed in participatory and inclusive manner. It intends to support 25000 (1021 returnees, 5000 IDPs and 18979 residents) drought and conflict affected population in two counties (Kapoeta North and Kapoeta East) in Eastern Equatoria State of South Sudan to deter famine. The intervention is humanitarian, Peace and development linked which is thought to support vulnerable 4200 households whose livelihoods are eroded by drought and conflicts in the counties. It targets highly affected households who will be identified by intensive selection criteria with the local communities and authorities in the project locations. This project is believed to prevent famine and improve the livelihood and food security of the households to help them improve their mental, physical and social wellbeing. It targets 14900 children, 7900 adults and 2200 elderly. The expected impact of this project is reduced number of people in need of Food Security and Livelihood humanitarian assistance in Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North by 2026 through saving life intervention and community Resilience building. This can be achieved by realizing the following outcomes: 1. Supported Provision of food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for 1021 returnees most affected in the populations in collaboration and coordination with WFP in the identification of beneficiaries and distribution of food assistance in the two counties, using appropriate modalities in achieving this objective; 2. Enhanced and sustained emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock distribution; and 3. Increased community resilience to support and strengthen 4200 households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. This project is feasible as NSDO has the staff capacity required for the project implementation and NSDO will work with all stakeholders in collaborative manner and contribute to the Food Security and Livelihood Cluster's objectives and its standard indicators to measure progress and report timely and share information to the cluster and other stakeholders to achieve the Humanitarian Response Strategic Objectives. NSDO will establish project management committees in the project locations and trained to apply adaptive and participatory project implementation approaches to carry out the project activities of this proposed project in close coordination through the county FSL Cluster and other clusters especially Protection cluster to promote complementarity and synergy. these approach will ensure efficient and cost effective implementation of the projects while building community capacity to achieve the localization agenda. Therefore, NSDO is seeking a funding value of 278, 000 USD to finance the implementation of the project to achieve its intended impact on the target beneficiaries most in needs out of over 50000 people in emergency needs in Kapoeta East County and Kapoeta North County.

1. Needs

Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North Counties located in Eastern Equatoria State are the proposed locations of this project. The two counties are inhabited by indigenous resident and returnees Toposa and Jie community intermingled with IDPs. There are approximately 175,078 people and 162,158 people who are Agro-pastoralists, populating Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North respectively. These counties experience below average sparse rainfall throughout a year characterised by normally long dry spells. 78% of the population practice farming of grains and 85% of the population rear cattle, Goats, Sheep and poultry. This project will be implemented in 4 payams of Kapoeta East and 6 payams of Kapoeta North. These locations are prone to climate change usually manifested by erratic rainfalls and drought. Climate shocks and stressors such as droughts and increased temperatures have already stressed natural resources and increased competition for water and food and increased risk of livestock pests and disease and inter-communal conflicts. This year the first cropping season of Kapoeta North and East was affected by long dry spell and hence crops failed in the first season. Conflicts had also been observed in the counties over water sources that caused displacement of farmers and interrupted farming and cattle raiding was also observed in the communities of Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North blamed on the neighboring community of Pibor which also interrupted farming and loss of lives and livelihood. These consequently led to increased vulnerability of people to food insecurity and hunger, increasing the prevalence of Gender based Violence, child neglect and abuse in the counties. The GBV AoR 2025 Humanitarian Needs and Response plan identifies Kapoeta East in Priority 2 and Kapoeta South is in Priority 3 of GBV severity. Similarly, The FSL Cluster intersectoral severity and prioritization map (2025) identifies Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North as severely food insecure in priority 2 and 3 respectively. Based on the IPC reports recently released, FAO, WFP and UNICEF in a press release on the 18/11/2024 warned jointly that there is deepening hunger crises looming in South Sudan with 57% of the population foreseen to be severely food insecure in the upcoming lean season. According to the IPC Report, Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North counties are currently in Phase 3 and are both projected to be in Phase 4 in the coming lean seasons. Currently, there are 26,000 people in emergency in Kapoeta East and 24,000 people in Kapoeta North are in emergency need of humanitarian assistance. They lack food, safe water, sanitation, protection and health services which was exacerbated by long dry spells, inter-communal cattle raids and conflicts. Most of the section of the population affected are children, women, people with disability and elderly age groups. NSDO Office in Kapoeta South conducted community engagement meetings with local community structures including local authorities and NGOs in Kapoeta East after releases of the IRNA Reports from Jie and Kauto payams conducted early last year. NSDO found that there are needs to prevent hunger and restore the livelihood while addressing protection issues of 50,000 population of these Counties through saving life and building community climate resilience. The consultation, NSDO had with communities, NNGOs, INGOs, UN Agencies present in Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North established that there is great opportunity for NSDO to collaborate and coordinate in the implementation of intervention to ensure complementarity and Inter-Cluster Synergy. This proposed project therefore seeks fund to contribute to emergency response to avert hunger in Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North for 25,000 beneficiaries who are most affected by the drought and conflict in the two counties.

2. Project activities

The project targets 15,000 and 10,000 beneficiaries in Kapoeta East County and Kapoeta North County respectively. The project will last for 6 months in Kapoeta North during the lean season from April to September and 12 months in Kapoeta East. The intended impact of this project is reduced number of people in need of Food Security and Livelihood humanitarian assistance in Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North by 2026 through Community Resilience building. This can be achieved by realizing the following outcomes: 1. Supported Provision of food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for 1021 returnees most affected in the populations in collaboration and coordination with WFP in the identification of beneficiaries and distribution of food assistance in the two counties, using appropriate modalities in achieving this objective; 2. Enhanced and sustained emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock distribution; and 3. Increased community resilience to support and strengthen 4200 households’ ability to absorb and adapt to contextual changes. To achieve these objectives, NSDO will implement the following activities with full involvement of all stakeholders in the project location across age group, gender and disability for six months in Kapoeta North and 12 months in Kapoeta East. In all the processes protection protection issues and Accountability will be at the heart of NSDO. For objective 1 of this proposal to be reached, NSDO will 1.1. Support General Food Distribution to 1021 most vulnerable returnees in emergency in the counties who are in severe hunger situation. This includes selection and registration of beneficiaries who are most in need in consideration of households that host returnees, people with disability and elderly. 1.2. Implement Food for Asset to complement GFD for 4200 households who are in crises state for six months in Kapoeta North and 12 Months in Kapoeta East to contribute to strengthening of the community resilience building activities, particularly development of water harvesting structures for irrigation within the project period. Objective 2 of this proposal can be achieved by implementing the following activities for 7900 people in severely food insecure households during the project period; 2.1. Distribution of dry season agricultural kits from January to March months in Kapoeta East only; 2.2. Distribution of lean season agricultural kits in the two counties from April to September; 2.3. Establishing/Re-establishing 500 kitchen Garden sites at Nutrition Facilities and schools during the respective project periods; 2.3. Trainings of 7900 farmers on Climate-Smart practices during the period of the project; 2.4. Treatment and Vaccination of 30000 cattle by the end of the project. To achieve objective 3 for 4200 households in the project locations, NSDO will carry out activities including; 3.1. Training of 20 Community Animal Health Workers and community-based extension service providers during the respective project period of the counties; 3.2. Training of 7900 farmers in groups on Good Agronomic practices and support Integrated Pest Management during the project period; 3.4. Provision of water pumps, Irrigation training/support; Ox Ploughs & training for 50 farmer groups during the respective project period in the Counties; 3.5. Support Post Harvest Loss Storage granaries; support the Installation or strengthening of community based EWS for peace and climate change by training and providing require equipment for the two counties during respective project periods; and Support dissemination of climate information. In order to track the progress of the activities and evaluation of the objectives, Participatory M&E system will be set that use the cluster indicators on weekly and biweekly to learn and inform decision making to improve the project.
