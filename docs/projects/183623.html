Project Summary

The project is HRP 2021 endorsed and will contribute to the HRP strategic objectives 3, and the Food Security cluster objective in the WB. It will do so by providing rehabilitation, productive assets and inputs to farmers with lands in Area C, specifically in 3 villages in Qalqilya Governorate. These farmers face specific challenges to cultivate their lands due to the different measures imposed by the occupation regime, including settler violence, demolition, confiscation, expansion of the wall, etc.
The project is based on an initial needs assessment conducted by PUI and UAWC in September 2020 that identified the most pressing needs of vulnerable farmers working in area C close to the wall or settlements. This assessment have been updated in September 2021 to reflect the recent changing of the needs. In addition, PUI coordinated with FSS, MoA Central and MoA directorates of Qalqilya, in order to identify and prioritize FSS needs and localities in this Governorate. In order to respond to the identified needs and to contribute to the HRP and cluster objectives, PUI and UAWC will provide 3 types of assistance:
- Provide 4 kilometres of water carrier lines to ensure the delivery of water from the resources to the agricultural lands. The project will also provide water storage (a 300m3 reservoir is planned to be constructed in one of the 2 communities; Sanniriya, and Beit Amin). The Village Councils will contribute meaningfully in providing feedback regarding the impact of this activity. It will benefit 200 farmers in total.
- Support 180 vulnerable farmers working in lands adjacent to the settlements or the wall and whose livelihoods are threatened by occupation measures. 6 km of agricultural roads will be rehabilitated. This will increase safe access to lands and contribute to the prevention of land confiscations. An additional 15 farmers will receive land rehabilitation support to increase their production capacity. The project will also provide cisterns to another 6 farmers to enhance the supplementary irrigation to trees and crops in their lands.
In total, 401 farmers households (1925 individuals) will benefit directly from the Action. Selection criteria for the farmers will depend mainly on socio-economic vulnerability, as PUI understands that the needs of people intersect with their different characteristics. Groups of people identified as being in greater need (FHHs, PwD, older persons, etc.), people facing increased IHL violation, or located closer to settlements, the wall or ARAs, will be prioritized. Technical criteria including topography of the land, slope percentage, soil depth, land ownership, and planted trees will also be taken into consideration.
The project will be implemented with UAWC and each organization will bring their expertise: UAWC supports agriculture from a development perspective, focusing on water activities, while PUI rehabilitates lands and assets, easing access and providing support to farmers through emergency and protective responses to settler-related incidents. Furthermore, the project will complement the Action of the West Bank Protection Consortium by covering a gap in the provision of assistance and livelihood support to vulnerable farmers in Area C. Moreover, PUI will mobilize its Consortium experience (tools, methodology, network) at all different stages of the project.
PUI will ensure the mainstreaming of PSEA throughout service provision as an effort to respect and promote the principle of do-no-harm that guides humanitarian work.

1 - Needs

One of the major occupation regime measures affecting Palestinian livelihoods remains settlements expansion and subsequent Settlers Violence (SV). Settlements have been established and expanded across the WB and East Jerusalem for the past 54 years. This expansion has a continuous negative impact on Palestinian daily life (attacks on Palestinian livelihoods, residential movement restrictions and other types of harassment). From 1 January 2017 to 31 August 2021, OCHA recorded more than 3,480 incidents of settler attack or harassment, averaging in excess of two incidents per day. During this period, 9 Palestinians were killed by settlers and 548 injured. Indeed, the number of incidents resulting in Palestinian casualties has risen year-on-year since 2016. In addition, 39,779 trees and 1,735 vehicles were vandalized during this period. As of 31 August, in 2021 alone, 7,738 trees have been vandalized. The COVID-19 pandemic is still having a negative impact in the daily life of Palestinians, adding additional layer of distresses to the population. The vaccination process have been launched earlier in 2021 by the Palestinian Ministry of Health. However, the region is currently facing the fourth wave of the pandemic. According to the Food Security Sector Humanitarian Needs for 2022: “The pandemic is placing significant stress on all dimensions of the food system (i. food security, nutrition and health; ii. socio-economy; iii. territorial balance and equity ;and iv. environment), threatening the food security and livelihoods of thousands of vulnerable people in both West Bank and Gaza”. The FSS cluster identified 1,361,090 People In Need https://projects.hpc.tools/project/183623/edit/review2/11 10/16/21, 5:59 PM HPC - Project Module: Edit Projectin the West Bank and Gaza Strip. 27% of those are from the West Bank (362,859 individuals). PUI/UAWC conducted a rapid needs assessment in September 2020 with the communities of Qalqiliya (Jit, Sanniriya, and Beit Amin), and Tulkarem (Qaffin, Zeita, and Kafr Jammal, in addition to Kafr Sour). Outcomes indicated that 46.1% of the families in these locations depend on agricultural practices as their main source of income, while 10% depend on agriculture as a secondary source of income. The average monthly income for 35.5% of the surveyed HHs ranges between 1450 NIS- 2999 NIS. 52.5% of the participants reported being in debt, (20% of them for the purchase of agricultural inputs). Based on FGDs conducted with community representatives, the protracted humanitarian crisis associated with the Israeli occupation and restrictions is affecting the use and access to around 51,750 dunums of land, of which 6,200 dunums are located near to the coordination area, and can be easily accessed by the Israeli settlers. In area C, around 25,500 dunums of agricultural land can be accessed. 20,000 of those are currently being cultivated, while the remaining lands needs rehabilitation or reclamation. 51 Km of roads are also in need for rehabilitation. These roads are vital for two reasons. Firstly, to facilitate reaching the land for all family members and take the required tools and plants to the land using vehicles. Secondly, it is considered in most cases as a safe exit for the farmers. New roads opening to access to 14,359 dunums of land would benefit 5,150 farmers. Part of the targeted communities (Kufur Sour and Qaffin) stated that the lack of water access can be observed up to 90% of time, while it is estimated at 50% of the time in Kufur Jamal and 32% in the community of Sinira. Following the farmer’s HH assessment as well as the FGDs with experienced farmers and community representatives, the following needs were highlighted: 1. Road and Land Rehabilitation 2.Rehabilitation / provision of water carrier lines 3/ Distribution of agricultural tools. The priority of interventions was discussed with MoA and village counsels. Kafr Sur village has been excluded from the project as the needs assessment indicated that most farmers' lands are located in Area B or in the seam zone. It is worth noting that HHs belonging to different age, gender and diversity (AGD) groups (FHHs, PwD, older persons, etc.) were already facing increased risks in terms of livelihoods, which are now further exacerbated by the current pandemic. SEA, as a form of GBV, is a risk that is always present in humanitarian interventions. Thus the need to ensure beneficiaries are protected from SEA should always be highlighted.it is important to note that the risk and protection from sea are associated with the unique age, gender and diversity characteristics and how they are perceived in the social sphere. PUI takes into consideration the disproportionate burden falling into different AGD groups and aims to address it by ensuring people with different needs and capacities can safely access all provided services and that activities are tailored to fit their characteristics and needs. To support this process, PUI is currently developing a gender analysis to act as a roadmap reflecting needs, risks and opportunities related to different gender and age groups. The specific project-related nuances associated with gender and age will be identified through planned community consultations and complement the general gender analysis. PUI/UAWC was able to address part of those needs by applying for the HF allocation end of 2020 and targeting the villages of Qaffin, Zeita, and Kafr Jammal in Tulkarem Governorate. However, a gap remains in the three villages in Qalqiliya Governorate. PUI/UAWC had contacted the village counsels of Jit, Sanniriya, and Beit Amin to check if the needs are still valid and not addressed by any partner since September 2020. The three representatives confirmed that the needs are vital for their communities and it should be a priority for any planned projects. The same message was conveyed by MoA Qalqiliya directory.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Land rehabilitation will be carried out for 60 dunums in Qalqilya Governorate, including building of retaining walls, ploughing, fencing, cleaning and planting seedlings and seeds for intercropping agriculture. 15 vulnerable farmers will benefit from rehabilitation of their lands. Rehabilitation works will be implemented directly by the farmers. Mixed approach of cash and in-kind support will be used to implement this activity. The activity will target 4 dunums of land per farmer. The cost per farmer would be 4000$. Out of which, 2000$ will be dedicated to provide farmer with fencing, seedlings, and seeds. The other 2000$ will be provided as cash payments on 40-40-20 installments following the implementation of ploughing, cleaning, or building retaining walls. Agreements with farmers will be signed to regulate the payment methods, conditionality of assistance, standards and qualifications of the expected outputs. Activity 2: The construction of 6 cisterns to benefit additional 6 farmers. The objective of this activity is to enhance the supplementary irrigation to trees and crops. That would increase the production by 20-30% per dunum according to MoA (FSS cluster). Support will be given to farmers whose lands are adjacent to the Prior Coordination areas or the wall, part of which was confiscated by the IF earlier, and in areas exposed to SV/vandalism. Cash approach will be used to implement this activity. Agreements with farmers will be signed to govern the payment methods, conditionality of assistance, standards and quality of the expected outputs. The cisterns will have an average capacity of 70 cubic meter each. PUI/UAWC staff will follow up the construction and support the farmers throughout. The support will include technical support and legal advises. Activity 3: https://projects.hpc.tools/project/183623/edit/review3/11 10/16/21, 5:59 PM HPC - Project Module: Edit ProjectRehabilitation of agricultural roads. This activity includes the rehabilitation of 6 km of agricultural in the governorate of Qalqilya. 180 HHs will benefit from increased safe access to their agricultural lands, which will accelerate pruning and/or support land use -which will contribute to preventing land confiscations. The rehabilitation of agricultural roads will increase the number of persons accessing the land, resulting in increased protection of farmers; furthermore, it will secure the access of ambulances/fire fighters in case of protection incidents related to SV. Materials will be provided by PUI/UAWC and the local councils/municipalities will carry out the work. Agricultural roads will be selected through a consultative process involving local councils/municipalities, MoA, and UAWC committees, giving priority to areas exposed to threats of SV, settlement expansion, demolition, etc. The selection will be based on a scoring system that will take into account protection concerns and the number of farmers who can benefit from the rehabilitated road. Different and diverse groups from the community shall be part of the selection process. Activity 4: Rehabilitation/ Provision of water carrier lines / water storage. This activity includes the provision of 4 km of water carriers’ lines in Qalqilya governorate. 100 HHs will benefit from the sustainable source of water to use for irrigation of their trees and crops. Consequently, this will increase the production of their lands. This activity will encourage farmers to invest in their lands and diversify their crops. In parallel, a water storage (reservoir) of a capacity of 300m3 will be constructed in one village based on needs assessment to complement the activity of water carrier lines provision. This additional activity will reach another 100 HHs. All activities will take into consideration specific age, gender and diversity (AGD) needs and opportunities by consulting in an accessible and appropriate manner diverse community members. Specific modifications will be made to ensure different AGD groups enjoy equal access to service provision, as well as to mitigate any possible risks associated with the assistance itself. Including the voices of groups of people traditionally excluded from decision-making and the public sphere, like women, children. PwDs. provides a unique opportunity to enrich humanitarian aid, and promote equitable social norms and pluralism within the target communities. Men, Women, PwDs, and youth should be represented in the selection committees to be consulted and part of the decision process. This action will encourage the participation of people from different AGD groups and promote equal access to productive resources and joint decision-making by engaging a diverse representation. The methodology of working with entire HH instead of only with HH representatives will be adopted. Additionally women, PwDs and people belonging to different AGD groups will actively be called upon to apply for rehabilitation activities and state their specific needs. They will be prioritized through the scoring system used for beneficiary selection.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Main activities will be implemented by a supplier who will be selected through the procurement protocols of PUI/UAWC. The supplier will be handling 70% of the work of the project. Thus this modality will be considered as in-kind. This will be complemented by cash transfer to beneficiaries upon the delivery of agreed tasks. In this project, cash will be used to complement the in-kind support and in cases where the risk of providing the in-kind assistance through the supplier is high (e.g. in case of ISF presence / intimidation to the supplier). The decision will be taken after consulting the community representatives and HH members. There are no evidence that in-kind modality is associated with higher or lower risk of SEA. Thus PUI will ensure to promote PSEA regardless of the service modality, as it is understood that SEA is a prevalent issue regardless of the sector, modality or location. It is important to note nonetheless that different service modalities may be associated with different types of SEA. PUI will be vigilant in ensuring SEA risks are monitored and appropriate prevention and mitigation plans are in place.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Beneficiaries are involved in all aspects of the Action through a shared decision making process. The community-based protection approach involves a continuous process of self-assessment and analysis of their vulnerabilities and needs, used as an entry point for the elaboration of local protection strategies. As soon as the action starts, PUI/UAWC will meet with community representatives and all selected beneficiaries to inform them about the project and explain how beneficiaries are expected to participate and contribute for the success of the action. This meeting will allow participants to inform PUI/UAWC about their concerns and involve them in a participatory approach. All participants will be informed about PUI Feedback & Response Mechanism (FRM) as well as PUI’s commitment to ensuring PSEA. During those meetings, PUI / UAWC staff members will ensure to hand out information cards on the mechanism and explain the FRM. They will pay attention to highlight that feedback, whether positive or negative, will be taken into consideration and addressed by PUI representatives (independent from project team) and be dealt with respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. PUI will work with the established committees to ensure full transparency and make sure to hand out cards and posters to partners in sufficient quantity. Meanwhile, feedbacks may be provided verbally in the field, through text message, email or phone (using the dedicated address and phone number provided on information cards). Feedbacks may originate from beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries impacted by PUI / partners’ interventions as well. PUI FRM document and SoP might be provided upon request. All activities, assessments, community consultations and the FRM will be adapted in terms of modes and modalities to ensure different age, gender and diversity groups can access them equitably.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

By adopting a gender, age, and diversity (AGD) approach, PUI will ensure that its FRM is inclusive to all different groups of people. More specifically, information and awareness about the existence of the FRM and how it is operated will be disseminated to different groups of people and not only to the heads of the household. PUI has developed a child-friendly FRM information material to ensure children know how to report effectively. For people with visual disabilities or those not able to read and write, PUI will use verbal communication at household level. The reporting channels will also be diverse, to ensure access. People who can use a phone will be able to submit their inputs through the PUI hotline. Those who are unable to use a phone (older people, children, PwD, etc.) will be able to make verbal complaints to PUI staff during their field visits. Soliciting feedback is also of utmost importance, considering the prevailing social norms around who can complain. Thus, during the regular beneficiary satisfaction surveys, people will be asked if they are aware of the FRM, and if they are not, they will be informed on how to use it and will be asked if they want to make a complaint. To keep communication effective, PUI has a specific budget to guarantee the inclusion of different groups. So, if a person who is deaf wants to raise a complaint, PUI will be able to consult a sign language interpreter. PUI makes sure to have dedicated staff included in the FRM process that are representative of the targeted communities, to make sure that the person feels comfortable in raising an issue. Lastly, the FRM can be a channel to receive Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) complaints. To ensure the safety of the survivors reporting, PUI has established separate procedures in receiving and processing SEA-related complaints, and staff members involved in the FRM will be trained in safely receiving disclosures from survivors and PSEA minimum standards.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Protection mainstreaming is crucial in humanitarian aid delivery. PUI is dedicated to ensuring do no harm and dignity to all people accessing services regardless of their characteristics. PUI has a global Protection mainstreaming framework and a country-level Protection and Gender Mainstreaming strategy to reflect on the type of actions required at each stage of the project cycle to ensure Protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. Based on this strategy and planned community consultations, a project-specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming action plan will be developed. Some of the key intervention areas to ensure do-no-harm will be: • A protection and gender risk and mitigation measures analysis will be drafted project onset. The analysis will be further refined and triangulated with the solicitation of direct feedback from the target community regarding the perceived protection risks, needs and capacity. The analysis will have a special focus on the risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and challenges for people with disabilities. According to the initial Protection and Gender risk analysis, project-specific and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identified risks and gaps. • Community consultation, through different means (FGDs, Key informants Interviews (KIIs), surveys, etc.) will be conducted throughout the project implementation to monitor the progress of the mitigation measures, as well as to detect any new or emerging protection concerns. All tools, SOPs, and materials used during each of the project steps will include Protection mainstreaming considerations. Protection and Gender Mainstreaming indicator(s) will be measured and monitored regularly through different data collection sources (observations, consultations, FRM, etc.). • SEA is a GBV issue that might negatively impact all aspects of Protection mainstreaming and have lasting consequences in the lives of survivors. To ensure PSEA, PUI has and is currently further strengthening its SEA reporting mechanism. The reporting mechanism is accessible through different channels for beneficiaries, staff, and other stakeholders. PSEA training will be provided to all staff members. Accessible and clear awareness-raising and key messaging on PSEA will also be disseminated to project beneficiaries. All partners are required to sign and adhere to the PUI PSEA policy. • Project beneficiaries (with or without specific vulnerabilities) in need of more specialized Protection or psychosocial support (PSS) services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed or drafted. At the same time, staff members will be trained on “Safe Protection Referrals” training module, to include “ Communicating and responding to GBV disclosures” . • A protection capacity building plan will be drafted action onset to reflect on the needs identified during the initial Protection and Gender Risks Analysis, as well as subsequent consultations with staff and beneficiaries. Some of the topics to be included in the capacity building plan will be: Protection, age, gender, and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, GBV, child protection, psychological first aid, safe referrals, PSEA, and child safeguarding and inclusion of people with disabilities. • To ensure all Protection and gender mainstreaming activities are at the forefront of the project, selected field staff members will be trained and mentored to act as Protection mainstreaming focal points. They will be tasked with specific Protection and gender mainstreaming activities while also raising the awareness of other staff members and beneficiaries on Protection related issues.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As described above, PUI is very vigilant in acknowledging and addressing the intersectionality of different characteristics with different needs and capacities. Specific mitigation measures will be adopted to ensure people with diverse characteristics, and coping mechanisms can access assistance equally. These mitigation measures will derive from community consultations with people from diverse groups and different vulnerabilities. The protection and gender risk and mitigation analysis will be further enriched and complimented throughout the project duration to identify the potential risks and systematizes the response. Access can be both physical and psychological. In terms of physical access, our teams will be available to conduct home visits to individuals who might have difficulties traveling to consultation sessions, like older people, PwD, female-headed households, etc. Our field team is gender-balanced and culturally trained to ensure home visits do no harm to individuals and families. There is also the possibility to provide transportation for vulnerable individuals to access specific services or consultations. PUI has a specific budget for protection mainstreaming activities that could be utilized for this purpose. Our selection criteria for the activities that are conducted at household level, take into consideration the increased ascribed vulnerability of different groups of people like female-headed households and will be included in priority. In terms of psychological access, our approach includes the mobilization of the whole community. People who might be traditionally excluded from decision-making and public discourse like children and PwD will be consulted and included in the project, thus promoting a positive community-wide participation. To do that, field staff are trained in community mobilization, inclusion of PwD, communicating with survivors of GBV, amongst others topics.
