Project Summary

According to the HNO 2022, more than 93% of the population is facing challenges related to access to water, hygiene and sanitation, and 59% of the population is living in acute need of WASH services, across the four governorates Aleppo, Raqqa, Deir ez Zour and Hasaka. This impact came from the water crisis in which the extremely low water level in the Euphrates, WASH systems suffered from damage owing to conflict and inter-communal tensions, strain from years of functioning at high capacity due to the growing demand, limited or no maintenance, a continuous drain of technical staff and poor water resource management, exaggerated by cascade effects of climate change, economic downturn, and electricity and fuel supply crisis. More than 70 % of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional. In Raqqa and Deir ez Zour, all the untreated sewage is discharged to the Euphrates river, also, most of the IDPs sites sewage is openly discharged causing serious health issues. WASH needs in schools remain very high, more than 40 % of caretakers in households with children attending school received complaints from children on WASH-related issues. As well as, Ar-Raqqa, Deir ez Zour, Al-Hasakeh, and Aleppo are currently the most affected by the cholera/AWS outbreak.
The program seeks to improve WASH services by rehabilitating water stations, networks and sewage systems, undertaking vector control, chlorination mapping, capacity building for local water departments (training, water safety plans, essential supplies and equipment), emergency WASH in camps and settlements (water trucking, installation of latrines and water distribution points), and sustainable water supply solutions for camps in the form of a water network, with a focus on those areas of where populations are currently reliant on unsafe water direct from the Euphrates or via water trucking.
There will be a particular focus on Deir Ez Zor and Manbij where it is estimated that around 60% of the population are in acute WASH need, limited access to safe and sufficient quantity of water, availability and affordability of hygiene items, solid waste disposal, and effective waste management including access to toilets.

4 - Implementation of activities

This NGO will rehabilitate and re-commission 18 water supply systems, 8 water station and 10 water networks focusing especiall in Deir Ez Zour, but working as well in Raqqa and Aleppo governorates. These sites will be rehabilitated (were possible) to meet the designed capacity. To increase the water quality/safety, a total of 30 chlorination systems will be installed in water station lacking chlorination systems or non-functional ones. Chlorine will be provided to these water stations, and to the rehabilitated stations. In order to detect the most urgent needs this NGO will engage with authorities and the cluster, and will as well prepare exhaustive assessments to clearly identify the needs. This NGO will install and rehabilitate 160 latrines by this rehabilitation of WASH facilities ensuring that IDPs and host communities will have access to gender-segregated latrines including hand washing stations as per SPHERE, per latrine does not exceed 20 person. Vector Control activities to control the spread of vector borne diseases, specially Leshmaniasis, will target 62 communities in Deir Ez Zor and Membij, with each community on average comprising 3,000 individuals. This NGO will also distribute 1,000 hygiene kits to IDP HH in and out camps. Hygiene promotion activities will be conducted in these same areas where hygiene kits are distributed. emergency WASH activities will target all new IDPs displaced according to their needs and in coordination with other organizations.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Related to WASH, the design of latrines includes, in the blocks for women, privacy walls with basins inside the wall space to provide privacy. All blocks of latrines, not only women’s but men’s as well, have solar lights to reduce the risks of GBV (the lights are included as well in men’s latrines as well to avoid creating a social hub in the women’s latrines by night). This NGO is improving its designs and will include a specific latrine in each block to be used by children. Water supply rehabilitation will be designed and implemented to allow community members to access water in their homes, or in some cases, a water source nearby their residence, the water distribution points are designed to be used by children with taps at a comfortable height. Accessible latrines and wash facilities are also installed. Any vulnerable protection cases identified by this NGO WASH team in the course of implementing the activities listed above will be referred to other service providers in NES. To aid this, in-house protection specialists will provide comprehensive training to all staff and partners on the referral mechanism and pathways. These protection specialists will also join different field teams (WASH, FSL etc) on a regular basis to provide protection advice and on-the-job mentoring to other staff. This NGO has a specific policy in place that all staff and contractors have to sign and abide, and that clearly states the expected behaviour to avoid abuse and exploitation.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The NGO's Code of Conduct and Associated policies are consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Risks related to this project have been identified in a separate multi-sectoral strategy with appropriate mitigation measures identified. The latter includes, but is not limited to, the appointment and training of PSEA focal points in all project sites and offices and trained with support from the Syria PSEA network. It also includes the use of the PSEA networks developed checklists for the observation of operations to identify, prevent and mitigate potential SEA and SEA risk factors in ongoing field activities.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks- Further escalation of the conﬂict, or failure in the security situation, in NES as a result of any of the following, 1- Escalation in the ongoing conflict between the SDF and government of Turkey, extending beyond the current areas of operation. 2- IS resurgence in Deir ez Zour with extension of attacks and activities to surrounding areas. 3- Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conﬂict, jeopardising access to affected project areas. 4- Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, continued border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations.Assumptions, 1-This project will be able to access beneficiaries and assess their specific needs. 2-There will be no signiﬁcant shifts in control over key locations in NES 3 - Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and goods are available locally/regionally.Mitigating Measures: * Strengthen operational resilience. In the event of increased insecurity in an area, shift operations to the nearest unaffected base of operations *Flexibly shifting modalities as required between in-kind, voucher and cash depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and availability of key goods* Full engagement in, and the development of robust, continuity of services plans to minimise the potential humanitarian consequences of any disruption in services to target area •
