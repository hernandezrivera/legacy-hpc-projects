Project Summary

Organization for welfare and Development in Action (OWDA) is proposing continuation of Lifesaving emergency health Response Project for drought and conflict -affected communities through provision of essential health services in Moyale (SM), Hudet, and Mubarak Woredas of Dawa zone, Somali Region.
The aim of the project is to provide life-saving assistance on essential health services to deliver comprehensive primary health services including sexual reproductive health integrated with Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP), management of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) mental health, COVID-19 pandemic measures to contain the spread of the virus and with priority given children under the age of five, elderly, people with physical injuries, disabilities and mental health needs, psychosocial support, SGBV clinical case management, public health emergency, focusing to reduce the causes of morbidity and mortality rates resulted from public health events. Likewise the project will provide cross-cutting support to Gender, Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA).
The project will provide support to 66,970 beneficiaries by an estimated budget of $ 650,000 the proposed district will be an estimated hotspot classification priority 1 woredas for the drought response.
The proposed Lifesaving emergency health Response project will support a variety of health services including outpatient consultations and treatment, health education, routine vaccination for children under five, antenatal care, delivery services, postnatal care, family planning, communicable diseases, non-communicable diseases, and referrals to higher services. MPHSS activities and COVID-19 measures will be taken to ensure the response is an integrated approach and promoting to maintain the essential health services and benefit from the health intervention for the targeted vulnerable communities in the project's target locations. Patients with pre-existing, new physical and mental disabilities will be also supported for clinical care and linked to other related services in the continuum of care. Likewise, the project will be supported and strengthened, by the existing integrated disease surveillance and response system. Preparedness actions will be undertaken in IDPs and host communities for the project's target locations to mitigate the impact of outbreaks when they occur.
The project will liaise and support the other ongoing response including the COVID-19 pandemic in order to enhance and contribute to the minimization of caseloads, deaths, and the impact on the health system. Similarly, the project will help prevent, detect and treat cute malnutrition through Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) including the Therapeutic Feeding Program (TFP). Mainstreaming of Gender and Protection activities will be prioritized during the intervention. In line with the 2022 Ethiopian Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), the proposed project will deliver a full- spectrum of emergency health interventions including prevention, treatment, case management, community mobilization, and case identification to achieve and sustain the project outcome. OWDA will adopt COVID-19 prevention and control measures during the implementation activities and special consideration will be given to staff conducting screening and staff capacity building and community mobilization gathering, and this will help reduce the risk of COVID-19 transmission.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

OWDA is committed to uphold the Core Humanitarian Standards on Accountability and Quality Management, including appropriateness, relevance, and timeliness of response, strengthening of local capacity, participation, and feedback mechanisms, and incorporate learning across all activities. To this end, OWDA strives to ensure transparent and participatory processes throughout the project cycle, during project development, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation. Feedback from community members, including disability people, people with special need and children, on the quality and accessibility of project resources and services, is collected regularly through participatory monitoring mechanisms including community feedback and response meetings, focus group discussions, and suggestion boxes. Appropriate and confidential feedback/complaint mechanisms will be strengthened in consultation with beneficiaries to enhance OWDA’s ability to protect beneficiaries from various forms of exploitation and abuse. Existing project/context-specific accountability systems will be established, and the project will have a detailed accountability plan. All responses will be delivered verbally, in writing, and in recorded form. This mechanism improves the quality of program implementation, strengthens relationships, increases acceptance by communities, reduces the risk of fraud and inefficient use of funds, and reduces the risk of harm to beneficiaries.
