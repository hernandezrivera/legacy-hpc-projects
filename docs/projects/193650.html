Project Summary

The unique socio-economic conditions of blockade, with its movement restrictions and destruction of productive capacity, are further compounded by repeated cycle of conflicts as well as the global emergencies of the Ukraine Crisis followed by the COVID-19 pandemic. For the second consecutive year, UNRWA opened the 2022-2023 academic year with full face-to-face learning, providing quality education to 294,086 students (142,363 girls and 151,723boys, including 5,000 students with disabilities).
In order to ensure that no child is left behind and that access to education is available to all students regardless of their available resources, UNRWA will provide: (1) additional learning support to enhance children literacy and numeracy skills, (2) material support to assist students’ learning experience, (3) structured recreational (after-school and summer), (4) cleaning supplies and materials to continue meeting hygiene requirements outlined by the WHO and MoH, (5) support to special education for children with disabilities through Community Based Rehabilitation Centers, (6) mental health and psychosocial support to students and parents.

1 - Needs

In Gaza, the economic blockade, which entered its 16th year in June 2022, continues to push the population into deeper poverty and despair, compounded by recurrent hostilities and the global emergencies related to the Ukraine Crisis and the aftermath of COVID-19 pandemic affecting the increase in the cost of living.The Education Cluster identified that an estimated 608,556 Palestinians (49 per cent females) across the oPt, of which nearly 65 per cent live in the Gaza Strip meaning over 395,000 children, will need humanitarian assistance to access education in 2023 (HNO 2023). The 2022 Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment shows that a year after the resumption of in-person learning, the need for remedial learning remains high, 81.2 per cent of refugee households reporting a need for catch-up learning to fill the gap of the learning loss during the school closures due to COVID-19 or other reasons. The same study found that 7.1 per cent of surveyed households with school-aged children are not attending school, and the most frequently reported reason for non-attendance is because the households cannot afford to pay school related expenses at 35.6 per cent. Another striking reason answered by 9.4 per cent is because children are working to support the family. Furthermore, 9.1 per cent of households with school-ages children were not planning to enroll all eligible children in school at the beginning of the 2022-2023 school year, and the most frequently reported reasons for non-enrolment at 15.2 per cent was because the households cannot afford school related expenses. The situation is further aggravated for children with disabilities. Of the 9.3 per cent of Gaza households who reported having a child with a disability, 93.5 per cent perceive additional challenges in accessing educational services, and in fact, of the 5.7 per cent of children that dropped out of school during the last school year, around 19 per cent dropped out because the disability-specific needs were not met. There is a general lack of school-based psychosocial support for children in Gaza, where children are still dealing with psychological distress as a consequence of the recurrent hostilities (HNO 2023). Based on the Sectoral PiN Aggregation Model for the education cluster, around 57,000 households in Gaza reported that at least one child is showing signs of psychosocial distress, and only 66.2 per cent of households reported that psychosocial support by trained counsellors is available at schools for children if needed (MSNA 2022). Furthermore, children studying in these schools in particular lack opportunities to participate in extracurricular activities (HNO 2023).

2 - Activities

In 2023, UNRWA will continue to provide basic education to 294,086 elementary and preparatory school students in 284 Agency schools in Gaza, providing a quality, inclusive and equitable education to Palestine refugee students. The Agency will continue to apply its EiE approach, which was designed to ensure the continued provision of education to children impacted by crises, such as COVID-19 and recurring hostilities. In order to support students to meet minimum academic standards in core subjects such as Arabic and Mathematics, additional educational support for refugee students will be provided during the normal school hours as well as the after-school hours. This intervention will mainly target UNRWA students who have experienced learning loss due to the COVID-19 pandemic and the August 2022 and May 2021 hostilities. UNRWA will ensure that an estimated 22 percent of UNRWA students in need will receive support to catch up with the adequate literacy and numeracy level. Furthermore, in light of deepening poverty due to socioeconomic situation, many parents struggle to secure the learning and wellbeing needs for their children at school. To safeguard children’s right to education, the Agency aims to provide at least 235,269 students with the necessary back-to-school supplies, including stationery and school bags. Throughout the school year, the Agency will ensure continued implementation of hygiene and mitigation measures in all 284 UNRWA schools. This will include ensuring the continued availability of cleaning materials.To ensure access to education for refugee children with certain disabilities, who cannot be mainstreamed in UNRWA schools and require specialized learning modules, UNRWA will continue partnering with community-based rehabilitation centres (CBRCs). Here, the aim will be to provide basic education for at least 573 children with disabilities, in line with the Agency’s inclusive education principles.Recurrent rounds of hostilities, including the large-scale hostilities in May 2021 and more recently the August 2022 escalation, has forced many Palestine refugees in Gaza and children in particular to experience additional psychological trauma. To ensure that MHPSS is not disrupted during such a critical period, UNRWA will implement MHPSS interventions by trained counsellors, aimed at strengthening children’s psycho-social resilience, coping capacities and mental well-being. This support will be provided in UNRWA schools by way of after-school extracurricular activities and the organization of summer fun weeks. UNRWA will implement structured recreational activities during the after-school hours for at least 40,000 students to mitigate the effect of multiple stressors affecting the students’ learning. This initiative will create a safe space for students to play and develop new skills through participating in sports, arts and cultural activities. Through summer activities in 2023, students will be engaged in constructive and fun activities in a protective environment, building concrete life skills and positive values to support mental, physical and emotional wellbeing. With reference to differing vulnerability profiles, in recognition that crisis impacts individuals differently, girls will especially benefit from this intervention as they are generally facing limited opportunities for fun activities outside the home boundaries. Summer activities also ensure the continuity of counselling services for students receiving PSS during the school year. In addition, the Agency will ensure that school psychosocial counsellors are deployed across all UNRWA schools in the Gaza Strip to provide both preventive and responsive PSS to all students. Through this intervention, individual and group counselling will be offered in UNRWA schools. Counsellors will employ a holistic and integrated approach to child and family well-being, providing consultations and guidance for students, parents and teachers and will continue to be in contact with parents to enhance their partnership in PSS for children. Counsellors will also be available for consultations with parents, either by phone, through school online platforms or through group sessions. The GFO MHPSS unit plans to conduct 573 group sessions in 2023 for parents.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations, as well as made a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestinian refugees’ rights. UNRWA regularly assesses the level of compliance with Protection standards of the services provided to refugees through a Protection Audit. UNRWA schools are regularly monitored against the four main protection mainstreaming pillars, as defined by the Global Protection Cluster and echoed by UNRWA, the assessed principles are: (i) Prioritize safety, dignity and do no harm; (ii) Meaningful access to services; (iii) Accountability; and (iv) Participation and empowerment of refugees. UNRWA provides basic education to protect the safety and well-being of the students. UNRWA schools are equipped with child-friendly tools providing space for boys and girls including students with disabilities to study comfortably eliminating the risk of getting harmed or exposed to any danger as much as possible. The pandemic and the deteriorating socio-economic situation resulted in an increase in the protection threats facing groups with multiple overlapping vulnerabilities, which includes those at risk of domestic and intimate partner violence and the elderly. The risk of sexual exploitation and abuse may have also increased, as evidenced in other crisis situations. The Agency is ensuring that protection principles are mainstreamed and integrated in the response, and that special attention is paid to the needs of the most vulnerable, including children. Providing support to children who were forced to adjust their learning routine due to any kind of emergencies will protect their right to education during this challenging time.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA welcomes all Palestine refugee children from grade 1 to 9 in its schools across the Gaza Strip. The location of schools takes into consideration of safety concerns, especially for girls, including the accessibility, potential risks to reach the area for different groups of population. Furthermore, schools are clearly marked and visible, respecting UNRWA neutrality policy. Most of UNRWA schools are equipped with elevators to ease access for students with mobility difficulties, and some schools also implemented a child-friendly design. Educational staff, who are regularly targeted by capacity building activities including on protection and gender mainstreaming, are able to refer specific protection cases to MHPSS counsellors for follow up. Community Based Rehabilitation Centres provides education to children with disabilities in order to ensure that these vulnerable children are not left behind.
