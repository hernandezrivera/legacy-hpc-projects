Project Summary

This intervention aims to strengthened access to housing, land and property for displaced women and girls in Konduga, Gwoza, Dikwa and Damasak in Borno State, North East Nigeria. This will be achieved through the provision of information, counselling and legal services to the beneficiaries and capacity building of stakeholders for collaborative resolution of HLP dispute.

1 - Needs

According to an OCHA report, as the conflict between the Government of Nigeria (GoN) and Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) in North-East Nigeria enters its 12th year, the people in the states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (referred to as the BAY states) continue to face widespread insecurity and ongoing military operations, damaged infrastructure, tattered local economies, loss of livelihoods and gross violations of HLP rights by all parties to the conflict. Among them, 4.2 million people live in Borno state alone with around 1.6 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) out of the 2.2 million persons displaced across the three North-Eastern BAY states. Based on customary practices in the North East, and when available, traditional leaders assign plots of land to Internally Displaced Persons for housing and farming purposes. Unfortunately, the HLP rights of these displaced communities are not respected. These customary agreements need to be strengthened to prevent forced evictions and conflicts. In this region, a steady return movement is observed, Borno state, hosts the majority of conflict-affected and displaced persons across the BAY states and remains the hotspot of the crisis in North-East Nigeria. It sheds light on three concerning developments and their protection implications for the civilian population in Borno. Camp closures in Maiduguri Metropolitan Centre (MMC) and Jere LGAs with enforced relocations and returns to various areas across Borno, which may include, denial or impediments to resources, opportunities, services resulting in high levels of food insecurity Curtailment of the freedom of movement. As IDPs return to their place of origin, various HLP issues arise including secondary occupation, destroyed/inhabitable homes, missing property ownership documentation, scarcity of agro-pastoral land, and difficulty accessing land especially for female heads of household. Challenges in relation to HLP rights following displacement and return also result in major conflicts among displaced persons and host communities, leading to disruption in social cohesion and escalating tensions.

2 - Activities

During this intervention, the following activities will be implemented: Provide options with HLP issues on legal remedies particularly issues of misplaced documents, institutions, and land registration. a). Mobilize the referrals of a persons with HLP needs to non-food items, safeguarding and other concerns to access the needed assistance. b).To build capacity of the camp and community structure on land registration and policy development. c). Make provision for legal counseling and psychosocial support including community sensitization and awareness-raising on HLP rights and alternative dispute resolutions. d). Give support to persons with HLP related issues to access alternative dispute mechanisms including mediation, negotiation, and arbitration. e). Conduct HLP training for the community members comprised of Returnees, IDPs, and host community on Human rights, HLP rights, local and international legal frameworks including traditional customary laws.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Through the integration of protection principles into our delivery, we will ensure that our activities target the most vulnerable, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the human rights of the beneficiaries without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect. To enhance protection mainstreaming, NRM will ensure that all protection principles are incorporated in the implementation. The organization will prioritize safety and dignity and avoid causing harm, also ensure meaningful access, accountability, and participation and empowerment. NRM will do this by having dedicated staff members on the project that will oversee all protection issues and attend to them accordingly considering vulnerability.
