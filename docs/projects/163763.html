Project Summary

Focus population: Children affected by armed conflict and their caregivers
(including host communities, internally displaced, returnee, migrants and refugees).
Key interventions: 1. Community and school-based child protection and mental health and psychosocial support services, including identification and referral of vulnerable children (children on the move, children with disabilities, as well as rehabilitation and reintegration of children associated with armed groups and other vulnerable conflict affected and displaced children)
2. Specialized Child Protection services: Provision of specialized child protection services to survivors of all types of violence, including child survivors of GBV, and close collaboration with other sectors through established referral pathways and strengthening of child protection sub-sector coordination.
3. Awareness raising and increased capacity of local actors and community members on key child protection issues including child recruitment, GBV, discrimination and violence against children, explosive ordnance risk education, access to services that prevent and respond to violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect.
4. Skills development and capacity strengthening of frontline workers, social workers and other child protection services providers
5. Strengthening the monitoring, reporting and response to grave child rights violations.

1 - Needs

Ongoing conflict and displacement in Libya continue to present greater instability and negatively affect the mental health and psychosocial wellbeing of boys and girls, who are increasingly exposed to violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect (displaced children living in camps, collective shelters or with host families/communities as well as returnee children and families living in EO-contaminated areas among others). Violations of international human rights and humanitarian law and violence against children continue to be prevalent throughout Libya. According to HNO 2020, over 301,000 Libyans remain displaced, including 128,000 people due to the Tripoli conflict and 447,000 people are returning to their homes. Among the affected populations, children represent 45 per cent of the displaced population, 10 per cent of the migrant population, and 33 per cent of the refugee population .An estimated 160,006 children (70,560 girls and 89,446 boys) and 59,871 caregivers (28,297 females and 31,574 males) will require child protection assistance in 2020. When boys and girls experience or are exposed to violence, including but not limited to loss of or separation from family members and friends, GBV, risk of injury from explosive ordnance, use and recruitment, and other child protection violations, their mental health and psychosocial wellbeing is affected in a diverse way , including but not limited to delayed cognitive development, poor school performances and school dropout, with long-term consequences. Risks include reduced access to basic services, particularly health and education, deterioration in living conditions, presence of armed groups etc. Moreover, the presence and control of militias within communities in which children live, increases risk of association with armed groups.Children who have been exposed to armed conflict and instability (including children on the move) are in critical need of psychosocial support, safe environment and spaces to play, learn and be supported.Limited supportive child protection and family welfare services within communities, including scale up of focused services for vulnerable children affected by armed conflict, continue to be a major gap and need. Parents/caregivers are also affected, with conflict and displacement hampering their capacity to provide a safe environment and nurturing care for their children. A substantial investment on the Children Affected by Armed Conflict (CAAC) monitoring work is significantly needed to strengthen the monitoring, reporting and response to grave child rights violations. There is also a need to strengthening inter-agency coordination, leadership and advocacy in the area of child protection, and undertake child protection needs assessment.

5 - Mitigation measures

UNICEF will allocate designated emergency capacity to closely follow emerging trends and will build internal emergency readiness, in continuous consultation with conflict analysts. Enhance coordination mechanism and de-confliction through Humanitarian Access mechanism. Raise awareness of national and international partners on prospective risks and threats and undertake regular security risk analysis

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

UNICEF will ensure commitment to accountability to affected populations by ensuring transparent feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into project proposals, monitoring and evaluations, recruitment, staff inductions, trainings, partnerships et. This will ensure that the affected community accesses timely sufficient information, guidance, and support that enable the target population to understand and influence key decisions in the project to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices through active participation and facilitate a dialogue between the organization and its affected populations over information provision. The project activities will involve community leaders, parents and the project will work on getting feedback by the children. Focus Group Discussions will be carried out with the women, men, boys and girls ensuring gender considerations. Complaint mechanisms will be made available to the children and families as well. The action will be monitored and evaluated, through a direct monitoring conducted by implementing partners staff and the third-party monitoring.
