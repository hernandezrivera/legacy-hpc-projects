Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to improve access to legal identity and housing, land and property rights for returnees, displaced persons and host communities in Mayo Sava, Mayo Tsanaga and Logone and Chari division in Far North region of Cameroon.
NRC will assist beneficiaries to access civil and identity documentation, through the provision of information, counselling and legal assistance.
One of the main targets for these services will be children along with their parents who need birth certificates to sit public exams as well as people affected by displacement who meet obstacles to access civil and identity documents to enjoy freedom of movement and access to other rights and services.
NRC will reinforce its response and provide support to displaced populations and vulnerable host communities to access their HLP rights.
NRC will assist vulnerable individuals to obtain signed agreements from local authorities and traditional leaders (chief of villages) for beneficiaries at risk of forced eviction. Priority will be given to female heads of household, widows, displaced persons, and vulnerable or affected host community members. This process will contribute to the prevention of forced evictions.

Besoins / Needs

Access to legal identity : During NRC’s recent assessments in the far North Region conducted in January 2019, IDPs and affected communities reported lack of birth certificates for themselves and their children as a real challenge. For instance, according to the National Civil Status Registration Office (BUNEC), an estimated 400,000 pupils in the Far North do not have birth certificates and more than 41, 000 pupils in the last year of primary school will not be able to take national examinations during the academic year 2019/2020 due to lack of birth certificates. Moreover, according to a baseline study conducted in July/August 2019 by NRC in 20 localities of the Far North region, a very low rate of birth registration (around 31,5%) was reported. According to the same study, there is a significant gap between women and men as far as possessing identity and civil documents is concerned (23,2% for women and 42% for men). The reasons mostly given by respondents to explain the low birth registration rate and low possession of civil documents in the region are mainly the lack of financial means due to reduced income; the increased distance to civil registration centers as many have been closed due to the conflict so people have to go farther: as well as the limited knowledge on procedures and conditions to obtain legal and civil documentation by refugees and IDPs. Protection of House, Land and Property: According to a baseline conducted by NRC in August 2019 in 20 villages affected by displacement in the Far North Region, only 21% of displaced persons reported having secured access to farming land. Moreover, in those localities, conflicts between IDPs/refugees and host communities are frequent. According to the same baseline study, only 20,6% of people affected by displacement (IDPs and refugees) has access to land with a certain level of security of tenure, and 50 % of refugees reported having experienced HLP related conflicts with host communities. Another HLP issue in the Far North is related to military operations conducted by the Cameroon army against Boko Haram. In fact, trenches were dug by the army to refrain armed groups from entering the Cameroon territory and perpetrate atrocities. Those military operations affected many persons (around 7500 households) who were either evacuated from their homes or whose access to their farming lands was blocked. Challenges in relation to HLP rights during displacement and upon return can result in major conflicts among displaced and host communities, leading to disruption in social cohesion and escalating tensions.
