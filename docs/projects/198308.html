Project Summary

INTERSOS is a non-profit humanitarian organization that is committed to working at the front-line to bring humanitarian assistance to people in danger, victims of natural disasters and armed conflicts. INTERSOS has been implementing integrated multi-sector lifesaving and protection interventions in the proposed project sites since July 2017.
Through its proposed budget, INTERSOS will continue to manage the Reception centres and ensure safe relocation while decongesting dangerously overcrowded areas. Site Improvement and management is critical to Camp management agencies to ensure equitable access to services in the sites, this can be achieved through maximized coordination mechanism with service providers and key actors including, LGAs and communities. INTERSOS plans to facilitate this intervention considering all avenues that will enhance sites standards in all its sites. CCCM site management to enhance capacity of the community structures, response capacity to floods and fire outbreaks in congested sites would be a focus area in all the CCCM sites, Coordination with all key stakeholders would continue in all sites to ensure equitable access to services for beneficiaries.
INTERSOS will continue to strengthen its communication with communities to support the drive for Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), with a strong focus on making service delivery equitable and transparent, through regular community meetings with community representatives and service providers on service provision, site management and site planning in close coordination with local authorities. In addition, INTERSOS will ensure that space is given for AAP mechanisms such as information desks and the operation of a complaints and feedback mechanism.
Likewise, the protection project is designed to alleviate suffering, save lives and maintain human dignity with the aim to build the resilience of the community and promote local ownership so the benefits of the project are relevant beyond the emergency phase into development phase. Thus, engaging the community in planning, implementing and monitoring is a fundamental strategy to ensure that the project is guided by the identified needs and capacities. Gender-based violence is considered a public health issue and a human rights violation, which means that mitigation and response to GBV are among immediate life-saving priorities in emergencies and need to be integrated in all sectors of humanitarian response. Since the experience of GBV can be a distressing event, MHPSS will be a component intertwined to GBV response, prevention and risk mitigation and a part of care and support in this project.
The project will focus on provision of protection and multisectoral assistance to beneficiaries through capacity building, awareness and sensitization, sexual and gender-based violence related services, psychosocial support and material assistance. The protection interventions aim at providing the targeted population with quality services to respond to current protection needs but also contributing to strengthen their capacity for self-protection reinforcing positive coping mechanisms to stressful events.

1 - Needs

The prevailing security situation in the BAY states remains volatile, characterized by non-state armed groups (NSAG) attacks, the killing and abduction of civilians, destruction of property and widespread human rights abuses. The ongoing military counterinsurgency operations have also restricted the affected populations' freedom of movement. Given the pervasive insecurity, killings, abductions, illegal vehicular checkpoints, complex suicide attacks using improvised explosive devices (IEDs), incidents of detonation of unexploded ordnances (UXO) and explosive remnants of war (ERW), widespread human rights violations continue. NSAGs have destroyed essential utilities and public infrastructure, which has aggravated poverty and worsened the economic conditions of people in the BAY states. The crisis coupled with the COVID pandemic, has compounded the vulnerabilities of the displaced men, women, girls, and boys resulting in massive unmet needs. A combination of the above factors has triggered new displacements. In the BAY states, a total of 8.4 million people in need of protection and humanitarian assistance according to the Northeast 2022 Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview. This includes 2.2 million IDPs, 1.5 million IDPs, 3.9 million host community members, and 700,000 individuals in areas not accessible to humanitarian actors. Of the total persons in need, 80% are women and children. The global percentage of persons with disabilities is 15% and is expected to be higher in locations with conflict. Despite COVID-19 and related restrictions, movements across the borders continue to be recorded. From January 2022 to June 2022, over 13,000 spontaneous Nigerian refugee returnees were registered with 63% returning from Niger, 36% from Cameroon and 1% from Chad. The closure of official camps in Maiduguri municipality by the Borno State Government has resulted in secondary and tertiary displacement which an upwards of 150,000 IDPs in Maiduguri will be affected. According to the CCCM Sector’s analysis in the HNO 2022 for Northeast Nigeria, high levels of congestion are an ongoing challenge in displacement settings in the BAY states, including in government buildings allocated to shelter IDPs and reception centers. The average available area per individual is between 6-24m2, well below the Sphere standard of 45m2. IDPs residing in camps and host community settlements continue to experience limited access to basic services, crowded living conditions, significant protection risks, and inadequate shelter and WASH facilities. The destruction of shelters by strong winds, flash flooding, heavy rains and outbreaks of fire have further deteriorated the situation. Without minimal coordination and management structures in place, people in these displacement locations, especially in hard-to-reach areas, will have limited access to CCCM services and unless these gaps are addressed, the protective environment of camp and camp-like settings will continue to deteriorate, resulting in aggravated risks and abuse. To respond to the CCCM needs, UNHCR will provide CCCM services and activities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project team will continue to assess the situation and respond to immediate needs among vulnerable groups, especially PLW, in the areas of intervention. INTERSOS has a strong reputation on protection and GBV prevention and risk mitigation, as supported by the international protection coordinator and GBV/PSS specialist. In addition, INTERSOS protection staff work alongside the health, WASH and nutrition teams to ensure mainstreaming of protection and GBV issues across all activities. Furthermore, all INTERSOS staff is bounded to INTERSOS Code of Ethics, an official document that sets the rights, duties and responsibilities for all INTERSOS operators, collaborators, partners, advisers and members of the statutory bodies to which it is addressed. It also reaffirms the observance of values and ethical principles common to every human being, such as honesty, loyalty, probity and those pertaining to its nature as a humanitarian organization: humanitarian relief, neutrality, impartiality, independence, non-discrimination, transparency. Moreover, protection will be mainstreamed in this project as per the following consolidated procedures:- Prioritizing safety and dignity, and avoiding causing harm, by preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects of activities;- Ensuring access, by arranging for beneficiaries’ easy access to assistance and services, and paying special attention to individuals who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services;- Participation and empowerment, by supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assisting beneficiaries to claim their rights through their full involvement in activities.

9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

INTERSOS embraces participatory community-led approaches, that involve target beneficiaries and key local leaders and influential persons to understand the project targeting criteria as well as expected contributions from the community, for instance social capital and indigenous knowledge. Community entry messaging include information like source of funding (donor), selection criteria, targeting criteria and expected humanitarian support among others. As part of the CHS commitments, INTERSOS establishes functional complaint and feedback mechanisms in areas of project interventions. A compliant help desks are set up in INTERSOS supported safe space (funded by OFDA) where people can report their complaints or give feedback about the project. Beneficiaries feedback will be gathered through several sources, including: face to face with field staff, WhatsApp, suggestion boxes, help desk, and email. Feedback received from adults and children in the community is gathered and organized by project and sector. This information is sent to the INTERSOS office in Maiduguri, where it is added to the Complaints Response Mechanism Register and referred to the most relevant person. In addition to the aforementioned set-up, the community-based approach, through active involvement of men, women, boys and girls, is at the base of the project design. Since the onset of INTERSOS action in the region, it has become well-known and respected within the targeted communities, positively engaging with both the hosting and IDP communities. INTERSOS conducts regular focus group discussions with target beneficiaries to improve its programming, taking into consideration the suggestions coming directly from the affected communities.
