Project Summary

This project is aligning with cluster objectives that aiming to support newly displaced individuals in open spaces, public buildings, and camps, as well as those residing with friends/relatives, including populations in transit at border points or within the country with emergency shelter and basic NFIs, mitigate the negative impact of hazards, and ensure preparedness to people affected by crisis, ensure people affected by crisis receives timely shelter solutions and NFI assistance to reduce mortality and morbidity, mitigate health threats, and improve living conditions, and to improve access to shelter solutions for volunerable people in order to support the transition to recovery. The project will target 114,132 (30268 girls, 29081 boys, 28487 women, and 26296 men) individuals including 1911 elderly and 490 people with disabilities in need living in 37 localities within 13 Save the Children priority states including North Kordofan, North Darfur, South Kordofan, Centeral Darfur, West Darfur, Khartoum, Gazira, Red Sea, Gedaref, River Nile, White Nile, Sennar, Blue Nile, and West Kordofan. The project will be focusing in mitigating the negative impact of hazards through community sensitization, capacity building and training for affected communities to reduce the impact of hazards, provision of timely Shelter solutions and NFI assistance for new and protracted displacement people to reduce mortality and morbidity, mitigate health threats, and improve living conditions through construction of shelter accompanied with essential NFI items, or distribution of life-saving emergency shelter items and materials in addition to life-saving non-food items, shelter repairs, as well as provision of cash/voucher for NFIs, and Shelter, in addition to cash for rent. The project will also focus on supporting transition, recovery and reintegration of returnees, volunerable and protracted households, and protracted IDPs through provision of transitional shelter items, cash for shelter, cash for non-food items, more over the project will be supporting prepositioning of NFIs and shelter items and materials to support timely response for anticipated and potential hazards. The project will work to ensure cross cutting issues have been addressed through ensuring households have sufficient living space, in addition to ensure as part of accountability to affected populations; complains and feedback mechanisms are in place. The project will be implemented in 12 months with estimated budget at $10,524,351.

1 - Needs

As per Humanitarian Response Plan 2021, EN-Shelter sector estimates that about 1.9 million people need NFIs and shelter solutions. Blue, Nile, Centeral, East, West, South, and North Darfur, South and West Kordofan shows very acute as well as chronic Shelter and NFIs needs sever (5) and extremely severe (4) on severity scale) with 43 localities (22%) of total localities, states like Khartoum, Gazira, Sinnar, Blue Nile, Gedaref, and Kassala remain volunerable to potential hazards such as floods. However armed conflicts declined since 2015, and the number of new displaced people significantly reduces, Shelter and NFI needs and assistants have increased during 2020 onward, the ongoing economic crisis, seasonal flooding and COVID-19 pandemic are the main factors and key drivers of deteriorating humanitarian needs, and increasing the cost and time taken to delivering shelter and NFI assistance.Unprecedented levels of rainfall began in Sudan in July and have continued to adversely affect population across Sudan Blue Nile, River Nile, and Sennar are amongst the most affected states, while floods have also affected vulnerable populations in Red Sea. Nearly 875,000 people have had their houses damaged or destroyed and over 150 died, and they need emergency shelter and NFIs.Around 1.2 M including protracted IDPs in camps around towns in Centeral, North, South and West Darfur, and South Kordofan, in addition to around 335140 returnees are in need for durable solution and urban integration through provision of environmentally friendly transition shelters, accompanied with training on building techniques and livelihood opportunities to promote self-reliance and resilience, adding to that around 350039 volunerable residents including pregnant and lactating women, people with disabilities, chronically ill, elders and unaccompanied people are also need for NFIs and shelter replenishment or upgrading. Clashes between SAF and RSF that broke out on 15 April 2023 have continued for five consecutive weeks, especially in and around Khartoum, as of 20 May. At least 705 people have been killed and 5,287 injured, according to the Federal Ministry of Health (FMoH), as of 16 May. Over 1 million people have been displaced since the conflict began on 15 April and have fled to safer locations inside and outside the country (OCHA Flash update 21 May 2023). fighting has escalated in Ag Geneina, the capital of West Darfur. All 86 gathering sites for displaced people throughout Ag Geneina have reportedly been burnt to the ground, forcing over 85,000 people into secondary displacement in neighborhoods in the northern parts of the town. Shelter and Non-Food Item (NFI) needs were already acute, exacerbated by significant underfunding of the Cluster. Underserved communities, forced to depend on their sparse resources for reconstruction, inadvertently amplified existing vulnerabilities and exposure to recurring hazards. PiN for shelter solutions and NFIs assistance have increased by 90% from 3 million in the beginning of the year to 5.7 million since the beginning of the crises. Most families in Khartoum are not facing issue with shelter or NFIs in the current situation although few households experienced partial damage to their houses due to the shelling. Most of families in North Darfur lost their household items due to looting that happened during their absence and many are currently in need of essential household/non-food items for instance cooking utensils, plastic mats and mattresses etc. All the gathering points in Genina town were completely burned down and all IDPs have fled to different locations and currently without shelter. Some are using some governmental offices and humanitarian organization premises as shelter. The people in need for shelter and NFIs estimates more than 194,593 are in need for shelter and NFIs support.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

SC will work on medium impact on environment with mitigation or enhancement to ensure that the Proposed activities will have no negative impact on the environment. The project will contribute to resilience and a sustainable use of natural resources through involving and encouraging children and local communities to be part of planning mitigation measures and better environmental preservation. The project will respond to the basic and life-saving needs of IDPs, returnees and host communities while increasing their self-reliance. Environment will be considered as an integral component for this project, and a number of factors will be essential for improving the humanitarian response such as coordination with different actors at the communities, prioritization and awareness raising amongst practitioners, and effective policy frame work. The mitigation plan will include safe and secure sites selection, such as those at minimum risk from natural disasters, assessing the infrastructure and services such as sanitation, water, drainage and ensure adequate availability. SCI will conduct rapid assessment for environmental impact of construction materials, harvesting and manufacturing to ensure environment dose not impacted because of our actions. The project will work to ensure communities have been supported with maximum number of reusable materials to reduce continues purchasing or harming the environment. Using of local materials should be used in preference to imported materials when they are available in sufficient quantities and at appropriate costs. Sites will be as safe as possible and free of harmful elements such as UXOs.The frame will be designed to resist hazards, especially from wind loads, additional strengthening at joints may be added as required. Walls, roofs (structure) will be selected an designed to ensure maximum protection during winds, to be light as maximum possible and water proof. Materials used will minimize flammability and flame spread.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project designed to consider protection issues, concerns and the needs of women, men, girls and boys, in addition to Persons with Special Needs (PSN) and other vulnerable groups. Shelters will be designs in consultation with women as women in most cases are responsible for erecting and taking down traditional shelters. Shelters should therefore be designed in consultation with women, in addition to assessing transport of materials from distribution site to site of shelters. The project will assess households with disabled persons or pregnant women and identify any need for adjustment of shelter materials. Exploitation and S/GBV can be serious threats in IDP settlements; SCI will advocate for or provide fixed lighting, flashlights and/or whistles to increase personal safety. Save the Children will work to raise the awareness of community leaders on the importance of including marginalized groups, including unaccompanied children, the chronically ill or disabled, and child/elderly/disabled and vulnerable single headed households. Work with community structures to identify these people within the community and ensure their inclusion. During data collection, registration and verification process, SCI will be collecting data on age and sex of household head, presence of unrelated children or individuals with chronic illness or disability, in addition to analyzing the collected data to ensure whether or not particularly vulnerable children and households are being reached.The project will ensure adherance to child safe guarding protection, and core humanitarian principles during implementation of the project to ensure all community members and especially children were protected, not harmed by our actions. The project considers conflict sensitivity by understanding the situation, examining how each activity may influence the conflict and work to maximize the positive and minimize the negative impacts of the proposed interventions. Both the newly IDPs and host communities will benefit from the project interventions. Save the Children will strive to ensure that the proposed interventions will minimize unforeseen or unintended negative effects on the targeted population which could potentially expose them to further physical and psychosocial risks. Save the Children will work closely with its various stakeholders (community members, government partners, local NGOs) to analyze the security situation and to avoid tensions between host communities and IDPs through its holistic approach to projects whereby both old and new IDPs as well as host communities are targeted based on the prioritization of needs.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Stakeholders’ participation and accountability to affected people is part of SCI’s core values. In practice, our participation and accountability approaches covers the following: • Regular, timely and accessible information sharing • Participation – discussing together, deciding together and working together • Proactively seeking feedback from children and communities and handling their complaints • Generate data and evidence for continuous learning SCI is signatory to Core Humanitarian Standard, and considering this SCI Sudan has already established strong accountability mechanisms which include three critical components of information sharing, participation, and complaints and feedback for the targeted communities and children. SCI will pprovide regular, timely and accessible information to targeted communities about: • Who we are and the principles we adhere to; • Project interventions and what SCI intends to deliver (e.g. what people are entitled to receive and when); • Our staff and how we expect our staff to behave (including our Child Safeguarding policy and Code of Conduct); • How communities can be involved. This information will be communicated in the local language and medium that are easily understood, respectful and culturally appropriate for different members of the targeted communities, especially vulnerable and marginalized groups. To ensure accountability to the beneficiaries during the consultation phase, SCI has will consult and engage the existing community structures/bodies (traditional leaders, representatives from IDPs, returnees, refugees, host communities, youth groups, women groups, and local government authorities. During the implementation phase, SCI will ensure that targeted communities have been provided with opportunities to express their views and influence decision-making through discussing together, deciding together and working together. Particular attention will be given to the vulnerable groups to enable for active participation in the implementation of the project activities. Key stakeholders such as partners and the community (women, men, boys, and girls of diverse backgrounds; beneficiaries' committees; and local authorities) will be involved in the monitoring of the project activities. SCI Sudan will establish a Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) with multiple community friendly channels. This will include both reactive (hotlines, suggestions box) and proactive (community satisfaction consultations, focus group discussions) channels. The complaints and feedback channels introduced in the projects will allow targeted communities and children to safely and confidentially report serious complaints (including child safeguarding concerns, sexual abuse and exploitation concerns and breaches of the code of conduct). Any serious complaints will be immediately elevated to the designated focal point (e.g. child safeguarding focal point, HR Manager, Director for Program Development & Quality, Country Director
