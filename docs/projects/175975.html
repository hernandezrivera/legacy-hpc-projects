Project Summary

This project will focus on increasing the ability for populations to access safe water supply and to improve hygiene and sanitation practices through context-specific approaches that take into consideration the limited access for sustained material support and the potential for future displacement of the population. More specifically this, medium ¬term emergency WASH intervention will target to increase the basic access to WASH services through improving the ability of populations to treat and store drinking water and increase the hygiene practices through a combined approach of age and gender-specific messaging and community mobilization campaigns. This project will work through a gender-sensitive approach to improve access to safe water sources for the communities by rehabilitating boreholes and training gender¬ balanced water user committees and technicians for future repairs and sustainability. This will improve water supply, sanitation, and hygiene behavior for the affected populations and host families and reduce waterborne diseases.
High open defecation rates also expose women, children, and people living with disabilities to a higher risk of violence and discrimination, particularly in IDP sites and other crowded settlements. Sanitation will be improved through the construction or rehabilitation of emergency latrines and alternative measures for sanitation depending on the geography of the area will be encouraged.
The project, therefore, intends to contribute to safe, equitable, and dignified access to WASH services through the provision of lifesaving integrated WASH services to vulnerable populations, especially women, girls, men, and boys through a flexible, responsive and synergistic approach.
This project will be targeting 40,000 individuals (Male: 4,800, Female: 9,600, Boys: 11,200, and Girls: 14,400) in Warrap and Lakes State. 15% of the target population (6,000 individuals) will be Persons with Special Needs.
Prior to project implementation, CCOSS will conduct, focus group discussions (FGD) with women, men, boys, and girls at targeted community settlements will be conducted to get feedback on design, implementation, and monitoring of sanitation facilities, ensuring safety and mitigation of Protection / GBV risks, especially for females. These FGDs will also be utilized to trigger community action towards ending open defecation.
The project will be achieved through the following interventions
• Conduct a needs assessment to assess the felt needs and identify the most vulnerable affected population for targeting.
• Rehabilitation or Construction of two-stance block latrines at key health facilities, market places, and crowded settlement areas.
• Rehabilitation of non-functioning boreholes
• Clean- up campaigns to improve environmental sanitation at schools, health facilities, market centers, and settlement areas since these are key areas where people gather and transmission of infectious diseases such as cholera is at a heightened risk.
• Conduct hygiene awareness campaigns each month of implementation to improve hygiene practices and health-seeking behavior for WASH-related infectious diseases. The awareness shall also provide knowledge on appropriate behaviors to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases and COVID-19 and encourage good hygiene practices.
• Train community hygiene promoters (60% of them women) on hygiene awareness and promotion. These hygiene promoters will be identified and recruited through a participatory approach with the community members playing a central role.
• Assess and identify at least 2000 most vulnerable households for distribution of WASH non-food items (NFIs) such as jerry cans, buckets, PUR tabs, and soap.
• Assess and identify women and girls of menstrual age for distribution of MHM kits. Criteria for identification will include low-income girls in schools of adolescent age and women, unaccompanied or orphaned girls.

1 - Needs

Warrap and Lakes state has remained a priority location since the start of the conflict and continues to have a significant IDP population and affected host population that remains vulnerable under the current circumstances. The convergence of multiple shocks in already vulnerable areas in 2020 and 2021 could lead to severe outcomes. Crucial information gaps remain a challenge, with poor access to many parts of Warrap and Lakes due to insecurity and inadequate infrastructure. Warrap and Lakes states have continued to remain one of the most difficult locations especially for humanitarian agencies to operate. This is due to drivers and multipliers of crises that have remained present over time. These include insecurity and violence, local and inter-communal violence (IVC), ongoing displacement, sparse basic services, disease, climate shocks, flooding, economic instability, and insecure access to food and livelihoods. Warrap and Lakes state broadly face myriad of WASH-related challenges emanating from accessibility challenges by partners, the influx of IDPs as a result of forced displacement due to cattle raids from neighboring Cuiebet that spills over the Warrap State border into Manyangok Payam. The majority of residents drink water from unprotected sources, almost no existence of pit latrines thus open defecation, while acute watery diarrhea is the main health challenge. Open defecation is a common practice of disposal of human excreta and the community knowledge on good hygiene practice is very low. This situation has exposed the populations to a high-level risk of vulnerability to water-related illnesses. Having to defecate in the open undermines the dignity and safety of women, children, and people living with disabilities, and poses them to high risk of violence and discrimination. During the rainy season, most counties in Warrap (Tonj East and Tonj South), as well as Cueibet in Lakes, are affected by heavy and flash floods which continue to strain access to the very limited basic resources. Flood-induced displacement limits access to WASH infrastructure and places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites. The limited access to WASH services impacts people across all population groups, but the needs are the most severe among the newly displaced population, and in areas hosting recently returned refugees and IDPs According to the IPC report for October 2020 – July 2021; in the lean season of April to July 2021, the food security situation will deteriorate in Warrap state and an estimated 13,000 people will likely be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Tonj North County. During this period, all counties are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, except for Twic County which is classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. This deterioration will be driven by the seasonal depletion of food stocks, likelihood of high food prices, reduced market functionality, and access challenges occasioned by the onset of rainfall that leads to the worsening of road conditions, as well as the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, economic shocks and currency devaluation that will continue to erode households’ purchasing power. IDPs and Host Communities in Cueibet, Tonj East, and Tonj South have the highest severity of needs. Within these population groups, some of the vulnerable groups that may have specific needs include children, women at risk, the elderly, people with disabilities, single-headed household members, and the extremely poor. High WASH severity can be attributed to a heavy reliance on surface water, lacking sanitation facilities, and the repeated presence of insecurity in these locations. Women and girls face an increased risk of harassment, assault, and sexual violence when collecting water, using communal latrines, and accessing menstrual hygiene products. Additionally, appropriate and dignified washing locations remain critical needs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

A complaint/feedback mechanism will be agreed upon and established prior to the commencement of the project. Monitoring of the complaints and remedial action will be documented during the project implementation cycle. This will be done with the goal of promoting community engagement and accountability. Community participation in the planning, implementation, and monitoring of the post-distribution will also be solicited. For instance, the community will be involved in the identification and selection of community volunteers to help in distribution among others. After the completion of the distribution, a monitoring team will be sent to visit selected samples of beneficiaries to confirm the actual quantity received and the quality of the relief commodities, as well as to collect further feedback from beneficiaries. Once complaints are received, CCOSS will look for the most appropriate way to respond to the complaints Complaint and Feedback desks will be set in strategic places in the community. Some of these desks will be manned by some community members who will assist other community members about the issues they want to raise. Illiterate community members will be assisted with those who are able to read and right by documenting their complaints and feedback. Households with members with disabilities will be visited and their complaints and feedback received. The project will ensure that Complaint and feedback mechanism and integration of vulnerability (disability, PSEA and Old age) is achieved through the following means;• Communities/users exercise their voice through elections of those to be involved in the WASH services such as Water Management Committees • Improving access to information (explanation and justification) and participation of users in the planning, monitoring, and management of services;• Specific gendered needs of women and men, and boys and girls are taken into account in the design and location of the facilities (access, privacy, safety, menstrual hygiene management) such as raised platforms and ramps at water points to ensure safe manual lifting to all, especially children, pregnant women, and persons with disabilities.• Establishing a referral pathway for GBV survivors including promoting confidentiality and ensuring anonymity of survivors.• Considering that most often women and children collect water, talk to them directly about the safety of the location and the routes to the location of boreholes to be rehabilitated, and accommodate their specific needs or suggestions. PSEA compliance:• CCOSS has already trained the staff on Do-No-Harm, Referral pathway, GBV, PSEA, and they have committed through signing to and adhere to a code of conduct that respects and upholds the rights of beneficiaries and PSEA• CCOSS will also train volunteers, some representatives from community groups and enumerators on Do-No-Harm, Referral pathway, GBV, PSEA and they will commit through signing to and adhere to code of conduct• Our project team will ensure that the distribution locations are not located close to potential threats such close to pools of water, near bushes that will cause risk or threats of gender-based violence by conducting safety audits • People with special needs such as PLWs, elderly, disabled, women with small children will be given priority during distributions or creating a separate line (queue) for them

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

WASH needs are primarily linked to conflict-induced displacement, but at times also to food insecurity, economic deterioration, and disease outbreaks. Poor access to clean water, hygiene and sanitation services combined with high levels of food insecurity compromises people’s health and nutrition conditions. The continued and protracted macro-economic crisis, accompanied by currency depreciation, has also resulted in high food prices, eroded household purchasing power and made food unaffordable for a significant proportion of the population. The project will target populations under IPC 3/4/5 to help them cushion against adverse food insecurity through accessing basic WASH services.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

This project will largely ensure integration with other clusters such as ES/NFIs, Food Livelihoods and Security, Health and Protection as the needs of the target communities go beyond the WASH. CCOSS will work closely with other partners implementing ES/NFIs, FSL, Protection, Nutrition, and Health activities to enhance multi-dimensional needs beyond WASH. This will ensure a complementary approach by all actors thus saving time on individual needs assessments, or distributions, thus saving time. It also ensures proper coordination among key actors targeting the same beneficiaries

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Those to be targeted include women and children who will remain especially vulnerable due to their roles in society and the protection risks inherent in being without shelter or social support networks. The project will ensure children under age 5 benefit through the WASH NFIs that will be distributed. They will also benefit from access to safe drinking water through the boreholes that will be rehabilitated.
