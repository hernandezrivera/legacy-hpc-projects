Project Summary

The project will contribute to the HRP strategic objectives 3, and the Food Security cluster objective 1 in the WB, by improving access to sustainable livelihoods for farmers working inside the seam zone of Qalqilya and Tulkarm. It will do so by increasing the efficiency of agricultural water wells and irrigation networks inside the targeted seam zone area. The farmers inside the seam zone face specific challenges to cultivate their lands due to the different measures imposed by the occupation regime, which involve access restriction, settlement expansion, demolition, confiscation, limitation on goods transfer, etc.
The project is based on an a rapid needs assessment conducted by PUI in October 2022, which identified the most pressing needs of vulnerable farmers working in the seam zone of Northern Qalqilya and Southern Tulkarm. Additionally, PUI coordinated with FSS, MoA Central and MoA Directorates of Qalqilya and Tulkarm, in order to identify and prioritize FSS needs and responses that could be provided in the area. In order to respond to the identified needs and to contribute to the HRP and cluster objectives, PUI and Al’Ard will provide 2 types of support.
• Support the connection of 4 water wells inside the seam zone with power supply for a capacity of 0.5 Megawatt (MW) / around 543 Ampere.
• Additionally, the project will provide 5 water storages / reservoirs (500m3 each). Each will be connected to one of the water wells. This will benefit 270 farmers directly in addition to 500 workers inside the seam zone.
In total, 270 farmers’ households (1,269 individuals; 254 boys, 254 girls, 411 men, 350 women; 63 elderly; 51 PwD), as well as 500 workers (75 women and 425 men) will benefit directly from the action.
The project will be implemented in partnership with Al’Ard, each organization bringing its specific expertise: Al’Ard will focus on water activities with the support of reservoirs construction, while PUI will ensure coordination with authorities needed to carry out the provision of power supply for the water wells, building on its relationship with Palestinian District Coordination and Liaison offices (PDCL) and other Palestinian Authority entities. Furthermore, the project will complement the action of the West Bank Protection Consortium (WBPC) by supporting the resilience of farmers through investing in their livelihood inside the seam zone. As such, PUI will mobilize its Consortium experience (tools, methodology, network) at different stages of the project.

1 - Needs

One of the major occupation regime measures affecting Palestinian livelihoods is the separation wall. The wall that was established in 2002 isolates 10% of the West Bank inside the seam zone. The wall separates and surrounds 32 villages, around 11,000 Palestinians and cuts off farmers from 150 communities from their land. Farmer’s access to their lands is restricted by gates, 11 of which that open on daily basis, 10 that open once a week, and/or 53 that open only during specific seasons. Thousands of Palestinians living in these closed areas, or needing access for agricultural use, require specific permits to access this zone. The number of permits granted is decreasing continuously and significantly each year. 73% of requested permits to access the seam zone in 2020 were rejected compared to 63% in 2019. PUI conducted a rapid needs assessment through FGD with landowners and artesian wells’ owners inside the seam zone area at the junction of Qalqiliya and Tulkarm governorates in October 2022, to assess the agricultural needs for this marginalized area, and to discuss the possible interventions to enhance their resilience and sustain their livelihood sources. It indicated that 270 farmers from Qalqiliya (Qalqiliya, Falamiya, Jayyous, and Sir) and Tulkarm (Kafr Jammal, Kafr Zebad, and Kafr ‘Abbush) rely on farming activities in their lands inside the seam zone area as their main source of income. Farmers and all their family members (1,269 individuals; 254 boys, 254 girls, 411 men, 350 women; 63 elderly; 51 PwD) should access their lands to protect them and in order not to lose their permission to access the land. In addition to the 270 farming households, around 500 daily workers (75 women, 425 men) are working in farming activities in these lands and asking for permission to access the farming lands. As a consequence of this imperative of going on the land regularly in order not to lose access, all HH members able to work are usually mobilized for farming activities, including women, girls, boys, elder persons and sometimes persons with disabilities (PwD). The condition of agricultural roads makes many of them impossible to travel by car, and sometimes even by animal cart, implying that family members have to walk significant distances every day, sometimes carrying heavy loads of farming material. This increased hardship bears significant risks for individuals with specific needs in terms of health (notably for PwDs, older and chronically ill persons) as well as protection (i.e. increased GBV risks for women and girls on the roads).Based on discussions with active farmers in the location, there are around 5,000 dunums of land inside the seam zone, of which 2,000 dunums are rain-fed, 2,000 dunums are irrigated by 5 artesian wells, and 1,000 dunums of unused lands. Out of the 5 artesian wells, only one well is connected to the electricity grid, and the other 4 wells are operated by electricity diesel generators. There are around 8 km of main agricultural roads and more than 8 km of subsidiary agricultural roads in need of rehabilitation. Moreover, there is a need to open and construct 3 km of agricultural road to reach at least 500 dunums of land that will benefit at least 40 farmers. These roads are vital to facilitate reaching the land for all family members and take the required tools and agricultural assets and plants to the land using vehicles. The average agricultural holding is 15 dunums per farming household; the lands are productive and fertile, cultivated with Citrus, Sugar apple, Guava, Avocado, Thyme, Strawberry, and vegetable greenhouses. The farmers’ main need is water accessibility. Indeed, the water is available inside the seam zone from five artesian wells, and each well can produce an average of 80 m3/hour, which should be enough in normal cases, but this is not the case due to:1. Punctures in water carrier line; the continuous exposure to high water pressure on the irrigation network shortens the life span of the water irrigation network pipes and causes a lot of water losses. 2. Farmers cannot irrigate at night due to permission restrictions from the Israeli’ side, which implies that the artesian well is not working for 24 hours.3. The continuous malfunctioning of the diesel generator interrupts the irrigation process. It is worth mentioning that one of the water generators has been out of order for one month and still waiting for maintenance. 4. The water vendors sell the water to the farmers by the hour and not by cubic meters, therefore, some farmers are forced to buy more water than they need, irrigate the crops over the saturation point, or discharge the excess water into unused lands.The following needs were highlighted by the farmers as their main priorities:1. Switch the source of power for 4 artesian wells from diesel generators to electricity grid, reducing the cost of 1 hour of pumping water from 140 NIS to 60 NIS. This will also ensure water availability at all times. 2. Construction of 5 corrugated steel water reservoirs of 500 m3 each - one per artesian well and provision of water carrier lines. 3. Rehabilitating agricultural roads (8 km) to facilitate farmers’ access to their lands, and thus reduce hardship of work, as well as protection and health risks for most fragile family members and those with specific needs. 4. Internal water networks for farmers to enhance farming activities and rehabilitate unused lands. The main challenge for the implementation of these activities lies in the high cost of implementation, in addition to coordination and work permission to be obtained from Israeli authorities. Close coordination with MoA at central and directory level shall be put in place during the design and implementation of this project, in parallel with regular follow up and close cooperation with FSS cluster to ensure no overlapping or duplication of assistance.

2 - Activities

• Activity 1: Preparatory work including advocacy / lobbying campaign should be initiated at the beginning of the project, given the type of intervention and sensitive location of this project. This shall include the support of the humanitarian coordinator, UN relevant agencies, ICRC, PDCL, and other stakeholders which might help in the lobbying efforts to ensure the access to the seam zone, and the permission to carry out the proposed activities. This will set the ground for the other two activities to be implemented smoothly and as planned in the project. • Activity 2: Connect 4 artisan water wells inside the seam zone with electricity power supply to reduce the running cost of using diesel to operate the water pumps attached to the wells. The current operating modality cost around 140 ILS for water pumping per hour, which will be reduced 60 ILS with electricity power supply. This 40% of reduction in running cost for water use will eventually boost the agriculture business inside the seam zone and encourage farmers to invest more money in agricultural assets to reinforce their livelihood activity. PUI & Al’Ard shall ensure coordination with and between different stakeholders to ensure the implementation of this activity. PDCL will be the counterpart of Israeli Civil Administration (ICA) to follow up all the access issues. MoA will be the umbrella for farmers and the final owner of the infrastructure. Qalqilya and Tulkarm Municipalities will be the counterparts with the support of the Palestinian Power Authority against the Israeli electricity supplier. • Activity 3: Construction of 5 reservoirs with 500 cubic meters each (corrugated steel reservoir) and the provision of pipelines connecting them with water wells and fittings. The objective of this activity is to enhance the irrigation to trees and crops inside the seam zone. The construction of reservoir will increase the water distribution efficiency, as all farmers benefiting from the same well will be connected to water source from the reservoir at the same time taking their exact needs of water similar to the domestic water network. Contrary to the current modality where farmers have the water coming directly from well where they have to wait for their turn during the day based on the allocation plan. Farmers in the current setting loose water, as the connection is only available per the hour, while their needs may sometime be less than that. That means that the extra amount of water will be wasted in vain. Another important factor of the construction of these reservoirs is the fact that farmers will have more flexibility to use the water in their preferred time. They will no longer be subjected to the time set by the well administrator who allocates the time per farmer, which might not be convenient for all farmers (i.e. when the turn of one farmer comes during the night hours). This system would increase the pumps efficiency by concentrating the pumping process in few hours toward the reservoir instead of making it function for longer hours if pumping towards lands of farmers. Further, benefit of this activity, will be to encourage farmers to use smart irrigation technology solutions and irrigate their farms in the proper timing of the day/night. The West Bank Protection Consortium project will complement those two activities with the rehabilitation of agricultural roads inside this area of the seam zone during October 2022. This will respond to the identified needs during the needs assessment exercise. All activities will take into consideration specific age, gender and diversity (AGD) needs and opportunities by consulting in an accessible and appropriate manner diverse community members. Men, Women, PwDs, and youth should be represented in the selection committees to be consulted and part of the decision process, as well as to mitigate any possible risks associated with the assistance itself. This action will encourage the participation of people from different AGD groups and promote equal access to productive resources and joint decision-making. The methodology of working with entire HH instead of only with HH representatives will be adopted. Additionally women, PwDs and people belonging to different AGD groups will actively be called upon to apply for rehabilitation activities and state their specific needs. They will be prioritized through the scoring system used for beneficiary selection.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PUI’s feedback and response mechanism (FRM) is systematically capturing the views and feedback from affected people to ensure they inform decision-making, and close the “feedback loop”. The MEAL team will strive to ensure that feedback, whether positive or negative, will collected and followed up on by PUI staff independent from project team and be dealt with respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. All relevant feedback and recommendations will be shared with the project manager and relevant staff for improvement. Feedback received through the FRM will be documented and shared in writing, via email, between the MEAL Manager and the program team (in a secured and confidential manner). Decisions and steps taken will be duly reported as well, until the closure of the case. More informal feedback received by any PUI staff in the field will be dealt with depending on its nature: either through oral response to complainants, or through the opening of dedicated case file, as described above. MEAL department will provide monthly anonymous FRM report to support the project quality check, and participate in the close up meeting to integrate feedback for further actions. The yearly strategy exercises will also be the opportunity to discuss and integrate received feedback into future programming.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

PUI has developed a country level Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Action Plan, which is updated yearly, to reflect on the type of actions required on each stage of the project cycle to ensure protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. Based on the Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Action Plan, a project specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming action plan will be developed at the beginning of the Action. Some of the key intervention areas will be: • A protection and gender risk and mitigation measures analysis for the project, with the solicitation of direct feedback from the target community, regarding the perceived protection risks, needs and capacity. The analysis will have special focus on the risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and challenges for people with disability. According to the initial Protection and Gender risk analysis, project specific and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identified risks and gaps. • Community consultation, through different means (FGDs, Key informants Interviews (KIIs), surveys, etc.) will be conducted throughout the project implementation to monitor the progress of the mitigation measures, as well as to detect any new or emerging protection concerns. All tools, SOPs and materials used during each of the project steps will include protection mainstreaming considerations. Protection and Gender Mainstreaming indicator(s) will be measured and monitored regularly through different data collection sources (observations, consultations, FRM, etc.). • SEAH is a GBV issue that might negatively impact all aspects of protection mainstreaming and have lasting consequences in the lives of survivors. To ensure PSEAH, PUI has and is currently further strengthening its SEAH reporting mechanism. The reporting mechanism is accessible through different channels for beneficiaries, staff and other stakeholders. PSEAH training has been provided in 2021 or 2022 to all staff members. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEAH will also be disseminated to project beneficiaries. • Project beneficiaries with or without specific vulnerabilities in need of more specialized protection or psychosocial support (PSS) services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed (i.e. the Protection Cluster Service Directory) and complemented by other resources identified by PUI if needed. Furthermore, all PUI field staff have been trained in 2022 on a “Safe Protection Referrals” training module, to include “Communicating and responding to GBV disclosures”. • A protection capacity building plan will be drafted action onset, to reflect on the needs identified during the initial Protection and Gender Risks Analysis, as well as subsequent consultations with staff and beneficiaries. Some on the topics to be included in the capacity building plan will be: Protection, age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, GBV, child protection, psychological first aid, safe referrals, PSEA and child safeguarding, and inclusion of people with disabilities. • To ensure all protection and gender mainstreaming activities are in the forefront of the project, selected field staff members, both from PUI and from its implementing partner, will be trained and mentored to act as protection mainstreaming focal points. They will be tasked with specific protection and gender mainstreaming activities, while also raising the awareness of other staff members and beneficiaries on protection related issues.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through the use of interviews, focus groups and surveys, PUI aims to involve vulnerable groups and people with specific needs in all aspects of the project cycle and promote the general concept of age and gender diversity (AGD) in selecting the beneficiaries (BNF). PUI will also ensure assessment methodology complies to the standards and includes protection considerations; put additional effort to include representative from all different groups within the targeted community; ensure the assessment tools are accessible for different groups of people (for instance by including the Washington Group Short Set of Questions on disabilities in its questionnaires to gather information on barriers, needs and priorities); conduct vulnerability mapping and socioeconomic survey to all the potential BNFs, and besides promotes the Disability Mainstreaming through the project’s cycle. In regards to AGD mainstreaming in the needs assessment conducted before the project, FGDs were held with community members from different age groups. It should be noted however that due to movement restrictions imposed on PUI staff at the time, the female staff who were to take part in the field visits could not attend, and therefore, no FGDs were conducted specifically with women. This concern will be addressed at the beginning of the project, through the conduct of FGDs with women of different age groups from the targeted communities. Moreover, PUI has a specific budget and human resources dedicated to protection mainstreaming, and thus is able to ensure support for different people and to offer them equal access to our services. Accessibility is not only physical but psychological as well, thus, all PUI staff received several trainings on different disciplines (such as Protection Mainstreaming, PSEAH, conducting inter-sectorial referral, child safeguarding and child protection) in order to ensure that they can promote and defend inclusion in our day-to-day implementation. Regarding PSEAH specifically, PUI has strengthened its SEAH reporting mechanism with the opening of our whistleblowing mechanism to others and different channels for beneficiaries, staff, and other stakeholders. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEAH to project beneficiaries come to complete the scheme. Besides, PUI staff members will also be further trained on age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, psychological first aid, safe referrals, GBV and inclusion of people with disabilities.
