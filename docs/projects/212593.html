Project Summary

Needs:
The proposed action aims to address the WASH needs of the people affected by the humanitarian crisis in northern Syria, based on needs assessments and stakeholder consultations. The action also includes longer-term solutions to improve water, sanitation, and hygiene conditions and resilience in the region. The HNO for 2024 shows that most people in northeast Syria need humanitarian and WASH assistance, due to critical water issues, hygiene challenges, and the impact of the 2023 earthquake.
Implementation and intervention activities:
• The organization will provide comprehensive WASH support package for targeted informal settlements and host communities Raqqa and Hasakeh governorates.
•The organization will improve access to safe water to 85,231 individuals. In Raqqa, by installation new Water Treatment Units (4,935 individuals), the rehabilitation of water networks in 4 villages and water trucking rehabilitation support (19,942 individuals), the chlorination of filling points (9,243 individuals) and the continuation of support to 3 water stations (29,700 individuals). In Hasakeh, this will include additional water trucking support during the summer months (increasing the total reach to 13,830 individuals) and the rehabilitation of the Markada water network (6,500 individuals).
•To support the safe storage of water, the organization will also provide communal and household-level water tanks to vulnerable populations in informal settlements and villages in Raqqa, and household-level tanks in Markada town, benefiting a total of 24,297 individuals.
•The organization will reach a total of 13,838 individuals through sanitation activities. In informal settlements in Raqqa the organization will increase the number of household latrines provided, install household showers for selected PwSN, and conduct maintenance of sanitation infrastructures. In host communities, the organization will cover sanitation pits and provide desludging in 7 villages in Raqqa District (3,198 individuals). In Markada town.
•The organization will extend hygiene promotion activities to Markada village in Hasakeh, and villages and new informal settlements in Raqqa (34,598 individuals). Soap distributions will be organized in a targeted manner to individuals in households and informal settlements (7,795 individuals), and the organization will distribute menstrual hygiene kits to the 988 households in the informal settlements in Raqqa.
•As part of its emergency contingency stockpile, the organization will supplement its originally planned cholera kits with 10 emergency kits in Raqqa and 700 emergency WASH kits.
•The organization will begin solid waste management activities in NES, including support to the municipal waste collection system in Raqqa District, and the organization of an environmental health campaign in Markada town. To support these activities, the organization will distribute communal and household bins across the SWM locations.
Risk and assumption:
Economy: Price and exchange rate fluctuations; Closure of banks or restrictions from banking system negatively impact the flow of funds inside Syria; Partial or total disruption of the supply chain; Lack of proper controls for distributions resulting in theft/loss; Lack of fuel with impact on project activities; Cash transfer modalities are disrupted or restricted, money transfer agents increase their commissions due to the restrictions of USD liquidity in Iraq and KRI.
Natural disaster/Epidemics: Natural disasters, catastrophes or epidemics occurred in the project areas: droughts are more frequent and are affecting underground water levels causing dried up boreholes;; risk of cholera outbreak due to water supply constraints.
Environment: The project generates significant greenhouse gas emissions coming from energy production and transport of goods, plastic pollution, and groundwater pollution.
Escalation of conflict affects the project activities in operational areas.

The potential risks include GBV and Prevention of PSEA. Likelihood: High. Impact: HighMitigating measures: 1. Raise awareness of FCRM to promote understanding and response. 2.Install temporary latrines to provide alternative facilities during construction periods. 3. The potential consequences include violence, exploitation and abuse. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: MediumMitigating measures: 1. Proactive communication with communities to ensure all members have the same level of information if a significant number of complaints is received on the same topic or if the feedback and complaints mechanism is underutilized.
