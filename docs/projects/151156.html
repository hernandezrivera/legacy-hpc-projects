Project Summary

Despite recent improvement in rainfall levels in Somalia, an estimated 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) 1 or worse through December according to findings from the post-Gu seasonal assessment conducted in June and July 2018 (FSNAU, September 2018). Acute food insecurity remains prevalent among pastoral populations who lost most of their livestock and became destitute during the 2016/2017 drought and Cyclone Sagar; riverine populations affected by flooding in April and May 2018; and others experiencing large-scale, protracted displacement due to conflict and natural hazards. These groups are expected to face food consumption gaps or engage in unsustainable coping strategies to meet their food needs, and humanitarian assistance is required to prevent further deterioration of their food security and nutrition situation. This project will focus on target population of 3,450 (575 HHs) comprising returnees, pastoral communities, and vulnerable population who need immediate assistance within the Buurhakaba district. The project will provide immediate access to food, protect and restore livelihoods related to food and income and support resilience of vulnerable populations against shocks through cash for work activities and unrestricted cash/voucher transfers.

Needs

The Somalia humanitarian crisis is among the most longstanding and complex globally. While large-scale famine continues to be averted in 2017, most parts of the country received above average rainfall in 2018 that lead to the floods and loss of crops among riverine communities and other inhabitants. In addition, ongoing conflict reduces the resilience of communities, triggering displacement, and impeding civilians’ access to sustainable basic services thus putting half of the population in need of humanitarian assistance and protection. Despite recent improvement in rainfall patterns, an estimated 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse through December according to findings from the post-Gu seasonal assessment conducted in June and July 2018. According to the FSNAU report, it is estimated that 4.6 million people are acutely food insecure (IPC phase 2 or worse). Two in five Somalis remain acutely food insecure, more than 2.6 million people are internally displaced and the seasonal assessment results indicates severe levels of acute food insecurity and malnutrition among IDPs. The national level of acute malnutrition has improved to Serious (10–14.9 percent Global Acute Malnutrition-GAM) with a median GAM of 14.0 percent, compared to a Critical prevalence (15–29.9 percent GAM) in the 2017 Gu, which recorded a median GAM of 17.4 percent. Despite this improvement, a high level of acute malnutrition persists in several population groups due to underlying/structural causes. According to the FSNAU report, Bay region whose population is approximately 792,182 a total of 261,000 are stressed (IPC 2), 101,000 are in crisis (IPC 3) and 7,000 are in emergency situation (IPC 4). Under these circumstances, Somali household’s recent food security improvements risk deterioration if the humanitarian assistance is not sustained particularly among vulnerable population groups.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Involving the marginalised populations, including women and girls, minorities and IDPs will provide a stable and safe environment that will increase community, development partners and government in terms of collaboration, inclusiveness, capacity building and effective implementation of the project. The quality of participation will ensure that respect and attention is given to opinions, ideas and perspectives of the community in planning and implementation. The result of broad-based consultation and engagement is expected to lead to greater ownership of the programme by communities, government and other stakeholders. The programme will be built upon relationships the PASOS already have with local communities, leaders, and government representatives, and complemented by long-term programming and other sectoral projects each agency is implementing in the area. This will considerably mitigate negative impacts of the programme’s closure further helping to enhance the target beneficiaries’ resilience. Sustainability is exceptionally important to build resilience and depends heavily on two key conditions: 1) communities’ willingness and ability to properly manage the project beyond the agencies’ intervention period; 2) the appropriateness and adaptability of the project itself. Community-led vulnerability assessment and analysis provide understand on local conditions, resources, needs, skills and knowledge. Ongoing engagement and communication with local and regional authorities, cluster partners as well as government officials will be critical for the success of the programme to ensure buy-in, ownership and sustainability.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The returnees targeted population risk of exclusion is medium since PASOS will ensure that data is collected among the beneficiaries with the help of already community structures and effective approaches such as outreach services so that all the vulnerable populations will be reached. Lack of documentation among the returnees does not mean that they are excluded but rather their vulnerability analysis. PASOS will also ensure that all the affected population understand the process of obtaining food assistance and livelihoods opportunities at the appropriate time, date and safe location.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The risks of exclusion based on gender and age is medium. The project’s gender focus creates new challenges for women and/or HH and community conflict by promoting women’s and age equality. PASOS will mitigate this risk by ensuring that activities targeting women include men in appropriate ways; male leaders engaged to support women’s involvement in decision making; gender impact of project (positive & negative) closely monitored; implementing agencies trained to refer GBV cases.Issues related to age exclusion will also be mitigated. There is a risk of excluding child headed household in the project. Minors will not be considered in restricted cash interventions but rather PASOS will involve the relevant local agencies to ensure that the child-headed households benefit from the unrestricted cash interventions.The risk of beneficiaries experience intimidation, extortion or theft of resources following distributions is medium and PASOS will Inform beneficiaries of potential risks, ensure distributions are conducted in secure areas and allow beneficiaries ample time to travel during daylight hours, ensure robust complaints mechanism and post distribution monitoring is in place

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The main risks include: a) Inaccessibility of project locations due to security concerns. The risk is medium and PASOS will ensure that all the project locations are in both secure and potentially insecure areas to ensure continuous implementation of activities; and will also regularly review security status with local authorities and institutions.2) There is also the risk of taxation by local authorities/power holders on distributed resources that the beneficiaries will face. The risk is medium and PASOS will mitigate the above mentioned risk by work with local authorities and other stakeholders to create awareness that these resources and assets are not subject to taxation, monitor and take immediate action on reports of taxation through accountability and feedback mechanisms
