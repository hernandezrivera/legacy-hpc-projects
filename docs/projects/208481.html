Project Summary

This project proposal outlines a comprehensive initiative aimed at enhancing Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH) and Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) for vulnerable groups, including displaced girls, young women, and women with disabilities, in selected regions of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe State. The primary objective is to empower these demographics with the confidence, knowledge, and skills necessary for managing their reproductive and menstrual health challenges effectively and with dignity. This will be achieved by overcoming barriers to information, supplies, and service access, thereby mitigating significant health risks.
The project is structured around four fundamental pillars of MHM programming and aims to impact seven Local Government Areas, directly benefiting approximately 10,214 girls and young women. The target groups include displaced adolescent girls aged 9 to 20 and women of reproductive age (22-36 years), focusing particularly on those living with disabilities. In partnership with the Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development, and with technical support from local Gender-Based Violence Sub-Sector Working Groups and Women-led NGOs, the project envisages reaching 4,914 direct and over 5,300 indirect beneficiaries. These beneficiaries are women and girls either currently impacted or at high risk of complications arising from reproductive health issues and inadequate menstrual management practices.
A participatory approach will be employed, engaging Community-Based Organizations, healthcare personnel, community leaders, educational authorities, corporations, and a network of volunteers and youth leaders. The project will also forge linkages with other reproductive health services, including those addressing HIV/AIDS and STDs. Community awareness campaigns and outreach programs, predominantly conducted by health workers and volunteers, will form a key component of the initiative. Educational clubs, comprising 30 participants each, will be established to disseminate key aspects of SRH and MHM over a three-month period per cohort, using certified modules.
Special attention will be given to women and girls with disabilities, who will be reached through personalized and door-to-door outreach. Participants will receive support in the form of free reusable menstrual pads and comprehensive SRH services, including medical testing, counseling, and treatment. Additionally, a community improvement team will be established to advocate for better access to medical and SRH services within their communities.
The project also includes an initiative to expand a nearly complete reusable pad production center, ensuring a steady supply of these essential items to beneficiaries. An emergency livelihood component is incorporated to assist the most vulnerable and at-risk individuals. The entire project is slated for a 12-month implementation period, with a proposed total budget of $193,196

1 - Needs

The regions of Borno, Adamawa, Yobe, and the broader Northeast have historically faced significant challenges, including pervasive poverty, lack of educational opportunities, and cultural norms that often undermine the reproductive health, needs, and rights of women and girls. The protracted crisis in these areas has only intensified these issues, gravely impacting the lives, safety, and dignity of women and girls. This situation is further complicated by limited knowledge and access to services related to reproductive and menstrual hygiene management. Current circumstances have rendered Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH) and menstrual products as unaffordable luxuries for most, with less than 21% of the population being able to access them. This scarcity is coupled with a rising incidence of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV), placing women and girls in an increasingly vulnerable position. According to UNFPA estimates, over 1.4 million women of reproductive age are affected, with approximately 276,000 pregnancies each year, necessitating vital reproductive health services and support. The communities in question, particularly in remote or conflict-affected areas, face isolation from necessary services and access, due to challenging terrain, security concerns, socio-cultural norms, and a lack of funding that often prioritizes basic needs such as food and shelter. A survey conducted by EGI in five communities (both urban and rural) revealed that over 592,000 women and girls of reproductive age suffer from infections, often without understanding the causes or available treatments. In displaced communities and camps, a significant majority of girls and women cannot afford menstrual products. Many resort to using unhygienic and unsafe materials like old rags, husks, leaves, grass, ash, sand, or newspapers. The scarcity of sanitary products in local shops further exacerbates this problem. This lack of access has profound implications, particularly in rural areas where 43% of girls cite menstruation as a reason for discontinuing their education, leading to detrimental effects on their personal development, economic status, and overall health. Furthermore, UNICEF's MHM report highlights that 56% of girls, both in and out of school, receive no education about menstruation. The ongoing conflict has led to the destruction of healthcare facilities and the displacement of significant numbers of women and girls, including over 1.8 million of childbearing age, severing their connection to essential reproductive health services. The conflict is also marked by prevalent abduction and sexual violence, with about half of the women in the northeast experiencing gender-based violence. This project seeks to address these critical issues by providing access to accurate information and developing comprehensive support and care services for vulnerable young girls and women. It aims to dismantle existing barriers to information, supplies, and services concerning sexual reproductive and menstrual health in displaced communities and camps. The implementation of this project will not only provide immediate relief but also establish a foundation for sustainable intervention, ultimately making a lifesaving difference for adolescent girls and women in these affected regions.

2 - Activities

This proposal outlines a multi-faceted initiative designed to enhance Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH) and Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) in select communities. The project encompasses a series of strategic actions: 1. Advocacy and Community Engagement: Key activities include advocacy visits to stakeholders at both state and community levels. This approach serves to inform and sensitize stakeholders about the project, seeking their support for mobilizing necessary resources, both human and material, for effective program delivery. Stakeholder engagement will focus on community and traditional leaders, state and local health and education ministries, religious leaders, heads of corporations, and both local and international NGOs. 2. Capacity Building through Training: The project will conduct comprehensive training programs for volunteers, staff, peer educators, traditional birth attendants, women leaders, and selected adolescent girls and young women from targeted schools and communities. The training will focus on the Minimum Initial Service Package, comprehensive menstrual hygiene, and reproductive health education, adopting a Training of Trainers (TOT) model to enhance the capabilities of participants in SRH and MHM. 3. Community Awareness and Outreach: The initiative will include community awareness programs and outreach activities, primarily conducted by health workers, volunteers, and staff. These will focus on the fundamentals of SRH management and menstrual hygiene, delivered in a club format with 30 participants per club, spanning a three-month period per cohort, utilizing certified modules. 4. Infrastructure Development: Construction of menstrual health-friendly toilets in girls' schools will be a critical component, ensuring safe and hygienic facilities for students. 5. Targeted Support for Women and Girls with Disabilities: This will involve one-on-one and door-to-door outreach by trained staff and volunteer coaches to ensure inclusive support and engagement. 6. Provision of Menstrual and SRH Products: The project plans to supply free reusable menstrual pads regularly, aiming to eradicate period poverty and the associated health risks due to the lack of these products. 7. Free SRH Services: Regular provision of mobile medical tests, counseling support, SRH products, and treatment will be available, in collaboration with partner organizations. Beneficiaries will also receive Reproductive Health Dignity Kits. 8. Community Improvement Team: A multi-stakeholder forum will be established and trained to advocate for the accessibility and availability of SRH services within their communities. 9. Expansion of Menstrual Pad Production Facility: The expansion of EGI’s Reusable Menstrual Pad Production outlet is planned for increased viability and wider reach.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CNEF is dedicated to implementing protection mainstreaming as a core aspect of our program. This commitment will be actualized through a holistic approach, encompassing various strategies to ensure the efficacy and sensitivity of our services. Central to our approach is the regular consultation with the communities we serve. These engagements are essential for understanding and addressing the unique needs and challenges faced by the community members. In tandem with this, we place a strong emphasis on the training and capacity building of our staff. This training particularly focuses on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), ensuring effective reporting channels and robust case management modalities are in place. Our service delivery is designed to be needs-based, inclusive, and non-discriminatory. A key aspect of this is ensuring the availability of female health workers to provide culturally appropriate services for women and girls. This consideration is vital in contexts where cultural norms may dictate gender-specific interactions in healthcare settings. Our facilities will be equipped with dedicated spaces for women, including private consultation and examination rooms, and adequate waiting areas, ensuring privacy and comfort. Further, our health workers will receive specialized training to sensitively and effectively respond to survivors of gender-based violence. They will be well-versed in the Standard Operational Procedure on Gender-Based Violence and will uphold the utmost respect for confidentiality. This is particularly important in our collaboration with the GBV Sub-Sector, where maintaining the dignity and privacy of survivors is paramount. Moreover, our team will be equipped with the knowledge and resources to refer individuals to appropriate psycho-social and legal support services. We recognize the importance of comprehensive care that extends beyond immediate medical needs, particularly for survivors of gender-based violence. Providing open and unimpeded access to our services for these survivors will be a priority, ensuring they receive the support and care they need in a respectful and empowering environment.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In our commitment to ensuring the effectiveness and sustainability of our initiatives, we propose the establishment and utilization of a Community Project Management Committee (CPMC) and a Community Improvement Team (CIT). These bodies will play a pivotal role in fostering a seamless interface between the community and our organization. The CPMC and CIT will function as vital community monitoring teams, consistently overseeing the progress and impact of the project. Their involvement is crucial in maintaining transparency and ensuring that the project's objectives align with the community's needs. These teams will comprise members drawn from the community, including representatives of the most vulnerable groups, to ensure diverse perspectives and inclusive decision-making. Furthermore, our Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) team will collaborate closely with community members to conduct routine evaluations. This collaborative approach is designed to facilitate the regular collection of feedback, particularly from the most vulnerable segments of the community. Such feedback is invaluable for assessing the project’s impact, identifying areas for improvement, and making timely adjustments to our strategies. In addition to their monitoring and evaluative roles, members of the most vulnerable groups will also be actively involved as volunteers in the project. This inclusion not only empowers these individuals but also ensures that their unique insights and experiences contribute significantly to the project's overall effectiveness. By integrating community members directly into the project's management and evaluation processes, we aim to cultivate a sense of ownership and partnership among all stakeholders. This approach is fundamental to the success and sustainability of the project, as it ensures that the interventions are not only community-driven but also responsive to the evolving needs of the population we serve.
