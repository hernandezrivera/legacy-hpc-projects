Project Summary

Acted will provide WASH assistance in Upper Nile and NBeG states, focusing on Renk and Aweil East counties, to address critical needs among the most vulnerable populations, including returnees, IDPs, and host community members. The intervention aims to reach 250,000 individuals, who face significant WASH-related challenges due to displacement caused by the Sudan crisis and the chronic lack of WASH infrastructure.
In alignment with WASH Cluster objectives, Acted’s intervention will contribute to CLWSH/CO2 by ensuring access to basic WASH services in emergencies, especially in underserved counties classified as WSC 4 and 5, and CLWSH/CO4 by reducing exposure to environmental hazards through the construction of disaster-resistant WASH infrastructure. Acted will integrate these interventions into its multi-sectoral approach, which includes Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) and protection, ensuring a holistic response to displacement challenges and fostering a safer environment for vulnerable groups.
In Renk and Aweil East Transit Centers, IDP settlements, host communities as relevant, activities will include rehabilitating boreholes, solar upgrading, repairing handpumps, constructing emergency latrines and communal showers, environmental sanitation, monitoring waterpoints for peaceful access, and maintaining emergency water treatment stockpiles. Hygiene promotion campaigns and the distribution of essential hygiene items will support improved practices and reduce health risks. Acted will rehabilitate and repair boreholes and handpumps, construct VIP latrines in institutions, establish handwashing facilities, and distribute menstrual health and hygiene kits. Capacity-building initiatives, such as training water management committees, will enhance the sustainability and community ownership of WASH services.
Acted will also mainstream Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) by incorporating flood-resistant designs into all WASH infrastructure to ensure long-term functionality. The intervention will be closely coordinated with food security, health, and nutrition partners, ensuring that IDPs, returnees, and host communities receive comprehensive, multi-sectoral support tailored to their needs. By addressing immediate WASH needs while fostering long-term resilience and integration, Acted will contribute to reducing WASH-related health risks and promoting the well-being and dignity of the affected populations.

1. Needs

The WASH situation in South Sudan, particularly in regions such as Renk, Joda, and Aweil East, has become increasingly dire with the recent influx of refugees and returnees from Sudan. These areas, which have historically faced challenges in water, sanitation, and hygiene access, are now under greater strain due to the ongoing conflict in Sudan and its spillover effects on South Sudan. The situation is compounded by the effects of climate change, displacement, and pre-existing deficiencies in WASH infrastructure, all of which have exacerbated the needs of vulnerable populations, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities. The WASH situation in Renk and Joda has worsened due to the large influx of returnees and refugees from Sudan. Since mid-2023, over 270,000 returnees have arrived in Renk, while Joda hosts approximately 50,000 refugees and returnees. These populations are putting immense pressure on already fragile water, sanitation, and hygiene infrastructure. Many existing water points, such as boreholes and hand pumps, are either non-functional or insufficient for the growing population, leading to competition for water and, at times, security incidents. Sanitation is equally challenging, with latrines often collapsing during the rainy season, resulting in open defecation. This worsens the risk of waterborne diseases, including cholera, particularly in overcrowded conditions. For women and girls, the situation is especially precarious, as they are exposed to gender-based violence while collecting water in unsafe areas and lack access to essential hygiene products. Aweil East, located in Northern Bahr El Ghazal, faces its own set of WASH challenges, particularly as the area experiences significant flooding and the impact of returnees from Sudan. Water infrastructure in the region has been severely degraded due to both conflict and climatic events. Boreholes and hand pumps have been damaged or destroyed, further limiting access to safe water. The influx of returnees has exacerbated these challenges, with many relying on unsafe water sources and facing increased vulnerability to diseases such as cholera and typhoid fever. Sanitation infrastructure is also lacking, with inadequate latrines and waste management systems contributing to poor hygiene and the spread of waterborne diseases. The region is particularly vulnerable during the rainy season, when flooding destroys existing water and sanitation facilities, further heightening the risk of disease outbreaks. In response to these escalating needs, WASH interventions in these regions must prioritize providing safe and sustainable access to water, rehabilitating existing water points, and constructing new ones. Improving sanitation infrastructure, including the construction of latrines and waste management systems, is critical to mitigating the risks of disease transmission. Hygiene promotion and the distribution of hygiene kits, including soap, water containers, and menstrual hygiene products, are also essential to improving the overall health and well-being of vulnerable populations. Additionally, addressing the needs of women and children, who are disproportionately affected by these WASH gaps, is crucial. The WASH sector's response in these areas is vital not only to reduce the immediate health risks posed by poor water and sanitation conditions but also to promote longer-term resilience in communities affected by conflict and displacement. The situation in these areas highlights the urgent need for international and local organizations to coordinate and scale up their efforts to meet the growing WASH needs of displaced populations and host communities. Ensuring that WASH interventions are contextually appropriate, sustainable, and inclusive will be key to improving health outcomes and reducing the impact of future shocks on these vulnerable populations.

2. Project activities

Acted will implement the following activities to increase vulnerable populations’ from IDPs, returnees and host communities’ access to WASH services - Rehabilitation of non-functional boreholes and solar upgrading - Rehabilitation/repair of handpumps - Emergency latrine construction and decommissioning - Hygiene promotion sessions/campaigns - Installation of handwashing points - Establishing and training Water Committees Users - Implementation of community-led solid waste management In parallel, and in link with its site management duty in Renk and Aweil East transit centres and Joda reception centre, Acted will provide the following support to returnees: - Provision of emergency water treatment stockpile - Waterpoint monitoring to ensure peaceful access to water - Latrine maintenance - Emergency communal showers construction - Environmental sanitation - Construction of waste pits - Construction of emergency latrines for the most vulnerable - Construction for VIP latrines in institutions (including health facilities and schools) - Installation of hand washing facilities - Hygiene promotion . Wherever relevant, Acted will maintain capacity to implement specific activities aimed at prevent or respond to cholera-related risks. In parallel, a specific attention will be granted to mainstream Disaster Risk Reduction component to ensure that facilities are resistant to flooding as much as possible.
