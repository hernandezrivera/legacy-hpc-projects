Project Summary

The project aims to provide life-saving WASH intervention to 79,174 in 8 localities of White Nile & Blue Nile.
Women and girls form majority (58%) of the projects’ beneficiaries and ADD will put a special emphasis to address their needs and interests in accessing WASH related services by mainstreaming gender and protection in the intervention and involvement of women and girls in all the stages of project cycle. Moreover, presence of project-specific ‘Gender Action Plan’ goes beyond isolated attention on females and analysis of power within a context of aid delivery to include a concern for men, boys, elderly and people with disability. Apart from a recent interagency (UN, line ministries, ADD, NGOs) assessment to 2 states numerous consultations with the IDPs, returnees and host population led to selection of some activities over others. As always, ADD accepted technical inputs from related line ministries and UNICEF and shall continue to do so.
Some major activities include maintenance and upgrading of water systems, hygiene promotion sessions, distribution of WASH NFIs, household latrine construction through CLTs approach , training for water users committees/community health workers, etc.
ADD will strengthen the target population’s capacity to self-manage the WASH system so that they can still have basic services with little assistance from NGOs.
Project specific Monitoring and Evaluation (M and E) framework shall guide the achievement of activities and results. ADD will be primarily responsible for implementation of the activities while CBOs will help in community mobilization. ADD will coordinate with other actors like SMoH, HAC, UNICEF and WES to implement and monitor the project

1 - Needs

Accordingly to HNO 2019 an estimated 1.3 million people are at risk of having to migrate due to water resource depletion because of climatic shocks and could require water trucking. Water supply infrastructure will also require upgrading and frequent repair. Water source depletion and floods can have spill-over multiplier effects, including disease outbreak and displacement. To avert disease outbreak, there will be a need for improved environmental sanitation and personal hygiene practices. Of the 3.5 million people in need of WASH assistance, 2.1 million are IDPs and the remainders are residents in areas with poor access to WASH facilities such as in eastern Sudan, conflict-affected people from the Kordofan region, White and Blue Nile States refugees or returnees. Over one third of Sudan’s population is considered vulnerable to the impact of climate shocks. The anticipated continued impact of climatic shocks on WASH includes water resource depletion, disease outbreak and further displacement due to conflict over water resources. Sector analysis shows that over 40 per cent of hand pumps and 30 per cent of motorized water schemes in Sudan are at high risk of running dry. Increased operation and maintenance costs will place a higher burden on vulnerable communities.

2 - Activities / Outputs

Activities: Water Supply Activities: • Replace motorized diesel pumps in 3 locations with new solar pumping systems • Support Operation and Maintenance of 2 water supply schemes • Upgrading of 2 hand pumps to mini water yard in Blue Nile & White Nile to increase access of target communities to water supply. Sanitation Activities: • Apply CLTS approach at (45) communities in Blue Nile & White Nile a • Support construction of school sanitation/hand washing facilities and hygiene promotion at 5 schools in Blue Nile state in target localities to increase access of school children to sanitation facilities. Hygiene Promotion Activities: • Provision of tools and equipment for environmental cleaning campaign • Provision of hygiene kits for 1000 women . • Conduct hygiene promotion education sessions, support campaign activities and distribution of IEC materials fro 3000 household. • Acute watery diarrhea (AWD) preparedness and response Capacity Building Activities: • Support Community Management Committees (CMC) and WASH Committees to sustainably manage their WASH facilities. through conducting training on maintenance of the hand pumps at 4 locations. This project is part of a multi-sectoral program (WASH, Health & Nutrition) provided tobeneficiaries by ADD in the targeted areas. This approach ensures synergistic effect to maximize impactand hence achieve the aim of the project.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

ADD will pay attention to the Integration of environmental issues during any WASH assessments and ADD will ensure that environmental harm is reduced or eliminated and environmental benefits are maximized., In the Blue Nile in Sudan, deforestation and water scarcity are the two main environmental problems, and projects that could potentially affect forests and other land vegetation and water sources need to eliminate and/or mitigate negative impacts as much as possible. ADD in designing the response – key environmental concerns will be built into the project. To maximize negative impacts on the forestry resources ADD will be the introduction of alternative construction material such as Stabilised Soil Blocks (SSB) or Cement Stabilised Blocks (CSB) and iron bar to replace fired bricks and wooden for construction activities, ADD will monitor and evaluation of the outcomes of a response is important to ensure that the effects sought for were indeed delivered, and to help identify possible negative environmental effects in time to allow for an adjustment of activities. ADD will pay attention to Community consultation and participation which is a key part of environmental management, including working with the national and state governmental authorities. Civil society organizations including CBOs will be consulted, promoted and their capacities enhanced. People in these community structure and CBOs have invaluable knowledge and skill in the management of the local environment, and will be responsible for building the systems for equitable sustainable management in future

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

ADD staff trained on prevention of sexual exploitation of beneficiaries by aid workers will orient the field staff on the subject matter as a part of ADD’s commitment to ADD’s values and Humanitarian Accountability Framework. Envisioned complaint response mechanism for ADD shall have adequate scope to address the protection concerns of the target population. Wherever applicable, ADD will develop selection criteria to choose individuals/households for assistance with dignity kits, soap, latrines, etc. Relatively vulnerable individuals and households shall be prioritized, when the services are scarce. ADD and CBOs will also mainstream protection concern in activities like latrine site/design selection, water point location selection by identifying locations that women and girls (and the wider community) consider as safe. ADD and CBOs will ensure that the women have a say in when and where the WUC meetings are conducted. ADD will work with WUC to address specific protection issues in a community related to WASH intervention

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Feedback and Complaint Mechanism: ADD will consult communities on establishing a viable feedback or complaint mechanism. Most possible options are use of mobile feedback and complaint boxes and/or a telephone hotline system to collect feedback from beneficiaries. Complaints will be validated through initial screening processes; an investigation will take place in case of valid and serious cases. ADD has a pool of senior staff with experience in handling complaints during the project cycle, ADD will make necessary arrangement to ensure that staff in field locations are well trained in handing complaints. ADD will train its CBOs in Feedback and Complaint Mechanism. Information sharing: ADD will organize sessions with beneficiaries and WUCs to communicate relevant details about the project, especially related to the activity to be implemented in their respective community. ADD’s mission, presence of feedback mechanism, entitlement of the community (if applicable) is few other elements that will be shared. Do no Harm: ADD will follow principles of ‘Do no harm’ prior to and during the intervention of the project. One of the necessary things is to be aware of ‘connectors’ and dividers. In order to avoid confrontation between host communities and IDPs, a portion of activities will be allocated to adjacent host community. Some WASH non-food items like dignity/hygiene kits, soap will be provided also to vulnerable host community members. ADD understands that many communities in the project areas are diverse and will explore ways to unite the community; one of the ways is to promote participation of people from various ethnic backgrounds among IDPs, returnees and host communities together as members of WUCs. ADD hopes that serving in a social platform like WUCs can be a way to increase coherence and connectedness. ADD may include the IDPs, returnees and host communities around target in its intervention related to conflict resolution through creation of economic opportunities
