Project Summary

Target Population
18,679 individuals – 18,324 children and 355 adults, it is anticipated that additional children and adults will be identified throughout the project lifecycle. The total population targeted: 13,679 individuals – 18,324 children (9,950 boys; 8,374 girls) and 355 adults (344 educators and 8 focal points and 3 team leader - 213 women and 142 men).
• Training, education personnel (composed of teachers, principals, assistants) and TdH IT focal points and frontline workers, on child-centred, protective pedagogy, positive learning, child protection and gender-based violence. The training will be organized in 5 days for each location for a total of 6 locations. In Tripoli, the training will be repeated three times (one will be dedicated to the educational personnel in Tripoli, one to the education personnel from Sabha, and one to TdH IT focal points based in the West/South). Subsequent cascade training will be implemented for activity facilitators and volunteers to ensure best practices and minimum standards are maintained during service delivery, and TDH systems are utilized for protection issues and/or children/youth/families requiring specialized support. This will include training on psychological first aid, incident reporting, disclosure, referrals and the referral pathways of each location, and case management intake procedure.
• Remedial education (developmental education) designed to assist children, with or without special needs, who have fallen behind the rest of the class to catch up on specific subjects. This activity will be implemented in the schools assessed (39 public and fee-based schools in 5 areas, 6 collective shelters in Tripoli, and 7 Francophone/Migrant schools for non-Libyans). In the post-training phase, the selected educational staff dedicated to the remedial classes will be supported, during the activity start-up and all along with its implementation, by mentoring from TdH IT focal points. The Monitoring & Evaluation will be specifically designed for this activity but will also be present throughout the whole project as a transversal tool. An international expert will be fostering the capacity of the field team and of the Liaison/M&E Officer in particular, on KOBO software usage for collection and elaboration of data.
• Extra-curricular activities: recreational/creative expression/psychosocial support activities (arts, sports, craft skills, etc.) in schools and learning spaces. The activities will also include specific awareness for school-aged children on GBV prevention and children’s rights. This activity will be implemented in the 52 sites already identified (schools for Libyans and migrants and refugees), learning spaces outside school (to be selected during the project cycle) and in 6 collective shelters. Some of the trained educational personnel will be selected and dedicated to this activity and will be supported, during the start-up and all along with the implementation, through continued mentoring by 1 TdH IT focal point and 1 team leader.
• Essential learning materials/school supplies for school-aged children. Materials will be provided to cover the daily school needs of the target populations for the duration of the project cycle. Learning material to be distributed will be UNICEF school-in-a-box kit and school-in-a-box refill kit; in Sabha direct procurement will be performed to align as closely as possible to UNICEF standards.
• Repaired educational facilities/prefabricated classrooms to improve access for school-aged children, with attention to small rehabilitation and maintenance works.
Where needed, rehabilitation, equipment and maintenance of the spaces will consider the accessibility for children with disabilities. although according to the actual possibility to remove architectural barriers. A study of the needs will be undertaken by an engineer, who will also compile the BOQs (Bill of Quantities) and feasibility analysis;TDH staff will conduct safety audits.

1 - Needs

EducationApproximately 343,300 Libyan, migrant and refugee school-aged children (6-17 years) face challenges in accessing education due to the ongoing conflict, displacement, or legal status in the country. Overall, 15% of school-aged children do not regularly attend school across Libya. The ongoing conflict has damaged a large number of education buildings, and initial assessments by TdH IT have shown that 50% of schools surveyed have been damaged by conflict/disrepair. In 2018, before the resurgence of the conflict in 2019, 489 schools had been affected: 40 had been fully damaged, 423 partially damaged, and 26 were accommodating IDPs. The lack of safe education infrastructure, the overcapacity of some schools, the lack of gender-sensitive WASH facilities (83% of schools surveyed) and proper access for children with disabilities (57% of schools surveyed), are all detrimental elements to the education of school-aged children. In 2019, the negative impact of the current conflict on academic performance was most clearly demonstrated by the performance of students in their final year of high school with only 38% passing their final exams after the conflict disrupted classes and delayed the final exams period. Academic performance of children in Libya is further hampered by the poor pedagogical skills of teachers and other educational personnel, paired with the lack of instructional materials (43% of schools surveyed) in the context of formal education. According to the MSNA 2018, 16% of households interviewed cited poor quality of education as a reason for not attending and/or dropping out of school. Finally, a major obstacle to the education of school-aged children in Libya is the widespread lack of access to psychosocial support services: in a context of conflict and displacement such as Libya, the ability of children to learn requires the complement of sustained recreational activities and psychosocial support. Currently, 268,629 have been displaced from their homes due to the ongoing conflict. The impacts of direct exposure to the conflict have been severe, including psychological distress and trauma. Many children, due to the conflict, are suffering from psychosocial distress or trauma, which affects their ability to learn. Vulnerable populations identified by TdH IT face compounding, pre-existing needs as well as new, emerging challenges during this period of crisis and will require education, protection, and MHPSS services. Protection/Child Protection/GBVAccording to UNHCR figures from 03/03/20202, there are currently 343,180 IDPs in Libya; not representative of Tripoli only, but the primary population of IDPs is from Tripoli and Tripoli Area - Western Region. This adds to a pre-existing displaced population of 16,227 that were living in informal shelters and camps/settlements. The impacts of direct exposure to the conflict have been severe, including psychological distress and trauma. Since April 2019, there have been1,000 civilian deaths since, over 1,000 strikes by UAVs, airstrikes, and daily armed clashes, including heavy and light artillery. Vulnerable populations identified by TdH IT will require protection and MHPSS services (including targeted CP/GBV structured sessions), Case Management services and capacity-building on Child Protection and Gender-Based Violence with educators and other local actors. Populations face compounding, pre-existing needs as well as new, emerging challenges during this period of crisis. Beyond the identified populations of the project, it is anticipated that additional children and adults will be identified throughout the project lifecycle.

5 - Mitigation measures

-Abuse and neglect, gender-based violence, early marriage - A "do no harm" approach will guide our emphasis on programming acceptance, which will be achieved by working with community leaders, school staff, and parents and help to mitigate any adverse responses to activities that endanger the target population. Child safeguarding measures, including agencies procedures for responding to protection issues, will be put in place and align with sector and sub-sector standards. Membership on the Case Management Task Force will guide interventions requiring case management. Training for staff and education personnel participating in the activities will include education on these protection issues and how to support mitigation, prevention and intervention.-Child labour and forced recruitment - The primary focus of this intervention is to provide support to improving the learning environment and helping children and youth prioritize their education. The pull of economic opportunities and/or recruitment into gangs or militia will require identification of children susceptible to this risk and a concerted effort through mediation with family and individualized education plans seeking to support academic success. -Kidnapping - This risk is present for both beneficiaries and aid workers. For the beneficiaries, life skill sessions and consultations with education personnel to ensure all measures are in place to mitigate against this threat which faces students that typically travel long distances to school or who have family affiliation with gangs or militias. For humanitarians, security procedures, updated risk assessments, movement plans and live-tracking will be critical to ensuring safety.-Attacks on aid workers and traffic accidents - Security procedures, updated risk assessments, movement plans and live-tracking will be critical to ensuring safety.
