Project Summary

Dara’a is a governorate located in the south of Syria, and one of the most affected areas by the Syrian crisis, with damage, exceeds 70% of the area including medical centers and related health sector facilities, with hazardous remnants of war scattered everywhere, such as landmines and unexploded ordnance. However, the area is now considered under government control.
Youth Charity aims through this project will provide its medical services to 13,200 of the 224,106 poor people and health needs by:
1) Increasing access to health care through the provision of comprehensive and integrated essential health services to cover the health needs of vulnerable populations, including Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, and Child Health (RMNCH), as well as continuity of treatment for people who live with non-communicable disease.
2) Improving access to Trauma care management including comprehensive phased trauma during both the acute and post-acute management phases, with support to the referral pathways for trauma cases.
3) Restore, strengthen, expand health services to deliver an essential package of care adapted to a rapidly increasing number of consumers to provide an integrated, comprehensive basic essential health care package that addresses common diseases, and injuries, including diagnostic services at primary and secondary levels targeting the most vulnerable groups while promoting community cohesion among populations, served, addressing inequalities in access to care targeting returning and receiving communities.
And referral system will be executed in coordination with local hospitals in the area (to beneficiaries who are unable to travel) and with hospitals in Damascus (to beneficiaries how are able to travel). And medical services include free medication and the source of medicines is the Pharmacists Syndicate. Trauma services will be provided in the emergency clinic in mobile medical clinics by the emergency physician in the following cases: emergency wounds, fractures, and abscesses.
Youth Charity looking for support people under poverty line, people with medical need’s, people that don’t have any carrier to start with, children’s who faced the financial difficulties to follow-up with their schools years, children who had warfare danger to avoid and to be dropouts, pregnant ladies who can’t handle the cost of The medical attention she required, spread of COVID-19, lack of services In the clinics that request more laboratories.
The issue of non-occurrence of gender-based violence, violence against children, and child labor will be pursued.
Precautionary procedures are taken in the project related to Covid-19 virus prevention measures:
- Daily sterilization of clinics and community center
- Keeping safe distances among people.
- Sterilization for each beneficiary of medical services and psychological support upon entry
- Posting of educational posters to prevent coronavirus.

4 - Needs

Dara’a is one of the most affected areas by the Syrian crisis, with a damage exceeds 70% of the area including medical centers and related health sector facilities, with hazardous remnants of war scattered everywhere, such as landmines and unexploded ordnance. However, the area is now considered under government control.Dara’a including (Dara’a city and neighboring rural areas and As-Sanamayn) is an area that has a population of over 600,000. Most residents are currently very poor and internally displaced after seven years of war and encircle, resulted in more than 224,106 of them need medical care & services. High percentage of the population are children and families who rely on women as their main breadwinners. The whole targeted area has only few hospitals some are public with no services and the others are private and very costly for the people, as follows:- In Dara’a City there are four private hospitals (Al Rahma, Al Khass, Al Yarmouk and Al Sharq fospitals) and one public hospital (Dara’a Hospital), (Military Hospital).Due to the high population of Dara’a city including surroundings and the inability of many of them to leave the area (due to security/financial reasons i.e. medical compensations). The project aims to provide free medical services will provide it,Through Mobile Medical Clinic,that were previously manufactured in the mobile medical youth clinics project in Daraa, (Primary Health Services, Secondary Health Services and Trauma Services) with referral system in addition to provide free medication, and find job opportunities to jobless people of the area (mainly women).Most people in the area have no capacity to pay for proper health services due to their financial situation.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risk of failure to provide full free service, and this will be addressed through permanent monitoring and daily contact with beneficiaries and by implementing active strict quality procedure and penalties and if impossible to deal with this case in Daraa without the budget we will transfer the case for executive management team of the YC association In referral system there is the risk of the non-availability of a specified medical service and doctors in the local area, and this will be addressed by transferring referrals of beneficiaries to Damascus and provide beneficiaries with medical attendance cards to facilitate mobility. The risk of continuous power outages and this will be addressed by providing generator to mobile clinic. The difficulty of reaching (security) from the nearby countryside, and here it is addressed by choosing secure strategic locations of the mobile medical clinic which are easily accessible by beneficiaries in addition to the fact that most villages in the area have returned to government control. The risk of lacking commitment of some beneficiaries of the continuing treatment process and will be addressed by daily follow-up medical system and conducting daily questionnaires to beneficiaries and closing each case separately. The issue of non-occurrence of gender-based violence (women & children), violence against children, and child labor will be pursued, The raising in exchange rates due to the economic situation in the country, which was addressed by leaving additional financial margin in the budget as a lesson learned from previous projects, The difficulty to get medical instruments and consumables due to the instability of exchange rates was addressed as the association has many tenders
