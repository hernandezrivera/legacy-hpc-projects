Project Summary

The Nutrition response in 2022 will prioritise implementation of the nutrition lifesaving activities (inpatient, outpatient, and targeted supplemental feeding programmes) and targeting of LGAs with high rates of GAM (above 10%) such as MMC/Jere, Nganzai, Bama, Machina. Hard-to-reach areas (such as Gubio, Rann, Magumeri), areas with large IDP and returnee populations (like Monguno) and high-risk/inaccessible locations such as Marte LGA will also be prioritised for nutrition assistance.
UNICEF will primarily target acutely malnourished children and PLW who are at a significantly high risk of death. The nutrition response will also target the total under-five and PLW with preventive nutrition services e.g. micronutrient supplementation to ensure levels of acute malnutrition are not above average thresholds, and to also reduce morbidity and mortality. The response will prioritise households with persons living with disabilities, who face barriers in accessing health, nutrition and WASH services and are disproportionately food insecure due to high levels of exclusion.
The nutrition status of the targeted population in the BAY states significantly deteriorates during the period from May-August every year, which is the lean season and coincides with the rainy season. The season is characterised by very high rates of acute malnutrition with medical complications due to poor food access and a high prevalence of diarrhoea and malaria. UNICEF will need to adequately prepare for the lean season period by prepositioning supplies, increasing the overall sector capacity to treat acute malnutrition with a focus on inpatient care and IYCF-E
The nutrition response in BAY states will be contextualised in 2022 based on the following categorisation:
1. Protracted crisis e.g north and central Borno LGAs where the rates of acute malnutrition are high, worsened by poor access to food and health systems. The approach in these areas will include INGO/NNGO direct support to the affected population, and prioritisation of lifesaving nutrition activities.
3. New emergencies – locations of new crisis and fluctuations such as relocations, displacements and spike in acute malnutrition. The approach will focus on the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) with a focus on lifesaving activities.
4. Hard-to-reach / inaccessible areas – locations with limited humanitarian footprints and access e.g Gubio and Magumeri. The approach will include RRM and remote management strategy (where feasible) with a focus on life-saving interventions. UNICEF will also strengthen the implementation of nutrition interventions through local organizations or community-based structures to implement nutrition services in hard-to-reach/inaccessible areas.
UNICEF response is primarily through services and in-kind deliveries. UNICEF will employ the use of cash and voucher assistance (CVA) to fill the nutrient gap in the Nutrition/Food Minimum Expenditure Basket to ensure that populations have access to a varied diet e.g CVA for meats and vegetables.
The nutrition status of the affected population is an outcome of various underlying issues including access to health, WASH, food security, child protection, and gender issues. The main cause of acute malnutrition among children and women in BAY states is the high morbidity rates. High diarrhoea prevalence is the major cause of acute malnutrition among the IDPs and urban poor population due to the prevailing poor hygiene and sanitation conditions worsened by congested living conditions. The response will strengthen the integration of nutrition-sensitive interventions including WASH, food security, health, livelihoods and child protection, and gender-based violence risk mitigation.
UNICEF will prioritise projects that include basic nutrition packages such as integrated treatment of acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM) and prevention services including integration with WASH, food security, health and protections services.

1 - Needs

According to the North-East Nigeria Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance conducted in October 2021, the global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates have significantly increased in Borno (11.8%) and Yobe (14.1%) states, which are the highest levels of acute malnutrition recorded since the start of the nutrition surveillance in 2016. The GAM rates in Adamawa (6.1%) has remained relatively stable but still above the acceptable threshold. The monthly Famine Monitoring System (FMS) results show that new arrival IDPs from inaccessible areas have extremely high rates of GAM rates of about 30%.In 2022, approximately 1.0 million children under-five and 150,000 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition and will require highly specialised nutrition, supplemental feeding, and health services to save their lives and prevent overall spike in mortality. In addition, approximately 2.0 million children under-five and PLW will require nutrition preventive services. To meet the targeted population's nutritional needs, the Sector will need to significantly scale-up nutrition treatment services including outpatient therapeutic programmes, supplemental/blanket feeding programmes, micronutrient supplementation, and promotion of maternal, infant, and young children practices.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

This project will work closely with the Protection sector to ensure that necessary mechanisms are put in place to ensure the rights of the beneficiaries, mainly children and women who will be visiting this service frequently are protected. There will be provision of child friendly spaces in all health facilities for children to play under the watch of the caregivers, especially at service provision sites. To ensures protection and gender principles are incorporated into the delivery of the interventions and ensure that activities enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the rights of the beneficiaries. Primary consideration will be given to UNICEF’s accountability to affected populations, to identify, understand and support their own protection measures. UNICEF will ensure that children in the nutrition programme and their mothers are provided with psychosocial counselling also through collaborating with Child protection partners on the ground. Adequate monitoring will be done to ensure beneficiaries are both aware and are receiving their entitlements. Efforts will be made to link all the children discharged from existing facilities to available community services.Specifically Sex disaggregation of data, gender balanced selection of trained health workers in service sites, specialized training on PSEA and GBV for UNICEF field teams and partners and gender sensitization and GBV awareness during training of health workers will be done

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNICEF uses third party facilitators, community mobilizers working under UNICEF supported projects and UNICEF staff are associated or linked to community leaders and harness concerns from the communities to inform implementation. Community nutrition mobilizer structures will be used to get feedback on quality of services and satisfaction level of beneficiaries. Such feedback will be analyzed by the SPHCMB and UNICEF for possible adjustments in implementation strategy, when and where. UNICEF has set up U-report 24x7 system in North-eastern Nigeria to ensure community feedback is collected and analysed systematically. U-Report 24x7 is a free SMS-based platform for community participation designed to collect real-time data including reports and complaints from affected citizens and to communicate life-saving information with them. All nutrition implementing partners and Government counterparts have been trained to work with respective communities to fully utilize the U-report platform. This U-reporting system will be used to get feedback from beneficiaries, communities and other stakeholders on project implementation. U-Report is easy to use and requires only a subscription on any telephone network in Nigeria to receive messages and send feedback. The U-report will complement other formal which include LGA level coordination forums, complaint handling boxes in health facilities, Focus Group Discussions and informal beneficiary feedback systems through the community volunteer network, cognizant of the fact that the platform relies on availability of telephone network to receive and send SMS freely
