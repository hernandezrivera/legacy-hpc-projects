Provide logistics services to support the humanitarian community's response to

Needs

Recurring conflict and the effects of climate change have resulted in multiple displacements and returns of displaced people throughout the last decade; without immediate and timely assistance, large scale populations are increasingly vulnerable. The urgent need for timely transportation of humanitarian relief items is crucial, to enable the delivery of assistance; particularly to rural areas where access to basic services is constrained, and there is a limited presence of humanitarian actors. According to the latest update provided by OCHA (October 1, 2017), 152,600 persons were displaced in Darfur in 2016. Since January 2017, a reduction in the number of newly displaced people has been observed, but protracted displacement is still an issue across Darfur. In addition, IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) system in West Kordofan, is currently registering and verifying over 100,000 affected populations, who are currently living in displacement sites or hosted by local communities. Beyond the conflict related displacements, around 3.5 million people in Sudan are at risk of being affected by natural disasters resulting from climate change, which could result in further displacements. Displacement sites across Sudan remain hard to reach (e.g Kutum, Golo, Kebkabiya) and agencies find it difficult to sustain the cost associated with the transportation of relief items. Whilst agencies may have contingency or buffer stock, they do not have contingency planning for the high cost of transportation, resulting in delays to transport life saving items and a reduction in sectorial response. IOM proposes to mitigate transportation related delays by having an emergency transportation system that can respond timely and reliably during critical emergency situations.

Activities

Activities or outputsOutcome 1:- Populations affected by natural or man made disaster receive timely assistance during and in the aftermath of a shock. This project proposes to ensure timely and predictable transport services remain available to humanitarian partners urgently needing to deploy relief items by road or air (through UNHAS) to displacement sites hosting populations affected by natural disaster and conflicts. Through this project, IOM will mitigate morbidity and related negative consequences caused by delays in the transportation of relief items. Each partner will submit transportation requests for relief items that urgently need to be transported to target areas in full coordination with the LET sector and OCHA, ensuring a coordinated approach between the work of IOM, WFP and OCHA and UNAMID, where relevant. Output 1: - Affected people receive timely life saving humanitarian assistance and protection. Administrate logistic database to track transported items and track fleet management:1. Receive transportation requests from agencies that include beneficiaries’ gender and age breakdown, where possible.2. In close coordination with the LET sector lead and OCHA at the field level, prioritize requests in accordance type and identified gap of commodities.3. Select the best and most cost effective means of air or land transportation.4. Process escorts request depending on the security situation of final destination (UNAMID, Police or both).5. Process waybill and associated transportation clearances based on the type of transportation method (air/land).6. Loading and offloading of relief items.7. Report on delivery of relief items compared to detailed inventory of transported items.8. Support capacity building of HAC counterparts in logistics related training
