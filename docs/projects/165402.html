Project Summary

The project will provide a range of MHPSS services including mental health case management, psychosocial support, group support and outreach in order to provide services for individuals who cannot access health facilities as well as referral to assessment and treatment of priority mental health conditions as per WHO mhGAP Programme, provided under a different project. Community based awareness raising activities, events and trainings will be provided within the community to sensitize community members to the mental health needs of the community, improve acceptance and awareness of MHPSS services and reduce the stigma of seeking help as well as promoting resilience at the community level. The project will target all community members, making a special effort to target and increase access for the most isolated and vulnerable through outreach, integrating MHPSS services into primary healthcare facilities, embedding MHPSS staff within Mobile Medical Units, Physical Rehabilitation facilities, and during distribute fsl inputs and MHPSS services will be available for all community members, both host and displaced, regardless of age, gender or impairment.

4 - Needs

The ongoing or recurrent conflict environment experienced by many boys and girls over the past seven years has profoundly affected their psychosocial well-being – weakening their sense of security, purpose, control and significance, or exercising social agency, and causing profound distress. Many are without civil documentation to prove their identity, access basic services and safeguard their rights. It is estimated that more than 2.6 million children remain displaced and separation from caregivers is a reported child protection issue in all 14 governorates of Syria putting children at risk of exploitation, abuse or neglect . Events leading to family separation indicate how related protection and child protection issues increase vulnerability of affected boys and girls, with death or disappearance of caregivers, disappearance of children, separation due to displacement or conflict, child marriage and child recruitment among cited reasons for family separations . Respondents in those assessed communities where discrimination in the delivery of humanitarian assistance emerged, identified unaccompanied girls as the most likely group to face such discrimination .Another layer of analysis derives from comparing responses provided by male and female respondents in the MSNA exercise . For example, this analysis indicated how “discrimination/exclusion” is a primary concern for female respondents in relation to the delivery of humanitarian assistance in significantly different proportions than for male respondents (83% of female respondents in assessed communities where concerns in the delivery of humanitarian assistance were reported as opposed to 37% male respondents).

5 - Implementation of activities

All activities will be implemented in northern syria, Activities will be implemented directly, Individual PSS Services An integrated and holistic approach to MHPSS service delivery for those with MNS Disorders and/or living with acute and chronic psychological distress will be provided at Primary Healthcare Centers. This approach will include Mental Health Case Management, including brief psychological and advanced psychosocial support via trained and supervised Psychosocial Workers (PSWs). PSWs will provide follow up and referral/linking with other services as required as part of the case management approach as well as ongoing, targeted interventions such as training on coping, self-help and problem management techniques in order to empower service users to take more control of their wellbeing. Group Support In order to compliment individual PSS services, PSWs will facilitate Peer Group Activities which will compliment individual support for PSS clients and enhance functionality and positive coping and wellbeing for care givers to promote empowerment of positive wellbeing, improve social skills, strengthen the role of participants in the family and the community by providing them with an opportunity to increase their functioning and social interactions through exercise, mutual support, skills-building activities, psychoeducation and coping techniques and strategies. Additionally, ADH’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) unit conducts Post Distribution Monitoring (PDMs) s with a random sample of protection recipients each month, and supports programme learning and development by assessing the impact of the program. ADH’s Protection Guidelines outline proper coordination of protection assessments and ensure decision-making is informed by three considerations (sustainability, suitability, and safety), all of which must be met for inclusion.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of sexual exploitation: All MHPSS staff will receive SGBV training and will know how and when to report suspected cases of exploitation. Each PSS team will employ an equal or similar number of male and female staff, so that beneficiaries will have a choice of the gender of case manager, PSW, CSW that they feel more comfortable receiving services from. If beneficiaries are making a completing a referral with another service provider they may ask their case manager to accompany them. All PSS staff will be trained on internal PSEA guidelines also. Risk of violence/harm toward staff: Staff never works alone, either inside the clinic or in the community. Security guards will be employed at each PHC/PSS Center. Staff providing outreach, emergency outreach and community activities and events will be participated in by pairs and/or full teams. Each PSS staff member will have a mobile phone with them also and check in with their team leader regularly. Risk of work related stress, burnout and vicarious trauma: Staff Wellbeing is provided by the organisation in the form of activities, staff welfare meetings, trainings on stress reduction, positive coping, provided by the staff wellbeing officer, as well as referral to more advanced services should they be required.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk of military incursion, forcing cessation of service delivery: A contingency plan will be in place for such as event, where PSS beneficiaries will be provided with prior training on personal wellness plans, designed with beneficiaries and caregivers for personal follow up care in case MHPSS services provision would have to stop for a period of time.
