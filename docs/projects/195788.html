Project Summary

Farmers’ inability to afford inputs including fertilizers, herbicides, and pesticides due to climbing prices over the past year have resulted in poor production and crop failures across NES in 2021 with CARI severity scores of 4 (extreme) across the region. For all sub-districts in DZ, RQ and HK, the CARI agricultural production score as of October 2021 was level 5 (catastrophic). Without sufficient income many farmers are likely to face further deterioration in food security, and to acquire necessary inputs for the next planting season further compromising the 2022/23 harvest cycle. The high cost of agricultural machinery for ploughing and harvest due to expense of fuel and lack of available equipment further compromise production. To mitigate this production decline, this project will support young people with family lands who wish to resume farming activities. Support will take the form of training, the provision of agricultural inputs (seeds, fertilisers, pesticides) and assistance with irrigation, ploughing and harvest across two calendar year in three governorates Deir-ez-Zor, Raqqa and Hasakeh, reaching 19683 direct beneficiaries and 1,085,500 indirect beneficiaries (Oct 21 pop.), implemented in collaboration with the NES FSL through cluster response packages, technical guidance and coordination with all FSL actors.
1. Irrigation
The lands of farmers will be irrigated to enable improved crop production amounting to 22480 dunums total (DZ: 12480; RQ: 4000; HK: 6000). Care will be taken to ensure that water sources are not over sourced which could disrupt production of existing farmers. Only farmers with access to a well will be selected for irrigation reparation and rehabilitation. In DZ, 12480 dunums will be irrigated.
2. Crop Production
In Deir Ez-Zor and Raqqa the project will focus on crops that are in high local demand (wheat, cotton, corn, rice, vegetables, potatoes, fodder for livestock) however in Hasakeh, owing to the water shortages, there will be a focus on tunnel farming cultivation using drip irrigation methods. The project will additionally assist 2000 people in Hasakeh with inputs to start household level cultivation. In total 5470 people will benefit from agricultural inputs. A total of 9,980 people will be employed to support harvesting as seasonal workers. Crop and fodder production will return 17,500 donums of land to cultivation in DZ and RQ and support 600 greenhouses in HK. The total number of crops that will be cultivated from this land is 19,600,000 kilos.
3.Agricultural machinery maintenance and repair
Given northeast Syria’s position as the former ‘breadbasket of Syria’ and capacity for farming, this programme with create skilled professions through the revival of the agricultural industry. CAID identifies a job creation as minimum of 3-4 month full time paid employment at the minimum wage. This applies to all direct beneficiaries, which include national project staff in-country, trainers, trainee technician beneficiaries, and un-skilled assistants to trainee technicians. The project will generate income opportunities for 5,456 people through vocational training of sustainable, modernised farming, including Repairing generator engines; Repairing Tractor and agricultural engines and maintenance; Provision of rice harvesters, ploughing equipment, and training on how to drive tractors. The intervention will be localised, so that skilled income opportunities are varied subject to the specific conditions of the markets, agricultural conditions, geography and topography and labour demands of the area.
4. Agricultural Marketing and Manufacturing
Facilitating market linkages for producers and selling produce from the above farmers, facilitating off-taker contracts for farmers with wholesalers, supporting market sellers with selling produce generated by farmer beneficiaries, manufacturing and selling non-fuel generators and providing in-kind support to agricultural and livestock start-up enterprises.

3 - Needs

Agricultural production has deteriorated substantially as a consequence of the destruction of tools and irrigation systems; the scarcity and high prices of agricultural inputs and fuel; inputs of unreliable quality; damaged irrigation systems; low levels of mechanization; shortages and the high-cost of farm labor; destruction of standing crops, and difficulties for farmers to access their lands in many regions of NES. The country has transitioned from being an exporter to an importer of wheat and the Government of Syria's measures aimed at better managing wheat stocks – doubling the cost of subsidized bread, reducing its size and capping the number of bread bundles that families can buy – have transferred the economic costs to the Syrian people. The effects are felt most keenly in NES where it was the region’s primary employer, and the collapse of this agricultural industry has compounded the vulnerability of the unemployed and pushed many into food insecurity. Farmers also suffer from limited access to markets due to physical and economic constraints, limited access to finance, lack of water for irrigation due to infrastructural damage, limited access to relevant early warning information and the impact of COVID-19 on agricultural value chains. OCHA’s HNO (2021) notes that people’s ability to buy basic food nationwide has been severely compromised with 50% spending at least 75% of their income on food and 28% are adopting crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of discrimination/perceptions of discrimination if agricultural inputs are provided to specific groups/individuals at the exclusion of others. Mitigating measures: 1. Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed; 2. Ensure consultation with agricultural associations and local councils related to location understand the individual community perspectives; 3. Carry out safety audits for beneficiaries’ access to locations of cultivationRisk of GBV from exposed, rural areas when picking/harvesting crops. Mitigating measures: Ensure female harvesters are never alone when in the fields; B) Consultations with women and men should be organized among the project with training centre staff and engineers.Risk of Injury to project staff and beneficiaries (ERW, Hazardous Materials) Mitigating measures: Site safety assessment completed by agricultural engineers; Inspection reports to be completed prior to undertaking work; No farming undertaken unless signed off by CAID technical expert; No farming undertaken at sites with suspicion of ERW threats; Clear SOPs for dealing with ERW discovery; Clear SOPs for managing hazardous materials;


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse among staff towards beneficiaries. Mitigating measures: CAID Best Practice Guidelines agreed by all stakeholders; Clear anti-fraud, corruption, abuse and harassment SOPs and training; bribery and corruption clauses included in contractual commitments and policies; ; Sensitize stakeholders and community about PSEA and how to report incidents of harassment SEA, as well as abuse, fraud, bribery and corruption on the whistleblowing hotline by distributing cards with numbers. The project has a Community Response Mechanism (CRM) where participants can directly provide feedback or complaints about the centre and activities and staff and systems via email, whatsapp messaging, telephone and designated complaints boxes. Continually over the two year project, CAID Field Officers will be responsible for disseminating CRM cards to beneficiaries and their household's and give clear guidelines on why and how all feedback should be reported. CAID and the partners' Code of Conduct and Associated policies are in line with the principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Partners' safeguarding and child protection codes of conduct are vetted by CAID HQ DD team. Risks related to this project have been identified in a separate risk analysis and mitigation measures identified together with the partners in line with do no harm principles.
