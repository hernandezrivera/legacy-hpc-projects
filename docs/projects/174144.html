Project Summary

The PROBES project is in line with WHO’s strategic shift to the Global programme of work13 (GPW13) goal which focuses on 3 interconnected strategic priorities aimed at ensuring healthy lives and promotion of wellbeing at all ages i.e Achieving universal health coverage (UHC), addressing health emergencies and promoting healthier population. All this aligns to the Health Sector priorities i.e Maintenance and continuation of essential health care services in all priority locations and IDP camps and expansion of health services in hard to reach accessible areas and underserved communities. The project will take into consideration the current needs and gaps in the ongoing humanitarian situation in the BAY states and undertake measures to decrease morbidity and mortality among vulnerable groups, including older people and those with underlying health conditions and disabilities.
PROBES is a 12 months’ project aim at reducing mortality and morbidity in areas with low and no health service coverage by ensuring increase access to high impact health and nutrition services for the targeted population. It more particularly aims at:
• Delivering of extended basic and essential health packages in areas with low/no access through a modulation of health delivery platforms taking into account the situation in the targeted areas (security, number of facilities, population, etc.) to ensure comprehensive access to quality care combining treatment of common ailment, antenatal care to pregnant women, immunization, vitamin A and deworming to children, screening of children for malnutrition, health promotion activities and outbreak detection, integrated Community Case Management of childhood illnesses (incl. treatment of malaria, pneumonia and diarrhea, screening for potential high-risk health conditions, health education services and referral to health and vaccination services) and medical support for non-communicable diseases, including GBV, HIV, STI, mental health, etc.
• Establishing strong ambulance service and referral links between the complicated lifesaving health services and the secondary levels of care available in the targeted states to ensure progressive integration of the services provided through the MHT and CORPs into a strengthened state health system
PROBES will target 783,00 people distributed as 250,560 IDPs, 156,600 Returnees and 375,840 Host community in BAY States. The project implementation will be achieving through various platforms of service delivery to include: Hard to reach mobile health teams (HTR MHT) providing basic essential PHC services, Community Oriented Resource Persons (CORPs) dealing with integrated community case management of childhood illnesses within children homes in the community, interventions to curtail the burden of malaria, strengthening PHC services to provide mental health and GBV services as well as having a robust referral system to secondary and tertiary health institution for further care.
The proposed intervention has been designed with measures to prevent or minimize any unintended negative effects and protection concerns for women and children would be ensured at all health care services including Gender based Violence interventions would render with dignity.
WHO is committed to promoting Accountability to the affected population, for the project by involving the beneficiaries in the design of the project and taken into account feedback from the beneficiaries in the monitoring of the intervention.

1 - Needs

Health Resources and Service Availability Monitoring assessment report presents baseline data from 809 assessed health facilities across 27 LGAs in Borno State. Only 41% of the health facilities in those LGAs are fully functional, while up to 48% of the facilities are not functioning (including fully damaged ones) and 11% partially functioning. Additionally, 64% of the assessed facilities were either fully (31%) or partially (33%) damaged with only 34% of the facilities not damaged. Non-functionality of the health facilities resulted from damage to infrastructures (60%) and lack of access due to security constraints (32%). From the fully and partially functioning facilities, just over 25% had operational in-patient services, 20% had trauma services, and 25% had the necessary stocks of essential medicines and supplies. The increase in insecurity and hostilities has had serious implications for health service delivery as the conflict continuously hampers the movement of health workers and patients, the supply of drugs and other commodities. This leads to a shortage of skilled healthcare workers, particularly doctors, nurses and midwives to provide services. Increased new displacements has contributed to shrinking of humanitarian access in BAY States with resultant shifts in access and potential increases in humanitarian needs. Cost: Access to health services is also hampered by high medical costs as reported by 75% of IDPs, 61% of returnees and 80% of non-displaced householdsGender based violence remains a key challenge in the BAY states, especially with female IDPs who are frequently exposed to sexual violence, forced prostitution and childhood marriages. Mental health remains a health challenge in BAY States, statistics indicate that around 20% of affected people in humanitarian emergencies develop mental health conditions. WHO started to address mental health in 2017 in NEN by ensuring delivery of appropriate quality mental health care services in non-specialized health center’s to affected populations. Malaria remains a leading cause of ill health in Africa and Nigeria. From world malaria report, 2018 53million annual cases in Nigeria (1 in 4 persons), contributing 25% global burden and 53% of cases in West Africa. In Nigeria alone, 81,640 deaths are recorded annually (9 deaths per hour), which accounts for 19% global malaria deaths (1 in 5 global malaria deaths) and 45% malaria deaths in west Africa. The Nigeria Malaria Strategic Plan (NMSP) 2014-2020 has as it goal- to reduce malaria burden to pre-elimination levels and bring malaria-related mortality to zero. According to NDHS 2018 report, malaria prevalence in the country stands at 23% amongst children under five. Fever in children in north east is 35% while state prevalence range is (Borno: 2%- 12%; Adamawa and Yobe: 13% – 22%). Data from the WHO Early Warning and Response System in of February 2020 indicate that malaria compounded by malnutrition is the leading cause of morbidity and mortality for 31.5% of cases and 22.5% of reported deaths. Analysis commissioned by the WHO Global Malaria Programme to guide the next steps in the response to the malaria interventions in the complex humanitarian emergency in Borno concluded that “malaria is the number one health risk that the populations in the affected areas in NE Nigeria are confronting. It was not only the number one cause of morbidity during the past 9 months, but one can predict with great certainly that it is going to be the main cause of disease and death over the coming months as malaria transmission picks up, unless something drastic is done”. Gender-based violence (GBV) has always been a feature of the humanitarian crisis in north-east Nigeria, as NSAGs have targeted women and girls and committed massive human rights violations, including abduction, forced marriage and rape. Women and girls who have been abducted by armed groups face stigmatization, suspicion and rejection when they return home.

2 - Activities

In 2021, WHO aims to strengthen the BAY states’ capacity to provide high quality essential health services to People in need, in the areas of. o GVB: A comprehensive capacity strengthening package for health workers on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) to increase access to quality care for all victims of GBV across the BAY states. To further address the mental health burden associated with GBV, MHPSS will be integrated in GBV services to ensure timely detection and response to mental health conditions in victims of GBV. o Mental Health: WHO will improve MHPSS service provision by supporting referral structures to the Federal Neuro Psychiatric Hospital (FNPH) and by supporting case management in host community clinics and IDP camps. To enhance sustainability, WHO will support Mental Health Gap Action Program (mhGAP) response at PHC levels.Community Mental Health Outreach services involves procurement and provision of psychotropic drugs, community screening and treatment of mental health conditions referral of complicated mental conditions to the secondary level, management chronic mental health illness counseling to GBV clients. Psychiatric nurses from Federal Neuro Psychiatric Hospital (FNPH) will be conducting MH outreach sessions at the selected health facilities. Services will be provided at fixed health facilities made up of PHC and IDP Camp clinics. o Hard-to-Reach Mobile Health Teams (H2R MHT): Through the deployment of H2R teams, WHO will ensure comprehensive access to quality and high impact health services to the targeted beneficiaries by delivering comprehensive free quality lifesaving health services that will include, treatment of common ailments, antenatal care to pregnant women, vaccines, provision of immunization, vitamin A and deworming to children, screening of children for malnutrition, Strong community engagement, risk communication, health promotion activities outbreak detection.’, Referral to the secondary level for management, First line support for gender Based victims and HIV counselling and testing. o Community Oriented Resource Persons (CORPs): The CORP provides Integrated Community Case Management (iCCM) of childhood illness. CORPs members are selected through involvement of community leaders and supervised by health workers from the nearest health facilities. Each CORP is expected to treat 50 children per month. Through the deployment of CORPs and supervisors, WHO will provide iCCM services addressing the main vulnerabilities and risks affecting this target group including: treatment of malaria, pneumonia and diarrhea, screening for malnutrition and other potential high-risk health conditions, health promotion services and referral for health and vaccination services. o Malaria: WHO will continue to support response activities by providing support to the states and partners. To target children and the general population in 2021, WHO will expand SMC and improve case management by training 900 health workers on diagnosis, data capturing and management of uncomplicated malaria. In addition, WHO will continue to support behavioural change communication activities for Malaria prevention. WHO will provide a comprehensive Malaria prevention and response package in IDP camps and host community in North East Nigeria.o Tuberculosis, HIV/AIDS and Hepatitis, Neglected Tropical Diseases, Non-Communicable Diseases, Environmental Determinants of Health: WHO will support the states and partners in addressing the disease burdens of Tuberculosis, HIV/AIDS and Hepatitis, Neglected Tropical Diseases, and Non-Communicable Diseases through strengthening referral systems, advocacy and sensitization, and active case search. o Referral: WHO aims to increase local ownership for referral services at the secondary care level by signing Memorandums of Understanding (MoU) with Hospital Management Boards across the BAY states.
