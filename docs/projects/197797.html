1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Based on the Shelter NFI cluster strategy and districts accessibility, returns and displacement, 32,000 persons are supported in Shelter and NFI. Type of shelters will be based on the vulnerability criteria and situation of the persons in each targeted district. Persons in situation of return or newly accessible will benefit from complete NFI kits and sustainable shelters improving living condition and dignity. New displaced persons will be accompanied through survival light kits distribution, and persons in resettlement sites will be supported with NFI and transitional shelter. Shelter support will be based on vulnerability criteria integrating sepcifically PWSN (women, disable etc). All activities will be designed in collaboration with the affected population and community-designed to integrate PHP, age and gender specificities. Training and capacity reinforcement of the targeted population will be ensured as well as distribution of tools. The intervention will consider potential challenges regarding access to local materials, their effects on the environment and safeguard support from local authorities.In complement and to ensure transparency, accountability, monitoring and evaluation, the monitoring and evaluation (M&E) strategy is based on the principles of accountability and community participation. At an institutional level, SI has an established Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) policy.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project seeks to address shelter and NFI issues in transit, relocation, and returnee sites. The shelter response will aim to ensure coordinated and adequate delivery of shelter materials and tools to facilitate access to enhanced and fortified shelter solutions for targeted households. The intervention will consider potential challenges regarding access to local materials, their effects on the environment and safeguard support from local authorities. Where transitional shelters are used, they will allow a household to build a semi-permanent shelter with a lifespan of more than two years. The project aims at implementing a comprehensive response to the most urgent needs of IDPs, returnees and affected communities, developing a double approach: (i) Emergency rapid multi-sectorial response to support newly arrived IDP in Mueda transit centers, and returnees in Mocimboa. (ii) NFI and Shelter support to sustain safe and dignify access to basic needs of affected population (i) Distribution of a survival kits for a family of 5 persons, including: Tarpaulin, Mosquito nets, Blankets, Sleeping, mats, Kitchen sets, Solar lamps. All activities will be implemented involving IDPs and returnees. (ii) Distribution of NFI and shelter kits will be carried out in relocation centers where IDPs families are settling for longer term, as well affected host communities. Coordination will be ensured with Community leaders, authorities and humanitarian partners to set up sustainable activities, improving life condition of the affected communities in the targeted districts. Capacity building and community-based activities will include PWSN at all stage of the project. Solidarites will identify and verify households, in some case using status-based targeting and in other cases vulnerability-based targeting. PDM will be conducted after final rounds of distributions to verify that items have been used correctly and to adapt future activities.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

SI is committed to ensuring the protection of affected populations from sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by institutionalizing and integrating PBA approaches into the program cycle. SI will use the “Ligna Verde” complaint mechanism for reporting of PSEA cases as well as other mechanisms, including SI internal mechanism. The complaint mechanism system will be adapted in order to ensure accessibility to all. Communities will be involved in deciding the best complaint mechanisms to ensure safe and confidential access for all. Through every step of planning, implementation, and evaluation, SI will follow its internal policy and guidelines regarding prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse and will systematically integrate them to induction trainings for expatriate and national staff.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Beyond program design or activity selection, certain choices will be made in order to minimize the protection risks during the implementation stage and to integrate protection. Persons of heightened risk – including female headed households, very large families, child headed-households, single women living alone, single parents or caregivers, individuals with medical conditions including physical and mental disabilities, survivors of GBV or trauma, and others, will be carefully identified through community consultations ensuring a “do no harm” approach. Implementation of the activities will be community-based, and sensitasation will be tailored to support integration of vulnerable individuals and households. The Complaints Response Mechanism will be adapted to ensure that written, verbal, and confidential reports can be received regardless of access to mobile phone, charge, or literacy level. Beneficiaries are engaged via focus group discussions to define appropriate shelter types, list of materials, and construction techniques which are beneficiary-led. The outcome of the pilot will inform the final design including size, list of materials, type of labour, knowledge/capacities of the beneficiaries, availability of local materials, timeframe needed to support the construction of one shelter and the overall cost, among others. The designs will incorporate beneficiary feedback throughout the construction process and act as a point of reference during the implementation phase.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Implementation of activities will be community-based, and sensitization enhanced to lead the community to integrate and support the most vulnerable persons. Activities will be adapted, and most vulnerable beneficiaries supported to ensure and completed integration and allow meaningful access to the services provided (provision of labour, material, adaptation of the infrastructures etc.). To verify the efficiency of these choices, the Complaints Response Mechanism will help the organization to readjust its practices during the intervention and function as a real-time monitoring tool. All infrastructure included (including sanitation facilities) will incorporate additional needs for those with disabilities or who require additional support.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

SI always employs use of local materials and sources all materials from local markets when and where available. For this project, it includes bamboo, wood poles, etc. Efforts are also in progress to enhance rain water collection to address water issues in a sustainable manner. Kits could include solar powered lamps as well.In order to mitigate and reduce the impact that we may have on the environment, actions such as reducing packaging, eliminating single use plastics, purchasing materials with a lower carbon footprint, providing fuel-efficient stoves, and solar powered lamps will be integrated into emergency responses.
