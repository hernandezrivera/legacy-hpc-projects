Objectives

1. To ensure regular, sufficient, equitable and dignified access for all refugees living in camps and affected Host Communities to sufficient and safe water for drinking and domestic needs. 2. To ensure all refugees living in camps and affected Host Communities have adequate, appropriate and acceptable sanitation facilities to allow rapid, safe and secure access at all times.3. To ensure the change of potentially dangerous behaviours through access and exposure to innovative hygiene promotion (including hygiene items) for all refugees living in camps and affected host communities.

Needs

As a result of the protracted context of systematic discrimination, statelessness, human rights deprivation and violence in the Rakhine State of Myanmar, Rohingyas fled to Bangladesh – the majority of them settling in the southern tip of Cox’s Bazar district. The latest massive influx which started in August 2017 has put an enormous pressure on the Bangladeshi community and natural resources in Cox’s Bazar, with Rohingyas in the camps accounting for at least 906526 refugees (RRRC-UNHCR Family Counting exercise). Additionally, many Rohingyas from previous influxes since the 90’s still reside across the host community villages of the Ukhia and especially Teknaf Upazila. The Government of Bangladesh has maintained the border open to new arrivals, as the situation in Rakhine state has not seen any positive evolution, with 1,030 Rohingya new arrivals recorded in Cox’s Bazar since January 2019 (UNHCR Population Factsheet June 2019). However, as per latest reports, the border is not anymore open to new arrivals as of August 2019. Teknaf Upazila in particular remains extremely affected by the additional pressure on resources resulting from the presence of the Rohingya population.From a natural resources point of view, the area of Teknaf has historically been suffering from lack of access to freshwater due to its specific hydrogeological context, with critical peaks during the dry season. The additional pressure posed by the extensive number of wells to supply the camps further stressed the groundwater situation, in combination with a more extensive use of existing surface water sources. Moreover, access to improved sanitation facilities remains limited in host communities (HC). The context exacerbates protection risks, especially dangerous forms of child labor, severe forms of exploitation, SGBVs and early and forced marriage. Besides, life in the camps remain difficult, although services provision has been increased and needs are better covered by humanitarian actors on the ground, in line with the last Joint Response Plan (JRP). Even though last JRP Mid Term Review (MTR) figures show a continued effort in improving life of communities in the camps, only half of them think that the aid they receive currently covers their most important needs . This situation is mostly due to the structural lack of livelihood opportunities , the significant protection challenges and gaps still existing across the response and the remaining gaps in terms of equitable access to basic services. WASH remains an important need for both host communities and refugee camps. Rohingyas in Teknaf Upazilas are facing interrelated vulnerabilities associated with natural hazards, congested settlements and associated public health risks due to an access to basic WaSH services still insufficient. Safe drinking water is still reported as the second most important unmet need in host communities , while is the third one in Rohingya camps. Besides these needs, the operation & maintenance of existing WaSH facilities remains of fundamental importance for the life of refugees who still lack any access to livelihood and sustainable income sources, thus making it impossible for them to bear such costs and repairs without humanitarian actors’ support. Hygiene promotion also still represent a necessary action to tackle the underlying causes of water contamination at point-of-use and to ensure safe sanitation management behaviors. More participatory processes need to be established, again benefitting from the volunteers support in the camps and with tailored and experimented approaches in host communities. Overall, most people attained good knowledge of basic hygiene practices by now. However, some gaps remain in the consistent practicing of positive hygiene behaviors, with contamination of water at point-of-use, solid waste management and under-5 excreta disposal remaining some of the points of concerns.

Activities or outputs

SI will continue its work from 2019 in improving the conditions of sanitation and hygiene in the areas of intervention (camps and host communities). SI will be aiming to provide a full WaSH package to those its targets including; bathing cubicles, latrines, handwashing points, desludging and hygiene promotion sessions at both household and community levels. In addition SI will continue to provide water for its target population (which normally exceeds those who receive full WaSH package), the project will aim to uphold standards of 15 litres per day per person. In addition, SI will continue to maintain, upgrade and develop new sustainable water systems in geographically viable locations. SI aims to use new and existing technology to make the most of the challenging water tables and unique topography. In this scope SI is looking to develop a specific with Ground Water Relief, an organization specifized in aquifer modeling and water management capacity building. FSM sites have been a real focus of SI works through 2018, and will continue to be a key point through 2019. Si aims to modify the treatment plants, installed during the first phase of the emergency, by providing more efficient and sustainable systems and continue supporting the ongoing desludging that SI covers in all camps we have a presence in. SI would like also to build additional stations in camps that currently do not have these facilities, in collaboration with the larger systems already planned (by UNHCR) in several camps. All new builds, rehabilitations and upgraded designs as going to be both resilient (thus including DRR planning) and inclusive (thus ensuring an integrated protection approach) to ensure that all WaSH services provided by SI are accessible to all. All activities will be following sector standards Piloting projects (as bathing place at HH level) will be continued and evaluated. As per SI mandate the activities will be covered in both camps and communities. However, to be able to retain focus on quality and quantity, SI will strategically withdraw from one camp in 2019 ensuring full handover with other actors, so that it can instead ensure less people are reached with a higher quality output. To do this SI will gradually withdraw from camp 26. As a final note, SI also intends to target WaSH in schools for Host communities, developing and enhancing infrastructure to ensure that children have access to water and sanitation.
