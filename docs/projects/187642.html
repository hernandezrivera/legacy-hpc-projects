Project Summary

This project delivers a strategic and integrated set of interventions to restore the local economy in Northwest. Idleb and Aleppo in the aftermath of protracted conflict further aggravated by the impact of the COVID19 pandemic, and deteriorating economic situation. The main objective is to create income-generating opportunities for the most vulnerable IDPs and host community members in the targeted areas. The project proposes an integrated livelihood and infrastructures rehabilitation intervention where the most vulnerable households (HHs) will be directly targeted to enhance their resilience and promote their access to employment and entrepreneurship opportunities. Besides, firsthand consultations, conducted specifically for this project with local stakeholders, have also informed the design of this proposal. By pursuing the referred main objective, the project aims to achieve the following outcomes:
1- Access to inclusive livelihoods and entrepreneurship support is enhanced through vocational training, where unemployed people (females and males) receive livelihoods support through vocational training and micro-grants at Dana sub-district in 4 communities (Atma, Qah, Aqrabat, and Mashhad Rohin).
2- Essential socioeconomic services are restored through rehabilitating public parks and markets in Al-Bab, Azaz, and Jarablus communities, and providing cash for work against working in community campaigns.
For this purpose, a set of interlinked activities will be conducted in coordination with ERL and Protection clusters. Firstly, the project foresees the identification of 300 HHs with a specific focus on severe economic vulnerability to provide them with Vocational training. 100 of the trainees will receive micro-grants support for start-up projects. Another 50 trainees will be directed to shops/workshops where they can work there. These shops/workshops will be supported to employ these people and develop their enterprises. Moreover, these activities will target both host communities and IDPs to enhance social cohesion in targeted communities as well as incresing the coordinaation and harmonization among targeted people. Secondly, 6 essential socioeconomic infrastructures will be rehabilitated in Azaz, Al-Bab, and Jarablus communities, where a park and a market will be rehabilitated in each community. In addition, 450 unskilled workers of the most vulnerable HHs will receive short-term work opportunities through conditional cash-for-work for 2 months for conducting community campaigns like road cleaning, road afforestation, and sidewalk restoration and painting campaigns. Moreocer, local scouncils, key people will be consulted regarding CFW intervention in order to apply Do No Harm in the targeted communities regarding daily wages, number of working hours per day, and duration of of the project, and to ensure project CFW activities in the targeted communitaties will not negatively affect the local labour market and will not draw workers away from local emplyment opportunities

4 - Needs

Early recovery needs remain considerable in scale, severity, and complexity across Syria, with an estimated 11.6 million people needing dignifying early recovery and livelihood support, 74 percent of the available basic infrastructures facilities are reported damaged, with 53 percent of them reporting significant damage. (HNO-2021)The poor conditions of access ways and roads affected by ten years of conflict, in addition to the harm caused because of natural events such as heavy rains, negatively affect the possibility to boost the speed of local economic recovery. Similarly, markets and shops’ poor conditions hinder the extent to which economic transactions can trigger positive chain reactions vis-à-vis access to livelihood opportunities. Specifically, 51 per cent of the existing markets are damaged and 14 per cent of them result extremely damaged. (HNO-2021)Based on the needs assessment conducted by ATAA team, there are many main roads connecting districts to villages and camps were found in need of a complete repair process, including graveling and paving and lighting, in addition to rehabilitating public parks to be available to all residents of these areas, including the host community and the IDPs, which increases the state of social cohesion among them, as well as enhancing access to inclusive livelihoods and entrepreneurship support is throw through vocational trainings.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Poor knowledge delivered to trainees. Impact: Low qualified graduated trainees and lack of job opportunities. Mitigation: high experienced trainers will be hired to deliver high-quality information. Risk 2: Project items not available in the market to be provided. Impact: Beneficiaries will not be able to implement/develop their projects.Mitigation: availability of products in the market will be one of the criteria to select successful proposals to be funded with micro-grants. Risk 3: Fluctuation in rehabilitation general costs and raw materials. Impact: Poor rehabilitation quality.Mitigation: the technical assessment will be followed immediately by the determination of quantities and this will be followed by the tendering process, therefore the tendering process will be based on updated prices. Risk 4: Delay in implementation activities. Impact: Rehabilitation works are delayed rainy season.Mitigation: Regular follow-up to the work plan, and good coordination with local communities and local councils, the rehabilitation works will be conducted in the dry season. Risk 5: High and critical markets volatilityImpact: Rehabilitation may not be handed over at the required quality.Mitigation: Market assessment will be conducted at the beginning of the project.
