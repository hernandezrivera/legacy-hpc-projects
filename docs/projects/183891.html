1 - Needs

The 2022 HNO identifies 2.4 million IDPs, returnees and vulnerable residents as having shelter and NFI needs, driven by the multitude of conflict-related, ecological, and economic factors, further exacerbated by the still looming threat of COVID 19. The sector will target 1.3 million people with most severe needs (56% of the PiN) in 77 localities across Darfurs, Kordofans, Blue Nile and flood-affected states, by delivering timely emergency assistance to newly displaced as well as addressing mid to longer-term needs of returnees and communities living in protracted displacement, through the following two objectives: • Provide lifesaving NFI assistance to crisis affected populations• Enable crisis affected populations to access adequate shelter solutions that provide protection, safety, security, and space to live in a dignified mannerImmediate emergency response will continue to be critical in light of the deteriorating security situation and recurrent flooding events that drive displacement, and will require country-wide prepositioning of contingency stocks of non-food items and emergency shelter kits. Where conducive conditions exist, the sector will aim to secure more permanent shelter solutions for returnees and communities living in protracted displacement, especially considering the strong prevalence of emergency/ makeshift shelter typologies and overall poor shelter conditions where as much as 68% of affected households report living in damaged shelters. Protracted caseload assistance will also include targeted replenishment of household items necessary to support daily domestic activities. Considering Shelter and Settlements are inextricably linked, which becomes increasingly important in protracted displacement, the sector will focus on small-scale settlement development and maintenance activities to improve accessibility, access to essential services, and increase resilience to hazards which will benefit both displaced and surrounding hosting populations, decrease the strain on already limited resources as well as support peaceful coexistence and contribute to overall peacebuilding efforts.

2 - Activities/Outputs

UNHCR activities for 2022 will include overall need assessments, verification of beneficiaries with partners and government counterparts, planning , dispatch and distribution of shelter &NFIs and post distribution monitoring through PDM partner or counterpart. First) preparedness will be ensured by timely procurement and pre-positioning of key NFI kits in strategic locations by UNHCR as the manager for the NFI Core Pipeline on behalf of the Shelter/NFI Sector in Sudan. This will support front line responders in hot-spots receiving IDPs, and in newly accessed locations and communities affected by conflict, floods or fires. Second) UNHCR will also support beneficiaries in those areas chronically affected by floods to organize preparedness committees and do sensitization and awareness on disaster preparedness like techniques and guidelines to improve their settlements against fire outbakes or floods. This could also include trainings on shelter construction techniques that could support the communities to improve their shelters to reduce the risk of fire or include their water collection and build back better techniques. Third) UNHCR comprehensive response is to ensure timely emergency shelter and NFI assistance is provided to people affected by crisis or natural disasters. Emergency shelter and NFIs materials are purchased internationally and/or locally by UNHCR supply unit and delivered to the UNHCR warehouses in Khartoum (Central Warehouse), Kosti(supporting White Nile State),El Obeid (supporting South and West Kordofan), and Nyala (supporting East Darfur) and onward to partners at the various distribution locations as and when required. Given the limitation of resources, UNHCR will strengthen coordination of resources with partners to ensure coverage of emergency shelter & NFI needs. As sector lead UNCHR is flexible with the localities targeted to be able to reach everyone in need in case a new displacement of crises occurs and there is lack of resources or parnters in the area. Also, UNHCR is presence in the main IDP camps and settlements that receive regular influx of displaced. While looking for shelter support IDPs shelter themselves in gathering points that usually are government facilities increasing the tension with the host community and affecting the functionality of the institutions like schools. In orther to be able to support to government counterparts when this situations occure, UNCHR is willing to build collective shelters in the key displacement localities like for instance Kriding camps in El Geneina. Fourth) UNHCR will continue ongoing activities that support durable solutions and support the transition from dependency to self-reliance. This includes income-generating and livelihood activities, including training on basic construction, skills, production of domestic items and construction of fuel-efficient stoves. The need to continue recovery and longer-term development activities in Darfur is considered a priority and therefore shelter upgrades/repair and construction of durable shelter will be needs based and will prioritize identified people with specific needs (PSN) and most vulnerable individuals (EVIs) in line with the protection vulnerability criteria. The modalities of the distribution can be either in kind or cash depending on the context of the locality, specific response plan and market assessments. Feasibility analysis will consider prevailing market conditions; the availability of financial services; the preferences of the affected population; the safety and security of beneficiaries and host community; and collateral effects on social cohesion. Simultaneous community training on building techniques will be conducted in line with humanitarian early recovery activities, to promote self-reliance and provide some livelihood opportunities.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

UNHCR will integrate environmental considerations ensuring that the proposed project will have a positive and sustainable environmental impact by encouraging partners to work with beneficiaries on disaster risk activities such as awareness sessions, gender balance, environmental impacts, and safe construction of shelter and disposal of NFIs. The training sessions will also promote alternatives to firewood, such as fuel-efficient cooking and livelihood options to preserve environment. Furthermore, UNHCR will promote shelter solutions considering the geographical context, climate, cultural practice and habits of the communities serve, local availability of skills and access to adequate and locally available construction materials that ensure minimum impact in the local environment.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

NHCR aims to strengthen protection, gender mainstreaming and accountability to affected populations as they link with the principle of “do no harm”. The particular needs of such individuals will be reflected in NFI assessments, programme design, implementation and monitoring and evaluation. In these areas, the sector will ensure the following elements are taken into account: (1) safety, security, and dignity; (2) equitable access; (3) accountability through community-based feedback mechanisms, with information collected based on on-site and post-distribution monitoring that will feed into the sector’s program cycle; and (4) sharing information, consultation, participation and community empowerment. The sector will work with partners to strengthen the implementation of the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse through awareness-raising for both partners and suppliers. UNHCR within its capacity as Shelter/NFI Sector lead will advocate to draw attention to shelter issues such as housing, land and property rights, particularly for returnees and/or integrated IDPs. HLP due diligence tools will be set in place with cooperation of the HLP WG to harmonize the shelter responses among partners ensuring that before building durable shelter partners will ensure in cooperation with the communities that beneficiaries’ land if free of claim and refer those conflictive cases to protection partners providing ICLA support through a referral pathway. This is to reduce the risk of further harm and mainstream protection on the shelter construction programs.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

UNHCR will continue to improve PSEA across the operation. This includes strengthening the culture of accountability through the integration of PSEA actions within protection functions and broader accountability to affected populations (AAP) strategy. This will be achieved through regular PSEA training for UNHCR staff, partner staff, and contractors; regular SEA risk assessment and development and implementation of annual PSEA action plans for field offices and collaboration with operational partners to establish inter-agency and community-based complaint mechanisms accessible to IDPs and returnees. The Operation will continue to maintain its active participation in the inter-agency PSEA fora, including the RC/HC’s PSEA Network through which UNHCR will mainstream.
