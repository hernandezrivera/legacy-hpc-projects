Project Summary

Handicap International (HI) has been running its Inclusive Education (IE) projects for children with disabilities and 2 Adapted Learning Centers (ALC) for children with medium and severe disabilities for 3 years in Nayapara and Kutupalong Registered camps. HI strongly advocates for children with disabilities to be integrated into mainstream education whenever possible. That is why in 2020, HI will continue its IE projects through the provision of Individualized Education Action Plans to 334 children with disabilities meanwhile working on diminishing the social, communication and environmental barriers. HI will support 30 schools and 32 TLCs in both host communities and camps providing them with adapted education learning material (adapted chairs). Its support also includes accessibility works (ramps, accessible latrines) for 31 facilities and the training of 124 teachers on IE, methodology, pedagogical techniques and materials, the set-up of Individualized Education Plan and their follow up. The teachers will learn the different types of disabilities and impairments and will benefit from adapted modules and tools to be implemented in the LC. HI will also ensure the Gvt Education facilities are accessible and inclusive, through the provision of accessibility works and awareness sessions on Disability and Inclusion, with a component of IDRR, at school-level for both parents and teachers. HI will continue to provide Speech and Language, Physical and Occupational therapy sessions to all children with disabilities in need, including the provision of assistive and mobility devices (bedsore mattresses, crutches), to improve their life conditions and to reinforce their autonomy. Training and advice will be provided to their caregivers on basic home-based rehabilitation and proper use, maintenance and repair of mobility devices. Finally, the team will ensure internal and external referrals to all necessary services for children with disabilities in need.
It is in addition to this approach that HI will strengthen its two ALC and establish two additional ALCs to support children with medium and severe disabilities who are not able to attend the regular schools. In these ALCs, focus will be given to conduct the life-skill learning approach adopting the Activities of Daily Living such as self-care, mobility, communication improvement through adapted learning materials. HI will base its approach on Early Childhood Development which encompasses physical, social, cognitive and motor development activities. Facilitators will ensure the day-to-day teaching and learning activities. The Rehabilitation Officers (RO) will provide Physiotherapy sessions to the children in need and will also train and guide the Facilitators. The RO will provide assistive devices to children with disabilities, training and advices to their caregivers on basic home-based rehabilitation and proper use, maintenance and repair of mobility devices. They will also benefit from the awareness sessions organized at community level under the IE project which will reduce the social stigma and the environmental barriers. Finally, the RO will ensure internal and external referrals to necessary services including Speech and Language, Occupational services. The Rehabilitation sessions will contribute to improve their life conditions and reinforce their autonomy. The team will coordinate with the CIC of the camps 4 and 27 to find a place to establish the 2 new facilities.
HI is an active member of the ADWG the aim of which is to mainstream Age and Disability across all sectors through the creation of tools, trainings on data collection (WGSQ), Inclusion Programming, type of disabilities, barriers and facilitators. HI will train 180 Education Sector partner staff.
Along with a strong standardized Complaints Feedback and Response Mechanism using different channels including individual and group feedback sessions and a hotline, HI ensures the respect and promotion of its PSEA policy.

Needs

In 2017, 721,641 Rohingya refugees fleeing violence and persecutions in Rakhine State, Myanmar have sought protection in Cox’s Bazar district, triggering the largest and fastest refugee influx into Bangladesh. Two years later, the Rohingya refugee community has stabilised around one million individuals living in extremely precarious conditions in 34 camps spread between Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazilas in Cox’s Bazar district. The average prevalence rate of persons with disabilities in the camps is 9% (from 2 to 15%), considering that children represent 55% (UNHCR, June 2019) of the overall population, the prevalence rate of children with disabilities in the camps is 5%.or approximately 45,550 individuals. Nayapara Registered Camp counts with 607 households reporting at least one person with disabilities, Kutupalong Registered Camp counts with 344 households reporting at least one person with disabilities, camp 4 counts with 203 households reporting at least one person with disabilities and camp 27 counts with 99 households reporting at least one person with disabilities (UNHCR, June 2019). The needs are high accross all camps with no NGO providing support to children with severe disabilities in the camps except HI. As HI is already working in the 4 mentioned camps through other existing programs, the first screening of the children with disabilities is ongoing which will ease the identification of children with medium and severe disabilities. In addition, children with disabilties are often excluded from mainstream education facilities in the camps. Indeed, the attendance rate for children with disabilities is 10 to 40% lower than children without disability (JRP 2020). Moreover, even if children with disabilities are enrolled in school, their participation is limited due to negative attitudes or conduct (discrimination, bullying, violence, neglect and abuse) experienced by the children with disabilities. They face other major barriers to accessing education such as a lack of adequate resources and support. As a result, the findings of the field assessment conducted by HI in Camp 27 showed that half of the children with disabilities interviewed did not attend any education facilities. Besides, those who did attend school reported that the facilities were not well equipped to meet their specific needs (i.e. ramps, larger doorways, accessible toilets, level school yards etc) (Jadimura Assessment, 2019). Following a data analysis of children with disabilities and their needs in Nayapara and Kutupalong Registered Camps and in some host communities villages, the findings show that the Finally, Inclusion has been raised as a strong necessity by all Education partners for 2020. Indeed, children with severe and moderate level of disabilities are often the first ones that are excluded as they require an extensive support which cannot be provided in an overcrowded classroom. That is why a double approach combining support to Education partners on Inclusion and Disability along with complementary learning opportunities for children with severe disabilities tailored to their specific needs and for them to develop their full potential is required to ensure access to Education for all children.
