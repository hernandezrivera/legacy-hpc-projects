Project Summary

This project is designed to ensure access to WASH services for all women, men, girls, boys with special consideration for Persons with Disabilities (PwD) in the Rohingya refugee camps and targeted host communities. The project will intervene in the three following subsectors:
Improved Water Services: two small-scale solar-powered piped water networks will be installed to provide safe water in the newly developed areas for the relocated. IOM will continue the operations, maintenance and upgrade of the existing deep tube wells in the camps within IOM’s area of responsibility (AoR) and of water networks in Camps 12, 13, 18, 19, 24, and 20 Extension.
In the newly developed areas, deep tube wells will be installed in camps and host communities to ensure that the most vulnerable people in need have improved and sustained access. IOM will design and upgrade surface water treatment and supply system in Camps 24 and 25 to cover the additional refugee and host community caseload. IOM will procure, replenish and replace the emergency stocks for cyclones, fires, floods, or other potential emergencies. IOM will also conduct a study to map the host community boreholes in Ukhiya and Balukhali areas exposed to groundwater levels reduction. IOM will also support the WASH sector in assessing the existing water network systems in camps to update the water strategy.
Access to Sanitation: To ensure dignified and safe access to latrine facilities, IOM will continue to upgrade the existing latrine facilities with gender-inclusive and disability-friendly features in consultation with the community (users) while considering the field needs. IOM will also construct gender-segregated WASH blocks (septic tank or pit type) in the newly developed valleys to avoid frequent desludging and risk of groundwater contamination in order to meet the sanitation needs of the relocated people. IOM will provide assistive devices (commode chair, commode chair with potty, urinal bottles for males and females) to persons with disabilities to ensure their unique sanitation requirements are met. IOM will continue the durable household latrine construction work to cover sanitation gaps at targeted host community villages in Ukhiya. Decentralized Wastewater Treatment Systems (DEWATS) will be built and the upgrade of lime-based fecal sludge treatment plants to DEWATS conducted to meet the coverage gaps. IOM will continue regular desludging and disinfection activities to ensure safe use of sanitation facilities. In order to ensure complete chain of Solid Waste Management (SWM), IOM will build Material Recovery Facilities-MRFs (small scale composting and segregation units) and secondary drain waste segregation sites at each camp based on the identified gaps and geographical coverage. The complete chain of SWM monitoring system will be continued and strengthened at all levels.
Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement: IOM will provide critical supplies of hygiene items (soap, water containers, Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) materials) and ensure the related community education and mobilization activities targeting all age groups, genders, and persons with special WASH needs. IOM will scale up the SCOPE distribution system in all camps within the IOM AoR to better support the people in need with hygiene kits . IOM will also pilot the production of reusable sanitary pads in Camp 9 to ensure appropriate distribution according to the needs of female beneficiaries of reproductive age. The community will be furthermore engaged in operations and maintenance of WASH facilities, waste segregation, and prevention of COVID-19 disease transmission. IOM will continue the implementation of a real-time service monitoring and community feedback mechanisms, which shall be strengthened and systematized to enhance accountability to the affected population (AAP).

1 - Identified Needs

Water Services: There is a total of 66 water networks in IOM AoR. 16 more are to be completed, and nine not yet started. Certain mini networks serve 76% of the population, although equitable access has not been assessed and assured. IOM water quality surveillance shows that a total of 37 per cent of tap stands were found fully functional, 56 per cent are functional but have various with repair needs, and 8 per cent are completely non-functional. Moreover, 71 per cent of the water networks do not comply with sector standards on Free Residual Chlorine (FRC) presence at distribution points. Access to water supply in Teknaf remains a major challenge during the dry season with water trucking and water diversion was needed in 2021. Frequent repair and maintenance of handpumps are necessary meet daily needs. The fire in Camp 9 underscored the need to ensure the possibility of a rapid provision of water supply or the availability of a viable source of drinking water with supplemented Aquatab distribution. Sanitation: Latrine coverage at the camp level meets the Sphere standards (1 latrine for 20 users), but the data at the block level shows that at least 5 of the 12 camps (Camps 9, 10, 11, 13, 24) are unable to facilitate the necessary minimum ratio due to the high population density. The average current functionality rate of the WASH facilities is 92 per cent and is a continual process for partners. Although the gap in bathing spaces has reduced, there has been a significant increase in private spaces, with 86 per cent of households reporting private bathing space inside but often those spaces are unhygienic and poorly maintained or used as alternatives for urination and defecation, with 11 per cent not connected to any form of drainage. Women and girls face issues of overcrowding and lack of privacy when using latrines and bathing spaces during the day, and at night risk harassment and violence with 95 per cent preferring private bathing spaces. Pilot programmes have been initiated to install WASH blocks in women-friendly spaces but there are challenges related to space and the lack of direct water supplies to each facility. A continual concern regarding sanitation is the coverage of the fecal sludge treatment as facilities require upgrades, improvements, or scale-up. Solid Waste Management remains one of the main challenges as only 51 per cent of the refugee households use designated areas for waste disposal, and 16 per cent reported disposing waste in the open areas. Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement: Hygiene knowledge and access to soap remain high although behavior change remains an issue that requires further research to assess the impact of knowledge, access, incentives and other factors. Access to MHM remains a barrier for many females caused by stigma, taboo and restrictive practices but also from a lack of MHM kit individuality due to common practices of one size fits all or variations of inter-sector kit and insufficient coordination. Most households in the host communities (88%) have access to water from tube wells, but only 77 per cent of households are reporting having enough water for their domestic needs. An additional 2,840 tube wells have been installed. Continued monitoring and updated modeling have shown that despite high pumping in the aquifers below megacamp, groundwater availability has been proven sustainable, however, the impact on the surrounding communities is significant due to depression from pumping cone requiring deeper tube wells and better handpumps in host communities. In 2020, 63 per cent of households have at least basic access to sanitation with an additional 7,000 latrines constructed in 2021, however, 20 per cent of the population reported using an unimproved sanitation facility. Host communities have been less supported by hygiene items and awareness-raising initiatives, but finding shows that 92 per cent of the households reported having access to soap.
