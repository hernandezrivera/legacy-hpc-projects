Project Summary

The school meals programme aims to restore and stabilize access to education and to enhance health and nutrition status of children by addressing short-term hunger and reducing micronutrient deficiencies particularly in areas hosting large number of affected populations, IDPs and returnees with low food security and education indicators . By encouraging regular attendance and enrollment, the programme contributes to providing protection to children from reverting to negative coping mechanism such as child labor and child marriage in addition to the education, health, and nutrition benefits. The programme meets the first objective of the education sector “ Increased safe and equitable availability and access to in-person and home-based formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth”
The School meals activity includes:
- In kind food distribution including daily distribution of fresh meals or fortified snacks to children in formal education (pre and primary schools) during school days and to children enrolled in non-formal education, including children in Self learning centres supported by UNICEF in North East Syria.
- Provision of electronic vouchers to students who were out of school and have returned to education and are currently enrolled in curriculum B or non-formal education programmes supported by UNICEF. The provision of the electronic voucher is conditional to student’s attendance rate of 80%.
School meals activities will target boys and girls aged 5-15 years in formal and non-formal settings, including children with disability, who are attending the targeted schools and learning centres. The Programme is implemented in coordination with the Ministry of Education and other partners of the education sector in Syria including UN agencies and National NGOs. Considering the high needs and limited resources, the project will focus on areas with acute and immediate needs of humanitarian assistance and mainly in areas hosting large number of affected populations, IDPs and returnees with low food security and education indicators. In this regard, targeted sub-districts will be prioritized based on food and education severity score of 3 and above. In case of lower funding, priority will be given to locations where severity of food security and education of more than 4 and to newly accessible areas where education sector is picking up its activities.

3 - Needs

The education is impacted by the protracted conflict and overall economic situation in the country. HNO 23 shows increase in number of children in need for education support to 6.9 M.According to the education needs assessment, the national average level of attendance for the 21-22 increase to 88. The non- attendance is the highest in NES. Starting 12 years, school attendance drops mainly for male adolescents. Family economic situation and child Labour remain the main drivers for 48% of families to have their children out of schools. Children don’t attend schools due to lack of learning facilities, lack of interest or lack of time and motivation. The education assessment shows that families consider improving schools’ infrastructures a priority, then comes the need to provide support to offset the need for child labour. The provision of school meals to children in both formal and non-formal education will contribute to retention and attendance, while providing children in class rooms with key nutrients essential to learn and grow. In addition, the provision of the snack and the voucher contributes to alleviating part of the economic burden on the family, by allowing it to shift its limited resources to address other needs. Considering high needs and limited resources, the project will focus on areas with acute and immediate needs of humanitarian assistance. Targeted sub-districts will be prioritized based on food and education severity of 3 and above.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat: Risk of explosives, as landmines, exploitations, harassment that students encounter on their ways to and from schools/learning facilitiesMitigation measures : Provide students, their caregivers and school personnel information on what to do and what not to do if they face different types of threats. Engage community leaders/members and change makers to support awareness raising interventions to highlight the importance of safety and related solutions Protection Risk/Threat: Students participate in bullying or act violently towards other students and/or to teachers.Mitigation measures : Teachers and school personnel should be trained on prevention and reporting of, and issues related to, violence, physical punishment and bullying and the use of positive discipline The application of these skills by all school personnel should be monitored as part of ongoing professional development and support. Student caregivers should be provided information and support on how to utilize positive discipline techniques. Students and their caregivers should be sensitized on the prevention, and issues related to, physical punishment and bullying and the systems in place to prevent, identify and response to it. Students, their caregivers, teachers and school personnel should be encouraged to report issues and have safe access to a trusted person they can report instances of violence, physical punishment or bullying or other threats that cause fear.
