Project Summary

The overall aim of this project is to contribute to improving the overall water, sanitation, hygiene, gender equality, and resilience of Rohingya Refugees and vulnerable people in host communities of Cox’s Bazar District through community engagement and participation. The project has mainly three sub-sectors (water, sanitation, hygiene) covering Rohingya Refugee camps and the host communities. The target populations of the project are 53,467 Rohingya refugees living in two refugee camps (Camp 2 East and 2 West) and 5,325 vulnerable host community people living in Teknaf and Uhkia in Cox’s Bazar District.
Considering the field level needs, IRB has designed the project for 12 months to address the WASH vulnerability in Camp 2 West and Camp 2 East along with the surrounding host communities living in Ukhiya (Haldia Palong, Jalia Palon, Ratna Palong unions) and Teknaf Upazila (Whyknog and Nhilla unions) under Cox’s Bazar district. Major WASH interventions will be Pit latrine construction/repair, Sludge desludging, bathing cubicle, hand washing station construction/renovation, Deep tube well installation, construction of WASH block, Women’s Hygiene center construction, Communal and household level solid waste management and hygiene promotion in the FDMN’s camps and host community areas. IRB works in C&D blocks in Camp 2E and A&B blocks in 2W.
In coordination with BRAC, IRB will complete the full transition in 2E (Full camp) from January 2025 and slowly take the handover of 2W (full camp) from August 2025 with BRAC. IRB has a good funding capacity, IRB will be in full charge of Camp 2 East and 2 West on new infrastructures or upgrades (including support to DPHE water networks) and also be accountable for the WASH situation.
Islamic Relief Bangladesh has a plan for the construction/decommission of 102 cubicles of latrines, 75 Bathing cubicles, and one rainwater harvesting system/ water network (Pilot project of alternative drinking water) at Camp 2 East and 2 West. IRB will continue to repair and maintain 1955 latrine cubicles and 870 bating facilities at the mentioned 02 camps. Furthermore, IRB will continue the operation and maintenance of existing water supply pipe networks, repair & maintenance of 2450 water points and ensure water quality in Camp 2W and Camp 2E. Consequently, considering the fecal sludge management, the IRB has a plan for 7700 latrines desludging to reduce environmental detrimental effects and control AWD in camps 2W and 2E. Considering the inclusion, IRB plans to rehabilitate 3 Women's hygiene centers and continue the operation and maintenance of existing Women's hygiene centers in 2 camps. 5 WASH blocks will be constructed in the educational institutions of Ukhiya and Teknaf HC area. Again, 90 single-cubicle household latrines will be constructed in Ukhiya and Teknaf upazila. There is also a provision for the installation of 42 deep tube wells in the affected host community, especially the vulnerable host community areas of Ukhiya upazila.
Provisions have been made for drainage cleaning and solid waste management in camps 2 East and 2 West. IRB has provision of water storage kits, 17,900 menstrual hygiene kits, 10,000 toilet cleaning kits, and 5,900 household waste segregation bins to the FDMNs at camps 2W and 2E. IRB will conduct hygiene awareness sessions at the block level as well as provide need-based gender and age-appropriate hygiene kits will be distributed to the most vulnerable families in camps 2W and 2E. IRB will provide a full WASH package in Camp 2 East and Camp 2W including Hygiene promotion, Solid Waste Management, Water, and sanitation. According to the WASH sector Strategy, IRB will focus on the operation and maintenance of existing WASH infrastructure in the mentioned Camps and blocks. Islamic Relief Bangladesh will implement this 12-month (January 2025- December 2025) project with funding support from Islamic Relief USA and Islamic Relief Canada.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

IRB followed an integrated approach to ensure inter-sectoral camp-based coordination and service delivery to the FDMNs and the host community in consultation with the WASH sector, UNHCR, and camp-level actors. IRB will work as WASH camp focal for Camp 2 West and 2 East, responsible for providing WASH-related support. There are 298 piped water networks across all 33 camps, six in camp-2W and thirteen in Camp 2E. About 15% of households reported not having enough water to meet all their HH needs; consequently, they do not meet WASH sector standards (WASH Sector ISNA-2024). 68% of HH are using tap stands as the primary source for drinking water. According to ISNA findings, 85% of households reported having enough water to meet all their HH needs.According to the WASH sectors monitoring report (October 2024), 50 % of HHs are using tap stands as a primary source of drinking water in Camp 2E and 43 % in Camp 2W. Continuation of the operation and maintenance of existing water supply pipe networks and water points will ensure a safe water supply for the targeted FDMNs in Camp 2W and Camp 2E. Only 16% of tap stands met the FRC level on the source, 43% of flow meters & 58% of dossing pumps did not work properly as a result 56% of users were dissatisfied. There is a high-level need for regular repair and maintenance of water points and pipe water networks to ensure an uninterrupted supply of water.As per the WASH sector Assessment Report, 56 % of female family members feel unsafe using the communal latrine during the night for Camp 2E and 35% in 2W respectively. 68% of female family members reported feeling safe using the communal latrine at night. In Camp 2E, 22 people use every functional latrine, whereas 23 people in Camp 2W. There is a need for functional latrines in these two camps. 79% of FSTPs do not meet acceptable COD standards in Ukhiya camps. Repair and maintenance of sanitation facilities are the most urgent needs in camps 2W and 2E.Based on the WASH monitoring report, 20% and 7% of households have visible waste in the vicinity of their accommodation, respectively, at Camp 2E and 2W. Considering the environmental issues, gender, age, disability inclusion, and accountability, with emphasis on preparedness and response for natural disasters and public health risks, including disease outbreaks needs to be addressed. There is also a most demanding need for drainage cleaning and waste management in camps 2 East and 2 West. According to the October 2024 report, 84% of HH segregates waste at the HH level in Camps. Distributing segregated household waste bins for organic and inorganic waste in the camp areas is a critical step towards effective solid waste management, and the WaSH sector is encouraged to practice it among the camps. Distribution of menstrual hygiene kits twice a year is a regular need in the FDMN camp context. According to the survey, the percentage of soap received is 85%. Only 83% have access to the MHM kit. The project has a provision of MHM kits, especially for females and adolescent girls aged between 12 to 49 years. Consequently, providing menstrual hygiene kits for reproductive women (Age limit 12-59 years; for adolescents and & females) will be an important activity of this project. Besides this, the conduction of hygiene promotion-related awareness sessions for FDMNs, adolescent girls, targeted groups on MHM, personal health hygiene, communicable and non-communicable diseases, HP awareness sessions aiming access to reduce the stigma and challenges associated with menstruation, improving overall female health and well-being.IRB will spend 25% of its budget on the WASH gap interventions in the host community under Ukhiya Upazila. There is a need for safe drinking water sources like DTW in host community areas, a lack of HH-level sanitation facilities, and WASH blocks in host community schools. This 12-month project will be implemented to address the critical WASH needs.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will apply protection principles across all stages to safeguard girls, women, children, and vulnerable groups. Islamic Relief Bangladesh (IRB) is already providing WASH interventions in the same camps where this project will be implemented. Every WASH activity will be guided by a protection lens, with staff well-oriented in protection mainstreaming to recognize and mitigate protection risks. Our protection mainstreaming involves integrating protection principles to ensure access, safety, and dignity in FDMN camps. Islamic Relief has established complaints and Feedback mechanisms for affected populations to assess interventions, voice concerns, and seek resolutions to ensure accountability. This project will ensure information about PSEA, including rules and code of conduct for humanitarian workers, has to be exhibited clearly in various formats in distribution points. Distribution point staff and volunteers will be trained on PSEA referrals and the IRB’s code of conduct. This WASH project will consider extremely vulnerable right holders (18-59 years old), and consult HHs on specific changes required in their WASH facilities to ensure accessibility. Include gender-segregated latrines for males and females. IRB will arrange training on gender, GBV, code of conduct, and PSEA for all staff and volunteers of this WASH assistance project. This project will emphasize the importance of privacy and dignity during WASH block, latrine, and bathing construction, and design an MHM and hygiene kits package that meets the special needs of women and adolescent girls. This project will arrange hygiene promotion awareness sessions addressing the critical issues of health, hygiene, and protection. Through adherence to "Do No Harm" and "Leave No One Behind" principles, the project aims to create a safe, inclusive hygienic environment that addresses protection risks and empowers rights holders within the project framework.

2.3 - Age & Disability

This Water, sanitation, and hygiene assistance project will emphasize the needs of extremely vulnerable individuals of different ages such as adolescent girls, elderly people, and persons with disabilities during the implementation of WASH activities latrines, bathing, and WASH block design, MHM, and hygiene kits distributions. As part of the WASH activity planning, it is considered the specific risks faced by women, girls, and persons with disabilities. For example, during WASH block, latrine, and bathing construction, the locations of WASH structures will be taken into consideration for the different access needs of the rights-holders. The project will prioritize age, diversity, and disability inclusion across all activities to ensure that every rights holder has equitable access to WASH services. During the project implementation, the team will actively include persons with disabilities in all project activities especially disability-friendly latrines and bathing cubicle construction, and ensure they are aware of relevant risks, similar to other rights holders. Our project team will collect SADDD (Sex, Age, and Disability, Desegregated Data) information for this project’s activity implementation. Targeted activities will be developed for various age groups; for instance, MHM and hygiene kits will be provided especially to women and adolescent girls aged between 12 to 49 years. Additionally, the project will aim for at least 3% of all rights holders to be individuals with disabilities, ensuring meaningful participation and representation.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The project will orient and ensure that 100% of project staff (frontline staff, enumerators, volunteers, senior management) and related personnel (including vendors, suppliers, and third parties) receive mandatory PSEA training (including regular refreshers). We must mainstream PSEA in all of our project activities and stages of the project cycle to ensure proper prevention. IEC materials on PSEA will be displayed at dedicated camp offices/ distribution points. A designated PSEA focal person (in each camp) will be available for community members and rights holders to report cases and receive immediate support. A robust mechanism will be in place to address and respond to PSEA issues effectively. As a member of the PSEA network in Cox’s Bazar, IRB will adhere to all guidelines provided by the network and follow its protocols closely. IRB will ensure any SEA allegation, investigate properly, and take necessary actions in line with standard operating procedures (SOP) for SEA complaint referral in the Rohingya response. We will identify risks that must be addressed with the technical support of the PSEA network. PSEA awareness materials (such as posters) including key PSEA messages, and the available CFM will be visible to beneficiaries. The project will ensure that 100% of project beneficiaries will be aware of PSEA and reporting mechanisms. IRB will also ensure that 100% of project staff receive training on how to receive and refer SEA disclosures safely, respectfully, and confidentially. This will ensure safe, accessible, confidential, culturally appropriate reporting channels for sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment, tailored to beneficiaries' needs and preferences. All contractors, vendors, and third parties engaged in this project will sign the code of conduct and abide by PSEA principles.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, Cox's Bazar experienced severe risks of natural disasters that were worsened by climate change and increased its intensity dramatically. This project aims to enhance climate change adaptation & mitigation efforts and environmental protection in Refugee camps. Floods being the primary driver, recorded the highest number of disaster displacements, and faced escalating climate-related threats, impacting camp life, safety during irregular movement, and prospects for return. Rohingya camps are vulnerable to extreme weather events, and urgent climate mitigation and disaster preparedness measures are needed to mitigate risks such as cyclones and landslides. Climate change adaptation and mitigation activities are incorporated into the project, including climate resilience toilet facilities, bathing cubicles, tap stands, tube-well platforms, FSM, and solid waste management facilities. The project also has provisions for persons with disability-friendly & climate resilience latrines and WASH blocks by ensuring hardware establishment above the highest flood level, using disaster resilience construction materials, and enhancing environment-friendly building materials to protect from cyclones, heavy floods, landslides, earthquakes, dots, soil erosion, etc. Community people will be aware of the consequences of climate change and its effects through community awareness sessions, and health hygiene sessions. Regular awareness sessions with the community will focus on building resilience to potential climate and environmental risks, empowering individuals to take proactive steps in protecting their surroundings during emergencies. This project provides emergency wash kits, water purification activities, emergency portable water support, etc. for the affected and suffering rights holders to settle, mitigate & adapt to climate changes, especially in climate hazard-prone areas, exacerbating the risk of climate-related movement on a larger scale.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, in the event of emergencies within the camps, the project team will coordinate closely with the Emergency Preparedness and Response Unit and WASH sector to ensure a swift and effective response and provide joint and coordinated support to the affected community. To enhance community resilience before, during, and after emergencies, the project will conduct WASH infrastructure damage assessment, and provide water and sanitation support that informs community members of disaster risks that may intensify in crises. These will include guidance on emergency WASH response during disasters, reporting mechanisms, and safe access to water and sanitation services, helping to reduce vulnerabilities associated with emergency conditions. Through these efforts, the project aims to support the Rohingya community with safe water, sanitation, and hygiene facilities. To ensure emergency preparedness and response (EPR), the project has kept a provision of contingency funds.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project actively engages youth aged 15 to 24 in various volunteer activities to support the FDMN communities. Youth will participate in WASH structure construction, repair, and maintenance works. They will also join community consultation meetings alongside local representatives, promoting a shared commitment to water and sanitation support. Through capacity-building training and consistent WASH activities, youth will be empowered to play a key role in contributing to their latrine and bathing construction within their communities. Additionally, youth will take part in beneficiary mobilization activities that will aim to equip young people as proactive contributors to a safer and hygienic living environment. Above all, the project has a dedicated team including community mobilizers, field facilitators, and community volunteers who will contribute and engage in joint response in any emergency that occurs in camps. This project will create participatory approaches that actively engage youth in project design, implementation, and monitoring, ensuring their voices are heard and recognized. To reflect the youth’s participation in the project cycle, this project will focus on camp cleaning initiatives by engaging in draining cleaning and solid waste management. The project will develop age-appropriate youth engagement strategies. IRB will ensure specialized training for staff and volunteers who work directly with adolescents and youth (15-24 years old) on effectively and appropriately communicating with this age group.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project promotes resilience by providing safe water and sanitation services and engaging the community people through regular repair and maintenance initiatives. Hygiene promotion & community engagement, capacity development of community WASH structures, WASH supplies distribution, emergency WASH response, and knowledge management through cost-efficient solutions will enhance self-reliance. The improved latrine designs will not only provide critically needed fire and weather resistance but will also consider the local availability of the proposed materials and technical capacity for safe installation. In addition, this project will enhance the capacity of the refugee community which will promote refugees' self-reliance and resilience. All the project activities will empower refugees to gradually reduce dependency on external aid and enhance their capacity to cope with future challenges. This project will help the beneficiaries become self-reliant.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the influx exacerbated issues for a region already suffering from poverty, lack of livelihoods, and limited access to infrastructure and basic services, leading to tensions over jobs and resources in host communities. Considering the severity of the affected people, this project aims to provide host community development activities for increasing social well-being & reducing the suffering of affected people. Disaster-resilient latrines will be constructed for the most vulnerable host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila. Community-level Deep Tube well will be provided based on district development and growth priorities of Cox’s Bazar. WASH block will be constructed to host community educational institutions for ensuring sanitation facilities.
