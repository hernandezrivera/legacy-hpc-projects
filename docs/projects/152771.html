Project Summary

This Project seek to meet the emergency and Primary health care needs of vulnerable and Internally displaced population in three counties of greater Jonglei state, with focus on post conflict movements in Bor, Twic East and Duk counties. We seek meet significant gaps in affected areas targeting 29,434 beneficiaries in Bor, Twice and Duk Counties. Our focus will be on lives lifesaving, which translate to health, water & sanitation and food security. We will improve the health status of the IDPs through provision of effective and Equitable basic health services and emergency health care to support the ongoing and already affected primary health care services through mobile clinics and Establishing Primary Health Care package in form of Mobile, PHCU and PHCCs, Supporting the most vulnerable group of rural communities to have access to improved health services, Infrastructure and disease prevention and control. The Primary health care package & the emergency services include Ante-Natal care (ANC) maternal &Child health, Reproductive health. Expanded program on Immunization and curatives services in One hand, while the emergency component will focus on the disability, trauma, referral and GBV screening and management. The Psycho-social needs with Infrastructure rehabilitation components to improve and reopen the previously affected facilities in line with the security improvement.

Needs

After three years of conflict, the population is highly susceptible to the disease, and more than An estimated 4,058,392 South Sudanese Women, men and Children will need Humanitarian health care services in 2019.Due to High diseases burden resulting from Endemic, Epidemic, Pandemic and neglected diseases the targeted for humanitarian health care services is 2,272,700 with Millions of refuges in the neighboring countries and an unknown number of returnees projected to need health services as a result of the peace agreement. The Prevailing health systems challenges are exacerbated by lack of water Quality control, infection prevention and control measures, healthcare waste management, expansive malnutrition, necessitating a need for holistic integration of health responses to ensure the necessary health securities to mitigate disease spread and enhance beneficiaries. In 2018, 472 attacks on healthcare has been document including death of 115 health workers,(SSA2017/18) with extensive looting and damage of health infrastructure, additionally, there is a widespread shortages of essential medicines, and seasonal floods in areas of Bor exacerbating displacements. In Jonglei, particularly in our three targeted counties 16 health facilities been closed due to funding challenges. In 2018, 7 health facilities where suddenly closed in Bor South and Duk counties due to the funding constrains from 1st October 2018. Based on our previous surveys and community discussion, the urgent need are related to the basic needs such as maintaining the health service delivery functionality, reopen the closed PHCCs and PHCUs and establishment of mobiles team in area hard to reach in the three counties of Bor, Duk and Twice East Counties, targeting areas with High density of IDPs such as Padiet, Pajut, Bangachorot, Kuei, Biol,Payuen, Panlueth Panpandiar, and Panakuach,which many are islands in Jalle Payam of Bor south County. Twic East, dhiam-dhiam as hotspot for cholera outbreak, which require constant surveillance and service deliveries. These areas ,which have been life hiding sites, where thousands of IDPs with need of health services and sanitation facilities have been settled.Scaling up by re-opening more health facilities with integrated health services as well as establishing mobiles team to hard to reach areas along the river Nile hot spot islands, which will include HIV/AIDS and TB. This means robust human resources will be required to run the mobiles teams and support the already existing health services. Bor and Duk Counties remains among the most affected counties during the years of the ongoing unrest. Out of 29,434 people in need, 14,835 Males, 14,599 females,3,238 boys, 2,943 girls and 27,962 IDPs will be targeted, representing 95 % with 1,472 host communities members. These represent the segregation of our beneficiaries to receive humanitarian healthcare in Bor, Duk and twice east Counties of Jonglei. We are proposing 3 PHCCs, 2 phcus and 1 Mobile clinic to serve the targeted communities.

Activities

1-Provision of Basic health services with focus on maternal child health at the supported PHCCs,PHCUs and Mobiles clinics in Bor south, Duk and Twice East counties of Jonglei state. 2-provision of routine and Outreach Immunization activities to the most vulnerable and Internally displaced person targeting Under 5 years and mother in their child bearing age.3-Provision of basic medical Equipment and Supplies during the the Implementation Period.4- Conduct Health Education and promotion sessions in all our operational health facilities in communities including hard to reach areas on diseases prevention.5-Distribution of Medical supplies such as drugs and vaccines to all functional health facilities in the three Counties of Bor, Duk and Twice east.5-Monthly supportive supervision and Joint monitoring with the CHDs and community leaders in all the IDPs supported locations and health facilities setting.6- Provision of Cold chain systems to 3PHCCs and 2PHCUs in the three counties of Bor, Duk and twice east of Jonglei state.7-training of Home and Hygiene promoters and community mobilizers on infection prevention and control.8- Rehabilitation of Heath facilities infrastructure, targeting 2 PHCCs and 2 PHCUs in Bor and Duk counties of Jonglei state.9-Provision of Solar systems for lights in 6 PHCC and 3PHCU in Bor, Duk and Twice east Counties of Jonglei state.10-training of health staff by strengthening their capacity on case management, diseases surveillance and outbreak response.11- Monthly provision of staff salaries through the Project duration.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The Current conflict led to the people of South Sudan to face indignity as the results of the deliberate personal violence,deprivation and restricted freedom of movement. security risks to the detriment of staff health and safety risks. Despite the increase in security risks, the vast majority of aid workers working in the field still face significant risks of ill health through injury, illness or stress. Vehicle and work-related accidents, malaria, and cumulative stress can affect you whether you’re working in a secure or an insecure environment. Therefore, good safety and security management must be considered an integral part of all programme in all countries, not just those affected by conflict or political violence. Our Institution works with IDPs, believe that our focus goes behind people immediate needs to wider questions of personal safety and dignity of the civilian population. With a security Officer on board, We will Identifying threats, work out mitigations plans in coordination with communities or beneficiaries of the project and monitor the progress are keys elements it is clear that no single agency can undertake this task and we will be directly and coordinating with partners with expertise working in these areas of protection. the More sensitive uncounted risk were miraculously managed in coordination with the community, local authorities ,engaging the other stake holders such as CHD, RRC,local administration and Humanitarian partners by sharing Information on daily basis. the major being the rampant security threat as the result of the current on going war, cattle raiding Incidents and heavy rains, the latest, which is a natural disaster remains a great challenge adding to the current challenges due to poor access in some areas during the rainy season, particularly in two of our operating counties of Jonglei state.
