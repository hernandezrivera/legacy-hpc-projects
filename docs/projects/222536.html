Project Summary

Due to the recurrent operation of insurgency in the Northeastern region of Nigeria, which has rendered a large number of the population vulnerable, the three states of Borno Adamawa and Yobe have been crossly devastated, with thousands of people affected leaving them without homes or means of livelihood, while being exposed to diseases and health challenges.
This project is aimed at strengthening the Provision of Primary Health Care Services and bridging the gaps in Cholera Response to IDPs and Host Communities in Selected LGAs of Adamawa State (Guyuk and Lamurde LGAs)
It will also focus on strengthening the readiness and response actions for Cholera Outbreak response in Adamawa State. ZSF in partnership with the SPHCDA plans to reach the communities selected in Adamawa through the provision of an integrated package of health services to children under five years old, women and their families in IDP camp clinics as well as facilitation of referral services to secondary and tertiary health institutions. The funds will be used for addressing health needs including scaling-up of the emergency health services delivery, medicines and supplies in these overburdened health facilities to effectively cater to vulnerable populations most of whom are women and children.
ZSF will support Adamawa State Primary Health Care Development Agency (SPHCDA) and the State Ministry of Health (SMoH) to provide emergency integrated PHC services which include: treatment of common diseases (malaria, RTI, diarrhea, etc.), provision of antenatal care and safe delivery services, immunization and referral services in IDP camp and host community clinics.
ZSF will work with the Adamawa State MOH, SPHCDA and other partners to address the critical gaps hindering access to emergency primary health care services including addressing emerging challenges and critical needs. These needs include: inadequate number and distribution of health care workers, renovation/reconstruction of health facilities because most of the facilities have been damaged, poor storage capacity and dilapidated warehouses, poor logistics management system, lack of emergency drugs and supplies and absence of referral systems due to lack of ambulance and security.
In Adamawa state, ZSF in partnership with the SPHCDA and the State Ministries of Health will ensure that basic supplies and commodities required for effective Cholera OBR available and prepositioned to reduce mortalities associated with the outbreak.

1 - Needs

Today over 1.9 million people have been internally displaced in the Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states of which over 70% are women and children. Thousands of new arrivals recorded in recent months, largely coming from hard-to-reach areas for reasons related to insecurity and military operations. From November 2017 to mid-August 2018, Borno and Adamawa states have seen the movement of nearly 190,000 individuals (153,000 IDP new arrivals and 36,000 returnees). Other causes of movement are poor living conditions in some camp-like settlements, improving security in some locations, voluntary relocation, fear of attacks and involuntary relocation. In its eleventh year, the crisis in northeast Nigeria remains one of the most severe in the world. Since the start of the conflict in 2009, more than 270,001 people have been killed in the BAY states, thousands of women and girls abducted and children used as so-called “suicide” bombers. In 2017, 1,462 children (mainly girls) used as human bombs. In 2018, 43 children used in attacks in the first six months. However, In September 2019, the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Round XXVIII conducted by IOM, NEMA and the Nigerian Red Cross indicated that there are 200,011 displaced persons in Adamawa State, with an increase of 7,477 for the data of the previous round. Out of these populations, 14,467 are living in IDPs camps and Camp-like settings, while 185,144 are living in Host communities. Guyuk LGA is one of the LGA that witness new influx of IDPs recently, the condition of those people are terrible and in urgent need of attention. ZSF adopted the primary and secondary data sources in carrying out need assessment and administrated structured questionnaire using her staff and trained field volunteers in the following LGAs (Guyuk and Lamurde) to ascertain the level of severity of conditions faced by the IDPs and host communities. Upon successfully identifying, collecting, and analyzing the datum, the outcome reveals a need for strengthening health and WASH conditions in IDPs camps and host communities. Issues identified range from poor health and water facilities, unmotivated workforce, inadequate and functional water facilities, limited essential health commodities and equipment among st others (Field survey 2019). Additionally, secondary datum, i.e. Adamawa health sector bulletin October, 2019 Early Warning, Surveillance and Outbreak Reports (EWARS), Population estimates from IOM DTM datasets. Displaced population in Adamawa state continue to experience challenges in accessing quality health services largely because indigenous IDPs hardly adapt to the culture of dwelling in formal camps but rather prefer to lodge with relatives, rent low profile accommodation depending on the income of the household. This compound health and water-related challenges except in recent times (December 2019) when a bill which considers unemployed youth, IDPs and other vulnerable groups to have access to medical care services. • Available at; Nigeria: Northeast Response - Adamawa Health Sector Working Group Bulletin No. 10, October 2019 | Humanitarian Response• Data Displacement Matrix• Adamawa State Health Sector Bulletin

2 - Activities

In 2020, 7.9 million people, of which 23% and 57% are women and children respectively, need humanitarian assistance in north-east Nigeria as a result of a crisis that is now in its eleventh year. The crisis, which is fundamentally protection of civilians’ crisis, has largely been triggered by an ongoing regionalized armed conflict, characterized by massive and widespread abuse against civilians, including killings, rape and other sexual violence, abduction, child recruitment, burning of homes, pillaging, forced displacement, arbitrary detention, and the use of explosive hazards, including in deliberate attacks on civilian targets.An increase in the activity of armed groups as the rainy season approaches continues to contribute to the displacement of population from various location with fear of losing life and properties. The commencement of the rainy season also comes with an increased risk of flooding, destruction of temporary shelters, blocking of roads with a consequent increased vulnerability to faeco-oral and vector borne diseases such as Cholera and Malaria amongst others. In furtherance to this, COVID-19 pandemic is currently contributing to the worsen situation of healthcare delivery via stressing out of the available infrastructure and human resources. It is highly imperative to mention here that he Adamawa State Health Sector Bulletin indicated that poor living conditions coupled with inadequate WASH facilities contributed immensely to creating an ideal condition for the spreading and thriving of vibrio cholerae which is responsible for transmitting of cholera outbreak within 17 high risk areas within the state. During the last outbreak, there are 778 suspected cases of cholera with a total of 04 associated deaths (CFR: 0.51%). Aside from the proposed awareness-raising prevention strategy, ZSF with the help of mobile team support will ensure prompt referral of suspected cases to higher facilities for screening and treatment to reduce effects associated with the disease. This project will support the control efforts of partners through the provision of necessary supplies for first aid management, training of health workers, community volunteers for early detection and referrals on cholera surveillance and case management as well as increasing community awareness on prevention of cholera and improving care-seeking behavior.This intervention will contribute to the following strategic objectives:• Save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and interventions to the most vulnerable.• To improve community health services delivery through the provision of integrated primary health care services, including sexual and reproductive health, including the provision of essential drugs and medical supplies in IDP camps and host community’s health facilities.
