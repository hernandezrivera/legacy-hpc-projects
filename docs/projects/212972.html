Project Summary

Improving and sustaining essential, equitable, quality health services at primary, secondary and community levels are pivotal for effective response to the needs of the affected population. In 2024-2025, both humanitarian and economic indicators continue to deteriorate, with strains from the ongoing fuel crisis, February earthquakes and its aftershocks, and multiple disease outbreaks - such as measles, and cholera, and climate-related shocks exacerbating an already dire situation in Syria. According to the HNO (Health Chapter, 2024), over 14.9 million people in Syria are estimated to need healthcare assistance in 2024. Access and availability of health services are further compromised, especially for the most vulnerable groups, and the National Health System capacity to address the population’s needs is more strained than ever. 45% of public hospitals and 47% of PHCs are either non- or only partially functioning. Post-earthquake, the health system capacity suffered an estimated decrease of 10% in affected areas. Additionally, a 28% increase in morbidity was reported for common conditions including diarrhea, with concerns for a spike in the ongoing Cholera outbreak and a subsequent exponential increase in rates of severe malnutrition in children. The Mental Health WG has reported an increase in psychological support needs, with cases of adults suffering from PTSD and panic attacks, and children suffering from micturition reflex, hyperactivity, and speech impairment. In NES, Al-Hassakeh continues to suffer from a severe shortage of health services; the ACF-supported Al-Lou'loua PHC is the only public health facility in government-controlled areas that provides in-patient services. The facility is undergoing transformation to a fully operational hospital to address the dire needs for secondary and emergency healthcare in the government-controlled areas of the governorate. ACF conducted a multi-sectoral community study in 2023 with 3,600 households in Aleppo, Al-Hassakeh and Hama, which revealed that health-seeking behaviours appear to be deteriorating in the communities surveyed, indicating poor uptake of healthcare services. Only 6% of surveyed women said they sought sexual and reproductive health services when needed and zero reported seeking mental healthcare. The percentage of home deliveries was high in the three governorates. C-sections were reported to be twice the standard. Most of the surveyed women lack access to trusted sources of information when it comes to their sexual and reproductive health, as well as their mental health.
In 2024-2025, ACF response strategy will be aligned with Health Sector strategy in enhancing access to life-saving integrated primary and emergency healthcare health services, with a focus on surveillance and early detection of disease outbreak and increasing community and health system preparedness in targeted locations in Aleppo, Hama, Al-Hassakeh and Lattakia. Through its Health programme, ACF aims at improving access to comprehensive health services by enhancing entire health ecosystems through a layered and scalable strategy. This includes renovating targeted PHCs and mobilising medical outreach teams, providing training for quality primary healthcare services, providing medical supplies and providing renewable energy sources to operate effectively and provide safe and dignified health services. Linked secondary care facilities will also receive support with essential items and training to deliver critical life-saving services. A robust and trained community health network will deliver health (and hygiene) messages and facilitate the complete referral process. Moreover, feasibility studies will be conducted on medical procurement and Health Information Systems at PHC level to further strengthen the health ecosystem. In 2024, a particular emphasis will be placed on the mental health of the targeted population.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In 2024, a particular emphasis will be placed on the mental health of the targeted population. ACF will improve access to both basic and specialised mental health interventions at primary healthcare facilities and advanced healthcare facilities. In the area of RMNCH, ACF will focus on promoting safe deliveries, improving access to Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) services, and facilitating proper referrals. Gender sensitive approaches and safeguarding of beneficiaries are integral to ACF programming, with dedicated staff upholding gender minimum standards and mainstreaming safeguarding processes. ACF recognises the right of everyone to be treated with dignity and respect and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. ACF’s policy on PSEA complies with the IASC six principles relating to sexual exploitation and abuse. ACF provides compulsory training to each new staff member and refresher sessions to existing staff on organisational ethical policies. Internal policies relating to fraud, anti-corruption and abuse of power and whistleblowing/reporting mechanisms are followed with employees receiving instruction on these internal policies. ACF employees and any third parties contracted by ACF sign a Code of Conduct and are inducted on its contents. There are various gender-balanced focal points against harassment at both capital and base levels in Syria, as well as a Safeguarding lead for ACF Syria based in Damascus.ACF staff are trained in Gender and Protection mainstreaming. Protection standards are followed to ensure safe access to Health services. Child Protection charters are included in contracts and HR procedures, with zero tolerance for child labour and abuse. ACF upholds the IASC principles on PSEA and has a zero-tolerance policy on SEA. ACF programming such as during assessment, consultation, or feedback mechanisms. All employees are trained in safeguarding and have access to reporting systems. ACF also upholds a multi-modal and accessible Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanism, through which people can freely provide feedback to ACF, which is handled according to a stringent internal protocol. Assumptions are related to securing timely approval for proposed projects, including by the concerned Line Ministries. An unstable security situation, restricted access to targeted locations, and lengthy administrative procedures may cause a high impact. Mitigation strategies include advocacy, ongoing context analysis and monitoring, and coordination and access negotiation with local authorities. Additionally, there is a risk of currency devaluation, fraud, lack of data access, and ethical concerns. Mitigation strategies for these risks include close and direct monitoring, training staff members on organisational policies, transparency, and trust-building.
