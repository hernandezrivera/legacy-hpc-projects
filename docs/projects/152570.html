Project Summary

Education remains a challenge in Greater Upper Nile as a result of multiple displacements, food insecurity, lack of infrastructure and human resource coupled with negative community attitude towards education.
The project aims at providing equitable and uninterrupted access to safe, protective and inclusive learning, improving cognitive skills and coping abilities of male and female children, adolescents and school aged youths through education in emergency ECD, basic education, youth education and ALP, and ensuring girls and boys are protected through increased access to psychosocial support, life skills and referral pathways.
Schools will be used as platforms to provide other services such as nutritional support, WASH interventions, health services and as a protection safety net.
The project thus contributes to the return of children to school in a school environment that favours their instruction and protection; and offers an alternative to early marriage, and widens economic opportunities for youth beyond the traditional agriculturalist/pastoralist activities which aren’t sufficient to sustain the existing population. This is an effective and cost-effective sustainable intervention that CMD is pursuing to close equity gaps by preserving over 30,000 children and youths in schools. Furthermore, CMD intends to wage an advocacy crusade alongside skills development for out-of-school adolescents by pressing for recognition of youth as pivotal in achieving sustainable livelihood profiles across Jonglei, Upper Nile States and, indeed, South Sudan at large.
CMD’s solid experience in undertaking large-scale interventions and its unparalleled country-level knowledge and relationships at the grassroots will guide this endeavour.

Needs

The locations of Ayod, Nyirol and Ulang, Longechuk in Greater Upper Nile continue to experience several challenges in meeting up to acceptable education standards. With joint IDP populations close to 161,283, learning for mainly girls and boys has been severely disrupted in the states of Jonglei and Upper Nile which prior to the current situation, where already below thresholds in education and literacy. The counties targeted in Jonglei (Ayod, Nyirol); Upper Nile (Ulang and Longechuk) jointly have populations exceeding 519,131(OCHA Est. 2018) Jonglei and Upper Nile states have been recorded as hosting the greatest IDP sites without access to work, education and basic services . Most recent IPC classifications place the counties between phase 3 – 4, an attribute linked to displacement, poor seasonal harvests, crop and livestock disease. Severe levels of food insecurity has further lessened likelihood of communities to attend learning, due to hunger and malnutrition of children. State Pupil to Teacher Ratios (PTR) average 91.3 with Pupil Classroom Ratio (PCR) averages of up to 177.65. 1.43% of the children in schools in these states are children with special needs and 46.8% of the teachers untrained. Available structures are strained with learning spaces usually overcrowded, above acceptable thresholds. These locations have witnessed major disruptions in education activities due to armed incursions, erratic weather patterns, lack of inputs and staffing; a situation further compounded by the use of public infrastructure including schools, as shelter by affected communities. Gender disparity is evident amongst both the pupils and teachers with only 6.2% of teachers being female. Child marriages are prevalent in the target communities and missing substantial periods of schooling makes girls more susceptible. Given the levels of extreme deprivation girls are particularly vulnerable to sexual exploitation. Boys are at a high risk of forced recruitment into armed groups.Despite a desire to commence education activities in the afore mentioned counties, occupation of schools and learning spaces has made it impossible to continue with their normal schedules. Inconsistent school schedules have led to majority of the school dropouts especially of youth who resort to dangerous activities such as cattle raiding and abuse, necessitating introduction of Accelerated Learning Programs in these locations. Erratic Seasonal patterns along the Sobat and Eastern flood plains in which two of the targeted states of Jonglei and Upper Nile lie are expected to further exacerbate the situation. Heavy rains in these areas have greatly contributed to the inefficiency of the education systems due to open air learning spaces making it impossible for children especially girls to attend school. A discontinuation of teachers’ salaries by Government for a period of over 3 years in areas deemed to be anti – government is seen as the main reason for majority of school closures. Despite humanitarian advocacy efforts to see schools reopen in these areas, unavailability of funds to cater for teacher incentives has become a bottle neck, not mentioning the lack of basic education learning and training materials. CMD will cover schools in the targeted areas not covered by the IMPACT project. Provision of access to inclusive, multi sector and quality learning for conflict affected children and youths will enable achieve lifesaving initiatives by the humanitarian community and further provide a platform for provision of basic services

Activities

The following activities are planned for the locations of Ayod, Nyirol, Ulang and Longechuk. They include; • Rehabilitation of existing learning spaces and/or construction of TLSs where there is influx of new IDPs• Rehabilitation and/or construction of school fences, roofs, doors and/or windows locks, shelter to improve the security and safety of learners. • Provision of emergency school meals in areas most affected by food insecurity and exhibiting high GAM rates. • Strengthen referral mechanisms to nutrition facilities using school governing bodies (PTAs/SMCs) in areas where nutrition/health centers exist. • Provision of ECD services targeting mother groups and pre school children combining nutritional supplementation and socio emotional stimulation. • Integrated WASH training for the safe preparation, handling, cooking and storage of food• Provision of NFIs for school feeding• Strengthening of Referral to cholera treatment / health centers by school based cholera response teams (PTAs/SMCs, teachers and adolescents/youth)• Integration of child protection policy and Comprehensive Sexuality Education (CSE) into the youth training• Training of teachers on: 1) Life skills curriculum and materials, 2) Content of primary education curriculum including mother tongue, 3) Basic pedagogy with focus on participatory and active methods, 4) Basic adult literacy training in English for Arabic instruction teachers, 5) classroom management and lesson delivery, 6) locally made instructional material, 7) design & development of age appropriate locally made materials for instruction • Provision of textbooks, learning and teaching materials• Provision of hygiene kits for girls to support their menstruation• Paying of incentives to teachers who are not targeted under the IMPACT project. • Strengthening of Accelerated Learning Programme• Facilitate Secondary education• Provide peer support to adolescents and youth to improve ALP and secondary education learning outcomes, to increase the number of learners, and build their self esteem • Provide Technical and Vocational Education Training (TVET) based on the youth’s education levels and labour assessments to identify existing and emerging livelihood opportunities• Carry out nutrition screening and messaging using school governing bodies (PTAs/SMCs) in areas where GAM is above the emergency threshold• Provide WASH facilities in school, especially for hand washing and latrines• Establish school gardening for vegetable crops; training children on basic agriculture skills• Establishment of nutrition clubs to popularize availability, diversity and accessibility of food, benefits of food values/balanced diet• Creating school hygiene clubs to support messaging, provide awareness on cleaning and safe use of latrines• Recreational activities, establishing sports activities for children in school; training teachers on coaching sports activities.• Equip SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management and governance.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

It's entailed in the organisational values to provide beneficiaries with information, as well as a donor requirement and demand from beneficiaries. Several methodologies have been adopted which include publication of progressive reports that are availed in hard and soft copies to stakeholders, focus group constitution as a means to pass on information, involvement of beneficiary representatives in project review exercises, project literacy campaigns, use of visibility items such as banners, posters, T - Shirts, inorder to create awareness of the project. CMD also runs a social media platform through which project achievements are detailed. A suggestion box is placed at major project locations, through which formal feedback is obtained from stakeholders and responses provided.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are designed to reach beneficiaries in the language they best understand. Design of messages involves a representative of the different gender groups including women, boys, girls, men and the elderly. Special needs persons are also included, with support from key actors such as Handicap International and HelpAge. Messages are mindful of beliefs, cultures, attitudes and practices of communities, and are aimed at delivering results based on project plans and goals.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The organisation has a PSEA policy in place, alongside a child safeguarding policy to prevent harm to children. These include PSEA procedures applies at staff recruitment, induction/training, performance management, whistle- blowing, discipline and grievance, programming guidelines, partnership arrangements and complaints/reporting. Specic responsibilities have been assigned for PSEA, and the organisation ensures staff, volunteers and associates understand and work to PSEA requirements. CMD engages communities to ensure that communities are fully aware of the expected behavior of the staff, how to complain if commitments are not met and ensuring appropriate awareness raising tools are available. This also applies to partners, suppliers and contractors, by assessment of capacity to implement PSEA measures and inclusion of clauses on PSEA in contract agreements. The project seeks to further identify potential risks of sexual exploitation and abuse, and has planned activities on PSEA awareness and sensitivity. CMD has developed a comprehensive complaints mechanism that aims to instill an organisational culture in which all complaints are taken seriously and acted upon.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

CMD carried out several consultations across different strata, aiming at obtaining maximum response from all gender groups including women, girls, boys, men, elderly and persons with special needs. These included focus group discussions, house to house surveys, interviews with key informants, interviews with key beneficiaries at institutions, communities as well as village structures.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

As a commitment to IASC standards on accountability to affected populations, CMD has adopted and enforced clear feedback and complaints pathways, aimed at obtaining information on key programmatic aspects from the beneficiaries. These include provision of suggestion boxes at key project sites, that are periodically checked and feedback/complaints addressed, provision of feedback telephone/email addresses and focal persons with whom the community will be free with to provide information about the project. Review meetings are organised at every level with beneficiaries represented, aimed at getting feedback on project related matters.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

A monitoring and evaluations framework is designed at the inception of the project, with participation of the community, different gender group representatives and people with special needs. These are orientated on the tools to be used for the exercise and then form part of the team that carries out the exercise with focus on particular areas of common interest.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project has identified specific areas of risk which include safety and dignity, humanitarian access to crisis affected populations, populations access to aid, beneficiary privacy, individuals with special needs/risks, social relations, fraud and diversion with protection implications and market impacts with protection implications. The project will include affected communities as participants in all phases of the programme cycle to ensure that affected populations identify their own protection risks, benefits and self protection mechanisms. CMD will consider whether intervention will create or exacerbate protection risks or benefits for individuals, households and communities, engage individuals with different and specifc needs and protection risks, establish two way feedback mechanisms and focal points to ensure regular communication. CMD has done a gender, age and diversity analysis.

Through feedback mechanisms instituted, barriers to access have been identied by the different groups constituted which range from physical, cultural and economic barriers. Mitigation measures have been instituted, in collaboration with communities affected.
