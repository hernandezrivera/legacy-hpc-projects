Project Summary

North East Syria (NES) continues to experience conflict and displacement and access to health care has
been further eroded due to the Turkish military operation started on Oct. 2019, particularly in
conflict areas and IDPs camps/settlements in Hassakeh, Deir-ez Zor, Raqqa and Aleppo Gov. Moreover, the Covid-19 pandemic has further eroded the fragile local health system and severely impacted an already sluggish economy.
This action aims to target in-camp and out-of-camp high-need/neglected areas with a comprehensive package of primary health care services, specialized assistance for PWDs, health waste management (HWM), and community medicine outreach. A coordinated approach with other partners will allow to target effectively and efficiently vulnerable population, ensuring continuity of service to key facilities already supported and looking at health care delivery responding to quality enhancement through better awareness in the community, augmented accessibility, and the offer of a wider portfolio of inclusive services. This will ensure a holistic response to priority needs.
Action will improve safe and equal access to primary and secondary care, including reproductive health and referral management system, together with increased health and protection prevention and outreach. The Action aims at supporting 25 fixed health care facilities, including 15 PHC clinics, 4 rural Health Posts, 4 public hospitals (maternities and covid-19 ICUs), 2 covid-19 centers for treatment of moderate cases. Access to quality and timely emergency care and secondary health care will be further improved through the continued management and support of a NES-wide Emergency Management Coordination Center for an enhanced ambulance referrals management system. Modality of response will focus on strengthening the existing referral mechanism and towards external services and other humanitarian actors providing assistance in targeted areas.
Self-protection capacities of war-affected NES population will be enhanced through increased protection
mainstreaming, awareness-raising, and capacity building of local actors. Through community-based
approaches partners will provide prevention outreach in health and protection to support local resilience. It
will provide health and all other services to all free of charge.
The project meets different gender and age markers. Indeed, a comprehensive needs analysis was conducted to assess the protection threats in the area, including GBV, IPV, early marriage, child labor, SEA. Gender and age-related vulnerabilities were taken into account to ensure assistance tailored to everyone, including elderly and PWDs. Feedback boxes are available in each facility, and FGDs and KIIs are conducted to design activities and adapt them according to the feedback to ensure the active participation of the population.
The expected result is to reach 493,884 beneficiaries (IDPs, returnees, refugees, local population) over 12 months. 300 local staff (management, medical, non-medical, protection) are expected to be trained on combined health, protection, and inclusion capacity building programs. In such a volatile context Action provides the capacity to quickly adapt to emerging health needs in other NES locations.

4 - Needs

Reaching its 10 year, the Syrian conflict remains one of the largest, protracted, and most complex humanitarian emergencies in the world, with nearly 11.1 million people in need in early 2021. In North East Syria (NES), the deterioration of the health system continues to represent a key challenge for the population, and the impact of covid-19 has further eroded local authorities’ capacity to support public basic health services. According to HNAP 2021, compared to other areas in Syria, in NES there is a higher rate of households reporting health services as a priority need, with 37% of communities reporting it as one of their top three priority needs (compared to 24% nationally). Moreover, in a NES REACH assessment of January 2021, 69% of the communities interviewed reported health care as a priority need, and KIs in 79% of communities reporting barriers to healthcare, cited the high cost of health services as a common challenge. In March, a REACH study reports that medicines were a priority health need in about half of assessed communities while a lack of medicines and equipment was reported as a barrier to accessing healthcare in over 40% of communities. Even when medicines were available, households reportedly struggled to afford them. Finally secondary levels of care have been partially neglected while responding to emergency most acute needs and local authorities have never been able to earmark basic resources for them. People with disabilities (PWD) have been among the groups most impacted by this reality, in terms of both health and protection needs. For such reasons, a comprehensive and inclusive health intervention in NES emerges as a priority action.

5 - Implementation of activities

The implementation of activities will focus on ensuring continuity of services established by UPP between 2017-20 in project locations, including PHCCs, rural Health Posts, Hospitals, mobile teams and ambulance network. Health PHC services are in line with the Essential Package of Primary Care, adapted to the peculiarities and limitations of the NES context. For instance, inclusion of MHPSS package is limited to PSS only, because of lack of specialized human resources. Similarly, in most locations nutrition services are limited to screening and referral to the nearest treatment center (i.e. referrals from Raqqah to Tabqa NH). GBV awareness and trainings are mainstreamed across locations, however referrals are possible only where external services of CM are available. In Raqqah, UPP set up a WGSS and CFS in the hospital perimeter to ensure safe referrals. All PHC centers include specialized services for chronic patients (i.e. insulin therapy), ANC/PNC care, pediatric and internal medicine. Additionally, UPP supports the Emergency Management Coordination Centre (EMCC) for ambulance' referrals covering the whole NES. Capacity building in medical and protection modules is a key component of the response. Medical modules will include: RH; IPC/CD; NCD; Emergency/Referrals; Pharma Management; Community Health; Child and Nutrition Care. Efforts to strengthen ProM and resilience in the affected population through a community based approach remains key. Adapted MEAL strategies are associated to each key area: delivery of health care (static&mobile HFs), ambulances management and coordination, stock&warehousing, health&protection prevention awareness and outreach, capacity building.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: Limited access to services, especially for vulnerable groups identified (women, children, persons with specific needs, PWD, foreign families). Likelihood: Medium/Impact: High. Mitigating measures: 1) Carry out safety audits and consultations for safe beneficiaries’ access to site locations, including gender separate areas, confidential spaces, inclusiveness; 2) Anti discrimination measures include staff training and sensitization, coordination with PwG and advocacy; 3) Staff recruitment/composition will be culturally and gender sensitive; 4) Staff training in outreach and prevention will increase access for women and girls; 5) Dedicated trained staff for referral to protection specialized staff for case management; 6) Outreach activities and awareness campaigns on free and available to all services. Protection Risk 2: Attack to health facilities. Likelihood medium/low; Impact high. Mitigation measures: 1) ensure humanitarian principles 2) participation in the deconfliction mechanism 3) advocacy to relevant actors 4) reporting attacks through established channel 5) maintaining low profile. Protection risk 3: Risk of Sexual Exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers in activities including in clinics, mobile units and in the community. Likelihood: Medium/Impact: High. Mitigating measures: 1) Establish a confidential complaint mechanism; 2) Train staff on code of conduct and PSEA mechanisms; 3) Increase awareness among communities about PSEA and the complaints mechanism; 4) Deploy Protection specialized staff to monitor protection standards including do-no-harm, ensuring gender representation amongst staff;

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

UPP mainstreams protection in all Actions’ locations and as part of the ProM strategy UPP provides capacity building to all project staff on protection core concepts, including GBV, CP & PSEA. For each facility, a ProM Action Plan (AP) lists activities, including timeline and staff responsible, to ensure that health services are provided in a safe, dignified, accessible, accountable and participatory manner. Implementation of AP is monitored through project and during midline&endline assmts. To ensure incorporation of principles and mitigate risks of GBV in health service provision the partner org. adopts Code of Conduct (CoC), Child Protection/safeguarding policy (CPS) & PSEA Policy applicable to all staff. The policies are translated into Arabic and accessible to benefs. in all HF. All staff are trained in and agree to abide by the CoC, CPS & PSEA policies. To ensure benefs. access to a safe&formal complaint mechanism (FCRM), SOPs are established to register, process and respond to feedback & complaints including SEA allegations. UPP ensures PSEA with implementing partners through training, sensitization & PSEA mainstreaming in FCRM, and coordination with NES PSEA Network, co-chaired by UPP. In Jan.‘21, UPP conducted a protection prevention assessment to identify main protection concerns affecting vulnerable groups and to set up protection&prevention awareness-raising and community engagement activities, including PSEA.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The assumption: supported HFs, ambulances and MUs to be operative 24/7 to provide life-saving, PHC and emergency referrals to the war-affected population. Main risks are: - Further escalation of violence between SDF and GoT, and increased waves of displacement (high risk); - Regained administrative and political control over NES by the GoS (medium risk); - Violence break out between GoS and SDF (high risk); - Failure of the joint patrolling agreement reached by GoT and GoR and conflict escalation (medium risk); - ISIS resurgence and asymmetric attacks (high risk); - Use of chemical weapons (low risk); - Lack of access and difficulties to provide services and supplies for implementing partner; difficulty to monitor the activities in case of remote management (medium risk); - XB NGOs evacuate expat staff: more limited in their capacity to respond during hostilities (high risk); - HFs, referral hospitals and project staff become a target in the conflict (high risk); Covid-19 pandemic and closure of HFs. Mitigation strategies include flexible health structures to be relocated according to conflict scenarios. All equipment and staff may be deployed in other locations to respond to emerging priority needs. Back up warehouse in Hassakeh to mitigate the risk of stock loss/inaccessibility in case Amuda is included in conflict area. Paper-less policy to protect staff and benefs. confidentiality. Joint commission of UPP & KRC boards in place to ensure common decision making committed to humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality & independence. Rational use of PPEs.
