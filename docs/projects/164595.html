Project Summary

The project aims at supporting reproductive health services in northwest Syria, specifically in north of Aleppo and north of Idleb governorates. The provided medical services will comprise: provision of RH consultation, conducting normal and c-section deliveries, offering ANC/PNC services and Family Planning services, as well as other RH services. The project will include supporting three facilities with salaries for the service providers, running costs, and equipment; these facilities are:
a) Moaz Abu Mehdi hospital (CeMONC), in Shamarekh community.
b) Al-Ikhaa Hospital (CeMONC), in Atmeh community.
c) Al-Farabi Hospital (CeMONC), in Albab community.
The supported facilities HeRAMS codes are:

SEMA believes that strong, easy-going and successful coordination with all the related stakeholders will contribute effectively in achieving the project’s goals and objectives, so the project team is so keen to maintain a high level of coordination with all concerned parties throughout the implementation period. This includes:
- SEMA has good coordination with Donors and the health cluster partners to ensure smooth service delivery and avoid duplication and bridge any gaps to occur.
- SEMA is the focal point toUNFPA to receive RH kits and distribute them to partners.
- The coordination with local councils, camps management, and organizations working in the area, was a priority before and during the implementation process to facilitate the workflow and consolidate the efforts for overcoming gaps and challenges.
The project also includes a capacity building plan for service providers on various subjects including RH protocols, CMR, ANC and PNC SOPs

4 - Needs

According to HNO With the crisis in its eighth year, staggering across Syria. An estimated 11.7 million various forms of humanitarian assistance as of the end of 2018, representing a reduction since the beginning of the year. An estimated 6.2 million people remained internally displaced, with well over 1.6 million population movements recorded between January and December 2018. Close to 1.4 million displaced persons reportedly returned home spontaneously during the same period. The UN estimates that 25 per cent of internally displaced persons(IDPs) are women of reproductive age, and 4 per cent are pregnant women that require sustained maternal health services, including emergency obstetric care.Al-Ikhaa Hospital: The hospital provides MCH care and neonatal care services in a highly-populated area, especially, with IDPs and camps 500,000 as catchment population in November 2019. The importance of the hospital is being the referral destination of deliveries both normal and c-section, for a community with a high number of IDPs and camps, where people are in dire need to such services. The hospital provides SRH and neonatal healthcare services for around 6,500 beneficiaries per month.Moaz Abu Mehdi Hospital is located in Shamarin in Azaz Aleppo countryside, in an underserved area, the hospital's catchment population reaches 120,000 with the monthly number of beneficiaries around 3,100. Al-Farabi Hospital is located in Al-Bab a major city in northern Aleppo countryside. As a major city, Al-Bab is a destination for people from the surrounding countryside, where they seek healthcare services including SRH and neonatal care services. Al-Farabi hospital catchment population reaches 180,000 people and the hospital provides its services for more than 4,000 beneficiaries.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In traditional communities, social mores expect women to maintain modesty and chastity, and anything perceived to threaten these values is regarded as a threat. This has resulted in a gender gap that has been exacerbated by the conflict. Women and girls now face different layers of vulnerabilities ranging from the heightened risk of physical, sexual, and gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, early marriage, restricted mobility that limits access to services, and other forms of negative expressions of masculinity resulting from displacement and insecurity. SEMA, in general, responds to such issues by upholding individual rights in accordance with the humanitarian principals. In that regard SEMA implements all of its activities, taking into consideration doing no harm to the target communities, and providing all services and activities to beneficiaries regardless of their gender, religion, sect, ethnic group, political or regional affiliation. In addition, SEMA works to improve gender equality in term of recruitment, considers protection mainstreaming in all of its projects, provides PSEA training for all staff, provides self-care sessions for staff in order to help them feel more comfortable when dealing with beneficiaries, and introduces staff on how to refer CP and GBV cases to active protection programmes whether implemented by SEMA or other active organisations. Specifically to this project, SEMA initiated a GBV case management team in each of the three supported facilities to receive referral cases from the healthcare providers where detecting such cases is rather easy. SEMA will hire localized and gender-balanced staff, the staff will be trained on GBV core concepts and referral mechanism to refer any GBV survivor upon disclosure.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1:• The local authorities decide to prevent us from implementing the project or part of it.(likelihood: weak, the impact: mid)Mitigation:• Intensive advocacy, negotiation, and coordination with the local council, stakeholders, and main actors will be launched and followed up.Risk 2:• The ever-changing security situation may badly affect the response, and resulting in mass influx of new IDPs including children and children with disabilities.(likelihood: weak, the impact: high)Mitigation:• Safety and security measures and emergency plans will be set and considered before and during the implementation period. Project activities will be designed flexibly to be modified appropriately with the changeable security situation. Implementation sites could be located to safer places as needed in coordination with OCHA.Risk 3:• The one-off emergency case funds to enable immediate response when needed may be used by the caregiver for another irrelevant purpose.(likelihood: weak, the impact: high)Mitigation:• The project team will draw attention to this issue while conducting the project activities.
