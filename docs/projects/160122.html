Project Summary

This project seeks to provide access to life saving emergency shelters; nonfood items and cash assistance to the returnees, newly displace persons and the affected host communities of Akobo, Fangak, Maiwut, Nasir, Nyirol, Pigi/canal, Pochalla and Uror Counties. The project will assist twenty thousand (20,000 individual: 8,820 returnees, 7,045 IDPs and 4,135 host community) comprising of 6,514women, 5,050 girls, 5011boys and 3,425men. Akobo County (Akobo east and west) has three thousand five hundred 3,500 individuals segregated into 1305returnees, 1230IDPs, 965host, comprising of 1110women, 878 girls, 832boys and 690men. Fangak has three thousand (3,000) individuals composed of 1072 returnees, 1034 IDPs, 894 host segregated into 890women, 751girls, 750 boys, 609men. Maiwut has 3300 individuals composed of 1350 returnees, 1105IDPs and 845hosts community segregated into 976women, 777girls, 855boys and 692men. Nasir County has 3700 individual including 1450 returnees, 1330IDPs and 920host communities segregated into 1010women, 1067girls, 997boys and 626men. Nyirol has 2654 individuals including 1013returnees, 987 IDPs and 663-host community segregated as follows: 881women, 670girls, 691boys and 412men. Pigi/canal County has 2346 composed of 962retunrees, 927 IDPs and 457host community including 700women, 555girls, 650 boys and 441men. Pochalla County has only 1500 returnees, IDPs and host community segregated as follows: returnees are 573; IDPs are 548 and host community has 401 segregated into 515women, 375girls, 400boys and 210men.
This project seeks to provide in-kind emergency life-saving shelters, nonfood items and cash assistance to the returnees, IDPs and the affected host communities of the eight (8) targeted counties. The project will support the conduct of need analysis; conduct GBV awareness campaigns, formed and support complain mechanism, conduct stakeholders workshop currently taking place in Akobo, and train enumerators on the distribution processes in accordance with the cluster guidelines and requirement. The project will also support the conduct of post distribution monitoring, identify, register, verify, distributes shelters & nonfood items, and cash assistance to the affected populations of the targeted counties as well as monitor and document movements of returnees and IDPs at the project locations. This project will construct 100 shelters for selected displaced families (Akobo-15, Nasir-15, Fangak-15, Maiwut-10, Uror-15, Nyirol-10, Pigi-10 and Pochalla-10), mainstreaming component of Housing, Land and Property (HLP). Nile Hope will provide cash assistance 80 families with business ideas and skills as a settlement start up kits, adopting existing cluster CBI modalities for in Akobo-15, Fangak-15, Maiwut-15, Nasir-15 and Uror-15, Pigi/canal-5 due to their existing market access. Process of selection of beneficiaries will be a collaborative work between Nile Hope, community leaders and the existing authority on ground. Data entries documented during the monitoring of the movements of the returnees and IDPs will serve the basis of beneficiaries list for distribution of the supplies. Nile Hope is accountable to its beneficiaries, who are the integral part of this project from its concept design. Beneficiaries will be responsible to maintain, renovate and or repair their houses once constructed and handed over. Nile Hope will use a minimum standard of a durable approached in constructing these shelters to improve on the sustainability aspect. Nile Hope will benefit from the hubs UNOCHA plans to construct in Nasir, Fangak and Uror counties by having its office premises and uses storage facilities for prepositioning SNFIs supplies. Nile Hope will work closely with mobile team (PAH and ACTED) for capacity building and learn new techniques in rapid need assessments and responses. For coordination purposes, Nile Hope will closely work with respective State focal points, the core pipeline and the SNFIs cluster.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is implementing the signed revitalized peace agreement now extended the second time for another one hundred days. It is based upon this signed agreement whether the country will fully return to stability or experience more displacements altogether. There are documented spontaneous returnees and displacement in various parts of the country, most notably, in greater Upper Nile states: former Upper Nile, Jonglei and Unity states. Specifically, Sobat corridor (Akobo, Maiwut, Nasir, Ulang, Longechuk) from where over 183,000 returnees and IDPs are reported registered by relevant authorities and humanitarian actors alike (sources: REACH, HNO, DMT). In central and northern jonglei (Fangak, Pigi/canal, Uror, Nyirol) there 57,000 returnees and IDPs reports (IRNA, REACH, HNO) in addition to the flood affected 162,000 flood-affected communities according to ICWG Note: 05/11/2019. The spontaneous return of communities in the Sobat corridor is attributed to the confident build on the revitalized peace agreement, and currently due to flooding. Some communities return into the country to the ongoing insecurity in Ethiopia refugee camps notably because of tribal conflict between Anywaa and Nuer communities and the recent infighting between the SPLA IO factions in Maiwut County. This project will support a total of 6082 women, 5073 girls, 5175boys and 3689men comprising of 44.1% returnees, 35.2% IDPs and 20.7% host population. This mean, the respond will specifically provide emergency assistance to 55.68% female and 44.32% male. Akobo, Maiwut and Nasir possess both the exit and entry routes in and out of South Sudan into Ethiopia while Uror and Nyirol host majority of those IDPs from Bor and Juba. Fangak and Pigi/canal host majority of those from Bor, Bentiu and from Sudan. UNOCHA led IRNA conducted in March 2019 in Nasir, Maiwut and Longechuk counties reported an estimated total of 183,000 returnees and IDPs. Protection assessment conducted in January 2019 by protection partners in Akobo: Save the children, Oxfam, Nile Hope, INTERSOS and ACTED, coupled by the IDPs facts sheets by Relief Organization for South Sudan in June 2019 affirmed the present of some 10,000 plus IDPs and returnees in Akobo east County. In Maiwut County, joint agency assessment report conducted in Sept 2019 on the conflict between the SPLA IO forces, recorded 18,802 displaced persons and over 23,000 affected host communities. On the other hand, South Sudan is experiencing the worst flooding of its kind, although not yet declared a national disaster by the government of South Sudan. The flooding is causing mass displacement of communities from their homes and places of settlement. It is worst affecting the whole country, specifically the lowland area of Baro, Sobat and Pibor rivers corridor – Akobo, Maiwut and Nasir according to the recent inter-agency Coordinated Flooding Disaster Assessment conducted in Nasir County Aug. 21 and Oct 7-10, 2019, Jekow County on Oct. The flooding is also affecting most plan lands of country including Uror county. 13, Akobo County on July 27 and Sept 23-26, and Uror counties on Sept 27, 2019 respectively. Bieh State government secretary for information of the SPLA IO side reported 26 people killed by flooding in parts of Nyirol and Uror counties due to submerging and subsequent collapse of houses. The flood has destroyed crops, severely affected animals and caused wide spread displacements along Baro, Sobat and Pibor rivers as well as in Akobo west, Nyirol and Uror counties. (https://radiotamazuj.org/en/news/article/floods-kill-26-people-in-waat-county-official).

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Beneficiaries right is protection generally by the law of the land and the law of humanitarian work. Beneficiaries right to access services is guaranteed through humanitarian principle as well as locally safeguarded by the norm of the people, whose custodians are the traditional chiefs and spiritual leaders. In a nutshell, the right of the beneficiaries is protected that is why they will, in the first place, selected to benefit in this process. After all the project is mean for the affected ones of the communities.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Beneficiaries to this project are displaced persons and the returnees in addition to the affected host communities. A displace persons always lose everything including even self worth, dignity and belonging. almost 50% of the project beneficiaries are displaced communities that are affected by the conflict that forced them to leave where they call home or a refuge inhabitation.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The target populations is selected using traditional authority leadership. Traditional leaders are the custodian of the targeted beneficiaries. It will be through the community governance systems selection of beneficiaries will be made, without which, any other process could goes against the traditional governance.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes. This project is in line with the national objective of providing access to the affect populations including the displaced persons and the returnees. almost half of the target beneficiaries are internally displaced persons to be assisted within the framework of humanitarian standard.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. This project does not have environment effect although there will be construction of shelters. The shelters to be constructed are local and environment friendly by using traditional method of shelter construction. The shelters are semi permanent and will give durability of up to 5 years latest after which, the owner will be required to renovate.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project has the sustainability plan in that once construction of the shelters is completed, it will be hand over to the beneficiaries who will now take charge of maintaining it by smearing, cleaning the compound, and renovation. The constructed shelter provides required protection from environmental prey as well as give the beneficiaries a sense of belonging.
