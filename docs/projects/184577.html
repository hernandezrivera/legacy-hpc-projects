Project Summary

The availability of basic health services in health facilities is limited, a situation exacerbated by low governance and scarce resources. Many areas do not even have any access to health services. OCHA’s 2021 Humanitarian Need Overview reported that there are two health workers for every 100,000 people, which is among the lowest figures in the world and just 8 percent of the global average of 25 health workers per 100,000 people52, as well as a lack and accessibility of health services. As such, Somalia has one of the highest maternal mortality rates and the highest under-five mortality rate in the world – estimated to be 122 child deaths per 100,000 live births. As per the SHDS 2020, 1 in 1,000 women aged 15-49 years dies due to pregnancy-related complications. The disease outbreaks, Covid-19 resurgence, and poor health systems are the main drivers of the high mortality and morbidity across the country. Contributing to the bleak health outlook is the limited access to safe drinking water and sanitation services, particularly among rural and nomadic populations, and in crowded IDP settlements. Just over half the population has access to basic water services, though this declines to only 28 per cent in rural areas. This has led to an ongoing outbreak of AWD and cholera, aggravated by recurrent flooding. COVID-19 has added to the mental suffering of the affected population and aggravated gender and interpersonal violence, in addition to further exacerbating population vulnerabilities and disrupting health system gains.
According to the health cluster September, 2021 Bulletin, in Lower Shabelle, the partners working there has raised concern about chronic funding gaps that deteriorated the humanitarian situation in the region. This lack of health response to both IDPs and Host communities in lower Shabelle with cases of AWD/Cholera already being managed in the health facilities in Afgoye, Marka and Qoryooley. Wala weyn district is one the districts facing budget constraints as most of the NGOs working there are now facing budget constrains to continue the Humanitarian assistance. The district has no adequate functional health facilities and this has caused pregnant women with complications to seek further assistance in Mogadishu district is 90km from Wanlaweyn. This will contribute to the high rate of morbidity and mortality across the country
The project intend to provide both both preventive and curative services targeting women (56,875), Men (48561), under five children (Girls 53199 and Boys 39113) and through treatment of minor ailments strengthened early warning for disease outbreaks, reproductive health services( maternal &family planning), case management, counseling and psychosocial support, health education, community dialogues, medical supplies and immunization. The project will also support capacity development of the health workers through classroom training, on the job training, and continuous mentorship to ensure they have adequate and relevant knowledge and skills to provide quality services to the population affected. In addition the project will ensure there is effective disease surveillance mechanisms is in place to monitor any significant outbreaks and deterioration in the health and nutrition status of the target community.
HIJRA in collaboration with MoH plans to implement the project in lower Shabelle and Middle Shabelle through already existing 8 health facilities and 4mobile clinics while leveraging the already existing programs to ensure complementarily and avoid the duplication of effort and resources. The project objective is increase access to emergency lifesaving primary services particularly IDPs and host communities with an emphasis on disease prevention, treatment, and health system strengthening in Lower Shabelle, and Middle Shabelle Regions.

1. - Projects activities

1. Provision of life-saving primary care health care services targeting 48561 men, Women, 56875, 39113 boys and 53199girls through fixed and mobile outreach services, including: Essential PHC (EPHS) clinical care, child health care, emergency and trauma care, communicable disease treatment,NCD treatment, STD / HIV treatment, TB screening and treatment, Integrated Health and Nutrition services and Integrated MHPSS referral-level services. 2. Provide age and culturally appropriate mental health and psychosocial support to people undergoing stressful situations. The services to be provided include screening adults, adolescents and children to identify those with signs of stress and depression for support which could include counselling, medication or referral for further specialized care. 3. Provisions for case management of GBV through 8 fixed health facilities and mobile clinics. Survivors of sexual assault will be managed through treatment of injuries, treatment of STI, clinical management of rape, emergency contraception, psychosocial First Aid, individual counselling, and referral for further psychosocial support on need basis. The project will link up with other Clinical Management of Rape network service providers within the four district in lower and middle Shabelle for further support. 4. Train and sensitize 150 (at least 50% female) of front line health workers on trauma, Mass Causality Management plan and case management so that they have knowledge and skills to provide support to affected victims who have undergone any form of SGBV in course of the emergency. The project will also train midwifes and nurses on Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC) to equip them with skills and knowledge useful in provision of quality maternal reproductive service to the affected beneficiaries 5. Provision of emergency obstetric services to 6328 women of reproductive age. The services will include skilled assisted delivery for emergency cases, neonatal care and post natal care to be done by the midwife and nurses. 6. Provide immunization service to children under 4493 1 year (2365 boys, 2128 girl) and 6328 of women childbearing age (WCBA) against childhood and infectious disease. 7. Provide medical supplies to 8 health facilities and mobile outreaches team which include drugs, vaccines, micronutrient supplements, syringes, dressing materials) equipment (BP machines, thermometers, weighing scales, MUAC tapes, heightboards, stethoscopes) data capture and reporting tools (registers, mother and child health hand booklets, summary tools)8. Strengthen disease surveillance and early warning reporting system through sensitization and provision of IEC materials on epidemic disease case detection, investigation, and reporting and referral systems. 9. The project will rehabilitated three health facilities in Jowhar affected by floods to promote health services utilization by targeted catchment areas. 10. The project will procure protective gears (Mask, sanitizer, gloves, gowns, face shield, boots and distribute them to health workers across in the targeted clinics to ensure prevention and response of covid-19 infection. Besides the project will install handwashing station to the 6 targeted health facilities to promote hygiene promotion while accessing health services. 11. The project will provide health education on COVID-19 prevention and response, counselling and support on appropriate IYCF practices to the targeted beneficiaries. When possible, lactating mother will be supported to initiate breastfeeding within 1 hour after delivery by practicing proper breast attachment and positioning of the newborn while those with challenges to breastfeeding will be assisted to express their breast milk and feed their infants with a clean cup.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The project will ensure the location and access routes to health facilities and services are safe. These will be enhanced through identifying central locations and avoiding areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire, or violence. The project will ensure that the health services are respectful and inclusive of cultural and religious practices by Having a proportionate number of female health staff. If female doctors/nurses are not available, consider a female doctor rotation between locations. In this case, women must be adequately informed of which days a female doctor will be available and employ health staff with skills and experiences working with children. In addition, ensure that patient consultations and documentation are confidential and private.
