Project Summary

The continuation of protracted crises in South Sudan now compounded by the effects of Covid-19, and inter-communal conflicts has resulted in massive challenges pertaining to WASH. The already dire humanitarian situation has been compounded by a second consecutive year of massive floods, affecting approximately 1 million people each year in 2019 and 2020. (HNO 2021, IPC Oct 2020–July 2021)
RCO proposes viable and holistic actions that aims to contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality attributable to the consumption and use of unsafe water, the lack of or inadequate sanitation facilities during emergency situations and inadequate hygiene practices. The project will respond to the prevailing WASH gaps affecting the vulnerable populations in most complicated emergency zones – using its past experiences in emergency. RCO will to target 6,000 households (36,000 beneﬁciaries amongst the most vulnerable emergency affected IDPs, returnees and host communities directly affected by the floods & conﬂict with a WASH/GBV, WASH/NUT & WASH/Health integrated response in line with 2021 HRP WASH cluster strategic objectives through addressing and provision of acute WASH needs among beneﬁciaries at risk of gender and sexual based violence, those at risk of health-related WASH $ malnutrition.
Panyikang, Wau, Jur River, Raga, Duk, uror, Bor South, Ayod and Fangak counties are among the other counties in the country where WASH needs are demanding, particularly among host communities, internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. This is attributed to by the impact of inter communal clashes and war experienced in some parts of the region in the recent past that led to vandalizing of the already limited existing WASH infrastructures. Second consecutive year of massive floods wreaked havoc in many parts of the country with more than 72,000 already vulnerable people being affected and rendered with no access to safe drinking water, basic sanitation and hygiene services and therefore being prone to COVID-19, cholera and acute watery diarrhoea and malaria which calls for unhindered humanitarian support and close monitoring.
The project will equally address malnutrition and health related cases by providing WASH services at OTP/SC centers and PHCC/PHCU health facilities as well as addressing the root causes of active of WASH related diseases and protection especially among IDPs, women and girls who face GBV, a key protection concern while they trekking for long distances in search for water among others.
To ensure this succeeds, the project (with strict adherence to COVID-19 guidelines) will train WASH/Nutrition/Health hygiene promoters on core WASH’Nut, WASH’Health and WASH’GBV competencies including referrals and case reporting and they will in turn train others and conduct Mass hygiene promotion campaigns.
Boreholes and hand dug wells will be rehabilitated and drilled by the trained pump mechanics and Water Management Committees who will be flushing out to ensure they (bore holes & hand dug well) are well cleaned and removed turbidity by air compressors and treated with chlorine for disinfection and improve water quality as well as replacing the worn-out parts. RCO will address the sanitation situation both at the communal level and institutions like OTP/SC, schools and PHCC/PHCU health centers through rehabilitations of gender/disability segregated latrines with doors and locks as well as installation of a hand washing facilities at each latrine.
Finally the project will synergize and integrate multi sector response by coordinating with counterpart agencies on ground to ensure effectiveness and referrals of other cluster objectives in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches like COVID-19, AAP principles, protection including GBV/ Sexual exploitation mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conﬂict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention and sustainability.

1 - Needs

2021 Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview predicts a more complex wave of humanitarian challenges, with many counties of Upper Nile, Jonglei and WBeG States indicating unprecedented levels of severity from a combination of other factors. Jonglei and Upper Nile are still experiencing protracted conflict compounded by cyclical waves of intra and inter-communal violence, territorial, generational/age-set and inter-ethnic violence which is often triggered by spats of tit for tat revenge killings, children and women abductions, cattle raiding. This has been exacerbated by the second consecutive years of massive flooding that has wreaked havoc on WASH infrastructures and caused multiple displacements of people. These events have been further complicated by COVID-19 and the already unprecedented levels of food insecurity with consequent malnutrition-IPC findings of October-November 2020 which estimates 6.3 million people estimated to be extremely food insecure; and an estimated 6.3 Million South Sudanese women, men and children will not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene in 2021(South Sudan IPC Oct 2020 – July 2021) Worst still majority of the population in Duk, uror, Bor South, Ayod and Fangak counties are struggling to survive due to the accumulative effects of the conflictS, COVID-19 and the second year of massive floods that have hurt livelihoods, submerged latrines and boreholes and erode coping mechanisms. This situation continues to challenge humanitarian actors with the wrath of matching limited resources and increasingly humanitarian needs of possible IDPs and Returnees. In WBeG, Jonglei and Upper Nile states for example inadequate bore hole and untreated surface water has been attributed as the main cause of water borne diseases; in addition, poor sanitation coverage and lack of hand washing facilities, poor knowledge and practices on personal and environmental further fuel the situation which constitute to nearly 50% of the total AWD and cholera cases. (South Sudan Situation Reports on Cholera 2017/18). Latrine coverage in Jonglei and Upper Nile states remains critically low (3%); thus open defecation is predominant in these areas and considering the current flooding, water sources may get contaminated by faecal matter hence potential for water borne disease epidemics such as cholera. The available limited facilities are not gender disaggregated and do not take care of the needs of those with disabilities. Community Knowledge Attitudes and Practices related to water safety, COVID-19, cholera preparedness/control, hygiene and sanitation practices still has a long way to go, with open defecation, lack of hand washing during critical COVID-19 times, poor disposal of solid waste etc being widespread. (FSNMS round 24). Deteriorating WASH conditions are further exacerbated by increased cases of protection concerns among girls, boys, men and women with subsequent conflict related sexual violence and abuse. Women and girls have been particularly vulnerable as they move long unsafe distances in search of water and other social amenities (Protection Trends 2020; Protection Cluster Updates). The above scenario is predominant in conflict/floods prone counties such as Panyikang, Bor South, Raga, Duk, Fangak, Ayod among others; where only a few schools, markets and health centers have functional sanitation facilities that are not gender/disability tailored. Should these conditions persist, the food security, WASH and IPC situation is expected to deteriorate further. This calls for immediate and unhindered provision of humanitarian assistance and close monitoring.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

RCO has internal mechanisms in place to provide beneficiaries information on PSEA during project implementation. This is achieved through periodic PSEA sensitization to all staffs and volunteers after which they all sign the PSEA policy as a commitment to adhere to it. During project implementation, all staffs are expected to sensitize the beneficiaries on SEA in order for them to understand the adverse effects SEA may have on them and to the organization and also to help them know their rights and entitlement of service provision especially during beneficiary selection for the distribution of WASH NFIs. The beneficiaries will also be provided with information on how to report cases of SEA whenever it arises through our established complaint mechanisms in the areas of operations.Notably, PSEA information will be shared to the beneficiaries at the inception of the project to ensure they understand it and can give feedback and make complaints. RCO staff have sufficient knowledge on how to receive, refer and report protection concerns safely and ethically, and adhering to our policies to ensure PSEA, including SOPs, investigation and response mechanisms are in place.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

RCO will be flexible with its mechanism models to accommodate the people with special needs and those whose access to feedback and complaint mechanism is limited. For instance, the beneficiaries with various disabilities will be visited at households’ level and communicated to in their local languages to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate, RCO will encourage use of local language so that every person understands and contributes in the discussion. Equal consideration and participation during all sessions will be a priority so that the minority and vulnerable group can feel to be part of the group.Further, the beneficiaries will be made aware of the complaints and feedback mechanism, as well having the opportunity to provide feedback directly to RCO team. At the community level, suggestion box will be used so that people who feel shy to talk can drop their written suggestion into the box or contact the focal person privately to give his/her inputs. The community hygiene promoters will also be instrumental in getting feedbacks from the most vulnerable members in the community as they engage them directly during their daily household visits.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

RCO has in the past will be transparent with the beneficiaries by providing sufficient information; guidance and support that will enable them understand and influence key decisions in the project including consulting with the beneficiaries to enquire on how they want to communicate (feedback, complaints, general inquiries) with the organization before putting in place a feedback and complaints mechanism/desk. Moreover, RCO has established clear guidelines and practices on engaging the target population by ensuring that the most vulnerable in hard to reach areas are represented with full participation and influence.All the beneficiaries and other key stakeholders including the local leaders will be directly involved throughout the entire project cycle. The beneficiaries will be taught on their roles and responsibilities before the launch of the project and they will have an opportunity to ask questions and get clarifications concerning the project. In addition, RCO will establish a multi-layered feedback mechanisms which include a complaint desk, suggestion box and providing the primary contact number which will give the beneficiaries a multiple avenues to channel their concerns and complaints. RCO has been doing this so that the community gets timely information which enhances involvement and sense of ownership of the projects.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

RCO has in the past will be transparent with the beneficiaries by providing sufficient information; guidance and support that will enable them understand and influence key decisions in the project including consulting with the beneficiaries to enquire on how they want to communicate (feedback, complaints, general inquiries) with the organization before putting in place a feedback and complaints mechanism/desk. Moreover, RCO has established clear guidelines and practices on engaging the target population by ensuring that the most vulnerable in hard to reach areas are represented with full participation and influence.Beneficiaries’ feedback especially during project inception, safety mappings and assessment visits will be an integral part during the project development and implementation as it will provide a platform to accommodate new ideas and different perspectives and improve programming. In doing so, RCO will have an opportunity to demonstrate its accountability to the community and ensure that the project reflects local needs, has local support and does not have any adverse unintended consequences.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

8.1 Safety mappings and GBV risk analysis will be conducted in order to address and solve the problem of risk of exposure to violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services. This will help RCO to identify suitable and central place where beneficiaries feel safe during distributions of WASH NFIs and to ensure the urgent water pumps and latrines are rehabilitated.8.2 RCO will ensure all the staffs and beneficiaries are sensitized on SEA before project implementation begins in order for them to understand their rights and entitlement and also to know where to report cases of SEA whenever it happens to them. This will help prevent cases of SEA as the beneficiaries seek humanitarian assistance.8.3 RCO has a well-established and functional internal complaint mechanisms system, ie it has a well-trained PSEA focal person in each of our implanting locations who leads in the handling all the SEA cases.8.4 RCO in collaboration with partners on the ground will develop and continuously update the referral pathway to ensure survivors receive timely and quality multi-sectorial services.8.5 RCO will ensure all groups of people including the minorities such as the women, girls, elderly and disables are included in the entire program especially during the consultations meetings, trainings, awareness raising and selection of direct beneficiaries of WASH NFIs.8.6 RCO will recruit a well-trained PSEA focal persons to handle cases related to SEA. The focal person will maintain high level of professionalisms especially in the management of data where high level of confidentiality and privacy will be upheld. 8.7 The problem of Interference of external stakeholders in impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries will be solved by screening and verification of the list of beneficiaries presented by enumerators working closely with the community leaders. This will ensure to right beneficiaries receives the humanitarian service intended for them.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

This project intends to address the protection concerns of populations affected by conflicts and COVID 19 pandemic by integrating cross-cutting issues such as GBV and COVID19 prevention and response services into the programming. RCO will develop, disseminate and continuously update the referral pathways for survivors to access multi-sectorial services.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Provision of clean and safe water for communities will leverage them from the long distances and time they used to travel to look water. In addition, access to clean and safe water will reduce health issues and also avert conflicts which usually results from sharing of water resources that is limited. This will therefore provide them the opportunity, especially women and girls to engage in meaningful livelihood activities such as garden farming which will ultimately reduce the proportion of people in the IPC 3, 4 and 5. Further the sanitation will ensure a safe, hygienic environment with clean water, sanitation and healthy daily practices in terms of hand-washing which will significantly prevent malnutrition in the family.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

RCO will ensure all the vulnerable groups including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons are included in this program. This will be made possible by working closely with the local leaders and the representatives of the marginalized groups to ensure equity and inclusiveness. In addition RCO will ensure the beneficiaries access cross-sectorial services by integrating, collaborating and coordinating with partners on ground.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

RCO will provide access to safe and clean water in school, homes, and nutrition and health facilities for drinking and domestic use and improve the hygiene and sanitation practices through CHPs among the communities in Ayod, Duk, Uror, Bor South, Raga, Wau, Jur River and Panyikang counties which fall under severity level 3 and 4. This will ensure children U5 and their care givers have access to safe and clean water for drinking thus reducing the cases of diarrhoea and other water borne diseases which are the leading cause of GAM among U5 children.
