Project Summary

The proposed project will be targeting 4,000 vulnerable households including women, girls, persons with disabilities and the marginalized people in Aweil East and Aweil South counties, Northern Bahr-El-Ghazal State who are affected by flooding, dry spell, macro-economic crisis, the COVID-19 pandemic and the prevalence of GAM deterioration
The intervention purposes to guarantee that lives are saved and poverty minimized through these objectives to achieve the overall goal:
1- To ensure that lives are saved and human suffering is reduced through provision of food security production inputs, to targeted beneficiaries, in order to ensure immediate access to inputs and subsequently to food for the most vulnerable populations
2- Protect and rehabilitate livelihoods for the vulnerable population at risk of hunger and malnutrition
3- Improve independence and coping capacities of people in need by protecting, restoring and promoting their livelihoods
The key activities to be carried out to realize these objectives and achieve the overall goal will include;
1) Distribution of fishing kits, crop seeds, tools, and quick maturing vegetable seeds, cash-based interventions (conditional and unconditional)
2) Livestock treatment and vaccination; livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers Training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment especially zoonoses e.g. RVF
3) Food assistance for the vulnerable and affected population
4) Procurement and distribution of small ruminants-goats, oxen for ploughing for agricultural expansions and poultry-chicken to households lacking livestock
5) Awareness on COVID-19 preventive measures
6) Form 4 groups (25 each; 85% women) for village savings and loans Associations (VSLAs)
7) Provide business skills training for each group.
8) Continuous monitoring (including post-distribution monitoring)
9) Mid-term reviews and end line survey

1 - Needs

After signing the revitalized Peace Agreement in the past two years, South Sudan has remained a protection crisis in 2020 as a result of lack of durability in the peace, limitation in basic services investment/essential services like health care, water, hygiene and sanitation, education and protection and increased violence against aid workers and assets and operational interference making the citizens unable to stabilize and have sustainable development (UNOCHA, 2020). Communities were hit-hard by intensified shocks of insecurity that caused population displacement of people, climatic shocks (drought and flooding) which led to low crop production, macro-economic crisis, the effects of COVID-19 pandemic, pests such as desert locusts and inadequate multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance as stated by government report released in mid-December 2020 worsening the food security situation whereby children face acute and severe malnutrition which has majorly caused the child mortality and morbidity in the country compared to the last three (3) years, women and girls face extreme levels of gender-based violence and psychosocial stress and weakening the people’s coping mechanisms leading them to adopt negative practices such as forced labor and child marriage which will further deteriorate in 2021 (South Sudan IPC Classification, December 2020). Some 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021 (IPC phase classification projection for December 2020 to July 2021). Among the states affected is Northern Bahr-El-Ghazal according to the Press Release on the 18th.Dec.2020 by the National Bureau of Statistics, IPC South Sudan Technical Working Group, and all Food Security and Nutrition stakeholders, between October and November 2020, in the second projection the period from April to July 2021, people are likely to be in catastrophe (IPC 5) acute food insecurity whereby 7,000 were from Aweil South and also according to inter-cluster coordination group reports on flooding as of 18th.Nov.2020, people have been affected by flooding whereby eight (8) of the ten states were badly affected including Northern Bahr-El-Ghazal are in need of food and livelihoods support and is in dire humanitarian need and mostly affected counties include Aweil South and Aweil East where the displaced people came to take refuge and also as stated by the key informants report below-average rainfall/dry spell in localized areas in Aweil East and Aweil South, staple food prices which are reaching inflated levels in several key reference markets whereby in August 2020, the average cost of malwa (3.5kg) of Sorghum in Aweil is 1,440 SSP that is, equivalent to 215-565% above the five-year average, the sharp depreciation of the South Sudanese Pounds from 385 to 500 SSP/USD on the corresponding market has compounded the effect of long term currency depreciation, conflict .i.e. inter-communal clashes between the Dinka and Misseriya communities which has caused displacement of people in the area, high taxation and a new, mandatory COVID-19 testing fee of 65 USD levied against cargo drivers may contribute to higher costs, and COVID-19 preventive measures on the costs of importing and transporting staple foods to the market, and unfavorable terms of trade for majority of the households have largely contributed to the food insecurity in these counties. Additionally, GAM prevalence deteriorated due to the limited access to and availability of food during this period coupled with seasonal increase in morbidity –such as malaria, and lack of adequate access to health services (Feb VAM Weekly price monitoring; 2021). 8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project consultation meeting with beneficiaries will consider the attendance to have 50% female, 50% youth, 15% minority group, 15% child-headed households, and 15% persons with disability during consultation and decision-making processes. Beneficiaries will be informed about this criterion to ensure voices of female, youth, and persons with disability are heard in relation to project design and implementation. The beneficiaries will be provided with information about program activities before the commencement of the project to involve them in project management, create awareness and sense of ownership, and provide accountability mechanism. • Charity and Empowerment Foundation will ensure staff are trained on protection of persons of all ages including child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) risks. • Charity and Empowerment Foundation (CEF) recognizes the community engagement and therefore will ensure full participation of community members in activities at community level hence championing community protection mechanisms. • Charity and Empowerment Foundation (CEF) will train community existing protection structures such as church leaders, school administrations, and local authorities on protection principles including GBV risk and referral pathway existing in the community. Charity and Empowerment (CEF) will work closely with local leaders and authorities to create community awareness on the targeting and selection criteria. • Charity and Empowerment (CEF) will engage some resident community and returnees and displaced members during the identification of project beneficiaries in order to build a strong relationship between returnees, displaced and host community members. • CEF will consider all age groups and gender in the project implementation. The confidential complaint mechanism will be established and all the issues that may arise will be addressed without discrimination.  CEF will position a complaint desk and box in a visible area and place accountability to the affected population officer to receive complaints. Complaints received will go through the case management process to generate appropriate solutions and feedback.
