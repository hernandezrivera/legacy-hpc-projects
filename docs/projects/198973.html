Project Summary

Although access to portable water and sanitation is a human right, these services continue to be a luxury for large parts of the internally displaced and host communities of the North West – South West Armed conflict in Cameroon. OCHA estimates that about 1,876,555 people, internally displaced, host communities and IDP returnees are in dire need of WASH services in the conflict affected regions. This need is even more evident for children especially those studying in rural communities. The impact of the school boycott campaign that characterized the conflict and the exigencies of some armed groups on community school notion has led to the creation of many makeshift structures to host school children. While some of these structures like palaces, child premises and town halls that presently host many schools in some crisis areas are not adequately equipped for academic purposes and lack minimum sanitation and hygiene services including toilets, latrines and water, the over four years of non-usage of established schools have suffered fron advanced dilapidation and damage in WASH facilities. There is also a visible absence of knowledge and culture of proper hand washing with soap especially amongst IDPs children struggling for daily meals and depending on humanitarian food relief. In municipalities such as Bamenda II and III subdivisions where community water and Camwater services exist, there are payable and do not provide any public taps too meet the needs of poor IDPs and their children.
GLOWA is proposing to construct six solar powered boreholes in three subdivisions in the north West Regions to meet the WASH needs of IDPs and host communities and carryout a behaviour change campaign for proper handwashing and toilet hygiene in the project areas. Through a series of activities, teachers and students will be empowered trained on key solutions to improve the water, sanitation and hygiene situation, within the framework of an on-going program of personal hyegine education. The project will also provide opportunities for GLOWA to strengthen its capacity in the delivery of behaviour change communication and WASH Programs to vulnerable populations.
