Project Summary

The housing crisis in Gaza is driven by over 15 years of Israeli restrictions on the import of building materials and equipment, the deteriorating socio-economic situation, and the recurring waves of hostilities. The COVID-19 pandemic adds extra strain to shelter need and generates extra pressure for families living in substandard shelters. Those factors have undermined the capacities of poor households to access decent shelter and rendering them vulnerable to health and protection threats. According to the (2022 HNO), it is estimated that 943,000 Palestinians across the oPt are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022 to access adequate shelter, some 92 per cent of them are in Gaza Strip. The proposed project has been designed in line with HRP strategic objectives 2 “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under International Humanitarian Law” and it will contribute to the Shelter/NFIs Cluster Objective 2 “Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity”. Specifically, the project aims to contribute to the protection of the acutely vulnerable people and conflict-affected households in the Gaza Strip through creating safe, adequate, and healthy living environment for 250 HHs in Rafah governorate who are living in substandard houses and lack basic shelter infrastructure and services (amounting to approximately 1,400 individuals, including 374 women, 377 men, 315 girls and 335 boys; among them are 32 PwDs). The FHHs, PwDs and elderly-headed HHs will be prioritized, in addition to cases of GBV and protection concerns. Throughout the project, a range of urgent rehabilitation works will be conducted upon the actual needs and priorities of targeted HHs, and this will be complemented by the provision of essential NFIs. The works will include rehabilitating the damaged and/or substandard shelters to meet basic shelter needs and minimum standards, including works related to physical protection and overcrowding, maintenance of toilet and kitchen facilities, improving living areas and providing adequate and essential shelter NFIs and hygiene materials and adaptation for PWDs (if any). The project will use the self-help approach, utilizing family and community assets, which included volunteer labor, cash and discounted building supplies. The project will provide unconditional restricted cash assistance at an average of $2,560/HH (Based on the findings of technical assessment of the needs at each Housing Unit, including NFIs). The targeted households will be will be oriented and mentored to prepare their individual rehabilitation plans, identifying the most priority rehabilitation works such as roofs, windows, doors, fixing toilets and kitchens. Also, the households will purchase the needed shelter NFI packages including bedding items, and/or kitchen sets, as well as basic Hygiene items. The project will enhance beneficiaries’ resilience by enhancing their adaptive capacities to cope with the risks of deterioration of living conditions and will empower them through the transfer of skills and expertise on the use of cash. IRW’s solid experience in the sector will enable the project team to benefit from the lessons learnt, improving the project design and implementation modalities. The project will be implemented in partnership with local CBO of relevant and appropriate capacity and it will ensure strong cooperation and coordination with local authorities, shelter/NFIs cluster and other key stakeholders. Also, the project will ensure close consultation and regular communication with beneficiaries, ensuring their awareness of the complaint’s mechanisms and continued feedback from the community.

1 - Needs

Adequate shelter is a basic human need, which is crucial for survival and it is essential for normal, healthy and dignified living. In the Gaza Strip, the access of many families to adequate and affordable shelter has been affected by the continuous blockade, material restrictions and the deteriorated economic situation. The needs are exacerbated by the continuing displacement following conflict escalations, as well as exposure to harsh weather conditions. Gaza Strip suffers from a chronic shortage in housing that is expected to exceed 120,000 units in 2022 according to the Ministry of Public Work and Housing (MoPWH). An annual average of 14,000 housing unit is needed to meet natural growth. According to the MoPWH’s assessment in Jan 2020, 21,500 inhabited housing units across Gaza are classified as substandard and do not meet the minimum requirements for adequate living space, weather protection, hygiene facilities and privacy, and require different degrees of rehabilitation. Another 7,000 units were found beyond repair. Overcrowding compounded with unhygienic living conditions is common in the substandard shelters in Gaza. Rafah governorate has the highest in need of shelter interventions (26%) as per the poverty and vulnerability in the Gaza Strip. Based on the Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA), conducted in July 2021, 14 per cent of the surveyed housing units in Gaza reported living in critical shelter and 62 per cent reported damage to their shelter. 28,000 individuals are at risk of eviction for not being able to pay their rent. Some 130,000 people living in ground floors located in low depression areas, or next to sewage pumping stations, are at constant risk of flooding during the rainy season. This is primarily due to the lack of proper storm water drainage and winterization preparedness. The COVID-19 shows the inability of poor families living in substandard shelters to protect themselves, adding to the shelter needs to include interventions such as the improvement of covered areas, the separation of the shared facilities like toilets and kitchen and the provision of hygienic and disinfectant materials. Additionally, 58,000 housing units were damaged or destroyed in the recent escalation of May 2021; of which 9,500 housing units had moderate to severe damages and 1,255 housing units destroyed, and rendered uninhabitable, leaving 12,000 people displaced. According to the MSNA, 85 per cent of households that had their homes damaged as a result of hostilities had no capacity to repair their homes. According to HNO 2022, It is estimated that 943,000 Palestinians across the oPt are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022 to access adequate shelter (including basic households supplies), indispensable to providing people with a minimum level of privacy, protection and dignity. Some 92 per cent of them are in Gaza. Across the oPt, there are over 104,000 Female Headed Households, 8,000 of which live in severe shelter conditions. Similarly, 23,000 people with disabilities (including 10,000 children) of whom 9,000 individuals live in severe shelter conditions. Substandard housing disproportionately affects women, girls, PwDs, and the elderly, who are likely to spend most of their time at home, so their decision on the work is essential. Shelter needs and priorities of the different groups are dissimilar as women prioritize kitchens, bathrooms, and privacy, men prioritize electricity, insulation, and high ceilings/roofs, children, desire space to play, locks on doors for privacy and adequate lighting for homework, while the elderly and PwDs require accommodations such as ramps, handrails and wider doorways to facilitate access and mobility. This action will ensure providing at least the minimum standards for a dignified and protected living environment, while reflecting the shared decision-making to meet the needs and priorities of each member.

2 - Activities

IRW will carry out the project activities, considering the active engagement of the targeted HHs in the design, planning, implementation and evaluation of activities. The project will be implemented through local partner in close collaboration with Shelter/NFIs cluster, sector stakeholders and the governmental counterparts. IRW will contract with existing CBO as partner and will hire the project team. The project team and partners will receive information on PSEA and CFM and will be required to sign the Code of Conduct which directly refers to the PSEA. IRW plans to achieve the project’s results by delivering the following activities: (1) Conduct Beneficiaries Selection; IRW and local partner teams will finalize the selection of 250 housing units from the database of MoPWH according to the results of the technical assessment visit which will be conducted by the project team using two rankings for shelter and households vulnerabilities, and putting specific focus on FHHs, PwDs and elderly-headed HHs. The data from the assessment will be entered and analyzed in a scoring system, presenting the most 250 eligible households. The project team will specify the amounts to be allocated for each house according to the actual needs at an average of $2,560/HH. The final list of beneficiaries will be shared/agreed in close cooperation with shelter cluster and line ministry (MoPWH) as well as peer organizations. (2) Conduct orientation and communication with beneficiaries: To discuss project details, selection criteria and scope, mechanism of self-help approach in rehabilitation, safety and security measures, two-way communication mechanisms with target group with safe access and smooth procedures, concise information/advices about COVID-19 and the most importance of pre-caution measures and induction about the feedback and complaint mechanisms. (3) Provide financial and technical support to 250 HHs to rehabilitate/adapt their substandard houses through self-help approach (restricted cash): An agreement will be signed with each beneficiary individually, clarifying their roles and obligations. The project team will agree with HHs on priorities of works and NFIs and provide technical assistance in terms of material, workmanship and alternatives. The agreement has all project details including: Project scope, donors, bio data, details, responsibilities of both parties, details of financial details and instalments, and the BOQ of the intended repairs and rehabilitation works. Cash will be disbursed in multiple payments (Advance payment of 40%, 2nd payment of 40% after 70% progress and 20% after finalizing all agreed works) and they will be provided to HHs after reviewing and verifying all agreements, payment reconciliation and approving the technical reports by the engineer will be conducted to proceed with the next payments. Engineers will conduct regular field visit every week to follow up, monitor and provide technical assistance to ensure adherence to the minimum requirements. Also, they will be ready to follow up and give advices to all HHs on daily basis via other communication tools. The health promoters will conduct regular sessions with the family’s members for hygienic and public health awareness to influence behavior change. IRW will conduct MEAL activities for shelter rehabilitation works and the provision of NFIs by beneficiaries and finally conduct administrative and financial closure.

3 - Indicators

The Quality Assurance Unit at IRW will be in charge of conducting a range of MEAL activities, gathering and analyzing all monitoring data in order to measuring progress against indicators and provide cluster and donor with all data requested. The progress of implementation will be systematically monitored through field visits, following up and reporting mechanisms, addressing any challenges and adopting the necessary amendments. Also, feedback from beneficiaries will be regularly collected and their satisfaction will be assessed in the post distributions. Throughout the implementation, lessons learnt will be documented to inform better future programming and scaling up of shelter interventions. MEAL activities will enable reporting detailed gender and age disaggregated data and measuring project outcomes of different targeted groups. The proposed project supports the shelter cluster efforts to address its priority need 2 that “Vulnerable households living in substandard shelter exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity”. IRW will measure the success of the project through the following shelter cluster indicators: # of individuals protected and have improved access to adequate shelter (disaggregated by female/elderlies head of the households, gender, age groups and protection concerns such as overcrowding, privacy, risks and hazards) # of PWDs have improved access to shelter and # of individuals living in substandard shelters, overcrowded conditions and at risk of being affected from COVID 19 supported with Shelter assistance to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacities. In addition to that, IRW will further measure the following outcome indicators: % of assisted FHHs, PwDs and elderlies reported less concerns about safety, privacy and security. %of trained beneficiaries reported improved knowledge, skills and practices on the use of cash. %of HHs reporting satisfaction about the intervention. %of HHs report knowledge/access to complaints mechanism and % of women, PwDs and elderlies reported participating in the decision making process. The output indicators to be measured with be: #of housing units upgraded/adapted in accordance with the minimum standards. #of individual rehabilitation plans prepared addressing the protection concerns. # # of HH assisted with essential NFIs. To further ensure protection, quality and accountability are mainstreamed throughout the project implementation, the following indicators will be measures: % of project beneficiaries (male/female) reporting that humanitarian WASH assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner”. % of the affected population (disaggregated by sex and age) reached through consultation in the establishment of community-based complaint mechanisms (CBCMs), awareness activities and community mobilization interventions on PSEA including how to report SEA-related complaints (disaggregated by type of PSEA communication materials developed for each population group identified).

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

IRPAL has an effective and reliable complaint and feedback mechanism that will be applied to provides a chance for all beneficiaries especially the most vulnerable to express their concerns freely and provide their feedback on the delivery of project activities. Before the implementation of activities, IRPAL will organize an inception workshop and introduce the complaint and feedback mechanisms to all stakeholders and target group. The received feedback from beneficiaries will be used to inform programming and improve the project implementation. IRPAL will determine to what extent it is necessary to adapt the project and messages to the needs captured through feedback and complaints. Should any issue raised exceed the project’s scope, IRPAL will advocate this situation to the relevant Cluster, local authorities and other humanitarian partners to tackle the issue and ensure onset needs can be covered.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Ensuring all different groups of beneficiaries and communities have access to safe complaint and feedback mechanisms is one of the first steps in mainstreaming protection across the activities of the project. IRW is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its project to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on IRW work, and to rely any arising issue, including potential PSEA or other protection issues. The project beneficiaries will be engaged in all phases and their needs, preferences and priorities will be considered. During the preliminary stages, IRW provides accurate, timely and accessible information to affected people about its project objectives and intended outcomes in the orientation sessions. Information will be clearly understandable by everyone, irrespective of their age, gender or other characteristics. During implementation, regular meetings and sessions will be held with different community groups to solicit their feedback about any necessary changes or adaptations needed to make the project a success. IRW owns a range of communication tools which are designed in participation with the targeted communities, including the most vulnerable groups. Whenever possible, complaint boxes will be set up, as well as contact information (mobile, WhatsApp and e-mails and online communications) will be shared and disseminated to the project beneficiaries through SMS and will be on banners in the project locations. If the beneficiaries cannot write their feedback or issue their complaints, they will be able to reach the project staff and partner staff who received CRM training and will be available to receive complaints and feedback during collective activities and household visits. On a regular basis, the CRM focal point will gather the complaints received by this mechanism. IRW monitors the proportion of assisted people informed about the project &satisfied about the intervention.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention has been developed with comprehensive understanding of the protection principle, especially “Do No Harm”. The project design had considered protection risks analysis and the activities are screened not to increase risks for all especially women, girls and children. IRW will ensure that women, PwDs and elderlies are represented in to ensure that their rights, needs, and views are well taken into consideration. During the verification and final selection of the target groups, all project information will be shared with the target communities to inform them about the provided assistance, and the project will prioritize the HHs occupying the vulnerable individuals at risk of protection issues (including GBV and child protection issues). GBV and PSEA issues, will be the specific focus of capacity building for staff and partners to ensure that they are able to detect cases of abuse. The project includes effective use of referral pathways for specialist support, in areas related to gender-based violence (GBV), child protection and psychosocial support. The project staff and partners are required to sign the Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). IRW will first do a conflict analysis, understanding the context where we will be intervening and identifying the relations between the different groups that will be targeted. IRW will act upon understanding these interactions in order to avoid negative impacts. IRW staff will perform interviews with beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries to ensure that tensions are not rising in any way as an outcome of the project. Also, local cultural practices and gender sensitivity will be respected at all times by the project team and partners during works implementation. Accountability to the affected population has been well mainstreamed in the project design, implementation, and monitoring. A set of mechanisms will be established to ensure accountable implementation of the project activities. Beneficiaries and stakeholders will have a voice in the project through complain mechanisms held in place. A satisfaction survey will be completed at the end of the intervention to assess the final valuation of the beneficiaries toward the project works. Regarding specific harm related to COVID-19, emphasis will be put on applying protective measures and behaviors to ensure the safety and protection of all people involved in the project (HHs, project team and laborers). Eventually, the project will increase proper access for safe and healthy shelter, and thus contribute to increase access to human right and ensure beneficiaries safety and dignity through improving their life conditions.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

IRPAL seeks to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. The project directly improves access for people with specific vulnerabilities in terms of socioeconomic and protection to basic shelter needs. A participatory approach was used to identify the needs and requirement by including participants at all levels and in all phases, starting with the design phase (appropriateness analysis) followed by appropriateness and adaptation planning to ensure adapted assistance accessible to all people of all genders, ages and disabilities. Based on previous experience and primary assessment results to ensure equitable access of the marginalized groups. The beneficiaries' selection process is need-based and will consider transparent and accountable processes including the feedback and complaint mechanism. women headed households, PWDs and elderlies are disproportionately affected by poor housing conditions, and will be a prioritized during the selection of beneficiaries. The technical needs for selected shelters will be evaluated individually and customized to meet the needs of the groups in terms of access, safety, protection and privacy rather than a standardized implementation for all shelters. In cases where HHs are unable to effectively conduct the shelter rehabilitation, IRW technical teams will directly implement the rehabilitation work for disadvantaged households. Further, the implementation team will ensure all planned rehabilitation work by the beneficiaries through Self-help approach, and the targeted HHs will receive a customized training on the use and management of cash. Safe access of groups at-risk of COVID-19 complications will be ensured through considering the precaution safety measures and protective tools in all sites. The NFIs and hygienic materials will be distributed to HHs meetings this criteria based on the rapid technical assessment, and the awareness sessions will promote equal knowledge and participation between female and male of good hygiene practices in the households. The protection approach will be followed throughout the whole implementation phase to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level.
