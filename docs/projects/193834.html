Project Summary

The proposed intervention links with and contributes to the Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) Sector strategic objectives for the upcoming humanitarian cycle, such as improving the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life sustaining food assistance; supporting the self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives; and improving communities’ capacity to sustain households’ livelihoods through the rehabilitation/building of productive infrastructure as well as supporting services. It targets supporting displaced populations, returnees and host communities to meet their basic needs and access to essential basic services in West Darfur, West Kordofan, and South Kordofan States. As inadequate investment in agriculture, protracted displacements, frequent insecurity, impacts of the ever-changing climate, lack of investment in social services have condemned a large number of people to food insecurity and lack of livelihood opportunities, this intervention will also contribute directly to the rest of FSL sector sub-objectives by addressing life threatening and life sustaining needs. In addition, it will indirectly contribute to the sector’s third strategic objective by providing investment in rehabilitation and construction of productive agricultural infrastructures and supporting initiatives related to addressing the negative impact of climate change. The continued insecurity in the state means that access to agricultural land remains limited as farmers abandoned their farms. In 2023, Concern aims at reaching 75,460 individuals (33,957 men and 41,503 women) across West Darfur, South and West Kordofan through a set of multi-funded interventions combining in-kind food assistance, cash, and vouchers to respond to the specific food security and nutrition needs of internally displaced persons (IDPs), hosting and returning population groups, in addition to agricultural and livestock interventions (including animal vaccination campaigns).

1 - Needs

The situation in Sudan started to deteriorate, and humanitarian needs have significantly increased as the country experienced a political crisis following the military takeover on 25 October 2021, a continued deterioration of the economy, conflict and displacement, especially in the Darfur and Kordofan states. In addition, the spread of dry spells and crop failure in 115 localities in 14 states across the country have affected 5.6 million people, while high prices of agricultural inputs have affected the harvest, which is down by around 35 per cent compared to 2021. The Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) sector estimates that the number of people in need (PiN) of FSL support in 2023 reached almost 12 million individuals. According to the 2023 Sudan IPC analysis, 7,738,781 people are going to be in IPC +3 by February 2023 (16% of the total population). Out of this number, 320,177 (16%) are located in South Kordofan; 302,134 (16%) in West Kordofan; and 632,138 (32%) in West Darfur. The combined effects of conflict, economic crisis, and poor harvests are significantly affecting people’s access to food. Moreover, the war in Ukraine is compounding the existing challenges as agricultural production and trade from one of the world›s major food-exporting regions has been disrupted. The war threatens to drive rising food prices higher and create scarcity, especially for countries like Sudan that depend on wheat and other exports (sunflower oil) from Russia and Ukraine. Since 2016, Russia and Ukraine accounted for more than half of Sudan’s imported wheat, according to the International Food Police Research Institute. From operational areas across West Darfur, West and South Kordofan, Concern staff confirms high needs for food assistance through in-kind food assistance, cash, and vouchers to respond to the specific food security and nutrition needs of internally displaced persons (IDPs), hosting and returning population groups, in addition to agricultural and livestock interventions (including animal vaccination campaigns) as well as vocational training and rehabilitation activities.

The project will not have direct significant impact on the environment. For instance, in some of the localitieswhere the project will be implemented, environmental degradation is a major concern.This is caused by cutting down of trees, poor agricultural practices and over-grazing,among other factors. The project seeks to improve agricultural techniques andpractices such as soil and water management practices will be promoted. Moreover,environmental education will be mainstreamed in the training of farmers and isestimated to reach 1,950 farmers. Lack of viable alternative livelihood strategies alsolead to wanton cutting down of trees for firewood and charcoal and by seeking toprovide agriculture and livelihood opportunities, this project will directly lead to thecutting down of trees. Climate change adaptation practices will be prompted throughdifferent climate smart agriculture practices such as the use of certified short-cycleand drought tolerant seed varieties, tree planting and drip irrigation practices amongothers.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communitieswe assist; ii) the specifi c risks of the most vulnerable groups within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create; and reducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied for this intervention. Among other vulnerable individuals, refugee mothers and their children from the host communities who walk many hours to reach the centers will be oriented about ways to protect themselves on the way. Each HF, where the mobile services will refer to, will maintain the availability of a protection focal person and the communities will be informed about the role of this person. Traditional healers will be targeted by behavior change activities at community level. Focal persons in each HF (one male and one female) will be responsible for dealing with GBV cases and related protection issues. In the community where refugees settled, there will also have one-one protection focal point (possibly one female, and one male) for dealing with sensitive issues. Children will be in high focus, with field staff duly reporting on safeguarding. Beneficiaries will be oriented about their rights regarding the project objectives (e.g. access to information, access to services). In order to increase equal access for the most vulnerable from different ethical group sand backgrounds (IDPs, refugees, and host communities), information sharing about project implementation, achievements, selection of beneficiaries, will be provided continuously through a selected focal person in each group. Understanding of "Do No Harm" approach will be mainstreamed through training sessions and sensitization across project activities. In order to create a constant dialogue between connectors and dividers for conflict-sensitive decision making, conflict-sensitive activities are to be mainstreamed across all activities to promote community interaction between different groups e.g. agro-pastoralists and farmers through facilitation of conflict sensitive dialogue platforms; identification of existing community structures for dialogue (e.g. CBRM, VDAs, Pastoralists Associations, CPC, etc.); sensitization and strengthening of these groups to undertake project activities in a conflict sensitive way through Do No Harm workshops, social cohesion building through cultural and locally sensitive events; and training for the groups on identification and mapping of conflict-triggered hazards, with round-tables to be organized upon drafting of risk hazard mapping plans and related action points (ensuring cross-gender and inter-tribal participation)

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As a member of the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability (CHS), Concern continuously strengthens and expands systems and processes to ensure accountability across its development and humanitarian programmes. Concern first certified in 2017, and a re-certification audit took place in 2022 with re-certification confirmed in April 2022. Concern’s approach to accountability is based on the four pillars of: 1) information sharing; 2) participation; 3) feedback mechanisms; and 4) continual learning. Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) is in place for each area of operation, through which queries are invited, feedback provided and complaints are tracked. At the end of each year, country offices prepare a CRM report, which analyses the information regarding complaints received and highlights any learning and/or changes made by the country teams as a result of complaints received, as well as some of the successes and challenges reported by country offices. Country Management Teams provides overall direction and leadership support to implement CRM. Relevant Country Management Team members initiate investigations where necessary and are involved in decision-making based on outcome of investigations. All staff are sensitised on the CRM system and accountability principles, and information sessions and consultations are carried out with beneficiaries in relation to their rights and methods of access. All sensitive cases including sexual abuse, sexual harassment, fraud, and any serious breaches of Concern polices fall under a system of direct referral to senior management, specifically, to the Country Director. The remaining valid complaints/feedback coming to the CRM are responded or referred to the relevant programme teams for follow-up. In the event of low literacy level among programme participants, Concern will consult them to design an alternative accessible. This will be tailored to FSL project activities and ensured at every project site. Specific sector activities will be undertaken to ensure AAP, e.g. PDM after livestock/seeds distribution to ensure that recipients can express their opinions on quality of the assistance received.
