Project Summary

This project (approved 2019 HRP HPSE19-WSH-150683-1) will support critical public facilities in the Gaza Strip (GS) in maintaining safe and sufficient drinking water and adequate WASH services to reduce public health risks (Strategic Objectives 2 and 3 and WASH Cluster Strategic Objective 1). Interventions will focus on improved water quality and basic sanitation in the communities, schools, and health institutions.

The proposed intervention builds on the detailed assessments completed by Norwegian
Refugee Council (NRC) and Action Against Hunger (AAH) in 2017. These assessments were updated by AAH in 2018 on the WASH needs/gaps in public desalination plants, schools and health institutions operated by CMWU, MoEHE, and MoH and mid-2019 on WASH facilities in 4 public trauma hospitals (Aqsa, Beit Hanoun, Abu Yousef Al Najjar hospitals and Nasser Medical Complex); and Islamic Relief Palestine (IRPAL) interventions for rehabilitating desalination plants in schools and health facilities. Based on these, critical interventions will be made through a hardware approach (construction, minor rehabilitation/maintenance, and procurements related to WASH services and water quality) and soft component solutions (capacity building of technical staff in Operation Maintenance (O+M) of small-scale desalination plants/RO-units, Hygiene ToT for school health coordinators, and awareness raising for truckers/consumers of publicly supplied drinking water about safe water handling).

Based on the evidence from assessments, including AAH’s 2018 assessment of public desalination plants, other work completed in the WASH Cluster’s Water Quality Working Group (WQWG), and AAH’s 2019 assessment of 4 public trauma hospitals treating GMR injured patients, tailored advocacy and awareness messaging will be conducted to hold accountable duty bearers on the right to safe water. The partnership between AAH as the lead of the WQWG and NRC as the previous lead, and with strong advocacy capacity within each organisation, will maximise the outcomes of advocacy campaigns and water quality interventions. The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and is taking into account safety and dignity; meaningful access; accountability as well as participation and empowerment.

1 - Needs

GS has experienced limited operational support for water and wastewater services over the years, due to the electricity deficit; the lack of spare parts; limited infrastructural operation and maintenance; regular interruptions of access to water and sanitation; recent damage to water infrastructure; import restrictions on materials; and decreased funding from donors (HNO 2020). The protracted crisis seen in GS has been exacerbated in 2018 & urgent response is required to enable basic access to water and sanitation. According to PWA, public health related to water quality & access to adequate WASH facilities is a major concern in public institutions. Infrastructure in schools has deteriorated significantly over the past 12 years with negative impact on quality of education. Lack of regular budget for running costs, overcrowding, and the electricity crisis have seriously affected WASH conditions in 283 public schools (UN 2017: Gaza – 10 years later). Toilet blocks are poorly maintained, insufficient to meet the needs of male and female students, and inaccessible for students with disabilities. Adolescent girls have specific needs during menstruation and often avoid coming to school during this time due to the condition of toilet facilities. Provision of clean and sufficient quantities of water is important to ensure basic hygiene is maintained and will prevent school dropouts.Brackish or seawater desalination RO units lack repair/maintenance, are insufficient to meet daily drinking water needs, and stop functioning when electricity cuts. Insufficient water availability & lack of basic cleaning & hygiene materials negatively affect a hygienic school environment & personal hygiene for children (boys and girls). Support in meeting general needs for minor rehabilitation/regular maintenance of existing WASH facilities is requested for toilets, drinking water points, drinking/domestic water storage tanks, and 100 desalination plants operated by MoEHE which are being maintained by IRPAL since mid-2019. Construction of new toilet blocks, installation of new drinking water points, solar panels, & equipping 5 MoEHE laboratories (1 per Governorate) for water testing is a priority, accompanied by capacity building measures for technical staff responsible for O+M of desalination plants/RO units & water quality testing (AAH 2018: Public Desalination Plants Assessment). AAH 2018 assessment demonstrates that MoH and health service providers in GS, including 13 public hospitals & 29 primary health centers, face challenges in their drinking water supply and WASH facilities that are in poor conditions. AAH repaired 14 plants during 2018-19 (under Gaza Partnership-ECHO funds), however 19 plants still need maintenance and spare parts. In Shifa hospital, the largest in GS providing health services to 40% of GS’s population, 5 out of 8 desalination plants need urgent maintenance and rehabilitation, as currently only 50% of needs are met. AAH carried out a technical assessment in 2019 of 4 trauma hospitals, highlighting the need to conduct intensive maintenance (they have never been replaced or rehabilitated) to avoid risks of failure or interruption of services and ensure full and continuous operation. According to AAH assessment for public desalination plants, CMWU operates 45 small scale desalination plants that need rehabilitation, regular maintenance & spare parts, and solar panels to be operational during emergencies. Lack of O+M & lack of technical capacity of responsible staff affects access to sufficient & safe drinking water, that leads to produce only around 12% of the plants’ capacities. 68% of desalinated water (from small scale public/private providers) being consumed in GS is prone to biological contamination. AAH successfully repaired 15 plants in 2018 under the GP-ECHO funds; 20 plants were equipped with PC solar panels under OCHA Humanitarian Fund, 16 plants are under maintenance works by Oxfam in 2019, and 14 still uncovered.

The following activities are proposed to ensure critical access to WASH services, prioritising safety and dignity, taking into account gender, age & diversity. 3 - Indicators

The proposed intervention will support public service providers to cover parts of the gaps and needs in WASH facilities to maintain their critical services. AAH and NRC will maintain close coordination with public service providers, WASH cluster and other organizations working in the targeted areas to avoid duplication and ensure complementarity. Strong from their experience working together in the Gaza Partnership, AAH and NRC will exchange information/lessons learned, prepare and share fact sheets and stories among the cluster community, to be used for advocacy purposes. Project staff, in collaboration with M&E units in both organizations, will ensure that a joint results-based monitoring system is in place to document project progress toward desired outcomes and provide corrective measures where needed. Joint AAH and NRC quality control measures, accountability and transparency towards beneficiaries through established well-known feedback mechanisms and clear communication channels will be defined to allow beneficiaries to access both organizations timely. Within each community where activities will take place, in schools and hospitals, the respective community, including women’s committees, will be informed in meetings and through leaflets about the core project information including feedback and complaints mechanisms. Joint baseline/end line survey will be conducted at the beginning and after hygiene promotion activities in the targeted schools and communities, to capture the improvements in knowledge and practices. In addition, the M&E unit will conduct regular field visits to monitor progress and collect BNFs’ feedback on key project achievements and key learnings for continuous improvement. AAH and NRC with the support of local partners will ensure communities’ engagement. In public schools, female and male students & teaching staff and parents will be informed in meetings about the project objective and main activities. FGDs will be conducted with male and female student representatives to address specific hygiene-related concerns and discuss appropriate response, considering students with special needs, age and gender specific concerns and educational approaches. This will be coupled with outreach messaging related to safe water handling and water quality.Expected results and indicators:-Improve drinking water quality and access to safe drinking water by rehabilitating and resuming operation of 43 public desalination plants and connect 24 plants to PV solar energy systems. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Through the whole project cycle, NRC and AAH ensure protection mainstreaming is guided by the protection principles of Do No Harm. Both NRC and AAH consider safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. Risks related to protection in this intervention are mainly linked to safety and physical protection. During the verification and final selection of the pre-identified target facilities, all project information will be shared with the target communities to inform them about the provided assistance and services. The project will prioritize marginalized/economic vulnerable communities that do not have sufficient resources/capacities. The communication between service providers and communities will empower affected populations to request information, raise concerns, and provide feedback related to provided services. During the implementation phase, the target communities will be provided with clear and effective information on available complaint and feedback mechanisms. In addition, sharing project information and discussing the pre-identified targeted area and works separately through women’s committees will provide a safe space for women to have a strong involvement in infrastructure-related decisions impacting their communities. During the actual implementation at projects sites, physical protection and safety measures will be implemented to avoid physical safety and protection risks of affected populations (especially around targeted schools). The strong community involvement, risk analysis and management and feedback mechanism of the project prevent harm in the first place and allow project management to quickly react in case of concerns of beneficiaries throughout the project duration. The targeted schools will receive awareness sessions on the water situation in the Gaza Strip as well as on drinking water quality and purification process, public health related risks and mitigation procedures, and the role of students in preventing negative environmental impacts on their resources. Students will also receive induction on suitable safety measures to avoid accidents during construction and infrastructure rehabilitation activities, all of that through direct meetings with students and teachers in girls and boys schools, and through developing and disseminating safety messages in the schools. With regards to the dignity and safety of beneficiaries, particular attention has been paid to the participatory approach. Furthermore, in order to inform its assessments and implementation AAH and NRC continuously work on understanding gender roles. By understanding gender roles and household dynamics, we believe that the organisation mitigates the risks of doing harm. In 2019, AAH conducted a masculinity study in both Gaza and the West Bank, focused on identities/roles of men in order to fully understand men, women, girls and boys and gender dynamics. AAH and NRC have in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. Even more, the protection cluster facilitated trainings for staff with the aim to know how to detect and refer potential cases of GBV.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As one of AAH’s and NRC’s core values, AAH and NRC thrive to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. In 2019, AAH has conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation for which the consultant spent the month of March in oPt. Particular attention was paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments. AAH and NRC aim for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. Also, AAH started engaging with Humanity & Inclusion as inclusion experts to sensitise and inform programming. AAH and NRC are reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age; though pending the reporting format, AAH and NRC usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. In addition, AAH and NRC started in its assessments to consider ‘barriers’ of particularly vulnerable groups and ‘enabling factors’ for people with specific needs. This intervention is designed to respond to the WASH urgent needs in public facilities, in line with the Right to Water. As explained previously, the Gazan population is facing serious lack of access to good quality drinking water and correct quantities of domestic water. This project will ensure access to good quality water, and reduce public health related risks, considering and prioritizing the impacts on vulnerable communities, especially those most vulnerable to crises. The specific locations and facilities have been chosen according to two main criteria: community capacity (vulnerability), and number of affected people (the impact). The target communities in this project live among the most vulnerable and marginalized areas who suffer from the lack of employment opportunities, lack of access to basic services, (lack of access to water, electricity cuts) and do not have the financial means to pay for such services. With regards to access, the intervention concerns public facilities (managed by MoH and MoEHE) thereby making sure we reach equally and impartially to the population needs.
