Project Summary

The Joint Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment (JMCNA), 2021, projects that the scale of shelter and NFI (SNFI) needs in 2022 is expected to increase in comparison to the needs in 2021, mainly driven by extensive flooding that damaged shelters and caused the loss of NFIs, displacement due to conflict, drought and forced eviction, and the inability of households to repair and construct shelters or buy basic non-food items due to limited financial resources. UNHCR post-return monitoring data suggests that a high proportion of refugee returnees live in housing they do not own, are squatting or have no documentation; leading to protection concerns, including housing insecurity and risk of eviction. While access to land ownership remains a barrier to adequate shelter and durable solutions, more municipalities provide land for IDPs and property titles as they understand that land ownership is the foundation for durable solutions and integration. New IDPs in informal settlements are in high need of emergency SNFIs with their dignity and protection at risk. The large majority of IDPs are in a protracted situation, averaging more than seven years exemplifying the need for access to land, durable shelters, jobs and access to services. Women, children, the elderly, child- and female-headed households (CHHH and FHHH), persons living with disabilities (PLWDs), people living in conflict zones, and marginalized groups are mostly exposed to the highest risks. Diakonie Katastrophenhilfe (DKH) and its implementing partners in Somalia (IPs) seek to improve the living conditions of the newly displaced, protracted IDPs, refugees returnees, refugees and asylum seekers. The most vulnerable IDP households in areas of new or recent arrivals, and vulnerable IDPs in protracted displacement, refugee returnees and asylum seekers, will be prioritized for emergency SNFI kits. SNFI kits shall be distributed through flexible vouchers where the list of eligible items for redemption from contracted traders, is not fixed but gives the flexibility to prioritize individual items, and DKH and IPs aim to provide standard cluster recommended emergency SNFI kits to IDPs in response to their emergency needs. The project will be implemented in Banadir, Gedo, Bay, Hiraan, Galgaduud, and Mudug regions, targeting a total of 3000 households (18,000 persons) in 55 IDP camps and host communities. While DKH and IPs will prioritize the displaced populations for the ESK support, the project will also identify most vulnerable host community households to benefit too, especially to ensure cohesive and peaceful co-existence. The project incorporates gender dynamics and specific needs of different groups through needs assessments and response recommendations. The SNFI voucher includes core relief items for daily household use. The shelter and NFIs package will be for the Cluster standard package and informed by the priority needs in the targeted locations. For example, priority shelter support will address leakage during rains, insulation, and provide bathing facilities, while the NFI priorities will be blankets, sleeping mats, mosquito nets, solar lamps and kitchen utensils in addition to plastic sheeting. Hygiene and other WASH items such as jerry cans for safe water storage and will also be prioritized especially for the control of the spread of diseases including COVID-19. The unconditional and restricted flexible voucher will be handed to new IDPs (including newly evicted IDPs) and the beneficiaries will prioritize items from the list. PDM will be conducted to ensure accountability, appropriateness of materials and effectiveness of distribution, in addition to supporting the establishment and strengthening of complaints response mechanism (CRM). Protection will be mainstreamed in all the interventions, with data disaggregated by vulnerabilities, disabilities, age and gender, and awareness created on the same, and the beneficiary selection criteria applied for target groups.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

DKH will use an unconditional but restricted voucher system where the beneficiaries will have the flexibility to redeem household items of choice from the list of items approved by the cluster for emergency kits. This will enhance the decision-making and dignity of the beneficiary as they are able to make an independent choice, with the flexibility provided by cash, to purchase what they need, including quantity, but guided by the list allowed by the Shelter and NFI Cluster. While cash enhances the beneficiaries' income level, it also contributes to strengthening local market functionality with the injection of the cash into the local markets, especially in the Somalia context where purchasing power among the vulnerable is low. DKH and IPs will use paper vouchers with assigned values and list of ESK and NFI items, as per the Cluster guidelines. The vouchers will be restricted, but flexible to the beneficiaries to choose what they would need most from the list. The flexibility in the choice of items will make the beneficiaries address their immediate SNFI needs, unlike if the distribution was in-kind, where they would not have a choice. In order to deter the diversion of aid, DKH will ensure that the identified beneficiaries are needy through conducting assessments and utilizing Cluster-approved vulnerability criteria. This will ensure that the needy households will be reached with support. Further, before the vendors are engaged, they are sensitized on their responsibilities in ensuring that the beneficiaries receive the items. DKH and IPs have also put in place mechanisms to monitor the distribution processes, with the vendors supplied with beneficiary registrations forms to capture the details on the beneficiaries and support given. In addition, DKH and IPs will conduct post-distribution monitoring, with sampled beneficiaries to assess the access and utilization of the support provided. Given that the vouchers will be flexible, the beneficiaries will only access items that they will need, limiting the possibility of diverting the support.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The project has considered market functionality through tagging the amounts distributed being dependent on the market assessments on costs of Shelter and NFI per region. This ensures that while the beneficiaries purchasing power is increased they will be able to get access to livelihoods from the existing markets. DKH will implement the project in an integrated approach where results from market assessments conducted and seasonality analysis under the food security (FS) sector among others will inform the Shelter and NFI project implementation decisions, to ensure that the markets function as normal and beneficiaries are able to access goods and services from the markets; and to better understand, accommodate, and make use of market systems. The cash provided based on market values of the shelter and NFI in the existing local markets thereby supporting the local economy. During the implementation of the project, the team will liaise with other stakeholders to monitor the price behaviour and supplies for commodities to inform the effects the cash intervention is having on the local economy. Decisions will be made appropriately to ensure the cash is used for the right purpose, with or little effect on the market functionality.
