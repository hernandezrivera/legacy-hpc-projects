Project Summary

This project aims to support vulnerable individuals overcome barriers to accessing healthcare in Lebanon, while supporting Lebanon’s ailing health system against deteriorating economic conditions and against the continued challenges posed by COVID-19 (C19). The project will provide essential health services across the continuum of care either through an implementing partner or in close coordination with partners already providing the services. These include primary healthcare (PHC), advanced diagnostics, secondary and tertiary healthcare. All interventions will be closely coordinated with Ministry of Public Health (MoPH), WHO, health sector and partners. The selection of supported facilities is based on the geographical distribution of migrants and is done in close collaboration with MOPH's PHC department which facilitates support provided to the MoPH, to strengthen PHC facilities. PHC centres providing a range of specialized services will be prioritized. The modalities follow the MoPH's existing support schemes, by providing subsidized consultation fees for healthcare packages. Focus will also be given to providing mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) as well as reproductive health services to the most vulnerable migrants. Finally, the project will continue supporting the C19 response in complementarity with existing support. Under the C19 section, IOM will cover the mandatory PCR tests required by various institutions for admission, e.g. in case of surgeries, or admission to mental health facilities, or shelters. Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) trainings for all front liners are planned and supported under the C19 response of the Middle East Response (MER) which IOM is the primary recipient of funds. A network of community mobilizers will be engaged in accordance with the sector guidance and equipped with the necessary trainings and materials to deliver quality, integrated health messaging including C19 prevention and vaccination. Communication will be done following different channels and in the different migrant languages. Upon the request of multiple partners and noting that many partners are new to migrant programming; IOM aims to provide a cultural adaptation training. The main objective is to render the services migrant friendly and accessible to this vulnerable group. Building on IOM’s work to assess Points Of Entries’ (POEs) C19 preventive measures, IOM aims to strengthen the C19 preventive measures at 5 POEs: Masnaa, Qaa, Aaboudieh, Aarida, Bokayaa. To be done by strengthening MoPH capacity at POEs with deployment of Port Health Officers, and development and implementation of standard operating procedures and training of trainers for non-health actors.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Provision of inpatient care will be in facilities already selected by WHO, and the Syrian American Medical Society Foundation (SAMS) who IOM are already working with. IOM will provide support to deliveries via a third-party administrator. Access to delivery assistance, secondary, tertiary healthcare, and advanced diagnostics, based on IOM’s secondary healthcare standard operating procedures, and medical approval in case of cesarian section. Mental health support, both at PHC level, and via psychiatric facilities in hospitals, will be specialized to culturally sensitive considerations, and provided through a skilled and well-established implementing partner. IOM will continue to select PHCs through discussion with MOPH, to identify locations to support through subsidized consultations. In case of PHC medical shortages, IOM will provide medical supplies on an individual basis. POE activities will see deployment of health officers, provision of TOT sessions, and equipment such as medical waste bins, PPEs. IOM will adapt and translate RCCE material, train community members to carry out outreach.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

All staff working under the project including front liners, and as per IOM standards, are trained on PSEA. All staff and IPs are as well introduced to IOM’s code of conduct and are bound to sign it. Information on the complaint mechanism is shared with all beneficiaries through visuals posted in service delivery points with a hotline to call when needed. Project teams work in close coordination with protection teams to ensure emerging risks are identified and addressed promptly. Project staff and beneficiaries alike are provided with PPEs and COVID-19 prevention measures are respected in all activities and service delivery. In addition, staff are provided with the needed contacts for mental health support through the National System and two IOM staff wellbeing officers provide confidential counselling to staff wishing to access this service. Regular workshops and a well being network have been put in place in IOM Lebanon mission to provide the needed support to all staff.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

To meet AAP and related principles, modalities will integrate consultative procedures to maximize target groups input into project design. Community engagement will shape RCCE delivery, and adaptation of material to enhance, communication, and translation of material. PHCs will have feedback slips allowing patients to rate the service and provide feedback on improvements. IOM as the mandated agency to address migrants needs has solid channels of communication with migrant community leaders, activists, consulates and embassies, all of which form a valuable base from which to extract migrant-oriented perspectives and concerns, to inform programming. These strategic relations will be leveraged in the service of the target population. In addition, project reports will be shared for transparency purposes. The project implementation is reviewed quarterly with the different stakeholders through a series of bilateral discussions and lessons learned, are developed, and disseminated.
