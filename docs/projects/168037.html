Project Summary

In order to meet the needs of the most food insecure and vulnerable populations in Zimbabwe, through the rural Lean Season Assistance (LSA), WFP proposes to provide emergency food assistance to COVID-19 quarantine centers for returnees and to allocate most of the funding towards the provision of emergency food assistance in districts with the highest prevalence of food insecurity (IPC 4 and IPC 3). Through LSA, WFP will distribute a mix of cereals, pulses, and vegetable oil to cover 2100kcal of the daily requirements per person. The LSA programme provides immediate access to food for the most vulnerable while addressing the underlying causes of food insecurity and malnutrition of vulnerable populations affected by drought, economic instability and COVID-19.

1 - Activities

LSA Specific Output Result 1: Effective delivery of food assistance to the most food insecure and vulnerable rural households.Under Output 1, the LSA intervention will be split between short-and medium-term support. The short-term support (3 months) will reserve a limited number of in-kind rations to supplement food supplies in COVID-19 quarantine centres for returnees while they undergo the GoZ mandated 14-day quarantine period. The medium-term support (6 months) will go towards providing food assistance to highly food insecure districts across Zimbabwe. Short-term COVID-19 quarantine center support As of 13 June, a total of 8,783 people returned to Zimbabwe from some 48 different countries. The majority of these returnees came from South Africa (some 4,457 individuals) and Botswana (over 2,000 individuals). They traveled with limited personal items and supplies. Most returnees entered Zimbabwe through the Beitbridge, Plumtree and Forbes borders. From the border posts, returnees are then transferred to the nearest provincial quarantine centers. Most of these quarantine centers have insufficient food to provide returnees with during the quarantine period. Amongst other factors, due to hunger, a total of 169 returnees have escaped quarantine centres country wide. The provision of food assistance will not only supplement what little food quarantine centres have but will also reduce the impetus for returnees to flee these centres during the mandatory quarantine period. Most of the returnees from South Africa and Botswana are economic refugees, therefore, most travel with insufficient food to cover the quarantine period. To compound matters, the number of returnees increases daily. IOM projects that up to 20,000 returning migrants are expected over the next 3 to 6 months. WFP has been discussing the food shortages affecting quarantine centers with the Resident Coordinator, IOM and the Ministry of Social Welfare. While there seems to be some food available to cover the needs, preliminary assessments by IOM could not establish the precise food made available to the returning migrants in quarantine centres in terms of quantity, diversity and frequency of the returning migrants in quarantine centres, both the Ministry of Social Welfare and IOM have raised concerns on the sustainability of the food provision. IOM's initial assessment also did not fully account for food security. For LSA to begin providing food the food insecure returnees, a food security assessment would need to be done and WFP would need to receive an official request from the GoZ. In light of recent conversations, this is a highly probable outcome. Given the projected number of returning migrants is expected to increase, WFP is proposing to provide rations (of cereals, pulses and oil) to cover 20,000 individuals (2,100kcal per day) for a period of 14 days, which will be available to meet the temporary food needs of the returnees through wet feeding for six month. The returnees would be receiving 450g cereal, 80g pulses and 25g vegetable oil per person per day. The provision of food assistance will specifically target entry point quarantine centers, with the aim of encouraging returnees to use formal crossing points.
