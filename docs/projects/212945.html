Project Summary

The project aims to address the economic challenges faced by Syrian communities by providing inclusive, equitable, and sustainable economic recovery and resilience. The project focuses on urgent livelihoods and employment support through addressing distinct facets of economic recovery, private sector revitalization, human capital development, and active labour markets while aiming for medium to long-term poverty reduction, economic recovery, and peacebuilding. The project is structured on two distinct levels, addressing both macro and micro dimensions. Here’s how the project will be implemented, and the main activities involved:
1. Macro Level: The project seeks to enhance the overarching employment and business environment. This involves improving those environments and strengthening related capacities for better outcomes.
2. Micro Level: The project directly supports crisis-affected individuals and communities through an area-based approach. This includes providing vocational and skills training, employment and placement support, freelancing support, and provision of productive assets for self-employment.
The 2023 Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview reports 15.3 million people in need of assistance, with a rise in internally displaced individuals. The country faces a multifaceted socio-economic crisis exacerbated by factors such as the Lebanese financial crisis, the Covid-19 pandemic and an Earthquake in 2023 further worsened the situation. Micro-small and medium enterprises (MSMEs), constituting 95% of total enterprises, have been severely impacted, facing challenges in finance, infrastructure, and support. Unemployment rates exceed 44%, with youth unemployment surpassing 66%, exacerbating poverty and inequalities. Technical Education and Vocational Training (TEVT) capacity is below 30%, affecting skilled labour availability.
The project addresses immediate needs and provides urgent livelihoods and employment support to affected individuals and communities. It also focuses on medium to long-term poverty reduction, economic recovery, and peacebuilding. Efforts are made to revitalize the private sector, enhancing skills, human capital development and creating opportunities for employment and income generation.
Some of the project’s activities include:
• Tailored skills development and access to employment initiatives aligned with market needs, emphasizing gender-responsive approaches.
• Inclusive self-employment support by offering the target group the hard skills such as public works, carpentry, solar systems installations, vehicle maintenance, home appliance maintenance, etc.
• Freelancing support (remote jobs) through the provision of market-relevant in-demand technical skills.
As for the PSEA Strategy, UNDP will prepare training and awareness gender materials according to UNDP guideline, then conduct training/orientation/ awareness sessions on promoting gender sensitivity behaviors with targeted communities and institutions of civil society.
The project in Syria, operates in a challenging environment that presents several risks:
1. Operational Risks: The project operates in a context of political uncertainty and high fiduciary risks. Mobility and access restrictions can hamper operations.
2. Donor and Regulatory Risks: Donor redlines, sanctions, and the high level of scrutiny on humanitarian actors operating within Syria pose additional risks and challenges.
3. Security Risks: The project needs to maintain an appropriate security plan considering the security situation in the country. Failure to maintain and implement an appropriate security plan can lead to risks and liabilities. To mitigate these risks, the UNDP has developed a risk log identifying both risks associated with the project and its mitigation measures. The project will regularly update critical risk analysis and risk logs to ensure that timely preventive mitigation measures are taken to avoid program disruption and harm to the communities it serves.
