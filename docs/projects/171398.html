Project Summary

Shelter remaining key gap in Somalia, over 2.6 million IDPs in over 2,300 sites in Somalia have shelter challenges. These are include protected IDPs and those who have recently displaced with no proper shelter at all. Recently flood affected in riverine areas remain in key gap. the DRC assessment conducted in Hiran during recent flood affected shows that communities especially IDPS lost all their shelter. in addition over 60 percent women headed households reside in sub-standard, unsecure and overcrowded shelters and that are rendering them vulnerable to risk of gender-based violence. some IDPs reside in poor host communities where shelter is insufficient.
The project aims to improve the physical protection and dignity of 48,500 individuals (2,300 Elderly, 6,866 men, 7,903 women, 15,348 girls and16093 boys and 1,869PWDs) including IDPs, returnees and poor host communities in 10 districts of Banadir , Bay, Hiran , Lower juba , lower Shabelle and, Gedo regions by providing 8,000 Non Food Items (NFIs), 6,000 Emergency Shelter Kits (ESKs) and construction of 2,000 Transitional Shelters. DRC will prioritize the displaced but excluded from cash food humanitarian assistance, those in congested settlements and hosts accommodating IDPs focusing on marginalized families and vulnerable female-headed households. DRC will also include Persons with disabilities challenged physical access and information in shelter and NFI assistance. Transitional shelter will benefit those challenged by recovery and reconstruction shelter needs after the disasters/conflict integrating community involvements. All households benefitting from ESKs and Transitional shelter will receive a household standard NFI Kit. ESK/NFI will distributed through cash/voucher/in-kind modalities based on market assessments. DRC will conduct site planning and training in settlements to facilitate access and other essential services while also decongesting them. DRC will carry out Joint analysis prior to implementation to inform the most needy sites and priorities. Predetermined beneficiary selection criteria will be used to reach the most vulnerable HHs/individuals. Gender will be mainstreamed into the project through the specific consideration of the different needs of women, men, boys and girls during the design of activities.
Monitoring and evaluation will be based on gender, age and diversity dis-segregated data. pre intervention assessment will be carried targeting selected beneficiaries before the intervention. after shelter construction and NFI distribution, DRC will conduct Post Distribution Monitoring and Post Construction Monitoring (PCM) for transitional and emergency Shelters to analyze outcomes, the beneficiaries’ level of satisfaction and draw lessons learnt for future programming. Key stakeholders including shelter cluster partners, communities and local authorities will be coordinated with this response.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Use of cash has shown greater impact compared to in kind approach, mainly because it gives flexibility to the beneficiaries to allocate and design the limited resources available to their needs appropriately, it also contributes sustainability and ownership. However quite substantial considerations have to be looked in to including market access to inform which approach to be used and where. In this application, DRC foresees applicability of cash in some areas particularly the big towns, hence where possible DRC will adapt cash approach in order to ease access for the households. Nevertheless, market assessment will be carried out to inform such decision. DRC has recently implemented similar cash approaches where the Proposed items (ESK & NFIs) were locally available as well community’s preference shown cash VS in kind. to minimize risks associated with Cash approach, DRC has in placed mitigation plans including splitting the transfers and forming respective committees to monitor the implementations.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

DRC will undertake market assessment to determine availability of standard ESKs within the local market and/or nearby markets. This will help DRC to design the intervention in terms of whether in-kind distribution will be undertaken or cash/voucher system will be used. DRC will check the availability of Shelter/NFIs materials in the local market , available local materials will be prioritized

Mitigation Measures

Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and servicesMitigation measure : DRC will address security issues to ensure the project is implemented smoothly without any security implications and challenges affecting programming by imploring the DRC extensive risk management practices and support this by robust country level safety standard operating procedures and comprehensive context specific contingency plans. DRC has done mapping of Somalia and categorized regions based on the Local Security Codes. High risky areas require that more procedures are followed for project implementation to happen smoothly. In term risk mitigation, DRC will avert delays in implementation of activities by negotiations and discussions with local authorities.For risks like particularly for the distribution of NFI and ESK was fear of contagious COVID-19 in the distribution sites. DRC team will ensure protection protocols were observed and kept social distancing during distribution. DRC will ensure technical support to all works using the robust technical staff at country and field levels Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activitiesMitigation measure: DRC will conduct community entry meetings followed by intensive community mobilization with all stakeholders, beneficiaries and minority groups. The beneficiary selection will be conducted in a participatory approach with the community leaders and the local authorities that will identify the most vulnerable people to avert exploitation of the beneficiary selectionRisk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources.Mitigation; Entitlements, complain handling and hot-lines for feedback and criteria will be displayed in the public areas. All beneficiaries were verified physically by DRC staff together with the community committees. Beneficiary feedback will be handled confidentially and in a timely manner. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project areaMitigation: DRC already has enacted a system to ensure shelter beneficiaries are not affected by land tenure issues. DRC will facilitate the signing of agreements betwwen camp leadrs and DRC witnessed by local authorities. Risk of explosive hazard contamination in project target areaDRC also undertake a rigorous risk analysis in areas of operation. This includes mine risk education (MRE) which falls under DDG a specialized agency of DRC.
