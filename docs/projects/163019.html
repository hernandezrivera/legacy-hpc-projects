Project Summary

Save the Children proposes voucher project aiming to address the most critical and unmet humanitarian needs for 30,000 individuals in 3 districts( Beitbridge, Murehwa and Matobo) in Zimbabwe from January to December 2020. These districts are at the moment in crisis and according to FEWSNET report of 13 December, the countries in Southern African will need food assistance for the whole of 2020. The rains as from March will below normal. These district are already among those which are in crisis.
The proposed action will be achieved through provision of food vouchers and integrated protection interventions supporting safe and dignified access to food. The action will provide 62% calculated to meet the monthly basic food basket as agreed by the working group. Beneficiaries will be selected from the most food insecure wards in districts where there are no other players. The Action will prioritize food voucher distribution for vulnerable HHs with children >5, child headed HHs, pregnant and lactating women (PLW), people living with disabilities and the chronically ill. An assessment of protection risks in using vouchers as a modality will be implemented to ensure that the correct mitigation measures are put in place to provide a safe and dignified approach for the most vulnerable persons. Systems will be developed in child safeguarding and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and the programme will be connected to referral pathways and response programming should there be any reports made. Consideration will be made to ensure that feedback mechanisms are age and gender appropriate and confidential.

1 - Activities

District Level Launches with Stakeholders: This activity will be led by Save the Children in the districts, it will also be responsible for mobilising stakeholders and having a sensitisation workshop at district level. This is where the programme will be launched. Stakeholders including local government authorities are made aware of the implementation process where their roles and responsibilities are agreed and these will inform SC on wards of operation. The budget will be shared in these fora . Community consultations, targeting, registration and verification of beneficiaries.IPC4 wards will be selected considering as well guidance by the DDRC. Beneficiary selection will be done by communities themselves, with traditional leaders playing an oversight role, using a 3-tier approach to enhance transparency of the process of the action. Tier 1: Economic means-testing; asset ownership (livestock), gainful employment (in spite of the eroded disposable incomes), remittances. Tier 2: Cross-cutting social factors; key considerations will be on vulnerability factors as determined by status such as age, gender (pregnant or lactating women), child-HHs/women-HHs, chronic illness, widowed, disability. Tier 3: beneficiary self-selection, where communities will engage in self-selection through need-based, food insecurity household ranking. The 3-tier approach will be triangulated by reference to appropriate social protection data, and HHs verifications, whereby a % of the caseload will be verified through physical homestead checks to ascertain their food insecurity status using food security indicators including variables such as HH production and stocks, livestock ownership, arable land for cultivation, income source, casual labour availability. The food assistance will vary depending on HH size. A common beneficiary selection and targeting criteria will be used. Once beneficiary demographic data collection is complete, a verification process will be conducted at different levels beginning with the village assembly where selection criteria will be shared and the purpose of the verification explained. Finally, demographic data will be validated in coordination with local authorities, stakeholders and other players .This approach will eliminate double-dipping, taking into account parallel programmes that may be on-going in the districts. HHs will be encouraged to register women as primary recipients of the entitlements, studies have proved that food in this case will reach home.Community stakeholder meetings will be conducted to discuss the voucher modalities. This will present an opportunity to raise awareness on Child safeguarding and Protection Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Save the Children will also identify traders whom they will work with.Trainings on Child Protection,Child safeguarding and Sexual ExploitationCommunity Feedback Accountability Mechanisms will be established in all meeting points. Community members will be encouraged to give feedback through the different channels that will be established. These include suggestion box, help desk, Deloitte toll free line and engagement with community gender and accountability focal person. Post registration monitoring will be done to ensure that errors of inclusion and exclusion are dealt with. The distribution of Vouchers to the most vulnerable households will followUpon completion of the Beneficiary Registration and Verification exercises, submission of the registers will follow . Post distribution monitoring will be done. The modality of distributing will be flexible and revised depending on the evolving economic and market availability situation.
