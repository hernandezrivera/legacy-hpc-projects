Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in the Gaza Strip continues to deteriorate as a result of the accumulation of the humanitarian crises that affecting all aspects of life, including the decline in the quantity and quality of the health services provided to patients in need. As the 2023 HNO indicated that 5,300 patients are in need for surgical interventions and scheduled on the MoH waiting lists. Noting that the No. of those patients usually ranges between 9,000 to 16,000 patients, which means an increase in patients' suffering and a high incidence of life-threatening complications.
To respond to these needs and to bridge the gap in providing surgical services to patients registered on the MoH waiting lists, AWDA through this project will conduct elective surgeries targeting 1,000 patients (women, men, girls, boys including PWDs) at its following health facilities:
1. Al Awda Hospital- Jabalia
2. Al Awda Hospital- Al Nuseirat
3. The Daily Surgery Unit at Al Awda Health Centre- Rafah
The spread of AWDA’s facilities in the 3 governorates of the Gaza Strip (North – Middle and South) will facilitate the beneficiaries' access to the project services.
Knowing that this project is an extension of previous interventions implemented by AWDA and funded by OPT-HF, which targeted 3,129 patients during the years 2019-2021.
In the context of the optimal response to the needs of patients in the Gaza Strip, coordination has been made with the Ministry of Health, which in turn supported the project idea and provided us with the types of operations and numbers of patients in need of these surgical operations. The project idea also was discussed with the Health Cluster Coordinator who stressed the importance of this intervention as it meets a vital need in the health sector. Moreover, coordination has been done with Al Ahli Arab Hospital to promote cooperation and avoid duplication of services provided. upon which AWDA will cover the following No. and type of surgeries:
- 300 ENT
- 400 General
- 100 Urology
- 50 Orthopedic
- 100 Pediatric
- 50 Gynecological
• 700 moderate
• 300 major
In order for the targeted patients to access information about the project activities and locations, advertisements will be carried out on social media and radio, moreover, the advertisement will include information about AWDA’s Feedback, Complaints and Response mechanisms.
To ensure maximum protection for the project beneficiaries from SEA, 12 of AWDA medical staff will be trained on PSEA.
In order to implement the principle of accountability to affected populations (AAP), and within the framework of community participation, the local community will be involved in all the project cycle process.
To respond to gender and age needs, medical staff of both sexes (M&F) will be available. Furthermore, pediatric surgeons will be available as well.

1 - Needs

The HNO 2023 indicated that there are 5,300 patients in need of surgical interventions and are scheduled on the MoH waiting lists, while the numbers of those registered on waiting lists usually range between 9,000 to 16,000 patients, and many of them are expected to suffer from deteriorating in their health status. Although thousands of elective surgeries were performed to patients who referred from the MoH to NGOs hospitals in the Gaza Strip, through funding from the OPT-HF, UNRWA and others, the need still exists in light of the deterioration of the humanitarian situation in the Gaza Strip, As the MoH suffers from a series of crises related to the severe shortage of health resources, including the shortage of essential medications and medical disposables that reached 41% and 17% respectively (MoH report Sept. 2022). This in addition to the long-hour power outages that reached 12 hours per day (OCHA 2022) and the lack of fuel to run the electrical generators, adding to the breakdown of many medical devices and the difficulty of repairing them. Within the framework of the community’s involvement in determining its needs and priorities, AWDA organized 5 focus groups in each governorate. Each group included 10 persons from both sexes and different age groups, including PWDs in which the participants stressed that surgeries for patients scheduled on the MoH waiting list is an important priority, as increasing waiting time for patients in need of surgical interventions increases their suffering and pain. Besides possible complications due to delays in conducting the necessary surgeries. It is worth mentioning that the idea of the project was discussed with the Health Cluster Coordinator and the Ministry of Health, whom stressed the importance of this intervention as it meets a vital need in the health sector. Coordination was also done with Al Ahli Arab Hospital to avoid duplication of services provided.

2 - Activities

The project will include the following activities: Activity 1: Conducting 1000 elective surgeries free of charge to patients (250 men, 250 women, 250 boys, 250 girls including 20 PWD) who are registered on the MOH waiting lists. The lists will be received from the MoH on a monthly basis and the surgical operations will be conducted in Al Awda Hospital – Jabalia, Al Awda Hospital - Al Nusirat and Al Awda Health Center - Rafah. The surgical operations services include: • Medical examination by specialists. • Pre and post-op investigations • Surgical operations. • Hospitalization. • Post-op Follow up. The No. and type of surgeries will be as follows:No. and type of surgeries:- 300 ENT- 400 General - 100 Urology- 50 Orthopedic- 100 Pediatric- 50 Gynecological • 700 moderate• 300 major Activity 2: Conducting 6 hours training course for 12 of AWDA medical staff (6 male – 6 female) in PSEA policy of AWDA. The training aims to ensure maximum protection for the project beneficiaries from the SEA by training participants on protection mechanisms and reporting channels. The trainer of this training will be AWDA representative in the PSEA Network who has obtained ToT training in PSEA and is accredited for training in this topic. Activity 3: Informing the community about the project activities and location adding to raising their awareness about AWDA complain mechanisms and PSEA through 2 radio spots and 2 Facebook advertisements.

3 - Indicators

The success of the project will be measured by achieving the following indicators: # of patients benefitted from provision of elective surgery intervention, # of personnel trained in PSEA, # of Persons From the community who received information about AWDA complain system and PSEA, % of patients' satisfaction with the services provided in AWDA’s health facilities. knowing that a MEAL plan will be prepared by AWDA MEAL officer including methods for data collection, frequency, responsibilities and reporting Indicators as follows: MEAL Plan: will be divided into the levels of the log frame so that the project staff ensures each level's achievement by describing the levels, indicators, and baselines. It also will identify the process of collecting data, including the tools, responsibility, data sources, frequency of data collection, types of analysis, and finally, presenting and submitting the information obtained by making comparisons between groups and variables to the intended users. Indicator Performance Tracking Table (IPTT): The MEAL Plan will contain IPTT, which clarifies the time frame of tracking the performance of the indicators of each level of the log frame. Regarding the project, a time frame of 12 months will be adopted, as it will contain the expected performance ratio and the realized ones during the implementation of the project. It is worth to mention that, once the project started, the project coordinator will conduct a meeting with the project staff, where he/she will present the project activities, goals, indicators and results to the project staff to prepare action plan and to distribute responsibilities and duties. The project coordinator and the MEAL Officer will follow up and monitor activities on the ground on a daily basis through direct contact with the project team. AWDA project coordinator will conduct regular meetings and field visits to the project location to insure continuous flow of information on the progress of activities, and to see work on the ground. Weekly reports and statistics will be sent from the field to the project coordinator where all statistics will be desegregated according to age, gender and PWDs. AWDA project coordinator will coordinate with the donor for issues related to the implementation of the project. Moreover, he/she will compare the financial plan with the real expenses as well as action plan with the achieved activities in order to find out if there is any deviation in the implementation of activities according to the time plan or the financial cost. In the context of involving the local community in the project monitoring, 3 focus groups meetings will be held with the project beneficiaries from both sexes and from different age groups including PWDs in the middle of the project duration with the aim of discovering any deviations in the implementation of activities and to work on modifying them. At the end of the project duration, satisfaction measurement questionnaires will be filled by 278 beneficiaries to determine the percentage of their satisfaction with the project's services and to benefit from their suggestions for future work strategies.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Feedback and complaints will help AWDA decision makers to: • Develop future intervention strategies that include the provision of new services or the development of existing services based on the needs and suggestions of beneficiaries. • Hold accountable employees involved in violations of the protection policy and code of conduct. • Helps to respond quickly to the needs of beneficiaries.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

WhatsApp, Face to face complaint, call center

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

AWDA set several mechanisms to receive feedback & complaints while ensuring that they can be accessible to all population groups, including PWD (whether hearing, visual or physical disabilities) as the means of submitting the complaint varied in terms of written complaints directly to the complaint box or voice complaints through the use of the Toll-free No. (which is a free No. specified for complaints), this in addition to electronic complaints via a generic e-mail, besides receiving complaints face to face or by filling out questionnaires. AWDA developed a Feedback, Complain and Response Mechanism (FCRM) which is active and includes the following classifications: • Sensitive complaint. • Complaint & dissatisfaction. • Inquire about existing services. • Request for individual support & assistance. • Thanks & appreciation. Where the call center employee, through the electronic form, fills in all the details of the complaint and forwarded it to the MEAL manager, except for sensitive complaints that are forwarded to the General Manager directly. As for the complaints on the General Manager himself, they are forwarded to the Chairman. In order to response to gender needs, there are call center workers of both sexes. All the above-mentioned complaints mechanisms are announced to our beneficiaries through social media and radio advertisements.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project idea, services and implementation mechanisms built on respecting the principles of protection stipulated in the humanitarian charter including the right to live with dignity, the right to receive assistance and the right to protection. Through this project various procedures will be taken to ensure the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries, and the principles of Do No Harm will be considered as follows: • In order to prevent SEA during the provision of the project services; 12 of AWDA medical staff will attend PSEA training. As well as the contracts of the project staff will include standard clause on PSEA in accordance with IASC principles and guidance. • In order not to expose the beneficiaries to more harm, they will be informed about PSEA and the complaints mechanism. • The project services will be provided without any discrimination. • Patient consultations and documentation are private and confidential. • To ensure that beneficiaries are not harmed while receiving services, it is worth to mention that 12 from Al Awda Hospital's medical staff from both sexes were trained in Sphere standards and Do No Harm principles, to create a positive deviation in the behavior of workers while providing health services to beneficiaries in a positive manner and away from any discrimination. • The project services will be provided to the project beneficiaries totally free of charge in accordance with the human rights-based approach. • All necessary information for the cases related to their status and mechanisms of intervention will be provided to them. • Gender needs will be taken into account through the presence of a female physician and nurses. • Age needs will be taken into account through the presence of pediatrician. • AWDA’s hospitals are designed to suit the people with physical disabilities to ensure easy access to the services. • Providing security and protection through the hospital's security staff. • The availability of ICU ambulance to refer emergency cases to MoH hospitals. • Providing work accident insurance for the project staff. • Providing the project staff with health insurance at AWDA health facilities. • The project services will be provided to the beneficiaries regardless their color, race, religious or political affiliation, where the project will contribute to enable equal and impartial access to the provided health services to the marginalized and vulnerable people.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AWDA adopts the principle of equality and impartially in providing its services to beneficiaries, especially marginalized groups that need humanitarian interventions to improve their health and humanitarian status. AWDA provides its services without any discrimination on the basis of age, sex, religion, race or political affiliation. Knowing that through this project AWDA will receive the lists of beneficiaries from the Ministry of Health, and to achieve the principle of impartiality in the provision of services AWDA will provide the surgical services to all those mentioned in the lists on the basis that each patient has its individual characteristics and special needs, especially individuals with disabilities, children and women as the most vulnerable in the society and who need special attention and protection depend on Human dignity, confidentiality and non-discrimination. Where AWDA provides its services according to the slogan (health service is a right to whoever needs it). Access to the project services will be easy as it will be provided through AWDA’s 3 health facilities (Al Awda Hospital – Jabalia – Northern Gaza Strip, Al Awda Hospital – Al Nusirat – Middle governorate of Gaza Strip and Al Awda Health Center in the south governorate of the Gaza Strip). The geographical locations of these facilities are characterized by their presence in the middle of the governorates, which is easily accessible and is known to the public since their establishment. The project services will be provided to the target groups including PWDs in a safety and dignity manner, and without any discrimination.
