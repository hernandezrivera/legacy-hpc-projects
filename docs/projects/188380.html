Project Summary

Through the proposed intervention, TGH will contribute to achieving objectives 1, 3, 4 and 5 of the 2022 Protection Sector’s Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Syria through providing quality, integrated and sustainable protection services through community based approaches to children, young adults and women. Psychosocial support (PSS), including the identification of cases facing protection risks, their access to specialized services and case-management will be proposed. The project will also include awareness raising on a high range of protection concerns such as GBV, child protection (CP) and risk education (RE) toward explosive hazards. Besides, the action will provide GBV response services especially for the most vulnerable women at most risk linked to empowerment activities through community centers and outreach mechanisms. The proposed intervention will be part of an integrated programme linked to Education project in public schools; which will consist in providing support in the communities given that both sectors’ needs are intertwined. TGH is already implementing CP programs under Education sector in schools in Hama, Dar’a and Rural Damascus and aims at scaling up its intervention through providing assistance to communities at risk to a total of 4,900 conflict-affected people in Madiq Castle and Harbanafse (Hama), Dar’a, Tassil and Sheikh Miskine (Dar’a), Dhameer, Ghizlaniyyeh and Hajar Al Aswad (Rural Damascus). The intervention will provide approximatively 4,900 children with CP in public schools and 280 adolescents in safe spaces referrals and case management at home and protection awareness campaigns among the parents and the community. These sub-districts were selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) with all the localities being rated over 3 (29% of level 3, 42% of level 4 and 29% of level 5), TGH 2021-2022 field assessments and coordination with municipalities SARC and other partners. The proposed intervention will consist in:
- Providing quality and integrated protection services, with a focus on community based approaches including awareness raising, psychosocial assistance through community centres and outreach mechanisms
- Provide quality and lifesaving GBV response services including case management and psychosocial support with a focus on girls and women on the most at risk
- Providing GBV prevention and empowerment activities with a focus on girls and women on the most at risk
- Conducting community-based risk education regarding explosive hazards;
- Building the capacities of facilitators, counsellors and case managers to prevent and respond to specific CP issues;
- Building the capacities of outreach volunteers (ORVs) to conduct data collection, awareness sessions and case identification and management, under the supervision of TGH case workers, in the surrounding communities;
- Providing PSS including risk education and recreational activities to children, including parenting programs
- Establishing and building the capacities of community based volunteers with the objective increasing awareness about child protection risks and ensure sustainability of the project.
TGH will consider and ensure gender-and-age aspects across all sub-sectors of focus – CP, GBV, and general protection. Cultural preferences and sensitivities will be considered through consultations with the staff – the majority of whom will be from local community and the affected populations. Specific needs of children, people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups have been considered.

4 - Needs

TGH’s proposed intervention is built on extensive protection risks identified through TGH field assessment conducted in Nov. 2021 and Jan 2022. The conflict had a profound impact on children’s well-being and development, and putting them in a state of psychological distress. Victims are thus more likely to drop out from school than other children because of psychological traumas. It has been reported that around 20% of the children in all targeted areas are either orphans or unaccompanied due to the death or imprisonment of their parents. Child labour is also very common due to the poor economic situation worsened by the economic crisis and COVID-19. It is preventing school attendance and exposing children to a high range of risks, including injuries, work exploitation and harassment. Besides, girls and women continue to be strongly affected reporting to be subject to violence including intimate partner violence, sexual violence, early marriage, forced marriage, deprivation of freedom of movement and forced pregnancy. Lack of education and livelihood opportunities limit their freedom and erode their resilience. Access to specialized service providers including specialized medical care, MHPSS, legal service and civil documentation and alternative care services is very limited due to lack of capacity and resources in the assessed areas.TGH team reported that around 75% of the civilians assessed are still exposed to mine risks as a consequence of the hostilities.

5 - Implementation of activities

Integrated protection services including PSS, CM and referrals, GVB and CP raising awareness will be conducted towards the community through the establishment of community centres and mobile units. Case workers will be able to provide direct support or external referrals such as medical care, MHPSS, legal service and civil documentation and alternative care services, etc. Where decision by mandated government agency is warranted, cases will be referred to the Directorate of Social Affairs and Labour (DoSAL) for appropriate action and legal decisions. TGH will provide services based on community approach and initiatives in order to ensure the coherence, acceptance and the sustainability of the activities. Protection awareness sessions will be conducted among the community with the support of community outreach volunteers. TGH will establish and train community based volunteers in each location who will support TGH team in PSS, identifying the protection risks and organizing the campaign awareness toward parents and community. Specific empowerment activities including PSS, life skills, literacy and numeracy as well as grant based initiatives will be provided in community centres to early married girls, adolescents male and women. Child protection assistance will be complementary to TGH education programs with a focus on the most marginalized groups such as early married girls and out of school adolescents.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations if one community is served and a neighbouring community not served Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation/ Ensure a needs and services mapping is undertaken before final selection as well as consultation with community, local authorities and coordination with other partners are made. - Different vulnerable groups might not have the same access to services and assistance especially in the referrals pathway due to lack of services Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation Ensure service mapping and share the information with community and ensure good communication among the community in order to not create fake expectation if service not available- Backlash from family or community on women participating in recreational, vocational or empowerment activities/ Community and family stigmatization for participating in programming, family resistance to girls’ mobility/ Low participation of girls because of family and community resistance Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: Engage community from onset of project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders. Adaptation of GBV messaging to ensure sensitivity to the context and culture. Targeting adolescent girls with socially accepted activities like life skills as safe entry point


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

- Children victim of abuse or violence from TGH staff or from other children.Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: application of Child Safeguardingand PSEA measures, transparency of recruitment process; training of all staff andassociates who engage with children on PSEA, signature of code of conduct alsoposted on walls; establishment of effective child-friendly complaint mechanism;awareness sessions on children's rights and GBV; PSS sessions on child protectionrisks, similarities and differences/accepting others; gender-balanced staffing.
