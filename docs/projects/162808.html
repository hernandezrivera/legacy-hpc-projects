Project Summary

In North Darfur, GOAL works closely with the Ministries of Agriculture and Animal Resources in Khartoum and in North Darfur as well as FAO on identifying needs, gaps in coverage, and on programme implementation. In addition, GOAL remains an active participant in the FSL Cluster at both national and state levels where FSL programming is implemented, including North Darfur. GOAL’s FSL programming is closely integrated with other sectors, especially nutrition, health, and protection through targeting vulnerable households (including affected by malnutrition, people living with disabilities, and women-headed households) in catchment areas of supported clinics. GOAL will also incorporate protection and women’s empowerment approaches. With OFDA funding, GOAL proposes to focus on the IDP populations in Kutum Town as well as Kassab and Fata Borno Camps near Kutum, and complementary funding (SHF) will be used to reach vulnerable host communities in Kutum and Al Waha localities to ensure all relevant communities have their needs addressed. Activities are closely coordinated with other FSL actors in North Darfur to eliminate any duplication of effort. Through the FSL Cluster and Cash Working Groups, GOAL incorporates and shares lessons learned and harmonized approaches to livelihoods activities in order to maintain high technical standards in its work.
In South Kordofan, GOAL will work with Village Development Committee (VDC) in areas where they are already established and functional. If the VDC are not available, GOAL will work community leaders to form a VDC consisting of community leaders, influential community members (such as teachers, health workers, religious leaders etc.) as selected by community members. A close effort will be made to ensure that gender representation is observed in the composition of VDC members. And then, GOAL will work with the VDC to identify 200 households in each locality (400 total) to benefit from the first and second phase distribution. The first 100 households will receive 3 local species female goats per family directly from GOAL in the first phase of distribution and this group will make commitment with the VDC to pass on the first generation to the remaining 100 households. The commitment will be between among the first phase beneficiaries, VDC and the second phase beneficiary. This means each beneficiary from the second phase is tied with specific households from the first phase. Male goats will be distributed to the VDC and managed by them to ensure that they are accessed equally by all recipients of female goats.
GOAL will identify and support existing Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) in the targeted areas to provide training and ongoing monitoring and care for the distributed animals. GOAL will provide refresher training to CAHWs and facilitate their work, in particular by supporting their transportation to rural and IDPs areas, as the lack of vehicles or resources for transport has been identified as a major challenge to effective support for animal welfare services across South Kordofan.

1 - Needs

The food security situation in Sudan, which was already among the poorest countries per capita in the world, deteriorated significantly in 2018 due to economic crisis, which saw inflation as well as shortages of cash and basic goods impact According to the most recent food security analysis, 5.67 million people are estimated to be in Crisis and Emergency (IPC Phases 3 and 4), representing 12.8% of the analysed population . Almost 1 million people (986,174, representing 2.2% of the population analyzed) are in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and more than 4.5 million people (4,688,042, representing 10.6% of the population analyzed) are in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis). North Darfur’s population is even worse off, with 17% of the population facing a food security crisis or emergency in the 1st quarter of 2019, with Kutum locality expected to further deteriorate to full food security Crisis in the 2nd and 3rd quarters due to the lean season and continued high staple food prices due to countrywide inflation . The Darfur region is situated in a fragile environment that over 60% of people rely on for their livelihoods, and where years of conflict has cut people off from their largely agropastoral livelihoods. Inter-related WASH and food security factors puts women and girls at high risk, who are forced to move long distances to isolated areas in search of water and firewood, which exposes them to massive protection risks (sexual abuse, robbery/deprivation of livelihoods), and causing severe psychological harm. Increased tree cutting for firewood, charcoal burning, and brick firing has also resulted into serious environmental degradation. The food security situation in North Darfur in 2019 presents multi-faceted vulnerability and challenges, but also significant opportunity. The results of various recent assessments, including Post-Harvest Assessment (PHA) for the 2018 rainy season, GOAL focus groups with IDP communities and local leaders, along with various market data all exemplify this mixed picture. The land area planted in North Darfur, including Kutum locality, in 2018 was 36% higher than 2017, and the total harvested area of cereal crops increased from 56% of planted area to 87% (81% in Kutum); these increases resulted from both by earlier, more regular, evenly-distributed rains as well as the reduction in violence . South Kordofan’s population also face widespread and critical needs in food security and basic livelihoods, linked to the compounding crises of long-term displacement, ongoing conflict, and severe economic deterioration in 2018. FEWSNet identified conflict-affected areas of South Kordofan as a high area of concern for 2019, with the cereal harvest reduced by nearly 20% from the 2017-18 harvest, and inflation significantly and rapidly eroding households’ purchasing power. Multiple sources have shown prices across all areas of life more than doubling, with sorghum prices in Kadugli in increased between November and December 2018, which were already 175% above the 5-year average ; a basket of basic food and hygiene items for household consumption increased by 119%; and the cost of transportation up nearly 115% - which will further increase prices and reduce access to basic needs in isolated, rural areas such as Rashad and Talodi. For people who already have difficulty in accessing livelihoods due to displacement or ongoing presence of conflict actors, this eroded value of their currency and assets makes it more difficult to re-establish their livelihoods. The main crops planted in South Kordofan are staple cereal crops, namely sorghum and millet, and groundnuts, sesame, maize, and cowpeas are also grown for consumption and cash. Staple cereal crop harvests in South Kordofan for the 2018-19 season were, as noted above, down by 20% from 2017-18, returning to typical levels during the years of active conflict in the state .

2 - Activities / Outputs

• Identification of 200 vulnerable IDP households suffering from or at risk of malnutrition, working with local development committees; agreement to distribute goats to first 100 beneficiaries and next 100 will receive the first generation of offspring from the distributed goats. • Procurement and distribution of 300 female local species goats and 10 male hybrid species goats• Identification of 100 vulnerable IDP households with knowledge and interest in poultry production, working with local development committees• Procurement and distribution of five hens and one rooster per household along with initial supply of feed• Refresher training for livestock recipient households on relevant improved rearing techniques for goat and poultry beneficiaries• Refresher training for CAHWs and facilitation of visits and training by CAHWs for goat and poultry beneficiaries

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

GOAL Sudan incorporates protection principles in every aspect of programming and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian action while making sure that response is participatory and that it is accountable to the people GOAL seeks to assist. For GOAL Sudan, protection mainstreaming is crucial both internally and externally to ensure that extremely vulnerable groups including children, women, people with disabilities and the elderly can access services and are protected from both intentional and unintentional harm. GOAL has ensured that all staff and partners are aware of protection issues through training, and protection mainstreaming tools have been used in programming and internally to ensure the safety of the extremely vulnerable groups. Throughout its intervention GOAL is promoting gender equality and promoting beliefs and norms that foster respectful, non-violent gender norms to help prevent and mitigate GBV. Safety, respect, confidentiality and non-discrimination in relation to survivors and those at risk are vital considerations for GOAL.
