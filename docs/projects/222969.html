Project Summary

The Strengthening Food Assistance and Support for Conflict-Affected Communities project directly responds to critical food insecurity and malnutrition challenges affecting vulnerable populations in Borno, Zamfara, Sokoto, Katsina, Yobe, and Adamawa States in Northern Nigeria. These areas face severe humanitarian crises due to conflict, displacement, and economic instability, leaving many households without reliable access to food and essential nutrition services.
The project aims to provide immediate life-saving food assistance and long-term nutritional support to meet the urgent needs of crisis-affected populations. Specific targets include children under five, pregnant and lactating women, and other vulnerable groups at high risk of malnutrition. Interventions include the provision of emergency food rations, cash-based transfers where operationally viable, and specialized nutrition services to prevent and treat acute malnutrition.
To enhance the resilience of communities, the project integrates community-based approaches that empower affected populations to adopt sustainable solutions for food and nutrition security. This involves training community health volunteers to identify and manage malnutrition cases, promoting nutrition-sensitive practices, and supporting livelihood restoration through income-generating activities that improve food access.
The intervention is aligned with national and global humanitarian frameworks and emphasizes collaboration with government authorities, local organizations, and international partners. This coordination ensures that the project strengthens existing systems and delivers assistance efficiently to the most vulnerable households. Data-driven decision-making and continuous monitoring are key components to maximize impact and accountability.
By addressing immediate food and nutrition needs while building community resilience, this project aims to reduce hunger, improve nutrition outcomes, and foster stability and recovery in conflict-affected regions of Northern Nigeria.

1 - Needs

The ongoing conflict in Northern Nigeria, compounded by the lean season, recurrent flood disasters, inflation, and market disruptions, has left millions of people facing critical food insecurity. In the targeted LGAs of Borno, Zamfara, Sokoto, Katsina, Yobe, and Adamawa States, these challenges have significantly disrupted livelihoods, agricultural activities, and access to food, particularly for internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host communities in these regions in Nigeria. Recent assessments, including Food Security Outcome Monitoring (FSOM), indicate that over 65% of households in these areas are adopting negative coping strategies such as reducing food portions or skipping meals entirely. The lean season has further strained already fragile household food supplies, while flooding has destroyed farmlands, reduced crop yields, and worsened food availability. Inflation and market volatility have made staple foods increasingly unaffordable to the poor, further limiting access for vulnerable populations. The collapse of agricultural systems due to prolonged conflict and displacement has intensified dependence on external food assistance. Many households lack the means to cultivate crops or engage in income-generating activities, leaving them vulnerable and unable to secure their basic food needs. This project seeks to address these pressing food security challenges by delivering targeted food assistance to the most vulnerable populations, including IDPs, host communities, and households severely affected by the lean season and natural disasters. The intervention will ensure immediate access to food, reduce reliance on harmful coping mechanisms, and contribute to stabilizing food security in the targeted LGAs.

2 - Activities

Activities Focused on Food SecurityThe project aims to address critical food insecurity in Borno, Zamfara, Sokoto, Katsina, Yobe, and Adamawa States through targeted interventions focusing on immediate relief and building resilience among vulnerable populations, including IDPs, host communities, and disaster-affected households. 1. Emergency Food Assistance DistributionDescription: Provide in-kind food assistance to 20,000 households (100,000 individuals) to meet immediate needs and prevent acute hunger.• Implementation: Distribution of pre-packaged staple foods (e.g., rice, beans, maize, and oil) through local partners at established centers, prioritizing highly vulnerable households.• Modality: In-kind food baskets; cash-based transfers considered in accessible markets. 2. Cash-Based Transfers for Food PurchaseDescription: Deliver cash transfers to 5,000 households in areas with functional markets, enabling beneficiaries to purchase food locally and stimulate the economy.• Implementation: Transfers via mobile money platforms or vouchers for three months, supporting households based on food security assessments.• Modality: Direct cash or vouchers, monitored to ensure spending meets food needs. 3. Provision of Seeds and Tools for Post-Harvest SupportDescription: Equip 2,500 households with drought-resistant seeds and agricultural tools to promote food production and reduce reliance on aid.• Implementation: Distribution of maize, millet, and bean seeds alongside basic tools (e.g., hoes, rakes) during planting season, supported by agricultural extension services.• Modality: Training on crop management and soil conservation for sustainable agriculture. 4. Nutritional Support and Awareness CampaignsDescription: Conduct awareness campaigns targeting 15,000 individuals, focusing on improved nutrition and food utilization practices, particularly for pregnant women and children under five.• Implementation: Community-based sessions in partnership with health workers, covering balanced diets, breastfeeding, and food preservation.• Modality: Delivered through community groups with visual and audio aids. 5. MEAL Framework for Food Security InterventionsMonitoring:• Routine tracking of food distribution, market prices, and post-distribution satisfaction using digital tools.Evaluation:• Baseline and endline surveys to assess food security indicators like Food Consumption Score (FCS) and Household Dietary Diversity Score (HDDS).Accountability:• Feedback mechanisms, community meetings, and monthly analysis of complaints to improve service delivery.Learning:• Periodic reviews to document lessons learned, adaptive management, and stakeholder reporting through visual reports and workshops. MEAL Indicators• Output: Households receiving food assistance or cash transfers.• Outcome: Improved FCS and reduced negative coping strategies.• Impact: Reduction in moderate and severe food insecurity prevalence.This integrated approach ensures the delivery of immediate food relief while fostering resilience and sustainability through community-centered initiatives, capacity building, and evidence-based learning.

The Humanitarian-Development-Peace (HDP) package aims to address the immediate needs of vulnerable populations while fostering sustainable development and promoting peacebuilding in conflict-affected areas. The package integrates three components:1. Humanitarian Assistance – Thus, provides life-saving support, including food aid, healthcare, and shelter, to address urgent needs.2. Development Initiatives – strengthens local systems and builds resilience through livelihood programs, education, and capacity-building efforts.3. Peacebuilding Activities - Focuses on conflict resolution, social cohesion, and governance to address the root causes of insecurity.Key Actors:The HDP package involves collaboration between international humanitarian organizations, development agencies, local governments, and community-based organizations. Key stakeholders include the UN agencies such as WFP and IOM, INGOs such as Plan International, local NGOs, and host governments, supported by donor institutions.Interaction with the Project:Our food security project aligns with the HDP package by addressing critical humanitarian needs while contributing to long-term development goals. Through activities such as emergency food assistance, cash-based transfers, and agricultural support, the project bridges short-term relief with recovery and resilience-building. Additionally, our collaboration with local actors and community leaders supports peacebuilding by promoting equitable access to resources and fostering social cohesion.Through integrating with HDP actors and leveraging their expertise, the project ensures coordinated and sustainable outcomes that address immediate needs and lay the foundation for long-term stability.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Our project is designed to ensure protection mainstreaming by embedding inclusivity, conflict sensitivity, and safeguarding mechanisms throughout its implementation.Inclusive Consultations: Marginalized groups, including people with disabilities, women, girls, and youth, will be actively engaged in consultations at all project phases. This ensures that their perspectives inform decision-making processes and project design.Gender and Youth Inclusion: Women, girls, and youth will participate in planning, implementation, and monitoring activities through community meetings, focus groups, and decision-making committees. Their inclusion ensures that interventions address specific needs and enhance empowerment.Conflict Sensitivity: The project will assess the potential impact of humanitarian assistance on peaceful coexistence and take measures to mitigate tensions. Transparent beneficiary selection criteria, equitable distribution, and community engagement will be prioritized to prevent resource-based conflicts.Safeguarding and PSEA Mechanisms: A Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) communication strategy will be implemented, alongside accessible complaints mechanisms such as toll-free hotlines, helpdesks, and suggestion boxes. These channels will ensure accountability and provide a safe space for beneficiaries to report concerns.With these modalities, we will ensure the protection and dignity of all beneficiaries, aligning the project with humanitarian principles and best practices.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will ensure inclusive and systematic feedback collection from the most vulnerable community members through the following approaches:a) Dedicated Feedback Channels: Establish multiple, accessible feedback mechanisms, including toll-free hotlines, community helpdesks, and suggestion boxes strategically placed in communal areas. These will be designed to accommodate people with disabilities and non-literate beneficiaries through audio and verbal reporting options.b) Community Outreach and Engagement: Conduct regular community meetings and focus group discussions with marginalized groups, such as persons with disabilities, women, girls, and youth. This allows for direct engagement and tailored feedback collection.c) Mobile and Door-to-Door Surveys: Use trained field staff to conduct mobile and household-level surveys targeting vulnerable populations who may face mobility challenges or social barriers to participation in public forums.d) Integration with Existing Structures: Collaborate with Lindii Peace Foundation’s community networks and volunteers, leveraging their local knowledge to identify and engage the most vulnerable individuals effectively.e) Confidential Reporting Options: Provide confidential reporting channels to ensure that individuals can share sensitive feedback without fear of stigma or reprisal.f) Regular Monitoring and Adaptation: Feedback will be reviewed regularly to identify trends and inform adaptive management, ensuring the project remains responsive to the needs of the most vulnerable groups.
