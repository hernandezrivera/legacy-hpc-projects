Project Summary

According to OCHA, approximately 393,000 people across 26 districts are in need of GBV services in 2022, a significant increase from 67,000 people in need in the 2021 ERP. Around a quarter of Lebanese households reported having safety and security concerns for women in their community (OCHA, 2022). The GBVIMS shows that in the last quarter of 2022, the most common types of GBV incidents were physical assault and emotional abuse mostly happening in the context of intimate partner violence, followed by forced marriage. Protection risks for all targeted populations remains high, with women and girls facing significant risks of multiple forms of GBV. The economic insecurity combined with different levels of crisis affecting the country as well as risks related to legal status, add to these vulnerabilities. Shortage in electricity and increased transportation and telecom prices all act as contributing factors to different forms of GBV in all locations. Sexual harassment through online platform and cyber abuse is an emerging GBV trend. Another alarming trend is the increased rates of sexual exploitation by landlords and employers especially affecting female heads of households with financial vulnerability. Preventing, mitigating, and responding to GBV in emergencies is a lifesaving priority and an essential component of humanitarian action, especially in a country with extreme structural gender inequalities.
The below proposed activities focus on survivor-centered care with case management teams working to meet the immediate, lifesaving needs of women and girls while laying the groundwork for survivors of GBV, their families and their communities to recover. Under the GBV prevention component, the project will be providing dignity kits to 3000 beneficiaries and awareness raising sessions to 2500 non-unique beneficiaries. There will be an overlap of beneficiaries attending awareness sessions and receiving dignity kits. Also, 120 men will receive men engagement activities (Cycle of Violence), and those are already counted under the awareness sessions target. The COV is composed of three sessions including: Life and daily stressors, Violence and How to deal with stressors.
Aim of the sessions:
During the dire economic situation in the country, the team will look at the effects of the family on this period, and its goal is the ability to mitigate its effects on both, society and family.
In the three sessions, the team will discuss together with the men about the stress and frustrations of the situation as well its effects on the family members and their reactions towards it in a positive or negative way.
Under the GBV response component, IMC will be providing focused PSS groups for 300 beneficiaries as well as GBV case management (including emergency cash assistance). IMC will also provide non-focused PSS for 500 unique beneficiaries (which overlap with the focused PSS activities as well as the dignity kits beneficiaries). IMC will provide the services in three safe spaces in Akkar, Beirut and South. GBV department is well established in these areas with well-trained case workers and social workers.
Based on IMC’s presence in the different areas proposed under this project, the objectives of the ERP directly address the needs of the communities especially vulnerable women and girls. IMC will organize empowerment activities for women and girls and will engage with men as allies to prevent GBV. IMC will coordinate with local actors, authorities, and other sectors to strengthen GBV prevention and response and will continue working with communities to shift social norms that contribute to GBV and threaten the protection and wellbeing of women and children.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Specific measures and interventions to mitigate, respond to and prevent GBV must be undertaken from the onset of a crisis and continue through transition and recovery efforts. This intervention will also support in the following: - Ensure that quality case management services continue to be available. - Strengthen community protection mechanisms to ensure that vulnerable populations, especially persons that identify as LGBTIQ+, persons with disabilities, women head of households and elderly have access to required services, such as health care, and case management. - Strengthen the collaboration with the Mental Health and Psychosocial Support sector for the availability of mental health and psychosocial services to all persons. - Foster collaboration and promote joint initiatives with Child Protection actors to mitigate the risks of GBV against children including adolescent girls/boys and in particular against sexual abuse and exploitation. - Evaluate the effectiveness of access to remote activities for women, girls and marginalized groups and develop a plan to improve inclusiveness of remote activities. - Ensure the distribution of dignity kits for vulnerable women and girls. - Provide emergency cash assistance to reduce the risks of protection issues among vulnerable women.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

IMC operates using best practices of safeguarding and protection mainstreaming. All GBV activities are implemented while taking into account principles of safety and do no harm, meaningful access including to minorities and people with disability, participation of community members in project design and implementation (through existing women committees and outreach volunteers from the community), and accountability through established strong complaints and feedback mechanisms. IMC Lebanon has a built-in solid PSEA framework throughout all its operations. Staff are trained on PSEA and are aware of internal reporting mechanisms and dedicated PSEA trained focal points are mobilized across the different project locations. Through its existing projects, IMC monitors trends of protection and GBV risks by collecting feedback from program beneficiaries, community volunteers and community-based actors and adapts its project focus accordingly.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

IMC Lebanon conducts needs assessments to identify areas of interventions where affected communities are critically in need. In addition to key informant interviews, Lebanon team conducts focus groups discussions with the affected communities in order to identify beneficiaries’ needs and areas of intervention. IMC Lebanon employs mechanisms and approaches to ensure the participation of the affected communities and enable them to play a role in taking decisions affecting their lives. In order to make sure that beneficiaries’ feedback is received on a regular basis, IMC Lebanon conducts yearly patient satisfaction surveys and post-distribution monitoring. IMC Lebanon implements a Community Based Feedback and Response Mechanism, a set of clear, transparent procedures that provide affected communities with access to safe, timely and confidential means of voicing complaints related to supported services and activities.
