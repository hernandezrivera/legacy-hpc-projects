Project Summary

The Project aims to alleviate the suffering of internally displaced persons (IDPs) and communities affected by the crisis in the Northwest (NW) and Southwest (SW) Regions of Cameroon, through and integrated response, providing food, water, sanitation and hygiene, protection, shelter, nonfood items assistance and efficient humanitarian coordination. The proposed integrated approach will respond to the emergency needs of displacement affected populations who have seen their livelihood affected, face challenges accessing water, sanitation and hygiene, and lack a safe and secure shelter. Contextually, the goal aligns with strategic objective 1 of Cameroon’s Humanitarian response plan (HRP) that seeks to Reduce the mortality and morbidity of people faced with immediate humanitarian consequences by responding to their short-term needs for Food Security, Water Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), Shelter and Non-Food Items (NFI). It equally aligns with strategic objective 2 of the HRP that seeks to respond to the fundamental rights of people affected by crisis, including those lacking legal and civil documentation, ensuring their protection through access to legal and civil documentation will contribute to the protection of crisis-affected populations in the NW and SW.
The initiative builds on the theory of change that ‘ If internally displaced persons and host communities are provided with urgently needed food assistance, and if their water, sanitation hygiene and shelter needs are met, and their protection needs improved through access to legal and civil documentation, and if effective coordination is assured among humanitarian actors, then vulnerable populations will be able cover their basic needs and better recover from crisis and shocks;.
The project is structured to achieve the following purposes:
Purpose #1 Improve the food security situation of IDP populations;
Purpose #2 Enhancing legal protection and improving access to information, counselling and legal services for people affected by displacement;
Purpose #3 Reduce risk of waterborne diseases for displacement-affected persons;
Purpose #4 Shelter and Settlements: Improve living conditions, dignity, and physical protection needs of the most vulnerable displacement affected people;
Purpose #5 Coordination: Strengthen coordination amongst INGO members and with other stakeholders (Government, UN agencies, donors, national NGOs, diplomatic community) and improve joint-advocacy so that crises-affected populations in Cameroon can effectively access quality humanitarian assistance.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The humanitarian situation in the Northwest and Soutwest regions remains concerning with increased displacements and protection challenges, due recurrent violence against civilians and clashes between non-State armed groups (NSAGs) and State Security Forces (SSFs). The longstanding crisis has left 1.8million people in need of humanitarian assistance including over 583,112 internally displaced persons and over 459,605 returnees in 2024 (September 2024 situation report –OCHA). 2024 humanitarian needs overview depicted that protection, water sanitation and hygiene and protection were among the most pressing humanitarian needs. According to the IOM multisector Needs assessment conducted on September 2024, access to food security and WASH equally stood as the main humanitarian needs for displacement affected populations. In the NWSW regions, 931,768 individuals were reported food insecure according to IPC analysis dated March 2024 and 54% of this food insecure population were recorded in the Northwest region. The protection cluster strategy identified 607,000 persons in need of physical and legal protection interventions in the NWSW in 2024. This includes persons in need of decent housing, land, and property (HLP) and legal and civil documentation (LCD). Most IDPs and hosts continue to face difficulties trying to access land for their shelter, settlement and agriculture for food security. They lack information on procedures for accessing land, face discrimination and other social and economic challenges WASH and Shelter needs equally remain persistent in the context due to major structural damages on houses including roofs (42%) and walls (28%). IDPs and returnees unfortunately have no option but to live in these damaged houses, due to lack of resources to reconstruct the shelters. The influx of IDPs in some localities equally strain available water points in host communities and they are usually far away from the shelter units (40%) or not being enough (60%). In response to the abovementioned needs, NRC will provide a multisector response covering the WASH, Shelter, food security and protection needs of displacement affected populations, through the 12-months integrated response project.
