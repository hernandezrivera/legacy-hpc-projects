Project Summary

Like in other parts of the country, the farming techniques used by most farmers in Tigrai are labor based and the dominant farming system is crop-livestock mixed farming. Crop production in Tigrai is almost entirely dependent on rainfall. Although there is considerable potential for irrigation development, the existing experience on irrigation is very limited. Since the middle of 2000 the share of irrigation has been increasing and multiple sources of water for irrigation like micro earthen dams, small river diversions and lift irrigation. The average land holding in Tigrai is about 0.75 hectares per household (BoPED), 2018). The major crops grown in Tigrai include barley, wheat, finger millet, maize, chickpeas and horse bean. Average yields of the major crops are estimated to be varying between six and fifteen quintals per hectare.
Livestock husbandry is the main integral part of the farming system in Tigrai. In rural areas, livestock serve as a source of draught power, cash income, and food supply to farming households, animal dung for fuel and land fertilization and serve to transport goods and people. A study by the Bureau of Agriculture and Rural Development (BoARD) indicated that in 2019, there were about 4.85 million cattle, 24 million sheep and goats, 0.32 million equine, 3.66 million chickens and 0.26 million beehives in the region.
Even though detail assessments on the aftermath of the ongoing war in Tigrai is required, according to the rapid assessments conducted by BoARD indicates that most farmers have lost their livestock assets mainly draught oxen, small ruminants and poultry either slaughtered and/or looted by different factions who invaded the entire region. Similar reports demonstrating that many households have lost their basic seed assets (both cereals and vegetable seeds) because of the war that aggravates scarcity of vegetable seeds and largely affect vegetable production in the region in the coming years unless immediate remedies are designed and implemented as quick as possible.
Given the agricultural extension services in Tigrai are totally collapsed coupled with no supply of improved seeds from external sources, the gap in demand and supply of vegetable seeds remain critical. On the other hand, motivations of farmers to restart irrigation practice and production of vegetable crops is found to be significant attracted by good price for vegetable products like tomato and onion in local markets. Moreover, large portion of IDPs have returned back to their original places and want to involve in livestock production just like before the war mainly in sheep, goat and poultry production despite their resource limitations. Hence, the possibility of supplying improved agricultural inputs to protect and sustain livelihoods of conflict affected households remains as an assignment to the people and government of Tigrai as well as other development partners. In relation to this, ERDA has taken a leading role and designed a project proposal on the supplies of improved breeds of chickens, sheep and goat as well as certified vegetable seeds to be implemented in the coming 12 months.
Environmental Consideration:
The distribution of agricultural inputs and related feed as well as veterinary services to the beneficiary households by its nature has not major environmental pollution effects. Moreover, the major inputs to be supplied for the beneficiaries HHs are dominantly organic breeds and seeds. These all materials are natural which can’t induce severe environmental distraction, rather by products such as manure, urine and biomasses of the farms will serve as organic fertilizer to enhance productivity of the intended farms. On the other hand, some by products like dung and urine could bring a release of unpleasant smell or affect hygiene of the area unless and otherwise they are properly managed and utilized. The rearing of shoats within the beneficiary households will also be implemented.

1. - Projects activities

To protect and sustain core-agricultural livelihoods of climate and conflict affected households in Tigrai through humanitarian interventions in 2022. To address this cluster objective (CO1) ERDA will focus on the following major outcome indicators:Indicator #1 25,000 Households that received animal feed interventions: will improve production and productivity the animals in terms of breed quality and quantity to support the target HHs to improve their livelihood. Therefore, output indicators to measure this outcome indicator will incorporate during the detail project proposal. Some of the expected output Indicators are: # of animals served through the provisioning of feed intervention, volume of feed supplied to the target HHs, # of animals show an improvement due to the supply of feed, and etc. Indicator #2 35,000 of households that received animal health interventions: to protect major animal diseases and to contribute for the improvement of quality and quantity breeds as well as this helps to assure the sustainability of animal production. Similarly, under this outcome indicator there are potential output indicators to be incorporated. Some of them are: # of animals received outreach services for vaccine and treatment, # of veterinary clinics provide health service to the target HHs, # of HHs who have access to veterinary service for animal health intervention and etc. Indicator #3: 40,000 of households that received agricultural inputs: to improve the livelihoods of the war affected people in Tigrai the project is proposed to provide different agricultural inputs and this strengthening by the following output indicators: # of HHs who received shoats for livelihood improvement, # of HHs who received improved chicken breeds and # of HHs who received vegetable seeds, size of hectares of land cultivated for improved onion and tomato, # of HHs who start to generate additional income from the agricultural inputs and supplies. 1. - Projects activities * (Max value: 4000 characters) As the envisaged project is going to focus on protecting and sustain livelihoods of conflict affected households in Tigrai. Its components will mainly emphasize on three major interventions include provision of improved breed chickens, supply of local breed sheep and goat as well as distribution of quality vegetable seeds to be implemented as per the interest of the beneficiary households and potentiality of the project areas. From these project activities more than 40,000 war affected households will be benefited throughout Tigrai with major focus on the highly affected areas. ERDA in collaboration with government sector lines, local administrations, local institutions and local communities will undertake screening of eligible beneficiaries based on transparent and concrete selection criteria then short term technical training will be given according to the types of activity to be involved. In general 25,000 households (15,000 women headed households, 5,000 men, 2,500 youth and 2,500 people with special interests) will receive improved breed chickens throughout Tigrai with 10 chickens each. For this purpose ERDA will establish chicken growing centres to serve as main distribution hubs in Tigrai. Accordingly, ERDA will bring day old chicks either from other parts of Ethiopia or abroad and keep in the growing centres for 45 days before distribution to the beneficiary households. The centre will also supply for three months processed poultry feeds, vaccination and medicines to the beneficiary households. The provision of improved breed chickens will be implemented in all woredas of Tigrai. In each wereda five to six tabias will be selected and it will be implemented in 500 tabias and in each tabia 50 households will be targeted over one year of implementation period. The supply of local breed shoats to 10,000 households who lost their livestock assets will be implemented similarly in all parts of Tigrai in 330 Tabias.

maximum efforts to consult and involve all relevant government and non-government stakeholders in the whole process. In this regard, major stakeholders for the envisioned project are considered five regional sectors such as; Bureau of Agriculture and Rural Development (BoARD), Tigray Agricultural Research Institution (TARI), Agency for Micro and Small Enterprises Promotion (AMSEP), Tigrai Agricultural Marketing Promotion Agency (TAMPA), Bureau of Women Affairs (BoWA) and Micro Finance Institutions (MFI). These sectors will directly involve in the implementation of the proposed project individually as a sector to their respective responsibilities and collectively in an integrated approach, to facilitate the integration among these sectors a steering committee comprises of key personnel from each sector will be established and coordinated by ERDA . In this regard, BoARD will play the leading role with the responsibilities in distributing improved inputs (seed, fertilizer, breed, feed, veterinary services etc.) and provision of technical trainings to the beneficiaries. The bureau through its extension workers located at woreda and tabia levels will undertake close follow up to provide technical supports to the beneficiary households in order to establish and run successful businesses for sustainable rehabilitation of the victims. On the other hand, TARI will play vital role in undertaking of community based researches to address the challenges in supply of improved inputs and disseminate best practices among beneficiary households. TAMPA is working on market promotion of agricultural products of the region. It collects, administers, analyses and disseminates prices information on agricultural products including livestock and livestock products, vegetables, cereals, pulses, oil crops and fruits from markets of woreda capitals throughout the region. The agency also works on promoting of small and micro enterprises as well as private investors involvement in agricultural commodities throughout the region. As the beneficiary households are expected to involve in agricultural related IGAs, the agency is expected to work in the envisaged project through provision of market information and establish sound market value chain system among various actors. Given the overall goal of Bureau of Woman Affairs (BoWA) is to improve the political, socio-economic and socio-cultural situations and participation of women in all spheres of the poverty reduction endeavours undergoing in the region, serves as key stakeholder of the proposed project in supporting of the war affected vulnerable women. More specifically, BoWA will facilitate the rehabilitation of Gender Biased Violence (GBV) and widowed women by providing awareness creation and life skill trainings as well as introduction of Income Generating Activities (IGAs) for sustainable livelihood of the beneficiary households and contribute to the empowerment of women. The bureau will be involved in the identification of potential IGAs and facilitate related trainings in cooperation with other stakeholders in order to speed up the rehabilitations of the war affected women. Microfinance institutions and cooperatives play an important role in providing saving and credit services to the beneficiary households. Dedebit Credit and Saving Institution (DECSI) is one the institutions responsible to provide credit and saving services all over the region through its branches and sub-branches base at wereda capitals and selective Tabias. The beneficiary households will receive starting up capital in kind (agricultural inputs) from the project to reinstate their agricultural activities. The beneficiary households are expected to scale up their farms by receiving additional working capital through credit service to be received from microfinances. DECSI is, therefore, one of the potential and accessible microfinances in the region to provide reliable credit service that would enable the benfs.
