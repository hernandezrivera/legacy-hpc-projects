Project Summary

This HRP project will implement lifesaving nutrition interventions across 4 states and 11 OCHA priority 1,2&3 counties namely Warrap (Twic, Tonj North), Upper Nile (Renk, Fashoda, Panyikang, Melut), Western Equaoria (Tambura, Ezo and Nagero) and Northern Bhar el Ghazal (Aweil East, Aweil West); using CMAM and MICYN approaches and will be integrated into the ongoing Health, Nutrition and WASH programs that World Vision is implementing in those locations. WV will prioritize children under five years of age and PLW as the most vulnerable groups to acute malnutrition due to their increased biological and physiological needs.
The response will integrate nutrition with other clusters to address key drivers of malnutrition: food insecurity, sub-optional childcare and feeding practices, morbidity, and lack of safe water and sanitation. Specific target locations will be within WV program areas with existing projects where high prevalence of acute malnutrition, conflict and displacement co-exist. The project will enable WV to contribute to the nutrition cluster’s target of reaching 1 million children and 800 PLW with emergency nutrition support and collaborate with food and livelihood, health, protection, WASH and Education clusters to provide multi-sectoral interventions. WV will be implementing the project in line with the 2025 cluster objectives.
The project implementation will be done through existing government seconded staff and WV project staff who will Integrate/link all the interventions to the existing government health facilities for sustainability. WV will also ensure that reporting is done through the government systems that include NIS and DHIS2; and will make use of government data collection and reporting tools for sustainability purposes.
To contribute to wasting reduction and prevention of malnutrition, WV will establish kitchen gardens in the community; Expand household nutrient rich food production (Sorghum, sweet potatoes, beans etc). WV will engage its FSL team to explore economic empowerment and transformation through a Savings for Transformation (S4T) model.
The IPC 2025 revealed that up to 6.3 m (47% of the country’s population) persons will be food insecure and all the 6 WV targeted locations are in Phase 4 which is critical and requiring emergency response. WV has established static and mobile nutrition facilities in Warrap (Twic, Tonj North), WVI-SS is currently implementing a nutrition project founded by UNICEF in Twic County. In Upper Nile (Renk, Fashoda, Panyikang), WV has emergency static and mobile facilities that integrate nutrition. In Northern Bhar el Ghazal (Aweil East), World Vision has community-based nutrition program where we partner with the community to manage severe malnutrition by enriching the children’s diet using the locally available foods in Parot Village, In Western Equatoria, WV is implementing the HSTP project in Tambura, Nagero and Ezo addressing both the host and IDP health needs. The project will be aligned with the HSTP of which World Vision is part of for sustainability and health system strengthening.

1. Needs

The malnutrition crisis in South Sudan continues to deteriorate in 2025, driven by multiple, converging humanitarian crises. About 4.3 million children under five, as well as pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, will need life-saving treatment and preventive care. This includes 0.65 million children under five at risk of severe acute malnutrition, 1.4 million at risk of moderate acute malnutrition, and 1.1 million mothers requiring urgent treatment (IPC-AMN 2024). WV is targeting 11 counties whose acute malnutrition rates exceed the emergency threshold of 15% or are affected by natural or manmade disasters.The continuous influx of returnees and refugees continues to pose a big challenge to Health and Nutrition in Renk Transit in Upper Nile. WV established a health facility in Transit Camp 2 in Renk serving 8,402 refugees and returnees and would like to integrate nutrition services. Since July 2023, WV screened 5381 children for malnutrition and 658 (12%) were identified as having malnutrition. 392 PLW screened, 56 (14%) had malnutrition showing a significant need for nutrition services. In NBeG, there are over 22,770 returnees in Aweil West and Aweil East requiring health, WASH, and nutrition services. Currently, WV supports protection activities with support from UNHCR. Additionally, WV provides FSL, nutrition, and shelter through GFFO. The WASH services in terms of water yard and latrine construction were provided but the project ended. In Nov-2024, John Dau Foundation, an organization that was providing health services, approached WVI-SS for a possible collaboration. WVI-SS in provision of health service in Wedwill camp. A recent assessment (Nov, 2024) conducted by WVI-SS among children aged 6-35 months in Parot village revealed an alarming GAM rate of 32% confirming the IPC classification that put Aweil East in Phase 4. Melut County has continued to host IDPs fleeing the floods and the conflict in Manyo.In August 2024 WVI-SS led an initial rapid assessment in Tonga, Panyikang county following floods displacement. Up to 1710 persons were displaced from Kueidab, Abaab, Kuerkan, Chotbura, Tambung and are currently settled in Tonga area. The most pressing needs included health, nutrition, shelter and WASH.In November, floods ravaged Lul, Dethwok and Kodok Rural Payam in Fashoda County, Upper Nile State displacing over 15,816 individuals (2,636 HHs). The IDPs are settled within Kodok town center with health, nutrition and WASH needs. Moreover, there are several returnees from Sudan settling in Kodok town as well.In WES, Tambura, Ezo, and Nagero continue to be fluid following the intercommunal violence that happened in 2022 in Tambura County. The HRP estimates the IDPs as 23,975 for Tambura, 19,103 for Ezo, and 24,866 for Nagero county. Most IDPs returned to their homes with a great number settling within a 15km radius from the nearest health facility. WVI-SS provides health services to the affected communities through the HSTP. On November 24, a shooting of a health worker happened behind Tambura hospital. There are constant fears that conflict could return at any time since the two communities have continued to attack each other.Twic and Tonj North counties have continued to bear the impact of climate change and intercommunal violence. The conflict that happened between Abyei and Twic community has seen 10 IDP camps continue to operate in Twic county. The HRP 2025 gives the Twic IDP population of 52,737 and returnees 7,242. WVI-SS operates emergency nutrition shelters in Twic and has since reached 16,227 malnourished children with nutrition services. WV managed to strengthen and contain nutrition activities in 6 IDP sites in Twic County, also providing treatment to all 3,507 IDP children aged 6-59 months with, PLW-3,507 SAM cases without medical complications, 12,720 with MAM and 11,345 pregnant and lactating mothers with MAM. The 6 OTP/TSFP shelters were earlier constructed for service delivery through SSHF funding.

2. Project activities

To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents, and women in prioritized locations, World Vision will:• Provide BSFP to children aged 6-23 months.• Provide BSFP to Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women.• Conduct cooking demonstrations.• Provide Cash Assistance to breastfeeding Women and Girls.• Provide Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) Counseling to primary caregivers of children aged 0-23 months.• Provide MIYCN counseling services to Pregnant Women and Girls who received • Establish mother-to-mother support groups • Traim CNVs/BHWs on MIYCN/prevention • Train health workers trained on MIYCN/Prevention of malnutrition• Provide cash assistance to households with children under two years receiving cash assistance • Facilitate round 1 &2 national Vit A supplementation to children aged 6-59 month.• Facilitate national round 1 and 2 national deworming for children aged 12-59 months. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PBW in prioritized locations by the end of 2024, WV will: • Screen children aged 6-59 months for acute malnutrition• Admit and treat girls and boys aged 6-59 months with SAM • Provide MAM treatment to girls and boys aged 6-59 months.• Screen PBW for acute malnutrition• Provide treatment to PBWGs with acute malnutrition.• Train PBWGs and caregivers on FMUAC to screen children under five for acute malnutrition.• Conduct quarterly supportive supervision visits to the health/nutrition sites.• Established community feedback mechanism at the nutrition sites. To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2025, WV will: • Compile monthly NIS and 5Ws reports and share with partners and donors.• Establish kitchen demonstration gardens at the nutrition sites.• Integrating Early Childhood Development (ECD) program in the nutrition sites• Train frontline nutrition staff on GBV basics, including how to respond to GBV disclosures safely and appropriately.• Conduct nutrition sites Gender Based Violence (GBV) safety audits at least once a year.• Provide nutrition sites with information on the management of diarrhea.
