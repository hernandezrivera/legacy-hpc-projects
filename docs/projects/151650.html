Project Summary

The project is aimed at reducing WASH-related mortality and morbidity in the targeted regions of South Central Somalia affected by various disasters namely; drought, flooding and/or facing population displacements through increased access to water and sanitation facilities, improved hygiene practices and WASH governance. The project will focus on providing sustained access to safe water and promotion of hygiene and sanitation best practices. The response will be coordinated through the community committees already existing; WASH committees, CCCM committees, local organization of neighborhood groups. DKH will mainstream protection in all the interventions by using a beneficiary selection criteria guiding on vulnerable groups to be selected for support, gender-sensitivity programming, and reducing risks through sensitization in the communities on protection issues. Communities along the Shabelle River are plagued by frequent floods. DKH together with the partner SYPD has prepositioned Emergency Water Kits (EmWatKits) with water filters and chlorination, water bladders and tap-stands in Beletweyn and Jowhar. During the last floods in May/June 2018 the kits were in use and SYPD distributed treated water to more than 20000 flood-related temporarily displaced people mostly in Beletweyn. Amongst them were also protracted and recently displaced IDPs from the B/weyn IDP camps. These EmWatKits remain on stand-by and will be used by DKH in partnership with SYPD during the next floods, too, for temporary access to safe water (the technology is not meant for permanent water supply). Such an intervention also comprises the eventual prepositioning of latrine slabs and the installation of emergency latrines in spontaneously flood-related evolving IDP camps. Target: Total 18,000 [9,180 Male; 8,820 Female]; Boys and girls (11,880) number of affected individuals (men, women, boys and girls).

Needs

Somalia faces a shortage of water resources, sanitation facilities and hygiene education. Inadequate access to safe water supply and appropriate sanitation and hygiene facilities within communities, IDP camps, schools and health facilities are significant contributors to the high rates of disease. 29% of Somalis get water from unimproved water source and an additional 12% surface water, with open defecation practiced by 39% of the population (HRP, 2019). WHO estimates 5.5 million Somalis continue to be at risk of water-borne diseases. Handwashing with soap at critical moments practiced by only a third of the population as access to soap and availability of functional handwashing point is limited. Banadir and Hirshabelle were badly affected by AWD/Cholera (WASH Cluster, 2018). Reducing malnutrition and disease needs to include access to sanitation and safe water services and addressing hygiene behaviour change. In this context, water treatment solutions on household-level could do a great job and emergency latrines in IDP camps need continuous desludging (WASH Cluster 2017). Heavy rains 2018, culminating into floods affected a number of WASH infrastructure, which led to cases of AWD/Cholera and risk factors for an outbreak persist in Hiraan, Middle and Lower Shabelle (WASH Cluster, 2018). Floods often inundate shallow wells and most of water sources along the Shabelle River. Flood response needs to comprise provision of safe drinking water and emergency latrines to temporally displaced populations from the flooded areas. A needs assessment by CPD in Galmudug IDP camps reports 3005 households not having access to safe water, 2746 no access to hygiene kits, 144 shallow wells submerged and residents fetching stagnant water. Several water points are dysfunctional due to overuse forcing IDPs to buy water at prohibitive costs.

Activities

Activities will be implemented with a protective lens to ensure reduced vulnerabilities. Main activities: A1.1: Promote HHWT solutions for safe water in IDP camps and communities using surface water sources through stationary and mobile water filters distribution of aqua tabs during acute crisis to 2,384 HHs in Beletweyn & Jowhar. A1.2: Contract water kiosks already supplying water in the localities to supply water during periods of water shortage due to crisis based on restricted water vouchers, targeting 18,000 people. Water distribution based on WASH Cluster standards in emergencies. A1.3: Pre-positioning of EmWatKits in Beletweyn and Jowhar at Shabelle River to produce and distribute safe drinking water during floods to 18000 flood-affected displaced and non-displaced people in riverine communities. A1.4: Prepositioning of emergency latrine slabs installed with super structure in flood related displacement sites, targeting 14,303 people. A1.5: Provide emergency hygiene kits to 2,384 HHs affected during disasters. A1.5: Disaster preparedness through protection of 12 shallow wells along the banks of the Shabelle river and decontamination of shallow wells after flooding. A2.1: Support camp clean-ups campaigns for disposal of solid waste that improve the sanitary conditions in the IDP settlement through CfW with identified IDP women. A2.2: Desludging of the existing emergency latrines through CfW. A3.1: Rehabilitate 6 existing community operated boreholes and 12 shallow wells in accordance with the cluster standards. These will also include water infrastructure in common utilities in the IDP settlements and host communities. A3.2. Support construction of 200 gender sensitive-latrines in the affected IDP settlements & host communities A3.3: Formation and training of 18 WMCs for the rehabilitated water points on operation, management & cost recovery. A3.4: Conduct Hygiene promotion reaching 18,000 people. DKH will perform a PDM to ensure quality after distribution.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project is designed to embed sustainability and ensure resilience of the affected communities to the shocks they are faced with. The project will rehabilitate existing boreholes and shallow wells in the target localities and train respective water point management committees on operation, management and cost-recovery for maintenance of the rehabilitated facilities. The rehabilitated shallow wells will also be protected from floods to ensure access to safe water by the affected populations. The response efforts will be coordinated through a community-centred approach. The community committees will participate in the mobilization efforts and identification of the most vulnerable beneficiaries, facilitating ownership and hence sustainability. Durable solutions will further be enhanced through hygiene promotion among the affected populations, addressing key practices that are leading to public health risk. The project interventions are also designed to ensure governance of community assets is done by trained committees, and this will enhance transformative capacities of the affected population. The cash for work interventions of the project will seek to diversify the livelihood opportunities for the participating households, and this will ensure their resilience to shocks affecting them. The project will also be implemented in an integrated manner and the dykes built along river banks through FSL interventions will build help reduce the effects of floods in the subsequent seasons. The hygiene promotion campaigns and sanitation efforts will also be linked to the local authorities and ministry of health efforts.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The risks of exclusion based on displacement are:1. Inadequate access to services 2. Being a minority in a settlement 3. Risk of eviction4. Risk of not being selectedDKH will mainstream protection in all the interventions by using a beneficiary selection criteria guiding on vulnerable groups to be selected for support and reducing risks through sensitization in the communities on protection issues. This will ensure that the beneficiaries are selected without discrimination on the IDPs. Vulnerabilities brought about by being minority will clearly be taken into consideration during implementation. Employment opportunities through the cash schemes will also be made open to every vulnerable groups in the settlement, determined by the selection criteria. On the other hand, DKH is a member of the Somalia CCCM cluster and will ensure that all the IDP residents access services through coordination and complementarity. Through CCCM cluster, DKH will ensure that the displaced populations have access to information on the services available in their places of residence, in addition to coordinating with other service providers to reach out to those deserving cases to be reached with services in the various camps. DKH will ensure that the IDPs are given dignified treatment even during evictions through advocating for the IDPs to be allocated longer land tenure, provision of enough notice prior to eviction. All these efforts will ensure that displaced populations are not discriminated against in any way. There will be a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) in place to gather feedback from beneficiaries on their experiences with the project interventions, reporting any incidence of exclusions and this will be dealt with accordingly.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The risks associated with Gender and age are;1.Gender-based Violence, including attacks at night while accessing the WASH facilities2.Early marriage for girls3.Risk of recruitment into extremist groups for boys4.Risks of school-drop-out for schools-going-age children5.Exclusion from services for the female- and child-headed families, and the oldDKH has designed this project, ensuring that the needs of all affected populations are taken into consideration, regardless of gender and age. In water access, DKH will ensure that rehabilitated water infrastructure consider protection aspects; distance covered by the womenfolk is reduced. Water points that are closer to the settlements will be rehabilitated to reduce the risk on the women & girls being attacked while going to fetch water. This will also allow more women to be involved in other IGAs since they use less time fetching water. All sanitation activities will be gender-sensitive, ensuring segregated units for women & men. DKH will also ensure the gender segregated VIP latrines are constructed closer to the settlements, with a lockable latch from inside. The project will ensure that women are selected for the soft CfW schemes targeting ensuring environmental sanity through garbage collection. Further, as a member of the CCCM cluster, coordination to ensure all the vulnerable groups are protected will be guaranteed. The interventions will be implemented from a rights-based approach i.e. the target populations in need have rights to services, and they have the right to a standard of living based on the Sphere standards. To ensure the dignity of the target populations are upheld and respected. Availing facilities in schools will also promote access to schools and enable the boys and girls to access education. There will be a Complaints Response Mechanism in place to gather feedback from beneficiaries on their experience with the project, reporting any incidence of exclusion and this will be dealt with accordingly

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Conflict and violence has taken a toll on the project plan target populations and the design of this project consider reduction and mitigation altogether on the effects of conflict and violence. The project aims to build the capacities of the affected communities on Natural resource management. This will entail training on management of land, water and pasture resources, which are majorly the source of conflicts in Somalia. Restoration of community assets and diversification of livelihood sources will also reduce conflicts emanating from competition on scarce resources. In collaboration with other stakeholders, DKH/Partner will link the communities with partners who would be able to provide peacebuilding and contextualized management; in addition to psychosocial counselling to those who have been devastated by armed violence and conflict. Women particularly, will be used in most instances to play their traditional role of peacebuilding among the Somalia community. DKH/Partner will also seek to work with the local authorities, including community and religious leaders with the aim of enhancing harmonious coexistence among the target communities. It is also believed that targeting the youth for training on vocational skills to be self-reliant economically will also reduce the risk of their recruitment into the extremist groups. DKH/Partner envision that from the design of the project and rights-based implementation approaches embedded into it, the impact of armed conflict and violence will be reduced.
