Project Summary

Sudan now hosts over 1 million refugees across the country. Refugees are highly vulnerable and need multi-sectoral interventions to address their vulnerabilities and needs while in asylum, and to maintain and fulfil their rights as refugees under the 1951 Refugee Convention. This project will respond to the life-saving, life-sustaining and protection needs of refugee populations across Sudan. This project will provide protection services especially to children, as well as lifesaving health, nutrition and WASH services to refugee children, their caregivers and communities. In addition, children will be supported to access basic education, providing them with a safe space to learn and to build a better future.
In South Kordofan, West Kordofan and Blue Nile, South Sudanese refugees will be the key project beneficiaries (of Protection, Health & Nutrition, WASH and Education interventions). In North and Central Darfur, refugees from Chad and Central African Republic will be the primary project beneficiaries (of Protection, Health & Nutrition, and WASH interventions). Finally, in Eastern Sudan, especially Gedaref and Blue Nile (Wad El Mahi locality), newly-arrived refugees from Ethiopia, caused by armed actions in Tigray from November 2020, will be assisted through emergency interventions (in the sectors of Protection, Health & Nutrition and Education).
The project will seek to support durable solutions by providing services both to refugees and host communities, in order to reduce discrepancies in service provision, and to support social cohesion. Wherever possible, efforts will be made to strengthen existing public service systems to enhance refugees’ access to basic services in and out-of-camps, rather than to create unsustainable parallel service systems, and to integrate refugees into national systems. The project aims to raise the living standards for refugees living in both camp and non-camp settings.

1 - Needs

This project will respond to the life-saving, life-sustaining and protection needs of refugee populations across Sudan. Refugees often arrive in critical conditions and in need of urgent lifesaving assistance – most recently this has been witnessed with the influx of Ethiopian refugees into East Sudan. Even once established in the country, over 30% of refugees are living in camps where they rely on assistance provided through humanitarian partners. Many urban refugees live in poorer areas and informal spontaneous settlements alongside other highly vulnerable families. Overall the humanitarian needs are extremely severe across Sudan, and so refugees suffer from the same challenges in access to life-saving and life-sustaining services as do the host communities, whilst often suffering from additional protection risks. Gaps in service provision remain across the sectors. The majority of refugee children remain out of school; water availability and latrine coverage are below standard, with high rates of open defecation reported; access to health services is inconsistent across the response, with supply gaps in basic medicines and lack of staff; there is a high food insecurity, with most refugees unable to afford the local food basket or purchase firewood; and critical malnutrition rates in many camps and settlements. This is compounded by limited access to income opportunities and prevalence of child labour, early marriage and other child protection issues as well as SGBV risks.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Protection: Protection services will include Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR) for the Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC), prevention and response to the needs of child victims of Sexual and Gender Based violence (SGBV), vocational and life skills training and other specialised support for extremely vulnerable children and adolescents. Psychological first aid and psychosocial support services will be provided. In Eastern Sudan, for Ethiopian Refugee children, Child Friendly Spaces will be provided. To prevent and mitigate child protection risks, the project will strengthen the capacity of social workers, teachers, law-enforcement staff. The project will support community based child protection mechanisms to disseminate information on relevant services and contribute to advocacy and the referral of child protection cases. Health: Support will be provided to health facilities to assist the treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases; ensure the availability of essential medicines; provide MIPS for RH to support reproductive, neonatal, and child health; strengthen the expanded program of immunization (EPI); prevent infections; undertake health education; and offer mental- psychosocial support in addition to supporting referral to secondary care services. The project will contribute to strengthening the surveillance system and respond to disease outbreaks. The COVID-19 pandemic response will include PPE provision and Risk Communication & Community Engagement (RCCE) as well as considering adaptation on program operation. In Eastern Sudan, in response to the Ethiopian refugee influx, teams will be mobile and offer initial screening at transit and reception points. The project will contribute to building the capacity of health care workers. Nutrition Life-saving nutrition services are targeted at acutely malnourished children (6-59 months of age) and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs). Key activities will include monthly screening for acute malnutrition; establishment of referral mechanisms between the community and the Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) sites, Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP), and Stabilization/Inpatient Care (S/IC). The project will also support the management of moderate acute malnutrition through TSFP services, and the implementation of infant and young child feeding in emergencies, behaviour change activities, provision of technical support and mentorship to health workers. WASH: The planned WASH interventions under this project will include provision of safe drinking water for both households and at the institutional level through construction, upgrading and/or rehabilitation of water sources and network, installation of water containers, and water quality monitoring including provision of water purification supplies. the project will target elimination of open defecation practices through construction of household and Institutional latrines, and hygiene promotion activities. Menstrual hygiene management for women and girls will be supported through dignity/hygiene kit provision. The project will contribute to capacity building of WASH community structures through training and strengthening committees, as well as hygiene clubs at schools and child friendly spaces. Education: Interventions will seek to improve access to education through the rehabilitation and construction of learning spaces and facilities; provision of learning materials and uniforms. Enrolment campaigns will be undertaken to increase the demand for education in particular for girls. Training of teachers, headmasters and PTAs will be undertaken. Girls and boys will be provided with psychosocial interventions; life-skills training; school feeding; and information on a range of topics. An early childhood care and development (ECCD) in emergencies intervention will be provided for younger Ethiopian refugee children.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The environment will be considered as an integral component for this project, and through coordination with different actors in the communities, prioritization and awareness-raising amongst practitioners, and effective policy framework, negative impacts can be mitigated against and positive interventions supported. The project will contribute to resilience and a sustainable use of natural resources through involving and encouraging children and local communities to be part of planning mitigation measures and better environmental preservation. The Community Based Child Protection Networks (CBCPNs) will attempt to intervene in other community issues including response to potential land conflict, and will support positive environmental messaging. Waste disposal mechanisms will be adopted to avoid contamination of water sources, disease vectors and odours. Healthcare and other forms of hazardous waste will be disposed of using appropriate methods, safe and hygienic disposal of needles and other medical supplies, safe management of solid and human waste to prevent the spread of communicable diseases. In WASH, programming will include environmental sanitation interventions to limit localised environmental degradation. Material sourcing will be considered across the project, including the relative benefits of local sourcing versus bulk procurement at a national level. The preferred materials for latrine construction will be those which are more long-lasting and sustainable and do not contribute to deforestation and local environmental damage - e.g. steel bars over locally cut down wood and sticks (which can contribute to deforestation and conflict). Where there are existing structures to accommodate facilities, the structure will be considered for use before new construction. The project will support efforts to reduce overall energy use and to use renewable sources of energy. The environmental/climate change impact will be monitored during the implementation of the project through updating the section of environment on Save the Children's CRSA "Child Rights Situation Analysis" and including it in the yearly reporting of project implementation.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project is designed to consider protection issues and concerns for those that have specific needs or higher levels of vulnerability. Protection programming aims specifically to support access to services, increase safety and uphold the dignity of identified vulnerable children and women. Protection mainstreaming is also integrated in the non-protection sector specific activities. To mainstream gender in this project, the IASC Guidelines for integrating Gender-based Violence interventions in Humanitarian Settings are adopted. There will be a reference document for ensuring quality in planning and coordination and staff will be trained on the guidelines. Save the Children will strive to ensure that the proposed interventions will minimize unforeseen or unintended negative effects on the targeted population which could potentially expose them to further physical and psychosocial risks. Gender, age and disability sensitive measures will be adopted, including adapting meeting/service schedules and meeting venues to ensure equitable participation, using single sex discussion groups, and considering ways to support the dignity and privacy of beneficiaries. The integrated protection and education interventions will enhance the capacity of communities through formation and training of community-based structures to understand the protection risks and differential needs of children and other vulnerable people within the communities. Health staff will be trained on first line GBV management and case management referral will be conducted in coordination with Protection and GBV agencies. Latrines will be designed to ensure maximum protection, privacy and dignity for women, and will be located near to dwellings to mitigate exposure to SGBV incidents especially during nights, also the needs of people with disabilities and elderly will be considered during establishing water points and other community facilities such as communal latrines. The project considers aims to be conflict sensitive by analysing the situation, examining how each activity may influence conflict dynamics and working to maximize the positive and minimize the negative impacts of the proposed interventions.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Stakeholders’ participation and accountability to affected people is part of Save the Children's core values and Save the Children is a signatory of the Core Humanitarian Standards. Save the Children has established mechanisms for ensuring accountability to affected populations, for gaining feedback and for responding to complaints including safeguarding and abuse concerns. Save the Children Sudan will ensure SCI AAP guidelines are followed and applied as one of key quality programming measures. SC will provide regular, timely and accessible information to targeted communities about: who we are and the principles we adhere to; project interventions and what SCI intends to deliver; our staff and how we expect our staff to behave (including our Child Safeguarding policy and Code of Conduct); and how communities can be involved. MEAL and project staff ensure information on the projects are shared with beneficiaries and stakeholders to strengthen community participation and enable beneficiaries to provide constructive feedback that will improve performance and quality of implemented interventions, during needs assessment and throughout the intervention period. Information will be communicated in the local language and medium that are easily understood, respectful and culturally appropriate for different members of the targeted communities, especially vulnerable and marginalized groups. Save the Children use different means to share information with and gain feedback from beneficiaries including community meetings/ sessions and focus group discussions with specific groups, IEC materials and mass communication modalities (Posters, Banners, Social Media and Radio Messaging), feedback boxes, and complaints desks. Additionally, Save the Children Sudan has a dedicated accountability number for beneficiaries or their representatives to share their feedback or raise complaints. Complaints may also be received in person directly to staff. All feedback or complaints received through the different accountability mechanisms will be categorized based on SC agreed categories and accordingly shared with relevant staff for resolution. All received cases will be documented on the CO Action Plan Tracker for follow up. Cases on the Action Plan Tracker will not be closed unless feedback is provided to feedback provider or complainant. All feedback from communities is dealt with in confidentiality. Any serious complaints (including child safeguarding concerns, sexual abuse and exploitation concerns and breaches of the code of conduct).will be immediately elevated to the designated focal point (e.g. child safeguarding focal point, HR Manager, Director for Program Development & Quality, Country Director). Save the Children will participate and engage in interagency feedback mechanisms, including AGDM consultations, regular community meetings and camp-based complaints feedback mechanisms.
