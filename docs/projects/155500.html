Project Summary

Vulnerable IDPs and host communities, especially women and youth in the North West, South West, West and Littoral have access to food through direct provision of unconditional cash and agricultural support. The project aims to provide immediate food assistance to save lives and reduce suffering of households unable to access food that meets basic food and nutritional requirements. The project will be implemented in the North West, South West and Littoral regions targeting 4,200 individuals (40% male and 60% female) including IDPs (60%) and vulnerable host communities (40%) affected by the crisis these regions. NRC will pay attention to youth and women affected by displacement. Participation of all beneficiary groups will be key in ensuring ownership of the project. In order to reach people hidden in hard to reach areas, NRC will work with local partner NGOs that have better access to communities.
To achieve the above objectives, NRC will strengthen the resilience and economic security of vulnerable households through the following activities:
To achieve our objectives, NRC will strengthen the resilience and economic security of vulnerable households through:
1. Provision of cash/voucher to most vulnerable people to meet food needs
Despite security concerns in the indicated North West and South West regions, local markets in small and medium business centers are accessible by the IDPs. The cash approach will help ensure that households have unrestricted access to food and additional income that would protect their remaining assets and ensuring that the agricultural inputs are not sold and rather used as intended. This food assistance will also allow vulnerable individuals to build their resilience helping households, especially the youth to avoid adverse coping mechanisms. Cash approach will help ensure that households have unrestricted access to nutritious foods and additional income that would protect their remaining livelihoods assets as well as ensuring that the agricultural inputs, livestock or IGA start-up kits will not be sold and that they will be used as intended. This food assistance will also allow vulnerable individuals to build their resilience helping households not to further deplete their productive assets and avoid further use of negative coping strategies.
2. Reestablishment of agricultural activities
NRC will negotiate with the local communities and where needed secure access to land for targeted IDPs and returnees. Once the access to land secured, NRC will provide small ruminants, seeds, inputs (agricultural tools, fertilizers, etc.) and training. This will improve agricultural production and provide better access to nutrient-rich food. NRC will also promote gardening, which provides households with a source of food or income during the lean season.
3. Provision of improved stoves
NRC will also distribute food handling kits that will allow target households to prepare food in a more effective and environment-friendly way. Improved stoves will save households money on firewood and help curb deforestation. For better community acceptance, those stoves will be made locally. They will have a more efficient use of charcoal, thus reducing the quantity needed and reduce the expenses of households for it.
4.Provision of awareness raising on food hygiene, food diversity, food storage and food preparation
All emergency activities will be also integrated by sessions to raise the awareness of the targeted populations on food hygiene and diversity, food preparation, and food storage. Through this training, beneficiaries are expected to acquire the knowledge and best practices they need to better use the food and improve the results on their nutritional status.

Needs

Overall, 2.6 million people are exposed to food insecurity in Cameroon and 17% of them live in the North West, South West, Western and Littoral. In depth food security assessments are not yet carried out in the areas due to insecurity but it's estimated that about 50% of the total IDPs in these regions are hidden in bushes with little access to food. Food insecurity akso affects host communities in the agricutural production areas where their crops and relevant assets have been lost or distroyed by the armed conflict.The most common livelihood activity among the target population is farming and petty trade but in the current displacement situation and with no sufficient food aid available rely on host communities and remittance from relatives who live in bigger cities such as Douala and Yaounde within Cameroon or abroad. A gender analysis conducted in the Fako division revealed that IDPs women are now the breadwinners of the family in displacement site due to the fact that men has lost their belongings during displacement. However they face issues accessing land as they are not owners due to cultural norms in the region.
