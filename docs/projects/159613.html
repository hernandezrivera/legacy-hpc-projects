Project Summary

According to FSNAU/FEWSNET post GU update indicates, Agricultural and livestock production in riverine, rain-fed, Agropastoral and pastoral area in Somalia have been severely affected due to consecutive unfavorable rainfall seasons and associated impacts. The Agro-pastoral and Riverine farmers in Hiran, Bay, and Bakool, Lower and Middle Shebelle have suffered successive below average and poor harvests for most of the seasons over the past three consecutive years. As per the cluster HNO an estimated 2.1 million Somalis are in Crisis and Emergency (IPC Phases 3 & 4) and face significant consumption gaps through December 2019, an extended hunger season following the poor performance of the 2019 Gu season rainfall. An additional 4.2 million Somalis are in IPC 2 (Stressed) and engage in negative coping strategies to meet their food and non-food needs. In total, 6.3 million Somalis face acute food insecurity through the end of 2019. SFH make this appeal to respond to the looming food security situation and aspire to significantly contribute to reverse the deterioration of the situation further. Lower juba remain a hub of returness and large IDP concentrations. This intervention target the returnees, IDPs, the pastoral dropout and agro-pastoral that have been hit by the successive droughts, floods, conflict and displacements and are in dire need of food assistance. SFH will address the immediate household food insecurity through direct food distribution and food vouchers to 1000 most vulnerable households including children and women headed households in Kismayo and Dobley districts of lower Juba. This Interventions plans to provide seasonally appropriate crop, fishing and Vegetable kits to the reverine farmers in Wirkoy, yontoy, Sanguuni locations of Kismayo district and some locations of Jamame in Lower juba, this will reduce the dependency syndrome on food aid while contributing to increased production at the community level. Besides, to create resilience and prevent sudden shocks, this intervention will contribute to household food security through income generating activities while restoring productive communal assets and infrastructures such as feeder roads, water catchments and water canals. On implementing this project SFH will build on its current programming and use successfully employed methodologies. Well done targeting will be a key step for success and the focus will be on community-based targeting - for villages and community members -to initiate the project. SFH has employed community based targeting for its asset creation projects in other countries. Local authorities, key informants and SFH staff will be involved in the selection of villages that:
1) Are vulnerable
2) Have potential to benefit from the intervention
Once villages are selected, sensitization sessions will be held with Village Relief Committees (VRCs)/Seed fair committees (SFC) to ensure interest, capacity and full understanding of the project objectives, deliverables and benefits.
This VRCs/SFC will assist in the targeting of eligible households. Communities will be urged to ensure that CFW participants include resource poor, but able-bodied community members that need to assure immediate household food security. The beneficiaries will be cross checked by the VRCs, children’s groups, SFH and other key informants to ensure that there is a balance in representation and that participants meet pre-agreed criteria such as:
• Villages considered poor by local authorities
• Villages with no/ few interventions by other NGOs
• Clan balance/minority interests
• Villages with higher levels of vulnerable children in the village as per nutrition data or local authorities
• Villages free from land disputes
• Willingness of the community to collaborate
The project aimed at achieving the following outcomes:
• Immediate food assistance through food vouchers
• Restoration of productive communal assets
• Increased access to emergency livelihood inputs.

Project Activities

The goal of the intervention is to increase the availability of food assistance through vouchers, distributing seasonally appropriate livelihood kits and restoration of communal assets. This intervention also aims to increase the availability of food through agricultural production. The vouchers are the most appropriate in this intervention:-• Market are functional and integrated• Vendors especially women traders have safe physical access to the market in Kismayo• Product are available within acceptable distance• Traders are willing and able participate• Target groups including women has physical access to shops/Market and it is culturally acceptable for them to do so.Selection criteria for crop and vegetable kits/seeds: • Selected households must be farmers or Agro-pastoralists; • Selected households shall have access to a land for planting; • Selected households shall have the capacity to mobilize enough labor for cropping (land preparation, planting, weeding, etc.• Selected households should have no access to crop seeds and tools or have lost them; • Selected households shall commit to attend eventual capacity development programs that will be carried out by SFH.Selection criteria for fishing kits (Twine, monofilament and Hooks): • Selected households must be fisher folk, farmers and/or Agro-pastoralists; • Selected households shall have access to a water source suitable for fishing; • Selected households shall commit to attend eventual capacity development programs that will be carried out by SFHRegistration SFH will form committees that shall participate in the identification of beneficiaries, their registration, and encourage them to be present during the distribution. Sensitization prior to the beneficiary selection is required, with an adequate gender proportion and involving all community leaders. Regular meetings between SFH and community will be organized so as to ratify beneficiary targets and brief stakeholders on the implementation progress. The local authorities in coordination with the community groups and committees will identify and select the beneficiaries from the community; women will be engaged in the registration process. SFH will produce a list of beneficiaries, and identify those that shall receive one or more of the livelihood kits, based on the minimum criteria set below. SFH in coordination will other Humanitarian actors and local authority will:-• Sensitize the community few days before the activities take place, explaining the purpose of the distribution and date and location where distribution will take place; • Establish a complaint and response mechanism for registration and distribution processes; • Document sensitization, crowd sourcing, broadcasts through pictures, complaints boxes videos diagrams or radio.Social inclusion, protection and access are considered in the beneficiary selection.1.1. Identification and registration of 9500 vulnerable beneficiaries1.2. Provision of food aid through vouchers that includes (Oil, rice, sugar and pulses) to 500 most vulnerable households in Kismayo and part of newly liberated Riverine farmers in Jamame including Barsanguni1.3. Distribution of seasonally appropriate emergency crop seed and tools, vegetable and fishing kits for 500 Households. The fishing kits comprised of Twine (2 spools), monofilament (1 coil) and Hooks (2 boxes).1.4. 250 Household will benefit from CFW income through rehabilitation of communal infrastructure (Canals and feeder roads).1.5. Provision of basic instructions/training on agricultural and fishing practices1.6. Monitoring and reporting

Project Indicators

The following standard outcome indicators will be used to measure the project activities and impact:1) Percentage (%) of targeted Households with acceptable food consumption score. The project target >42%.2) Percentage (%) of targeted Households with acceptable dietary diversity score. The project target at least >5 main groups regularly consumed.3) Percentage (%) of targeted Households with acceptable coping strategy index.4) Percentage (%) of people receiving assistance that report complaints or negative feedback that relate to their treatment with dignity. All complaints are regularly monitored and quickly responded to.5) Percentage (%) of the target population who diversify their income-generating activities6) Percentage (%) of the affected population with physical access to functioning markets.Besides the standard food security outcome indicators, the following specific indicators will be used to monitor the project activities.1.1. # of vulnerable beneficiaries targeted and registered1.2. # of Vulnerable Households receiving food aid through vouchers.1.3. # of Farmers receiving emergency livelihood kits (Crop seeds, vegetable and fishing kits).1.4. # of Households benefiting from CFW activities.1.5. # of DRR Trainings conducted Including good Agricultural practices and fall army worm control strategies.1.6. # of Reports compiled and submitted to the donor.

Mitigation Measures

Potential risks that may occur include conflict within the communities that may affect selection of eligible households, and climatic risks that would cause erratic migration or gender violence related incidence. SFH will comply with the IASC Guidelines for Gender-based Violence Interventions and the FSL cluster specific protection guides in Humanitarian programming. For community-related conflicts related to the project, SFH will strive for transparency and accountability to avoid any such events. Beneficiary feedback system will help capture some of these community related risks and resolve them promptly. SFH also has a strong security management system; in the event that inter/intra community security issues prevent direct monitoring, this will be done through community representatives and remote support. If climatic issues cause changes in project implementation, SFH will make every effort to adjust to the needs of the community perhaps with an adjustment to the timing of work.SFH will build on its current programming and use successfully employed methodologies.
