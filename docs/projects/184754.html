Project Summary

Water shortages are reported in parts of Puntland and Somaliland, especially the coastal parts of Bari, Nugaal and Sanaag, with about 500 pastoral families forced to move from their homes in Puntland. Water shortages had led to notable price increases in some areas, given that reliance on purchased water and water trucking began earlier than normal. In January, the price of a drum of water in Bossasso and Garowe was 18 percent and 60 percent above January 2020. Water is increasingly scarce especially in pastoral areas in northern Somalia. Households are relying on water trucking as their primary water source, even in the Juba regions, where water trucking is usually unnecessary. In the North, for example, the cost of water is the highest recorded since April 2019. According to price data collected in rural reference markets in Sool, Sanaag, Bari, and Nugaal in February, the average price of a 20-liter jerrican (SOS 6,375) doubled compared to one year ago and was 23 percent above the five-year average. Most households are obtaining water through loans, adding to already high debt levels. According to WASH cluster rapid assessment report, 50 % of Galkayo IDPs have no access to latrine, the rest use shared communal latrines, Open defecation practice is observed in all assessed IDPs in Galkayo, Most of the latrines used in the assessed IDP sites are public outside latrines which are 46% of the IDP used 2-20 sharing personnel while 33% used more than 50 personnel for sharing outside public latrines, 30% of latrines constructed in Galkayo IDPs are filled and need dislodging, there are no sanitation (latrine) sanitation facilities accessible to people with disabilities in all Galkayo IDP, 85% (6,320 HHs) of the total IDP do not have access to soap and do not use it as well, 50% of IDPs indicated they did not have access to hygiene education, Only 35% of IDPs in Galkayo IDPs practice proper hand washing with soap and there are no hand washing facilities seen in the IDP and only 4 out of the 20 IDPs in Galkayo have garbage disposal site and there is no system for solid waste collection or management in the IDPs. To help resolve the above needs, KAALO will undertake the following activities;
1. Provide safe water to 7,500 (1837men, 2273women, 1530boys and 1860girls) drought affected pastoralists through water voucher system, which they can redeem water at a specified water vendors (45liters/hh) for a period of 30 days.
2. Rehabilitate 9 berkads which includes include de-silting, repairing the cracked parts of the walls, floors and roofs.
3. Purchase and install 5 fiber water tanks (each 10m3 capacity) in five IDP camps and supply water through piping system to reduce the burden of women and girls. Activity
4. Train 14 WASH committees composing of 98 members sharing equally men and women on better water resource management & maintenance, HHWT use, O&M and collection of water revenues for the sustainability of the water sources. As cross cutting, held one-day workshop with 40 community members with equal participation of men and women (20men and 20women) on Disaster and Risk Reduction (DRR) and conflict resolution mechanisms to avoid the outbreak of AWD/cholera diseases and conflict caused by the drought respectively.
5. Construct 12 gender sensitive institutional twin block latrines in 8 schools and 4 in health facilities (each facility will have 1 twin block latrine for female and 1 for male) with lockable doors, septic tanks for desludging purposes when filled and hand washing facilities to encourage washing hands with soap practices.
6. Desludge filled 40 gender sensitive IDP latrines. The action includes: desludging the filled septic tanks, repairing the broken parts.
7. Provision of hygiene kits to 2000 vulnerable IDPs

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

KAALO will work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from the use of or lack of WASH facilities. KAALO will include women, men, boys, and girls including people with disabilities and minorities in all the consultation and decision-making meetings at the inception of the project, and during the project implementation. In the WASH committees, well operators, and community hygiene promotions, men and women will share equally. All the WASH services will be accessible to men, women, and children including people with disabilities and minorities. KAALO will locate water sources visible, central locations, and not more than 500 meters from settlement and install lights near communal WASH facilities which significantly increase the security of women and girls fetching the water. Involve women and girls insisting locations for communal WASH facilities considering their security and safety and ensure the location and access routes to WASH facilities and distribution points are safe. At the same time, latrines will be lockable and the location of the new latrines is discussed with the women and girls to increase security in regards to the risk of GBV.
