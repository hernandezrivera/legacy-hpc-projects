Project Summary

This program will target the affected population within NWS in both Aleppo and Idleb, including widows' camps through CP services within CFs, and mobile teams. which MF has full access in widows' camps through GBV program, The project will target (Women: 4000, Girls: 5500, Boys: 9500, Men: 4000), including the elderly and people with disabilities.
There is a huge need for protection services, especially CP in the targeted areas, and there is currently a large gap in the coverage of the CFS due to many reasons related to funds and lack of capacities, and most of the established centers are suffering from the fund cuts, MF will target Idleb city - Harim (Atma, Aqrabat, Qah, Deir Hassan, Mashhad Ruhin in DANA and in the north of Aleppo ( Azaz, Al Bab)
and extend to offer CP services via mobile teams and CFSs
This project will help these high-priority locations by expanding and improving child protection (CP) services.
The project will be conducted in stages: • working with the camp administration to obtain a location for the project activities while keeping confidentiality and safety in mind.
• MF will guarantee that all key stakeholders are included at all phases of the operations management cycle.
• In each camp, an elected committee of BNFs, including PWDs and old women, will engage in decision-making to decide the identity of the place and the sort of activities.
• MF will pick project employees to fit the BNFs types while taking into account the culture and situation on the ground, attempting to balance male and female staff as much as feasible. The project's activities will be implemented by mobile teams in each location, with each team consisting of a case manager (CP) who will provide CM both in-person and remotely, a PSS worker and female facilitator for each camp, and another male facilitator targeting men and boys as well, and local authorities.
•Establish friendly spaces for boys and girls while taking gender needs and capacities into account. 
• Providing secure locations in specific camps for interviews, intake, and case management for highly vulnerable boys and girls.
•Then Conduct training for MF field staff who are working on this project on (PFA&PSS), CPIE, referrals pathways, and case management
• Enhanced awareness and organize recreational activities in friendly spaces, ensuring that they are age-appropriate and accessible for beneficiaries
• providing case management and referrals (using 4Ws, CM reporting tool) for vulnerabilities among girls and boys
• Through a parenting curriculum, parents will learn how to deal with their children during a crisis. • All project activities, including (PWD, Men, women, girls, boy, and elders)
• Through mobile teams, for boys, girls, women, the elderly, and those with disabilities.
• The community will be targeted through capacity-building efforts, including humanitarian workers from many sectors, to guarantee greater overall quality and integration of services offered within safe zones. MF will be in charge of the stages of design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation (M&E), and closing. MF will manage the project, offer contractual services, guarantee cluster cooperation, and assure high-quality outcomes and efficient development by providing skilled programming specialist personnel

3 - Needs

According to the HNO 2023, More than 6 million children, including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse, and exploitation, and need critical child protection services. including the possibility of being murdered, injured, recruited, used in conflicts, tortured, incarcerated, abducted, and sexually assaulted. The ongoing hostilities, protracted displacement, and limited access to basic services continue to have a significant effect on the physical, mental and social well-being of children and caregivers. Grave violations against children remain a major concern, Insecurity and economic hardship exacerbate child protection concerns and fuel harmful coping mechanisms which promoted child labor child marriage and family separation, child recruitment. 96% of children live in communities where child labor and psychosocial distress among children are reported. 84% of children live in communities where child marriage is an issue for girls (15 -17 years) and 52 percent of people live in communities where family separation is reported especially in widows' camps. Many alone and separated children, as well as youngsters living with older caregivers or in camps, are at risk. or those living in camps are more vulnerable to violence.

4 - Implementation of activities

MF will establish CFS and mobile teams in the Idlep, Harim, and Aleppo districts, then employ personnel with a gender balance in mind; there will be male and female facilitators who will examine the context and gender distinctions amongst children. In addition, we will hold awareness sessions focusing on reducing the hazards of child labor and encouraging children to return to school, as well as addressing caregivers through parenting skills awareness training. This initiative aims to include protection concepts by assisting vulnerable persons such as women, girls, boys, and men through CP, awareness raising, PSS sessions, and implementing individual protection assistance (IPA) activities through case managementThis project will encourage girls and boys to engage in the CFS and provide them with the opportunity to discuss CP issues in a safe environment. In addition to the reporting procedure with the CP sub-cluster, MF will improve CP staff's capacity to recognize and assist particular vulnerabilities among girls and boys through thorough case management and referrals (4Ws, CM reporting tool). Then it will give training to the local community on core CP and certain associated themes, as well as establish social networks in local authorities to respond to any child protection concerns.MF is committed to providing quality assurance in compliance with the CPMS while implementing this project, and a feedback system with a complaints mechanism will be implemented.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Preventing women, girls, and boys from reaching centers to receive CP services within the center Possibility: Medium Impact: high Mitigation Strategy: Mitigation Measures: Ensuring the provision of CP services for males and family members of the families and couples during the implementation of the project activities and in particular, CP awareness activities in order to mitigate and reduce the risk of preventing the access of beneﬁciaries to CP services Risk: Lack of acceptance of society and the forces controlling the content of the CP activities provided they contain highly sensitive topics Possibility: High Impact: High Mitigation Strategy: The beneﬁciaries and local staff will be consulted about the possibility of implementing the sessions of the CP program and work on structuring the sessions in a contextual and speciﬁc manner when there is a need to address the CP topics indirectly and depends on the resources within the group and supports activities. Risk: Category of beneﬁciaries Risk: Disability of persons with disabilities, older persons, and areas away from CFS. Possibility: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: Mobile teams will be allocated to reach beneﬁciaries who cannot access the CFS to participate in CP activities for people with severe disabilities and elderly people with mobility diﬃculties in remote areas. Beneﬁciaries will be facilitated by providing transportation as needed to ensure their effective participation


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: the populations will be hesitant or unwilling to engage on this topic. Possible increased reporting of SEA with increased awareness about beneficiary rights and reporting mechanisms. Risk: the potential for the very difficult working environment in integrating the SOPs and best practices of PSEA due to the sensitive nature of the topic in the culture, and/or other risks include facing increased reporting of SEA without adequate capacity to respond. Maram foundation team will be conducted PSEA Awareness-raising and information-sharing efforts and scaled up where possible, by respective PSEA focal points supported by the PSEA Networks program and by Maram foundation with the presence inside Syria to communicate information on PSEA. At the least, The following are standards for the affected communities will be informed on: I. The definition of sexual exploitation and abuse. II. The standards of conduct for humanitarian workers. III. Humanitarian workers have a right to humanitarian assistance without being subjected to SEA IV. Where to report SEA incidents (all possible options/channels). V. What services are available, including complaint confidentiality assessments, and how to access them. VI. What to expect after making a complaint, including potential referrals, timeframes, and the roles, responsibilities, and any limitations of actors involved. VII. What steps the Maram and the concerned agency will take to ensure safety and confidentiality.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Because the project areas have been relatively peaceful and free of confrontations, they have been a popular destination for many IDPs. The MF safety and security field team is already monitoring and will continue to monitor the security situation in the CP project regions on a regular basis. Furthermore, MF teams in Syria are aware of emergency safety precautions and are conversant with project sites. MF will take all cautious precautions to prevent endangering MF's teams and project beneficiaries, particularly children. The Safety and Security Manager (SSM) is responsible for approving the security strategy, leading and participating in the risk management process, and taking ownership of risk mitigation/contingency planning and implementation. In order to ensure security within the targeted camps, MF employees must submit a mobility plan in advance, which must be authorized by the safety and security department in the event of an occurrence that activates the contingency plan. Project stakeholders aid in assessing risk action effectiveness and, if needed, engage in risk management.MF will ensure best practices in child safeguarding, Children's Safe standards, and improved responsibility to individuals who use or benefit from an organization's services. Each standard defines the important aspects that should be implemented to keep children safe and identifies the requirements that must be met in order to fulfill these standards.
