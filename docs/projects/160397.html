Project Summary

Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria States continue to experience chronic food insecurity, a lack of basic services, and few economic opportunities. FSNMS round 23 report covering up to end of 2018 indicates that Jonglei state had high prevalence of malnutrition with a GAM rate of 19.5%. SMART survey report (May 2018) by IMC show that Pibor County had malnutrition above emergency level with a GAM of 20.7%. FSNMS report round 22 show that Budi County had a GAM rate of 16.2% among children aged 6-59 months. SMART survey report (August 2017) by SCI show that Kapoeta North had malnutrition above emergency threshold with a GAM of 17.3%. Pibor, Budi, Kapoeta East and Kapoeta North counties are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) in the latest IPC report for August 2019. In Budi where GREDO is implementing nutrition activities, low livestock ownership at 31% of the population coupled with delayed rainfall, poor market access and high food prices are contributing to Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Thousands of people in are likely to face severe hunger due to lack of rainfall and unexpected crop failure according to situation on the ground in the greater Kapoeta consisting of Kapoeta North, Kapoeta East and Budi counties as the first phase of harvest has gone without food crop yields. This has been worsened by the current floods being experienced in most parts of the country. Furthermore, the IPC (AMN) report for Aug 2019 to April 2020 show that Budi, Kapoeta North and Pibor counties are in IPC Phase 4 and the nutrition situation is expected to deteriorate significantly as a result of unprecedented levels of food insecurity, high morbidity, outbreaks of diarrhea and other illness, poor infant and young feeding practices as well as limited access to services due to the heavy rains. During this time these three counties are expected to reach Critical levels of acute malnutrition. As a result, treatment of acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM) for children below five years and PLWs continue to be one of the key needs for saving lives of most at risk children and PLWs.
With continued high levels of malnutrition, there is need for continued provision of nutrition services through existing OTP and TSFP sites and scaling up services through outreaches. In order to have high impact response, SAM and MAM management need to be complemented with preventive responses targeting vulnerable and at risk children and PLWs. This proposal seeks to address nutritional needs of children <5yrs and PLWs affected by acute malnutrition in Budi, Pibor and Kapoeta North counties through both Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program(TSFP). This is as a result of the prolonged decline in the nutrition situation affecting consumption, access to services and severely disrupting life-saving activities pushing communities deeper into a nutrition emergency.
GREDO will be using both static and mobile/outreach response approaches in the three counties to reach all malnourished children and PLWs especially in the hard to reach areas of. GREDO plans to provide equitable lifesaving nutrition interventions to women and men, boys and girls at existing sites while increasing and continuing program reach to underserved locations through outreaches and mobile clinics. This will include integration of cluster priorities such as Integrating malaria testing, NFI distribution, WASH supplies into CMAM activities and MIYCN activities. GREDO through this approach, targets to provide quality nutrition services to 2,971 (F=1545, M=1426) children with SAM (1171 in Budi, 900 in Pibor and 900 in Kapoeta North), 9056 (F=4709, M=4347) children with MAM (4956 in Budi, 2100 in Pibor and 2000 in Kapoeta North) and 6245 PLWs (2113 in Budi, 2109 in Pibor and 2023 in Kapoeta North).

1 - Needs

The IPC report for August 2019 -April 2020 show that Budi, Kapoeta North and Pibor counties are in IPC Phase 4 and the nutrition situation is expected to deteriorate significantly as a result of unprecedented levels of food insecurity, flooding being experienced across the country, high morbidity, outbreaks of diarrhea and other illness, poor infant and young feeding practices as well as limited access to services due to the heavy rains. During this time these counties are expected to reach Critical levels of acute malnutrition. These counties continue to experience high levels of malnutrition due to the deteriorating situation necessitating continued provision of nutrition services through existing OTP and TSFP sites and scaling up services through outreaches. Based on the recent (August 2019) South Sudan national IPC analysis, it is expected that in Pibor, Budi and Kapoeta North Counties the nutrition situation will have deteriorated to critical levels of acute malnutrition in this case being classified as phase 4 (Emergency). The areas of Ngauro, Loudo and Nagishot Payams in Budi County can be considered hard to reach as a result of the inaccessibility of the area considering its mountainous topography which has led to a decline in the nutrition situation affecting consumption, access to services and severely disrupting life-saving activities pushing communities deeper into a nutrition emergency. Flooding in Pibor has hampered movement and delivery of services and families have been displaced. The ultimate result of this will be reduced access to food and other services pushing families deeper into malnutrition. Treatment of acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM) continue to be one of key need for saving lives of most at risk children < 5 years and PLWs in these counties. In order to have high impact response, SAM and MAM management need to be complemented with preventive responses targeting vulnerable and at risk children and PLWs.

2 - Activities

To address the aforementioned needs, GREDO will carry out several activities including but not limited to the following: • Active case finding and referral of malnourished children (both SAM and MAM) and PLWs into appropriate nutrition programs for appropriate care.• Active social mobilization to encourage communities to send malnourished children to the nearest nutrition sites. • Treatment of both severe and moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months • Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among PLWs• Training health and nutrition care providers on management of acute malnutrition• Provision of quality MIYCN services in nutrition sites and community level• Test and treatment of malaria among children with SAM aged 0/6-59 months • Increase access to Infant and Young Feeding in Emergencies (IYCF-E) to mothers and caregivers through social mobilization, individual counselling and group discussion by CNV’s and MIYCN groups at community level and Nutrition workers at community and health facility level respectively• Rehabilitate 10 nutrition sites (Nahochod, Kadumakuch, Ngauro, Tulugi, Kathac, Hibongorok, Haliba, Ngatuba, Kapeta and Ngathilani) and ensure quality service delivery is provided through strengthening referral system and linkage through other nutrition services and sectors. • Rehabilitate 5 nutrition sites in Pibor with 3 outreaches in Lekuangule in the greater Pibor County• Rehabilitate 4 nutrition sites in Kapoeta North with 3 outreaches • Ensure timely, accurate and credible information sharing of program activities • Ensuring safe and dignified participation by beneficiaries in the program activities• Implement complaint and feedback mechanisms• Conducting safety audits in ensure that GREDO’s nutrition sites are safe for our beneficiaries• Conduct one a SMART survey in Budi CountyGREDO will be implementing minimum package of Integrating Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) within existing health facilities by providing routine treatment using simple medical protocol to SAM and MAM children under 5 and Pregnant and Lactating Mother’s (PLW’s). In addition, GREDO will work towards integrating Health, WASH and FSL into Nutrition targeting 19 OTP/TSFP sites and 6 Outreaches. GREDO will provide these lifesaving nutrition services in collaboration with MoH, partners and local authorities through supporting initiatives for the decentralization of TSFP sites, increasing awareness through community mobilization. GREDO will in essence: - Carry out door-to-door screening of children and PLWs in the community for malnutrition using both MUAC and Weight-For Height assessment criteria once every month at the community level and routine screening at the health facilities; Advocate for IYCF/MIYCN Practices for prevention of Malnutrition.HRP funding will enable GREDO to provide targeted emergency nutrition supplies and services to a MAM caseload of up to 9056 for under 5 children and 6245 PLW’s, and to a SAM case load of 2971 for the year 2020. GREDO will train CNVs and MIYCN groups from host community to acquire skills and knowledge on Integrated Management of SAM/MAM and MIYCN so as to provide quality nutrition services.Existing health facility staff will be capacitated for integration of Acute Malnutrition management into health programs especially on malaria screening and referral of malnourished children under 5 and PLWs to the OTP/TSFP interventions. GREDO Project focal persons will undergo the mandatory IASC Gender Marker online assessment to ensure clear understanding of best practices of gender in the humanitarian context. GREDO's Health and nutrition staff including clinicians, nutrition assistants, nurses and midwives will be shared in this programme to increase cost effectiveness and strengthen output results.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project will at all times prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects resulting from the intervention, which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. Therefore displacement affected communities will access or benefit from the project

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

GREDO is implementing nutrition activities in collaboration with the CHD, SMoH and the national MoH. IT uses the community systems especially in mobilization and sensitization activities and the referral pathways

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is in line with the South Sudan nutrition cluster objectives and adheres to the national CMAM and MIYCN guidelines

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The protection environment is sufficiently safe and stable to cater for the needs of young children both boys and girls and the pregnant and lactating women. At all times GREDO always seeks to understand who is at risk, from what or whom as well as why, and the consequences their actions or inaction may have on the threats people experience and their vulnerability and capacity vis a vis these threats . This includes knowing how and where to refer people in need for specialist support to prevent or recover from violence and exploitation, as well as understanding when, how, and to whom to refer specialized protection issues. Therefore GREDO does risk analysis to fully understand the specific needs of different population groups.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

YesGREDO expects majority of beneficiaries targeted to have recovered from malnutrition at least by 80% of the total target (people in need) by the end of project phase. Majority of the CNVs shall undergo different nutrition trainings to ensure the knowledge sharing within the community. During project implementation, beneficiaries will be encouraged to consume locally available food and encouraged to cultivate short term maturing crops. GREDO also anticipates the involvement of other partners i.e. FAO to provide seeds and tools for the beneficiaries affected to improve the general nutrition status of the communities in the three counties. The beneficiaries will be informed of the entry and exit criteria as set by CMAM guidelines to avoid protection related issues at the end of project. Nutrition education shall be taught at the distribution points to ensure beneficiaries understand the objective of the program. Referrals shall be made for Severe Acute Malnutrition with medical complications cases to nearest health facility with SC. Constant project monitoring with monthly and quarterly reports shall be provided to assess the success or failure of project objectives and shared with donor for decision making and corrective actions.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

GREDO does not anticipate major risks to the project except for the insecurity that can result in looting of food from her stores. This risk will be mitigated by having security guards to guard the main warehouse 24/7 and only take adequate nutrition supplies to OTP/TSFP to meet the nutritional needs of those sites on the days of follow-up so that not too much supplies are kept at the nutrition site. Also, accessibility issues due to hard terrains considering this is a mountainous area. For this GREDO will seek to utilize local staffs who are familiar with the terrains and will pre-position supplies in the targeted locations to ensure proper flow of services to the community. GREDO will also engage the community to carry the supplies to the targeted location in cases where the location cannot be accessed by a vehicle. GREDO will engage all members of the community to ensure all inclusive approach is adhered to for all members of the community
