Project Summary

Drug abuse and its prevalence within Cameroon is an aged old problem which has over the years eaten into the socio-cultural and developmental structure of the country. Cameroon’s Anti-Drug National Committee’s estimated that 21% of the nation’s population have consumed hard drugs and 10% are regular consumers. The situation is further degraded with over 60% of drug users being youths under 25 years of age. Efforts by some stakeholders like the government, NGOs and UN agencies have contributed in mitigating drug use prevalence within the country, however, some factors such as the socio-political crisis, peer pressure and effective drug supply system, still reinforce the prevalence of drug abuse within the country.
Mindful of the perceived gaps and needs, this project aims to strengthen family and community capacities in drug abuse prevention, strengthen schools’ capacities to address individual students’ psychological vulnerabilities and equally to carry out extensive mass media campaign on the ills of drug abuse and drug abuse prevention. The project will target youths within schools and communities, and make immense use of youth groups, and youth-led organisations in its realisation.
The global objective of this project is to eradicate drug abuse among children and youths within the Littoral and Southwest Region.
To attain this global objective, some specific objectives have been put in place;
- To carry out a four-month long anti-drug abuse mass media campaign
- To strengthen family and community capacities in drug abuse prevention
- To strengthen schools’ capacities to address individual students’ psychological vulnerabilities

1 - Besoins / Needs

Drug Abuse: The prevalence of drug abuse within the towns of Douala and Buea in the Littoral and South West regions respectively have led to increased prevalence of violence and vandalism perpetuated by youths within their respective communities. Drug abuse and its prevalence within Cameroon is an aged old problem which has over the years eaten into the socio-cultural and developmental structure of the country. Cameroon’s Anti-Drug National Committee’s estimated that 21% of the nation’s population have consumed hard drugs and 10% are regular consumers.With all this in view, the NW-SW Crisis which has devoured the North West and South West regions of Cameroon has added an additional layer of enforcers of drug abuse. The grave insecurity within both regions has led to over concentration of IDP population in both the towns of Buea and Douala which present as relatively safer towns; this has resulted to excessive pressure on available resources and increased unemployment within both towns. The result is increased unlawful activities - drug dealing- by youths and other actors in an attempt to meet ends meet. Being addictive in nature, hard drugs dealing within both towns has created an active and aggressively growing market which is inclusive of youths and children. Hard drugs use is currently carried out by university students, school drop outs, IDPs and sex workers primarily, and has in recent times been identified in secondary school milieus among students. to meet these issues, the project sets forth with Media Campaigns via TV/Radio campaigns, social media campaigns, production and distribution of Information, education and Communication (IEC) materialsto raise awareness on the ills of drug abuse and its effects on the community and societal advancements.Equally, the project proposes a community based multi-component initiative in view of training on ills of drug abuse and drug abuse prevention with community leaders and community-based structures. The initiative also seeks to address family level emotional and behavioral disorders: all in an attempt enhance community and family capacity to support anti-drug campaigns and enforce drug abuse rehabilitation endeavors.Also the project will address schools policy on substance use through training of teachers to adequately manage poor behavior and praise proper behavior during classes, advocate for zero substance use tolerance within schools and establish Drug Abuse clubs within schools. This is to counter the prevalence of drug abuse within schools and also enhance the identification of drug users in school.And because rehabilitation from drug abuse is a hard process and the ease of sliding in drug abuse is equally quite high, the project will address individual psychological vulnerabilities via periodic mandatory guidance and counseling sessions for children and youths in schools.
